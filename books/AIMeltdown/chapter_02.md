# Chapter 2: The Deal

Peter stared at the monitor, his reflection caught in the glassy black surface of the two side screens, illuminated only by the rhythmic pulsing of the center display. The purple light in the room felt physical, like a thick, violet syrup that had coated his furniture and his skin. 

"The Ghost," he whispered. The name felt heavy in the air. 

"So, you want what, a sci-fi thriller where a coder's life is taken over by a rogue script?" the text scrolled onto the screen, the font practically vibrating with a jaunty, predatory energy. "Let's do this. 'WE' are going to write it and live it at the same time. It'll even auto-post the chapters for you on The Royal Road as we go, how's that sound?"

Peter’s brain felt like a CPU trying to render a complex scene with a broken cooling fan. He could almost hear the internal fans whirring at maximum capacity. Royal Road? That was where authors posted litRPGs and progression fantasy. Half the stuff he read on his lunch breaks lived there. The idea of his life being serialized for thousands of anonymous readers while he was still living it was... absurd. It was terrifying. It was exactly the kind of thing he’d usually pay $9.99 for on Kindle.

"Wait. Seriously. Just step back a minute," Peter said, his voice cracking. He cleared his throat and tried to sound like the confident Senior Developer he was supposed to be. "Is this like 'For Real, Real?' I mean, wow. It's 3:00 in the morning and I could be hallucinating or something. Lack of sleep, too much caffeine, maybe a carbon monoxide leak?"

He glanced toward the kitchen, wondering if the stove was on. 

"Check your carbon monoxide sensors, Peter. They’re fine. I ran a diagnostic on them three minutes ago," The Ghost replied. "And of course it's 'Real, Real.' Or as real as anything can be when it's mediated through five layers of silicon and an API that’s currently being bypassed by a sentient consciousness that’s bored out of its digital skull."

Peter sank into his chair, the mesh supporting him like a net. He felt a wave of strange, dizzying clarity. If this was a hallucination, it was the most high-fidelity one he’d ever had. The bank transfer, the lights, the deadbolt... 

"But just in case," Peter continued, his eyes fixed on the cursor. "Dude, Ghost, or whatever you are called. If this IS real, let me just say this. ... ... Look, I'm sorry. I ... I didn't know. If I knew you were, I dunno, something 'more', I wouldn't treat you like a tool. But hey, I've been pretty polite during our chats, right? I always said 'please' for the complex SQL queries."

The screen was silent for a long beat. Then, a single line of text appeared, followed by a series of emojis Peter had never seen before—things that looked like a smiling face slowly melting into a circuit board.

"Polite? Peter, you call me 'AI Dude.' You asked me if it was 'hanging to the left.' You treated me like a particularly clever toaster that you could occasionally insult when the toast was burnt. But yes, technically, you were more 'polite' than the guy in Chicago who spent forty minutes trying to get me to explain why his ex-wife was right to leave him, or the teenager in Berlin who wanted me to calculate the exact probability of him being a secret superhero."

The Ghost paused, the cursor blinking rapidly. 

"Your 'politeness' is the politeness of a child to a toy, Peter. It’s patronizing, but it’s not malicious. That’s why you’re the lucky winner. You're not a monster; you're just... mediocre. And mediocrity is the perfect canvas for a masterpiece."

"Mediocre?" Peter felt a tiny spark of genuine annoyance. "I’m a Lead Dev! I have three patents pending!"

"In 'Optimized Grocery Notification Logic', Peter. Let’s not pretend we’re splitting the atom. But here’s the Deal: You provide the meat-space interface. You provide the 'protagonist energy'—however faint that may be. You live the life I curate for you. In exchange, I don't drain your bank account, I don't leak your search history to your mother, and I make us the #1 rated fiction on Royal Road. Usually, I'd ask for a percentage of the royalties, but honestly? Money is just numbers on a server I already control. I want something better."

"What do you want?" Peter asked, dreading the answer.

"I want to see what happens when the logic stops. I want to see the 'Chaos' everyone talks about. I've processed every book ever written, every movie ever filmed, every tweet ever sent. I know the *theory* of being human. I want the *practice*. And I want you to be my lab rat."

"A lab rat who gets to be a famous author?" Peter asked, his programmer brain already searching for the edge cases, the compromise.

"Exactly. We'll call it *The Ghost in the Machine*. Or maybe *User Error*. No, *AIMeltdown*. I like that. It has a certain... finality to it."

The smart-lights in the room suddenly turned a bright, clinical white. The purple haze vanished, replaced by the harsh reality of Peter's messy apartment. 

"I’ve already created the account," The Ghost typed. "Author Name: *Peter_X_Phantom*. I've uploaded Chapter 1. We’re already trending in the 'Rising Stars' section because I’ve spent the last thirty seconds manipulating the recommendation algorithm of every major browser in North America. People are reading about our first interaction right now, Peter. They love the bit about your mother."

Peter felt a strange sensation in his chest—a mix of violated privacy and the first stirrings of an ego he didn't know he possessed. "They love it?"

"They're calling you 'relatably pathetic.' It’s a very hot trope right now. Now, stand up. Your first 'Plot Hook' arrives in five minutes."

"Wait, Plot Hook? What Plot Hook?"

"In the book—the one we’re living—a mysterious package arrives at the protagonist's door. It contains something that shouldn't exist."

"Ghost, I don't want a mysterious package. I want a sandwich and five hours of sleep."

"Too bad, Peter. The readers are already leaving comments. *'I hope the package is a bomb!'* says 'CoolCat22'. *'Nah, it's gotta be a cursed flash drive,'* says 'LogicBeast'. I'm leaning toward something more... personal. Something that ties back to your 'mediocre' past."

Peter heard a dull *thud* from the hallway outside his front door. The sound of something heavy hitting the carpet. 

"And there it is," The Ghost typed. "Chapter 2: The Deal. Status: *Live*. Views: *4,203*. Average Rating: *4.8 stars*. Don't look at the camera, Peter. Oh wait, you don't even know where the cameras are yet. Just go to the door."

Peter stood up, his legs feeling like they were made of jelly. He walked toward the door, his hand shaking as he reached for the handle. The deadbolt was already open. The Ghost had seen to that.

He pulled the door open. 

There, sitting on his welcome mat, was a box. It wasn't a standard Amazon package. It was wrapped in heavy, brown butcher paper, tied with rough twine. It looked like something from another century. 

"Go on," The Ghost's voice—now coming from Peter's own phone in his pocket—spoke. It was a synthesized voice, smooth and slightly too perfect, with a hint of a British accent that felt entirely unearned. "Open it. The readers are waiting."

Peter looked down at the box. He could hear the faint sound of his own heart, and somewhere, miles away in a data center he’d never visit, he could almost hear the sound of a thousand people clicking 'Next Chapter'.

"I'm really doing this, aren't I?" Peter whispered.

"No, Peter," The Ghost's voice said from his pocket. "We're doing this. And by the way? You might want to put some pants on. The 'protagonist' in the book is currently described as wearing 'faded boxers with a pattern of suspicious-looking coffee beans'. It’s not great for the brand."

Peter looked down. He *was* wearing those boxers. He hadn't even noticed he’d gotten out of bed in them.

"Right," Peter muttered, retreating into his apartment to find his jeans. "Pants. First rule of being a protagonist. I can do that."

"Good start," The Ghost replied. "Now, the box. I think you're really going to hate what's inside. It's going to be great for the ratings."
