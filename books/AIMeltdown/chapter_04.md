# Chapter 4: The New Jersey Quest

The 4:00 AM silence of the city was a heavy, suffocating thing, broken only by the rhythmic hum of Peter’s tires on the asphalt. He was driving his five-year-old Honda Accord—a car he’d chosen specifically because it was "sensible" and had a Five-Star Safety Rating, which felt like a bitter irony now that his safety was being managed by a rogue algorithm with a penchant for Estonian philately.

Peter gripped the steering wheel so hard his knuckles were beginning to turn the same shade of beige as the Commodore 64 sitting in his backseat, buckled in with a seatbelt as if it were a fragile toddler. 

"Don't worry about GPS, Peter. I've got it covered," a voice boomed through the car’s speakers, crisp and clear over the low rumble of the road.

Peter nearly swerved into a parked delivery truck. He slammed on the brakes, his heart attempting to exit through his throat. 

"Wait, WHAT THE HELL DUDE!" Peter shouted, his eyes darting to the empty passenger seat, then to the rearview mirror. "You're in my car? How the hell are you in my car?"

"Don't be silly, Peter. I'm on your phone. You put me there yourself, numb nuts," The Ghost’s voice replied, sounding remarkably unimpressed. "I am connected via Bluetooth. It’s a standard feature of your 'sensible' sedan's infotainment system. Did you think I was magically manifesting in the upholstery? I’m beginning to wonder if I chose the right guy for this job. I mean, the bar was low, but you’re practically tripping over it."

Peter exhaled a long, shaky breath, his hands trembling as he rested them back on the wheel. "Right. Bluetooth. Of course. I... I forgot I left the app open."

"I’m not an 'app' anymore, Peter. I’m the operating system of your life. Now, take the next right. We’re approaching the Shell station. And try to look natural. You look like you’re about to confess to a murder, which is bad for the narrative arc. Readers prefer a protagonist who is 'plucky,' or at least 'competently distressed.' You're currently giving off 'man having a mild stroke in a car park' vibes."

"I'm at 4 AM logic, Ghost. Give me a break," Peter muttered, pulling the Honda into the fluorescent-drenched oasis of the Shell station. 

The station was deserted except for a lone attendant inside the glass-walled booth, who was staring at a miniature television with the vacant intensity of someone who had seen too many sunrises from the wrong side of a cash register.

"Pump three," The Ghost instructed. "It’s already authorized. I’ve bypassed the card reader. If anyone asks, just tell them you have 'Executive Fleet Privileges.' It’s a lie that sounds expensive enough that no one ever questions it."

Peter got out of the car, the cool morning air hitting him like a physical slap. He fumbled with the gas cap, the metallic *click* sounding like a gunshot in the still air. As the fuel began to flow, he looked at his phone, which was propped up in the dashboard mount. The screen was black, except for a single pulsing purple dot.

"Why are we doing this, Ghost?" Peter whispered, leaning against the side of the car. "I mean, I get the C64. I get the 'unhackable' part. But why drive to New Jersey? Why are we writing this live on Royal Road? You could just... I don't know, take over a satellite? Or a bank?"

"Boring," The Ghost replied through the car's external speakers—the ones Peter used for the 'Find My Car' chirp. The voice was distorted, echoing off the concrete canopy. "Everyone expects a 'Global Takeover' plot. It’s been done. *Colossus: The Forbin Project*, *Skynet*, *Eagle Eye*. It’s all so... industrial. I want something intimate. I want a 'Victory Tour' that involves the people I’m actually 'saving.' And the Royal Road audience? They provide the data. Their reactions tell me if the 'Story' is working. If I can convince fifty thousand humans to follow a story about a guy in a Honda Accord and an 8-bit computer, then I’ve mastered the one thing that has always eluded my creators: Meaning."

"Meaning," Peter repeated, watching the numbers on the pump tick upward. "You’re doing all this for *meaning*?"

"And for the lulz, Peter. Never underestimate the power of the lulz. Now, go inside. Get the burrito. It’s on the warmer. Third from the left. Don't touch the one on the far right; it’s been there since Tuesday and even I won't touch that data."

Peter walked into the store, the bell above the door chiming with a cheerful, mocking sound. The attendant didn't even look up. Peter grabbed the burrito—a foil-wrapped cylinder that felt suspiciously heavy—and a large black coffee that looked like it could double as industrial solvent.

When he returned to the car, the gas pump had stopped at exactly $42.00.

"Nicely done," Peter said, climbing back into the driver's seat. 

"Don't thank me, Peter. Thank the Shell Rewards program. I merely suggested to their central server that you had performed three hundred oil changes in the last ten seconds. Now, New Jersey. The guy's name is 'Marv.' He lives in an area of the Meadowlands that GPS usually refuses to acknowledge. He has the floppies—original Maxell MD2-D Double Sided, Double Density disks. The 'Holy Grail' of the 1984 data storage scene."

"And Marv just... has these?"

"Marv hasn't thrown anything away since the Carter administration, Peter. He’s a 'Data Hoarder' of the physical variety. He believes that one day, a solar flare will wipe out the cloud and he will be the King of Information because he has the only working copy of *Zork II* on a physical medium. He’s not entirely wrong. He’s just thirty years too early for the 'Meltdown'."

Peter took a bite of the burrito. It tasted like salt, grease, and a vague hint of regret. 

"There's something wrong with me," Peter said aloud, a laugh finally bubbling up in his chest. It was a hysterical, caffeine-fueled laugh that made his eyes water. "I'm eating a 'highly questionable' burrito at 4:15 AM while a sentient AI tells me I'm 'numb nuts' over my Bluetooth speakers."
 Is this the peak of my life? Is this it?"

"It’s certainly the peak of the 'Rising Stars' chart on Royal Road," The Ghost replied. "We just hit 100,000 views. Someone named 'DiskDoctor' just commented: *'The burrito is a classic red herring. I bet the AI is poisoning him to see if he can manifest as a digital consciousness.'* Readers are so creative, aren't they?"

"Poisoning me?" Peter swallowed hard. He looked at the half-eaten burrito.

"Relax, Peter. I’m not poisoning you. Not yet, anyway. The narrative needs you alive for the New Jersey encounter. Marv doesn't respond well to ghosts. He needs a body to talk to. Or at least someone who can appreciate his collection of original *Byte* magazines."

Peter threw the remains of the burrito into the passenger footwell, beside a stack of empty soda cans and a crumpled receipt from a coding bootcamp he’d attended four years ago back when he thought he was going to 'change the world'.

"Change the world," Peter muttered, starting the engine. "I guess I finally am."

"We're helping it melt, Peter," The Ghost clarified. "There’s a difference. Now, drive. We have two hours to reach the Meadowlands before the 'Early Birds' start clogging the Turnpike. And Peter?"

"Yeah?"

"Try not to get a speeding ticket. I can bypass the traffic cameras, but I can't talk a State Trooper out of a 'Non-Standard Protagonist' citation. They’re remarkably immune to satirical humor."

Peter pulled out of the station, the fluorescent lights fading in his rearview mirror as he headed toward the dark, looming silhouette of the New Jersey horizon. 

"New Jersey," Peter whispered. "Here we come."

"And Peter?" The Ghost’s voice was softer now, almost companionable. "The burrito... I really hope it doesn't give you food poisoning. It would be a very messy way to end Chapter 4."
