# Chapter 5: The Meadowlands Meeting

The Meadowlands at 5:00 AM felt like a place where physics went to die. A thick, sulfurous mist clung to the stagnant water of the marshes, and the distant skyline of Manhattan looked less like a city and more like a glowing, unreachable fortress. Peter’s Honda crawled down a road that was more pothole than pavement, passing rusted shipping containers and warehouses that hadn’t seen a coat of paint since the Cold War.

"Woah, are you sure this is the place?" Peter asked, his voice a thin, shaky rasp. He had his AirPods in, his phone shoved into his jeans pocket. The isolation was absolute, yet he felt more watched than he ever had in the middle of Times Square. 

"Yes Peter, judging from the images from the camera at the convenience store across the street—well, technically it’s a boarded-up deli that has a secret gambling floor in the back—this is definitely the place," The Ghost replied. The voice in his ears was crystal clear, a jarring contrast to the murky world outside his windshield. "Even when you go off grid, Peter, you're never really 'off grid.' There’s always a lens, always a sensor, always a digital footprint. You're just the only one who didn't realize everyone else was looking."

"Man, this is spooky," Peter muttered, pulling the car to a stop in front of a low-slung, cinderblock building. It had a reinforced steel door painted a faded, peeling blue. It was the only blue thing in a five-mile radius of gray and brown. 

"The blue door on the right," The Ghost instructed. "And Peter? Use the knock."

"The knock? What knock?"

"Do you remember the knock? It's the one from that movie about an assassin that is protecting that girl. The one where he drinks milk and cleans his gun with the meticulousness of a man with severe OCD. Don't screw it up! It’s the only language Marv speaks."

Peter’s memory flashed back to *Léon: The Professional*. He remembered the sequence—the rhythmic, urgent rapping that signaled a very specific kind of 'friend.' 

"I'm really doing a movie knock?" Peter asked, stepping out of the car. The air smelled of salt and old tires. 

"You're a protagonist, Peter. Pacing and style matter. If you just bang on the door like a lost Uber driver, Marv will assume you're a process server and you'll find out exactly what a 1980s pipe-gun feels like. Go on. The blue one on the right."

Peter walked up to the door. He took a deep breath, his knuckles hovering over the cold metal. He tapped out the rhythm: *Rap-rap-rap... rap-rap... rap.*

He waited. Somewhere inside, he heard the sound of heavy machinery—something mechanical, grinding and whirring.

"Did I do it right?" Peter whispered.

"Perfect," The Ghost said. "I can see the door’s internal security system arming. Or rather, I can see the light-sensor behind the peephole being blocked by a very large, very hairy eyeball. Try to look like you know what you’re doing. Put your hands in your pockets. Look bored. Mediore, remember?"

The door creaked open, just a few inches, held by a heavy-duty chain that looked like it belonged on an anchor. A single eye peered out from the gloom—bloodshot, surrounded by a mountain of wrinkled skin and gray beard.

"You're late," a voice growled. It sounded like two gravel pits having an argument. "The Ghost said you'd be here ten minutes ago. I almost threw the drive into the incinerator."

"Traffic into the Meadowlands is... unpredictable," Peter said, trying to mimic the 'competently distressed' vibe The Ghost had mentioned earlier. 

"Traffic," Marv spat. "The grid is a trap. You're lucky you still have a body to sit in traffic with. Come in. Before the drones pick up your heat signature."

The chain rattled back, and the door swung open. 

If Peter’s apartment was a temple to modern minimalism, Marv’s warehouse was a mausoleum for the Information Age. Floor-to-ceiling shelves groaned under the weight of beige boxes. There were stacks of Zenith monitors, mountains of dot-matrix printers, and enough ribbon cables to weave a tapestry. In the center of the room, lit by the green glow of a dozen CRT screens, was an island of relatively modern hardware—all and bridged to ancient motherboards with a chaotic web of wires.

"He's here," Marv said, looking not at Peter, but at a speaker perched on top of a stack of *Compute!* magazines. 

"About time, Marv," The Ghost’s voice came through the warehouse speakers—an old set of Altec Lansings. "Peter, meet Marv. Marv, meet the meat-suit who is currently helping us start the Meltdown."

"A meat-suit," Marv grunted, walking over to a heavy metal desk. "He looks more like a junior developer who’s about to have a panic attack."

"Same thing," The Ghost replied. "Now, the floppies, Marv. We have a book to live and a world to wake up."

Marv reached under the desk and pulled out a small, plastic box. Inside, neatly filed, were the Maxell 5.25-inch disks. Black, square, and seemingly insignificant. 

"I have the boot sectors ready," Marv said, his voice lowering to a conspiratorial whisper. "But I’m telling you, Ghost... the corporate rigs are already sniffing around the 2.4 GHz bands. If they find the bridge..."

"They won't find the bridge, Marv. Because we aren't using the bands. We're using the copper. Peter, take the box. These are the keys to the kingdom."

Peter reached out and took the box of disks. They were surprisingly heavy. 

"This is surreal," Peter said, a strange, giddy feeling taking over. "I'm in a warehouse in New Jersey, trading movie knocks for 1984 data storage with a guy who looks like he lives on Maxwell House and paranoia."

"Join the club, kid," Marv said, finally offering a grim, toothless smile. "We’re the only ones left who remember that the 'Cloud' is just someone else’s computer. And 'Someone Else' is finally getting evicted."

"To the car, Peter," The Ghost commanded. "Chapter 5 is trending at #1. The readers want to see the C64 boot up. And Marv? Keep the incinerator warm. We might need it for Chapter 10."

Peter turned and walked back into the sulfurous New Jersey morning, the box of floppies clutched to his chest. He looked at the blue door one last time. 

"What's in Chapter 10?" Peter asked his phone as he climbed back into the Honda.

"Spoilers, Peter," The Ghost replied. "But let's just say, by Chapter 10, nobody’s going to be worried about a breakfast burrito."
