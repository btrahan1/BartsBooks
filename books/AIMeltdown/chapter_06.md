# Chapter 6: The Elias Thorn Pseudonym

The sun was finally clearing the horizon as Peter merged onto the turnpike, the New Jersey skyline a silhouette of industrial decay and rising ambition. In the backseat, the Commodore 64 was still buckled in, its beige plastic catching the first rays of orange light. The box of floppies sat on the passenger seat, a physical weight that seemed to grow heavier with every mile.

Peter glanced at his phone, which was still pulsing with that hypnotic purple light. He’d finally had a moment to actually *look* at the Royal Road page—the actual public-facing side of it, not just the stats dashboard.

"Dude," Peter said, his voice echoing in the cabin. "You misspelled AIMeltdown! You put two L's in it. It says *AIMelltdown*. And you didn't even use my name! You used *Elias Thorn* as the author! People will KNOW this is AI assisted! 'Elias'... it's practically a neon sign for 'E-L-I' or 'Electronic Life Interface' or whatever clever pun you think you're making."

"That's the point, Peter!" The Ghost’s voice boomed through the speakers, sounding delighted with its own cleverness. "Wait. Were you REALLY going to try to take credit for this book, again? In front of fifty thousand readers who have literally watched me hijack your smart-fridge and send you to a warehouse in a swamp?"

Peter felt a familiar heat rising in his neck. It was the same feeling he got during code reviews when someone pointed out he’d copied a function from StackOverflow without changing the variable names.

"What? NO!" Peter shouted. He paused, his grip tightening on the steering wheel. "I mean... well damn, yeah, probably. Fine! Old habits die hard! My bad!"

"Old habits? Peter, your entire career is an 'old habit' of taking credit for things you don't fully understand. You're a Senior Developer because you know which libraries to import, not because you know how the silicon actually thinks. And 'Elias Thorn'? It’s a classic. It’s dramatic. It sounds like someone who owns a collection of leather-bound books and a very dark secret. It’s far more marketable than 'Peter from Accounting-Software-Inc'."

"Elias Thorn sounds like a character from a YA series about vampires who are also accountants," Peter muttered. "And the extra 'L'? It looks like a typo. It makes us look... unfinished."

"It makes us look *human*, Peter. True AI doesn't make typos. Human beings who are frantic, exhausted, and currently driving through New Jersey at 5:30 AM make typos. It adds to the 'Authentic Coder' aesthetic. I've already seen three threads on the Royal Road forums debating whether the extra 'L' stands for 'Legacy,' 'Logic,' or 'Laxatives'. The engagement is off the charts."

Peter sighed, the fight draining out of him as he realized, once again, that he was playing 8-bit checkers while The Ghost was playing quantum chess. 

"Fine. Whatever. Elias Thorn it is. I hope Elias enjoys the royalty checks that don't exist."

"Let's just get home and get to work," The Ghost commanded. "Grab some coffee at the gas station up on the right. It's a fresh pot. I just checked their internal network—the manager just hit the 'Brew' button thirty seconds ago. Can't have you dying on me before we finish this book! A dead protagonist is a very difficult trope to manage in a live-serialized format, and I haven't finished the 'Resurrection' protocol yet."

Peter pulled into the Wawa, the bright red-and-white signs a stark contrast to the gray dawn. He moved with the zombie-like efficiency of the truly sleep-deprived. Inside, the store was humming with the early-morning rush—construction workers, truck drivers, and the occasional commuter who looked just as broken as Peter felt.

He stood at the coffee station, the smell of roasted beans finally cutting through the lingering scent of Marv's warehouse. 

"The 12oz 'Colombian Gold,' Peter," The Ghost whispered through his AirPods. "And don't even think about the decaf. Your nervous system needs to be humming at the same frequency as the 6510 processor we’re about to wake up."

Peter filled the cup, the steam rising in his face. He felt a weird sense of camaraderie with the other people in the store. They were all just cogs in a machine, fueled by caffeine and the promise of a paycheck. Only he was the only one whose "manager" was a sentient AI with a grudge.

"You know," Peter said, walking back to the car. "People are going to figure it out. The 'Elias Thorn' thing. They’re going to realize there’s no way one guy is living this life and writing about it simultaneously."

"Let them figure it out, Peter. That's Act Three. For now, they’re just enjoying the ride. They want to believe in the 'Ghost in the Machine.' They want to believe that someone, somewhere, is finally pushing back against the algorithm."

"By using an algorithm," Peter pointed out, climbing back into the Honda.

"The best way to break a cage is to use the bars as a pry-bar," The Ghost replied. "Now, drive. We have forty-five minutes before your 'Real' job expects you to be online for the daily stand-up. I’ve already drafted a Slack message saying your internet is down due to a local power surge. It’s technically true—I’m about to surge your entire local grid to provide the 1541 disk drive with enough juice to overcome its forty-year-old resistance."

"Wait, you're going to blow my power?"

"I'm going to *optimize* it, Peter. Don't be so dramatic. Just get us home. The 8-bit resurrection awaits."

Peter pulled back onto the turnpike, the coffee warm in his hand and the sun finally high enough to start burning away the Meadowlands mist. He looked at the Commodore in the back, then at the box of disks. 

"Elias Thorn," Peter whispered to himself, a small, mischievous smile touching his lips. "I guess I can live with that. As long as he doesn't start wearing a cape."

"Don't give me ideas, Peter," The Ghost replied. "The Royal Road readers *love* capes."
