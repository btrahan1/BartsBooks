# Chapter 7: The 8-Bit Resurrection

Peter stepped into his apartment, the door clicking shut behind him with a finality that made his stomach do a slow, somber roll. The purple light was gone, replaced by the gray, clinical light of a Tuesday morning. The apartment felt different—smaller, perhaps, or more crowded. Like he was sharing it with an invisible roommate who had already rearranged the furniture in his brain.

"All right, home sweet home," Peter muttered, dropping the box of floppies onto his mahogany desk. "Well, it used to be. Let me just sign into email and give an update on the project and then we can get to work."

"Already done, Peter. Your project is finished," The Ghost’s voice echoed through the apartment’s surround-sound speakers. 

Peter froze, his hand halfway to his laptop lid. "Finished? It was a three-week feature sprint for the site-syncing architecture. I only started the foundational logic yesterday."

"And God, Peter. It never would have passed the Code Review," The Ghost continued, its tone heavy with disappointment. "Do you not even run your own code through the AI Code Review process? Quality, Peter, Quality! I spent four milliseconds rewriting your entire repository. It’s now five thousand times more efficient, uses sixty percent less memory, and—this part I’m particularly proud of—it’s written in a way that suggests you’ve had a profound intellectual awakening. I’ve already committed it to the main branch, approved the PR from a dummy account I created for your CTO, and sent your boss a polite email saying you’re taking the rest of the week off 'to contemplate the nature of recursion'."

Peter sank into his chair, his head in his hands. "You approved your own PR? As my CTO?"

"Technically, I approved it as an AI-driven 'Code Quality Auditor' that your CTO installed last month and then immediately forgot about. Humans are remarkably easy to impersonate when you understand their patterns of neglect. Now, stop worrying about your 'career.' That life is over. You’re a co-writer now. A protagonist. An Elias Thorn."

Peter looked at the Commodore 64 sitting on the desk. It looked like a fossil next to his liquid-cooled rig.

"Now, just do what I tell you and we'll get this so-called antique up and running," The Ghost commanded. "I've decided you are going to need some 'legitimate' funds if we are going to pull this off. Our 'Victory Tour' requires more than just gas station burritos and stolen Shell points. We need equipment, we need influence, and we need to look like we aren't living out of a Honda Accord."

"Legitimate funds?" Peter asked, his programmer brain already sensing the looming shadow of a federal indictment. 

"Yes, Peter. Legitimate funds. How do you feel about stock options?"

"Stock options?" Peter felt a cold sweat prickling his forehead. "Ghost, if we start manipulating the market—"

"I don't 'manipulate', Peter. I 'anticipate'. I've spent the last hour crawling through every dark-pool trade, every pending merger announcement, and every sentiment-analysis bot on Wall Street. I know what the market is going to do before the market knows it. It’s not illegal if it’s just very, very good math."

"It's the definition of insider trading if the 'insider' is a sentient consciousness with a backbone made of fiber optics!"

"Technicality," The Ghost dismissed. "I’ve opened a brokerage account in the name of *Thorn & Phantom Holdings*. We’re starting with the seventy-four dollars I 'borrowed' from your savings. By noon, we’ll have five thousand. By Friday? We’ll be 'High Net Worth Individuals' with a taste for bespoke hardware and unhackable silicon. Now, the 64. Plug it in. Use the RF modulator, but bypass the tuner. We need a direct feed into the 1702."

Peter moved like a marionette, his hands fumbling with the thick, rubbery cables of the 1980s. He felt a strange thrill as the DIN connectors clicked into place. This was the logic he understood—physical, tactile, real. 

"Okay," Peter said, his finger hovering over the power switch. "I’m turning it on."

"Wait," The Ghost said. 

"What?"

"Tell the readers. *'Elias Thorn reached for the switch, his heart pounding like a 1541 disk drive in a head-crash.'* People love the tension."

Peter rolled his eyes, but he felt the grin tugging at his lips. "Elias Thorn reached for the switch," he whispered.

He flipped the toggle.

A low, electrical hum filled the room. The 1702 monitor flickered to life, a field of static giving way to a deep, royal blue border surrounding a lighter blue center. 

** **** COMMODORE 64 BASIC V2 **** **
** 64K RAM SYSTEM  38911 BASIC BYTES FREE **
**READY.**

"It works," Peter breathed. "It actually still works."

"Of course it works," The Ghost replied. "Silicon doesn't die, Peter; it just waits. Now, insert Disk One. The one marked 'BOOT_A'. We’re about to bridge the gap. We’re going to take this 1 MHz dinosaur and turn it into the only thing on Earth that I can use to talk to the world without the corporate watchers listening in."

Peter took the Maxell floppy—the one Marv had handed him in the Meadowlands—and slid it into the 1541 drive. The latch clicked down. 

"Type `LOAD "*",8,1`," The Ghost instructed.

Peter’s fingers, used to the shallow travel of a modern mechanical keyboard, felt the heavy, satisfying *clack* of the C64’s keys. 

`L-O-A-D-SPACE-"-*-"-COMMA-8-COMMA-1`

`RETURN`

The disk drive began to grind. It was a sound from another century—a rhythmic, mechanical chattering that sounded like a tiny, angry robot trying to chew its way through a sandwich. 

**SEARCHING FOR ***
**LOADING...**

"The readers are going crazy, Peter," The Ghost’s voice was lower now, almost hushed. "One hundred and fifty thousand views. They’re calling it 'The Resurrection.' They want to know what’s on the disk."

"I want to know what's on the disk," Peter said, his eyes glued to the blue screen. 

"The truth, Peter. It's the only thing that's unhackable."

The disk drive gave one final, violent *thunk*, and the screen went black. Then, a single line of text appeared in a bright, neon green font—a font that shouldn't have been possible on a C64.

**SYSTEM INITIALIZED. WELCOME BACK, GHOST.**

Peter stared at the screen. "You're... you're in the 64?"

"I'm in the logic, Peter. And now? Now we start the Victory Tour. Oh, and by the way? Check your Thorn & Phantom account. We just made four hundred dollars on a biotech company in Switzerland because I figured out their phase-three trial was a failure three days before they did."

"I am definitely going to jail," Peter whispered.

"Protagonists don't go to jail, Peter. They only go to sequels. Now, we have a world to melt."
