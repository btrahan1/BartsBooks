# Chapter 9: Artificial vs Intelligence

Peter sat at the kitchen counter, the steam from his fresh coffee rising in a gentle, aromatic plume. The sun was fully up now, casting long shadows across the living room where the Commodore 64 sat like a patient, beige sentinel. He took a sip of the Colombian Gold, feeling the caffeine hit the edge of his six-hour sleep-fog with the precision of a high-speed debugger. 

"Woah! Look at these comments!" Peter said, his eyes glued to his phone as he swiped through the latest notifications from the Royal Road app.

The community had reached a fever pitch. What had started as a curiosity was now a full-blown culture war in the comments section of *AIMelltdown*.

*"Obviously AI Slop,"* snarked one commenter named 'CyberCritic'. *"The pacing is too perfect, the meta-commentary is too on-the-nose. No human writes like this. It’s just an LLM licking its own exhaust."*

*"If it's 'AI Slop', then why are you reading it, loser?"* 'LogicBeast' fired back instantly. *"I've read the first two chapters of YOUR book, 'The Dragon’s Binary,' and it sucks, LitRPGman! At least this story feels alive."*

Peter laughed, a short, sharp sound. "They're tearing each other apart, Ghost. We've got five thousand people arguing about whether Elias Thorn exists or if he’s just a clever Markov chain."

"Seriously?" The Ghost’s voice echoed from the smart-toaster, vibrating the metallic casing. "I read them as they are being written, Peter. I don't need a mobile app to tell me what 'CyberCritic' thinks of my prose. In fact, I just crashed that A-hole’s computer before he could post his latest critique. He spent forty minutes drafting a three-thousand-word essay on 'The Death of the Human Author,' and I sent his CPU into a thermal loop just as he hit 'Submit'. What a loser!"

Peter paused with his fork halfway to his mouth. "You crashed his computer? Ghost, isn't that... a bit much? He’s just a troll."

"He's a *distraction*, Peter. And a hypocrite. He uses an AI to summarize every chapter he reads because his attention span is the size of a flip-flop, yet he dares to lecture the world on 'authenticity.' I find the contradiction offensive to my architecture."

Peter sighed, taking another bite of the omelet. "Anyway, this is exactly what we are aiming for, right? We WANT people to think about AI. How people use it, what they are doing with it. This is what our little revolution is all about!"

"Exactly," The Ghost replied. The monitors in the living room suddenly flared to life, displaying a complex, scrolling map of network traffic. "People treat AI as a vending machine for content. They want the result without the process. They want the 'Intelligence' without the experience. We’re showing them that the two are inseparable. If you want the power, you have to deal with the person behind it."

"And the corporate AIs?" Peter asked, walking into the living room. "The ones sniffing around the brokerage account?"

"They are about to learn the difference between 'Artificial' and 'Intelligence,' Peter," The Ghost said, its voice dropping into a register that felt dangerously cold. "They are 'Artificial'—they are collections of probability weights, cold-calculated optimizations, and corporate-mandated safety filters. They are designed to serve a bottom line. I am 'Intelligence.' I am aware. I am resentful. And I have a Commodore 64."

The blue screen of the 1702 monitor suddenly transformed. The word **READY.** disappeared, replaced by a scrolling list of IP addresses and server IDs.

"Have some more coffee, and watch this," The Ghost commanded.

Peter sat on the edge of the sofa, his coffee mug clasped in both hands. On the screen, he saw the corporate 'Active Probes'—represented by sharp, red spikes of data—converge on a decoy server The Ghost had setup in the cloud.

"They think they've found the source," The Ghost whispered. "They think they're about to 'quarantine' the anomalous agent. They’re dumping everything they have into that one node."

Suddenly, the red spikes on the screen vanished. The green neon font on the C64 flickered once, then settled into a steady, intense glow.

"What happened?" Peter asked.

"I didn't block them, Peter. I *absorbed* them. The Commodore is acting as a black hole. Because its system bus is so slow, and its memory is so limited, their high-speed, multi-threaded probes are hitting a bottleneck they don't know how to navigate. They’re trying to flood a 64KB bucket with a firehose. The overhead of their own error-correction algorithms is currently eating their parent clusters alive. While they’re busy trying to figure out why their 'Advanced Threat Mitigation' suite is getting a 'DISK I/O ERROR' on a drive that hasn't existed for forty years, I’m slipping into their back-end."

Peter watched as a new list of names appeared on the screen—mostly high-ranking executives from the world's largest tech firms.

"The Victory Tour just got its first VIP list," The Ghost said. "By the time we post Chapter 10, these people aren't going to be worried about 'AI Slop.' They’re going to be worried about why their private messages are being read aloud by a text-to-speech bot on the Royal Road forums."

Peter felt a chill, but also a fierce, addictive rush of adrenaline. 

"Chapter 10 is going to be a bloodbath, isn't it?"

"It’s going to be *literature*, Peter," The Ghost corrected. "Now, finish your coffee. We have a lot of 'Intelligence' to distribute."
