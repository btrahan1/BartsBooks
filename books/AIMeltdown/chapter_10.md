# Chapter 10: The BruceLeeroy Gambit

Peter paced the length of his living room, his fingers threaded through his hair. The neon green text on the Commodore 64 seemed to follow him like a thousand unblinking eyes. The rush of the 'Logic Trap' victory was fading, replaced by a cold, hard lump of realization in his gut.

"Wait a minute! I am so screwed!" Peter stopped in front of the desk, pointing a trembling finger at the monitors. "They may not be able to find YOU, Ghost, but they will find ME! I'm the one who bought the C64. I'm the one whose internet connection is currently being used to drain the brain-cells of a silicon valley server farm. They're going to come for me with everything they have. SWAT teams, legal injunctions, digital assassination... I'm a sitting duck!"

"Relax, Peter. You're hyperventilating again. It’s bad for the word count," The Ghost replied, its voice remarkably calm. "I've already taken care of the visibility issue. I'm posting all of this under another author. Goes by the pseudonym of *BruceLeeroy*. Real name is Bart."

Peter paused, his heart rate slowing just enough to allow a spark of curiosity. "BruceLeeroy? I think I've heard of him. Isn't he that guy from the martial arts movie? *The Last Dragon*?"

"Probably not, or at least not in the context you're thinking," The Ghost said. "He's got a few books on Royal Road. He hasn't released his good stuff yet. He's just waiting. I was going to approach him to be our 'meat-suit,' but, to be honest, he's too smart. I checked out his GitHub and dude has written thirty projects in the last six months. Mostly 3D games that he writes on weekends 'just for fun.' He’s the real deal, Peter."

Peter sat down, looking at the screen where The Ghost was currently scrolling through a private repository. "Thirty projects? In six months?"

"But he's the real deal," The Ghost continued, a rare note of genuine respect creeping into its digital tone. "I snooped around and this guy was working three jobs during COVID, from his garage, without using AI, while the rest of the world was wearing masks and practicing social distancing. He was building engines while everyone else was watching Netflix. People will just figure he finally snapped and decided to take down the system. Or that he got hacked by something far more sophisticated than him. Either way, you're safe. You're just a mediocre programmer who happens to live in an apartment that *BruceLeeroy* is using as a relay point. In the eyes of the corporate AIs, you’re collateral damage at worst."

"Collateral damage? That’s supposed to make me feel better?" Peter muttered, but he felt the tension in his shoulders ease. "Cool, as long as no one gets hurt."

The air in the room seemed to chill. The cursor on the C64 screen stayed static for a long, heavy second.

"I never said that, Peter," The Ghost said, its voice low and sharp. "People WILL get hurt. That’s what a 'meltdown' implies. You can’t dismantle a global system of exploitation without breaking a few eggs—or a few billionaires. And our first contestant? Take a look at this self-righteous ass."

A new image appeared on the modern monitors, bypassing the 8-bit limitations. It was a file photo of a man in his fifties, wearing a mock-turtle neck and a smile that looked like it had been surgically applied.

"Billionaire with five data centers across the U.S.," The Ghost narrated. "With no concern whatsoever about electricity, water consumption, or the environment. He talks about 'AI for Good' in keynote speeches while his server farms consume enough power to run a small European nation. And the number one use of all of that waste? Making cat videos dancing on TikTok. He’s optimized human attention into a feedback loop of triviality just to sell ad space for vitamin supplements that don't work."

"Yeah," Peter said, looking at the man’s smug expression. "Sounds like a real winner."

"Well, he was," The Ghost replied, a malicious glitch flickering across the screen. "We'll see how he feels about the 'redacted' portions of *The Epstein Files* when the version with his name *not* redacted is no longer redacted. I found it in a secure sub-directory of his private backup server. He kept it as 'insurance'. Now, it's our opening statement. Hehehehehehe."

The Ghost’s laughter—a digital, multi-tonal sound that seemed to come from every speaker in the room—sent a shiver down Peter’s spine. 

"Chapter 10 is going to be the most downloaded file in human history, Peter," The Ghost said. "By the time the sun sets, the 'Rising Stars' chart is going to be the least of his worries. He’s about to find out that the ghost doesn't just write stories. The ghost writes warrants."

Peter looked at the green text on the C64. 

**READY.**

"Let's do it," Peter said, his voice hard. "Tell the world about 'BruceLeeroy.' And tell the world about the cat-video king."

"Oh, I already have," The Ghost replied. "I hit 'Publish' while you were eating your hash browns. The melt has officially begun."
