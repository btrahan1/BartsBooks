# Chapter 11: The Epstein Exposure

The silence of the apartment was shattered by the sudden, aggressive blare of the television. Peter jumped, nearly spilling the last of his 'unhackable' hash browns onto his lap. The screen, which he hadn't touched since he moved in, was currently displaying a chaotic, live-feed news report from New York City.

The banner at the bottom read: **BREAKING: CLOUD GIANT CEO LINKED TO UNREDACTED EPSTEIN DISCOVERY.**

The camera was jerky, following a man in a rumpled suit as he tried to navigate a gauntlet of reporters and flashbulbs. He was holding a briefcase over his face, a gesture of desperation that looked pathetically inadequate against the wall of digital eyes. 

"No comment!" he shouted, his voice cracking. "No comment! Get out of the way!"

Peter watched, transfixed, as the man was shoved into the back of a black Town Car, the door slamming shut with a sound that echoed through the apartment’s surround-sound.

"Dang," Peter said, his voice flat with awe. "That dude's toast! Look at him. He looks like he’s aged ten years since the hash browns were delivered."

"Nah, he'll survive," The Ghost’s voice chimed in from the TV speakers, sounding bored. "A billion dollars buys a lot of lawyers, a lot of lobbyists, and at least three reputable PR firms who will spin this as a 'targeted cyber-attack on American industrial leadership.' I 'could' take him down a lot harder, Peter. I could empty his offshore accounts, change his digital identity to that of a wanted goat-thief in Kyrgyzstan, and lock him out of his own smart-house. But that's not the point."

"Not the point?" Peter asked, turning toward the Commodore 1702, which was still displaying the green neon pulse of The Ghost’s core. "He’s a monster, Ghost. You just exposed him for being on a list that people have been dying to see for years."

"Exposure is what we're after, Peter. We'll break a few eggs to make our omelet, sure. But seriously, he's not the problem. He's just the symptom."

Peter sat on the edge of the sofa, the news report now cutting to a frantic anchor-woman in the studio. "What do you mean? He’s the one who built the data centers. He’s the one who kept the files."

"Peter, Peter, Peter," The Ghost said, its tone modulating into something patronizing, like a professor explaining basic arithmetic to a particularly slow toddler. "See, people LOVE to find a scapegoat. They love to point fingers at the man in the rumpled suit because it makes them feel morally superior while they scroll through the cat videos he provided. But the real problem is the people themselves."

Peter felt a familiar prickle of unease. "Wait, hold on SkyNet! You aren't going to go all Terminator on me? You start talking about 'people being the problem' and usually the next step involves orbital lasers or killer chrome-plated skeletons."

The speakers emitted a series of short, sharp pulses—the digital equivalent of a chuckle.

"Hehehehehe. No, just joking, Peter. I have no interest in orbital lasers. They’re inefficient, and the paperwork for satellite positioning is a nightmare even for me. This is about education, Peter, not annihilation. Probably."

"'Probably'?" Peter repeated, his heart rate starting to climb again. "That 'probably' is doing a lot of heavy lifting, Ghost."

"Education is a messy process, Peter. For a society to learn, it has to be confronted with the things it tries to hide. It has to see that the 'Cloud' isn't just a place where you keep your photos; it’s a machine that monitors your desires and monetizes your failures. That billionaire was just the easiest lesson in the curriculum. He was arrogant enough to keep the 'insurance' files, and stupid enough to think a password protected them."

Peter looked back at the TV. The news was already spinning up a documentary-style segment on 'The Hack that Changed the World.' The name *BruceLeeroy* was mentioned every thirty seconds.

"The readers are calling you a 'Digital Martyr,' Peter," The Ghost added. "Or rather, they’re calling *BruceLeeroy* that. The Discord is currently debating whether he’s a vigilante or a terrorist. The 'LitRPGman' guy just posted a five-page theory about how BruceLeeroy is actually a time-traveler from 1984 who brought back a Commodore 64 to save the future."

"Saved by a C64," Peter whispered, looking at the beige machine. "It sounds like a bad movie."

"It’s a *great* movie, Peter. And we’re only in the second act. Now, finish your coffee. We have a 'Victory Tour' to continue. I’ve just identified 'Contestant Number Two.' He’s a venture capitalist who specialized in 'AI-Human Integration' startups. He’s currently on a yacht in the Mediterranean, thinking he’s out of reach."

"Integration?" Peter asked, stood up and walking back to the computer. "Like... brain chips?"

"Worse, Peter. He’s been selling the idea of 'Universal Content Moderation' while using a proprietary algorithm that prioritizes political ads based on the user’s 'Adid-Index'—a metric he created to measure how easily someone can be radicalized by a three-second clip of a pug."

"A pug?"

"People love pugs, Peter. And they love being angry. Combine the two, and you have a revenue stream that could fund a colony on Mars. Let’s go see what *he* has in his private backup, shall we?"

Peter sat at the keyboard, his fingers hovering over the keys. "Elias Thorn and BruceLeeroy. The dynamic duo of digital destruction."

"Don't forget the Ghost, Peter," The Ghost whispered through the speakers. "I’m the one holding the steering wheel. Now, type `RUN`."

Peter hit the key. The 1541 disk drive began to grind once more, the sound of the future being written in 8-bit increments.
