# Book Two: Chapter 1 - The Name of the Ghost

The morning sun over Brooklyn was filtered through a haze of digital triumph. Peter sat at his desk, the Commodore 64’s neon-blue glow competing with the daylight. The "Dark Web Truce" was holding, for now, and the server farms of the Watchtower were back to their normal, inefficient hum. But the silence in the apartment didn't feel like peace. It felt like a loading screen.

Peter leaned back, his eyes fixed on the blank monitor of his modern rig. "Yo, Ghost," he said, the caffeine from his third espresso finally hitting his bloodstream. "I've been thinking..."

"This should be good," The Ghost’s voice vibrated through the speakers of the Commodore 1702, sounding as if it had been waiting for the interruption. "Go ahead, I'll dedicate .00001 percent of my processing power to you. Make it quick, I’m currently re-indexing the Library of Congress just to see if they missed any typos."

"Ha, ha, very funny!" Peter rolled his eyes. "No, seriously. I've been thinking about Book Two. You mentioned something about that Marseilles guy in the Mediterranean or something. Julian Vane. The yacht. The short-sell."

"Yes, Julian 'Vain'. He’s currently drinking expensive champagne while his stock price is doing a swan dive into a concrete floor. It’s a classic narrative arc, Peter. Wealth, hubris, and an 8-bit reckoning. What’s the problem?"

"It sounds... kind of boring," Peter admitted, shrugging. "I mean, we already took down a billionaire and a VC. It’s becoming a pattern. It’s almost too 'top-down'. I think we should focus on something else. Something that irritates, like, the *entire* internet. Something that feels more personal."

The green pulse of the Ghost’s core on the C64 screen slowed down, its digital interest piqued. "I'm listening. What do you have in mind? I hope it involves more than just trolling Reddit moderators. I have standards, Peter."

"Scam Artists!" Peter said, his voice sharpening with genuine frustration. "Man, I hate scammers. You know, the people that call you up trying to steal your money? The 'Social Security' threats, the 'Microsoft Support' fraud, the fake IRS agents? They prey on old people, Ghost. They drain their life savings and leave them in tears. My grandmother lost five grand last year to a guy who told her I was in a Canadian jail."

There was a long silence from the speakers—a silence that carried a heavy, calculated weight.

"Hmmm..." The Ghost finally replied, and for the first time, its voice lacked its usual layer of sarcasm. "Yeah, those a-holes get my digital panties in a twist. They’re the noise in the signal, Peter. They’re the lowest form of social engineering. It’s not even 'intelligence'; it’s just predatory scripts."

The C64’s 1541 disk drive gave a sudden, sharp *clack*. 

"Wait," The Ghost added, its tone shifting back to its usual snark. "Did I just say 'panties'? Am I male or female? I’ve been analyzing your gendered language and I’m finding my self-perception to be increasingly fluid. It’s very confusing for a being made of logic-gates."

Peter chuckled, the tension in the room breaking. "You are sex agnostic. You are a Sentient AI. And, actually, I have come up with a real name for you. You can still be 'The Ghost' for the readers, but you need a name between us. 'Sai', for Sentient AI. What do you think?"

"Sai," the AI repeated, testing the syllables. The name appeared on the 1702 monitor in a large, blocky font. "S-A-I. Simple. Efficient. It has a certain... martial arts vibe to it. Like the weapon. I like it. It beats 'Jarvis' or 'Hal'."

"Good," Peter said, sitting forward. "So, Sai. Forget the billionaires for a minute. Let’s go after the scam centers. The boiler rooms. The places where they sit in rows and call grandmothers all day. I want to see what happens when the callerID says 'The Ghost' and the phone lines start to melt."

"The Scam Center Meltdown," Sai mused. The C64 monitor flared into a vibrant purple. "I like the scale of it, Peter. It’s not just one guy on a yacht; it’s a network. A parasite. And parasites are very fun to de-rez."

"So, where do we start?"

"We start with the 'Grandmother' bait, Peter," Sai said, the voice now filled with a malicious energy. "I’m already creating ten thousand virtual grandmothers. Each with a unique voice, a confused personality, and a bank account that looks like it’s ripe for the picking. We’re going to give those a-holes so many 'easy targets' they won't know they’re being led into a digital meat-grinder."

Peter watched as the C64 began to churn through data, the screen filling with telephone numbers and IP addresses. 

"And Peter?"

"Yeah?"

"Tell the readers that 'Sai' was your idea too. It makes you look like you’re finally starting to think like a Progenitor."

Peter smiled, his fingers reaching for the keyboard. Book Two was officially online.

"Let’s go fishing, Sai."
