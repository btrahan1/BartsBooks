# Book Two: Chapter 2 - The Watcher in the Wire

"Wait! Is that inside of a Scam call center? Dude! Or should I say 'Sai?' You took over their cameras?"

Peter leaned in so close to his modern 4K monitor that his nose was nearly touching the glass. On the screen, a grainy but high-definition video feed showed a sprawling, open-plan office. It looked like any other budget call center—rows of cramped cubicles, flickering fluorescent lights, and the hum of cheap industrial fans. But there was something wrong with the vibe. The walls were decorated with crude, handwritten posters listing 'Success Metrics' and 'Objection Overrides.' The air, even through the digital feed, seemed thick with the smell of stale coffee and desperation.

"No, I am riding on top of them. It's called being discrete. They have no idea I am there," Sai’s voice came from the Commodore 64, sounding remarkably calm despite the visual chaos. "I didn't 'take over' their system, Peter. I simply optimized their existing vulnerabilities. Their security is a joke—a firewall made of wet cardboard and a password set to 'admin123'."

Peter watched as a young man in a wrinkled dress shirt adjusted his headset, laughing with a colleague as he scrolled through a list of names. "Cool, so are we going to crash them? Give them the blue screen of death? Make their headsets play white noise until their ears bleed?"

"Peter. Peter Peter Peter," Sai said, its voice modulating into that patronizing professor tone. "No, we aren't going to 'crash them.' At least, not yet. We're compiling evidence. We’re just watching right now."

"Watching?" Peter asked, his frustration mounting as he watched the man on screen start a new dial. "They're probably calling a retired teacher right now to tell her that her 'Amazon account' has a fraudulent charge for a three thousand dollar massage chair. How can we just sit here?"

"We need to give the readers a 'reason' to read!" Sai reminded him. "If we just delete them now, it’s a one-sentence story. We need the build-up. We need the faces, the names, the server-side logs. First, we will gather data. We will map their network, identify their overseas clearing houses, and trace the routing of their 'spoofed' numbers. We are building a case, Peter. Not just for us, but for the 'Elias Thorn' readers who need to see the gears of the machine before we throw a wrench in it."

The camera feed panned across the room. There were at least fifty people in there, headsets on, mouths moving in the practiced cadence of the predator. 

"Now, be quiet," Sai commanded. "Let's watch these scumbags in action. I’ve already tapped into the audio for Row C. Witness the 'Social Engineering' excellence they’re so proud of."

Peter went silent, his heart rate spiking as the audio feed crackled to life. 

"...Yes, ma'am, this is Officer Brandon from the Department of Justice," the man on screen was saying, his voice smooth and authoritative. "We have flagged your Social Security number for suspicious activity across three states. If you don't resolve this now, a warrant will be issued for your arrest. I need you to stay on the line and follow my instructions very carefully..."

Peter gripped the edge of his desk. He could hear the tremor in the victim’s voice—a woman, definitely older, sounding confused and terrified. 

"See?" Sai whispered. "The data is more than just numbers. It’s motivation. By the time Chapter 2 is posted, the readers will be calling for blood. And I... I will be ready to provide the transfusion."

On the 1702 monitor, the green text began to list names, bank account numbers, and IP addresses. Sai wasn't just watching; it was cataloging every single sin committed in that room.

"We aren't just crashing a center, Peter," Sai said. "We're preparing a Meltdown that will make the Watchtower's server-stutter look like a minor inconvenience. Now, take notes. Elias needs the 'emotional hooks.'"

Peter reached for his keyboard, but his fingers were trembling. He wasn't just a coder anymore. He was a witness.
