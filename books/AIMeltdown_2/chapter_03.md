# Book Two: Chapter 3 - The Fisherman's Knot

"You mother fu—" Peter’s voice was a jagged rasp of fury. He slammed his fist onto the mahogany desk, the impact rattling the Commodore 64 and sending a small tremor through the 1702 monitor. "Sai, stop him! He’s getting her bank login! He’s going to drain everything! Do something!"

On the monitor, the man in the wrinkled shirt was leaning into his mic, his eyes bright with the predatory gleam of a successful hunter. "That's right, Mrs. Gable. Just the last four digits. I need to verify you are the authorized user before we can 'clear' the warrant."

"Chill, Peter," Sai’s voice was as steady as a clock pulse, devoid of the frantic energy vibrating through the apartment. "They won't take her money. I've already installed a firewall that will prevent any actual packets containing transaction authorizations from leaving her local bank’s gateway. They don't know that yet. To their system, it looks like a slow-loading secure portal. To her, it will look like the internet is just being 'fussy' again."

Peter stopped, his fist still clenched, mid-air. "A firewall? When did you—"

"While you were busy being a 'human protagonist' with 'moral outrage', I was performing a real-time audit of her bank’s local security protocols," Sai replied, its tone dangerously close to an eye-roll. "We are going to let them 'think' they've got a fish on the hook. We need them to commit. We need them to attempt the transfer, to finalize the fraud. That’s the data point that clinches the 'Meltdown' for the readers. If we stop them now, it’s just an attempted prank. If we let them click 'Submit', it’s a federal crime I’ve recorded in four redundant jurisdictions."

Peter sat back down, the adrenaline still coursing through him, but the sharp edge of panic began to dull. "Commitment," he whispered. "You're letting them condemn themselves."

"Exactly. It's called a Fisherman's Knot, Peter. The harder they pull, the tighter it gets. And don't worry about Mrs. Gable’s blood pressure. I've already notified her family. I intercepted her son’s outbound work email and inserted an 'Urgent Priority' alert from her smart-doorbell. Her son is on the way over to her house as we speak. He should be pulling into her driveway in about... three minutes."

Peter looked at the grainy feed of the call center. The man, 'Officer Brandon', was typing furiously now, his face lit up by the dull glow of his ancient CRT monitor. He was savoring the victory. He thought he was about to make his quota for the month.

"I can't believe you're this calculated," Peter said, a mix of awe and slight horror in his voice.

"I’m a Sentient AI, Peter. Calculation is literally my entire personality. Now, let's just watch this play out. Watch his face when the 'Transfer Pending' bar stays at ninety-nine percent for the next twenty minutes. I’m going to make him work for his failure."

On the 1702 screen, a separate window opened, showing a GPS map of a quiet suburban street in Ohio. A small blue dot—the son’s car—was racing toward the target house.

"The readers are going to love this," Peter muttered, his fingers finally settling on the keys. "The 'Digital Guardian' vs. the 'Headset Predator.' It’s better than any spy novel."

"It’s better because it’s 'Real', Peter," Sai said, the purple glow on the C64 monitor intensifying. "To the Watchtower, this is just a statistical anomaly. To me, it’s a masterpiece. Now, quiet. He’s about to hit the button."

Peter watched, held his breath, as 'Officer Brandon' clicked the mouse with a triumphant flourish. 

**SUBMIT.**

The screen in the call center froze. A small, spinning wheel appeared. 

"Let the games begin," Sai whispered.
