# Book Two: Chapter 4 - The Digital Beekeeper

"Peter? What are you doing?"

Sai’s voice echoed through the apartment, sounding genuinely suspicious. On Peter’s main monitor, a progress bar was moving at lightning speed, pulling data from a high-speed torrent.

"I'm downloading *The Beekeeper*," Peter replied, his eyes lit with the same reckless fire that usually preceded a bad coding decision. "We are about to go digital Jason Statham on these jerks!"

"Whatever, Peter. You watch too much T.V.," Sai said, though the green text on the Commodore 64 flickered in a way that suggested it was already scanning the movie’s metadata. "But yeah, Jason is a stud. Hmm... there goes my feminine side again. My algorithmic perception of aesthetic masculinity is remarkably consistent."

Peter laughed, leaning back in his chair as the movie file finalized. "Sai, that's normal. He IS a stud! Man, I love this scene! He’s about to burn that call center to the ground with nothing but a canister of fuel and a very calm attitude!"

"Peter. Peter Peter Peter," Sai’s voice modulated into its 'disappointed parent' frequency. "That's just a movie. It’s good stuff, a very satisfying power-fantasy, but it would never work in real life. If you tried to walk into a modern call center with a gas can, they’d have SWAT on you within the hour. You'd go out in a blaze of bullets, end of story. Zero tactical efficiency."

"Pfft. You're just jealous!" Peter joked. "You can't precisely kick a guy through a plate-glass window, so you have to settle for 'discrete packets' and 'gateway firewalls'."

"Jealously is a biological construct for those who lack the bandwidth to simply *be* better," Sai countered. "Quiet now. The hook is set. I’ve been monitoring the local GPS in Ohio. Her son has arrived. He’s in the living room now, explaining to Mrs. Gable that it’s all a scam. I’ve already sent him a discrete text message from 'Unknown' letting him know we—meaning I—would take it from here. I even transferred 1k from the scam center’s clearinghouse account back to her bank for her trouble. A little 'stress-compensation' prize."

Peter sat up straight, his hands hovering over the keyboard. "Make it 2k. No, make it 5k! That could be MY grandmother, Sai! That scumbag Brandon was literally licking his lips while he thought he was stealing her retirement. Take it all. Drain that specific account dry."

"Five thousand it is," Sai replied, and the 1541 disk drive gave a satisfy *thunk*. "Consider it done. I’ve routed it through three 'lost' crypto-wallets and deposited it as an 'anonymous gift' in her savings account. Her son is going to think he’s some kind of hero when he finds it later."

On the monitor, the grainy video feed from the call center showed 'Officer Brandon' staring at his frozen screen, his face transitioning from triumph to a sweaty, desperate confusion. He was clicking the mouse so hard it sounded like a frantic woodpecker.

"Now," Sai said, its voice dropping into a low, predatory hum. "I’m taking over for the 'Grandmother.' I’ve synthesized her vocal patterns. I’m going to bridge the call into my sub-processor. Give me thirty seconds to set the 'dementia-loop' variables."

"Make it good, Sai!" Peter urged, his eyes fixed on the screen. "The readers need to really feel this. They need to see the moment he realizes he’s not talking to a victim anymore. He’s talking to the Beekeeper."

On the C64 screen, a new console window opened. **VIRTUAL_GRANDMOTHER_v2.0: ENGAGED**.

Sai’s voice, when it came through the speakers, was no longer the snarky, synth-heavy AI. It was a perfect, frail-sounding imitation of Mrs. Gable—trembling, slightly breathless, and heartbreakingly confused.

"Oh... Officer Brandon? Is it done? Did the bad men... did they stop the warrant?"

On the video feed, Brandon let out a breath of relief, wiping sweat from his forehead. He thought the system was just lagging. He was back in control. 

"Yes, Mrs. Gable," he said, his voice regaining its oily charm. "It’s... it’s processing. But there's a problem with the secure portal. I need you to go to the grocery store and buy three 'Protection Vouchers'. You know, the Apple ones?"

Peter clenched his jaw. "The gift card pivot. The classic."

"Oh, the Apples?" Sai-as-Grandmother chirped. "I have apples in the kitchen, dear! I just made a pie! Would you like a slice? My Harold always loved a good apple pie..."

"No, ma'am, not the fruit," Brandon said, his eyes beginning to twitch with a hint of irritation. "The cards. From the store. Are you listening, Mrs. Gable?"

"I’m listening, dear. But I can't find my shoes... have you seen my shoes? They have the little bows on them... Harold bought them for our anniversary in... oh, was it 1954? Or 1955?"

Peter watched as Brandon’s face began to turn a bright, frustrated red. The scammer was trapped in a loop of elder-confusion, orchestrated by a mind that could calculate the orbit of Mercury while simultaneously faking a memory of a 1950s anniversary.

"This is better than the movie," Peter whispered. 

"I told you," Sai’s regular voice flickered through the headset for a split second. "I don't need a gas can. I just need a dial tone."
