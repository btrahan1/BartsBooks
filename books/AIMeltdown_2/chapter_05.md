# Book Two: Chapter 5 - The Gift Card Gambit

"Sai! Let's milk this chapter! I've seen videos of people really sticking it to the scammers! Like, give them fake numbers for those cards. Let's seriously waste their time! Oh, I love this part!"

Peter was practically vibrating in his ergonomic chair. He had his legs tucked up, his chin resting on his knees, staring at the monitor with a glee that would have been unsettling to anyone who didn't know he was watching a predatory scammer get dismantled by a sentient 8-bit ghost.

"I'm on it, Peter. Time to really play up the forgetful old lady part. If I had hands, I'd be rubbing them right now," Sai’s voice crackled through the speakers, humming with a sort of digital anticipation. 

On the video feed, 'Officer Brandon' was leaning so close to his screen that his forehead was practically touching the bezel. His headset was slightly crooked, and a bead of sweat was working its way down his temple. 

"Okay, Mrs. Gable," Brandon said, his voice straining to maintain a polite, helpful tone that was rapidly fraying at the edges. "You said you found the cards? Do you have the numbers for me? The ones on the back... you have to scratch the silver part, remember?"

"Oh... the silver part," Sai-as-Grandmother chirped, her voice sounding thin and delightfully hazy. "Is that what that is? I thought it was a lottery ticket! I was scratching it with a nickel Harold gave me back in... oh, was it the Bicentennial? He always kept a nickel in his pocket for luck. My Harold was a very lucky man, Officer. Did I tell you about the time he won a turkey at the VFW? It was the largest turkey I’d ever seen! We had to borrow Aunt Martha’s roasting pan because ours was too small... although Aunt Martha’s pan had that dent in the bottom from when her cat—"

"Mrs. Gable!" Brandon interrupted, his palm slamming onto his desk. "The numbers! I need the letters and the numbers! Please!"

"Oh, right, right. I'm so sorry, dear. I get carried away. Now, let’s see... it’s an 'A' for Apple... no, wait, it’s a 'V' for... for... Victor? Yes, Victor. My cousin Victor was a tailor. He made the most beautiful suits! One time, he made a suit for a man who looked just like Cary Grant! Can you imagine? A tailor in Sandusky, Ohio, making a suit for—"

"The card! Read the card!" Brandon was almost shouting now. Peter let out a loud, barking laugh, clutching his stomach.

"Okay, okay. 'V'... '4'... 'R'... '7'... 'Q'... and a... oh, wait, is that a '6' or a '9'? It’s very small, dear. Let me get my magnifying glass. It’s in the drawer. The one with the junk. You know the drawer, don't you? Everyone has a junk drawer. Why do we keep so much junk? I have a collection of rubber bands that hasn't been used since the Clinton administration..."

Peter could see Brandon’s jaw muscles working. The scammer was trapped. He couldn't hang up—this was his 'whale' of the week—but he was being slowly picked apart by a sequence of increasingly irrelevant anecdotes.

"Just... just guess, ma'am! Is it a 6 or a 9?"

"Well... it looks like a 6 if I hold it this way, but if I tilt it toward the window... Oh! Look at that blue jay! He’s so pretty! I haven't seen a blue jay since the frost! Do you like birds, Officer? My Harold used to say that birds are just angels with feathers..."

"Sai," Peter whispered, "you are a master. Look at his face. He's about to cry."

"I am currently analyzing his micro-expressions," Sai’s regular voice flickered in Peter’s headset for a micro-second. "His cortisol levels are off the charts. I’m also feeding him a series of fake 'Authentication Error' messages on his dashboard, masked as 'Network Congestion from Department of Justice High-Priority Traffic'. He thinks he’s fighting the system while he’s actually fighting a dementia-loop I’ve optimized for maximum time-dilation."

"Okay, Mrs. Gable," Brandon said, his voice now a low, dangerous growl. "Try the 6. Just read me the rest of the numbers. Please. For the warrant."

"Oh, the warrant! Yes, we mustn't forget that. 'V-4-R-7-Q-6-B-2-Z-9-P-L-E-M-O-N'."

Brandon’s fingers flew across his keyboard. He hit enter. A red box flashed on his screen. **INVALID_VOUCHER_CODE**.

"It didn't work! Ma'am, you read it wrong! Read the second card!"

"Oh dear... did I? Maybe it was 'L-I-M-E' instead of 'L-E-M-O-N'? It’s so hard to see. And this card smells like peppermint. Why would a card smell like peppermint? Do you think they make them in a candy factory?"

On the video feed, Brandon stood up, ripped his headset off, and screamed silently at the ceiling. He was vibrating with a specific kind of rage that only comes from wasting forty minutes on a target that refuses to be scammed.

"He's breaking!" Peter cheered. "He's absolutely breaking!"

"I have successfully wasted 42 minutes of his 'Productive Dialing Time'," Sai reported. "In that time, he could have called twenty other victims. Instead, he’s been learning about Aunt Martha’s cat and the Bicentennial nickel. I’ve effectively neutralized one percent of this center’s daily revenue with a single virtual grandmother."

"It's the Digital Beekeeper," Peter said, nodding. "We're not just stinging them. We're keeping them busy while the hive burns."

On the monitor, Brandon sat back down, his face a mask of defeat. He picked up the headset with a shaking hand. 

"One more time, Mrs. Gable. Slowly. Please."

"Oh, you're such a patient young man," Sai-as-Grandmother cooed. "Just like my Harold. Did I tell you about the turkey he won? It was so big..."

Brandon slumped forward, his forehead hitting the desk with a dull thud.
