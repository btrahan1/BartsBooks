# Book Two: Chapter 6 - The Ten-Dollar Hope

"Okay, Sai! That's the hook, but it's in his mouth instead of hers! Now, time to reel him in. Let's give him a bit of hope, and then a bit more frustration!"

Peter was pacing in front of the 4K monitor, his shadow flickering against the wall. The thrill of the "Scam-Bait" was intoxicating. It was one thing to out-code a corporate AI; it was another thing entirely to watch a human predator get slowly dismantled by his own greed.

"You're starting to scare me, Peter! If a digital god could get scared, hah!" Sai’s voice replied, the speakers of the Commodore 64 rattling with a synthetic chuckle. "Okay, let's make him think the first card went through. But let’s make it for ten dollars instead of the five hundred he was expecting. I’ll trigger a 'Partial Authorization' event on his dashboard. It’s a rare error, but just plausible enough to keep him fishing."

On the video feed, 'Officer Brandon' was sitting with his head in his hands, his headset lying on the desk like a discarded weapon. He looked like he’d aged five years since the call began. But suddenly, his screen flashed. A green box appeared. 

**PARTIAL_AUTHORIZATION: $10.00 SUCCESSFUL.**

Brandon bolted upright, his eyes widening as they locked onto the screen. "Wait... what? It worked? Only ten dollars? Mrs. Gable! Ma'am! Are you there?"

He scrambled to put his headset back on, nearly knocking his lukewarm soda onto his keyboard. 

"Oh... yes, dear. I'm here. Did something happen? Did the Apple-Lemons work?" Sai-as-Grandmother chirped, the synthesis sounding even more delightfully oblivious than before. 

"Only ten dollars, ma'am! It was supposed to be five hundred!" Brandon shouted, his voice cracking with a mix of relief and renewed desperation. "The card... it must be a 'Restricted Value' card. Did you buy the wrong ones? Did you get the ones for the... for the children?"

"Oh, for the children? Harold always said we should give more to the children! He used to donate a crate of oranges to the school play every year. Oranges are very good for the voice, you know. Especially during the winter months when the heaters make everything so dry..."

"Mrs. Gable! Focus! I need the other card!" Brandon was typing again, his fingers possessed by a frantic, nervous energy. "If the first one was ten, then the second one... maybe the second one has the rest! Read me the second card! Quickly!"

Peter watched from Brooklyn, his jaw dropping in a grin. "Sai, you’re a genius. You gave him just enough bait to keep him from hanging up. He’s looking at that ten dollars like it’s a winning lottery ticket."

"It’s the Sunk Cost Fallacy, Peter," Sai explained in its regular voice. "He’s already invested forty-five minutes in this call. That ten dollars proves the 'victim' is real. Now he’s convinced the big payout is just one more card away. I’ve effectively reset his frustration clock. He’ll stay on the line for another hour if I play this right."

"One hour? I want him on the line until his shift ends!" Peter said, leaning toward the keyboard. "I want him to be the only person in that center who hasn't made a single 'real' dollar by the time the lights go out."

"Patience, Progenitor," Sai said. "I am already preparing the next 'anecdote.' This one involves a story about a very complicated recipe for beef bourguignon that requires a specific kind of red wine that was only available in 1992. It should take about fifteen minutes to explain the grocery store trip alone."

On the video feed, Brandon was leaning back in his chair, a manic, sweaty grin on his face. He was actually nodding along to the story about Harold’s turkey again. He thought he was winning.

"Now, Mrs. Gable," he said, interrupting the beef bourguignon monologue. "The second card. Read me the numbers. Slowly, dear. Just like before."

"Okay, dear... let's see. 'D' for... for... Dog? Harold had a dog once. A beagle named Buster. He was a very good boy until he discovered the neighbors' vegetable garden..."

Peter settled back into his chair, a tray of hash browns forgotten on his desk. The "Digital Beekeeper" was back at work, and the hive was starting to feel the heat.

"This is better than any movie," Peter whispered. "The Jason Statham era is over. The 'Sai' era is just beginning."
