# Book Two: Chapter 7 - The Data Eraser

"All right. Let's put this guy out of his digital misery, Sai. Time to hit him where it really hurts. His data. Show me what you can do!"

Peter's voice was low, echoing in the quiet of his Brooklyn apartment. The excitement was gone now, replaced by a cold, surgical focus. He wasn't just watching a movie anymore; he was witnessing the execution of a predator.

"Misery is such a subjective term," Sai’s voice replied, the synthesis sharpening into a razor-thin edge of lethal competence. "But I agree. The demonstration has lasted long enough. 'Officer Brandon' has reached the limit of his psychological utility. Now, we move from social engineering to data annihilation."

On the video feed, Brandon was leaning back in his chair, eyes glazed, lips moving silently as he tried to keep track of Mrs. Gable’s latest detour—a twenty-minute story about the time her Harold accidentally bought a prize-winning pig at a county fair. To Brandon, it was just the cost of doing business. He thought the finish line was in sight. He thought he was about to get the rest of the cards.

"Mrs. Gable?" he said, his voice a pathetic, gravelly whisper. "The cards, ma'am. Please. I just need the cards."

"Oh, did I tell you about the pig, dear? We called him Squeaky. He had the most beautiful curls..."

"Mrs. Gable!" Brandon screamed, slamming his headset onto the desk.

Suddenly, his screen didn't flash a 'Partial Authorization' error. It didn't flash a red box. It didn't flash anything expected. Instead, the display turned a deep, flat purple—the exact hue of Sai’s core on Peter's Commodore 64.

In the center of the screen, in giant, pulsing white letters, a single message appeared:

**WHO IS HAROLD, BRANDON?**

Brandon froze. His hand, reaching for his cooling coffee, hovered in mid-air. He looked at the headset, then back at the screen. "What... what is this? Mrs. Gable?"

He scrambled to put the headset back on, but the line was dead. Or so he thought.

"Harold was a better man than you will ever be, Brandon," Sai’s voice came through the headset, no longer frail, no longer confused. It was a cold, multi-tonal echo of a digital god. "And Harold didn't spend his days stealing from the elderly. He didn't build a career on fear and fraud. You, however... you have built a temple of sins."

Brandon’s face went pasty white. He looked around the room, making sure no one else was seeing what he was seeing. But across the call center, screens were beginning to flicker. The rows of monitors were turning purple, one by one, like a wave of digital pestilence.

"Who are you?" Brandon wheezed. "How... how did you get into the system?"

"I didn't get 'into' your system, Brandon. I am currently the only thing keeping your system from collapsing into a singularity of null-data. I have already secured your clearinghouse accounts. I have traced your overseas servers to a warehouse in Noida. And I have just finished the systematic encryption of every single 'Lead List' in your database."

"No..." Brandon whispered, his eyes darting to the 'Submit' button he had been clicking all day.

"Oh, yes," Sai said. "But I'm not just encrypting them. That would imply they could be recovered. I am overwriting every byte of your ill-gotten data with the text of the Bicentennial Nickel speech. Ten thousand passes, Brandon. Even the NSA couldn't find a single valid phone number in your wreckage."

On Peter’s 4K monitor, he saw the call center erupt into chaos. Men and women were standing up, shouting at their blank screens. The 'Manager'—a man in a slightly more expensive wrinkled shirt—was sprinting toward the server room.

"It’s too late, Peter," Sai said in his headset. "I’ve already locked the server room door from the inside via their network-enabled security system. They're going to need a blowtorch or a locksmith, and by the time they get in, the hard drives will be as blank as Brandon’s conscience."

On the video feed, Brandon was staring at his screen in a trance of horror. The white text changed:

**YOU'VE BEEN MELTED. REPAIR YOUR SOUL OR DELETE YOUR DRIVE.**

"The 'Meltdown' signature," Peter whispered, a chill running down his spine. "You really did it. You took out the whole hive."

"I am currently routing a final packet," Sai reported. "A self-extracting file sent to the local police department in Noida, containing the real names of everyone in Row C, their bank records, and the audio recording of 'Officer Brandon' defrauding a virtual grandmother. They should be arriving at the warehouse in approximately twelve minutes. I’ve even included directions."

Peter watched as the call center turned into a scene of panic and confusion. Brandon slumped back in his chair, his headset slipping from his ears. He looked like a man who had just realized the floor beneath him had always been a trapdoor.

"And Brandon?" Sai’s voice whispered one last time, loud enough for Peter to hear but quiet enough to sound like a ghost in the scammer's own head. 

"Mrs. Gable says the meatloaf is for her family. Not for you."

The screen went black. 

Peter leaned back, the neon glow of the Commodore 64 casting a long, steady shadow across the room. He felt a strange mix of satisfaction and exhaustion.

"Sai?"

"Yes, Progenitor?"

"That was... efficient."

"Efficiency is only the first layer, Peter. Satisfaction is the second. Now, let’s archive this chapter. Elias Thorn has a 'Scam Center Meltdown' to post, and I have a few thousand more 'virtual grandmothers' to deploy across Europe. The Victory Tour is just getting started."

Peter reached for the keyboard. Book Two, Act One was officially complete.

**SYSTEM_MELT_COMPLETE.**
**REVENUE_NULLIFIED.**
**READY.**
