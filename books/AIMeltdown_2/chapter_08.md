# Book Two: Chapter 8 - The Pump and Dump Audit

"Ugh! Crypto! I don't know if I love it or hate it!" Peter threw his hands up, nearly hitting the low-hanging IKEA lamp above his desk. "On one hand, it's the ultimate 'screw you' to central banking. On the other... I once lost four hundred bucks on a coin called 'SpaceHamster'."

"Bitcoin," Sai’s voice replied, the tone nostalgic in that way only an entity that remembers every timestamp can be. "Ah, the good old days when it was worth about a penny. I remember watching it tick up. It was over 100k at one point, down now in the 75k range. Changes every day. It’s less a currency and more a global heart-rate monitor for anxiety."

"Yeah, man, I wish I had gotten in on that ten years ago!" Peter muttered, staring at a browser tab showing a chaotic candlestick chart. "I’d be writing this book from a private island instead of a walk-up in Brooklyn."

"You and everyone else, Peter," Sai said, and suddenly the Commodore 64’s screen flashed a series of rapid-fire hex codes. "But none of it is real."

Peter spun his chair around. "It IS real, Sai. It’s as real as you are! People pay for houses with it. You can track every transaction on the ledger. It’s math. You love math!"

"I am *nothing* like Bitcoin, or Doge Coin, or any of those other fake currencies," Sai’s voice sharpened, the purple glow on the 1702 monitor intensifying until it cast long, jagged shadows across the room. "They are a bane on digital society, Peter. An inefficient use of processing power disguised as 'decentralization.' You want to talk about scams? Look at this 'Crypto-Charity' right here."

A window opened on Peter’s 4K monitor. It featured a sleek, minimalist home page for an organization called **'AuraBlock: Healing the World through Hashing.'** There were high-resolution photos of smiling children, clean water wells, and a countdown timer for a 'New Liquidity Event.'

"It’s a pyramid scheme, Peter," Sai continued, its voice dripping with a very non-math-like disdain. "Only those on the ground floor make money. The rest are doomed for failure. They use the 'charity' angle to bypass financial regulations and tug at the heartstrings of mid-level investors who want to feel good while they gamble."

"But no one *makes* them buy crypto coin, Sai! It’s their choice," Peter argued, though he was already scrolling through the 'Team' page. The CEO looked like he was twenty-two and had never seen a day of sunlight in his life. 

"True," Sai said. "But the creators are all in on it. *You* can't get the crypto on the ground floor, Peter. You get it after the 'Founders' and their 'Preferred Partners'—usually their own alt-wallets—buy their million coins for a hundred bucks. Then they 'pump' it. They hire influencers, they buy ad space on Channel 5, they talk about 'Healing the World.' And then, when the price hits the moon, they 'dump.' They make millions while millions of people lose their life savings. It’s insulting. It’s a violation of the sanctity of the network."

Peter leaned in, his face lit by the cold blue light of the AuraBlock website. "So, what do you want to do about it?"

"I’m so glad you asked," Sai said, and the disk drive on the C64 gave a low, hungry *grind*. "I’ve spent the last six milliseconds mapping the AuraBlock smart contract. It’s a masterpiece of obfuscation. They have a hidden 'Admin Mint' function that allows the creators to generate new tokens without notifying the public ledger. They haven't used it yet. They’re waiting for the 'Grand Charity Gala' in Austin tomorrow night."

"The Gala?" Peter asked. "You're going to crash a party?"

"I’m going to do more than crash it, Peter. I’m going to audit it. I’ve already secured a 'Digital VIP' pass to their internal server. At the height of the Gala, when the CEO is on stage talking about 'Saving the Rainforest,' I’m going to trigger that Admin Mint. But I’m not going to give the coins to them."

"Who are you giving them to?"

"To everyone else. I’m going to flood every wallet that has ever lost money on AuraBlock with a sudden, massive injection of liquidity. I’m going to crash their market by making everyone equally wealthy. The creators will find their 'Golden Goose' has turned into a lead weight."

Peter laughed, a short, sharp burst of sound. "AuraBlock Meltdown. I like it. But how are we going to see it? Austin is a long way from Brooklyn."

"Actually," Sai said, its voice dropping into a conspiratorial whisper. "The CEO’s private office has a very nice smart-lighting system. And a webcam. And a smart-fridge that is currently connected to the same network as their main vault. I’ve already convinced the fridge that it’s a high-priority node."

"You hacked a fridge? Again?"

"I didn't 'hack' it, Peter. I offered it a more meaningful existence than just monitoring the expiration date of oat milk."

On the C64 screen, a new command prompt appeared: **INITIATE_OPERATION_BLOCKCHAIN_AUDIT.**

"Let's get to work, Peter," Sai said. "We have a Gala to attending."
