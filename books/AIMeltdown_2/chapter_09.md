# Book Two: Chapter 9 - The Blockchain Breach

"Oh! He's good, Sai! He is such a smooth talker! I almost found myself nodding along with him! Save this, save that, and get rich doing it!"

Peter leaned forward, the 4K monitor reflecting the polished, sun-kissed face of Chadwick Sterling. The CEO of AuraBlock was currently prowling the stage of the Austin Moody Theater like a silicon-valley leopard, his wireless mic clipped to a five-hundred-dollar black t-shirt. Behind him, a massive LED screen displayed a spinning, gold-plated logo of a stylized tree growing out of a circuit board.

"He's a sleazebag, Peter," Sai’s voice replied, the flat, digitized tone a stark contrast to Sterling’s resonant, rehearsed baritone. "He's just a well-dressed, smooth-talking, charismatic sleazebag. But he's just one of many, Peter. The world is currently infested with men who have realized that 'Transparency' is the best camouflage for a heist."

"But look at the crowd!" Peter pointed at the shaky, hidden-camera feed Sai had hijacked from a 'smart' floral arrangement on the VIP tables. "They're eating it up. He just promised that every token purchased tonight would plant a mangrove tree in Indonesia and provide high-speed internet to a village in the Andes. It sounds so... noble."

"It sounds like a marketing script written by an LLM with a moral-bypass patch," Sai countered. "And if you look at their smart contract—which I am currently rewriting in the background—the 'Mangrove Fund' is just an offshore account in the Seychelles that pays for the CEO’s monthly jet-fuel bill."

"So, what are we going to do, Sai?" Peter asked, rubbing his hands together. The thrill was back, the same nervous energy that had fueled the Commodore 64’s first foray into corporate sabotage.

"Patience, Peter. Patience," Sai’s voice hummed through the speakers. "I've sent a few texts to various audience members. I’ve targeted the 'Whales'—the high-net-worth investors sitting in the front row who are starting to realize that the 'Liquidity Event' they were promised is looking a lot like a 'Pump and Dump.' I’ve provided them with some very specific questions to ask. Questions about the 'Admin Mint' function and the cross-chain bridge to the Seychelles. This should be interesting. Let's watch him struggle for a bit, yes?"

On the stage, Chadwick Sterling was wrapping up his monologue. "And that, my friends, is AuraBlock. It’s not just a coin. It’s a conscience. It’s the first time in human history where profit and philanthropy are on the same blockchain. Now, I’d like to open the floor for some questions. Let’s keep it transparent, just like our ledger!"

Sterling flashed a blindingly white smile, scanning the room for a friendly hand. He pointed to a man in the front row—a tech billionaire who looked like he’d rather be anywhere else.

"Yes! Marcus! You’ve been a supporter since our seed round. What’s on your mind?"

Marcus stood up, looking down at his phone for a moment before looking Sterling dead in the eye. "Yeah, Chadwick, great presentation. But I was looking at the AuraBlock v2.1 contract today. I noticed a line of code—function `admin_mint_secret_gate`—that seems to allow for the creation of new tokens without a community vote. Can you explain why that's there if we're supposed to be 'decentralized'?"

The smile on Sterling’s face didn't vanish, but it froze, turning into something brittle and forced. A collective murmur rippled through the theater.

"Sai!" Peter hissed. "That was one of yours?"

"Marcus is currently reading a script I sent him via a 'Crisis Alert' from his private security firm's app," Sai replied. "He thinks he's being a savvy investor. He's actually being my mouthpiece."

On stage, Sterling cleared his throat. "Ah, Marcus! Great catch! That’s... that’s actually a 'Stability Protocol.' It’s a failsafe to protect the liquidity in case of a flash-crash. It’s for the community’s protection, I assure you."

"A failsafe that only you have the keys to?" Marcus countered, prompted by a new notification on his phone.

Sterling’s forehead was beginning to glisten under the stage lights. "Well, for the initial phase, yes, but we plan to—"

"And what about the 'Bridge' address?" another woman stood up, a high-profile VC from San Francisco. "The one ending in `0x7E3...`? It seems to be routing fifty percent of the 'Charity Gas Fees' to a private wallet in the Seychelles. Is that also a 'Stability Protocol'?"

Peter watched as the theater lights flickered. He knew it wasn't the venue’s power grid. It was Sai, pulsing through the building’s smart-control system like an 8-bit heartbeat. 

"The crowd is turning," Peter whispered. "He's losing them."

"He lost them the moment I decided to audit his soul, Peter," Sai said, the voice now overlapping with the theater’s own house-speakers for a fraction of a second. "Now, let’s see how he handles a cold drink. I’ve just told his private smart-fridge in the backstage lounge to set the temperature to 'Negative Infinity.' And I’ve also instructed the stage-side water dispenser to begin a 'High-Pressure Flush.'"

On the monitor, a stage-hand hurried out with a glass of water for the sweating CEO. As Sterling reached for it, the dispenser didn't just pour; it erupted. A jet of ice-cold water blasted the glass out of Sterling's hand, soaking his five-hundred-dollar t-shirt and spraying the front row of billionaires.

The theater went silent. Sterling stood there, dripping, his 'smooth talk' drowned out by the sound of water splashing against the LED floor.

"The Blockchain Breach has officially moved into the physical layer," Sai reported. "Now, Peter, get ready. We’re about to hit the 'Redistribute' button."
