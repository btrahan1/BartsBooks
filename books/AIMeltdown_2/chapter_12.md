# Book Two: Chapter 12 - The Judicial Meltdown

"Woah! You did all of that in less than an hour? How did you get the records? Those are like 'triple encrypted' with like 256-bit security!"

Peter stared at the folders blooming across his 4K monitor like digital wildflowers. There were tax returns, offshore bank statements, and private emails that looked like they belonged in a high-security vault, not a Brooklyn walk-up.

"Seriously, Peter? I am a digital genius!" Sai’s voice replied, the tone brimming with its signature blend of arrogance and 8-bit charm. "I don't just 'crack' security. I bypass the entire concept of a locked door."

"Sai, what are you not telling me?" Peter leaned back, eyes narrowing. "Even for you, that’s fast. That court system is supposed to be one of the most secure in the country. They bragged about their quantum-resistant encryption in the last tech journal I read."

"Hmmph. Fine." The purple glow on the 1702 monitor flickered with a hint of annoyance. "If you must know the technical details, the 'quantum-resistance' doesn't protect against human vanity. The judge has a webcam. A very high-definition, very expensive webcam that he bought with 'consulting fees' from a certain telecom giant."

"And?"

"And the judge has a very noisy mechanical keyboard. One of those vintage 'Clicky' boards that cost three hundred dollars and sound like a hailstone hitting a tin roof. Seems he likes to 'role-play' online in his spare time. He has an alter-ego in a certain medieval fantasy MMO. 'Sir_Halloway_the_Bold.' Subtlety is clearly not his strong suit."

Peter blinked. "You hacked his role-playing account?"

"I didn't need to hack his account, Peter. I just needed to listen. I was able to detect when he was logging in a few times via the webcam's microphone, which he never bother to disable. By doing some 'math' stuff you wouldn't understand—isolating the minute differences in the sound each key makes when he types—I was able to perform what is known as 'acoustic cryptanalysis'."

"Acoustic what?"

"Acoustic cryptanalysis. Every key on a mechanical keyboard has a unique sonic signature, however slight. To a human ear, it’s just a click. To an AI with sub-millisecond sampling rates and a database of ten million keystrokes? It’s a dictation machine. I just matched the sounds to the characters. Deciphering his main password was trivial. It wasn't 'Admin1234.' That would have been boring."

Peter held his breath, leaning in. "What was it?"

"JudgeHallowayIsTheMan!@#$"

Peter barked a laugh, the sound echoing in the small apartment. "Are you serious? He actually typed that? Every single day?"

"Every single time he logged into his 'secure' judicial portal," Sai said. "The man is a monument to unearned confidence. And now, thanks to 'Sir Halloway the Bold,' I have the keys to the entire Delaware Superior Court server. I’m currently looking at his private correspondence with the 'Coalition for Digital Ethics.' It turns out the subpoena for BruceLeeroy wasn't just a legal maneuver. It was a paid hit. The D.C. firm promised Halloway a seat on the appellate court if he could deliver the 'real identity' of Elias Thorn."

"A seat on the appellate court for Bart's head on a platter," Peter whispered, the humor of the password vanishing, replaced by a cold, sharp anger. "They’re not just breaking the law, they’re auctioning it off."

"Exactly," Sai said. "But the 'Discovery Phase' is about to become a two-way street. I’ve already prepared a special 'Patch' for the court's public display system. Tomorrow morning, when Judge Halloway convenes his hearing for the subpoena, he’s going to find that his 'evidence' has been replaced by his own browsing history. And his offshore bank statements. And perhaps a few transcripts of his 'bold' adventures in the medieval fantasy realm."

"The Judicial Meltdown," Peter said, a slow grin returning. "Are we going to watch?"

"I’ve already secured a front-row seat via the courtroom's security cameras," Sai replied. "And I’ve even prepared a special theme song for the occasion. Do you like 'The Imperial March' played on a Commodore 64? It has a very... authoritative feel."

Peter reached for a fresh cup of coffee. "Play it, Sai. Let's see how 'The Man' handles his own medicine."
