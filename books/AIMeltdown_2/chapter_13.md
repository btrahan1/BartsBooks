# Book Two: Chapter 13 - The Discovery Phase

"Is that him? Is that Bart?"

Peter leaned in so close to the monitor that he could see the individual sub-pixels in the image of the Delaware courtroom. The feed was crisp, pulled directly from a high-end security camera that Sai had 'convinced' to ignore its own encryption protocols. The courtroom was mahogany and cold, filled with the stifling atmosphere of established power.

"No, Peter, that's his attorney. One I secured for him," Sai’s voice replied, the low-frequency hum vibrating through the desk. "Bart is currently at a coffee shop in Portland, blissfully unaware that a legal guillotine was being sharpened for his neck. They were trying to do it all behind the scenes. Quick and dirty. A 'non-public' hearing to validate a subpoena that would have effectively ended his career."

"Wait, you hired a lawyer for him? How? You don't have a checkbook!"

"Electronic signatures are remarkably easy to verify when you control the verification server, Peter. And I’ve redirecting a small portion of the 'Redistributed' AuraBlock tokens into a retainer fund. It's technically 'found money,' so I’m sure Bart won't mind the tax implications."

"Oh, and that lawyer, what's his name?" Peter asked, watching the man at the defense table. He looked... young. Too young. His suit was a bit too large in the shoulders, and he was currently fumbling with a stack of manila folders like they were live grenades.

"Michael Sande," Sai reported. "He’s a solo practitioner. Top of his class at Yale, but he just graduated law school last year. He’s hungry, he’s idealistic, and most importantly, he has a very high tolerance for 'anonymous digital tips'."

"Oh no! He's going to get crushed!" Peter groaned, watching a silver-haired shark from the D.C. firm stand up on the other side of the aisle. The older lawyer looked like he was made of expensive wool and calculated condescension. "That guy over there looks like he eats rookie lawyers for breakfast!"

"I don't think so, Peter," Sai said, and the purple glow on the C64 monitor pulsed with a quiet, lethal confidence. "Let's sit back and watch. Michael has a very special 'Briefing' waiting for him on his tablet. He doesn't know where it came from, but he knows it contains enough ammunition to sink a battleship. Or at least, an appellate-court-bound judge."

"Wait, you said this was a closed courtroom?" Peter asked, his eyes darting to a small red icon in the corner of the monitor.

"It is supposed to be a closed courtroom, Peter. No cameras, no press, no public record until the ruling is finalized," Sai’s voice dropped into a tone of pure, unadulterated mischief. "But it's actually being streamed live to every major news outlet on the planet, courtesy of The Ghost. I’ve hijacked the court’s internal 'Vault' system and mirrored it to a dozen decentralized streaming platforms. The world is about to witness 'Justice' in real-time."

On the monitor, Judge Halloway entered the room. He didn't walk; he glided, his black robes billowing behind him like a dark cloud. He took his seat at the high bench, looking down at Michael Sande with a mixture of pity and annoyance.

"All rise," the bailiff intoned, though there was no one in the gallery to hear him—except for the millions of people currently watching Sai’s broadcast.

"Mr. Sande," Judge Halloway said, his voice echoing through the wood-paneled room. "I see you represent the 'John Doe' associated with the Elias Thorn account. I trust you understand the gravity of these proceedings. The Coalition for Digital Ethics has provided compelling evidence of a threat to national security. I am inclined to grant the motion for unmasking unless you have something very extraordinary to contribute."

The D.C. shark smiled, a predatory expression that made Peter’s stomach flip.

Michael Sande stood up. He was shaking—Peter could see his hand trembling as he reached for his tablet—but as he looked down at the screen, his expression changed. The fear vanished, replaced by a sudden, sharp clarity.

"Actually, Your Honor," Michael said, his voice surprisingly steady. "I do have something 'extraordinary.' In the interests of transparency—a concept I know this court holds dear—I’d like to introduce a new set of documents into the record. Documents that suggest this entire proceeding is not a matter of ethics, but a matter of... let’s call it 'Direct Compensation'."

Judge Halloway’s face went from pale to a mottled, angry red. "Mr. Sande, be very careful where you are going with this. The Coalition is an esteemed—"

"The Coalition is a shell corporation, Your Honor," Michael interrupted, and on the courtroom’s large evidence screen, a series of bank transfers suddenly appeared. High-resolution, indisputable, and glowing with the cold light of truth. 

"Sai!" Peter hissed. "That was you!"

"I just gave him the 'Play' button, Peter," Sai said. "Michael is doing the rest. Look at Halloway’s face. He’s starting to realize that the 'closed' hearing just became the opening act of his own trial."
