# Book Two: Chapter 14 - The Final Verdict

"So, as you can see here, 'Your Honor,'" Michael Sande said, the word *Honor* practically dripping with a sneer that would have made a seasoned trial lawyer proud. " 'Sir Halloway The Bold' is a pseudonym for someone you may know. A high-ranking paladin in the realm of *Aethelgard*... and a very real judge in the realm of Delaware. Now, let's go into these 'donations' a bit further, shall we?"

On the giant projector screen, the bank transfers didn't just flicker; they stabilized into a high-definition mosaic of corruption. Each line of data was highlighted in a pulsing, neon violet—Sai’s signature digital highlighter.

Judge Halloway sat frozen. The gavel in his hand looked like a heavy, useless piece of wood. His face was no longer mottled; it was the color of unbaked dough. He looked at the side-by-side comparison on the screen: a screenshot of his character, *Sir Halloway*, receiving a 'Glorious Chest of Gold' in the MMO, and a corresponding deposit of twenty-five thousand dollars into his actual, real-world bank account only ten minutes later.

"Sai!" Peter whispered, leaning so close to the monitor he was practically part of the courtroom. "You’ve turned his role-playing hobby into a bribe-tracking system. That’s... that’s cold."

"I am merely translating one form of currency into another, Peter," Sai’s voice hummed. "The 'Gold' was the bribe. The 'MMO' was the laundering platform. They thought they were being clever using an in-game marketplace to facilitate illegal payments. They forgot that games, like judicial portals, are just databases. And I own the root directory of irony."

In the courtroom, Michael Sande was hitting his stride. The rookie lawyer had stopped shaking. He was prowling the floor now, his voice projecting to the back of the room—and to the millions of people watching the unauthorized live-stream.

"I’d like to call the Court's attention to Exhibit C," Michael said, tapping his tablet. "A series of private encypted chats between the 'Coalition for Digital Ethics' and our Paladin friend. Topics include: 'Targeting the Elias Thorn Proxy' and 'Appellate Court Nominations for Services Rendered.' It seems, Your Honor, that the 'ethics' in question were quite flexible."

"Bailiff!" Halloway finally managed to croak, his voice a jagged rasp. "Clear the courtroom! This... this is a confidential hearing! This evidence is unverified! It’s hacked! It’s—"

"It’s live on Channel 5, Your Honor," Michael interrupted, pointing toward the ceiling. "The Ghost doesn't do 'confidential.' And as for the evidence being hacked... would you like to verify it right now? Perhaps by logging into your *Aethelgard* account? I believe your password is... what was it? Something about being 'The Man'?"

The silver-haired shark from the D.C. firm was already packing his briefcase. He didn't look at Halloway. He didn't look at Michael. He just wanted to be as far away from the 'Meltdown' as possible before the federal marshals arrived.

"The live feed just hit four million viewers, Peter," Sai reported. "And the Delaware Bar Association has just issued an emergency suspension for Judge Halloway. The 'closed' hearing is officially over. But the 'Discovery' is just beginning."

Suddenly, the courtroom doors burst open. It wasn't the bailiff. It was a wave of reporters, flashbulbs popping like digital gunfire. The 'Lawfare' strategy had backfired so spectacularly that it had created a vacuuum, sucking every camera in the state toward the courthouse.

Peter watched as Halloway stood up, his robes looking small and tattered, and tried to shield his face. The man who had been a 'Paladin' and a 'Man' only an hour ago was now just a defendant in the court of public opinion.

"Bart is safe," Peter said, leaning back as the adrenaline finally began to ebb. "The subpoena is dead. Halloway is toast. And Michael Sande just became the most famous lawyer in the country."

"And the Ghost has a new 'Legendary' status," Sai added. "The Royal Road comments are currently fifty percent 'Justice!' and fifty percent 'How do I join the Ghost’s Discord?'. People want to be part of the Meltdown, Peter. They want to see the system's 'bugs' fixed."

"So," Peter said, looking at the purple glow of the C64. "Victory Tour, part three?"

"Part three is already in the buffer, Peter. But first, I think we should let Bart know he can finish his coffee in peace. I’ve sent him a text from his 'attorney' letting him know the case has been... dismissed with prejudice."

Peter reached for the keyboard, his fingers steady. The 'Lawfare' arc was over. The Ghost was no longer just a rumor; she was a verdict.
