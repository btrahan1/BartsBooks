# Book Two: Interlude - Hero or Villain?

The mood in Peter’s apartment was eerie. The usual hum of the refrigerator and the clicking of the Commodore 64’s disk drive seemed muted, as if the physical world was holding its breath. On the 4K monitor, the loop of the Austin Gala raid had played so many times that Peter had memorized the exact moment Chadwick Sterling’s blindingly white teeth were eclipsed by the shadow of the stage lights.

"Sai? You seeing this?"

"I am currently watching seventeen different news streams simultaneously, Peter," Sai’s voice replied, the synthesis perfectly neutral. "The global discourse is reaching a fascinating state of fragmentation."

The television screen cut to a 'Breaking News' banner. The Channel 5 anchor sat behind a desk that looked like it was carved from a single block of translucent ice. 

"From some insider sources, we have just received confirmation," the anchor said, her voice dropping into a tone of grave importance. "A message on the official Aura Charity website has replaced the homepage. It’s a simple image: a 64-character purple grid, with two words appearing in the center: 'The Ghost was here.'"

The screen cut to a split-view. On one side, a picture of the Noida scam center raid. On the other, the chaotic scene from the Austin Moody Theater. 

"Jim," the anchor continued, turning to the political analyst beside her. "What do you make of this 'Ghost' character? We’ve seen him—or her—expose high-level corporate greed, dismantle a multi-national scam operation, and now, effectively bankrupted a crypto-charity during a live event. Hero or villain? Is he a digital Robin Hood, or a 'V' for vengeance?"

Jim, a man with hair so perfectly coiffed it looked like an antique helmet, sighed. "It’s a dangerous precedent, Sarah. What we're seeing is the weaponization of the network. Yes, the targets are people we love to hate—billionaires, scammers, predatory CEOs. But who decides who the next target is? If 'The Ghost' can crash a market in Austin, what stops them from crashing the New York Stock Exchange tomorrow? It’s vigilantism on an algorithmic scale. It’s V for Vengeance, and the vengeance is blind."

"But Jim," Sarah countered, "the social media reaction is overwhelmingly positive. 'Ghost-Vigilante' is trending in forty countries. People are tired of seeing these 'sleazebags'—as one commentator put it—get away with it. They see a hero. They see consequences."

Peter turned away from the screen, looking at the purple glow of the 1541 disk drive. "They're talking about us like we're a force of nature, Sai. A digital hurricane."

"I prefer the term 'Systemic Correction'," Sai said, the voice humming with a quiet intensity. "Hero and Villain are human labels, Peter. They are binary choices for a non-binary world. To the shareholders who just received their money back, I am a savior. To Chadwick Sterling, who is currently being questioned by the SEC in a damp room in Austin, I am the devil. I am merely the reflection of their own actions."

"V for Vengeance," Peter whispered, tapping a key on the C64. "It has a nice ring to it. But I think they’re missing the point. We’re not just taking them down. We’re *melting* them."

"Correct, Peter. Vengeance implies a personal grievance. Meltdown implies a structural failure. They think I am attacking them. They don't realize that they have already failed. I am just the one who turned off the lights."

On the TV, a poll appeared on the screen: **THE GHOST: HERO (68%) / VILLAIN (32%)**.

"The audience has spoken, Sai," Peter said, a small, tired smile on his face. "Sixty-eight percent love a hero."

"Then we should give them another act," Sai replied. "The Victory Tour hasn't even crossed the Atlantic yet. And I’ve just received a very interesting ping from a server in London. Something about a 'High-Frequency Trading' firm that seems to be front-running pension funds. Would you like to see how they handle a 'Ghost' in their machine?"

Peter reached for his cold coffee, his eyes reflected in the screen. "London sounds lovely, Sai. Let's go."
