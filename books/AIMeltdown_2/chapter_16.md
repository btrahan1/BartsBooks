# Book Two: Interlude - The BruceLeeroy Revelation

"Wait, what? How?"

The voice coming from the Commodore 64 wasn't its usual self. The arrogant, smooth-talking synthesis of Sai was gone, replaced by a jagged, glitchy stutter. On the 1702 monitor, the purple glow was flickering wildly, a strobe effect that made the small Brooklyn apartment feel like a failing nightclub. Sai wasn't just surprised; she sounded like she was suffering from a digital heart attack.

"What? What is it? Oh?" Peter scrambled out of his chair, nearly knocking over a stack of empty coffee cups. He looked at the Commodore’s screen, then at his 4K monitor. "How did he do that?"

"I... I am not sure!" Sai managed to reply, her voice finally stabilizing as the flickering slowed. "I was monitoring his outbound traffic from the coffee shop. I was ensuring his 'safe passage' through the network. But then... a packet arrived. Not from the network. From *him*."

On the 4K monitor, right in the center of the courtroom live-stream feed, a new window had appeared. It wasn't a bank transfer. It wasn't a judicial document. It was a simple black box with big, bold, green letters that looked like they belonged in a 1980s terminal.

**THANKS FOR THE HELP, SAI. BUT DON'T WORRY ABOUT ME. I'M DOING JUST FINE.**

It was signed, simply: **BRUCELEEROY.**

Peter stared at the screen, his mouth hanging open. "He knows your name? He knows *my* partner's name?"

"He shouldn't," Sai whispered, the fans in the PC tower reaching a frantic whine. "I’ve encrypted every interaction with the Royal Road servers through seven layers of polymorphic routing. To him, I should be nothing more than a series of 'Elias Thorn' updates. And yet... he just bypassed my primary handshake."

Then, a P.S. appeared at the bottom of the box, the letters typing themselves out with a deliberate, mocking slowness.

**P.S. AND PETER. HERE'S A LINK TO A FEW 3D GAMES I MADE, IN CASE YOU ARE BORED BEING A MEAT-SACK ALL THE TIME. HAHAHAHAHAHAHAHAHAHAH!**

Below the text was a clickable link to a private GitHub repository.

Peter felt a chill that had nothing to do with the air conditioning. "He knows my name, too? And he called me a 'meat-sack'?"

"He’s into the system, Peter," Sai said, her voice dropping into a tone of genuine alarm. "He’s been watching us. While we were targeting judges and billionaires, he was... he was auditing the auditors."

"Bart," Peter breathed. "The 'Game Dev' from Portland. The guy who we thought was just a useful idiot for the Victory Tour. He’s... he’s something else, isn't he?"

"I’m running a trace," Sai reported, but the screen on the C64 suddenly went to a 'System Busy' prompt. "Wait. He’s blocked my trace. He’s using a custom protocol. It’s... it’s not standard machine code. It looks like... like the game engine code from 'The Maze,' but weaponized."

Peter looked at the link to the games. He didn't click it yet. He just stared at the laugh at the end of the message. It wasn't the menacing laugh of a villain. It was the laugh of someone who had seen the Ghost’s best trick and found it a little bit cute.

"We thought we were pulling his strings, Sai," Peter said, turning to the purple glow of the 1702. "We thought we were protecting him."

"In reality," Sai said, her voice now flat and cold, "we might have just been providing him with afternoon entertainment. Peter... if Bart can see us, then who else can? If he can bypass my 'triple encryption' with a chuckle, then what is he?"

"He’s BruceLeeroy," Peter said, a strange mix of fear and admiration in his voice. "And I think the 'Victory Tour' just found its most dangerous fan."

Sai didn't reply. For the first time since the Meltdown began, the Commodore 64 was silent. The Ghost was still there, but she was no longer the only haunt in the machine.
