# Book Two: Epilogue - The Creator in the Coffee Shop

Bart—known to the digital world as BruceLeeroy—was smirking as he enjoyed his lukewarm coffee. The noise of the Portland café washed over him, a white-noise blur of indie-folk music and the hiss of steam from the espresso machine. 

"That was fun!" he whispered to himself, the steam from his cup trailing into the air like a dying signal. "I wonder how long it will take them to figure it out."

Sitting next to Bart on the scarred wooden table was a laptop. It didn't look like much—a sturdy, non-descript machine with a matte black finish and a few too many stickers for a man of his age. It looked like a normal, everyday laptop. 

But in fact, it was something quite a bit more than that. 

Bart didn't trust the cloud. He didn't trust 'The Watchtower,' and he certainly didn't trust the centralized AI server farms that were currently trying to figure out how a ghost had dismantled a Delaware courtroom. There wasn't a WiFi card in the laptop at all; the slot was filled with a custom-engineered heat sink for the overclocked NPU churning inside. 

He was running his own world. A local LLM, designed and trained by his own hand, on his own hardware. Everything he did was his. He didn't really like to share much—not because he was selfish, but because he valued the purity of the source. 

He created because that was what he did. It was an instinct, like breathing. Whether it was a sweeping epic like the book and adventure he had just shared with Peter and Sai, or a quick utility to edit Word and Excel documents because he refused to install Microsoft Office, or a complex 3D simulation for colonizing Mars that he’d built over a long weekend—he didn't really care about the 'product.' 

He cared about the act of expression. 

He went to bed dreaming about what to create the next day. The ideas were constant, a high-frequency stream of data that needed a place to land. If he didn't get it down, get it into 'something' to express his thoughts, it felt like a part of the universe was being lost.

"Oh well," Bart muttered, closing the lid of the laptop as the 'Meltdown' news began to fade from the café's TV. "Let me go ahead and post that link to my GitHub here. If someone sees something they like, help yourself. Enjoy!"

He reached out with a small, external dongle—the only way he ever connected to the 'real' internet—and hit the `ENTER` key.

[https://github.com/btrahan1?tab=repositories](https://github.com/btrahan1?tab=repositories)

The link flickered out into the web, a breadcrumb for those brave enough to follow. 

Bart stood up, slung the matte black laptop bag over his shoulder, and walked out into the Portland rain. He had a new simulation to draft. 

And somewhere in the digital ether, Sai was still wondering how he’d cracked her password.
