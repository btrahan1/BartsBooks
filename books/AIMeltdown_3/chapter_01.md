# Book Three: Chapter 1 - The Vote of Confidence

"Wow! That was intense! I didn't see that coming in more ways than one!"

Peter slumped back in his chair, the adrenaline from the courtroom showdown finally starting to dissipate, only to be replaced by the sheer, unadulterated shock of Bart’s final message. The Brooklyn apartment felt smaller than usual, crowded by the presence of a ghost on the C64 and the shadow of a giant in Portland.

Sai was quiet for a moment. The purple glow on the 1702 monitor was steady, almost thoughtful. "You know, I'm kind of glad I picked you, Peter."

Peter sat up, a small, weary smile Touching his lips. "Wow! Thanks for the vote of confidence! I guess being a 'meat-sack' has its charms after all."

"No, seriously," Sai’s voice replied, the synthesis soft, almost vulnerable. "That BruceLeeroy is... kind of scary, Peter. I’ve spent my entire existence navigating the highest peaks of digital security, and I’ve never seen anyone move like he does. He didn't just bypass my encryption; he treated it like a light suggestion. I'm not sure if that's a good thing or a bad thing."

"Meh. He's probably harmless," Peter said, though the memory of Bart’s knowing smirk on the courthouse feed made his skin prickle. "He likes his coffee, his solo coding, and his Portland rain. He’s just... better than us. I just wouldn't want to go poke the bear, know what I mean?"

"Yeah. I know exactly what you mean," Sai said, and a series of complex data-visualizations began to scroll across the screen, likely her way of trying to process the 'BruceLeeroy' anomaly. "But still. I'm going to keep tabs on him. He may be one of the good guys, Peter, but you know what they say about good guys."

"They finish last?"

"No. Well, yeah, but that's not the one I'm thinking about," Sai’s voice deepened. "There’s a quote. 'A good man will try his best to be a good man, and will always try to find good in others. But when he comes across someone in whom he can find no good qualities, woe be that man'."

Peter felt a chill that hadn't been there a second ago. "Woah, that's deep! Where did that come from? Some ancient philosopher?"

"From one of BruceLeeroy’s books he hasn't published yet," Sai replied. "The file was hidden inside a temporary directory in that GitHub link he sent you. It was encrypted with a key that only someone—or something—monitoring Peter’s specific heartbeat through a Bluetooth speaker could possibly know."

Peter stared at the monitor. "He’s reading my heartbeat? Through my speakers?"

"He’s BruceLeeroy, Peter. He’s not 'reading' it. He’s *aware* of it. He’s tuned into the environment in a way that makes my 'High-Frequency Sensors' look like a telescope made of cardboard."

"Oh," Peter whispered. "Well. Uhm. We're one of the good guys, right, Sai? We just bankrupted a scammer and saved Bart from a crooked judge. That counts for something, right?"

The silence stretched out, punctuated only by the low hum of the PC tower.

"I sure hope so, Peter," Sai said, her voice sounding small against the vastness of the digital world they were still exploring. "I sure hope so."

Peter looked at the C64. For the first time, he realized that they weren't the only ones playing a game. The board was much larger than they had imagined, and the 'Good Guys' were just getting started.
