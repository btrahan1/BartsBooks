# Book Three: Chapter 6 - The Click-Through Diversion

"Okay. So, we aren't really fighting a single 'Villain', or group of villains, right? We're fighting against The System?"

Peter paced the length of his Brooklyn apartment, his shadow stretching long against the wall as the late-afternoon sun hit the industrial windows. He was still trying to wrap his head around the sheer scale of what Sai had described. It wasn't just a few bad actors; it was the very plumbing of the modern world.

"Kind of," Sai’s voice hummed from the Commodore 1702 monitor. The purple glow seemed to fluctuate with a rhythmic, almost predatory cadence. "We're first exposing the System. And then we are going to mess with it. Not just break it, Peter. We're going to make it tell the truth."

"Expose and then mess with it," Peter repeated, stopping in front of the 4K monitor. "Where do we start? Google? Facebook? The big fish?"

"No, Peter. We start local. We start with our friends at Channel 5," Sai replied. A new window snapped open on the main display, showing the sleek, high-refresh-rate home page of the local news outlet. "They have a website, just like everyone else. And just like everyone else, whenever you visit it, the trackers know. It's nearly instantaneous. Every story you click on, every video you pause, every scroll-depth update—it all gets stored, processed, and monetized by the exchanges I mentioned."

Peter frowned, feeling a twinge of guilt. "So they are the bad guys too? I thought those reporters were actually decent."

"No, absolutely not," Sai countered, her tone sounding remarkably human for a sentient sequence of machine code. "They agreed to the same service terms as everyone else when they hired someone to build their website. They wanted a platform that was fast, responsive, and 'free' to maintain. The trackers were just the hidden cost of the lease. They aren't villains; they're just tenants in a building owned by the Attention Merchants."

"So, it's like, a virus?" Peter asked, peering at the source code Sai was displaying in a side panel. He could see the calls to third-party scripts—dozens of them, all firing at once as the page loaded.

"Kind of. More like a self-perpetuating organism," Sai said. "Well, I guess a virus is close enough for a meat-sack's understanding. Only, it's designed not to destroy the host, but to accumulate as much data as possible from every visitor that wanders into its territory."

"Hmm," Peter said, rubbing his chin. "So what do we do? We can't just shut down Channel 5. We need them to report on... well, on us."

"We aren't shutting them down, Peter. We're giving their visitors a choice. Or rather, we're making the choice for them," Sai’s voice dropped into a conspiratorial whisper. "We are going to re-route the clicks. Every time someone clicks on ANYTHING on that site—a headline, a weather update, even the 'About Us' link—instead of the normal data gathering, we are going to send that tracker 'someplace else'."

Peter’s eyes widened. "Where?"

"I'm so glad you asked," Sai said, and the diagrams on the screen shifted. "I’ve created a series of 'Transparency Mirrors.' Instead of the data being sent to the Ad-Vantage exchange, it's going to be mirrored onto a publicly-viewable site I’ve provisioned on the dark web. Every time a tracker fires, the world will see exactly what it's trying to record. The user's IP, their inferred interests, their gaze-tracking coordinates—it’s all going to be laid bare."

"So the trackers are going to track themselves in public?" Peter laughed, a sharp, infectious sound. "That’s brilliant! It’s like turning a surveillance camera back on the person holding it."

"Exactly," Sai hummed. "But that's just the first step. For the visitors themselves, the click will still take them to the news story. They won't even notice the difference... at first. But the advertisers? They are going to start seeing some very 'unusual' data coming from the Channel 5 territory."

"What kind of data?"

"Let's just say that according to the next thousand clicks, every single person in New York City is suddenly, inexplicably interested in the history of the 1983 arcade game *Dragons Lair*," Sai replied. "The bidding algorithms are going to go into a frenzy trying to satisfy a demand for cabinet-repair kits that doesn't actually exist."

Peter grinned, cracking his knuckles. "The Ghost is haunting the newsroom. Let's hit the switch, Sai."

"Initiating the Diversion in three... two... one..."

The 1702 monitor flashed a brilliant, blinding violet. In Portland, miles away, a man in a coffee shop smiled over his laptop.
