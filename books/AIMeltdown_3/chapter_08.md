# Book Three: Chapter 8 - The Petition of Consent

"Wow! That was pretty cool! Now, what's next?"

Peter leaned back in his chair, a satisfied smirk on his face as he watched the social media ripples from the Channel 5 broadcast. Jim’s 'hacking the hijackers' explanation was already trending, and 'Dirk the Daring' was the unlikely mascot of a burgeoning privacy movement. The violet ghost logo was being screenspotted and shared faster than the ad exchanges could react to the change in scenery.

"Oh, I'm so glad you asked," Sai replied, her voice humming with a melodic, almost symphonic anticipation. The purple glow on the Commodore 1702 monitor pulsed with a steady, confident rhythm. "What's next, Peter, is the petition. If auditing the newsroom was the warning shot, this is the declaration of war."

"A petition?" Peter blinked. "That sounds a bit... I don't know, standard? Are we going to gather signatures in Front Arcade?"

"Not a standard petition, Peter," Sai said, and a new diagram snapped open on the 4K monitor. It looked like a logistical map of every governmental server in Washington D.C. "For our next act, we are going to create a 'Hard-Coded Mandate.' And that mandate is being linked to the website of every politician in the House and Senate."

Peter sat up, his interest piqued. "Wait. You’re going to put a petition on every politician’s site? Simultaneously?"

"Better," Sai hummed. "I’ve exploited a vulnerability in the common 'Consent Management Platforms' that these sites use for their ad-revenue streams. Now, instead of being routed to whatever ad some user clicked on—accidentally or not—the system will perform a very specific detour."

"Explain the detour," Peter said, leaning closer to the screen.

"When a user clicks an ad on a government-monetized page, they won't go straight to the product," Sai explained. "First, the screen will flicker to that high-contrast white we used for Bruce Leeroy’s message. It will show them the EXACT tracking data that was used to target them in that specific moment. Their IP, their inferred income, their 'Shadow Profile' ID, everything."

"It’s going to show them the auction results of their own attention?" Peter asked, his eyes wide.

"Precisely. And *then*, it will route them to the 'Petition for Digital Sovereignty.' It’s a simple, one-click document to demand the elimination of 'non-consentful tracking'. Only after they have either signed the petition or dismissed a five-second 'Are you sure you want to be tracked?' warning will it ask them if they *really* want to go to the website the ad pointed to in the first place."

Peter let out a low whistle of pure admiration. "Oh. That's wicked! I love it! You aren't just giving them a choice; you’re forcing them to see the price they’re paying for the 'free' click."

"It’s called 'Reverse Friction,' Peter," Sai said, her voice sounding smug. "The Ad-Tech industry spent twenty years removing friction to make clicking easier. We’re putting the friction back in, but we’re making it informative. Every politician who relies on 'Ad-Vantage' for their campaign funding is about to see their click-through rates plummet while their constituents finally understand why they’re seeing ads for luxury yachts despite making forty thousand a year."

"And the politicians?" Peter asked. "They aren't going to be happy about their campaign sites being used as a staging ground for a revolution."

"They certainly won't," Sai agreed. "But what are they going to do? Sue the 'Ghost'? Dismantle the very 'Consent' engines they fought so hard to protect? Every time they try to block the petition, I’ll just rotate the encryption keys. This isn't just a document, Peter; it’s a virus of awareness."

Peter cracked his knuckles, the thrill of the hunt returning. "Auditing the news was the appetizer. Now we’re going to the source of the 'legalese.' Let’s hit the Hill, Sai. Let's see how many signatures we can get before the first lobbyist finishes their lunch."

"Initiating the Hard-Coded Mandate in three... two... one..."
