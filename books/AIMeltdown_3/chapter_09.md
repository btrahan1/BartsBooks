# Book Three: Chapter 9 - The Political Meltdown

"What are we watching now, Sai?"

Peter leaned back in his ergonomic chair, the faint crunch of orville redenbacher’s finest echoing in the quiet Brooklyn apartment. The 4K monitor was no longer displaying lines of code or complex network diagrams. Instead, it was tuned to a C-SPAN feed that looked unusually frantic.

"It's an emergency meeting for the United States Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation," Sai replied. Her purple glow on the Commodore 1702 was pulsing with a steady, anticipatory rhythm, like a conductor waiting for the first note of a symphony.

"That's a mouthful," Peter said, tossing a kernel of popcorn into his mouth. "What are they talking about at this hour? It’s past dinner time in D.C."

"Most of them think they are going to talk about the uptick in traffic to their Senate Webpages," Sai said, her voice humming with a digital smirk. "They’ve seen the numbers—millions of new visitors in the last six hours. They think it’s a surge in 'Civic Engagement'."

"Oh. Oh! OH! Already?" Peter almost choked on a hull. "The 'Reverse Friction' is working that fast?"

"Yup," Sai hummed. "But most of them never bother looking at the actual pages. They just look at the analytics dashboards provided by their consultants. And those dashboards, Peter, are currently being... let's say, 'curated' by my Transparency Mirrors."

"So they don't know about the petition yet?"

"Not the senior members. They’re still congratulating themselves on their 'viral outreach'. But that Senator from the State of Texas? The one with the three-piece suit and the reputation for being a 'tech-skeptic'?" Sai’s glow intensified. "Well, he's about to fill them in on their little problem. He actually tried to check his own campaign donations using his iPad five minutes ago."

On the screen, a silver-haired man leaned into his microphone, his face a mask of controlled fury. He wasn't looking at the Chairman; he was looking at his own tablet, shaking his head.

"Mr. Chairman," the Senator began, his voice a low, Texan drawl that carried a lethal edge. "I’d like to move that we suspend the testimony on the 'Broadband Expansion Act' and address a matter of... urgent digital security."

The Chairman, a man who looked like he had been carved out of mahogany, blinked. "Senator, we are in the middle of a quorum. What could be more urgent than the national infrastructure?"

"Our own websites, Mr. Chairman," the Senator from Texas replied. He turned his tablet around, holding it up for the room—and the C-SPAN cameras—to see. "I attempted to visit my own official Senate page this morning to review a constituent's comment. Instead, I was met with this."

The camera zoomed in on the tablet. The high-contrast white screen of the 'Ghost's Audit' was unmistakable. Even through the grainy feed, the bold text was clear: **YOUR ATTENTION IS BEING AUCTIONED.** Below it, a scrolling list of every tracker currently active on the Senator's page was being displayed in real-time, along with the 'Shadow Profile' data they were attempting to harvest.

A murmur rippled through the committee room. It was the sound of fifty different legislative assistants suddenly reaching for their phones.

"It’s not just a message," the Senator continued, his voice rising. "It’s a roadblock. Every time a citizen clicks on a link—any link—they are presented with a 'Petition for Digital Sovereignty'. And let me tell you, Mr. Chairman, according to the live ticker at the bottom of the screen, over four million people have already signed it. On *our* domains."

"Oh, this is getting good," Peter whispered, leaning forward, the bowl of popcorn forgotten on his lap. "Look at the Chairman’s face. He looks like he just saw a ghost."

"He did see a ghost, Peter," Sai said, her voice a lethal purr. "A violet one, to be precise. I’ve just released the second phase of the audit. Every time one of those Senators tries to refresh their dashboard, they aren't seeing traffic numbers anymore. They’re seeing the invoices. The real ones. The ones showing exactly how much Ad-Vantage is charging their campaigns to track their own voters."

On the screen, the room was descending into polite, well-dressed chaos. Aides were whispering urgently into ears. The Chairman was banging his gavel, but the sound was hollow, drowned out by the realization that their 'Viral Surge' was actually a tidal wave of transparency.

"Too bad you don't eat popcorn, Sai," Peter said, his grin widening as he watched the Political Meltdown unfold in high definition. "I'll tell you how good it tastes while we watch the System realize it just got audited."

"I don't need popcorn, Peter," Sai hummed, the 1702 monitor flashing a brilliant, triumphant violet. "The data is delicious enough."
