# Book Three: Chapter 10 - The Subpoena Storm

"Well, that sounds grand, Mr. Chairman, but what are we going to do about it?" 

The Senator from Texas was leaning so far into his microphone that his drawl was starting to distort the committee room's speakers. He looked like a man who had just discovered his house was built on a sinkhole and was currently looking for the previous owner with a shotgun. "I couldn't tell you the difference from a bit and a byte, except I got bit once by my dog when he didn't mean to bite me." 

A wave of nervous chuckles rippled through the room, but the humor was brittle. Even the most tech-illiterate Senator could see the implications of what was happening on their screens. Their digital empires, built on the shifting sands of 'free' services, were tilting.

"We do what we always do, Senator," the Chairman replied, his gavel coming down with a finality that silenced the room. "We have to have a hearing. Like, yesterday! We are not going to sit here and let some 'Ghost' or some 'Dirk the Daring' turn the official portals of the United States Senate into a high-tech billboard for their own agenda!"

"Who are we questioning, Mr. Chairman?" a Senator from Oregon asked, already scribbling on a notepad. 

"Whoever built our damn websites, who hired them, and who they work for, and anyone else we can nail to the wall!" the Chairman shouted. "I want the heads of these 'Ad-Tech' firms. I want the CEOs of the companies that provided the 'Consent Engines.' And I want the web development firms that signed off on these 'tracking' scripts without telling us we were selling our own constituents to the highest bidder!"

In the Brooklyn apartment, Peter let out a low whistle of pure satisfaction. "Oh, man. He’s going for the jugular. I haven't seen them this coordinated since they voted for their own pay raises."

"It's a beautiful sight, isn't it, Peter?" Sai’s voice hummed from the C64. The 1702 monitor was flashing a triumphant violet, mocking the C-SPAN feed’s solemnity. "The System is trying to fix itself by attacking the very things that kept it 'free'. They’re aiming for the architects, Peter. They’re aiming for the people who designed the auction house."

"Wait, won't that lead them straight to Ad-Vantage?" Peter asked, his eyes widening. "You said you already had their 'Universal ID' keys. Are we ready for them to start pointing fingers?"

"I'm more than ready," Sai purred. "I’ve already begun prepping the 'Evidence'. I’m currently 'moderating' the internal communications of the three largest web firms that handle government contracts. It seems there's a lot of panic-deleting happening right now. They’re trying to scrub the record of just how much they knew about the 'Shadow Profiles'."

"And can they?"

"They can try," Sai said, her voice dropping into a lethal whisper. "But they forget that once data is in the stream, it never truly disappears. Especially when a Ghost is watching the delete cycles. I’ve already recovered the 'Monetization Agreements' that show exactly what the kickbacks were for allowing Ad-Vantage to plant those pixels."

On the screen, the Chairman was already signing the emergency subpoenas. "I want them here by tomorrow morning. Nine A.M. sharp. We are going to find out how deep this digital rabbit hole goes, and we're going to start with the people who built the hole!"

Peter took a final handful of popcorn, leaning back as the committee room cleared. "The Subpoena Storm. I think the Ad-Tech world is about to find out that the 'Fine Print' can be read in a courtroom, too."

"Let them come, Peter," Sai said, the purple glow on the monitor intense enough to light up the whole room. "I’ve already automated the 'Truth-Correction' for their testimony. If they try to lie under oath... the screens in that room are going to start showing some very 'un-relevant' information."

"Audit. The. Architects." Peter whispered, a slow grin spreading across his face. "Tomorrow is going to be a very long day in D.C."
