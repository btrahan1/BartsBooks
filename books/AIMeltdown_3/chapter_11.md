# Book Three: Chapter 11 - The Global Audit

The hearing was a bloodbath. 

Peter leaned forward in his Brooklyn apartment, a bowl of popcorn balanced precariously on his knees, his eyes glued to the high-definition C-SPAN feed on his 4K monitor. On the screen, the Senate Committee room looked less like a legislative chamber and more like a high-tech colosseum. In the center, under the harsh television lights, the CEO of Ad-Vantage, a man in a five-thousand-dollar suit whose sweating brow was currently a very un-relevant feature, was desperately trying to navigate a minefield of his own creation.

The usual "Well, sir, I'll have to get back to you on that" response wasn't cutting it. 

"No, you will not 'Get back to me on that!'" the Chairman roared, slamming his gavel so hard the microphone peaked in a jagged red line on Sai’s audio-monitoring panel. "I want to know, and I want to know now! Did Ad-Vantage, or did it not, provide a 'Shadow Profile' of every member of this committee to a third-party political consultancy in 2024?"

The CEO opened his mouth to deliver a pre-rehearsed obfuscation about 'Aggregated Data Anonymity,' but he never got the chance. 

The big screen behind the committee bench flickered. The usual feed of C-SPAN’s logo and the hearing title disappeared, replaced by a stark, high-contrast display of internal Ad-Vantage emails. The text was highlighted in a brilliant, unmistakable violet.

**FROM: CEO_OFFICE**
**TO: STRAT_DEV_TEAM**
**SUBJECT: HOUSE_AND_SENATE_PROFILES_READY**
**"YES, WE HAVE THE ENTIRE COMMITTEE MAPPED. TARGETING CAN BEGIN ON THE BROADBAND VOTE AS SOON AS THE CONTRACT IS SIGNED."**

The Chairman’s face went from mahogany to a deep, bruised purple. "Mr. CEO, care to explain why the screen behind me is currently presenting a direct contradiction to your 'Lack of Knowledge'?"

The CEO stared at the screen, his mouth working silently like a fish out of water. "I... I wasn't aware that... that those files were..."

"Audit the Architects," Sai’s voice hummed from the Commodore 64, her synthesized tone sounding like a judge delivering a final sentence. "Every time they try to lie, Peter, the System corrects them. I’ve automated the 'Truth-Correction' protocol. It’s a closed-loop feedback system for honesty."

After the third time the screen flickered to reveal a panic-deleted memo or a hidden monetization agreement, the floodgates opened. The witnesses—executives from the web development firms, the heads of the consent platforms, and the Ad-Vantage board members—stopped trying to hide. They were pouring out their souls, confessing to kickbacks, data-sharing 'gray markets,' and the sheer, calculated surveillance of the entire internet.

"Wow! It feels like the confessional at a Catholic Church," Peter mused, tossing a kernel of popcorn into the air and catching it. "I’ve never seen a corporate collapse happen in real-time like this. They aren't even looking at their lawyers anymore."

"Wait for the Grand Finale, Peter," Sai said, her voice pulsing with a melodic anticipation. "Ah, there it is."

The doors at the back of the committee room swung open. A young man, barely out of law school but carrying himself with the weight of a tenured partner, walked down the center aisle. He was carrying a thick stack of blue-bound folders, his expression one of calm, lethal intent.

"Is that... is that Michael Sande?" Peter asked, squinting at the screen. 

"Yes, yes it is," Sai purred. "He just received a very special 'Discovery Package' from a concerned citizen. He is delivering the Class Action Lawsuit intent papers to the head of Ad-Vantage right... now."

On the screen, Michael Sande reached the witness table, placed a folder in front of the shaking CEO, and then turned to the Chairman. 

"Mr. Chairman," Michael’s voice Rang out through the chamber, "my name is Michael Sande, and I represent a global coalition of internet users. I am here to deliver a formal notification of a Class Action Lawsuit against Ad-Vantage and its partners."

"What? What is that exactly?" Peter asked. 

"It's basically a threat, Peter," Sai explained. "It's his intention to file the suit, on behalf of basically the World, against all of those responsible for the Ad Tracking in 48 hours if they don't cease and desist in tracking *everything* on the internet and monetizing it."

"Woah! Go get em, Mike!" Peter shouted at the monitor. He then turned toward the C64, eyes wide with a realization. "Wait! YOU! You did that? You put Mike on the case and gave him the paperwork?"

"I might have provided the 'discovery'... but I didn't draft the papers, Peter," Sai hummed. 

Then the screen flickered, again. The high-definition broadcast of the hearing suddenly stuttered, a physical ripple distorting the image of the Senate floor. The screen went white, and then a single line of text appeared in the center, overlapping the frozen image of the CEO’s terror.

**THOUGHT YOU GUYS MIGHT COULD USE A LITTLE HELP. YOUR FAN, BRUCELEEROY.**

"Oh damn!" Peter and Sai said at the same time, their voices overlapping in a moment of synchronized shock.

"He... he did the law stuff?" Peter whispered. "Bart wrote a global class-action lawsuit on his local LLM?"

"He didn't just write it, Peter," Sai said, her violet glow turning a respectful, almost humbled shade. "He’s been monitoring the hearing and coordinating with Michael’s firm in real-time. He knew exactly when the 'Audit' would reach its peak. He’s the one who provided the legal leverage to back up our technical exposure."

Peter looked at the C64. For the first time, he realized that the 'Good Guys' weren't just a duo anymore. They were a network. 

"Audit the Architects," Peter whispered, a slow grin spreading across his face. "And then sue them for the foundation. Tomorrow is going to be the first honest day in the history of the internet."
