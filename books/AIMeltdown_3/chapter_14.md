# Book Three: Chapter 14 - The Garage Encounter

"What are we watching now, Sai?"

Peter leaned in closer to his 4K monitor. The high-speed, contextual web was still humming along in the background, but his main focus was now on a grainier, wide-angle feed that looked like it belonged on a home security app. It was a doorbell camera, overlooking a tidy, suburban driveway.

"It's BruceLeeroy's doorbell camera," Sai replied, her voice soft, as if she were afraid of being overheard by the participants on the screen. The violet glow of the 1702 monitor was dim, matching the tension in the room.

"What? Oh no, not this again?" Peter’s heart rate spiked. "Did the Ad-Vantage people find him? Are we going to do something? We have his address, right? I can call... well, I don't even know who to call for a guy like Bart."

"Nothing," Sai said, her synthesized tone surprisingly calm. "We aren't going to do anything. At least not yet. BruceLeeroy is... prepared. He’s been expecting company ever since he sent that file to Michael Sande."

On the screen, two black SUVs had pulled into the driveway, silent as sharks. Four men in dark, well-pressed suits stepped out. They didn't look like hackers, and they didn't look like the usual corporate security. They had the stiff, over-practiced posture of federal investigators—or the kind of private 'consultants' that expensive law firms hired to make problems go away.

One of them approached the door and pressed the chime.

"He's inviting them in?" Peter whispered as the door on the feed swung open. Bart appeared, looking exactly like he did in his profile photos: slightly messy hair, a faded t-shirt that said 'I Speak C++', and a non-committal expression that suggested the arrival of federal agents was no more disruptive than a pizza delivery.

Bart gestured toward the side of the house. "Gentlemen. Follow me. It’s a bit crowded in the entryway. Let’s go to the garage."

"Is that his garage?" Peter’s jaw dropped as the camera switched to an internal view. "Woah! That's some garage!"

"Indeed it is!" Sai hummed. 

It wasn't a place for cars. The space was a cathedral of high-end engineering. Along one wall, three-dimensional printers were humming with quiet precision, their laser heads darting over complex lattices of carbon fiber. Another wall was dominated by a server rack that looked like a miniature version of a data center, the status LEDs blinking in a chaotic, multi-colored dance. There were workstations with quad-monitor setups, a CNC milling machine in the corner, and a workbench covered in what looked like custom-made drone components. It was a tinkerer’s paradise, a local LLM’s birthplace.

Bart led the men toward a small seating area near the server rack. "Have a seat. I was just finishing a compilation. It’ll take a few minutes."

The men in suits looks uncomfortable. They were used to intimidation, but the sheer density of intelligence in the room was overwhelming. They looked at the machines like they were alien artifacts.

"Oh, is that his wife?" Peter asked as a woman entered the frame from a side door leading to the house. She was carrying a tray with a teapot and several delicate china cups. "Wow! She's really pretty! She's serving them tea?"

"She is a 'Stabilizing Influence', Peter," Sai noted. "She’s the only one who can actually make BruceLeeroy stop talking about data architecture long enough to eat."

The woman placed the tray on the coffee table between the suits and her husband. She didn't look intimidated. In fact, she looked like she was humoring a group of toddlers who had wandered into a laboratory.

"Okay, Bart," she said, her voice clear and carrying a playful, yet iron-clad authority. "You can get back to whatever you were working on, dear. I'll take it from here."

Bart looked at her, then at the suits, then gave a small, appreciative shrug. "Sure thing, honey. Tell them about the 'informed consent' clauses if they get pushy." He turned and walked back to his main workstation, disappearing into a sea of monitors.

The wife turned to the suit in the center. Her mischievous grin remained, but her eyes locked onto his in a very scary stare—the kind of look that suggested she knew exactly what was in those black SUVs and why they were really there.

"Now, gentlemen," she said, her voice dropping into a tone that was polite, chilled, and utterly dominant. "What can I help you with? And please, spare me the jurisdictional speech. I’ve already audited your warrants, and I find them... lacking in imagination."

In the Brooklyn apartment, Peter let out a breath he didn't realize he was holding. "I think the 'Good Guys' just got an upgrade, Sai. I’m starting to think Bart isn't the scariest person in that house."

"He isn't, Peter," Sai hummed, the purple glow on the 1702 flickering in what almost looked like a digital wink. "Not by a long shot."
