# Book Three: Chapter 15 - The Tiger in the Garage

For over two hours, "The Wife" took charge of the "investigation." 

Peter sat in his Brooklyn loft, his bag of popcorn long since emptied and forgotten on the floor. His mouth had been open for the better part of the last ninety minutes, his eyes fixed on the 4K monitor. The doorbell camera's audio was crisp, capturing every nuance of the verbal slaughter occurring in Bart’s garage. Sai’s violet glow was steady, almost reverent, as if she were witnessing a masterclass in a discipline she hadn't quite mastered yet: the art of the social pounce.

She smiled politely, sipped her tea from a delicate china cup that looked absurdly fragile in the high-tech surroundings, and then devoured every question the men in suits asked like a starving tiger.

"Now, Mrs. Trahan," the lead investigator said, his voice tight with a frustration that was becoming increasingly visible in the way he gripped his leather-bound notepad. "We have reason to believe that the data-audit that paralyzed the Ad-Vantage exchange originated from a local node within this residence. We have the IP footprints. We have the timestamps."

"Oh really? Is that how you see it?" she replied, her voice as smooth as aged bourbon. She took a slow, deliberate sip of tea, her eyes never leaving his. "Because from where I’m sitting—and I do have a rather good seat—it looks like you’re attempting to manufacture a jurisdictional claim based on a 'footprint' that your own internal IT department admitted was spoofed three hours ago."

"We haven't admitted any such thing," the man countered, his face reddening.

"And then she pounces," Peter whispered, leaning so close to the monitor he could see the individual pixels of the suit's sweat.

"Perhaps you haven't checked your secure Slack channel in the last fifteen minutes, Agent... it is Agent, isn't it?" she asked, her smile widening just enough to show teeth. "Because if you had, you’d see the memo from your Digital Forensics chief. The one where he explains that the 'local node' you’re so excited about is actually a Transparency Mirror located in a server rack in Reykjavik. A rack that, coincidentally, is currently hosting a public audit of your department's own travel expenses for the last fiscal year."

The man in the suit froze. He didn't reach for his phone. He didn't look at his colleagues. He just stared at the woman in front of him, the realization of his собственного vulnerability finally starting to sink in.

"Wait, did she just...?" Peter looked at the C64. "Sai, did she just audit the FBI's travel budget?"

"No, Peter," Sai hummed, her tone vibrant with admiration. "She didn't audit them. She simply pointed out that the data was already public. She’s using the 'Reverse Friction' we built, but she’s applying it to their professional lives. She’s making it harder for them to lie than it is to simply leave."

Throughout the two-hour ordeal, every attempt at intimidation was met with the same terrifyingly polite deconstruction. If they asked about Bart’s 'unauthorized access,' she asked them about the definition of 'authorization' in a world where the government’s own websites were violating their own privacy standards. If they mentioned the 'disruption of commerce,' she sipped her tea and asked if they considered 'surveillance' to be a valid form of trade.

She started every response with that same, lethal hook: "Oh really? Is that how you see it?" 

And then she dismantled their world.

"She’s a monster," Peter whispered, his voice full of awe. "A beautiful, scary, tea-drinking monster."

"She is an expert in 'Informational Sovereignty', Peter," Sai added. "Bart might have built the tools, but she understands the terrain. She knows that their power relies on the illusion of secrecy. Once she removes the secrecy, they’re just men in suits standing in a garage, looking at a milling machine they don't understand."

On the screen, the lead investigator finally sighed, closing his notepad with a snap that sounded like a surrender. "We’ll be in touch, Mrs. Trahan. This isn't over."

"Oh really? Is that how you see it?" she replied, rising from her chair with a grace that made the men look clumsy as they scrambled to their feet. "Because from where I’m sitting, you have exactly twenty minutes before the 48-hour clock hits zero and the world finds out exactly who authorized the 'Shadow Profile' contracts for your field office. I suggest you spend that time driving back to the airport. The tea was on the house."

The men didn't wait. They practically tripped over each other getting back to the black SUVs. As the engines roared to life and they sped out of the driveway, Bart finally peeked his head out from behind his quad-monitor setup.

"Everything okay, honey?" he asked, his voice sounding genuinely curious.

"Fine, dear," she said, picking up the tray. "They just needed a little help with their perspective. You can get back to your compilation now. I think I’ll make some lunch."

Peter watched the SUVs disappear into the suburban distance. He looked at his own apartment, then at the violet glow of the C64. "I’m never, ever going to piss her off, Sai. Ever."

"A wise decision, Peter," Sai hummed. "A very wise decision."
