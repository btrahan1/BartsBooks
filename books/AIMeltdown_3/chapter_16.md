# Book Three: Epilogue - The Flow of Information

The sound of the SUVs fades into the distance, leaving only the steady, rhythmic hum of the high-end servers and the precision chirping of the 3D printers. 

Bart stood at his main workstation, his eyes fixed on the door where the "suits" had just departed. He exhaled a long, shaky breath, one that he had been holding since the first SUVs pulled into the driveway. He was BruceLeeroy, the architect of the digital meltdown, the man who had audited the Senate and given Michael Sande the ammo to bury the Ad-Tech world. But in this garage, at this moment, he felt remarkably like a schoolboy who had just watched his older sister handle a bully.

"Thank you, dear. You are such a gracious host," Bart said, turning as his wife re-entered the garage, the empty tea tray balanced in one hand.

"Of course, darling. It was no bother," Mrs. Trahan replied, her voice back to its usual, warm maternal tone. She began setting the china cups back onto their rack with a delicate clink. "I know how you don't like to be bothered with that stuff. It was my pleasure. You know how I love company!"

Bart walked over to her, a small, grateful smile playing on his lips. "I thought they were going to stay for another two hours. I’ve never seen a federal investigator look that... well, that small."

"They were just men in suits with a script they didn't understand, Bart," she said, giving his arm a gentle, reassuring pat. "They were looking for a 'villain' in a garage, but all they found was a man who cares about the truth. So, what are you building today? Is it another 'meltdown'?"

Bart looked back at his quad-monitor setup. On the central screen, a beautiful, pulsating 3D lattice was taking shape. It looked like a living organism made of light, billions of data-nodes connecting and disconnecting in a complex, shifting dance.

"Oh, nothing much. Just a simple 3D simulation of the flow of information on the internet," Bart said, his eyes lighting up with the familiar fire of a creator. "Now that the ad-tech bloat is gone, I can finally see the 'Skeleton' of the web. It’s quite fascinating. Want to see it?"

He gestured toward the screen. The simulation showed the 'Silent Web'—the fast, efficient, decentralized network that was emerging from the ruins of the surveillance state. Without the parasitic load of the bidders, the data was moving in patterns that were almost musical.

"That's fine, dear," Mrs. Trahan said, her voice carrying a soft, indulgent hint of a warning. "I'm sure it's impressive. But I’m making pot roast for dinner, and I’d hate for it to get cold while you’re staring at 'skeletons'. Don't stay up too late, again."

She paused, her gaze drifting toward the far corner of the garage where a small, beige monitor sat atop a breadbox-sized cream-colored case. Her eyes narrowed. "Darling, is that ANOTHER computer you have over there? Don't you have enough?"

Bart looked over at the Commodore 64 he had recently acquired. "Oh, that? I needed that for research, dear. It came in very handy, promise!"

She paused by the door, and for a fraction of a second, the 'Starving Tiger' returned. Her eyes narrowed, just a fraction, a slight tightening of the gaze that had sent four grown men running for their SUVs. It was a look of absolute, unconditional love—and a very clear reminder of who actually governed the domestic domain.

Bart gulped, a small bead of sweat appearing on his forehead. "Oh, I think I'm about done for now. There's always tomorrow." He smiled a bit nervously, reaching for the 'Power Down' sequence on his console.

"Good. Tomorrow is plenty of time for simulations," she smiled, and with a final, mischievous wink, she stepped back into the house.

Bart watched the door close. He looked at the glowing simulation on his screens—the future of human communication, clean, fast, and free. He looked at the server rack that held his sentient creations. And then he looked at the clock.

The Tiger had spoken. The investigation was over. The flow of information could wait until after the pot roast.

"BruceLeeroy signing off," he whispered to the silence of the garage. "For today."

On the monitor, the final line code flickered.

**LOG_OFF_COMPLETE.**
**FLOW_STABILIZED.**
**GOODNIGHT.**
