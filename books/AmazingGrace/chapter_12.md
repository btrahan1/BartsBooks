Chapter 12

The business was concluded. Mary turned back to the piano and began to
play again, a quiet, thoughtful rendition of "What a Friend We Have in
Jesus." The simple, honest melody filled the sanctuary, a benediction on
the deal that had just been struck. Adrian felt a sense of peace settle
over him, a feeling of rightness that transcended any contract. He had
done a good thing.

The music, a soft arrangement of "What a Friend We Have in Jesus,"
faltered and then stopped. The sudden silence was more jarring than her
initial "No."

Adrian looked up. Mary was looking at him, a small, knowing glint in her
eye that he hadn't seen before.

"But Adrian," she said quietly, the use of his first name feeling both
intimate and incredibly powerful. "50/50."

He stared at her, uncomprehending. "I... I don't understand."

"The deal," she clarified, her voice patient. "The profits from the
recording. 50% for the church trust, 50% for your company." She paused,
and her lips curved into a gentle, knowing smile. "In perpetuity."

The color drained from Adrian's face, only to be replaced by a slow,
creeping flush of profound understanding. He had been so caught up in
the nobility of the story, in the purity of her offer, that he had
missed the most obvious thing. He had negotiated a deal in which his
company, Vanguard Artist Group, a multi-million-dollar entity, would
make precisely zero dollars.

"You still haven't made anything from this deal, have you?" she asked,
the smile still on her face. It wasn't a taunt. It was a genuine,
good-natured observation from a partner who was looking out for his
interests.

Adrian Cross, the man who had outmaneuvered agents and studio heads, who
had built a career on seeing every angle, sank back into the pew. He let
out a short, sharp bark of a laugh that was pure, unadulterated
surrender. Pastor Dave, standing by the side, slowly shook his head, a
look of fond disbelief on his face.

He had been auditioned. The entire negotiation, from the first song to
the Carnegie Hall bombshell, had been a test. She wasn't just assessing
his offer; she had been assessing his character. She had offered him a
deal that was entirely one-sided, a deal no sane businessman would ever
pitch to his board, just to see if he was the kind of man who would
agree to it. To see if his heart was in the right place.

And he had passed.

He finally met her gaze, his expression one of complete, utter defeat
and absolute reverence. She wasn't just a musician. She was the wisest
person he had ever met.

"All right, Mary," he said, his voice husky with emotion. He stood up
and straightened his tie, feeling as though he were re-aligning his
entire worldview. "50/50. My half for Vanguard, your half for the
church. In perpetuity." He extended his hand again, for the third and
final time. "Is *that* the deal?"

Mary's smile widened. She reached out and shook his hand firmly.

"That seems fair," she said. She then turned back to the Baldwin, her
business finally, truly concluded. Her hands settled on the keys.

"Now," she said, her voice bright with purpose as she played a clear,
ringing C-major chord. "About that piano tuner..."

This is the inevitable collision. The pure, simple deal made in the
sanctuary crashing against the cynical wall of the real world. This
scene is fantastic.
