## Chapter 15

Adrian met Mary near the west entrance, away from the lingering, buzzing
crowd. She was standing by a potted ficus tree, clutching her tote bag,
looking shaken and a little overwhelmed by the spontaneous applause.

"Well done, Mary," Adrian said, his voice brimming with a relief so
profound it felt like he could float. "Well done."

"It's just Church Music," was her automatic reply, a familiar shield
against the unfamiliar feeling of public praise.

"No, Mary," Adrian said, placing a gentle hand on her shoulder to stop
her. He met her eyes, his own gaze full of a newfound reverence. "That
was Church Music, yes. But there is no 'just' in it. Not anymore. Not
after that."

He took a deep breath, the adrenaline of the last ten minutes slowly
beginning to recede, replaced by the cold reality of the next step. "He
knew, Mary. Arthur Pendleton. He knew it was a setup."

A flicker of alarm crossed her face. "How?"

Adrian shook his head. "I have no idea how he knew. Did Julian tell him?
Do they have spies? It doesn't matter. What matters is what he said."
Adrian leaned in, his voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper. "He
wants you to play for the full board. Next week."

The reality of it hit her. This was it. The formal audition. The real
gatekeepers. Her expression was a mixture of quiet fear and stoic
resolve.

"Now," Adrian said, switching into producer mode, his mind already
strategizing. "I want you to play the piece you played for me in the
church. The one you rearranged 'on the fly'." He made the air quotes,
just as she had. "'Reflections in a Shard of Glass'."

Mary nodded slowly. That piece felt like an old friend now, a difficult
one, but one she understood.

"That will be your first piece," Adrian continued, mapping out the
audition. "It will show them your artistry, your unique voice. But the
second piece... the second piece will be from them."

He saw the question in her eyes. "I don't know much about it," he
admitted. "But I can tell you it won't be easy. It will be a
sight-reading test. Something obscure, something technically brutal.
They will be testing your skill, your training, your nerve."

He looked at her, the full weight of the question hanging in the air
between them. The noise of the mall, the future of the church, the
impossible deal they had struck---it all came down to this moment.

"Do you think you can do it?"

Mary didn't answer right away. She turned her gaze from Adrian to the
shoppers passing by, their faces a blur of transient purpose. She
thought of the cold, jealous faces of the children at the recitals. She
thought of the warm, kind faces of the congregation at First
Presbyterian. She thought of the worn, familiar keys of her mother's
spinet. She thought of the sleek, responsive keys of the Fazioli she had
just played, an instrument that sang back to her.

She sat in contemplation for a long, quiet time. Adrian didn't rush her.
He just waited.

Finally, she turned back to him, her expression calm and centered.

"I know I can play the first piece," she said, her voice steady. "I will
play it as I played it for you. The way I hear it."

She paused, considering the second, more terrifying challenge. "The
unknown piece?" She took a small, quiet breath. "I cannot promise I will
play it perfectly. I cannot promise I will play it the way they expect."

She met Adrian's anxious gaze with a look of profound, unshakable
honesty.

"All I can say is that I will just play it the only way I know how."
