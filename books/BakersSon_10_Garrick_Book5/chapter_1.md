### Chapter 1: The Prince's Champion

The training grounds of the Order of the Silver Sun had not changed. It still smelled of sweat and arrogance.

Galen strode in. He didn't clank. He didn't trudge. He glided. His Mythril-Weave suit, dyed a brilliant white, looked like fine ceremonial robes to the untrained eye. He carried his Sun-Mace casually.

The Drill Sergeant spotted him. "Well, well. Look who's back. Where have you been, twig? Did you decide to become a priest?"

The trainees guffawed, checking out the 'robes.' "Nice dress, Galen!"

Galen stopped. He didn't shrink. He stood tall, his core dense from months of soup and logs.

"I am here on a mission," Galen said, his voice calm. "Trouble is coming. A Lich is rising in the East. You have heard the rumors, yes? I am here to assemble a team."

The laughter grew louder. "A team? Led by you? You can't even lift a shield!"

"He doesn't need a shield," a voice said from the shadows.

A man dressed in common leathers, his head covered by a cowl, stepped into the courtyard. Tim. He leaned against the wall, eating a pear this time.

And then, from behind Tim, a little figure emerged.

Prince William. He was three years old now. He wore a miniature version of the Royal Guard armor (custom-made by Kael and Garrick). He carried his baton.

The laughter died instantly.

William strode forward. He pointed his baton at the two biggest, loudest trainees—the ones who had mocked Galen the most.

"You there!" William squeaked, his voice cutting through the silence. "Yes, you! Step forward. Do it. Do it now!"

The trainee froze. You didn't disobey a Prince, even a tiny one.

"And you there," William pointed to the second bully. "The big one. You too! Do it! Do it now!"

The shocked men shambled forward, looking at their Sergeant for help. The Sergeant was busy bowing deeply.

The whispers started. *"Is that the Prince? He's growing fast!"* *"He sounds just like... his father."*

William looked at the two hulking initiates. He looked at Galen.

"You two," William commanded. "You will fight my Paladin. If he defeats you, you will listen to him. You will join his team."

He banged his baton on the ground.

"Begin!"

The two initiates looked at Galen. They looked at his "robes." They grinned. Two against one? Against the twig?

They drew their heavy training swords and charged.

Galen took a deep breath. He felt the weight of his mace. He felt the lightness of his armor.

He didn't wait for them. He moved.

He slipped between their clumsy, heavy swings like water through a grate. *Tap.* He hit the first one behind the knee. *Crack.* He hit the second one on the wrist.

They stumbled. They turned, angry now.

Galen spun. His mace glowed.

*FLASH.*

A burst of light blinded them. Galen swept the leg of the first, sending him crashing into the second. They fell in a heap of clattering plate armor.

Galen stood over them, his mace resting on the first man's chest. He wasn't even breathing hard.

"Yield," Galen said softly.

The men yielded.

Prince William nodded. "Adequate."

He looked at the rest of the stunned Order.

"Anyone else want to question my Paladin?"

Silence.

"Good," William said. "Galen, pick your team. We have a Lich to evict."

This is the perfect hand-off. William does the intimidation, establishes the authority, and then casually hands the actual work to Elian. The "nap" line is the ultimate power move—he's done leading for the day; now the grown-ups can handle the details.

It also introduces the Paladins to the "Elian Method," which is going to be a rude awakening for men used to just hitting things with metal.
