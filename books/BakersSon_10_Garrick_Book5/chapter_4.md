## Chapter 4: The School of the Silver Wind

The sun rose on a glittering line of warriors. The Paladins were no longer clanking tanks of iron. They were statues of woven starlight. The Mythril-Weave suits fit them perfectly, highlighting muscles built by carrying logs and fueled by magic soup. They looked... dangerous.

"Where's Prince William?" one of them asked, looking around nervously for the tiny tyrant.

"Play date," Galen said, adjusting his own white suit. "Or so I heard. He has a diplomatic meeting with a sandbox."

Galen stood before them. He wasn't the "twig" anymore. He was the prototype.

"Now... listen up," Galen said. "We will now start over. You were trained to be walls. But walls don't move. And the Lich won't wait for you to brace yourselves. You will 'relearn' how to fight, now that you can actually move."

He turned to the gate.

"Lord Valerius, if you please. We are yours to command."

Valerius strode in. He wore his own dueling leathers, his twin sabers at his hips. He looked at the shining line of Paladins. He grinned.

"Gentlemen," Valerius said. "You look magnificent. Shiny. Distracting. Perfect."

He drew his blades.

"Today, we learn the 'School of the Silver Wind'. It is a style born of wood, refined by steel, and perfected by a street rat."

He pointed a saber at them.

"Attack me," Valerius said. "All of you. At once."

The Paladins hesitated. Ten against one?

"Do it!" Galen barked.

They charged. But this time, they didn't lumber. They sprinted. The suits moved with them. They were fast.

Valerius laughed. He spun. He didn't block; he flowed. But the Paladins, lighter and faster, were able to turn with him. They weren't clumsy anymore. They were beginning to see the grain.

For the next week, Valerius drilled them. He taught them the circular parry. The redirect. The "soft" step.

And every night, they drank Bessie's milk and ate Oren's soup.

By the end of the month, they weren't Paladins. They were something new.

They were **Light-Dancers**.

And the Lich King had no idea what was coming for him.

This is the ultimate test. The "Kindergarten Commander" is on the field. Bringing William to the battle is risky, but it raises the stakes astronomically. The Paladins aren't just fighting for duty; they are fighting to protect a *child* who also happens to be their boss.

The setting—an ancient mausoleum—is perfect for a Lich. And William's command? It's not just an order; it's a taunt to the enemy. He's presenting himself as bait.
