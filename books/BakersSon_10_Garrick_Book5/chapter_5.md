## Chapter 5: The Bait

The Mausoleum of the Whispering Dead lay in a valley of gray fog. It was vast, an ancient city of the dead that had stood for centuries.

It had a new keeper. And the keeper had minions. Lots and lots of minions.

The Army of Wonders stood on the ridge. This wasn't the massive host that had fought the Hoard. It was a surgical strike team. The Paladin Fist. Valerius. Elian. Finn.

And Prince William.

His mother, the Queen, had let him go, much to her own chagrin. She had a weird feeling that he was growing into something special, something that needed to face the darkness to understand the light. She tried not to worry. Elian was with him. As was Finn. He would be fine.

William sat upon his 'Uncle' Finn's shoulders so he could see better.

"Before you lies the rabble," William announced, his voice carrying clearly in the dead air. "You must defeat them for the Lich to show himself. Or so says Uncle Finn."

He tapped Finn's shoulder. "Down, please."

Finn knelt, and William hopped off. He smoothed his miniature armor. He checked his baton.

Then, William strode forward. He walked past the front line of the Paladins. He walked until he was standing alone in the no-man's-land between the living and the dead.

The Paladins tensed. Galen took a step forward, but Elian held him back. "Wait," Elian said. "He has a plan."

William turned his back on the undead army. He looked at his Fists.

"You will protect me!" William shouted, his toddler voice fierce and commanding. "You will protect your Prince, or you will die trying! Do it! Do it now!"

It was a challenge. To them. To the enemy.

The undead heard the voice of a child. Life. Vibrant, unprotected life.

A groan rose from the mausoleum. The ground churned. Skeletons, zombies, and ghouls poured forth, a tide of rot hungry for the small, bright light standing alone in the field.

They charged.

William didn't flinch. He stood his ground, tapping his baton against his leg.

"Okay, boys," Galen whispered, raising his Sun-Mace. "You heard the boss."

The Paladins moved. They didn't form a wall. They formed a storm.

They surged past William, a blur of silver light. They met the undead wave not with a crash, but with a dance. They spun, they struck, they flowed.

William stood in the eye of the hurricane, watching his army work.

"Sloppy on the left!" he critiqued, pointing his baton at a Paladin who missed a parry. "More wrist!"

Elian watched from the ridge, shaking his head. "He's terrifying."

"He's perfect," Finn smiled. "Now... let's see if the Lich takes the bait."

That's the perfect hidden safety net. It adds a layer of reassurance for the reader while still maintaining the tension for the characters in the battle. Elian's nervousness shows his fatherly side, and Finn's quiet confidence is a great character beat.

And the idea of William being *disappointed* that he's magically protected is hilarious and completely in character.
