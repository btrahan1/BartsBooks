## Chapter 6: The Bubble of Annoyance

The Dance of Death met the Shuffling Horde, and it was a massacre. The Light-Dancers were a whirlwind of silver and gold. Their maces flashed, turning bone to dust. Their movements were a fluid, synchronized ballet of destruction.

But the undead were a tide. For every skeleton they shattered, two more crawled from the earth.

Elian watched from the ridge, his hand gripping his staff. The strategy was working, but the sheer numbers were overwhelming. He watched a ghoul get a little too close to the "bait."

He leaned over to Finn, his voice a nervous whisper. "Jasmine put that protection spell on him, right? The little leaf thing?"

Finn chuckled, not taking his eyes off the battle. "The Ward of the Green? Of course. Upgraded, in fact."

He lowered his voice. "If an undead gets within six feet of him, it will feel a sudden, overwhelming urge to be somewhere else. If it pushes through that... it will turn to ash."

Elian let out a breath he didn't know he was holding. "Good."

Finn smiled. "But let's not tell the Paladins that. Their desperation adds a certain... edge... to their performance. Nor William. He would be very disappointed to learn he has a safety net. He believes he is commanding through sheer force of will."

Down on the field, William was in his element. He strode behind the line of battle, his baton a conductor's wand.

"You! Ser Borin! Your flank is weak! It has the structural integrity of wet parchment! Fix it!"

"More light!" he commanded. "Less hitting! Let the maces do the work! You are channels, not hammers!"

The Paladins, driven by a mixture of terror for their Prince's safety and terror of his post-battle critique, fought with a ferocity they had never known.

A skeletal archer on a distant ridge took aim. It loosed an arrow, a black streak of ancient yew aimed directly at the small, shouting commander.

"INCOMING!" Galen screamed, and dove.

He tackled William, shielding the Prince with his own body as the arrow hissed over their heads.

They landed in a heap.

William pushed Galen off. He was indignant. "I saw it! My bubble would have stopped it!"

"Your what?" Galen asked, confused.

"My bubble of annoyance!" William declared. "They get close, and they get... annoyed. And then they leave. It's very effective."

He didn't know it was a divine ward. He just thought he was really, really good at making monsters feel uncomfortable.

He stood and dusted off his armor. He looked at Galen. "Good instincts, though. You get a pastry. Later."

He turned his attention back to the battle. The first wave was thinning.

"Alright!" William shouted. "That's enough practice! Time for the boss!"

He looked toward the dark maw of the mausoleum. "Hey, Lich! Your minions are boring! Come out and play!"

From the depths of the tomb, a cold, ancient, and deeply amused laughter echoed. The real fight was about to begin.

That's a fantastic moment. It's the culmination of Galen's journey. He's not just a competent fighter; he's a leader, a commander, and he has fully embraced the "William Method" of leadership.

The smirk is the key. He's not just following orders; he's in on the joke. He's become a partner in the glorious, terrifying absurdity of it all.
