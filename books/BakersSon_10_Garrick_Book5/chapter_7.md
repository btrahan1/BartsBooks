## Chapter 7: The Cyclone

The laughter of the Lich echoed across the battlefield, a sound that leeched the warmth from the air. The remaining undead foot soldiers crumbled to dust, their purpose served. The field was clear.

From the grand entrance of the mausoleum, the Lich King emerged. He was a figure of withered majesty, clad in robes of black and silver, a crown of jagged obsidian on his skeletal brow. In one hand, he held a staff topped with a screaming, soul-trapped gem.

"A child," the Lich King's voice was a dry, sibilant whisper that slithered into every mind. "You send a child to command your holy warriors. How... quaint."

He raised his staff. The ground trembled. The bones of the fallen began to knit back together, forming larger, more monstrous constructs—Bone Golems.

William, who had been enjoying his moment of triumph, looked at the new, bigger enemies. He looked at the Lich. He looked at his Paladins.

"Okay," William said, his voice now a little less commanding and a little more like a three-year-old who has just seen a very big spider. "New plan. Galen, you're in charge."

He then turned and ran, not in fear, but in a tactical retreat, to stand behind Elian's legs. "Daddy, he's scary."

Galen grinned. It was his turn.

He strode forward, his Sun-Mace glowing with a steady, defiant light. "You heard him, boys. I'm in charge."

He looked at the assembling Bone Golems. They were big. They were slow. They were made of brittle material.

"Formation Two!" Galen commanded, his voice ringing with a newfound authority he had earned in a hundred training spars. "On me! The Cyclone!"

Ten Paladins in their shimmering Mythril-Weave suits moved instantly. They didn't form a line. They formed a tight, circular vortex around Galen.

Galen smirked a bit as he looked at the Prince, who was peeking out from behind Elian's leg. He gave the small commander a respectful, conspiratorial nod.

He raised his mace. "Do it! Do it now!"

The Cyclone began to move. It was a spinning, whirling storm of silver light and golden maces. They rotated, a living hurricane of holy power, each man protecting the back of the man in front of him.

They met the first Bone Golem. They didn't smash it. They *ground* it. The spinning maces struck the Golem's legs, its torso, its arms, in a continuous, percussive assault. The holy resonance, combined with the sheer, relentless kinetic force, was too much. The Golem didn't just break; it disintegrated into a cloud of bone dust.

The Cyclone rolled on, a beautiful, unstoppable engine of destruction, carving a path directly towards the Lich King.

The Lich, for the first time in a thousand years, looked surprised. He had expected a shield wall. He had gotten a blender.

This is the perfect blend of high-stakes strategy and childlike simplicity. William treats locating the Lich's phylactery (the "Factory thing") with the same gravity as acquiring snacks—both are essential components of a successful operation.

It's a fantastic scene that shows how the team has adapted to their tiny commander, and how William himself has a strange, intuitive grasp of the big picture.
