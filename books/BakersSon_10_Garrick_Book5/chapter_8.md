## Chapter 8: The Factory Thing

The Cyclone was a beautiful, terrible machine. It rolled through the Lich King's Golem honor guard, turning them to dust with a relentless, grinding efficiency. The Lich, realizing his constructs were useless against this new, strange formation, changed tactics.

He raised his staff and chanted. A wave of black energy, a curse of pure, soul-chilling despair, washed over the battlefield.

But it broke against the Cyclone like water on rock. The Paladins, their minds fortified by Elian's lessons in balance and their bodies fueled by Oren's life-giving soup, were not just physically strong; they were spiritually centered. The despair could find no purchase.

From the ridge, Prince William watched the battle with a critical eye. He was no longer hiding. He was analyzing. He tugged on the hem of Finn's robe.

"Uncle Finn," William whispered, his voice all business. "Have you located it? That... Factory thing? The Lich's hideaway?"

Finn looked down at the small prince. He knew William meant the phylactery, the object that contained the Lich's soul and anchored him to the world.

"Yes, William," Finn whispered back, placing a calming hand on the boy's head. "We have it. Jasmine is locked on. The phylactery is not in the mausoleum. It is beneath it, in a hidden chamber. She can feel its... wrongness. We are waiting for the right moment."

William nodded, his expression grave. "Good."

He then patted Finn's leg. "I'll go get the snacks. Carry on."

He turned and trotted over to the supply wagon, where a very nervous-looking Chef Antoine was keeping a batch of "Victory Pastries" warm.

Finn smiled, shaking his head. The boy's ability to pivot from tactical oversight to snack acquisition was truly a wonder.

Down on the field, Galen felt the Lich's despair-wave wash over him. It felt like a cold, greasy film on his soul. He gritted his teeth and pushed back with the light of his own faith. The Cyclone did not falter.

They were getting closer. He could see the Lich's eyes, two points of burning, hateful red in its skeletal face.

"Now!" Galen roared.

The Cyclone broke apart. The ten Paladins exploded outward, each one a silver comet, charging the Lich from a different angle. It was a perfectly executed "Shatter-Strike," a maneuver Valerius had designed specifically for a single, powerful target.

The Lich, surrounded and overwhelmed, let out a shriek of rage. He could not block them all.

He was about to be destroyed.

That is a spectacular climax. Finn taking a backseat for most of the fight and then delivering the final, decisive blow with divine power is a perfect move for his character. And William's understated, almost bored reaction is the ultimate comedic punchline to a moment of epic, world-saving magic.
