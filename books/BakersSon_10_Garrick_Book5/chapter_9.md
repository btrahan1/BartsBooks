## Chapter 9: The Final Audit

The Paladin Fists were a blur of silver, a ten-pronged attack that the Lich King could not possibly counter. His skeletal form was about to be shattered into a thousand pieces.

But on the ridge, Finn raised his staff. He wasn't looking at the Lich. He was looking at the ground beneath the mausoleum, his eyes focused on a point no one else could see.

He started chanting. The words were not of the common tongue, but an ancient, sylvan language of life and light. The air around him grew thick with power. *Jasmine* began to glow, not with the warm light of healing, but with the cold, pure, incandescent fury of a star.

"Now, Jasmine!" Finn commanded.

A blinding light erupted from the staff. It was not a beam shot from the tip. The light shot straight *up*, tearing a hole in the gray, blighted sky.

And from that hole, a pillar of pure, divine judgment descended. It was stronger, denser, and more absolute than even the one they had used against the sea-drake.

It did not strike the Lich.

It fell from the sky and tore through the roof of the stone mausoleum behind him. It punched through the earth, through a hundred feet of solid rock, and struck the hidden chamber below.

There was no explosion. There was only a profound, earth-shattering *CRACK* of something ancient and evil being unmade.

"NO!" wailed the Lich King.

He felt it. The severing. His anchor to the mortal world, his carefully crafted phylactery, had been annihilated in an instant. His borrowed immortality was gone. The power that sustained his undead form vanished.

He began to crumble. His obsidian crown clattered to the ground. His robes of shadow dissolved. His bones, ancient and brittle, turned to dust, scattered by a wind no one else could feel.

In the space of a heartbeat, the Lich King was gone.

The ten Paladins skidded to a halt in the empty space, their maces raised against a foe that no longer existed.

From the supply wagon, Prince William watched the pillar of divine light vanish. He had a half-eaten honey-cake in his hand.

"Pretty," he stated, matter-of-factly. He then took another bite.

The battle was over. The silence on the field was absolute.

Galen looked at the spot where the Lich had been. He looked at the heavens. He looked at his men.

He raised his Sun-Mace. "Adequate," he said, with a grin.

The Paladins roared with laughter and triumph. The war was won. And it was, all agreed, time for snacks.

That's the perfect epilogue. It brings the epic, world-saving adventure back to its most essential, human core: a little boy telling his mom a bedtime story.

William's narration is the ideal blend of a child's enthusiastic hyperbole ("boom... bang!") and an adult's strategic understanding ("the Factory"). And the final line, "I saved a piece for you, Mommy," is just heartwarming perfection.
