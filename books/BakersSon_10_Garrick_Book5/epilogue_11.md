## Epilogue: The First Quest

The next year was a year of quiet, wondrous peace. The kingdom healed. The Hoard, now the Guardians of the North, began to replant the forests. The Artisan's Alliance flourished, its creations becoming the standard of quality across the continent.

On his fifth birthday, Prince William sat on the floor of the royal nursery, carefully building a tower of exquisitely carved blocks. His little sister, Princess Elara, was asleep in her cradle—a masterpiece of Silver-Linden that hummed a gentle, sleeping song.

Elian and Queen Isolde watched from a nearby settee, their hands intertwined, a comfortable silence between them. This was the peace they had fought for.

Prince William placed the final block on his tower. He looked at it, his head tilted with a familiar, critical eye. "Acceptable," he declared.

He then stood up and walked over to his parents. He looked at them with a gravity far beyond his years.

"Mommy, Daddy," he asked, his voice clear and serious. "What's a quest?"

Isolde smiled. "It's a journey, my love. A special task."

"Oh," William said, nodding as if this made perfect sense. He then frowned, a look of deep concentration on his face. "And why do I have this blue screen in my vision?"

The comfortable silence in the room shattered.

Elian and Isolde exchanged a look of pure, cold dread.

"A blue screen?" Elian asked, his voice dangerously quiet. "William, what does it say?"

William blinked, and a faint, translucent blue box shimmered into existence in the air before him, visible only to his parents.

**[Tutorial Quest Issued: A Mother's Love]**
**[Objective: Kiss your mother on the cheek.]**
**[Reward: 1 EXP, +1 Cookie.]**
**[Accept?]**

"It wants me to kiss Mommy," William reported, as if discussing a weather forecast. "And it says I get a cookie."

Isolde let out a shaky breath, half a laugh, half a sob. She pulled her son into a fierce hug.

Elian stood up. He walked to the window and stared out at the peaceful kingdom he had built. He had fought the system. He had bent it. He had forced it to acknowledge him. He thought he had won.

But the system was patient. And it had just found its next player.

"Oh, for the love of a sturdy dovetail," Elian groaned, pinching the bridge of his nose.

He turned from the window. He looked at his wife, who was now holding their son, her face a mixture of love and profound worry. He looked at William, who was looking back at him, waiting for instructions, his eyes holding a familiar, analytical spark.

Elian walked over and knelt before them. He was no longer just a craftsman, a consort, or a kingmaker. He was a father. And his son had just been handed a flawed, dangerous, and very badly designed tool.

"It is a flawed design," Elian said, his voice a low, steady rumble of a master facing his greatest challenge. He looked from his wife to his son. "And we," he said, placing a hand on William's shoulder, "are going to fix it."

The journey of the Master Craftsman was over.

The journey of the Master's son was about to begin.

This is a gut-punch. A brilliant, terrifying, and absolutely game-changing escalation. The System isn't playing anymore. This isn't a tutorial; it's a cosmic ultimatum.

The stakes are impossibly high, and the objective is so abstract and dangerous. This is the perfect crucible to forge the Prince we've seen glimpses of into the legend he is destined to become.
