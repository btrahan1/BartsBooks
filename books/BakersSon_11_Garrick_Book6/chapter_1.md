### Chapter 1: The Envoy

The carriage was a simple, unassuming thing, bearing no royal crest. It traveled along the dusty road that marked the ill-defined border of the Kingdom of Asgar.

It was a trap, and they were the bait.

Right on schedule, a column of cavalry appeared over the rise. They were not a royal guard. They were hard-faced men in mismatched black armor, their horses lean and mean. They thundered down the road and surrounded the carriage, their swords drawn.

The carriage door opened. Three figures stepped out. An old man in a simple robe, leaning on a cloth-wrapped walking stick. A young man in a similar robe, also holding a plain staff. And a child.

The Prince and his 'guard.'

The Asgarian captain, a scarred man with a cruel smirk, dismounted. This was too easy.

But Prince William did not cower. He did not cry. He strode forward confidently, his small hands clasped behind his back, and stopped ten feet from the captain. He tapped his foot impatiently.

"It's about time you got here," William said, his voice the clear, annoyed tone of a master addressing a tardy apprentice. "I assume you are our envoy?"

The captain's smirk vanished, replaced by a look of profound confusion. He looked from the small, imperious child to the two unarmed men behind him.

"Just the three of you?" the captain asked, his hand tightening on his sword. "And who, might I ask, are *you*?"

William drew himself up to his full height. His eyes, his father's eyes, flashed with a fire that was utterly out of place on a child's face.

"Who am I?" he asked, his voice rising, a perfect mimicry of a nobleman's outrage. "**WHO AM I!**"

He jabbed a small finger at his own chest. "I am Prince William, first son of the Greatest Queen this World has ever known! And," his voice dropped to a conspiratorial, but no less powerful, whisper, "also the son of a *GOD*!"

He then pointed that same finger at the stunned, speechless captain.

"Who," the six-year-old Prince demanded, "are *YOU*?"

The captain stared. He had come to kidnap a prince. He was being interrogated by a tiny, terrifying deity who was complaining about his punctuality.

Behind the Prince, Finn and Galen exchanged a look. Finn's was one of serene amusement. Galen's was one of pure, unadulterated pride.

The game had begun. And Prince William had just made the opening move.

That exchange is perfect. The captain's sarcasm and William's complete, literal acceptance of it create a fantastic comedic beat. William isn't playing the same game; he's operating on a level of confidence so absolute that the captain's mockery doesn't even register. And the demand for snacks? Classic William. It keeps the scene grounded in the reality that he is, after all, a six-year-old on a road trip.
