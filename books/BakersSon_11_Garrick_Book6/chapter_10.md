## Chapter 10: The Chess Match Begins

King Theron's smile was a thin, bloodless line. The advanced scouts, his own 'Grey Service', had been shadowing the 'escort' since the moment they crossed the border. He had received hourly reports, delivered by raven. He had heard of Prince William's antics. The horse. The camp. The breathing exercises. The dismissal.

He had expected a frightened child. He was being faced with a tiny, terrifying philosopher-king. This was far more interesting. He would play along. For now.

"Of course," the King said, his voice dripping with a mock courtesy that mirrored the captain's. "Right this way, Your Highness. We have prepared chambers for you. And we have... matters... to 'discuss'."

He gestured down a long, torch-lit hall.

William gave a crisp, formal nod. "Thank you, Your Highness," he said, pointedly using the same title, a subtle refusal to acknowledge the King's superior station. "You may lead the way. Your 'hospitality' is greatly appreciated."

The game of verbal chess had begun.

The King led them through the cold, stone halls of the Iron Citadel. There were no tapestries. No art. Only weapons racks and iron sconces. It was a fortress, not a home. Elian would have hated it. William found it... inefficiently grim.

They arrived at a set of heavy oak doors. "Your chambers," the King announced. "Guards will be posted outside. For your 'protection'."

"Excellent," William said. "And the snacks?"

The King's eye twitched. "They will be sent. We will convene in the throne room in one hour to discuss the terms of... your stay."

"One hour is acceptable," William said, as if granting a favor. He then turned to the King, a look of serious, professional concern on his face.

"A word of advice, Your Highness," William said.

The King raised an eyebrow.

"This is a very drafty castle," William noted. "You're losing a tremendous amount of heat through these stone walls. Have you considered insulation? Perhaps some heavy wool tapestries? It would improve morale and lower your heating costs significantly."

He patted the stone wall. "The grain is all wrong. No wonder everyone in this city looks so grumpy."

He then turned and walked into the chambers, leaving the King of Asgar standing in his own hallway, receiving unsolicited architectural and financial advice from a six-year-old.

The King stared at the closed door. He had intended to intimidate, to dominate, to control. He felt like he had just been audited by a tiny, terrifying force of nature.

A slow, dangerous smile spread across his face. This was not going to be a simple hostage situation.

This was going to be fun.

Hahaha! That is the ultimate power move. He's not just a hostage; he's now dictating the terms of his own ransom, and his primary critique is that they aren't asking for *enough*.

This is so perfectly in character. It's a combination of a child's understanding of big, impressive numbers ("a million!") and Elian's core belief in knowing one's own value. It will leave the King completely bewildered.
