## Chapter 11: The Counter-Offer

One hour later, Prince William strode into the throne room. Finn and Galen followed, taking up positions by the door. The chamber was a cavern of black iron and cold stone. King Theron sat on a throne of jagged, unpolished metal.

"Welcome, Prince," the King said. "I trust the... rations... were to your liking?"

"The hardtack was structurally unsound," William replied, climbing onto a large, cushioned chair that had clearly been brought in for him. It was the only comfortable-looking thing in the room. "But the water was wet. Adequate."

He folded his hands in his lap. "Let us proceed to business. I assume you have a ransom note prepared. Show me."

The King was taken aback by the directness. He smirked and nodded to his chancellor, a thin, weaselly man who scurried forward with a scroll.

William took the scroll. He read it, his small face a mask of concentration.

"Release of all border claims... ten years' worth of grain tribute... and..." His eyes narrowed. "Ten thousand gold?"

He looked up, his expression one of pure, unadulterated outrage.

"Ten thousand GOLD?" William's voice squeaked with disbelief. "**TEN THOUSAND GOLD!**"

He stood up on the chair, trembling with what appeared to be righteous fury.

"Is this a JOKE?" he demanded. "Do you have any idea of the asset you have acquired? I am Prince William! Heir to the Kingdom of Wonders! Son of the Queen of Light and the CRAFTING GOD! And you are valuing me at a mere ten thousand gold? It is an insult to my House! It is a disgrace to the very concept of kidnapping!"

The King and his chancellor just stared, speechless.

"Give me that quill," William commanded.

The chancellor, mesmerized, handed him the quill.

William dipped it in the inkpot. He scratched out the paltry figure of 10,000 gold. In its place, in large, slightly clumsy letters, he wrote:

**1,000,000 GOLD**

He then added, for dramatic effect: **...OR THE PRINCE DIES!** (He added a small, smiling face next to this line).

He blew on the ink to dry it, then handed the scroll back to the stunned chancellor.

"There," William said, sitting back down with a satisfied huff. "That is a figure that reflects my proper market value. Much better. It shows you are taking this negotiation seriously."

He smoothed his tunic. "Send it at once. My mother will appreciate the improved offer."

He then looked at the King, a look of pleasant expectation on his face.

"Now," he said. "About those snacks?"

This is a masterclass in psychological warfare. William isn't just a hostage; he's now the kingdom's unwelcome, and terrifyingly competent, financial advisor. He's not negotiating his release; he's lecturing the King on basic economics.

The slow, horrifying dawning of realization on Theron's face is perfect. He's been completely outmaneuvered, his "kidnapping" turned into a long-term, inconvenient, and deeply educational houseguest situation.
