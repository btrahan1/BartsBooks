## Chapter 12: The Economic Lesson

The ransom note, in all its absurd glory, was sent. King Theron sat on his iron throne, a goblet of wine in his hand, watching the small prince who was now humming to himself and examining the weave of a nearby (and very ugly) tapestry.

This was not going according to plan.

Prince William sighed heavily, a sound of profound, world-weary disappointment. He turned from the tapestry and looked directly at the King.

"Theron," he said, his tone that of a patient but frustrated teacher. "Do you mind if I call you Theron? You may call me William, if you please. We're going to be roommates, of sorts. Formality is inefficient."

King Theron, who had just taken a large gulp of wine, choked. He sputtered, wine dribbling down his chin. "Roommates?"

"Well, yes," William said. "The quest says a year. So, if I'm going to be here for a year, some things have got to change."

"A year?" Theron croaked, his voice an octave higher than usual. The plan had been to get the gold in a month and send the boy packing.

"Of course, a year!" William said, climbing back onto his chair as if it were a lecturer's podium. "Do you think my mother has one million gold crowns just lying around in a vault? What kind of incompetent idiot would do that?"

He looked at the King with genuine, pitying concern. "Theron, do you even know how an economy works? You have to 'circulate' the coin. You invest it. You build things. Coin makes coin. But coin doesn't make coin just sitting in a vault! What kind of fool would do that?"

'Theron', as he was now known, swallowed hard. The Asgarian Royal Treasury was, in fact, a massive vault filled with uncirculated gold.

William continued his lecture, now ticking points off on his small fingers. "My mother will need to issue a levy. A collection will be made across the entire kingdom. Debts will be called in. New trade tariffs will be implemented. Money will be borrowed from the Iron Bank of the Free Cities! That takes time, Theron! Financial quarters! Logistical planning!"

He leaned back, his lesson complete. "You'll be lucky to see the first payment in six months. The full sum will take at least a year. It's just basic economics."

Theron grimaced.

He had been played. By a six-year-old.

He had thought he was kidnapping a prince to hold a kingdom hostage. Instead, he had just accidentally signed up for a year-long financial seminar, hosted by a tiny, terrifying tyrant who was now on a first-name basis with him. And he had trapped himself. He couldn't just kill the boy now; the ransom note had been sent. To go back on it would be a sign of weakness.

He was stuck. With a hostage who was critiquing his monetary policy.

William, seeing the look of dawning horror on the King's face, patted the armrest of his chair comfortingly.

"Don't worry, Theron," the Prince said, his voice now gentle. "We have a whole year. I can teach you. We'll start with infrastructure." He looked around the grim, cold throne room. "This place has terrible insulation. We can fix that."

This is the perfect next step in William's friendly takeover. He's not even pretending to be a prisoner anymore. He's a consultant, a city planner, and he's requisitioning the King's own men for his "inspections."

The "Freudian slip" is a brilliant touch of sophisticated humor, showing that William isn't just mimicking adults; he understands the power of words. And the final line is the ultimate flex—he's using his own value as a hostage as leverage to get whatever he wants.
