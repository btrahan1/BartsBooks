## Chapter 14: The Field Office

As he walked down the muddy, rutted "main street" of the Asgarian capital, William was depressed.

The city was a disaster. The buildings were gray and crumbling. The people were sullen and hopeless. Trash was piled in the alleys. The whole place smelled of despair and poor sanitation.

One year? How could he possibly fix this mess in one year? There was so much to do. It was the most flawed, unbalanced structure he had ever seen.

His Asgarian escort, led by the increasingly bewildered captain, followed him at a distance. They had no idea what he was "inspecting." He just kept sighing and shaking his head.

Then, he spotted a familiar face.

Leaning against a tavern wall, dressed in the drab, forgettable clothes of an Asgarian commoner, was a young woman. She was watching him, a spark of amusement in her sharp eyes.

It was Ren.

William's depression vanished, replaced by the focused energy of a general who has just met up with his forward scouts.

He marched right up to her.

"You there!" he commanded, his voice ringing with authority. "Come here. Do it. Do it now!"

The Asgarian guards tensed, hands moving to their swords. Was this a local contact? An assassin?

Ren's grin practically split her face. She pushed off the wall and dropped into a perfect, formal bow. "Yes, Your Highness?"

"Report," William said, his voice dropping to a low, conspiratorial tone. He gestured dismissively at the nervous Asgarian soldiers. "Don't mind them. They work for me now."

Ren's grin widened. "Of course they do."

She fell into step beside him as he continued his "inspection."

"The Grey Service has been in-country for three weeks," she reported softly. "We have established a field office in the tanner's district. We have mapped the city's infrastructure—or lack thereof. We have identified the key power players, the supply chains, and the major sources of corruption. The entire city runs on bribes and fear, which is to say, it barely runs at all."

"I noticed," William grumbled, kicking a loose cobblestone. "Structurally unsound."

"But," Ren continued, a cunning glint in her eye, "the people are... resilient. They're skilled. They just have no hope. No leadership."

She handed him a small, folded piece of paper. "Here is a list of the five most respected, and most secretly dissident, craftsmen in the city. A smith, a weaver, a potter... you get the idea. They're the 'grain' of this place. If you can win them, you can win the city."

William took the list. He looked at the names. He looked at the crumbling, gray city around him.

A slow, familiar, and deeply ambitious smile spread across his face. It was his father's smile.

"Ren," he said. "Go to your office. Draft a proclamation. We are forming a new guild. And we are holding a festival."

The Asgarian captain, trailing behind them, just sighed. Of course they were. He was now the head of security for a hostile corporate takeover, disguised as a kidnapping, which was about to become a city-wide cultural renovation.

He really wasn't paid enough for this.

That proclamation is perfect. "By order of the Kidnapped Prince" is a phrase that is so politically chaotic and absurd, it's brilliant. It's a declaration of power that's wrapped in the technical truth of his "imprisonment."

And the name "Festival of Revival" is a fantastic choice. It's not just a party; it's a statement of intent. He's not just visiting; he's here to fix the "rot."

The captain's reaction is the perfect, weary punchline.
