## Chapter 16: The Homework

The main guild hall of Asgar was a place of dust and despair. The tools were rusty, the workbenches were scarred, and the five master craftsmen who had been summoned there stood with the slumped shoulders of men who had forgotten what pride felt like.

Prince William stood before them. Finn and Galen stood behind him, silent guardians.

"My father is a woodworker," William began, his voice quiet but carrying a strange weight in the cavernous hall. "He once told me that a city has a soul. That it has a heartbeat. And he was right."

He looked from one grim face to the next. "This city has a soul, but it is wounded. This city has a heartbeat, but it is that of a dying old man."

He began to pace, his small steps echoing in the silence. "A few years ago, my own capital was... unbalanced. The craft was dying. The soul was weak. My father took the city on his shoulders, and with the strength of his hands and the beauty of his craft, he transformed it."

He stopped and looked at them. "And it started in a room just like this. With the guild masters."

He leaned in, his eyes burning with an intensity that made the hardened craftsmen take a step back. "He gave them homework. Which I am giving to you now."

He pointed a finger at them. "Go. Return to your workshops. You are to make the first piece you ever made as an apprentice. A clumsy knife. A lopsided pot. A frayed leather strap. I want you to make it exactly as it was, with all its flaws."

He let the command sink in.

"Then," he continued, his voice dropping, becoming a powerful, persuasive current, "you will make the piece you *wish* you had made. The masterpiece that lived in your heart, even as your clumsy hands failed you. You will pour your years of skill, your frustration, your forgotten hope, into it. Give it a story. Give it *your* story!"

He clapped his hands, the sound like a gunshot in the silent hall.

"Be prepared to present your work here, tomorrow at this time. We will see if the soul of Asgar is truly dead, or if it is merely sleeping."

He turned, a final command on his lips. "Go now. Do it! Do it now!"

The five masters stood frozen for a moment, stunned by the sheer, audacious hope of the child before them. Then, a flicker of something long-dormant—a spark of pride, of a forgotten dream—ignited in the eyes of the master smith.

He turned without a word and strode from the hall. The others followed, a new, hesitant purpose in their steps. They had been given not just an order, but a challenge. A chance to remember who they were.

Finn smiled. "Like father, like son," he whispered.

"No," Galen corrected softly, watching the masters depart. "The father is a craftsman who became a king. This one... this one was born a king who just happens to understand the craft."

That line is perfect. It's a fantastic blend of William's learned authority and a child's unique way of offering a reward. The ultimate prize isn't gold or favor; it's a personal recommendation to his "God-like" father. It's a charming and incredibly effective motivator.
