## Chapter 18: The Story of the Smith

The Master Smith, a man whose hands were a testament to a lifetime of wrestling with fire and iron, stepped forward. He was visibly trembling, not with fear, but with a raw, powerful emotion.

He looked not at the Prince, but at his own two creations on the table.

"Yes, Your Highness," he said, his voice a low, gravelly rumble. He swallowed, the sound loud in the silent hall.

He didn't just begin his presentation. He made a plea.

"Please," he said, his gaze sweeping over the small audience. "Hear my story. Hear my pain. And hear my hopes and dreams."

He unwrapped the first object. It was a single, perfect, and utterly soulless nail. "My master was a blacksmith for the old king's army," the smith began. "He taught me that perfection was uniformity. A thousand nails, all alike. A thousand spearheads, all identical. We were a machine. This was the first nail I ever forged that he deemed 'acceptable'."

He then unwrapped his second piece.

It was a single, wrought-iron rose.

It was not a delicate, lifelike flower. It was a thing of brutal, beautiful honesty. The petals were forged from hammered steel, their edges still sharp. The stem was a twisted bar of iron, and the thorns were wicked and real. But in the center of the bloom, nestled in the heart of the iron, was a single, tiny, perfectly formed dewdrop, crafted from polished silver.

"Last night," the smith said, his voice cracking, "I did not forge a tool. I forged a memory. Of my wife. She passed ten years ago. She loved the wild roses that grew on the hills outside the city. She was strong, and sharp, and beautiful... but she was also gentle."

He reached out a calloused, trembling finger and touched the silver dewdrop.

"This," he whispered, "is the part of my craft I buried. The part that was not 'uniform'. The part that was not 'efficient'. It is the part that remembers her."

He looked at William, and there were tears tracing paths through the soot on his old cheeks.

"My hope," the smith said, his voice thick, "is to make things that are not just strong, but also beautiful. My dream... is to build a gate for the city, adorned not with spikes, but with a thousand of these roses. A gate that does not say 'keep out', but 'welcome home'."

He had not just presented an object. He had confessed his soul.

William sat on his stool, his small face a mask of solemn, profound understanding. He stood, walked to the table, and gently picked up the iron rose. It was heavy. It was dangerous. It was beautiful.

"Your wife," William said softly, "must have been a very strong woman."

He then looked at the smith, his gaze direct and filled with a leader's promise.

"You will build your gate," the Prince declared. "The Festival of Revival will be its dedication."

Perfect. Absolutely perfect.

After a moment of profound emotional connection and a kingdom-altering promise, William's "Next!" is the ultimate return to form. It's the snap back to reality.

It says:
*   "I have acknowledged your soul. I have validated your dream. The emotional part is over."
*   "We are on a schedule."
*   "Show me what you've got."

It's the voice of a leader who is both deeply empathetic and ruthlessly efficient. It's a fantastic, comedic, and character-defining button on a very emotional scene. The other masters, who were probably getting ready to cry, now have to snap back to attention. It keeps the momentum driving forward.
