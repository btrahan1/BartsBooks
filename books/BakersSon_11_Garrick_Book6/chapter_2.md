## Chapter 2: The Matter of Snacks

The Asgarian captain stared at the tiny, ferocious prince, his mind struggling to reconcile the boy's imperious tone with his small stature. He had been trained for ambushes, for battles, for ruthless kidnappings. He had not been trained for a theological and political cross-examination by a six-year-old.

He had to regain control of the situation.

The captain composed himself, forcing a thin, condescending smile onto his face. "Yes... yes... we are your 'envoy'," he said, making dismissive quote marks in the air with his fingers.

William watched the gesture with a critical, appraising eye, as if judging a new, clumsy dance step.

"If you would be so kind as to accompany us," the captain continued, his tone dripping with false courtesy, "we will escort you the rest of the way to the capital."

"Very well then," William said, giving a sharp, decisive nod as if he had just approved a new military budget. "Lead on. But I hope you have snacks. The journey has been tedious, and I'm hungry."

The captain's smile faltered. He looked at his second-in-command, a brutish man named Gorn. Gorn just shrugged.

"Snacks?" the captain asked, the word feeling ridiculous in his mouth.

"Snacks," William confirmed. "Honey-cakes are acceptable. Seed-biscuits, perhaps. Nothing with nuts. My Knight," he gestured to Galen, "has a mild allergy."

Galen, who had no such allergy, simply nodded with grave seriousness.

The captain stared. This was not how a kidnapping was supposed to go. There was supposed to be screaming. There was supposed to be fear. There was supposed to be a distinct lack of dietary requests.

He finally gritted his teeth. He was the one with the swords. He was in charge.

"We have... rations," the captain growled. "Dried meat. Hardtack."

William's face fell. He looked at the captain with an expression of profound, soul-deep disappointment. It was the exact look Elian used when he saw a piece of furniture made with nails.

"Dried meat," William repeated, the words a judgment. "Unacceptable. Your hospitality is... structurally unsound."

He sighed, a long, weary sound of a ruler burdened by the incompetence of his subjects. "No matter. We will manage. But this will be noted in my official report."

He turned and marched towards the Asgarian horses, leaving the captain utterly speechless.

Finn walked past the captain, his expression serene. "He gets grumpy when his blood sugar is low," the healer advised in a quiet, helpful whisper. "I wouldn't recommend being downwind of him until after lunch."

The captain watched as the small prince was lifted onto a large warhorse. He didn't look like a prisoner. He looked like a king inspecting his new, slightly disappointing, cavalry.

This was not a kidnapping. This was an audit. And the Kingdom of Asgar, it seemed, was already failing.

This is a fantastic beat. It immediately establishes William's character in this new, hostile environment.

1.  **He Sees the Flaw:** Just like his father, his first instinct is to spot what's wrong, what's "unbalanced" in the structure of things—even in an enemy's horse.
2.  **He Commands, He Doesn't Ask:** He doesn't say, "Sir, your horse seems to be in pain." He commands a soldier to give him a tool so he can fix the problem himself. It's an act of audacious, compassionate authority.
3.  **It Disarms the Enemy:** The soldiers are prepared for a prince who is arrogant or scared. They are not prepared for a prince who is a field veterinarian. It completely wrong-foots them.

This is a great, small moment that speaks volumes about who William has become.
