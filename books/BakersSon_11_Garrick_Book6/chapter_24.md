## Chapter 24: The Final Audit of Asgar

The Festival of Revival was a staggering success. The city of Asgar, once a place of gray despair, was reborn in a week of color, flavor, and wondrous craft. The profits were immense, far exceeding anyone's wildest expectations.

In the throne room of the Iron Citadel, King Theron sat with his royal treasurer, looking at a pile of ledgers. But they were not in charge of the meeting.

Prince William sat at the head of the great table, a small cup of juice in his hand. Ren, the Director of the Grey Service, stood beside him, her face a mask of professional neutrality.

"Are those the ledgers from the festival?" William demanded, pointing a small, authoritative finger. "Show me. Show me now."

The Royal Treasurer, a man who had once balanced the budgets of entire wars, nervously pushed the master ledger across the table to a six-year-old.

William flipped through the pages. His eyes, his father's eyes, scanned the columns of numbers with a terrifying, preternatural understanding.

"Hmmm," he said finally, closing the book. He looked at the King. "Adequate."

He then looked at Ren. "You have done well. Your Service has provided excellent security and logistical support." He gestured to the ledger. "You may keep half of the net profits. For operational expenses, and for the establishment of a permanent Asgarian branch of your... enterprise."

Ren's eyes widened. Half. It was a king's ransom. "Thank you, Your Highness," she said, a genuine respect in her voice.

William nodded. "The rest," he said, turning his gaze back to King Theron, "is going into reconstruction. We will establish a 'Wonders Fund' here, just as we did in my kingdom. We will build schools. We will pave the roads. We will repair the crumbling infrastructure."

He stood up on his chair, a tiny king delivering a royal decree to the actual king.

"We have their attention now," he declared. "We have their hope. We have lots to do. We have to strike while the iron is hot!"

He then looked King Theron directly in the eye, a long, challenging, and deeply meaningful stare.

"Wouldn't you agree... Your Highness?"

King Theron looked at the boy. He looked at the smiling, dangerous woman from the Grey Service. He looked at his treasurer, who was already nodding in vigorous agreement with the Prince's financial plan.

He had been outmaneuvered. He had been out-negotiated. He had been completely and utterly conquered, not by an army, but by a festival, a philosophy, and a six-year-old financial prodigy.

A slow, weary, and deeply admiring smile spread across the King's face.

"Yes, Prince William," he said, giving a slight, formal bow from his throne. "I would agree. Let the reconstruction begin."

The surrender was not a document signed on a battlefield. It was a quiet agreement in a throne room. The quest was complete. The hostage had taken the kingdom.
