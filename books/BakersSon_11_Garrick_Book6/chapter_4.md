## Chapter 4: An Upgrade in Accommodations

They rode for hours, the sun beating down on the dusty plains of Asgar. The Asgarian soldiers were silent, their earlier confidence replaced by a state of profound, collective confusion. They were an escort, following the lead of a six-year-old who occasionally shouted out critiques of their riding posture ("You're bouncing too much! Use your core!").

As the sun began to dip towards the horizon, casting long shadows across the land, the captain finally called a stop. "We make camp here for the night," he announced.

Before the men could even dismount, a small, clear voice cut through the air.

"Halt!" Prince William commanded from his horse.

The captain gritted his teeth. "Yes, *Your Highness*?"

"It is time to set up camp," William stated, as if the idea were his own. "And I am sleepy."

He slid down from his horse and began to issue a rapid-fire series of orders, his small baton appearing in his hand like a marshal's staff.

He pointed to the brutish soldier named Gorn. "You there! You're large. You have an axe. Build a fire! A proper one, with a keyhole draft, not a smoky mess."

He pointed to two other soldiers. "You there, and you! Gather some berries. The red ones on the thorny bushes. They are not poisonous. They are tart, but they will suffice."

He then fixed his gaze on a lanky soldier with a longbow slung over his back. "And you. You with the bow. If you have any skill with that thing, you will find us some meat. A rabbit. A large bird. Something. You have one hour!"

He clapped his hands, the sound startlingly loud in the quiet evening.

"We will not be eating these so-called 'rations' tonight!" he declared, with a look of utter disdain. "Do it. Do it now!"

The soldiers stood frozen. They looked at their captain.

The captain was just staring. A six-year-old had just, in the space of thirty seconds, completely commandeered his unit and organized their evening meal. And his plan... was actually a good one. It was efficient. It was logical.

"Well?" William demanded, his hands on his hips. "Are you deaf? Or just unbalanced?"

The captain finally snapped. But he didn't snap at William. He snapped at his men.

"You heard the Prince!" he roared. "Move!"

The Asgarian soldiers, a hardened troop of kidnappers and mercenaries, scrambled to obey the commands of a child. Gorn began chopping wood with a newfound, desperate energy. The archer sprinted off into the twilight, determined to find the plumpest rabbit in the entire kingdom.

William watched them for a moment, a look of grim satisfaction on his face. He then trotted over to where Finn and Galen were setting up a small tent.

"See?" William whispered to them. "Their structure is flawed. But they are trainable."

He yawned, a wide, jaw-cracking yawn. "Wake me when the rabbit is cooked."

He crawled into the tent, leaving his "captors" to hurriedly prepare a meal fit for the tiny, terrifying king they had accidentally invited into their lives.

Of course. Dinner was merely "acceptable." His standards are impossibly high.

The announcement of "drills" is the final, perfect nail in the coffin of the Asgarians' authority. He's not just managing their camp; he's now taking over their military training. This isn't a kidnapping anymore; it's a hostile takeover of a cavalry unit, orchestrated by a child with a bedtime.
