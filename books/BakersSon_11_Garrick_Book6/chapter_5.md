## Chapter 5: The Performance Review

The rabbit, roasted over Gorn's surprisingly well-made fire, was delicious. The berries, mashed into a tart sauce by Finn, were a revelation. It was the best meal the Asgarian soldiers had eaten in months.

They ate in a strange, subdued silence, watching the small prince who sat on a log, meticulously picking the meat from a leg bone.

When he was finished, he wiped his hands on a silk handkerchief (provided by Galen) and stood. He surveyed the camp, the well-built fire, the neatly stacked gear.

"That was... acceptable," William pronounced, his tone that of a master giving a passing grade to a struggling apprentice.

The captain, who had been enjoying his first decent meal in weeks, almost choked. *Acceptable?*

William walked over to the captain's saddlebags, where the maps were stored. "From these maps," he said, not asking for permission, but simply taking them, "I assume it is approximately three more days to the capital."

The captain just nodded, too stunned to speak.

"Good," William said, handing the maps back. "That gives us time."

"Time for what, Your Highness?" the captain asked cautiously.

William looked at the soldiers, who were now watching him with a mixture of fear, awe, and a strange, dawning respect.

"In the morning," William announced, "we will do drills. I need to see what I am working with here. Your charge is clumsy. Your formations are weak. We will work on balance, on flow."

He clapped his hands, a gesture of finality.

"That is all," he said. "Good night."

He turned and marched back to his tent, leaving a company of hardened soldiers staring after him, their minds reeling. They were being kidnapped, escorted to a hostile king. And their prisoner had just scheduled them for a performance review and a mandatory training seminar in the morning.

The captain looked at his men. He looked at the scraps of the best meal they'd ever had. He looked at his horse, which was now walking without a limp for the first time in a week.

He sighed. He didn't know what was happening anymore. But he knew one thing for certain.

He was definitely going to be up early for drills. He had a feeling the Prince was not a fan of tardiness.

This is a masterful blend of all the characters William has absorbed. He has Elian's critical eye ("Tsk tsk tsk," "unacceptable"), Valerius's noble swagger ("friendly neighbors"), and the Queen's commanding presence.

The "Finn... fix him" moment is hilarious. He's using the High Healer to fix bad posture. And Finn's placebo "healing" shows he's fully committed to playing his role in this bizarre theater.
