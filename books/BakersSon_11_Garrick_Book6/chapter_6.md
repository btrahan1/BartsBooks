## Chapter 6: The Morning Audit

The sun had barely crested the horizon when a small, clear voice cut through the morning mist.

"Unacceptable!"

The Asgarian soldiers, who had been grudgingly forming a line, flinched. Prince William was already up, dressed, and in the middle of his inspection. He walked down the line, his baton tucked under his arm, his hands clasped behind his back, a perfect, miniature imitation of his father.

"Tsk tsk tsk," he said, shaking his head with profound disappointment. "It's a good thing we are 'friendly neighbors'. You wouldn't last five minutes against our Royal Guard, much less our Elites."

He paused, a flicker of a conspiratorial smile on his face. "But no matter. We are 'friends', are we not?"

He continued his walk down the line. He stopped in front of a soldier who was slumping, his shoulders hunched from a long night on the cold ground.

"You there," William commanded. "Stand up straight! Did you sleep on a rock? What is wrong with you?" He peered closer. "Ah... I see. The grain is twisted." He turned. "Finn... fix him."

Finn, who was standing nearby, stepped forward. He mumbled a few impressive-sounding words of the Old Tongue and waved his hands over the soldier's back. He did nothing, of course. It was pure theater.

But the soldier, under the combined pressure of a Prince's command and a High Healer's "spell," immediately stood straighter, his back ramrod-stiff. "Thank you, Healer!" he said, feeling instantly better.

"Next!" William continued, moving to a man whose sword was still in its scabbard. "You there! Yes, you! Your sword is a disgrace." He tapped the rusty hilt with his baton. "Rust is un-ac-cept-a-ble! Do you understand me? It is a sign of a flawed character. Tomorrow, when I see that sword, I had better be able to see my own reflection in it!"

The soldier paled and nodded vigorously. "Yes, Your Highness!"

William finished his inspection. He stood before the troop of terrified, yet strangely motivated, soldiers.

"Mount up!" he commanded. "The map says three days to the capital, but I have a revised schedule. I want to make it in two. We are behind on this project."

He pointed a finger at all of them. "But mind your horses! Ride with the rhythm of the animal, not against it! Remember! Balance!"

He turned and marched to his own horse, leaving the captain to stare in disbelief. His unit of hardened kidnappers had just been audited, given a series of arbitrary and strangely effective performance reviews, and put on a deadline.

The captain looked at his men. They were standing straighter. They looked sharper. They looked... better.

He sighed. He swung himself into his saddle.

"You heard the Prince!" he bellowed. "Let's ride! And someone get that man a whetstone!"

This is a fantastic scene. After a day of physical critique, William is now addressing their spiritual and mental "flaws." He's not just a drill sergeant; he's a tiny, demanding guru.

The imagery he uses—home, family, a newborn—is so unexpected and disarming. These hardened soldiers are being forced to confront their own humanity, their own "True Form," by a six-year-old. It's a beautiful, slightly absurd moment of group therapy.
