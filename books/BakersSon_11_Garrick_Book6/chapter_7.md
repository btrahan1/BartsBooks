## Chapter 7: The Bellows and the Breath

They made it in two days. The forced march was grueling, but under William's relentless critiques of their riding ("Less bouncing, more gliding! You look like a sack of turnips!"), they arrived at the outskirts of the Asgarian capital faster than the captain had ever made the journey.

They set up their final camp in a scrubland a few miles from the city walls. The mood was tense. Tomorrow, they would deliver their "prisoner" to their king.

That night, as the men sat around the fire, sharpening their swords and oiling their armor, William walked into the center of the circle.

"Gather around," he said.

The men fell silent, their attention immediately fixed on the small prince.

William sat by the fire, his legs crossed. "Tonight, we do not drill. Tonight, we learn to breathe."

He looked at them, his expression one of deep disappointment. "You all breathe like a thirty-year-old bellows at a rundown smithy! All huffing and puffing. No control. No rhythm. Your breath is the forge of your Ki. Yours is a cold fire."

He gestured to the ground. "Sit. Lotus style. As best you can."

The large, armored men awkwardly tried to fold their legs into the meditative position. Gorn, the largest of them, nearly toppled over.

"Close your eyes," William commanded, his voice now soft, hypnotic, a perfect imitation of Finn's healing cadence. "Breathe in through your nose. Fill your belly. Not your chest. Now... breathe out through your mouth. Slowly."

He led them through the cycle a few times.

"Now," he continued, "imagine. Do not think of battle. Do not think of the king. Imagine yourselves somewhere nice. Imagine you are at home. With a wife. And a child. A newborn."

The hardened faces of the soldiers softened in the firelight.

"You feel the warmth of the hearth," William's voice guided them. "You feel the weight of the child in your arms. You feel peace. You feel contentment. There is no war. There is no king. There is only this. This perfect, quiet moment."

He paused, letting them live in that image.

"Breathe in that feeling," he whispered. "Hold it. Now... breathe out the fear. Breathe out the anger."

He continued the guided meditation for ten minutes. An unprecedented calm settled over the camp of rough-hewn soldiers. The tension of the coming day seemed to melt away, replaced by a quiet, centered strength.

The captain, who had been participating, opened his eyes. He felt... peaceful. He looked at his men. They were not the grim-faced killers he had ridden with. They were just men. Men who missed their homes.

He looked at the small prince, whose eyes were still closed, his breathing deep and even.

This child had taken them apart, critiqued their every flaw, and then, in a single evening, had put them back together, stronger and more whole than they had ever been.

The captain finally understood. This was not a kidnapping. This was not an audit.

This was a rescue mission. And they were the ones being saved.

That is the ultimate power move. He's not being delivered; he's arriving. He dismisses his "captors" as if they were a hired car service at the airport. It's the perfect, audacious end to their journey and the beginning of the next, even more challenging phase of his "diplomatic mission."
