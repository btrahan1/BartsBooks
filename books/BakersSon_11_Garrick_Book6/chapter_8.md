## Chapter 8: The Dismissal

The next morning, the small troop crested a hill. Below them, nestled in a valley, lay the capital of Asgar. It was a city of black stone and sharp angles, built for war, not for comfort. At its center stood the Iron Citadel, the King's palace.

The captain rode up beside William. "There it is, Your Highness. We will deliver you to the King's court."

William looked at the city. He looked at the captain. He held up a small hand.

"Halt," he commanded.

The entire column stopped.

"Thank you, Captain," William said, his tone formal, official. "We can take it from here."

The captain blinked. "What? Your Highness, my orders are to deliver you—"

"Your orders were to provide an escort," William corrected smoothly. "The escort is complete. I see the palace." He gestured down the road. "It's a straight line. I believe even *we* can manage not to get lost from here."

He slid down from his horse. Finn and Galen dismounted beside him.

"The horses will be returned to you later this afternoon," William continued, as if discussing the return of a rented carriage. "You were a fine escort. Your breathing has improved, and your swords are much shinier. I will include that in my official report."

He gave the captain a crisp, dismissive nod.

"Dismissed."

The captain sat on his horse, utterly speechless. He had kidnapped a prince, been commandeered, retrained, fed, psychoanalyzed, and was now being unceremoniously fired, a half-mile from his own capital.

He looked at his men. They were looking back at him, waiting for his order. He saw the respect in their eyes. Not for him. For the child.

The captain finally found his voice. He gave the only order that made sense.

He saluted the six-year-old prince.

"Ride!" he commanded his men.

The Asgarian cavalry wheeled their horses around and galloped back the way they came, leaving the three figures standing alone on the road.

William watched them go. He then turned to face the city. "Well," he said, smoothing the front of his tunic. "Phase one is complete. The welcome party was... adequate."

He looked at Finn and Galen. "Now for phase two. Let's go meet the man in charge."

The three of them—the healer, the paladin, and the tiny, terrifying king—began their long walk into the heart of the enemy's city. They were not prisoners. They were an invading force of three. And they were right on schedule.

It's the perfect continuation of his attitude. He's not intimidated by the palace guards any more than he was by the cavalry. He treats them like butlers who are slow to answer the door. The "Run along now" is so wonderfully condescending and powerful.
