## Chapter 9: The Unannounced Arrival

The gates of the Iron Citadel were massive, imposing constructs of black iron and sharpened spikes. Two guards, clad head-to-toe in heavy plate armor, blocked the entrance, their halberds crossed.

"Halt! Who goes there?" one of them boomed, his voice distorted by his helmet.

Prince William, who had been examining the gate's hinge mechanism with a critical eye ("Sloppy work. Prone to rust."), sighed. It was the deep, put-upon sigh of a very important person dealing with incompetent staff.

He strode forward.

"I believe we are expected," he said, his small voice cutting through the tension. He craned his neck to look up at the towering guard. "The 'kidnapped' prince? It's been all over the diplomatic channels. Do try to keep up."

The guards exchanged a confused look. They had been told to expect a prisoner, delivered by the captain's unit. Not a small boy who was critiquing their punctuality.

"We have orders," the first guard grunted. "No one enters without the captain's escort."

William shook his head with a look of profound disappointment. "The captain has been dismissed. He had other duties. Now, run along. Go tell your king that Prince William and his party have arrived."

He clapped his hands. "Do it. Do it now!"

The guards didn't move. They were soldiers. They followed orders. And their orders were clear.

"We do not leave our post," the second guard said, his voice a low growl.

"Fine," William said, his patience clearly at an end. "Plan B."

He turned to Galen. "My Knight. These gentlemen seem to have a structural issue with their knees. Would you be so kind as to... adjust them?"

Galen stepped forward, his simple wooden practice staff held loosely in his hand. The guards laughed. "You'll stop us with a stick, priest?"

Before the laugh had died, Galen moved. He was a blur. He didn't attack the men. He attacked their armor. *Tap. Tap.* He struck the complex locking mechanism at the back of each guard's knee greaves with the hardened tip of his staff.

*Click. Click.*

The two guards tried to take a step. Their legs buckled. The knee joints of their armor had been expertly, non-lethally jammed. They toppled over with a crash of metal, unable to bend their legs to stand back up.

They lay on the ground like overturned beetles, flailing.

William stepped over them. "See?" he said to no one in particular. "Shoddy craftsmanship. A fatal design flaw."

He walked up to the massive iron gate and kicked it. It didn't budge.

"Well," he said. "This is inconvenient."

Just then, the gate began to groan, swinging inward. Standing on the other side was a man in dark, regal robes, his face a mask of cold, cruel amusement. King Theron of Asgar.

"Welcome, Prince William," the King said, a voice like grinding stones. "I see you've made yourself at home."

"Your guards were blocking the door," William replied, unfazed. "And your hinges need oiling. Now, are we going to stand here all day, or do you have any snacks? The walk was tiresome."

This adds a great layer of intrigue. The King isn't just a generic, surprised villain. He's informed, intelligent, and cunning. He's not being outsmarted; he's playing a longer, more dangerous game.

This turns the confrontation from a simple clash into a chess match between two very different, but equally formidable, minds.
