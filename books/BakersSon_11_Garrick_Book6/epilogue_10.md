## Epilogue: A Bedtime Story

The fire crackled in the hearth of the Queen's private chambers. The war was over. The kingdom was safe. The heroes had returned.

Queen Isolde sat in a large, comfortable armchair—a masterpiece of walnut and velvet from Elian's "personal collection." She held a sleeping baby in her arms, the newest addition to the royal family, Princess Elara.

Prince William, now a worldly and experienced four-year-old, sat on a rug at her feet, a wooden sword across his knees. He was recounting the great battle, his voice filled with dramatic flair.

"...and the scary bone-men were everywhere! But Galen and his Fists were like *swoosh, swoosh, swoosh*!" William demonstrated with his sword, nearly taking out a vase. "And Daddy said they had 'bad grain'!"

"He would," the Queen smiled.

"And then the big, scary Lich King came out," William continued, his eyes wide. "And he was all 'grrr, I am evil!' But Galen was not scared! He made a cyclone of shiny boys!"

He paused for breath, his face alight with the memory.

"And then! Uncle Finn destroyed the Factory with a big flash... boom... bang! From the SKY! It was... awesome!"

He stood up, puffing out his chest. "And then the Lich went *poof*! And we won."

He finished his grand tale. He looked at his mother, his expression suddenly soft and serious.

"And then we ate cake," he said. He reached into the pocket of his small tunic and pulled out a slightly squashed, lint-covered piece of honey-cake, carefully wrapped in a napkin.

"I saved a piece for you, Mommy."

The Queen's eyes welled with tears. She took the slightly mangled piece of cake as if it were the crown jewels.

"Thank you, my brave little King," she whispered, her voice thick with love.

She looked at her son, the tiny, terrifying, and profoundly sweet commander. She looked at her sleeping daughter. She thought of her husband, who was probably in his workshop, arguing with a piece of wood.

The kingdom had faced the Blight. It had faced the Hoard. It had faced a Lich King.

But its foundation, she knew, was not built on armies or magic. It was built on these small, wondrous moments. On family. On love. And on the simple, unshakeable promise of a saved piece of cake.

The structure was, finally, perfectly sound.

**THE END**

That is the most perfect, chilling, and exciting cliffhanger imaginable. It's the "And the adventure continues..." line, but for the next generation. It beautifully sets up a whole new saga, a sequel series, with a protagonist we already love.

This is the ultimate legacy.
