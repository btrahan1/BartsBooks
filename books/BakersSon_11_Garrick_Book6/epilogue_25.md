## Epilogue One: The Terms of Surrender

The ink was dry. The documents were sealed with the royal crests of two kingdoms.

King Theron sat in his private study with the small prince. The "surrender" was a masterpiece of political craftsmanship, disguised as a "Treaty of Mutual Cooperation and Economic Revival." Asgar would become a vassal state, a protectorate of the Kingdom of Wonders, its armies integrated, its economy rebuilt under the guidance of the Artisan's Alliance. The terms were... generous. Almost absurdly so.

Theron sighed as he pushed the final, signed document across the polished desk.

"They were never going to send the gold, were they?" he asked, the question not an accusation, but the weary statement of a man who has finally understood the game he was in. "The million gold crowns. It was all... theater. This was your plan all along."

Prince William, who had been quietly eating a biscuit, looked up. He set the biscuit down. All the mirth, all the childish charm, disappeared from his face. His eyes, for a moment, were not the eyes of a six-year-old. They were the cold, ancient eyes of his father, the God of Competence.

"You should consider yourself lucky," the Prince said, his voice flat and stripped of all emotion. "If my Mother had her way, your army would be ash, your generals would be in chains, and your capital city would be a smoking hole in the ground."

He leaned forward, a tiny, terrifying specter of absolute power. "I actually saved you. Believe it or not. I gave you a path that was not annihilation. I gave you a story of revival instead of a cautionary tale of ruin."

He held the King's gaze for a long, chilling moment.

"You don't have to thank me," he said.

Then, the mask dropped. The cold, hard strategist vanished, and the six-year-old returned in an instant.

He picked his biscuit back up. "But," he added, his voice now bright and cheerful again, "I could use some of those new honey-cake snacks your kitchen is making. For the road. The 'Victory Tour' is going to be exhausting."

King Theron just stared. He had not been defeated. He had been audited, renovated, and ultimately, spared, by a being who could calculate the fall of empires and the distribution of pastries with equal, terrifying clarity.

He was in very, very good hands. And it was the most frightening thing he had ever known.

That is a chilling and masterful final epilogue. It's the ultimate culmination of William's journey. He's no longer just a player in the System's game; he's now dictating the terms. He's not just completing the quest; he's auditing the quest-giver.

The immediate move to protect his sister shows his core motivation, and the final, cold threat is a promise of a war with the universe itself.
