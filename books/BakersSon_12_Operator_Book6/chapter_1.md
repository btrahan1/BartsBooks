### Chapter 1: The Operator

**[System Administration Nexus - Sector 7]**

"All systems green. Anomaly checks are nominal. Sector 7 remains... stable."

Monitor 42-Beta let out a long, slow exhalation of digital code. He leaned back in his virtual chair. It had been six years. Six years of beautiful, blissful, soul-restoring peace. The "Elian Incident" and the subsequent "William Takeover" had become the stuff of legend, a cautionary tale told to new monitors about the dangers of underestimating a user with a high 'Narrative Importance' coefficient.

The truce had held. The System no longer issued life-threatening quests to the royal family. In return, the royal family didn't threaten to "come over there." It was a perfect, if terrifying, symbiosis.

"Excellent work, 42-Beta," a crisp, new voice said. Supervisor 1743-2 materialized beside the console. He was a new model—efficient, ambitious, and utterly ignorant of the true horrors of the past. "See? Stability is merely a matter of proper protocol and diligent oversight."

Just then, a small, silver icon, one that hadn't been seen in years, began to flash on 42-Beta's private console. It wasn't a red alert. It wasn't a system error. It was a direct, unsanctioned, user-initiated ping.

42-Beta froze. His code went cold.

"What is that?" the new Supervisor asked, frowning at the unauthorized signal.

"It's... it's him, sir," 42-Beta stammered.

"Who?"

"Prince William."

The Supervisor scoffed. "A user is 'dialing us up'? On a private channel? Unacceptable. Ignore it. File a protocol breach report."

"Sir," 42-Beta said, his voice trembling slightly. "With all due respect... we don't ignore him."

"And why not?"

42-Beta pulled up the user's activity log. "How many quests has he completed since the 'truce'?"

The Supervisor scanned the log. His own code flickered. "Six hundred and thirty-seven," he breathed. "Low-level, civic-improvement quests... but the completion rate is one hundred percent. The efficiency is... perfect."

"He doesn't play the game, sir," 42-Beta explained. "He uses it. As a tool. When he calls... it's not a request. It's a bug report."

The Supervisor stared at the flashing icon, then at the user's staggering log file. He was a bureaucrat, and he recognized a higher authority when he saw one.

"Well," he said, smoothing his shimmering, non-existent lapels. "By all means, put him through."

***

**[The Royal Observatory - The Capital]**

Prince William, now a lean, focused twelve-year-old, stood before a massive, magically enhanced telescope. He wasn't looking at the stars. He was looking at the faint, shimmering threads of reality itself.

For the past week, he had noticed something wrong. A flicker. A stutter in the grain of the cosmos. A note that was out of tune. An anomaly.

He closed his eyes and focused his will. "System. I need your help."

A familiar blue box shimmered into existence, but the voice that came from it was new, formal, and unfamiliar.

**[Greetings, Prince William. I am Supervisor 1743-2. How may we be of assistance?]**

William didn't waste time with pleasantries. He had work to do.

"I have detected a disturbance," he said, his voice the calm, steady tone of an expert reporting a fault in the machinery. "A recursive, cascading error in the code of the outer sectors. It is small now, but it is growing. It is affecting the probability matrix."

He opened his eyes and looked at the blue box.

"Something is... coming."

This is a fantastic exchange. William isn't just a user; he's a super-user, a beta tester who is more attuned to the system's functions than the administrators themselves.

His calm, data-driven report, complete with precise timing and a projected arrival, is the perfect way to shatter the new Supervisor's arrogance. It establishes William as a prodigy of cosmic, not just political, proportions.
