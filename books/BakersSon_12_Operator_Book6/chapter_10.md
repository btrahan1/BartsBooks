## Chapter 10: The Price of a Miracle

The cottage smelled of fresh bread and drying herbs. Oren's mother was humming in her rocking chair. His father was outside, mending a fence with a strength he hadn't possessed in twenty years. It was a haven of peace.

And Prince William was about to ask Oren to burn it to the ground.

The Prince sat at the small, rough-hewn kitchen table. With him were Finn and, for moral support, Bran the Baker, whose own simple origins made him a bridge between the two worlds.

"I don't understand!" Oren said, wringing his large, soil-stained hands. He was twisting his new, Garrick-made tunic into knots. "You want me to... leave? To give up our farm?" He looked at William, his eyes wide with a simple, profound confusion. "To... aliens?"

"To the invaders, yes," William corrected gently. "Not forever. Just for a time. We need them to land in one place, Oren. A place so rich in life, so full of the very energy they despise, that they will see it as a symbolic prize. A place to establish their beachhead. Your farm... your farm is the most alive place in the entire kingdom."

"But... this is our home," Oren whispered. "Ma and Pa... they're finally healthy. They're happy here."

"And they will be again," Finn said, his voice a calm, reassuring presence. "We have prepared a new home for them, in a protected valley near the capital. It will be safe. It will be comfortable. They will want for nothing."

"But it's not *this* home," Oren insisted, a stubborn, farmer's love for his land welling up in him. "This is *our* soil. *Our* water."

William looked at the humble, terrified man. He saw not a coward, but a protector. A man who, like him, was driven by the simple, fierce need to protect his family. He could command Oren. He was the Prince. But he knew, with his father's instinct, that this was not a problem of authority. It was a problem of the heart.

He slid off his chair and walked over to the window. He looked out at the vibrant, glowing fields.

"Oren," William said softly. "The soup you make... it heals people. It gives them strength. It is a miracle."

"It's just vegetables," Oren mumbled.

"No," William said, turning back. "It is hope, in a bowl. You have fed our armies. You have healed our citizens. You have, in your own quiet way, become one of the most important pillars of this kingdom's defense."

He looked Oren in the eye. "Now, the kingdom needs one more miracle from you. It needs a sacrifice. It needs you to give up this small piece of life, so that we may protect all of it."

He gestured to the fields. "This farm is a symbol of everything we are fighting for. And by giving them this target, you will be striking the first, and perhaps the most important, blow in this war. You will be the bait that saves the kingdom."

Oren looked at the Prince. He looked at Finn, his friend. He looked at Bran, a man who understood the simple love of a craft. He looked through the doorway at his parents, laughing in the afternoon sun.

He was just a farmer. A quiet man who liked to talk to his plants.

And his Prince had just asked him to become a hero.

He let out a long, shaky breath. "When... when do we need to leave?" he asked.

This is a devastatingly clever and heartbreaking strategy. William isn't just asking Oren to abandon his home; he's asking him to *unmake* it. To drain the life from his own creation. It's a profound sacrifice.

And the quest notification appearing for Oren is the perfect way to formalize this. It's the System itself acknowledging the weight and importance of his personal sacrifice, turning it into a legendary act.
