## Chapter 12: The Weight of Command

The sun was setting as the royal carriage rumbled back towards the capital. William sat in silence, staring out the window at the passing countryside. The meeting with Oren had taken a toll on him.

Elian, who had been waiting for them at the edge of the farmlands, sat opposite his son. He had watched the exchange from a distance, feeling a surge of both immense pride and deep, parental pain.

"Wow," William finally whispered, breaking the silence. "That was harder than I thought it would be." He looked at his father, his young face etched with a weariness that had nothing to do with a long day. "Asking him to do that... it felt... cruel."

"Agreed," Elian said softly, his voice a low rumble of empathy. "It is a terrible thing to rule at times. To have to sacrifice a piece of the structure to save the whole. The math is cold. But the feeling..."

He reached across and placed a heavy, calloused hand on his son's shoulder. "But I am proud of you, Son. You did not command him as a prince. You asked him as a man. You gave him the choice. It was the right move. The honorable move."

William nodded, taking a small measure of comfort from his father's words. He had made the hard choice. The first of many, he suspected.

He took a deep breath, the child giving way to the commander once more. He rubbed his hands together, a familiar, business-like gesture he had picked up from his father.

"So," he said, his voice regaining its focused, strategic edge. "Phase one is set. The bait is laid. Now for phase two."

Elian's lips curved into a small, grim smile. The moment of reflection was over. It was back to the work.

"Phase two," Elian confirmed. "The mousetrap."

The "shortage of resources" is a brilliant piece of logistical sabotage that perfectly complements the "scorched earth" plan for Oren's farm. And the wicked grin is the perfect visual cue—he's not just a commander; he's enjoying the intricate design of his trap.
