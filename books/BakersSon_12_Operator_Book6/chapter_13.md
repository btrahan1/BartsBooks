## Chapter 13: The Mousetrap

The War Table glowed in the Heart Room, illuminating the faces of the inner circle. The mood was somber but resolute. Oren's sacrifice had given their plan a heavy, sacred weight.

William stood at the head of the table, his small hands resting on the glowing map.

"Phase one is in motion," he began. "The trap is baited. Now, we design the spring."

He pointed to the area surrounding Oren's farm. "If I were a warring culture, a race of professional soldiers, the first thing I would do when I secured a beachhead would be to send out scouts. To map the terrain, to assess the local defenses, to find resources. It's basic warfare 101."

He looked at the faces around the table. "They will immediately start constructing some sort of fortress on Oren's land, a base of operations. But," he said, a slow, cunning smile spreading across his face, "they will find a shortage of resources, strangely enough."

He looked at Piper. "The logging camps within a fifty-mile radius of the farm will be... temporarily closed for a 'bark beetle infestation'," he said.

He looked at the Duke. "The quarries will be shut down for 'safety inspections'."

He looked at Ren. "And every village, every farm, every barn will be stripped bare of anything useful. Food, lumber, metal... all of it will be gone, moved to hidden caches by the Grey Service. The entire region will be a barren wasteland."

A wicked grin appeared on William's face.

"So," he continued, "their scouts will have to spread farther than they would like, desperately searching for supplies. And that's when we'll hit them."

He began placing small, glowing markers on the map—silver for their forces, red for the projected enemy scout parties.

"We will not engage in a pitched battle. We will provoke a few, small, running battles. Our Fists of Freedom, led by Valerius, will strike and fade back into the forests. We will test their strength. We will test their tactics. We will let them capture a few of our men, who will be carrying beautifully forged, and completely inaccurate, maps and strategic plans."

He looked at Ren. "And your agents will be shadows, gathering information. We need to know everything. What are their weapons capable of? What is their command structure? How do they communicate?"

He stepped back from the table. "For the first month, we do not fight a war. We conduct a study. We will be the scientists, and they," he said, tapping a red marker, "will be the rats in our maze."

This is a gut-wrenching, but strategically brilliant, move. It's the "hard choice" of a true commander. Using the children of the Grey Service is a massive risk, but as William lays it out, it's also the most effective possible strategy.

And Ren's reaction is perfect. She's not shocked by the ruthlessness of the plan; she's shocked by the absolute, unwavering trust the Prince has in her and her people. It's a profound moment of validation for her.
