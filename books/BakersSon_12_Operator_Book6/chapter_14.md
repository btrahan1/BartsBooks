## Chapter 14: The Unwitting Spies

The room was silent, the cold logic of the Prince's strategy settling over them.

"How do we ensure the safety of our captured men?"

The question came from Ren. She was frowning, a deep, troubled line between her brows. Her face, usually a mask of cool confidence, was clouded with an uneasy feeling. Sacrificing pawns was a part of her world, but this felt different.

William turned to her, his gaze direct and serious. He knew this was the most difficult, most dangerous part of his plan.

"Yes, Ren. This will be risky," he admitted, his voice quiet but firm. "We are taking a gamble. But a calculated one. The invaders are a proud, martial race. They see themselves as honorable warriors, not butchers. They will take prisoners for interrogation. But they will not harm non-combatants."

He paused, letting the weight of his next words sink in.

"We will let them capture... 'children'."

A gasp went through the room. The Queen took a step forward, a mother's protective instinct flaring. "William, no."

"Not just any children, Mother," William said, his eyes never leaving Ren's. "Not our typical children. Those in the Service. The best of them. The ones who can look a monster in the eye and lie with a straight face."

He was talking about her people. Pip. Kestrel. The street-hardened orphans she had forged into an elite intelligence unit.

"They will be disguised," William continued, laying out the terrible, brilliant strategy. "They will appear to be lost, terrified farm children, civilians with zero threat value. The invaders will take them. They will care for them, in their own brutish, martial way. And they will talk around them, thinking they are not understood."

He looked at Ren, his gaze a mixture of a commander's demand and a leader's trust.

"It's a job only your people can do, Ren," he said. "They will be invaluable. They will be both our greatest source of direct, inside information, and our most effective source of disinformation."

He was asking her to send her children, her family, into the heart of the enemy's camp.

Ren stared at her Prince in shock. But it was not the shock of a subordinate receiving an impossible order.

It was the shock of a master spy who had just been handed the most daring, high-stakes, and potentially effective intelligence operation in history. She was shocked not that he had suggested it, but that he could almost read her mind, that he understood the unique, terrifying capabilities of her Grey Service so perfectly.

And she was shocked by the absolute, unwavering faith he had in her to carry it out.

He wasn't just using her people. He was trusting them. With the fate of the entire war.

A slow, grim, and deeply proud smile touched Ren's lips.

"They won't even see it coming," she said. "When do we begin?"

That is a beautiful, intimate, and powerful scene. It's the perfect way to address the emotional weight of William's decision. It shows that he's not a cold, unfeeling strategist; he's a boy who still needs his mother's approval.

And the Queen's response, calling back to his own past sacrifices, is the perfect way to reassure him and validate his difficult choice. It's a wonderful, full-circle moment for his character.
