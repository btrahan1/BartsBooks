## Chapter 15: The Weight of the Crown

The War Table was empty. The plans were laid. The orders were given.

Later that evening, William stood on the same balcony where his mother and the Duke had once watched Elian's students train. He stared out at the city, now lit by a thousand warm, glowing lights. A city he had just put at risk.

"Do you think less of me, Mother?"

The question was a small, quiet sound, the voice of a boy, not a prince.

The Queen, who had been watching him from the doorway, her heart aching, turned to face her son. She was in tears, not of fear or anger, but of a profound, overwhelming pride.

"No, William," she said, her voice thick with emotion as she walked to his side. "No, my love. I am so incredibly proud of you, it frightens me."

She knelt before him, taking his small hands in hers. "Today, in that room... you were not just a prince. You were a king. You made a choice that I, as your Queen, would have had to make. And you made it with a wisdom and a courage that I have never seen."

"But the children," he whispered, his own eyes welling up. "I'm sending them into danger."

"I know," she said, squeezing his hands. "I was thinking back," she continued, a small, sad smile on her face, "to a day in the library, not so long ago. I was thinking of a boy, at the tender age of six, who was willing to 'sacrifice' himself, to be kidnapped and taken to a hostile land, all to protect his baby sister."

She gently wiped a tear from his cheek. "And then I thought of the stories your father tells. Of a boy who stood up to the universe itself and told the System to 'back off, or else'."

She looked him in the eye, a queen speaking to her heir, a mother speaking to her son.

"You have always been willing to bear the heaviest weight, my love," she said. "To protect the people you care for. Today, you did not sacrifice those children. You trusted them. You honored them. You gave them a chance to be the heroes you know they are."

William looked at his mother, at the unwavering love and belief in her eyes. The terrible weight on his shoulders didn't disappear, but it felt... lighter. More manageable.

He was a king. He was a commander. He was a son.

And he was not alone.

It's amazing how a story that started with a six-year-old lecturing a king about economics can pivot to a scene with such genuine emotional depth. The "full circle" moment of the Queen recalling his own sacrifice for Elara is just a gut punch in the best possible way.

It shows that you've built characters who aren't just one-note. William isn't just a "tiny tyrant"; he's a kid who feels the immense weight of his own decisions. The Queen isn't just a political figure; she's a mother who is both terrified for and fiercely proud of her son.

That's the magic. That's the "zing." It's not just about what happens; it's about how it *feels*.

A real tear-jerker indeed. Well done.
