## Chapter 17: The Refresher Course

The war council was in session, the energy in the Heart Room a mixture of grim determination and a strange, festive excitement. Plans for the "Grand Mid-Summer's Masquerade Ball" were proceeding at a furious pace.

"So, the trap is set," the Duke summarized, looking at the glowing map. "The army is hidden. The city is the bait. What happens when the hammer falls? What is our fallback plan if they are stronger than we anticipate?"

William, who had been listening silently, began to pace around the War Table. Round and round he paced, his small form a vortex of focused energy.

"The fallback plan," he said, "is also the main plan."

He looked at the generals. "We will have about one hundred thousand soldiers hidden in and around the city. Our Fists, our Schools, our Saws. But they are not our main force."

A stunned silence fell over the room. Grand Marshal Valerius looked at William in shock. "Your Highness? Not our main force? Do we have a backup army?"

William stopped pacing. He grinned. It was a wide, brilliant, and deeply unsettling grin. "In a way," he said.

"The festival is in two months," he continued. "Plenty of time to train."

"Train who?" Valerius asked, his voice a mixture of confusion and dawning awe.

"The citizens, of course," William said simply, as if stating the most obvious fact in the world.

He looked around the table at the blank faces. "Have you forgotten? For the last six years, since the first war, what have the children been playing in the streets? What have the craftsmen been practicing in their yards after work? What have the farmers been drilling in their fields during the off-season?"

He leaned over the table, his eyes burning with a fierce, proud light.

"They have all been training in the way of the Fist. They have been learning the Dance. The entire kingdom has been our training ground, practically since I was born! The philosophy is in their bones."

He straightened up. "Our army is not one hundred thousand soldiers. It is a nation of five million artisans, farmers, and merchants who know how to flow, how to balance, and how to fight as a unit."

"It's time," Prince William declared, "for a refresher course."

He turned to Valerius. "Marshal, you will oversee it. Every citizen, from the age of sixteen to sixty, will participate in daily 'Festival Drills'. We will disguise it as a preparation for a grand, city-wide choreographed dance for the ball. It is not a call to arms. It is a call to rehearsal."

The room was silent, the sheer, breathtaking audacity of the plan sinking in. He was going to arm and train the entire populace under the guise of a party.

"When the invaders breach our walls," William said, his voice a low, cold promise, "they will not find a city of soft, screaming civilians. They will find five million partners in the Dance of Death. They will not be fighting an army. They will be fighting a hornet's nest."

That is a brilliant tactical clarification. It assigns a clear, achievable, and less suicidal role to the citizen militia, turning them into a strategic asset rather than just cannon fodder. They aren't the hammer; they are the anvil.

And William's "WHAM!" is the perfect, childishly enthusiastic, and terrifyingly effective conclusion to his military briefing.
