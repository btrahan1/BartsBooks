## Chapter 18: The Anvil and the Hammer

"That sounds incredibly dangerous."

The voice of caution came, as it often did, from Finn. He looked at William, his healer's mind picturing a thousand scenarios of untrained citizens facing hardened alien soldiers. "Arming the populace is one thing. Asking them to fight an army of a million... it is a recipe for a massacre."

"You are right," William agreed, surprising everyone. "It would be. If I were asking them to fight."

He turned to the map, his small finger tracing the intricate network of streets and alleys within the capital city. "The citizens' job is not to win the battle. It is not to fight the enemy head-on. It is to contain them. To delay them. To frustrate them."

He looked at Ren, who was leaning against the wall, a slow, predatory smile on her face. She already understood.

"This is not a battlefield," William said, gesturing to the city. "It is a maze. It is a trap. The citizens are the walls of that trap. They will barricade the streets. They will create bottlenecks in the plazas. They will use their knowledge of every alley, every rooftop, every sewer grate, to harry the enemy, to split their formations, to lead them on false trails into dead ends."

He then turned back to Finn. "And that is where you and the Temple come in, my friend."

"You will not be with the main army," William commanded. "You will be with the city. Your healers will set up aid stations in every district. You will be the heart that keeps the city alive. You will mend the broken, you will soothe the fearful, and you will be the light that they rally to."

He looked around the room, his eyes blazing with the force of his strategy. "The invaders will push into the city, expecting to find a soft center. Instead, they will find themselves in a quagmire, a hornet's nest of citizens who will not break, who will not flee, but who will slow them down, bleed them, and drive them mad with a thousand tiny cuts."

He paused, letting the vision of a city transformed into a living weapon sink in.

"And then," he said, his voice dropping to a low, dramatic whisper. He raised his small fist high in the air.

"WHAM!"

He smashed his fist down on the War Table. The holographic map flickered violently. The Gnomish representative yelped and dove under the table.

"Our *real* army," William roared, "which will be waiting outside the city, will circle around and CRUSH THEM FROM BEHIND! We will catch them between the anvil of the city and the hammer of our Fists!"

He stood there, panting slightly, his face flushed with the pure, joyful genius of his own design.

The room was silent.

"Well," the Duke of Corvan finally said, a slow, deeply impressed grin spreading across his face. "That is a sound structure."

That is a powerful moment of vulnerability and justification. After the "WHAM!" and the theatrical roar, this quiet, somber explanation is what truly sells the plan to the council.

It shows he's not just a child playing at war. He's a commander who has weighed the options, calculated the risks, and is choosing this radical, dangerous path not out of arrogance, but out of a grim, logical necessity. It's the voice of a true leader, acknowledging the cost and taking responsibility.
