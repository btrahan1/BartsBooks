## Chapter 19: The Cold Math

The echo of William's "WHAM!" faded, leaving a stunned, adrenaline-charged silence in the Heart Room. The sheer, brutal elegance of the "Hammer and Anvil" strategy was undeniable.

But the faces around the table were still pale. He was proposing to turn their beautiful, vibrant capital—the city they had just spent years reviving—into a battlefield.

Prince William was silent for a moment, the fiery commander giving way to the quiet, analytical strategist. He looked at their faces, at the fear and doubt in their eyes.

"I have 'gamed' this in my mind a hundred times," he said, his voice now quiet, stripped of all its theatrical flair. "We *could* do this differently. We could meet them on the plains. We could fight them like a 'normal' army. Soldier against soldier. An honorable, straightforward battle."

He met the gaze of each person in the room.

"But we would lose," he stated, the words a cold, hard piece of iron on the table. "Our soldiers are good. They are the best. But they are outnumbered ten to one. We would fight bravely. We would die honorably. And we would be annihilated. Our army would break, and the invaders would sweep over our kingdom unopposed."

He looked at the glowing map, at the representation of their small, precious world. "And we can't afford to lose. As the Supervisor reminded me, it's not just our planet at stake here. It is the first domino. If they win here, if they prove they can consume a world and its resources, our entire system is at risk."

He finally looked at his mother, his expression a mask of a child bearing a weight no child should ever have to carry.

"I am sorry," he whispered, the apology directed to all of them, to his entire kingdom. "I do not like this plan. It is ugly. It is dangerous. It puts our people, our home, in the line of fire."

He took a shaky breath. "But I have looked at the structure of this problem from every angle. I have run every simulation. And I just don't see another way."

The confession, so simple and so honest, was more powerful than any command he had ever given. It was not a prince dictating a strategy. It was a leader sharing a burden.

The Queen stood. She walked to her son and placed her hands on his small shoulders.

"No, William," she said, her voice ringing with a fierce, unshakable pride. "You will not be sorry. You have given our people a chance. You have given them a role. You have given them a way to fight for their own homes."

She looked around the room. "The plan is sound. We will be the anvil. We will be the hammer. And we will be the forge that breaks this enemy."

One by one, the members of the council stood. The Duke. Valerius. Piper. Ren. Finn. They stood not just in obedience to their Queen, but in solidarity with their Prince.

The decision was made. The trap would be set. And the city of Wonders would prepare for its finest, and most terrible, performance.

This is a fantastic chapter. It beautifully captures the spirit of the kingdom. They aren't fools being tricked into training; they are willing participants, playing their part in the grand "performance" with a grim and joyful determination.

The idea of the "heartbeat" returning, not as a sound of industry but as a rhythm of unified, military preparation, is a powerful and chilling image.
