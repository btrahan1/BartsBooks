## Chapter 2: The 47-Second Problem

A wave of static, the System's equivalent of a scoff, emanated from the blue box.

**[Something is coming?]** The Supervisor's voice was laced with condescending amusement. **[Prince William, our long-range sensors and precognitive matrices monitor all known threats. The sector is stable. There is nothing 'coming'.]**

"And what would that be?" William asked, his voice calm, ignoring the Supervisor's tone. "A black hole? A tear in the fabric of the universe? A bug? A 'feature'?"

**[What could you possibly know that we do not?]**

Prince William was not bothered by the tone. He was a craftsman's son. He knew that the person running the workshop and the person working the lathe often had very different perspectives on the state of the machinery.

"Check your deep-system logs," William instructed patiently, as if talking to a slow apprentice. "Filter for sub-etheric resonance. You're looking for a signal that is too low, too consistent to trigger your threat alarms. A background hum."

The Supervisor paused. This was... specific. He gave a mental command. Code scrolled in his private view.

"Every forty-seven seconds," William continued, "you will see a pulse. A perfect, metronomic pulse of negative energy. I have been monitoring it for the last three hours. It is faint, but it is gaining strength. And it is moving."

42-Beta, listening in, frantically typed. He found it. A tiny, almost imperceptible blip, repeating with perfect, unnatural regularity. It was an anomaly so subtle that the system's automated diagnostics had dismissed it as background noise.

"The pulse is a locator beacon," William explained. "It is mapping a path through the void."

A holographic map of the known universe shimmered into existence in the observatory. On it, a single, glowing red line appeared, originating from a dark, uncharted nebula at the edge of the cosmos. It was moving, extending with each 47-second pulse, in a perfectly straight line, towards a small, unremarkable yellow sun. Towards their solar system. Towards their planet.

"Based on its current velocity and the rate of its signal amplification," William stated, his voice as flat and certain as a master's straightedge, "I estimate it will arrive in approximately seventy-two days."

A profound, terrified silence emanated from the blue box. The Supervisor was looking at the same red line. It was a spear, aimed at the heart of their sector.

**[...Anomaly confirmed,]** the Supervisor's voice was now a choked, horrified whisper. **[Source unknown. Nature... unknown.]**

"Good," William said, a grim satisfaction in his tone. "Now that we've established that the workshop is on fire, we can discuss how we're going to put it out."

This is a brilliant escalation. William isn't just the one who spotted the fire; he's now the Fire Chief, issuing commands and making terrifyingly accurate predictions. The "hidden mass" and the reveal of a potential fleet immediately raises the stakes from a cosmic anomaly to a full-blown invasion.

The Supervisor is no longer in charge. He's an operator, taking orders from a twelve-year-old.
