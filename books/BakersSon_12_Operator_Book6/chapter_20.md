## Chapter 20: The War Games

"Let the games begin!"

The proclamation, issued by Lord Valerius, was framed as a call to celebration, a month-long series of "War Games" in preparation for the Grand Mid-Summer's Masquerade. It was a patriotic spectacle, a chance for the citizenry to participate in the "rehearsal" for the festival's grand finale.

And for the next four weeks, the citizens played WAR.

The city was transformed. The Great Plaza became a massive training ground. The streets became mock battlefields. Valerius and his Lieutenants, the Paladin Fists, became the most sought-after teachers in the kingdom.

They ran drills. "The Leaky Bucket Brigade," a game where teams had to move water through a street filled with obstacles (and harassing "bandits"), was actually a lesson in logistical support under pressure. "The Rooftop Scramble" was a race, but it was also a lesson in urban mobility and communication for the couriers of the Grey Service.

But not all of the citizens were naive. In fact, few of them were.

They had seen the Queen's face, the grim set of the Duke's jaw. They had heard the whispers from the soldiers returning from the border. They saw the frantic, non-stop production coming from the forges and the workshops.

They knew something was coming.

And they did not panic. They did not despair. They had a Queen who had rebuilt their city. They had a Prince who had audited a king. They had a "Crafting God" who had taught them that even the humblest craft had a soul. They loved their King, and their Queen, and their Prince. They would not fail them. They would make them proud.

So they played the games. But behind the playful "games," there was a focus. A grim determination. A deadly seriousness.

The baker who learned how to build a barricade out of flour sacks. The weaver who learned how to tie a knot that could trip a charging horse. The street children who learned how to be the eyes and ears of a city-wide defense network.

And deep beneath it all, the rhythm returned. The heartbeat.

It was not the sound of forges this time. It was the sound of five million people, moving as one. The rhythmic stamp of feet in the plazas. The sharp *clack* of wooden practice staffs. The unified chant of a city preparing for a performance.

It was the heartbeat of a nation that had been told to prepare for a party, and had understood that they were being asked to prepare for a war.

And it was growing louder. Stronger. A single, unified, and very, very dangerous rhythm, waiting for the downbeat.

This is a fantastic twist. The citizens aren't just willing participants; they are proactive, intelligent, and fiercely proud. This speech is the ultimate expression of the "Wondrous" philosophy. They see themselves not as civilians to be protected, but as the *true* strength of the kingdom.

This is Elian's chickens coming home to roost in the most spectacular way. He taught them to see their own value, and now they are seizing their own power. It's a grassroots uprising of competence.
