## Chapter 21: The Anvil's Debate

The meeting was held in the cavernous main hall of the new Metalsmith's Guild, the air thick with the smell of coal smoke and righteous indignation. It was a secret gathering of the city's unofficial leaders: the shop foremen, the neighborhood elders, the mothers who ran the markets. The true heart of the citizenry.

Master Kael, the smith, stood before them, a hammer in his hand.

"So, that's how I see it," he rumbled, his voice a low fire. "An army is coming. A big one. And our beloved leaders, in their wisdom, are trying to protect us without causing a panic. They are trying to prepare us with these 'games'. How quaint."

A murmur of agreement went through the crowd.

"But we are many," Kael continued, "and the army, for all its skill, is few. They are likely wanting us to 'hold the line', to be the anvil, or some such poetic nonsense, while the *real* army takes the actual risks of fighting."

He looked from face to face. "I have spoken with the Provisions Master Silas. I have seen the requisitions. By my guess, the enemy likely outnumbers our standing army by at least five to one. Maybe more."

A wave of fear rippled through the hall. Kael let it settle, then he smashed it.

"But!" he roared, slamming his hammer down on the anvil for emphasis. *CLANG.* "That would mean that *we*... the citizens, the craftsmen, the mothers and fathers, the TRUE strength of this nation... outnumber the enemy by at least ten to one!"

A new, dangerous energy began to build in the room.

"And yes," Kael's voice dropped, becoming a conspiratorial growl. "We may not be soldiers. We are not the Fists of Freedom. But we are not typical 'citizens' either! We are the men and women who rebuilt this city with our own hands! We have been taught by the Master himself that our craft has a soul, that our will has a grain! We are not a soft, civilian center!"

He raised his hammer high. "They think to protect us, which is nice, in its own way. It is a sign of their love."

He paused, a slow, wolfish grin spreading across his face. "But I say, when this battle is done, let's just leave some for the army to mop up. WHAT SAY YOU?"

The hall did not cheer. It roared. A single, unified, and utterly terrifying sound of a people who had been told to be an anvil, and had collectively decided they would rather be the hammer.

The Queen's plan was to use them as a delay tactic.

The people's plan was to win the war before the army even arrived.

That's a powerful, full-circle moment. The leadership has been trying to protect the people, and now, the people are trying to protect their leaders. It's the ultimate proof of the unity and love that has been built.

William's frustration and the Queen's emotional pride create a beautiful, poignant scene right before the climax.
