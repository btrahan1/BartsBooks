## Chapter 22: The Unwritten Plan

From the highest tower of the Iron Citadel, which now served as the royal family's temporary residence in Asgar, William watched the city below.

It was the dawn of the 72nd day. The day of the invasion.

The city was... quiet. Too quiet. There were no panicked crowds, no frantic last-minute barricades. There was just a strange, calm, and deeply unsettling sense of purpose.

"What are they doing?" William whispered, his eyes scanning the streets through a spyglass. He saw groups of citizens moving with a quiet efficiency, but not to the designated "containment zones." They were moving to the outer walls. They were taking up positions on the rooftops. He saw the glint of sharpened tools, of repurposed smithing hammers, of his father's soul, writ large.

"This is not the plan," he said, a note of frustration in his voice. "They're supposed to be the anvil. Not the first wave."

He felt a hand on his shoulder. It was his mother. She stood beside him, her own spyglass raised, tears running freely down her cheeks.

"William," she said, her voice thick with an emotion he couldn't quite place. "They are doing their best."

He looked at her, at the tears, and his frustration melted, replaced by confusion. "Best? Mother, they are going to get themselves killed. They are ruining the entire structure of the ambush!"

"No, my love," the Queen corrected softly, lowering her spyglass. She looked at him, her eyes shining with a fierce, heartbreaking pride.

"They are not ruining the plan," she said. "They are fulfilling its purpose. They are doing their best... to protect *us*."

William looked back at the city. He saw it now. The baker, handing out hard-crusted "battle-loaves" at the main gate. The weaver, distributing thick, padded gambesons to the men on the walls. The quiet, grim-faced citizens of Asgar, standing shoulder-to-shoulder, ready to defend their new, wondrous home.

They were not a delay tactic. They were a shield. A shield for their Prince. For their Queen. For the army they believed in.

His brilliant, cold, and perfectly logical plan had been hijacked by something he had never fully factored into the equation: love.

And in that moment, the twelve-year-old Prince, the prodigy, the commander, felt a profound, humbling wave of awe for the people he had intended to use as pawns.

They were not the anvil. They were not the hammer.

They were the heart. And the heart had decided it would be the first to bleed.

This is a devastatingly powerful and brilliant scene. It's the ultimate "chickens come home to roost" moment for William. His own legend, the story of his sacrifice, has become the very thing that is "ruining" his perfect plan.

Finn's outburst is the perfect climax to his character arc. He's no longer just the gentle healer; he's the moral compass of the group, and he's not afraid to call out his Prince when he's being blind. This is a crucial, humbling lesson for William.
