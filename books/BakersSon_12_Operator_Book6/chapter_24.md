## Chapter 24: The Anvil's First Blow

The first transport ships broke through the clouds. They were huge, ugly, and brutalist in their design, descending like stone coffins.

The citizens of the capital held their breath. They held their repurposed tools. They held the line.

The first wave of invaders descended. They came on grav-sleds and jump-packs, expecting to land in a soft, unprepared city.

They were met with a storm of improvised, craftsman's fury.

Catapults, designed by Elian and built by the city's carpenters, launched not fire pots, but massive nets filled with Kael's ball bearings, which tangled in their grav-sleds. Archers, trained in the "Endless Arrow" doctrine, fired from the rooftops, their arrows tipped with Borin's razor-sharp ceramic heads.

The invaders' shock was absolute. This was not a soft target. This was a death trap.

Up in the war room, reports were flooding the War Table, the glowing map a chaotic swirl of red and silver icons.

"REPORT! DO IT. DO IT NOW!" William was on his feet, his small fists clenched, his face a mask of furious concentration as he watched the first, brutal clashes unfold.

He saw the first casualties among his people. He saw a section of the wall crumble. His perfect plan had been replaced by the messy, brutal reality of war. He was in a rage, not at the enemy, but at his own powerlessness.

Then, he felt a heavy, steady hand on his shoulder.

"Balance, William. Balance."

It was his father. Elian stood behind him, his presence a calm, grounding force. He was not looking at the map with a general's fury, but with a craftsman's diagnostic eye.

William took a deep, shuddering breath. He forced the panic down. He forced the rage into a cold, sharp point of focus.

"The scout's reports," William said, his voice now steady, controlled. "If you please. What are we facing?"

Ren, who was coordinating the Grey Service from a corner of the room, stepped forward. "They are exactly as the System predicted, Your Highness. Disciplined. Heavily armored. But... predictable. They advance in straight lines. They do not adapt to the terrain. They are a hammer looking for a nail."

"They've breached the West Gate," Valerius reported from his position. "But Joric's men have funneled them into the Weaver's District. A maze of narrow streets and dead ends."

"The anvil is holding," Elian murmured, looking at the map. "Bruised, but holding. They are drawing the invaders in, bleeding them, just as planned." He looked at his son. "The citizens are doing their part. They are buying us time."

William looked at the map. He saw the sacrifice. He saw the courage. He saw the trust they had placed in him.

He would not let them down.

He looked at Valerius. He looked at the Duke. He looked at his father.

"It is time," the twelve-year-old Prince said, his voice now the cold, clear command of a king. "Prepare the hammer."

This is a masterful piece of tactical brilliance from William. He's not just a commander; he's a showman, a director of a grand, deadly performance. He's using the unique, legendary skills of his most powerful allies not as direct weapons, but as the ultimate misdirection.

It's the "look over there!" trick, but on a cosmic, awe-inspiring scale.
