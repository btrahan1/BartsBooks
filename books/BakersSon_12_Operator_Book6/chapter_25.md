## Chapter 25: The Misdirection

The army was in position. Hidden in the rolling hills and deep forests to the east of the capital, a hundred thousand soldiers of the Kingdom of Wonders waited in absolute silence. They were a coiled spring, a silent hammer poised to fall.

In the command tent, William stood before the War Table. The map of the city was a chaotic bloom of red as the invaders pushed deeper, but the silver icons of the citizen-defenders were holding, bending but not breaking, channeling the enemy into the pre-planned kill zones.

"They are committed," Ren's voice reported through a communication stone. "Their command structure has moved inside the city walls. They think the battle is won. They are arrogant."

"Good," William said. He then turned his attention to the two most powerful, and as yet unused, pieces on his board.

He looked at the Duke of Corvan. "Your Grace," he said, his tone one of respectful but absolute command. "You floated down from your box at the tournament. It was very... dramatic. Could you do that floating, levitating thing again? High in the sky, above the city? A big, obvious, and very distracting display of power?"

The Duke, who had been expecting an order to lead a cavalry charge, was taken aback. "A distraction, Your Highness?"

"The biggest distraction," William confirmed. "I want every eye in that city, invader and citizen alike, to be looking up."

He then turned to Finn. "And you, Uncle Finn. Do you think you and Jasmine could do one of those flashy, Lich 'factory' destroying, light-beamy things? Not aimed at the city, of course. Just... up. A pillar of pure, divine, 'look at me' power."

Finn smiled. "I believe we could manage something... noticeable."

William grinned. It was a grin of pure, tactical genius. It was his father's grin.

"I want them all looking up," he said, "when they should have been looking behind them."

***

Minutes later, it began.

High above the besieged city, a figure appeared, wreathed in a powerful, golden aura. The Duke of Corvan, a living god of war, hovered in the sky, a silent, magnificent sentinel.

Then, from the city's highest spire where Finn stood, a beam of pure, incandescent light shot straight into the heavens, a second sun in the afternoon sky.

The effect was instantaneous.

Inside the city, the fighting sputtered to a halt. The invaders, who knew nothing of Ki masters or divine healers, stared at the sky in superstitious terror. What gods were these? The citizens, recognizing the symbols of their own power, let out a ragged, hopeful cheer.

Every eye was turned upward.

And to the east, the ground began to shake.

"Now," Elian's voice rumbled through the command tent.

The hammer fell.

A hundred thousand soldiers of the Kingdom of Wonders crested the hills, a silent, disciplined, and utterly devastating tide of steel and purpose. They were not a chaotic hoard. They were a hundred Fists, a dozen Saws, a score of Schools, all moving as one.

They descended on the invaders' completely undefended rear flank, the silent, deadly hammer falling upon the anvil of the city.

The trap had sprung. And the rats had been too busy looking at the sky to hear the snap.

This is the decapitation strike. The grand strategy has worked perfectly, and now it's time for the personal, heroic moment where the main characters step in to finish the job.

William delegating this "wet work" to his most trusted and powerful allies shows his maturity as a commander. He's not just a strategist; he's a leader who knows how to use his assets.
