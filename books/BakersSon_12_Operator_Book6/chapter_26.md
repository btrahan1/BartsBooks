## Chapter 26: The Decapitation Strike

The battle for the capital was not a battle; it was an annihilation.

The devastation was enormous. The invading army, caught completely by surprise, was now trapped. They had expected a soft city to plunder. Instead, they were caught between two fronts of a war they could not comprehend.

In front of them, the city itself had become a monster. Millions of citizens, their initial fear replaced by a cold, righteous fury, surged from the alleyways and rooftops. They were a chaotic, unstoppable wave of improvised weapons and local knowledge. They were all pissed off.

And behind them, a silent, disciplined army of tens of thousands of battle-hardened soldiers was slaughtering them, using fluid, circular tactics they had never seen. They flowed like water, struck like lightning, and were utterly, terrifyingly efficient. The invaders were terrified. Their rigid lines broke. Their command structure dissolved into chaos.

In the command tent, the War Table was a sea of victorious silver icons overwhelming the panicked red ones. The trap had not just worked; it had worked with a brutal perfection that was almost sickening.

William watched the unfolding victory, his face a mask of cold, grim necessity. The battle was won. But the war was not over until the head of the snake was cut off.

His Grey Service agents, who had been moving invisibly through the chaos, sent their final report. The enemy command—a council of Warlords—was barricaded in the old Guild Hall, surrounded by their elite guard.

William looked at the four men standing silently beside him in the tent. His inner circle. His family. His most powerful weapons.

"Father," he said, looking at Elian.
"Galen," he nodded to the Paladin, his Sun-Mace glowing.
"Finn," he met the healer's calm gaze.
"Tim," he acknowledged the cowled man who had appeared from the shadows, his hands resting on the hilts of his own twin blades.

"The enemy's leadership is cornered in the old Guild Hall," William stated, his voice now the quiet, final command of a king. "It is time to take them out."

He looked at each of them in turn. "If you would be so kind."

The four men didn't speak. They simply nodded.

Elian hefted his Iron-Root staff. Tim drew his silent, deadly blades. Galen's mace flared with holy light. Finn's hands began to glow.

They were not an army. They were not a Fist. They were a force of nature.

They walked out of the command tent and into the heart of the battle, four living legends on their way to end a war.

The craftsman, the paladin, the healer, and the ghost. The enemy command had no idea what was coming for them.
