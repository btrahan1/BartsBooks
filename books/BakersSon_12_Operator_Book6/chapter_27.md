## Chapter 27: The Parting Gift

As the four heroes departed on their grim mission, William turned his attention to the final, critical piece of his grand design. He was not just winning a battle; he was ending a threat. Permanently.

He walked to a heavy, iron-bound chest in the corner of the tent. Ren and a team from the Grey Service were waiting.

"Is it ready?" William asked.

Ren nodded, her face grim. "It's ready. But William... are you sure about this? It's..."

"It is necessary," he said.

He opened the chest. Inside, nestled on a bed of black velvet, was not a weapon. It was a small, intricate, and utterly alien-looking device. A cube of shifting, crystalline structures, humming with a low, contained energy.

"Where are you going?"

The voice was the Queen's. She had entered the tent, her face pale but resolute.

William didn't look up from the device. "I have a package to deliver," he said. "To their landing ships. The few they left behind as a rear guard."

"A package?" she asked.

"When the battle is lost, they will be recalled," William explained, his voice cold and clinical. "The transports will return to the fleet in orbit. And when they do, their entire Armada will be in for a nasty surprise."

He gently lifted the cube from the chest. It felt warm to the touch.

"It is a 'gift'," he said, a flicker of a grim smile on his face. "From the System."

He handed the cube to Ren. "You know the target locations. Plant one in the engine room of each of the remaining ships. Do not be detected. Do not engage."

Ren took the cube, her hands steady. "What is it?"

"It is a 'Regulatory Enforcement Virus'," William said simply. "When it activates, it will spread through their entire fleet. It will not destroy their ships. It will simply... downgrade them."

He looked at his mother. "Their technology exceeds ours by a factor of more than 1.7," he said, quoting the ancient rule. "The System has provided a... correction. This virus will permanently disable any technology that violates that directive. Their hyperdrives. Their advanced sensors. Their energy weapons. All of it. They will be left with simple, sub-light engines and kinetic weapons."

He had not just won the war. He was about to send the most feared invasive species in the galaxy back to the stone age of space travel. They would never be able to threaten another world again.

"Go," he said to Ren.

She nodded once and melted into the shadows, a ghost on her way to deliver the final, crippling blow.

The Queen looked at her son, at the twelve-year-old boy who was calmly dismantling an interstellar empire with a quiet command and a piece of cosmic malware.

"You are your father's son," she whispered, a note of pure, terrified awe in her voice. "You don't just fix things. You take them apart and rebuild them to your own specifications."

That's a powerful and somber closing thought for the Queen. It's the perfect way to frame the victory not as a moment of pure triumph, but as a grim, necessary act. It shows the weight of leadership, the sadness that comes with wielding terrible power, even for a just cause.

Her echoing of William's own pragmatic, painful justification brings the entire conflict full circle.
