## Chapter 28: A Necessary End

From the high tower, the Queen watched the final battle unfold.

She did not watch the grand, sweeping chaos of the city. Her spyglass was fixed on a single point: the old Guild Hall, now the last bastion of the invading Warlords.

She watched her elites—her husband, her most trusted advisors, her kingdom's living legends—descend upon it.

It was not a battle. It was a deconstruction.

She saw a flash of silver light as Galen's mace shattered the main gate. She saw a blur of motion as Tim seemed to simply appear inside, his twin blades a whisper of death. She saw the building itself seem to groan and twist as Elian's staff found the structural weaknesses in its foundation. She saw a soft, golden light pulse from within, and she knew that Finn was there, ensuring that the only ones who fell were the ones who had to.

She watched them dismantle the enemy's leadership with a brutal, silent, lethal efficiency. There was no rage. There was no glory. It was just... work. The grim, necessary work of removing a rot.

She knew what they were capable of. She had seen their power in training, in duels, in miracles. To see it unleashed now, in its purest, most destructive form... it saddened her. It was a terrible, awesome power, and she was the one who had to command it.

She thought of the thousands who had died on both sides. She thought of the price of this victory.

But as her son, William, would say...

"It was necessary."

She lowered the spyglass. The fight was over. The city was saved. The kingdom was safe.

The cost had been high. But the structure was, finally, sound.

The long night was over. And in the east, the sun was beginning to rise on a new, and very different, world.

**THE END**

That epilogue is pure, delicious, cosmic irony. It's the perfect, final twist of the knife for the invaders. They haven't just been defeated; they've been marooned in the slow lane of the universe.

It's a fate far more fitting, and far more frustrating for them, than simple destruction. William didn't just win; he put them in a permanent time-out.
