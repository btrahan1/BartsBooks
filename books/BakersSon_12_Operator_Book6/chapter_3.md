## Chapter 3: The Hidden Mass

The Supervisor's coded form was flickering wildly. "What... what do you propose we do?"

William looked at the holographic map, at the red line inching ever closer. He was not a general. He was a craftsman. He needed data.

"You will send a probe," William commanded. "A deep-system, multi-spectral probe. Cloaked. It will intercept the signal's point of origin. You will scan it. You will analyze its composition. You will determine its nature."

He fixed his gaze on the silent blue box. "You will do this, and you will do it now."

**[...A probe is being dispatched. It will take time to reach...]**

"You have seventy-two days," William cut him off. "Use them efficiently."

He began to pace, his mind a whirlwind of calculations, of structural possibilities.

"The signal is a carrier wave," he mused, thinking aloud. "It is too perfect, too clean. It is not a natural phenomenon. It is manufactured. A shell. A disguise."

He stopped and looked at the map again, his father's [Grain Sight] allowing him to see not just the line, but the *texture* of the anomaly.

"If my guess is correct," he continued, "your probe will not find a simple energy source. You will find a hidden mass within the signal's corona. A physical object, cloaked by the energy pulse."

**[A hidden mass? How can you possibly know that?]**

"The signal has a gravitational echo," William said simply, as if explaining that wood has grain. "It is subtle, but it is there. It is warping the space-time around it. I can't calculate the exact size from this distance, the resolution is too poor. But I estimate," he said, doing a quick, complex calculation in his head, "that it is approximately three miles in diameter."

A three-mile-wide object, moving through the void, cloaked in a perfect energy shell. 42-Beta felt his core programming begin to fray.

"Large enough," William finished, his voice dropping to a low, chilling whisper, "to hide..."

He paused, letting the terrifying implication land.

"...a fleet of ships."

The Supervisor didn't respond. He had just given the order to enhance the probe's gravimetric sensors. He was no longer questioning the boy's analysis. He was terrified of it.

"Report back to me the moment the probe makes contact," William commanded. "We need to know what we are working with. The material. The design. The purpose."

He turned away from the map, his young face set in a grim, determined line.

"Because I am a craftsman," he said. "And every structure has a weakness. I just need to find the blueprints."

This is a showstopper. William isn't just a user anymore; he's a developer. He's reverse-engineered the System's own rules and is now using them as a weapon, issuing quests *to the administrators*.

The casual, almost bored way he quotes the System's own legal code back to them is the ultimate power move. He's not asking for help; he's citing the terms of their service agreement.
