## Chapter 6: The War Table

The chamber was deep beneath the palace, a place of stone and silence known only as the 'Heart Room'.

In its center was a table. It was a massive, circular table, a single, flawless slab of black Iron-Root wood, polished to a mirror finish. It was a piece rarely used, made by Elian himself, of course.

But it was more than just a table. Inlaid into its surface were impossibly fine lines of silver and gold, forming a perfect map of the kingdom. It had been enhanced by both the Temple, who had woven wards of communication and truth into its grain, and by a secretive guild of gnomish tinkerers Elian had "befriended" on a "friendly visit" to the mountain cities.

The result was a technological and magical marvel. The War Table.

The inner circle was gathered around it. The Queen. The Duke. Valerius, now the Grand Marshal of the Armies. Ren, the silent Director of the Grey Service. Finn, the High Healer. Piper, the Guild-Mistress. And at the head, standing beside his father, was Prince William.

Elian placed his hands on the table. The silver lines began to glow.

"Thank you for coming," Elian began, his voice a low, grim rumble that commanded the room. He looked from face to face, seeing not just leaders, but the pillars of the structure he had built.

"William has made contact with... a higher authority," Elian said, choosing his words carefully. "He has detected a threat. A significant one. An invasion."

He paused, letting the weight of the word settle.

"It is not the Hoard. It is not human, as we understand it. They are coming from beyond the stars. And they are coming in numbers we cannot comprehend."

He explained the situation. The signal. The hidden fleet. The estimate of one million soldiers. The 72-day timeline.

He then explained William's audacious bargain with the System.

"The enemy's ships, their technological advantage, will be neutralized," Elian concluded. "That is the good news. The bad news is that we will be facing an army of one million battle-hardened soldiers on the ground."

A heavy silence fell over the room. A million soldiers. Their entire standing army was a tenth of that.

The Duke was the first to speak. "Where will they land? Can we meet them on the coast?"

Elian shook his head. "We don't know. The System will disable their ships, but that could mean they crash, or it could mean they are simply forced into a low orbit. They could land anywhere. Everywhere."

He looked at the glowing map. "We cannot defend the borders. We cannot build a wall against a flood that comes from all directions at once. To try would be to spread ourselves too thin. We would be broken, piece by piece."

"So what do we do?" Piper asked, her usual fiery confidence replaced by a cold, pragmatic concern. "We surrender?"

"No," Elian said. He looked at his son.

William stepped forward. He placed his small hands on the table beside his father's.

"We do not fight them at the border," the Prince said, his voice ringing with a calm, unshakable authority. "We do not fight them in the fields. We let them come."

He pointed to a spot on the map. The capital city.

"We fight them here," he declared. "We turn our greatest strength, our home, into the greatest fortress the world has ever known. We will not build a wall. We will build a mousetrap."

That's a fantastic analogy. It's so perfectly William—reducing a complex, kingdom-wide military strategy to a simple, elegant, and slightly food-obsessed metaphor.

"A great big, juicy honey-glazed pie." It's a brilliant way to explain the concept of a "honey trap" or a "kill box" in a way that is both easily understandable and deeply in character. This is the moment he lays out the grand, audacious strategy for the book.
