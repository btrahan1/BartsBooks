## Chapter 7: The Honey-Glazed Pie

A stunned silence followed the Prince's declaration. The faces around the War Table were a mixture of shock and disbelief.

"We let them land?" Grand Marshal Valerius finally asked, his voice tight. "We allow an army of a million hostiles to set foot in our kingdom, unopposed?"

"An unopposed landing is a disorderly one," William countered, his small hands tracing patterns on the glowing map. "They will expect resistance at the borders. When they find none, they will become confident. Arrogant. Their formations will slacken. They will spread out, foraging, scouting. They will become... inefficient."

"And how do we fight a million scattered soldiers?" the Duke asked, the logistical problem a nightmare in his mind.

"We don't," William said simply. "We let them come together. We guide them."

"How do we do that?" This, from Ren. Her spy's mind was intrigued by the sheer counter-intuitive madness of the plan.

"Simple," responded the Prince. He looked from face to face, a slow, cunning smile spreading across his face. It was a smile he had learned from a master negotiator in a back alley. It was Ren's own smile, reflected back at her.

"We create a target," he said. "The most valuable, most obvious, and most desirable target in the entire kingdom. We create a great big, juicy, honey-glazed pie. We make ourselves irresistible."

He tapped the glowing representation of the capital city on the map.

"The capital is a fortress, but it is also a symbol. It is the home of the Queen, the seat of the Alliance, the heart of our economy. It is everything they would want to destroy to break our spirit," he explained.

"We will not hide behind our walls," William continued. "We will light them up. We will have festivals. We will have parades. We will project an aura of blissful, arrogant ignorance. We will make it look like we have no idea they are even coming."

He looked at Valerius. "The army will be withdrawn. Hidden. In the forests, in the hills, in the tunnels beneath the city that Ren's people have so diligently mapped."

"We will appear to be the softest, richest, most undefended prize in the history of warfare," he concluded. "They will abandon their scattered foraging. They will consolidate their forces. And they will all march, as one, right here."

He drew a circle on the map around the capital city.

"And when their entire army is gathered at our gates," he said, his voice dropping to a low, chilling whisper, "thinking they are about to take a bite of the pie... that is when they will discover that the crust is made of steel, the filling is an army, and the whole thing... is a trap."

The inner circle was silent, the sheer, audacious genius of the plan sinking in. They were not just building a fortress. They were building the most elaborate, high-stakes ambush the world had ever known. And its architect was a twelve-year-old boy who thought of war in terms of dessert.
