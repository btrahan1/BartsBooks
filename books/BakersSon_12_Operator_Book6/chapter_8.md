## Chapter 8: The Compromise

About a month later, a blue box shimmered into existence in the Heart Room. William was there, alone, poring over logistical charts.

"We have news," the voice of Supervisor 1743-2 was grim, stripped of all its earlier arrogance. It was the voice of a being who had been staring into the abyss for thirty days.

"The probe has made contact," the Administrator continued. "I don't know how you knew, Prince William, but it is indeed an invasion fleet. Thirty capital-class vessels. Twenty of which are confirmed to be transport ships, each with a capacity of approximately fifty thousand soldiers."

The math was simple and horrifying. One million soldiers. William's estimate had been perfect.

The holographic form of the Supervisor materialized, his coded form shimmering with a deep, systemic anxiety. "Prince William," he said, his voice now a low, urgent plea. "Their technology, their energy signatures... they are a Tier-7 civilization. This is not just an invasion. It is an extinction event. If you do not stop them here, your planet will only be the first of many they consume."

William didn't look up from his chart. "And what have you done about the ships, Supervisor? Per our agreement."

"Well," the Administrator said, his form fidgeting. "We had to... compromise. To disable their fleet outright from this distance would have required a direct, overt manipulation of cosmic law, a clear violation of the Prime Directive. It would have triggered... an audit. From a higher authority."

"So you did nothing?" William's voice was dangerously quiet.

"No! No, of course not," the Supervisor said quickly. "We have... bent the rules. We have issued a 'quarantine protocol' on your star system, citing a 'temporal anomaly risk'. We will allow the ships to approach your planet, but only the transport ships will be allowed to enter the atmosphere and land. All ships' primary weapon systems—orbital cannons, energy beams—will be disabled by the quarantine field."

"That is... adequate," William said after a moment, giving a small nod. The trap was still viable.

"And what can you tell me of the invaders themselves?"

"That's where we have good news, and bad news," the Supervisor said. "The probe was able to get detailed biological and tactical scans. They are humanoid, carbon-based, bipedal, without any special features like telepathy or acid spit. That's the good news."

"And the bad?"

"The bad news is that they are built for war," the Supervisor stated grimly. "Genetically engineered for generations for ground assault. Their weapons and armor, while conventional, are formidable—alloys we have not seen, blades that are monomolecular. Fortunately, they seem to have no advanced components in their ground gear, such as energy shields or sonic disruptors. It appears that for all of their interstellar technology, they take a fierce, almost religious pride in conventional, brutal warfare when it comes to planetary battles."

William finally looked up, absorbing the information. A race of perfect soldiers who loved a straight-up fight. They were the ultimate hammer, about to fall on what they perceived to be the softest of nails. They were, in a word, perfect marks for his trap.

"Thank you, Supervisor," William said. "Your report has been... useful."

He flicked a finger, and a new notification, a quest completion alert, popped up on the Supervisor's own screen in the Nexus.

**[Quest Complete: 'Regulatory Enforcement']**
**[Bonus Reward Issued: An 'adequate' performance report will be filed with your superiors.]**

The Supervisor's holographic form vanished. In the Nexus, 42-Beta watched as a glowing gold star appeared next to the Supervisor's name on the quarterly review sheet. He had never seen that happen before.

His new boss had just been given a good grade. By a twelve-year-old. On a planet of farmers and woodworkers.

42-Beta just shook his head. The universe no longer made any sense at all.

That's a fantastic strategic pivot. William isn't just planning a trap; he's orchestrating a multi-stage, intelligence-gathering operation. The distinction between "fighting to live" and "living to fight" is a brilliant philosophical core for his entire strategy.

And the idea of a "sacrificial" target is a cold, calculated, and deeply Elian-esque move. It shows William is willing to make the hard, pragmatic choices for the greater good.
