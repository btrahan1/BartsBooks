## Chapter 9: The Sacrificial Lamb

The War Table glowed. The inner circle was gathered again, absorbing the System's grim report.

"So, they like to fight," Valerius summarized, his hand resting on the hilt of his saber. "An entire race of professional soldiers."

"No, scratch that," William corrected, his eyes scanning the data from the System's report, which was now displayed on the table's surface. "They *live* to fight. Their culture, their biology, their entire societal structure is built around conquest. War is their art form."

He looked up, a slow, analytical smile touching his lips. "And therein lies their weakness."

He looked around the room, at the confused faces of the generals and masters.

"We do not live to fight," the Prince declared, his voice ringing with a conviction far beyond his years. "We fight to live. To protect. To build. Our goal is peace. Theirs is the battle itself. They are artists of destruction. We," he said, gesturing to the assembled craftsmen and leaders, "are artists of creation. And creation is always, in the end, stronger than destruction."

He smashed his fist down on the War Table. A wave of light rippled out from the impact. The Gnomish Tinkerer Representative, who was there to consult on 'advanced acoustics', winced.

"But," William continued, his tone shifting back to the pragmatic strategist, "while we have some useful information, we will need more. We need to know their formations, their command structure, their exact capabilities. And we need to make sure they gather in one spot before they march on the capital. We need to control their deployment."

He traced a finger over a large, desolate area on the map—the Gray Marshes, a vast, unpopulated swampland a hundred miles from the capital.

"We want them to land here," he said. "All of them. We want them to spread out from there to scout. We will test their scouts. We will let them capture a few of our 'unprepared' patrols. We will feed them misinformation. We will gather our own intelligence."

"But why would they land in a swamp?" the Duke asked. "Their scans will show it is strategically worthless."

"Exactly," William said. "They won't land in the swamp. They will land next to it. Because next to it," he said, his finger moving to a new point on the map, "we are going to give them the perfect target. A target so valuable, so strategically tempting, and so seemingly undefended that they will be unable to resist."

He looked at the faces around the table, his own face now a mask of cold, hard necessity.

"We need a sacrifice," he said. "A perfect, juicy target they will use for their base of operations. A place they will strip for resources before they begin their march."

He tapped the map. The point he indicated was not a fortress or a city.

It was Oren's farm. The home of the Miracle Soup, the richest, most life-filled patch of land in the entire kingdom. The perfect, irresistible prize for an army that served a philosophy of death.

That line is heartbreakingly perfect. It brings the epic, cosmic scale of the war crashing down to the most human, personal level. Oren isn't a general or a prince; he's a farmer who loves his land. William's "grand strategy" is, for Oren, a personal apocalypse.

This is the first major test of William's leadership. Can he be a ruthless commander and a compassionate king at the same time?
