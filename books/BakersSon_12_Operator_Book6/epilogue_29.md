## Epilogue One: The Long Road Home

Aboard the flagship of the invasion fleet, floating in a high, stable orbit, Warlord Krax stared at the tactical display. It was a sea of red icons. Every ground unit was either "lost" or "in retreat." The capital city, which should have been a smoldering ruin, was a beacon of triumphant, silver light.

The battle was lost. The ground invasion was a catastrophic failure.

"Recall the transports," Krax snarled, his voice a low growl of fury. "All units, fall back to the fleet. Prepare for orbital bombardment. We will burn this insolent world from the sky."

"My Lord," a trembling technician reported, "the primary weapon systems... they are offline. All of them."

"What?" Krax roared.

"And... my Lord," the technician continued, his voice barely a whisper. "The hyperdrive... it is... gone. The navigation system is reporting only... sub-light capabilities."

Just then, the battered, smoking transport ships began to return, docking with the flagship.

A frantic, terrified captain burst onto the bridge. "Warlord Krax! The ground... it was a trap! We were slaughtered! We must leave! Now!"

Krax grabbed the captain by the collar. "What do you mean we can't leave? We're going home! And when we return with the Grand Armada, heads will roll for this!"

The chief engineer, a man whose face was pale as death, stepped forward, holding a diagnostic slate.

"Ahem," the engineer began, his voice trembling. "Sir... about that. I have run a full diagnostic on the fleet's propulsion systems." He looked at the slate, then back at the Warlord.

"At our current, maximum sub-light pace," he said, swallowing hard, "factoring in relativistic drift and the need to navigate known asteroid fields... it will take us... approximately... seven thousand years to get home."

The bridge was silent.

The Warlord stared. Seven... thousand... years.

He had not just been defeated. He had been castrated. Marooned. He was the commander of the most feared fleet in the galaxy, and he had just been grounded, an eternity from home.

He let go of the captain and sank slowly into his command chair, the weight of seven thousand years of slow, boring, and utterly humiliating failure crashing down upon him.

The war was over. The Kingdom of Wonders was safe. And the invaders... the invaders were just beginning their very, very long punishment.

That final line is a punch to the gut. It's the moment the mask of the commander slips, and we see the twelve-year-old boy underneath, exhausted by the weight of his own victory.

The shift from the imperious "Do it now!" to the weary, gentle "Do it... when you are ready" is a sign of profound growth and empathy. He's not just a strategist anymore; he's a king who understands the cost. It's a beautiful, somber, and perfect end note.
