## Chapter 1: The Rise

The village of Riverwood woke up to the crowing of roosters, but Bran woke up to the smell of fermentation.

It was 4:00 AM. The air in the *Golden Crust Bakery* was thick, warm, and smelled faintly of sour yeast—a scent Bran preferred to perfume.

"Right then," Bran whispered, rolling his shoulders. A sickening *pop-crack* echoed through the silent kitchen as his joints settled. "Time to make the donuts."

He moved to the corner of the room to retrieve the sack of flour. It was a fifty-pound sack of "Miller’s Heavy," a rough grain used for the cheap breakfast loaves. Bran grabbed it by the ear of the burlap with one hand, swung it over his shoulder like it was a throw pillow, and dumped it into the mixing trough.

"My Daddy always says, *'Respect the grain, and the grain respects your gut,'*" he muttered to himself.

He began the mix. This was the part he loved. The silence, the rhythm, the resistance. Most bakers used a wooden paddle to start the mix, but Bran used his hands. He liked to feel the moment the gluten strands began to tighten. As the water hit the flour, the mixture turned into a thick, glue-like paste. Bran churned it. His forearms, dusted white, bulged as he rotated the heavy sludge.

By 6:00 AM, the ovens were roaring.
By 7:00 AM, the shelves were stacked.
By 7:30 AM, Bran was loading the Delivery Tower.

"Bran! You're stacking it too high again!" his father, Thomas, called out from the front counter, where he was arranging tarts.

"It saves time, Dad!" Bran called back. "One trip means the buns are still warm when they get to the Inn!"

Bran finished tying the twine around the stack. It consisted of six large wooden crates, stacked vertically. It was roughly seven feet of solid oak and bread. A normal man would need a handcart.

Bran squatted down. He didn't use a cart. The wheels on the old handcart bounced too much and bruised the fruit tarts.

"Lift with the legs, not the back. *'A baker with a broken back makes flat bread,'*" Bran recited.

He hooked his hands under the bottom crate, took a breath, and stood up. The tower of crates rose with him, steady as a rock. He balanced the towering structure against his chest and chin, peering around the side to see where he was going.

"Bye, Dad!"

"Be careful of the—" Thomas started.

"—mud puddle by the blacksmith’s, I know!"

***

The village square was just waking up. Merchants were opening their stalls, and the early morning chill still hung in the air.

Bran walked with a smooth, gliding step. He had learned to walk in a way that kept his upper body perfectly still, isolating the movement in his legs so the pastries wouldn't jostle. It looked ridiculous—a boy half-buried under a tower of crates—but he moved with the grace of a temple dancer.

"Morning, Bran!"

Bran stopped, balancing the several hundred pounds of wood and wheat effortlessly. He peeked around the side of the crates.

It was Lira and her friends, gathered by the village well. Lira was the weaver's daughter, known for having the prettiest smile in Riverwood. She was currently twisting a lock of hair around her finger and staring at Bran’s arms, which were flexed to hold the bottom crate.

"Oh, morning, Lira," Bran said, smiling shyly. "Morning, girls."

"That looks... heavy," Lira said, her voice dropping an octave.

"Oh, this?" Bran shifted his weight. The muscles in his calves rippled. "Nah. It's mostly air. Bread is fluffy, you know? It's the yeast. It lifts everything up."

The girls exchanged a look. One of them giggled.

"Do you... need help?" Lira asked, batting her eyelashes.

"That's kind of you," Bran said earnestly, "but you have nice clean dresses on. You don't want flour on you. Plus, the weaving guild needs your nimble fingers. *'Rough hands for rough work, soft hands for silk,'* my Dad always says."

He nodded politely and continued on his way, leaving a group of disappointed but admiring girls in his wake.

He turned the corner toward the *Iron Anvil Smithy*. He had to drop off a savory meat pie for Old Man Grog.

As he approached, he heard cursing. Loud, creative cursing.

Bran rounded the bend to see Grog, a burly man with arms like tree trunks, red-faced and sweating. He was trying to lift a heavy iron axle onto a wagon mount, but the angle was awkward, and the mud was slippery.

"Morning, Mr. Grog!" Bran chirped.

Grog grunted, straining. "Can't talk, lad. Slipping. Gonna... drop it..."

Bran frowned. He couldn't put the crates down; the ground was muddy, and the bottom crate had the crusty rolls.

"Hang on," Bran said.

He walked up to the wagon. "Excuse me, Mr. Grog. If you just slide to the left..."

Bran stepped in. He kept the tower of crates balanced on his left shoulder and chest, clamping them tight with his left arm. He reached out with his free right hand and grabbed the iron axle.

"Just like flipping a baguette," Bran whispered.

He gripped the cold iron. His fingers, strengthened by years of pinching stiff rye dough, dug into the metal. He lifted.

With a grunt of effort—mostly to keep his balance in the mud—Bran hoisted the heavy iron axle up and slotted it onto the wagon mount with a metallic *clank*.

Grog stared. He looked at the heavy axle. He looked at Bran’s single arm. He looked at the tower of bread crates still balanced on Bran's other shoulder.

"Thanks, lad," Grog wheezed, wiping grease on his apron. "That thing weighs near ten stone."

"It's all about leverage, Mr. Grog," Bran said cheerfully, using his now-free hand to fish a meat pie out of his pocket. "Here's your breakfast. Careful, it's still hot. *'A cold pie is a sorrowful thing.'*"

Bran turned and walked away, humming a little tune.

Grog watched him go, then looked at his own massive biceps, then back at the boy. "What in the hell are they putting in that flour?"

***

The final stop was the *Tipsy Griffin Inn*. This was the big delivery.

Bran kicked the back door open with his foot (carefully, so as not to scuff the wood) and navigated the narrow hallway into the kitchen.

"Order up!" Bran called.

Martha, the head cook, turned around. She was a formidable woman who frightened most of the town guard, but her face softened when she saw Bran.

"There's my favorite boy," she boomed. "Set 'em down, love."

Bran lowered the stack. He didn't drop it; he squatted all the way down until the bottom crate kissed the floor without a sound.

"Fresh sourdough, three dozen baps, and the sweet rolls for the Mayor's lunch," Bran listed off, wiping his hands on his apron.

"You're a lifesaver, Bran," Martha said, handing him a glass of cold milk. "I don't know how you haul that lot every morning. The last boy we hired to carry wood threw his back out lifting a keg."

Bran drank the milk in one gulp. "It's not so bad. It’s just like kneading, but with your legs. Keeps the blood moving."

"You're too humble," Martha said, leaning in. "You know, there's a group of Adventurers in the common room. Real ones. From the Capital. They were asking about the bread at dinner last night. Said it gave them a 'buff'. Whatever that means."

Bran tilted his head. "A buff? Is that like a polish?"

"I think it means it made them feel strong," Martha laughed. "They wanted to know who the Alchemist was."

Bran laughed, a clear, honest sound. "Alchemist? It's just flour, water, salt, and yeast, Martha. And a little bit of love. *'The heart is the hidden ingredient,'* Dad says."

"Well," Martha winked. "You might want to steer clear of the main room. They looked like the recruiting type, and I know your Dad needs you here."

"Don't worry," Bran said, picking up his empty crates. "I'm not going anywhere. Who would feed the starter culture if I left? Besides, adventure sounds tiring. I like my bed, and I like my oven."

He headed for the door, pausing to check the frame. "Oh, Martha? Your door hinge is loose. It squeaks. *'A squeaky door scares the soufflé.'* I'll bring some oil tomorrow."

As Bran walked back out into the sunlight, he felt content. He had his town, his routine, and his dough. He had absolutely no idea that the "Adventurers" inside were currently arguing over whether the bread they just ate contained a minor healing potion, or that his life of quiet baking was about to rise into something much more complicated.

This is the perfect place to slow down. If Chapter 1 was **Strength and Routine**, Chapter 2 should be **Precision, Heart, and Motivation.**

To make the eventual contract with the Sect feel earned (and necessary), we need to establish two things:
1.  **Bran is an artist, not just a laborer.** He has high Dexterity/Perception, not just Strength.
2.  **The Bakery is struggling.** Not because the bread is bad, but because the world is harsh. This gives Bran a reason to leave his beloved home when the money is offered.
