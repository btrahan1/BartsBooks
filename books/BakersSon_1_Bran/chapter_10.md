## Chapter 9: The Morning Shadow

Elder Feng stood on the precipice of the Green Peak, facing the rising sun. The wind whipped his robes, but he stood immovable, a statue of serene power.

He began the "Dance of the Verdant Stalk."

It was a slow, fluid martial form. His hands moved like leaves drifting in the wind. His legs were rooted like ancient oak. With every movement, the Qi in the air swirled, visible as faint green ribbons drawn to his core.

Then, he heard a grunt.

*Hup. Hah.*

Feng didn't break his concentration, but he expanded his awareness.

Ten feet behind him, Bran was moving.

He wasn't doing the form perfectly. His stance was too wide. His hands were cupped as if he were holding a heavy bowl. But the *rhythm*... the rhythm was identical.

Feng moved his arm left. Bran moved his arm left.
Feng sank into a crouch. Bran sank into a crouch.

Feng finished the set and turned around slowly. Bran was wiping sweat from his brow with a towel.

"Good morning, Elder," Bran said cheerfully. "Good workout. Gets the stiffness out of the hips."

"You are mimicking the Sacred Forms," Feng observed. "That is forbidden for non-disciples."

"I'm not mimicking," Bran corrected. "I'm stretching. My Dad always said, *'A stiff baker makes tough bread.'* Besides, your moves... they make sense."

"Oh?" Feng raised an eyebrow. "Explain."

Bran demonstrated the first move—a sweeping arm motion meant to deflect energy.

"This one," Bran said. "It's just like spreading flour on a large table. You have to be even, or you get clumps."

He demonstrated the second move—a sharp, double-palm strike.

"And this? This is how you punch down the sourdough after the second rise. Quick, firm, don't tear the gluten."

Feng stared at him. For a moment, he was offended. To compare the Holy Arts to kitchen chores was blasphemy. But then, he replayed the motions in his mind.

*Spread the flour. Punch the dough.*

The mechanics were... disturbingly accurate. The intent was different, but the physics were the same.

"Bran," Feng said. "Why do you join me here?"

"Because it's quiet," Bran said, looking out over the clouds. "And because you don't look at me like I'm a monster."

Feng chuckled. "You are an anomaly, Bran. But perhaps not a monster."

Feng walked to the edge of the cliff. "The Sect Tournament approaches. As an employee, you are not required to fight. But the Council is pressing me. They want to see what you are."

"I'm a baker," Bran said stubbornly. "Read the contract. Paragraph 4, Subsection B: 'Duties restricted to culinary preparation and heavy lifting.'"

"I know the contract," Feng said. "I wrote it. But contracts can be... amended. If you enter, I will grant you access to the Royal Archives. I suspect there are recipes there from the Golden Age. Recipes for bread that never goes stale. Bread that heals wounds."

Bran’s ears perked up. "Healing bread?"

"Think about it," Feng said. He turned to leave. "Join me tomorrow. But fix your stance. Your left foot is dragging."

"It's the boots," Bran muttered as the Elder walked away. "No arch support."
