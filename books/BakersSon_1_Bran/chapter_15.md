## Chapter 14: The Well-Oiled Kitchen

"Move!" Bran’s voice boomed across the kitchen, authoritative but not angry. "Station Four, your water is boiling over! Heat control is discipline! *'A watched pot never boils, but an ignored pot ruins the soup!'*"

A young disciple rushed to lower the magical flame. "Sorry, Chef Bran!"

"Don't be sorry, be steady!" Bran corrected, sliding a tray of fifty perfectly shaped loaves into the massive stone oven.

The Outer Kitchen had changed.

Gone were the screams, the chaotic running, and the burnt garlic smell. The kitchen now moved like a clock. Bran had reorganized the stations. He had implemented an assembly line—The Prep Line, The Cook Line, The Plating Line.

He had taught the disciples that cooking wasn't a chore; it was a form of alchemy.

"Jin!" Bran called out. "Status on the Spirit-Stew?"

Jin, who used to be a terrified, scrawny kid, was now standing at the main cauldron. He wasn't cowering. He was stirring a ladle the size of a shovel with confident strokes.

"Broth has reduced by 10%!" Jin reported. "Viscosity is perfect. The Mana-Root has dissolved completely."

"Good," Bran nodded. "Hold it there. Don't let it thicken. We want a glaze, not a paste."

Bran moved down the line. He stopped at the pastry station.

Mei was there. She was no longer struggling with whisking. Her arms, though still slender, had developed defined, ropy muscles. She was kneading dough for the dumplings.

*Push. Fold. Turn.*

Her rhythm was identical to Bran's.

"Looking good, Mei," Bran said. "Shoulders down."

"Shoulders down," she repeated, adjusting instantly. "Bran, the new flour shipment... it's heavy on Spirit Qi. It’s fighting the yeast."

"Then let it rest longer," Bran advised. "It's just energetic. Like a puppy. Let it tire itself out, then punch it down."

Mei giggled. "Yes, Chef."

***

Outside the kitchen, the effects of Bran’s "regime" were rippling through the Outer Sect.

On the training grounds, the Outer Disciples were sparring. Usually, by mid-afternoon, they were exhausted, their Qi depleted, their movements sloppy.

But today? Today they were glowing.

A disciple landed a complex kick, shattering a wooden dummy. He landed, breathing hard but smiling.

"I don't know what was in those breakfast buns," he panted to his partner, "but my core feels... full. Like a furnace."

"It was the Honey-Glaze," his partner replied, wiping sweat. "I heard Bran infused it with Golden Sun Yeast."

"The stuff from the Inner Court? How did he get that?"

"Don't ask. Just eat."

Elder Feng stood on the observation deck, watching the sparring. Beside him stood the Sect’s Combat Instructor, a scarred man named Iron-Fist Han.

"This is unusual," Han grunted, crossing his arms. "The passing rate for the endurance trials is up 40% this month. Injuries are down. Recovery time is halved."

"Indeed," Feng murmured, a small smile playing on his lips.

"And their form," Han noted, squinting. "Watch that boy there. The way he shifts his weight before striking."

Feng watched. The disciple planted his feet, rotated his hips, and delivered a palm strike. It was grounded. Solid.

"It looks like... he is kneading dough," Han said, sounding confused. "He is using the earth to push, not just his muscles."

"The Baker's influence," Feng said. "He teaches them that power comes from the foundation. From the feet. From the rhythm."

"It works," Han admitted grudgingly. "It's not pretty. It lacks elegance. But by the ancestors, it hits hard."

Feng looked toward the kitchen, where smoke—sweet, savory smoke—was rising into the sky.

"This tournament will be interesting," Feng said. "The Inner Court expects to sweep the floor with these 'trash' disciples. They are in for a very rude awakening."

***

Inside the kitchen, the lunch rush ended. The disciples slumped against the counters, tired but satisfied.

Bran clapped his hands. The sound was like a gunshot.

"Alright!" Bran announced. "Good service. Clean down your stations. Remember: *'A dirty knife is a dangerous knife.'*"

"Yes, Chef!" the kitchen chorused.

Bran walked over to the prep table where he had left his uniform. The white jacket hung there, pristine. The heavy rolling pin leaned against the wall.

He picked up the jacket. He ran his thumb over the brass buttons.

Tomorrow was the Opening Ceremony.

"Bran?"

He turned. It was Mei and Jin. They had taken off their aprons.

"Are you ready?" Jin asked.

"For the fighting?" Bran asked.

"No," Mei said seriously. "For the snacks. We prepared the portable rations you asked for. The Energy-Bars."

She held up a tray of dense, oat-based bars packed with nuts, dried spirit-fruit, and honey. They were ugly, brick-like things.

Bran picked one up. He sniffed it.

"Smells like victory," Bran grinned. He looked at his two friends. "You guys are competing too, right? In the Junior Bracket?"

"Yeah," Jin swallowed nervously. "I drew a Sword Cultivator from the Azure Hall for my first match."

"Sword Cultivator," Bran mused. "Flashy. Lots of jumping. Probably skips breakfast."

Bran put a hand on Jin’s shoulder.

"Just remember the soup, Jin. Don't let him thicken. Keep him moving. Keep stirring him until he gets tired. Then..."

"Then glaze him?" Jin asked.

"Then crush him," Bran corrected. "Like a clove of garlic."

Jin nodded, a fierce light entering his eyes. "Yes, Chef."

Bran put on his hat. He looked at his reflection in the polished side of a cauldron. He didn't look like a warrior. He didn't look like a mage.

He looked like a Baker who was about to deal with a very large, very unruly customer complaint.

"Let's go," Bran said. "We have a tournament to cater."

That is a clever touch with the bracelet. If it were static, people would get suspicious when his stats *didn't* change after months of training. By having it "grow" incrementally, it maintains the illusion that he is a normal, hard-working laborer who is just slowly getting stronger, rather than a monstrous anomaly. It keeps his cover intact while allowing him to be impressive in a "believable" way.

Also, having him cater the tournament *while* waiting to fight is pure gold. It reinforces his priorities.

Let’s move to **Chapter 15: The Opening Ceremony**.
