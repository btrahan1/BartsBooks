## Chapter 16: The Lesson of the Loaf

The gong rang.

The crowd screamed for blood.

Disciple Li didn't wait. He exploded off the starting line, his blue aura flaring like a gas burner set to high.

"DIE!"

Li unleashed a *Wind-Blade Slash*. A crescent of visible, razor-sharp air tore across the arena floor, carving a groove in the stone. It was fast. It was lethal.

Bran stood still. He didn't have speed skills. He had timing.

He watched the arc of the wind. He waited until the last possible second—the moment the dough was about to burn—and he pivoted.

He didn't dodge away; he stepped *into* the curve, letting the wind blade pass inches from his chest. The force of it ruffled his white jacket, but didn't cut the silk.

Li was already there, following up the ranged attack with a thrust aimed at Bran’s throat.

Bran brought the Rolling Pin up. He didn't swing it like a club. He used it like a shield, angling the polished wood to deflect the steel.

*SKREEEE!*

Sparks flew. The "Gravity Stone" core of the rolling pin absorbed the kinetic energy of the strike, deadening the impact.

Li stumbled, his momentum carried away by the deflection. He was wide open. His entire right side was exposed.

Bran could have smashed Li’s ribs. A single swing of the heavy pin would have shattered bone.

Instead, Bran reached out with his free hand and tapped Li firmly on the shoulder.

"Too extended," Bran said. "You're leaning. Center yourself."

Li spun around, wild-eyed, slashing blindly. Bran stepped back, calm as a pond.

"Don't mock me!" Li roared. He launched a flurry of strikes—high, low, high. It was the *Storm of Thousand Cuts* technique.

Bran parried. *Clack. Clack. Clack.*

It looked like he was rolling out a particularly stubborn piece of pastry. He blocked the strikes with minimal movement, keeping the rolling pin close to his body.

"Your breathing is jagged," Bran commented, blocking a strike that would have taken his head off. "In, out. In, out. You're gasping like a fish."

"I am a Cultivator!" Li screamed, channeling more power. His skin began to turn red. Steam rose from his shoulders. He was burning his own blood essence to fuel the attacks. "I am power!"

He jumped high into the air, bringing his sword down in a two-handed smash—the *Mountain Cleaving Strike*.

Bran looked up. He saw the opening. Li was mid-air. He had no leverage. Bran could have simply stepped forward and uppercut him. It would have ended the match instantly.

Bran didn't. He stepped aside.

Li slammed into the ground. *BOOM.* Stone chips flew.

Li tried to recover, but he was stuck in the crater he had made. He looked up, expecting the finishing blow.

Bran was standing there, leaning on his rolling pin. waiting.

"Get up," Bran said.

Li scrambled to his feet, panting, sweat stinging his eyes. "Why? Why didn't you strike?"

"Because you're messy," Bran said. "You're rushing. You broke through to Foundation Establishment last week, didn't you?"

Li blinked. "Yes. So what?"

"Too fast," Bran shook his head. "My Daddy always said, *'A loaf that rises too fast is full of hot air.'* It looks big, but when you cut it, it's hollow. It crumbles."

Li froze. The words hit him harder than the rolling pin could have.

He felt it. He felt the hollowness in his core. The shakiness in his meridians. He had taken pills to rush his advancement. He had skipped the foundational exercises to gain power quickly.

"I..." Li faltered. "I needed the strength."

"Strength without structure is just weight," Bran said. He dropped into a stance. A proper one. His feet rooted. His center low. "Try again. But this time, don't jump. Don't scream. Just strike. Use your hips."

The crowd was silent. They had expected a massacre. Instead, they were watching a seminar.

Li looked at his sword. He looked at Bran. The red rage in his eyes began to fade, replaced by confusion, and then... focus.

He took a deep breath. *In. Out.*

He settled into a stance. He stopped burning his blood essence. The blue aura calmed down, becoming smaller but denser.

"Okay," Li whispered.

He stepped forward. No scream. He thrust the sword.

It was slower, but it was heavier. It had intent.

Bran blocked it, but this time, he actually had to brace himself. The impact traveled up his arm.

"Better," Bran nodded.

Li attacked again. A horizontal slash. Bran ducked.
A vertical chop. Bran sidestepped.

They traded blows for ten minutes. It wasn't a fight anymore; it was a rhythm.

*Strike. Block. Turn. Strike.*

Li was sweating, his muscles burning, but he felt... good. He felt connected to the ground. He felt the sword as an extension of his arm, not just a tool for violence.

Finally, Li lunged. It was his best strike yet. Perfect form.

Bran smiled.

He caught the sword with the rolling pin, trapping the blade against the wood. With a flick of his wrist—a motion used to flip heavy dough—he twisted.

The sword flew out of Li’s hand. It spun in the air and landed, point-down, in the dirt ten feet away.

Li stood there, empty-handed. His chest heaved.

Bran lowered his rolling pin.

"That was a good strike," Bran said. "Solid crust."

Li looked at his empty hands. He looked at Bran. He realized that Bran could have disarmed him at any moment in the last ten minutes. He realized that the "Baker" had just given him the best training session of his life, in front of the entire Sect.

The referee stepped forward. "Disarmed! The winner is—"

"Wait," Li said.

He walked over to his sword. He pulled it from the ground.

The crowd tensed. Was he going to attack again?

Li sheathed the sword.

He turned to Bran. He bowed. deeply. A full ninety-degree bow of respect.

"Thank you... Teacher," Li said, his voice trembling slightly. "You were right. I was hollow."

Bran blinked, surprised. He bowed back, awkwardly. "Uh. You're welcome. Just... maybe eat some carbs next time. You look pale."

Li straightened up and looked at the referee.

"I yield," Li announced.

The crowd erupted. Not in bloodlust, but in shock. A Foundation Establishment disciple yielding to a kitchen worker?

Bran waved to the crowd, feeling his face heat up. He walked over to Li.

"Hey," Bran said softly. "Come by the cart later. I saved a meat pie for you. It's got extra protein."

Li managed a weak smile. "I... I would like that."

As Bran walked off the field, wiping flour from his combat jacket, the Whispers were different again.

*"Did you see that?"*
*"He didn't just beat him. He fixed him."*
*"What kind of style is that?"*
*"The Dao of the Oven..."*

Up in the VIP box, the Sword Elder was silent. He looked at Elder Feng.

"Your baker," the Sword Elder grunted. "He understands the fundamentals better than my own instructors."

Feng smiled, sipping his tea. "He understands that everything needs time to rise. Even a swordsman."

Bran returned to his food cart. Mei and Jin were staring at him with their mouths open.

"Service is up!" Bran announced, grabbing a pair of tongs. "Who ordered the Spicy-Buns?"
