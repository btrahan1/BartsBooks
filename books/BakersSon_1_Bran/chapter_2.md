## Chapter 2 Narrative Goals

**1. The "Soft" Touch (Dexterity):**
We know he can lift a wagon, but can he frost a wedding cake? Yes. This establishes that his "cultivation" is balanced. He has surgical precision.
*   *Scene Idea:* He is working on a complex "Sugar-Glass" sculpture for a cake. It requires holding his breath and moving his hands with millimeter precision.

**2. The Best Friend (The Foil):**
Introduce **Kael**, the son of the Town Guard Captain. Kael is obsessed with Adventurers, levels, and skills. He is the audience surrogate who points out how weird Bran is, while Bran remains dismissive.
*   *Dynamic:* Kael wants to leave; Bran wants to stay. Kael trains with a sword; Bran trains with a whisk. Kael loses to Bran in "play wrestling," which frustrates Kael to no end.

**3. The Shadow of Debt (The Stakes):**
We need to see the cracks in the foundation. The flour merchant has raised prices because of "Sect Taxes." The oven needs new fire-bricks (which are expensive). Thomas’s health is failing—specifically his back or his lungs (Baker's Asthma).
*   *The Hook:* Bran overhears his dad counting coins and sighing. This plants the seed: *I need to help my father.*

**4. Bran's "Baker's Sight" (Perception):**
Bran analyzes people like ingredients. He notices a customer is sick because their skin looks "gray like under-proofed dough." He notices a guard is lying because he's "sweating like cheese in the sun."

### Sample Scene: The Sugar Tower & The Rusty Sword

**Chapter 2: The Delicate Touch**

The midday rush was over, and the *Warm Hearth* had settled into a lazy, golden afternoon silence. Dust motes danced in the shafts of light cutting through the windows.

Bran sat on a stool at the back prep table, holding a cone of parchment paper filled with royal icing. He wasn't moving. In fact, he was barely breathing.

In front of him was the Mayor’s birthday cake. It was a three-tiered monstrosity of sponge and jam, but the Mayor’s wife had requested a decoration of "Spun Sugar Swans."

"Steady..." Bran whispered.

His hand, the same hand that had crushed a fifty-pound bag of salt earlier that morning, hovered over the cake with the lightness of a butterfly. He squeezed. A thread of sugar, thinner than a hair, drifted down.

Bran’s eyes narrowed. He entered the *Zone*. The world fell away. He could feel the viscosity of the sugar, the humidity of the air, the slight vibration of a wagon passing outside. He compensated for it all instantly.

*Loop. Twist. Pull. Snip.*

He created a lattice wing structure that defied gravity. It was physically impossible for normal sugar to hold that shape, but Bran had whipped the mixture until the molecular bonds were screaming in submission.

"Boo!"

Bran didn't flinch. He didn't even blink. He finished the swan's beak, pulled the bag away with a flourish, and then turned around slowly.

"Hello, Kael," Bran said.

Kael, a lanky boy with messy red hair and a wooden practice sword strapped to his belt, leaned against the doorframe, looking disappointed. "You never jump. It's unnatural. I have *Stealth* Level 2 now, you know."

"You smell like pine resin and sword polish," Bran said, wiping the icing tip. "And you step heavy on your left foot. *'A heavy foot collapses the soufflé,'* Kael."

Kael rolled his eyes and walked in, grabbing a stray cookie from a cooling rack. "You and your dad’s sayings. Anyway, look at this!"

Kael drew his wooden sword. He fell into a clumsy stance, knees bent too deep, sword wavering. "The Town Guard Captain showed me the 'Stance of the Iron Mountain.' Come on, hit me. Try to move me."

Bran sighed, standing up to stretch. His apron tightened across his chest. "Kael, I have to finish the glazing."

"Just one push! I want to test my stability stat."

Bran wiped his hands on a rag. He walked over to Kael. Kael braced himself, gritting his teeth, his face turning red with exertion as he engaged every muscle in his scrawny body.

Bran reached out and gently poked Kael in the forehead with one finger.

Kael stumbled backward, flailing, and fell into a sack of onions.

"You're leaning too far forward," Bran critiqued, offering a hand to pull him up. "Your center of gravity is off. You're like a loaf that rose too fast on one side—you'll topple over in the oven."

Kael groaned, dusting onion skins off his tunic. "It's not fair. You don't even have a Class yet. How is your Strength so high? Have you been secretly killing slimes in the sewer?"

"I knead dough, Kael. It's resistance training."

"It's cheat codes, is what it is," Kael muttered. Then, his face turned serious. "Hey, did you hear? The *Verdant Grain Sect* is in town. They're at the inn."

Bran went back to his cake. "So? Just more hungry mouths."

"Not just mouths, Bran. *Cultivators*. People who can punch through stone walls. They say Elder Feng is with them. He's looking for recruits." Kael’s eyes shone. "Imagine if he picked me. I could leave this backwater. I could be a Sword Saint."

Bran carefully placed a sugar cherry on the cake. "Why would you want to punch a stone wall? Seems like a good way to ruin your hands."

"To be a hero! To have power! Don't you ever want to... I don't know, go see what's over the mountains?"

Bran looked out the window. He saw the rolling hills, the river where he fished on Sundays, and the smoke rising from his neighbors' chimneys. He looked at his father, Thomas, who was sitting by the counter, rubbing his lower back with a grimace of pain.

Thomas had been moving slower lately. The cough was getting worse, the "Miller's Lung" finally catching up to him after forty years of breathing flour dust. And last night, Bran had seen the "Final Notice" letter from the Tax Collector hidden under the ledger.

"No," Bran said softly, turning back to his work. "I don't need to see the mountains. I have everything I need right here. Besides, if I left, who would help Dad with the morning mix? His back is acting up again."

Kael slumped. "You're boring, Bran. You're the strongest guy in Riverwood, and you're boring."

"Boring is good," Bran said, picking up the icing bag again. "Boring means the bread is consistent. *'Excitement is just a fancy word for burnt toast.'*"

Kael grabbed another cookie. "Well, enjoy your toast. I'm going to spy on the Sect members. Maybe I can learn a technique just by watching them eat."

As Kael ran out, Bran’s smile faded. He watched his father struggle to stand up from the stool. Thomas let out a sharp hiss of breath, clutching his spine.

Bran stared at his own hands—large, steady, and strong. Kael was right about one thing. He *was* strong.

*Strong enough to crush dough,* Bran thought bitterly. *But not strong enough to fix a bad back or pay a tax collector.*

He didn't know it yet, but "excitement" was about to kick in his front door, and burnt toast would be the least of his worries.
