## Chapter 3: The Taste of Opportunity

The bell above the door didn’t just chime; it shivered.

The air in the bakery changed instantly. It grew heavier, charged with static, like the moments before a thunderstorm breaks. The chatter of the morning regulars died out. Old Man Grog lowered his meat pie.

Three men walked in.

They wore robes of emerald green silk, embroidered with patterns of wheat stalks that seemed to sway in an invisible breeze. They were clean—impossibly clean for a muddy village road. But it was the man in the center who commanded the room.

Elder Feng. He was shorter than the others, with a neatly trimmed white beard and eyes that scanned the room like a hawk looking for field mice.

"The *Warm Hearth*," Feng said. His voice was soft, but it carried to every corner of the room without effort. "I was told this is where the spiritual anomaly was located."

Bran was behind the counter, wiping down flour. He felt a prickle on the back of his neck. He stepped in front of his father, instinctively shielding Thomas, who was coughing into a handkerchief on his stool.

"Welcome," Bran said, keeping his voice steady. "We have fresh sourdough, brioche, and—"

"I am not here for the menu," Feng interrupted. He walked to the counter, ignoring the line of customers. The other villagers scrambled out of his way. "I am here for the baker."

Thomas struggled to his feet, wheezing. "I am the baker, My Lord. Thomas. How can I serve the Verdant Grain Sect?"

Feng looked at Thomas. He looked at the trembling hands, the curved spine, the gray pallor of the skin. He shook his head. "No. Not you. Your fire has burned down to embers, old man."

Feng’s eyes shifted to Bran.

"You," Feng said. "The boy."

"I'm just the assistant," Bran said, crossing his massive arms. "If you have business, you speak to my father. *'Respect the Master, or get out of the kitchen.'*"

The room gasped. You didn't talk to Cultivators like that.

One of Feng's guards stepped forward, hand reaching for a sword hilt. Feng stopped him with a single raised finger.

"Spirited," Feng mused. He reached out and picked up a plain dinner roll from a basket on the counter. He held it up to the light. "Tell me, boy. How did you make this?"

"Flour, water, yeast, salt," Bran said tightly.

Feng crushed the roll in his hand. He inhaled the scent. "Liar. There is something else. The grain structure... it is perfectly aligned. Every bubble of air is uniform. And there is a trace... a faint trace... of intent."

Feng took a bite. He chewed slowly. His eyes widened just a fraction.

"It clears the meridians," Feng whispered to his subordinate. "Only slightly. But it is there. He has refined the impurities out of common wheat simply by kneading it."

Feng looked at Bran with new interest. "Boy. My Sect controls the Spirit Rice fields in the Upper Valleys. The grain there is tough. It fights back. It contains energy that kills normal men who try to husk it. We need strong hands to process it for our Inner Disciples."

He placed a heavy leather pouch on the counter. The sound of gold coins clinking together was unmistakable.

"Fifty gold coins," Feng said. "For a five-year contract. You will come to the Sect. You will work in the Outer Kitchens. You will belong to us."

The silence in the room was absolute. Fifty gold coins could buy the bakery three times over. It could buy the best doctors in the capital. It could fix the roof, the oven, and pay the taxes for a decade.

"No," Bran said immediately. "I can't leave. My dad needs me."

"Bran..." Thomas whispered, staring at the bag.

"I said no," Bran said louder, glaring at the Cultivator. "My dad's back is bad. He can't work the heavy dough alone. Keep your money."

Elder Feng frowned. He wasn't used to being refused. "The offer is generous. Most peasants would kill their own kin for a chance to serve the Sect."

"I'm not most peasants. I'm a baker."

"Bran!" Thomas’s voice cracked like a whip.

Bran turned, shocked. His father never raised his voice.

Thomas stood up, leaning heavily on the counter. He looked at the gold, then he looked at Bran. There were tears in the old man’s eyes. Thomas knew he was dying. He knew that in a year, maybe two, he would be gone. And if Bran stayed here, he would be buried in debt and sadness.

"You will go," Thomas said, his voice trembling but firm.

"Dad? No! I can't leave you!"

"Look at me, boy!" Thomas shouted. "I am done! My lungs are full of dust. I can barely lift a sack of flour! Do you want to watch me rot in this kitchen? Do you want to waste your life nursing a ghost?"

"Dad, don't say that—"

"You are too big for this village, Bran!" Thomas grabbed Bran’s arm—his grip was weak, shaking against Bran’s rock-hard bicep. "You have a gift. I taught you everything I know. There is nothing left for you here but debt and duty. I don't want your duty. I want you to have a life."

Thomas pushed the bag of gold toward Bran. "Take the contract. Send the money home. That will help me more than you kneading dough here ever could. That money... that money means I can rest, Bran. Finally."

Bran looked at his father. He saw the exhaustion etched into every line of Thomas’s face. He realized, with a breaking heart, that his father was right. Staying was selfish. Staying meant watching his father work himself to death to support them both.

Bran swallowed a lump in his throat the size of a bagel. He turned back to Elder Feng.

"Five years?" Bran asked, his voice thick.

"Five years," Feng agreed. "Room and board included. You leave within the hour."

Bran reached out and took the contract. He didn't read it. He just signed his name in blocky, flour-dusted letters.

**BRAN**

"Good," Feng said, smiling that wolfish smile. "Pack your things. And bring that rolling pin. You're going to need it."

Bran turned to his father. He wanted to say a hundred things. He wanted to say he was scared. He wanted to say he loved him.

Instead, he hugged the old man gently, terrified he might crush him.

"I'll send more money, Dad," Bran whispered. "And I'll come back. I promise. *'Dough always rises if you give it time.'*"

Thomas patted his son's massive back, weeping openly now. "Just remember, son. No matter what fancy magic they show you up there... never trust a skinny baker."

An hour later, Bran was walking out of the village. He carried a small pack and his heavy iron-wood rolling pin strapped to his back like a broadsword.

Kael was waiting by the village gate, mouth agape.

"You?" Kael stammered. "They picked *you*? To be a Disciple?"

"Not a Disciple, Kael," Bran said, not looking back, his eyes fixed on the distant peaks where the Sect waited. "Just a cook. I'm just going to cook."

But as Bran stepped onto the road, the wind picked up, swirling the dust around his feet. The System—unseen, unnoticed—flickered in the air above him.

This is a fantastic beat. It adds agency to Bran. Instead of just accepting his fate or his father's command, he pushes back. It shows he understands his own value—or at least, the value of his labor—and he's fiercely protective. It turns him from a passive participant into a negotiator, proving he has a backbone of steel to match those arms.

Let’s slot this negotiation right into the climax of the scene. It amplifies the tension and earns him (and his father) more respect—and perhaps a unique item or perk that will be relevant later.

**[Revised Negotiation Sequence]**

Bran looked at his father. He saw the exhaustion etched into every line of Thomas’s face. He realized, with a breaking heart, that his father was right. Staying was selfish. Staying meant watching his father work himself to death to support them both.

But then Bran looked at the bag of gold. Fifty coins. It was a fortune to a peasant. But to a Sect that controlled mountains and rivers? To a man wearing silk that cost more than this entire bakery?

Bran’s hand didn't move toward the contract. He planted his feet. The floorboards groaned.

"No," Bran said.

The room went dead silent again. Even Thomas looked up, startled.

Elder Feng’s eyes narrowed dangerously. "Boy. Do not test the limits of my patience. I offered you a fortune."

"You offered a price," Bran corrected, his voice deepening. He looked the Sect Elder in the eyes—a feat most grown men couldn't manage. "But it’s not enough."

"Bran!" Thomas hissed, terrified. "What are you doing?"

"Quiet, Dad," Bran said gently, not looking away from Feng. "You’re taking his only son. You’re taking the only helper who can lift the heavy sacks. You’re taking his retirement plan."

Bran took a step forward. He towered over the Elder, though he slouched to hide it. "You said my bread clears meridians. You said I have 'intent.' If I’m that special, if I can do what your body-tempering disciples can’t... then fifty gold is cheap. It’s insults."

The guards drew their swords halfway. Feng didn't move. He stared up at Bran, his expression unreadable.

"You are haggling?" Feng asked, a cold amusement in his tone. "With an Elder of the Verdant Grain?"

"I’m negotiating," Bran said. "My Daddy always says, *'Cheap yeast makes flat bread.'* If you want the rise, you pay for the quality."

Feng stared at him for a long, stretching moment. The tension was thick enough to cut with a bread knife. Then, unexpectedly, Feng threw his head back and laughed. It was a sharp, barking sound.

"Audacious!" Feng declared. "You have no cultivation, no status, and you dare to squeeze me for coin?" He grinned. "I like it. It shows spirit."

Feng reached into his robe again. He didn't pull out more gold. Instead, he pulled out a small, jade bottle. He slammed it onto the counter next to the gold.

"One 'Breath of Spring' pill," Feng announced. "Alchemical grade: Earth. It cures chronic ailments of the lungs and bones. It will not make your father immortal, but it will clear the dust from his chest and straighten his spine. It will give him twenty healthy years."

Bran stared at the bottle. He looked at his father, wheezing on the stool. This was worth more than gold. This was life.

"And the gold stays?" Bran asked.

"The gold stays," Feng confirmed. "And I will double the monthly stipend sent to your family."

Bran nodded once. Firm. Decisive.

"Deal."

He grabbed the quill. He didn't hesitate this time. He signed his name with such force the nib nearly snapped.

**BRAN**

Feng snatched the contract up, looking pleased. "You drive a hard bargain, baker. See that you work as hard as you haggle."

Bran picked up the jade bottle and pressed it into his father's trembling hand.

"Take it, Dad," Bran whispered. "Take it now."

Thomas looked at the bottle, then up at his son with awe. He uncorked it and drank. Within seconds, his breathing deepened. The wheeze vanished. Color returned to his cheeks. He straightened up, his back popping audibly as the pain faded.

Thomas looked ten years younger. He gripped Bran's shoulder, and this time, his grip had strength.

"You..." Thomas choked up, his eyes wet. "You out-traded a Cultivator, son."

"Just business," Bran said, though his own hands were shaking slightly now that the adrenaline was fading. "If they want the best, they pay for the best. *'Quality has no discount.'*"

"Pack your things," Feng commanded, turning for the door. "And bring that rolling pin. You're going to need it. We leave in ten minutes."

Bran turned to head upstairs to his room, his heart pounding. He had just sold five years of his life, but he had bought his father’s future.

As he walked past Kael, who was still paralyzed with shock by the door, Bran paused.

"Hey, Kael," Bran murmured.

"Yeah?" Kael squeaked.

"Looks like I'm going to see those mountains after all."
