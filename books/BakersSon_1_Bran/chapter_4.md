## Chapter 4: The Bread of Exile

The goodbye was a blur of tears and flour.

Thomas stood tall—straighter than he had in a decade—and gripped Bran’s hand. He didn't say much. He didn't have to. The "Breath of Spring" pill had given him his life back, but it was Bran who had paid the price.

"Don't let them change you, Bran," Thomas had said, placing a small, heavy jar into Bran’s pack. It was the Mother Starter. The yeast culture that had been in their family for three generations. "You feed this every day. You hear me? If this dies, the family soul dies."

"I'll feed it before I feed myself, Dad," Bran promised.

Now, six hours later, Riverwood was a speck in the distance.

Bran sat in the back of a rattling supply wagon, squeezed between crates of rare herbs and sacks of Spirit Rice. The wagon was pulled by a creature that looked like a horse, if a horse had scales and breathed smoke.

Riding alongside the wagon on sleek, hovering swords were Elder Feng’s disciples. There were three of them. Two women and one young man. They wore pristine white robes that somehow repelled the road dust.

They hadn't spoken a word to Bran. They treated him like a sack of potatoes—cargo.

The sun began to dip below the jagged peaks of the Dragontooth Mountains. The air grew thin and cold.

"We camp here," Elder Feng announced from the front of the caravan.

The wagon lurched to a halt in a clearing. The disciples dismounted with fluid grace, their feet barely touching the dirt. They immediately sat down in a triangle formation, closing their eyes to meditate.

Bran climbed out of the wagon. His legs were stiff. His stomach gave a loud, treacherous growl that echoed in the quiet valley.

The male disciple, a youth named Li with a face that looked like it had been carved from haughty marble, opened one eye.

"Control your biological functions, mortal," Li sneered. "You disturb the flow of Qi."

"Sorry," Bran said, rubbing his stomach. "Qi doesn't fill the belly. *'An empty sack can't stand upright,'* my Dad says."

Li scoffed and closed his eye again. "Eat your dry rations and be silent."

Bran looked at the "rations" the Sect had provided. It was a hardtack biscuit that smelled like sawdust and despair. He tapped it against the wagon wheel. *Clack. Clack.* It was harder than the wheel.

"No," Bran muttered. "Absolutely not."

He walked to the edge of the clearing and gathered dry wood. He pulled a flint from his pocket. Within minutes, he had a small, crackling fire going.

Next, he pulled a cast-iron skillet from his pack (he never traveled without it) and set it on the rocks. He took a handful of flour, a pinch of salt from a pouch, and water from his canteen. He mixed it right there in his hand, a quick, slapdash dough.

He slapped the flat rounds of dough onto the hot skillet. *Sssssss.*

The smell hit the air instantly. Toasted wheat. Warmth. Home.

Disciple Li’s eyes snapped open. The two female disciples twitched, their noses wrinkling—not in disgust, but in involuntary interest.

Li stood up and marched over. "You! I told you to be silent! Now you pollute the air with the stench of... of grease and peasant food!"

"It's flatbread," Bran said calmly, flipping a piece. "And it's not grease, it's clarified butter. Want some?"

"I consume only Morning Dew and Spirit Pills," Li declared, his hand drifting to the sword at his waist. "Put that fire out. The smoke offends me."

Bran looked at the bread. It needed thirty more seconds to get that perfect char.

"I'll put it out when I'm done cooking," Bran said, turning his back to Li to check the crust.

"You dare turn your back on me?" Li’s voice rose. "I am an Inner Disciple! You are a servant!"

Li kicked out. It was a casual, disdainful kick, meant to scatter the fire and ruin the food. It was a kick that could snap a small tree.

Bran didn't see the kick, but he *felt* the movement of the air. He didn't think about combat. He thought about safety. *'Protect the product.'*

Bran pivoted. He didn't block with his arm. He blocked with the skillet.

*CLANG.*

The sound rang out like a temple bell.

Li yelped, hopping back on one foot, clutching his shin. The skillet hadn't moved an inch. Bran held it steady as a rock, his wrist locked.

"Careful," Bran said, genuinely concerned. "The pan is hot. You could burn your toes."

Li’s face went red. He drew his sword. The steel hummed with a faint blue light. "You insolent—"

"Enough."

Elder Feng’s voice wasn't loud, but it froze Li in place. The Elder was sitting on a rock nearby, watching.

"Sheathe your blade, Li," Feng commanded. "You kicked a piece of iron held by a boy who kneads granite for a living. The foolishness is yours."

Feng stood and walked over to the fire. He looked at the flatbread. It was perfectly golden, with charred bubbles of air.

"Spirit Pills nourish the core," Feng said, looking at his disciples. "But they leave the spirit cold. The boy is right. The body must be fueled."

Feng reached down, took a piece of hot flatbread, and tore it in half. Steam rose from the fluffy center. He ate it. He closed his eyes.

"Acceptable," Feng muttered. He looked at Bran. "Make more. Li will gather the wood since he was so eager to disturb the cook."

Li’s jaw dropped. "But Elder..."

"Wood," Feng ordered. "Now."

Bran watched the fuming disciple stomp off into the woods. He looked at his skillet, then at the Elder.

"He has bad form," Bran noted quietly. "Leans too much on his heels."

Feng smirked. "Indeed. Perhaps he needs to carry some flour sacks."

As Bran went back to cooking, flipping bread for the silent, hungry female disciples who had crept closer, a strange sensation washed over him. They were crossing a boundary line. The air felt thicker here.

Suddenly, a blue translucent box—flickering and distorted—popped into his vision. It hovered right over the skillet.

**[SYSTEM ERROR]**
**[Zone: Dragontooth Foothills (High Magic)]**
**[User Identified: Bran]**
**[Class Analysis...]**
**[Combat Class not found.]**
**[Mage Class not found.]**
**[Scanning Tools...]**
**[Item Detected: Cast Iron Skillet (Common).]**
**[Correction: Item has blocked a Spirit-Infused Kick.]**
**[Item Reclassified: Shield of the Hearth.]**
**[Skill Unlocked: Pan Block (Passive).]**
**[Description: If you can't stand the heat, stay out of the kitchen. 100% Fire Resistance. 50% Impact Absorption.]**

Bran blinked. He waved his hand through the blue box.

"Weird smoke," he muttered, and tossed another flatbread onto the fire.

We have his first skill! And it’s completely ridiculous but functional. **[Pan Block]**.

This is the right move. We need to let the atmosphere of the Sect soak in. It establishes the "Fish Out of Water" element. Bran isn't just a guy with a rolling pin anymore; he’s a peasant walking into a city of gods.

The "Whispers" mechanic is great because it highlights Bran’s high Perception (likely honed from listening to dough crackle or timers ticking) and reinforces how out of place he looks.
