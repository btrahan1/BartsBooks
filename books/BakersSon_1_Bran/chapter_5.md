## Chapter 5: The Ten Thousand Steps

The wagon stopped at the base of the mountain. From here, the beast of burden could go no further. The road simply ended, replaced by a staircase of white stone that cut straight up into the clouds.

"Out," Elder Feng commanded.

Bran hopped down, shouldering his pack. He looked up. And up. And up.

The *Verdant Grain Sect* wasn't just a building; it was a terraformed mountain. Giant, emerald-green terraces had been carved into the sheer cliff faces, growing rice stalks that shimmered with a faint, unnatural light. Waterfalls defied gravity, flowing from one floating island of rock to another, feeding the crops.

It was the most beautiful thing Bran had ever seen. It was also, he noted with a professional grimace, a logistical nightmare.

"How do they get the flour down?" he muttered to himself. "You'd need a chute three miles long."

"We fly, peasant," Disciple Li said, stepping off his sword and landing lightly on the first step. "Or we ascend the Steps of Ascension."

Li looked at Bran with a sneer. "All servants must walk the Ten Thousand Steps. It purifies the filth of the mortal world from your pores. Try not to die before you reach the halfway point."

Elder Feng didn't wait. He stepped onto his sword and shot upward like a green comet, disappearing into the mist. He had bought the goods; he didn't need to carry them.

Bran adjusted the straps of his pack. He felt the weight of his iron-wood rolling pin against his spine.

"Right," Bran exhaled. "Just a delivery run. A really, really long delivery run."

He started to climb.

***

The first thousand steps were easy. The air was still thick and warm.
The third thousand steps were harder. The air grew thin.
By the fifth thousand step, the "Whispers" began.

The path had widened into a plaza where lower-ranked disciples congregated. They wore gray robes—Outer Court members. They were training, sparring, or meditating on rocks.

As Bran walked past, a hulking figure in a stained tunic and trousers that were too tight around his thighs, the activity stopped.

Bran kept his eyes forward, focusing on his breathing—*in through the nose, out through the mouth, rhythm of the knead*—but he couldn't help but hear them. His ears, tuned to hear the faint *hiss* of a gas leak in a noisy kitchen, picked up everything.

*"...look at that ox..."*
*"...no aura at all. Is he a porter?"*
*"...look at those arms. Muscle-bound idiot. Probably a failed Body Tempering subject..."*
*"...bet he can't even read a sutra. Just brute force..."*
*"...why bring filth like that to the Sacred Peaks? He smells like yeast and sweat..."*

Bran tightened his grip on his pack straps. He felt a flush of shame heat his neck. In Riverwood, his strength was admired. Here, it was a sign of stupidity. A sign that he used his body because his mind wasn't sharp enough to use magic.

*‘My Daddy always said, “Let them talk. Words don’t bake the bread. The fire does.”’*

Bran forced his jaw to unclench. He kept walking.

Disciple Li and the two women were walking ahead of him. They were sweating now, their breathing ragged. The "Steps of Ascension" were enchanted. They pushed down on you, weighing your spirit.

Bran felt the pressure, too. It felt like walking through waist-deep dough. But Bran had spent his life fighting resistance. Every step was just a push. Every breath was just a fold.

By the eight thousandth step, Disciple Li was gasping. He was bent over, hands on his knees.

Bran walked past him.

He didn't speed up. He didn't slow down. He just maintained the rhythm. *Clomp. Clomp. Clomp.*

Li looked up, eyes bulging, sweat dripping from his nose. He watched the "peasant" trudge past him with the unstoppable momentum of a landslide.

*"...how?"* Bran heard Li wheeze. *"He has no Qi to reinforce his legs..."*

Bran didn't answer. He just kept climbing.

***

The Gate of the Outer Court was immense. It was made of petrified wood, inscribed with glowing runes that hummed with power.

Bran stepped onto the final platform. He wasn't gasping, but he was glistening. His shirt was soaked through. His legs burned with a familiar, pleasant ache—the kind you got after a double shift on Holiday Eve.

A man was waiting for him by the gate. He was round, red-faced, and wore an apron that was stained with multicolored sauces. He held a clipboard and looked like he hadn't slept in a week.

"You the new purchase?" the man barked.

"I'm Bran," Bran said, wiping his forehead.

"I don't care about your name. I care about your hands. Let me see 'em."

Bran held out his hands. They were large, calloused, and scarred from burns and cuts.

"Good," the man grunted. "I'm Head Chef Mol. Welcome to the Grinder."

Mol turned and kicked the massive gates open.

"Get inside. Dinner service is in three hours, and the scullery boy just lost three fingers to a Spirit-Crab. You're on peeling duty."

Bran stepped through the gates.

If the mountain was a garden, the Outer Kitchens were a foundry.

The room was the size of a cathedral. Instead of pews, there were rows of stone ovens that roared with green fire. The "pots" were cauldrons big enough to boil a cow. Disciples in gray aprons sprinted back and forth, carrying ingredients that defied logic.

Bran saw a girl wrestling a bundle of carrots that were screaming.
He saw a man chopping meat with a cleaver that sparked every time it hit the bone.
He smelled sulfur, star-anise, burnt sugar, and ozone.

It was chaos. It was terrifying.

Bran took a deep breath. He inhaled the madness. And underneath the magical chaos, he smelled it.

Burnt garlic.

"Someone's burning the garlic on station four," Bran said instinctively.

Head Chef Mol stopped. He looked at Bran, then turned to scream across the hall. "JIN! YOUR GARLIC IS BLACK! DUMP IT!"

Mol turned back to Bran, raising an eyebrow. "Good nose, Baker. Maybe you'll last the week."

Mol pointed to a corner of the kitchen where a mountain of root vegetables—gnarled, purple things with thorns—sat waiting.

"Iron-Root Turnips," Mol said. "Skins are as tough as leather armor. Peel them. All of them. Use the obsidian knife."

Bran walked over to his station. He looked at the mountain of turnips. He looked at the disciples sprinting around him, casting minor spells to stir soups or heat pans.

He heard the whispers again.

*"...fresh meat..."*
*"...he'll quit by tomorrow..."*
*"...look at him, he doesn't even know how to activate the knife..."*

Bran picked up the obsidian knife. It was heavy. He picked up an Iron-Root Turnip. The thorns pricked his skin.

He didn't try to use magic. He didn't try to be fancy.

He planted his feet. He found his rhythm.

*Grip. Slice. Turn.*

The peel curled away in a perfect, continuous ribbon.

Bran smiled. It was just a kitchen. A big, loud, magical kitchen full of people who hated him. But it was still a kitchen.

"Alright," Bran whispered to the turnip. "Let's get to work."

That sounds perfect. Bran's kindness should be his superpower in a place as cutthroat as a Cultivation Sect. While everyone else is hoarding resources and backstabbing for rank, Bran is the guy who helps you pick up the onions you dropped. That creates loyalty, even if it confuses people at first.

Let’s move into **Chapter 6: The Way of the Helping Hand.**

We need to introduce a peer for Bran—someone in the kitchen who isn't a bully. A fellow low-ranker who is struggling, whom Bran can help. This will act as the catalyst for the "kinder whispers."
