## Chapter 6: The Way of the Helping Hand

Three days in the Outer Kitchens felt like three years.

Bran had peeled four thousand Iron-Root Turnips. He had scrubbed cauldrons that were stained with alchemical residue that made his skin tingle. He had carried sacks of rice that weighed more than his father.

He hadn't complained once.

It was mid-morning prep. The kitchen was already at a dull roar. Bran was currently tasked with "tenderizing" the Thunder-Beef—slabs of meat from cows that grazed during lightning storms. The meat twitched on the cutting board.

Next to him was a girl. She looked about fourteen, small and bird-like, with messy hair tied back in a rag. Her gray robes were too big for her. Her name, he had learned from the chore roster, was **Mei**.

Mei was struggling. She was trying to whisk a bowl of "Cloud-Cream," a substance that became lighter than air when whipped. But if you didn't whip it fast enough, it turned into cement.

Her arm was shaking. Sweat dripped from her chin into the bowl. The cream was turning gray—starting to harden.

"Stupid arm," she hissed, tears welling in her eyes. "Move faster."

A Shadow-Chef (one of the supervisors) was prowling down the aisle. He had a cane made of bamboo and a nasty temper. He was three stations away, yelling at a boy for chopping chives unevenly.

Bran saw Mei panic. She knew if the Chef saw the ruined cream, she’d lose her meal token for the day.

Bran didn't stop pounding his beef. *Thwack. Thwack. Thwack.*

"Elbow down," Bran said softly, not looking at her.

Mei jumped. "What?"

"Your elbow is flying out," Bran murmured, timing his words to the rhythm of his mallet. "Wastes energy. Tuck it into your ribs. Use your wrist, not your shoulder. *'The wrist is the whip, the arm is the handle.'*"

Mei stared at him. The big, silent boy who everyone called "The Ox" was giving her technical advice?

"Like this?" She tucked her elbow.

"Looser grip," Bran added. "You're choking the whisk. Let it dance."

Mei loosened her grip. The whisk moved faster. The motion became less jagged, more fluid. The cream began to froth, turning a brilliant, fluffy white just as the Shadow-Chef stepped up to their station.

The Chef peered into Mei’s bowl. He frowned, looking for a flaw. The cream was perfect.

"Adequate," the Chef grunted. He turned to Bran. "You. The meat isn't fighting back anymore. Move to the ovens. The Fire-Buns need rotation."

"Yes, Chef," Bran said.

As the Chef walked away, Mei let out a shuddering breath. She looked at Bran, who was already wiping down his station with methodical care.

"Thank you," she whispered.

Bran just nodded. "Cloud-Cream is tricky. It smells fear."

***

Later that day, during the brief meal break, Bran sat outside on the kitchen steps. He was eating his ration—a bowl of watery gruel and a steamed bun that had the texture of a damp sock.

He took a bite of the bun and sighed. "Over-steamed. The gluten has collapsed."

He pulled a small jar from his pocket. It was a pinch of dried herbs he had gathered from the edge of the herb garden (the "weeds" that the alchemists threw away). He sprinkled some into his gruel. It wasn't much, just some wild savory and mountain thyme, but it made the gruel taste like actual food.

He felt eyes on him.

Mei was standing a few feet away, holding her bowl, looking hesitant. Behind her was another boy, a scrawny kid with glasses named **Jin** (the one who had burned the garlic on day one).

"Mind if we sit?" Mei asked.

Bran scooted over on the stone step. "Free country. Or... free Sect. Whatever this is."

They sat. Jin looked at Bran’s arms, then quickly at his gruel.

"You're really strong," Jin blurted out. "I saw you carry the Flour-Silos yesterday. Two at a time. Usually, it takes a Levitation Talisman to move those."

"Leverage," Bran said, taking a spoonful of gruel. "And good boots."

"Are you... a Body Cultivator?" Mei asked. "Like, undercover?"

Bran laughed. It was a warm sound that seemed to startle them. "No. I'm a baker. From Riverwood. I just... work a lot."

He saw Jin eyeing the herbs in his bowl. Bran reached into his pocket.

"Want some?" he offered the jar. "Makes the sludge go down easier. *'A dull knife hurts the hand; dull food hurts the soul.'*"

Jin’s eyes widened. "Are those... Silver-Leaf Thyme? Those grow near the Spirit Fields. Aren't they poisonous if not refined?"

"Only the stems," Bran said. "The leaves are just spicy. My Dad used to put them in meat pies."

Jin took a pinch cautiously. He tasted it. His face lit up. "Whoa. That's... actually good."

Mei took some too. For the first time in weeks, the two young disciples ate with enthusiasm.

As they ate, Bran listened. He tuned out the wind and the distant shouts of training warriors. He focused on the whispers drifting from the open kitchen door behind them.

*"...did you see him help Mei? The Ox?"*
*"...yeah. He fixed her whisking form. Weird, right?"*
*"...he's not mean. The other strong guys usually steal our food."*
*"...I heard he carried Jin’s crate yesterday when the Chef wasn't looking."*
*"...maybe he's not an idiot. Maybe he's just... quiet."*

Bran smiled into his gruel.

"So," Bran said, wiping his mouth. "You guys know where I can get some actual yeast in this place? This steamed bun is a tragedy, and I think I can fix it."

Mei and Jin exchanged a look. A look of conspiracy.

"The Inner Kitchens," Jin whispered. "They have 'Golden Sun Yeast.' But... it's guarded by the Spirit Cats."

Bran cracked his knuckles. "Cats, huh? My bakery back home had rats the size of dogs. I think I can handle a cat."

Mei giggled. It was a nervous sound, but it was genuine.

"We can show you the map," Mei said.

Bran nodded. He had allies. They weren't strong, and they weren't important, but they were hungry. And a hungry friend was the best kind to have.

This is the perfect comedic understatement for a LitRPG encounter. It immediately sets the tone: the threat is massive, but Bran's reaction is practical. He isn't terrified of the monster; he's annoyed that it's in his way.
