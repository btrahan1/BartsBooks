## Chapter 8: The Rising

The next morning, the Outer Kitchen smelled wrong.

It didn't smell like burnt garlic. It didn't smell like boiled cabbage. It smelled like heaven.

It was a rich, nutty, golden aroma that drifted out of the ovens and wound its way through the dining hall, hooking disciples by the nose and dragging them toward the serving line.

Bran stood behind the counter. He looked tired—he had been up all night nurturing the starter—but he was grinning.

Beside him, Mei and Jin were serving. They weren't slumping. They were standing tall, caught up in Bran’s infectious energy.

"What is this?" a disciple asked, staring at the basket. Instead of the usual gray "Spirit-Pucks," there were fluffy, golden-brown rolls that steamed in the morning air.

"Sun-Buns," Bran said cheerfully. "Careful, they're hot."

The disciple took a bite. His eyes widened. He didn't just chew; he moaned. The flavor exploded in his mouth—sweet, savory, with a hint of warmth that spread down to his stomach and made his Qi swirl lazily.

"Next!" Bran called.

The line moved fast. The whispers started again, but they were different.

*"...is this real?"*
*"...I feel... awake. My fatigue is gone."*
*"...who baked this? The Ox?"*
*"...man, I could eat ten of these."*

Then, the doors to the dining hall slammed open.

Silence fell. The happy chewing stopped.

Disciple Li strode in. He was flanked by two other Inner Court lackeys. They weren't supposed to be here—they had their own private dining hall with fine wines and spirit meats—but the smell had drifted up the mountain.

Li looked furious. He saw the happy faces of the Outer Court scum. He saw the steam rising from the baskets. He saw Bran.

Li marched to the front of the line, shoving a first-year girl out of the way. She dropped her bun. It hit the floor.

Bran’s smile vanished.

"What is the meaning of this!" Li shouted, pointing a trembling finger at the baskets. "You are distributing unauthorized alchemical substances! I smell Spirit Qi in this food!"

The cafeteria held its breath. Using unauthorized resources was a crime punishable by expulsion. Or worse.

Bran picked up a pair of tongs. He calmly picked up a fresh bun.

"It's bread," Bran said simply. "Would you like some?"

"Do not mock me, peasant!" Li slammed his hand on the counter. "Where did you get the materials? This amount of refined energy... you stole it!"

"Stole?" Bran tilted his head. "I used the flour provided. I used the water provided. The only thing I added was technique. And maybe a little patience."

"Liar!" Li drew his sword. The steel hissed. "Confess! You are using a demonic art to enhance the food! No mortal baker can create this!"

Bran looked at the sword. Then he looked at the girl on the floor, who was crying over her ruined breakfast.

He put the tongs down. He walked around the counter.

"Bran, don't," Mei whispered, terrified.

Bran ignored her. He walked until he was standing chest-to-chest with Li. Li was tall, but Bran was broad. He loomed over the cultivator like a cliff face.

"You knocked that girl's breakfast over," Bran said quietly.

"She was in my way," Li sneered. "Just as you are. Now, tell me your secret before I carve it out of you."

Bran sighed. "My Daddy always said, *'A man who wastes food has a hollow heart.'*"

"I don't care about your father!" Li raised his sword to strike—not to kill, but to maim. To humiliate.

He swung the flat of the blade toward Bran’s face.

Bran didn't use a pan this time. He didn't have one.

He used his hand.

He caught the blade.

*CLACK.*

The sound was like stone hitting stone. Bran’s calloused palm, hardened by years of gripping hot iron trays and crushing stiff rye, clamped around the flat of the spiritual steel.

Li’s eyes bulged. He tried to pull the sword back. It didn't move.

"Let go!" Li shrieked, channeling his Qi. The sword glowed blue. It became razor sharp. It burned with cold fire.

Bran felt the sting. It hurt. It burned his palm. But Bran was used to burns. He had scars from 500-degree ovens. A little cold fire was nothing.

"You're making a scene," Bran said. "And you're scaring the customers."

Bran squeezed.

**[Skill Activation: The Baker's Grip.]**
**[Target: Spirit Steel Sword (Low Grade).]**
**[Status: Brittle.]**

*CRACK.*

A spiderweb fracture appeared on the blade.

Li screamed in horror. "My sword! My Spirit Sword!"

Bran let go. He shoved Li backward with a gentle, open-palm push. Li flew ten feet, sliding across the cafeteria floor on his backside, knocking over a table.

Bran looked at his hand. There was a thin line of red blood across his palm. He wiped it on his apron.

"Apologies for the interruption," Bran announced to the stunned room. He bent down, picked up the girl’s dropped bun, dusted it off, and then handed her a fresh one from his pocket.

"Here," he smiled gently. "On the house."

He looked back at Li, who was staring at his cracked sword in disbelief.

"If you want to eat, get in line," Bran said. "If you want to fight... do it outside. *'No brawling in the bakery.'* Rule number one."

For a second, nobody moved.

Then, from the back of the room, someone started clapping.

It was Jin. Then Mei. Then the girl who dropped the bun.

Soon, the whole dining hall was cheering.

Bran turned red. He ducked his head and hurried back behind the counter.

"Alright, alright," he mumbled, grabbing the tongs. "Settle down. The buns are getting cold."

High above, on a balcony overlooking the Outer Court, Elder Feng watched the scene unfold. He stroked his beard.

"He cracked a spirit sword with his bare hand," Feng murmured.

"He is dangerous, Elder," a shadow whispered from behind him. "Unrefined. Brute force. He defies the laws of cultivation."

"No," Feng smiled. "He is rewriting them. Keep an eye on him. The Sect Tournament is next month. I think... I think our baker needs to enter."

Let's insert this as an Interlude between Chapter 8 and 9. It creates suspense and raises the stakes without Bran even knowing.
