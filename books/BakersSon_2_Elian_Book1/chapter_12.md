## Chapter 12: An Unwelcome Interruption

The work on the cradle was a joy. The pine hummed a happy, lullaby-like tune as Elian worked, and Lyra’s quiet, competent presence had become a comfortable part of the workshop's rhythm. For the first time in a long time, Elian felt a sliver of peace.

Which was, of course, the exact moment the world chose to interrupt him.

It started with shouting from the street outside. Loud, drunken, and aggressive. Then came a heavy *thud* against his front door, followed by mocking laughter.

Elian froze, his carving knife poised over a delicate piece of joinery. The pine cradle went silent, its happy hum replaced by a tense, fearful stillness.

*"Go away,"* the wood whispered.

"My thoughts exactly," Elian muttered.

He tried to ignore it. He focused on his work, on the precise angle of the cut. But another *thud*, harder this time, rattled the door in its frame.

Lyra looked up from the corner where she was sanding a spindle, her face pale with concern.

"Just some louts from the tavern," Elian growled, though his jaw was tight. "They'll get bored and wander off."

But they didn't. The shouting grew louder, more personal. They were calling him out. "Hey, Wood-Wizard! Come out and show us your magic stick!"

Elian put down his knife with a sharp click. His peace was shattered. His focus was broken. And they were upsetting the wood.

"Leave me alone! I am trying to get to work!" he roared at the door.

This was met with another round of derisive laughter.

Elian looked at Lyra's worried face. He looked at the half-finished cradle. He let out a long, slow sigh of pure, undiluted aggravation.

He stalked to the corner of the workshop and grabbed the staff resting there. It was the heavy, ugly, scarred oak staff. Finn's training staff. He hadn't touched it since the boy had left. The wood felt sluggish and resentful in his hands, a stark contrast to the living ash of Jasmine.

He strode to the front door and threw it open.

It closed behind him with a bang.

Three hooligans. They were barely men, their faces flushed with cheap ale and the stupid confidence of youth. They had been amusing themselves by throwing rocks at his shop sign. They stopped and stared as Elian emerged, a dark, imposing figure in the morning light.

"What do you want, old man?" the leader snickered, puffing out his chest.

Elian didn't answer. He just looked at them, his eyes cold and analytical. He used his [Grain Sight]. He saw the flawed, knotty grain of their characters. The arrogance. The insecurity. The weakness in their stances.

He sighed. This was a waste of his time.

He reached into his pocket, pulled out a silver coin, and tossed it to the leader. The young man fumbled, but caught it, his expression turning from aggression to confusion.

"What is this?" he asked.

"It's a donation," Elian said, his voice flat and bored. "Take it to the Temple of the Silent Leaf. Give it to the priests and then ask for Finn. He's new there."

The hooligans exchanged baffled looks. "Why would we do that?" the leader asked.

Elian leaned on the oak staff. He let a slow, thin, utterly mirthless smile spread across his face.

"To heal your wounds," he said.

The leader looked down at himself, then at his friends. "What wounds?"

Elian’s smile didn't waver. He took one step forward. The oak staff in his hands began to hum with a low, dangerous, and deeply unhappy energy.

"The ones you are about to receive."

That's the perfect way to frame the "fight." It's not a brawl; it's a chore. It’s Elian taking out the trash with grim efficiency. And Lyra's perspective is crucial—she's not seeing a hero; she's seeing a master craftsman applying his skills to a different, messier medium.

The final instruction to "be respectful" is the brilliant, comedic cherry on top.
