## Chapter 13: A Lesson in Applied Physics

Lyra watched from the dusty front window of the workshop, her heart pounding. She had only ever seen Elian's quiet, creative side. Now, she was about to witness the storm.

But it wasn't a storm. It was a lesson in applied physics.

It was efficient, slightly brutal, and almost silent.

The leader, emboldened by his friends, took a swing. It was a wild, telegraphed punch. Elian didn't even seem to move. He simply shifted his weight, and the punch whistled past his ear. As the hooligan's arm passed, Elian brought the heavy oak staff up in a short, sharp jab. It connected with the man's floating ribs. *Thump*. A sound like a mallet hitting a side of beef. The air left the man's lungs in a pained whoosh, and he folded, gasping.

One.

The other two charged together. Elian stepped forward to meet them, not back. He swept the staff low, hooking the ankle of the man on the right. As that man tumbled, Elian used the momentum of the staff's swing to bring the other end around in a rising arc, catching the second man under the chin. *Clack*. The man's head snapped back, his eyes rolling up, and he crumpled in a heap.

Two. Three.

The entire "fight" had taken less than five seconds. There was no shouting, no fancy footwork, no wasted motion. It was the same brutal economy he used to split a log, just applied to bone and sinew.

As Lyra watched, she marveled at the controlled violence. He hadn't raged. He hadn't even looked angry. He had looked... bored. Like a master carpenter dealing with a warped board that refused to lie flat.

The first hooligan was still on his knees, clutching his ribs, trying to breathe. Elian walked over and nudged him with the tip of the staff.

"Up," Elian commanded.

The man scrambled to his feet, eyes wide with terror. His friends were beginning to stir, groaning in the dirt.

"Get them on their feet," Elian said. "You have an appointment."

He pointed down the street with the staff.

"The Temple is that way," he said, his voice returning to its normal, flat tone. "Tell them you're making a donation." He looked the leader in the eye. "Remember... his name is Finn. He's a student there. He probably needs the practice."

He paused, a stern, schoolmasterly expression on his face.

"And remember to be respectful," Elian added. "The priestesses do not appreciate vulgarity. Now, go."

The three hooligans, bruised, battered, and utterly bewildered, helped each other up and began to stumble away in the direction of the Temple, looking back over their shoulders as if expecting him to attack again.

Elian watched them go, a look of deep satisfaction on his face. He had dealt with the interruption. He had sent Finn some much-needed practical experience. And he had made a donation to the Temple. All in a morning's work.

He turned and walked back into the workshop, the heavy oak staff thumping on the threshold. He placed it back in its corner and walked over to the cradle, picking up his delicate carving knife as if nothing had happened.

Lyra just stared at him, a new, profound layer of respect dawning in her eyes. She had thought she was working for a simple, grumpy woodworker. She now realized she was apprenticed to something far more ancient and dangerous.

"The pine," Elian said, not looking at her, his voice perfectly calm. "It's happy again. We can get back to work."

That is a small moment, but it speaks volumes. It reinforces his core character trait: his deepest respect is reserved for the wood itself. He sees the staff not just as a tool he used, but as a partner he forced into an unpleasant situation.

It's a beautiful, quiet beat that adds immense depth. Let's add it as the perfect coda to the scene.
