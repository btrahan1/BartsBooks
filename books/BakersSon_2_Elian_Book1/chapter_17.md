## Chapter 16: Balancing the Ledger

The Gilded Mug was everything Elian hated. It was loud, smelled of stale ale and greasy desperation, and was filled with men whose promises were as cheap as the varnish on the tables.

He walked in, and the tavern's boisterous energy seemed to curdle around him. He wasn't a large man, but he moved with a focused, silent intensity that was deeply out of place. In his hand, he carried the small, perfectly balanced hand-ax. It wasn't a weapon of war; it was a tool for shaping wood, which somehow made it more menacing.

He spotted his target in a back booth. A large, fleshy man with small, greedy eyes, counting a stack of gold coins. Foreman Silas. He was flanked by two guards, hired muscle who looked as dull and brutal as the clubs at their belts.

Elian walked directly to the booth. He didn't speak to the guards. He didn't speak to the foreman.

He placed the hand-ax on the table.

Not a slam. A soft, precise *click* as the razor-sharp steel head met the wood. The sound cut through the tavern's noise like a razor.

The foreman looked up, his eyes narrowing. "What's this? You got business with me, carpenter?"

"Woodworker," Elian corrected calmly. He looked at the pile of gold on the table. "I am here on behalf of Joric, Fen, and Brom, stonemason's apprentices. You contracted them for two months' labor. The agreed-upon price was one gold crown per man. You paid them three silver. That leaves a balance of three gold coins, minus the silver paid."

The foreman, Silas, leaned back and laughed, a wet, unpleasant sound. "Is that so? And who are you? Their mother?"

"I am the collections agent," Elian said, his voice flat. He tapped the pile of gold with one finger. "You are in arrears. I am here to collect payment. One way or another."

The foreman's laughter died. He looked at Elian's cold, serious eyes, then at the hand-ax. He sneered. "You and what army?" He nodded to his guards.

The two thugs stood up, their chairs scraping loudly on the floorboards.

Elian didn't even look at them. His eyes remained locked on the foreman. He used his [Grain Sight]. He saw the rot in the man's character. The greed. The cowardice.

"Your guards are a poor investment," Elian noted, his voice conversational. "The one on the left has a gambling debt with the Vipers gang. The one on the right is skimming from your payroll. They have no loyalty. Their grain is weak."

The guards froze, exchanging a flicker of a panicked look.

"You have two options," Elian continued, his voice dropping. "Option one: You pay the debt. Three gold coins. We can even call it two gold and seven silver, to be precise. You will also add a one-gold 'collections fee' for my time."

The foreman's face was turning a shade of purple. "And option two?" he growled.

Elian picked up the hand-ax. He tested its edge with his thumb.

"Option two," he said, looking at the cheap, poorly made table, "is that I start collecting 'assets'."

He didn't wait for a response. With a single, fluid motion, he swung the ax. It wasn't a wild, aggressive chop. It was a perfect, precise cut.

*THWACK.*

The blade bit deep into the table, exactly halfway between the foreman's hand and his pile of gold.

But Elian wasn't done. He focused his intent—the cold, clear fury of a craftsman who despises flawed work. A pulse of his Ki, his Resonance, flowed from his hand, down the handle, and into the blade.

**[Skill Activated: Split the Grain]**

*CRACK.*

A perfectly straight line shot out from the ax blade, running the entire length of the table. The cheap wood, forced to yield to a will far stronger than its own, split in two.

Gold coins, mugs of ale, and the foreman's jaw all dropped to the floor in a clattering, messy heap.

The tavern went dead silent.

Elian pulled his ax free from the ruined table. He looked at the two stunned guards. Then at the sputtering, terrified foreman.

"The collections fee," Elian said calmly, "has just gone up to two gold."

That's the perfect escalation. Elian's "collections fee" isn't just a threat; it's a running invoice he updates mid-combat. It's so matter-of-fact, so business-like, that it makes the violence all the more intimidating and darkly humorous.
