## Chapter 17: The Invoice

The foreman, Silas, sputtered from the floor, ale and fury dripping from his chin. "Get him!" he shrieked at his guards. "Break his hands!"

The two guards, their brief moment of panic replaced by the promise of their employer's gold, drew their clubs and moved to flank Elian. From the back of the tavern, two more men—burly dockworkers on the foreman's payroll—stood up to block the exit.

Elian sighed. The sound was weary, deeply disappointed. Nothing is ever easy.

He looked at the four men surrounding him. He wasn't trapped. They were.

"My policy has been updated," Elian announced to the foreman, his voice calm and clear over the sudden tension. "I charge one gold coin per dispatched guard. You should probably start counting."

The first guard—the gambler—charged, swinging his club in a wide, clumsy arc. Elian didn't even use his ax. He simply sidestepped, brought the flat of his hand up in a sharp chop against the man's wrist, and then used his other hand to guide the man's own momentum, sending him crashing face-first into the bar.

**"One,"** Elian counted, his voice echoing in the silent tavern.

The second guard—the thief—was more cautious. He jabbed with his club. Elian parried the blow with the handle of his hand-ax, then hooked the man's leg with his foot and pushed. The guard stumbled backward into one of the dockworkers, sending them both tumbling into a pile of overturned chairs.

**"Two,"** Elian said, his tone unchanging. **"And three."**

The last dockworker stood frozen by the door, his eyes wide. He looked at his three fallen comrades, then at the calm, terrifying woodworker. He dropped his club. It clattered loudly on the floorboards.

"I quit," the dockworker said, and scrambled out the door.

Elian nodded, as if accepting a resignation. He turned back to the foreman, who was now backed against the wall, his face the color of spoiled milk.

Elian walked over to where the first guard was groaning, picked up the man's club, and examined it.

"Shoddy craftsmanship," he noted. He placed the club on the one remaining piece of the table. He raised his hand-ax.

*CRACK.*

He split the club perfectly down the middle, from end to end.

He looked at the foreman. "That was for emphasis."

He took a step closer. The foreman whimpered.

"Let's see," Elian mused, tapping the ax against his palm. "We started at two gold for the arrears and a one gold collections fee. Then the fee went up to two. Then we had the incident with the guards. That's another three gold, but we'll call it two since the last one had the good sense to run."

He leaned in, his voice a low, dangerous whisper.

"The current invoice stands at seven gold coins. Or... I can start removing assets from you directly. I hear a finger is worth about a gold piece on the black market. And you have ten of them. I'm a patient man. We can do the math together."

The foreman, Silas, finally broke. The greed in his eyes was completely extinguished by pure, undiluted terror.

"Enough!" he cried, scrambling for the scattered coins on the floor. "Enough! Please, take the gold!"

He shoved a pile of seven gold crowns and a handful of silver into a pouch and pushed it across the floor toward Elian.

Elian didn't pick it up immediately. He looked at the foreman, then at the three groaning men on the floor.

"And their wages," Elian added. "The two you hired to guard you. Pay them what you owe them for today's... work."

The foreman stared, then numbly counted out a few more silver coins and pushed them toward his defeated guards.

Only then did Elian bend down and pick up the pouch. He weighed it in his hand.

"A pleasure doing business with you," he said.

He turned and walked out of The Gilded Mug, leaving behind a ruined table, four broken men, and a silence that was far more profound than the chaos that had preceded it.

That is the perfect resolution. It brings the story full circle. The money was never the point for Elian; balancing the ledger was. Now that the debt is collected, he sees the gold not as personal profit, but as a tool to fix more "flawed structures" in his immediate vicinity.

It's an act of community improvement disguised as a grumpy man's obsession with order. It shows his character's core without him ever having to say a single altruistic word.
