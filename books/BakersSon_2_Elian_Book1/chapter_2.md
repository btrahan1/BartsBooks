## Chapter 2: A Soul in Sycamore

Elian stood before the sycamore plank, his hand plane held loosely at his side. The wood was pale and unremarkable, its grain a lazy, meandering pattern. It was the lumber equivalent of a dull conversation.

He had found it in the yard of a commercial mill, a place that smelled of sawdust and finality. He had spent an hour walking the stacks, his hand outstretched, "listening" for a piece quiet enough to tolerate the Mayor's commission. This one hadn't screamed or moaned. It had just... sighed. A deep, weary sigh of resignation.

"Alright," Elian said, placing the plank on his workbench. "Let's get this over with. The sooner you're a desk, the sooner we can both forget this ever happened."

He set the plane to take the thinnest possible shaving and pushed it across the surface. A perfect, ribbon-like curl of wood peeled away.

*"Ouch! It hurts!"*

The voice in his mind was not a scream, but a high-pitched, theatrical wail, like a pampered noble who had stubbed his toe.

Elian stopped, pinching the bridge of his nose. "It does not hurt," he said through gritted teeth. "You are a plank of wood. You have no pain receptors."

*"It hurts my... SOUL!"* the sycamore wailed. *"You are shearing away my very essence! I was destined for more! I could have been... a hat rack! A very elegant hat rack in a fashionable haberdashery!"*

Elian slammed the plane down on the bench. "I am not harming your soul! I am giving you an identity... albeit a rather boring one, I will admit." He leaned in close to the plank. "Look, you think I want to do this? The man who commissioned this desk thinks 'dovetail' is a type of bird. But his coin pays for the roof that keeps the rain off the Iron-Root in the back. So, you will be a desk, and you will be a very sturdy, very imposing, and very, very quiet desk. Understand?"

The sycamore just hummed a low, mournful note of protest.

Elian sighed and picked up the plane again. He tried to tune out the wood's whining, focusing instead on the physical task. The hiss of the blade, the scent of fresh-cut sycamore, the satisfying feedback as the tool found the sweet spot on the grain.

As he worked, a familiar, translucent blue box flickered at the edge of his vision. He had been seeing them for years, ever since his sixteenth birthday.

**[Skill Increased: Woodworking (Adept) -> Woodworking (Master)]**
**[Perk Unlocked: Grain Sight.] You can now visually perceive the internal stress lines and potential of a piece of wood.]**

He swatted at the air as if it were a fly. "Go away." He hated The System. It was just like his clients—always trying to put a label on something complex and beautiful. It saw him planing a board and called it "Woodworking." It didn't understand the conversation he was having, the battle of wills, the negotiation with a stubborn soul.

He activated the new perk out of morbid curiosity. The world shifted.

The sycamore plank on his bench was suddenly overlaid with a web of faint, shimmering lines. Most were a calm, pale blue, indicating the natural flow of the grain. But there were angry red knots of stress near the center, and a single, brilliant thread of gold that ran along the edge—the "hat rack" the wood was whining about.

He looked around the workshop. Everything wooden was glowing. The oak beams in the ceiling had deep, steady blue lines of immense strength. The cheap pine chair in the corner was a chaotic mess of red stress fractures, ready to collapse.

Then he looked at his own hands. Faint, golden lines of energy were visible under his skin, mirroring the grain of the wood. The System called it Ki, or Mana. Elian just called it "The Resonance."

He dismissed the vision, the world returning to its normal, dull state. The perk was useful, he admitted grudgingly. It would make finding flaws easier. It would also make it much harder to ignore when a client was forcing him to cut against a piece of wood's golden potential.

He picked up a chisel and mallet. "Right," he said to the sycamore. "Time to cut the mortises. Try not to be a baby about it."

*"The horror!"* the wood shrieked as the chisel bit in. *"The sheer, unadulterated horror!"*

Elian began to hum loudly, trying to drown it out. It was going to be a very, very long day.

This is a fantastic turn. It shows he isn't just a bitter artisan; he's incapable of creating something that isn't, on some level, a masterpiece. It also adds a wonderful layer to his relationship with the wood. He's not just a carver; he's a comforter.

This will be a perfect, quiet chapter that contrasts with the loud whining of the sycamore, showing his true nature when left to his own devices.
