## Chapter 20: The New Arrangement

Piper didn't wait for a formal tour. She dropped her pack with a thud, scanned the workshop, and immediately claimed an unused bench in the corner by the front window—the one with the best light. Without a word, she began unpacking. It was a dizzying display of efficiency. Out came a well-worn tool roll, a set of delicate carving knives, and a small, meticulously organized box of inlay materials.

Elian watched her as she organized 'her' new workbench. She moved with a purpose that was both impressive and deeply irritating. She was invading his space, rearranging his quiet chaos into her own neat, aggressive order.

"I'll need my own set of planes," she announced without looking up, wiping down the bench with a rag. "Yours are set for hardwoods. I'll need a softer touch for the instrument repairs. And I'll need a stipend for materials. Varnish, gut strings, pearl inlay..."

As she started making demands, Elian held up a hand to stop the torrent of words. He pointed a thumb towards Lyra, who was watching the scene with a mixture of awe and amusement.

"You report to her," Elian stated. "She is the shop manager. All material requests, client negotiations, and scheduling go through her. You will not bother me unless the building is on fire."

Piper's green eyes flickered from Elian to Lyra, assessing this new power structure. She gave Lyra a quick, calculating smile.

Elian continued, "As for payment, you get ten percent of the profits of everything *we* sell. That includes your repairs and any commission work you take on."

Piper stopped her work and planted her hands on her hips. "Fifty," she shot back instantly.

Elian didn't even blink. "Fifteen."

"Forty," she countered, her eyes narrowing. "I'm the one who will be dealing with the insufferable clients. That's hazard pay."

"Ten," Elian said, his voice dropping, his arms crossed. He was enjoying this. It was a battle of wills, a negotiation of pure, stubborn grain.

Piper stared him down for a long, silent moment. She saw the unyielding set of his jaw. She was a good bargainer, but she knew when she had hit a wall of solid ironwood.

"Fifteen will work," she finally conceded, a grudging respect in her tone. Then she added a new term, quick as a flash. "And room and board."

Elian grunted. He looked at the small, dusty storage loft above the workshop. It was full of old lumber and forgotten projects. "The loft is yours. Don't snore."

"Deal," Piper said, a sharp, satisfied grin spreading across her face. She held out a hand.

Elian looked at her hand, then back at her face. He didn't shake it. He just nodded once and turned back to his own workbench.

Lyra stepped forward, a warm, welcoming smile on her face, and shook Piper's hand instead. "Welcome to The Knot & Burl," she said. "I'm Lyra. Let's talk about that materials stipend."

As the two women began to huddle over a piece of parchment, their voices a low murmur of business, Elian picked up his tools. The workshop was no longer just his. It was a strange, functioning ecosystem.

Piper, the fiery, mercenary engine of commerce.
Lyra, the calm, empathetic heart of the operation.
And him... the grumpy, resentful soul, finally free to focus on the work that truly mattered.

He looked over at the half-finished cradle. The pine was humming a soft, contented tune. He could hear the distant, rhythmic hammering of Joric's crew paving the street. He could smell the faint scent of Lyra's herbal tea and the sharp, resinous aroma of the lute Piper was beginning to examine.

The shop was noisy. It was complicated. It was full of people.

And for the first time, Elian realized, it was starting to feel less like a workshop and more like a home.

That dialogue is perfect. It's the inevitable clash between Elian's "Art" and Piper's "Commerce." They are the two opposing forces that will either tear the workshop apart or forge it into something stronger. This scene is the first real test of their new arrangement.
