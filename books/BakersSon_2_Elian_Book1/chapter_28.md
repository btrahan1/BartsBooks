## Chapter 27: A Wager of Craft

The tax assessor, Fisc, stood his ground, his bureaucratic indignation momentarily overriding his fear. "This is not a negotiation! The law is the law! If you do not pay, we will seize this workshop!"

"You will try," Elian said, his voice a low promise.

But as Fisc puffed himself up for another retort, Lyra stepped between them, her presence a calming balm. "Assessor, a moment. Master Elian is a craftsman, not an accountant. He is merely expressing his... professional disagreement with the code's structure. Please, allow us one week. Until the end of the fiscal moon. We will review our accounts and... and the relevant statutes. We will have an answer for you then."

Fisc looked from Lyra's reasonable face to Elian's thunderous one. A week's delay was acceptable. It would give the woodworker time to realize the futility of his position. "One week," he snapped, making a sharp note on his clipboard. "And not a day more." He turned and marched out of the shop, leaving a heavy silence in his wake.

"One hundred and twenty gold?" Piper finally breathed, her face pale. "They're trying to shut us down. We can't make that in a year!"

"We made ten last month," Lyra said, though her voice was strained. "At that rate..."

"It would take a year," Piper finished, "during which we'd accrue even more taxes. It's an impossible debt. They've built a box with no doors."

Elian was silent. He walked back to the chalkboard and stared at his drawing of the impossible, collapsing structure. He had tried to fight the foreman with force. But this was a different kind of enemy. This was a system. You couldn't split a system with an axe. You had to find the grain. You had to find a leverage point.

He looked at the drawing, at the impossibly heavy roof labeled "120 Gold." The problem wasn't the weight. The problem was that he was the only one holding it up.

He needed a bigger foundation. He needed a patron more powerful than the city's treasury.

He turned, a new, dangerous, and deeply ambitious light in his eyes. He looked directly at Piper.

"Piper," he said, his voice no longer angry, but filled with a sudden, razor-sharp focus. "We need a piece. Fit for a Queen."

Piper stared at him. "A... a Queen? You want to build something for the Queen?"

"Not just for her," Elian corrected. "To *impress* her. To indebt her. We have one week. It needs to be a masterpiece. Something so beautiful, so perfect, so utterly undeniable, that to seize it for a mere tax debt would be a political scandal. Something that makes us... untouchable."

He strode towards the back room, his mind already racing, calculating.

"Tell me what you can make," he commanded over his shoulder. "A lute? A music box? Something that sings. Something that will capture the heart of the court."

Piper's mind, which had been frozen in panic, suddenly roared to life. The sheer, audacious insanity of the plan was intoxicating. It was a mad gamble, a single throw of the dice against the entire system. And it was the most exciting thing she had ever heard.

"A harp," she said, her voice filled with a sudden, fierce inspiration. "A small, lap harp. Carved from a single piece of heartwood. It would be... legendary."

Elian stopped at the door to his sanctuary. He looked back at her, a rare, wolfish grin spreading across his face.

"There is more than one way to skin a cat," he said.

He disappeared into the back room and emerged a moment later, carrying a piece of wood that made Piper and Lyra both gasp.

It was a plank of Silver-Linden, a tree that was said to weep when the moon was full. It was pale as bone, and its grain shimmered with an ethereal, silvery light. It was a piece of living music, and Elian had been saving it for decades.

"The wood is ready," Elian said, placing the priceless timber on the workbench. "The client is waiting. You have one week."

The tax collector thought he had them in a box. Elian was about to prove that if you're a master woodworker, you can always build your own door.

This is a masterclass in negotiation. Elian sets the hook with the emotional threat, and Lyra, the cool-headed manager, reels the fish in with a precise, devastating counter-offer. Piper's smirk is the perfect punctuation mark. They're no longer just a workshop; they're a finely tuned machine of craft and commerce.
