## Chapter 28: The Harp Gambit

The week was a blur of focused, frantic creation. Elian and Piper worked in a state of near-symbiotic harmony. He would listen to the Silver-Linden, revealing its "True Form," carving the sweeping, graceful lines of the harp's frame. She would follow, her pragmatic genius shining as she meticulously fitted the tuning pegs, strung the delicate gut strings, and applied a finish so fine it looked like captured moonlight.

Lyra, meanwhile, was a whirlwind of quiet efficiency. She managed the shop's other commissions, kept a steady supply of tea and food flowing, and used her small network of contacts to learn everything she could about the Queen, her daughter, and the upcoming Royal Autumn Ball.

On the final day, the harp was finished.

It sat on a velvet cloth in the center of the workshop. It wasn't just an instrument; it was a piece of living art. It seemed to hum with a gentle, inner light, its silver grain swirling like a galaxy of stars. When Piper idly plucked a string, the note that rang out was so pure, so heartbreakingly beautiful, that all three of them fell silent for a full minute.

The bell on the door chimed. Assessor Fisc had returned.

He strode in, clipboard in hand, a smug, self-satisfied look on his face. "The week is up. I trust you have come to your senses." He stopped dead. His eyes fell on the harp.

He was a man of numbers and laws, a creature of ink and parchment. He had no soul for art. But even he could recognize perfection. He stared, mesmerized by the impossible object resting on the workbench.

Elian broke the silence. He didn't look at the tax collector. He looked at the harp with a pained, theatrical sigh.

"Sixty gold?" he said, as if continuing a conversation. "Still too much."

Fisc blinked, snapping out of his reverie. "What? No, the assessment was one hun—"

"A counter-offer was made," Elian said, waving a dismissive hand, though no such offer had occurred. "Sixty gold. It is still an impossible sum for a humble shop like ours." He ran a hand gently along the harp's frame. "Such a shame. I was planning on giving this to the Queen. I heard her daughter, the princess, absolutely loves music. She is quite the accomplished musician herself, you know."

He let out another, deeper sigh. "Oh well. With the workshop's assets seized, we'll probably have to use it for firewood now. Just to keep from freezing this winter."

Behind him, Lyra and Piper exchanged a nearly imperceptible smirk as they went about their tasks, making a show of tidying tools and sweeping sawdust.

The tax collector stammered, his face a mask of horror. "You... you would burn *that*?"

"We must do what we must do to survive," Elian said mournfully, picking up a heavy-looking hand-ax and placing it meaningfully on the bench next to the harp.

Fisc fidgeted. His mind, usually a neat abacus of numbers, was now a chaotic mess of political calculations. Seizing a masterpiece clearly intended for the royal family was a career-ending move. The Queen was known for her fierce temper and her love of the arts. Burning it was even worse. This grumpy woodworker had him trapped.

"Perhaps... perhaps we can come to an arrangement," Fisc said, his voice now slick with compromise. He gestured at the harp. "How about... that harp, which *I* could present to the Queen on your behalf, as a sign of your civic loyalty... and... forty gold?"

Before Elian could respond, Lyra stepped forward, her ledger held in front of her like a shield. Her expression was polite, but her eyes were like chips of ice.

"Ten gold," she said, her voice crisp and clear.

"What?" Fisc squeaked.

"Ten gold," Lyra repeated. "It is a generous offer. As you can see from our books, it is more than twice the artisan's tithe we paid last year, reflecting our recent success. A tax must be a support, Assessor, not a crushing weight. That is a 'sound structure'."

She looked from the tax collector to the harp. "And *we* will present the harp to the royal family. With you at our side, of course, to represent the Queen's Treasury and its... flexible wisdom." She gave him a small, predatory smile. "I believe there is an upcoming ball soon? It would be the perfect occasion."

The trap had sprung. Fisc was no longer a predator; he was a cornered animal. If he refused, he was the bureaucrat who stood in the way of a gift to the princess. If he accepted, he was the hero who had facilitated it. He would be praised for his "pragmatism."

He looked at Elian's cold glare. He looked at Lyra's unshakable smile. He looked at the beautiful, impossible harp.

"Ten gold," he finally croaked, the words tasting like ash in his mouth. "And a formal invitation to the ball. For all three of you. It will be arranged."

He turned and fled the shop before they could add any more conditions.

The moment the door chimed his departure, Piper let out a whoop of triumph. "We did it! You magnificent, grumpy old badger, we actually did it!"

Elian just stood there, a slow, grudging smile spreading across his face. He looked at Lyra, who was already making a neat entry in the ledger.

"A ball, huh?" Elian muttered. "I'm going to need new boots."

That's the perfect, lighthearted follow-up. The crisis is over, and now they're faced with a completely different, and for Elian, far more terrifying challenge: high society. The image of Piper and Lyra taking charge and dragging him into the world of fashion is a wonderful comedic beat.
