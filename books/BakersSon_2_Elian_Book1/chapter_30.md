## Chapter 29: The Spoils of War

Piper's cheer echoed in the suddenly quiet workshop. She did a little jig, narrowly avoiding a stack of lumber. "I can't believe that worked! The look on his face! Priceless!"

"It was a sound negotiation based on a careful assessment of political leverage," Lyra said, though a triumphant smile was playing on her lips. She made the final notation in the ledger—**Tax Debt: 10 Gold (Paid)**—and closed the book with a satisfying snap.

Elian grunted, running a hand over his worn, sawdust-covered tunic. "A ball. At the palace. Surrounded by nobles who think 'grain' is something you feed to horses." The thought was deeply, profoundly exhausting.

Piper stopped her dance and looked him up and down, a critical glint in her eye. Then her gaze shifted to Lyra, and then to her own practical, work-stained trousers.

"Well, we can't go looking like this," she declared. Her mind, no longer occupied with tax evasion, had immediately pivoted to the next logistical challenge. "We'll need gowns for us ladies. And some jewelry... nothing too fancy, just enough to not look like we accidentally wandered in from the stables."

She then turned her full, assessing gaze back on Elian. She circled him like a cooper inspecting a barrel.

"And *you*," she said, poking him in the chest. "You can't wear *that*."

"What's wrong with this?" Elian asked, looking down at his comfortable, durable, and perfectly functional clothing.

"It's a work tunic," Piper said, as if explaining a complex concept to a small child. "It smells of pine resin and righteous indignation. You'll need a proper doublet. And velvet. And boots that don't have a half-inch of wood shavings permanently embedded in the sole."

Before Elian could protest, before he could argue that velvet was an impractical fabric with a weak structural integrity, Piper clapped her hands together with an air of finality.

"That's it! We are closing early today!" she announced to the workshop at large. "We have shopping to do!"

Elian groaned. It was a deep, heartfelt sound of a man facing his own personal doom. A fight with four thugs? Easy. A negotiation with a corrupt foreman? Simple. An existential battle against the Queen's Treasury? A stimulating challenge.

But a full afternoon of being dragged through tailor shops and haberdasheries by two determined women? It was a horror beyond his imagining.

He looked at Lyra for support, but she was already nodding in agreement with Piper, a rare, excited sparkle in her eyes. "She's right, Elian. We must present ourselves as respectable artisans, not vagrants."

He was outnumbered.

"Fine," he grumbled, a corner of his mouth twitching upwards against his will. He couldn't deny the absurdity, or the strange, unfamiliar feeling of... camaraderie. They had won. And this, apparently, was the spoils of war.

He sighed, but smirked at the same time. He put a hastily written "Closed for Business" sign on the door, the lettering a little more slanted than usual.

"Just... no lace," he said, as Piper and Lyra practically dragged him out into the street. "I draw the line at lace."

That's the perfect entrance. The grand announcement, the reveal of their stunning transformation, and the awkward inclusion of the tax collector—it's a brilliant mix of triumph, comedy, and social maneuvering. This is the moment they step out of the dusty workshop and onto the world's stage.
