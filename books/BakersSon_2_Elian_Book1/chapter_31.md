## Chapter 30: The Unlikely Quartet

The Royal Autumn Ball was a symphony of overwhelming sensation. The Grand Hall of the palace was a galaxy of glittering chandeliers, the air thick with the scent of expensive perfume and beeswax candles. An orchestra played a sweeping waltz, the music a backdrop to the low hum of a thousand conversations, the clinking of crystal glasses, and the rustle of silk and satin.

Elian felt like a piece of rough-hewn oak in a forest of delicate, polished veneers.

He stood stiffly just inside the entrance, deeply uncomfortable in a doublet of dark green velvet that felt both too soft and too restrictive. His new boots were polished to a mirror shine, but they didn't feel right. They hadn't been properly broken in.

But as he looked at his companions, he had to admit, the results were undeniable.

Lyra was a vision. She wore a simple, elegant gown of deep blue that matched her calm eyes, her hair woven with a few small, silver ornaments. She looked not like a shop manager, but like a visiting dignitary from a serene, faraway land.

Piper was a spark of fire. Her gown was a vibrant autumn orange, her red hair piled high in an intricate cascade of curls. She looked less like a woodworker and more like a mischievous fey princess who had decided to grace the mortal world with her presence.

Even Fisc, the tax collector, looked different. Freed from his drab black robes, he wore the formal gray livery of the Treasury, looking nervous but also undeniably proud to be there.

The Royal Herald, a man with a voice like a brass trumpet, banged his staff on the marble floor. The conversations nearby lulled.

"Announcing," the Herald boomed, reading from a scroll presented to him by a flustered-looking Fisc. "The Master Artisan Elian, of The Knot & Burl workshop, and his associates, Mistress Piper and Mistress Lyra."

The Herald squinted at the next line, clearly confused by the addition.

"And... uhm... Royal Tax Assessor Fisc of the Queen's Treasury."

All heads in their vicinity turned towards the newly announced party. A wave of murmurs rippled through the crowd. The city's artisans rarely received personal invitations to a Royal Ball. And they certainly didn't arrive with their tax collector.

They looked marvelous.

Elian, despite his discomfort, stood tall, his posture straight, his expression a mask of grim neutrality. Piper gave a dazzling, confident smile, a silent challenge to anyone who dared to question their presence. Lyra offered a serene, graceful nod to the assembled nobles.

They were a strange, unlikely quartet: the grumpy master, the fiery prodigy, the serene manager, and the flustered bureaucrat. They were woodworkers and a taxman, a piece of the common world that had just been dropped into the gilded cage of the court.

And as the music swelled and the nobles began to whisper, Elian knew one thing for certain.

They might not belong here. But they were definitely going to be noticed.

Oh, that is smooth. Incredibly smooth.

He takes a question he should answer with a simple "yes" or "no" and transforms it into a profound statement about his craft, his philosophy, and himself. And then he flips the script entirely, inviting *her* to join *him*. It's a power move, but it's delivered with such unique, artistic charm that it's utterly disarming.

This is the moment he stops being just a craftsman and becomes a courtier, entirely on his own terms.
