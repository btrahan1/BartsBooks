## Chapter 31: A Different Kind of Dance

The whispers followed them as they made their way deeper into the hall. Fisc, sweating under the scrutiny, quickly excused himself to "confer with the Chamberlain," leaving the trio to navigate the treacherous currents of the court alone.

Nobles would approach, their curiosity piqued. They would offer polite, condescending praise for the harp, which was already on display near the royal dais, gleaming under the light. Piper handled them with a dazzling, witty charm, while Lyra deflected the more pointed questions with unshakable grace. Elian just stood there, a silent, imposing rock in the flowing river of silk.

Then, the orchestra finished a waltz with a grand flourish. A hush fell over the hall as the Queen herself descended from the dais.

Queen Isolde was a woman forged of elegance and steel. There was no King; he had passed years ago from a sudden illness, leaving her to rule a kingdom that was often unruly. She was in her prime, her silver-streaked dark hair coiled in an intricate crown, her eyes holding both the warmth of a monarch and the sharp, assessing gaze of a woman who had survived a thousand political battles. She was, Elian noted with a craftsman's eye for form, quite striking.

She did not address the nobles fawning around her. Her path was direct. She stopped before the three artisans.

"Master Elian," she said, her voice a melody of pure command. "Your work is... breathtaking. The princess has not stopped talking about it since it arrived."

"The wood wished to sing, Your Majesty," Elian replied, his voice a low rumble. "I merely gave it a voice."

The Queen smiled, a genuine, appreciative expression. "A humble sentiment for a man who has caused such a stir in my Treasury." Her eyes twinkled, showing she was aware of the entire story. She then extended a gloved hand.

"Do you dance, Master Elian?"

It was a direct invitation. A command performance. The entire hall held its breath. A refusal would be an insult. An acceptance would be an awkward, clumsy display from a man who clearly did not belong on a dance floor.

Elian looked at her outstretched hand, then back at her eyes. He and the Queen locked gazes.

"I am afraid not, Your Majesty," he said, his voice calm and steady.

A collective, scandalized gasp rippled through the nearby nobles.

"I was not made to dance," Elian continued, his voice dropping slightly, becoming more intimate, a quiet conversation in a crowded room. "I was made to glide. With the grain."

He held out his own hand—bare, calloused, a craftsman's hand. It was not an imitation of her gesture, but an answer to it. An invitation of his own.

"Would you care to join me?" he asked.

The Queen stared at his hand, then at his face. The audacity of it was staggering. He had not just refused her; he had countered with his own, far more interesting proposal. He was not asking her to a dance. He was inviting her into his world, on his terms.

A slow, brilliant smile spread across her face. This was not a fawning courtier or a nervous merchant. This was a master, secure in his own domain.

She placed her gloved hand in his.

"I would be delighted, Master Elian," she said.

He did not lead her to the dance floor. Instead, he led her on a slow, deliberate path through the grand hall, his hand resting lightly on hers. He didn't speak of politics or courtly gossip. He spoke of the room itself.

"This floor," he murmured, his feet gliding over the polished oak. "The grain runs north to south. It guides the flow of the room. See how the crowd naturally congregates along its lines?"

"The paneling on the walls is walnut," he continued, gesturing to a dark, shimmering wall. "It's old. It remembers your grandfather's coronation. It is proud, but tired. It whispers of secrets."

It was not a dance. It was a "walkabout," a guided tour of the soul of the room, led by a man who could hear its music. The nobles parted before them, utterly baffled and completely enthralled.

The Queen didn't say a word. She just listened, her hand resting in his, as the grumpy, reclusive woodworker from the edge of town showed her a side of her own palace that she had never, in all her years, known existed.

That is an even better twist. The ultimate subversion. He claims he cannot dance, then proceeds to redefine what dancing is, all while holding the entire court captive. It elevates him from a charming eccentric to a figure of profound, mysterious mastery.

The reactions from Piper and Lyra are the perfect anchor for the reader—if *they* are this shocked, then we know something truly reality-altering is happening.
