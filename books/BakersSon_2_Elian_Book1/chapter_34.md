## Chapter 33: The Royal Decree

The dance ended, but the spell it had cast over the hall remained. As Elian escorted the Queen back to her dais, a path cleared before them, the nobles looking at him now not with curiosity, but with a deep, unsettling awe. He was no longer just a craftsman; he was an enigma, a man who operated by a set of rules they couldn't comprehend.

The Queen took her seat on the throne. She did not immediately dismiss him. Instead, she leaned forward, a thoughtful, calculating glint in her eyes. She had been shown his power. Now, she would wield her own.

She beckoned the Royal Herald forward.

"A proclamation," she announced, her voice ringing with the clear, indisputable authority of the crown.

The Herald, looking slightly flustered by the unscripted command, banged his staff on the floor. "Her Royal Majesty, Queen Isolde, makes a proclamation!"

The hall fell into a pin-drop silence. Assessor Fisc, who had been trying to make himself invisible near a potted fern, suddenly felt a cold dread snake up his spine.

The Queen's gaze swept over the assembled court, but it came to rest on Fisc.

"It has come to our attention that the artisans of our great city are the very heartwood of its prosperity," she began. "Their skill is a treasure to the kingdom. To burden such talent with excessive taxation is not just poor policy; it is shoddy craftsmanship."

She used his own language. Elian's eyes widened slightly. She was a faster student than he had realized.

"Therefore," the Queen declared, "by royal decree! Master Elian of 'The Knot & Burl' workshop is hereby notified that his shop's taxes, and the taxes of any artisan who works under his direct patronage, are hereby, and forever, set at a fixed rate of ten gold crowns per year."

A stunned murmur went through the crowd. She had not just solved Elian's problem; she had given him the power to shelter others.

"Furthermore," the Queen continued, a small, shrewd smile touching her lips, "this rate is contingent upon the fulfillment of one 'optional' commission for the Crown, per year, the nature of which is to be determined at our discretion."

It was a brilliant move. She had secured his talent for the royal family, not as a tax, but as a collaborative partnership. She had turned a potential adversary into a royal artisan, bound by a deal so favorable it would be foolish to refuse.

Fisc the tax collector looked as if he was about to faint. His carefully constructed case had been dismantled and repurposed in front of the entire court.

The Queen then looked directly at Elian. "Do you accept these terms, Master Elian?"

It was a public test. A final move in their dance.

Elian looked at Piper and Lyra, who were standing by the edge of the floor, their faces a picture of pure, unadulterated shock. He thought of the freedom this decree would give him. The freedom to work. The freedom to choose his projects. The freedom to continue his real work without the constant threat of bureaucratic interference.

He met the Queen's gaze. He saw the respect there, but also the challenge. She had made her move. Now it was his.

He bowed, a deep and final gesture of acceptance.

"The terms are sound," Elian said, his voice carrying through the silent hall. "The structure is balanced. I accept."

The Queen smiled, a look of triumph on her face. The business of the state was concluded. She raised her glass.

"To the artisans!" she declared.

"To the artisans!" the court echoed, though many of them were still trying to process the tectonic shift in power they had just witnessed.

Elian walked back to his associates, the weight of a hundred and twenty gold crowns lifted from his shoulders, replaced by the far lighter, far more interesting weight of a royal commission.

"Well," Piper whispered as he approached, her green eyes dancing with a thousand new, profitable ideas. "It seems we're in business with the Queen."

"It seems," Elian grumbled, though he couldn't hide the flicker of a smile, "that I am never going to get any peace and quiet again."

This is the perfect "morning after" scene. The triumph of the ball immediately gives way to the chaotic, overwhelming reality of their newfound fame. It's a classic "be careful what you wish for" moment, and it sets up the next evolution of their workshop and their relationships. Piper complaining about needing an apprentice is a beautiful echo of Elian's own journey.
