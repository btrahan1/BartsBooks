## Chapter 34: The Price of Fame

The sun rose the morning after the ball, blissfully unaware of the chaos it was about to illuminate. Elian was in the workshop early, as always. He had expected the quiet hum of the wood, the familiar scent of sawdust, a return to normalcy.

He was wrong.

He stared out the front window of The Knot & Burl. A line had formed. It snaked down the newly paved street and around the corner. It was a line of nobles in fine carriages, merchants in pressed doublets, and guild masters holding scrolls. The shop wasn't even open yet.

Piper came down from the loft, rubbing sleep from her eyes. She stopped dead beside Elian, her jaw dropping as she took in the scene.

"What... what is all this?" she breathed.

"This," Elian said, his voice a low, horrified groan, "is the 'optional' part of the commission."

Lyra joined them, a cup of tea in her hand. She looked at the line, then back at the small, cramped workshop. Her calm, managerial mind immediately assessed the logistical nightmare.

"We're going to need a bigger shop," she stated, her voice filled with a quiet, unshakeable certainty.

Piper, whose initial shock was rapidly being replaced by the frantic calculations of a master merchant, started pacing. "Bigger shop, more benches, more lumber... We can't handle this volume. The waitlist will be a year long! They'll get impatient! They'll go elsewhere!"

She stopped pacing and looked at her own hands, then at the mountain of potential commissions waiting outside their door. She let out a long, unhappy groan that was laced with a hint of terrified excitement. It was the exact sound Elian had made a month ago.

"And I'm going to need an apprentice," Piper declared.

Elian and Lyra both turned to look at her. The irony was so thick you could carve it.

"An apprentice?" Elian asked, a single eyebrow raised. "But you hate teaching. You said it's inefficient."

"I also hate turning away a line of walking gold purses!" Piper shot back, gesturing wildly at the street. "I can't do all the 'Good' work myself! I need someone with decent hands and no soul to handle the spinning wheels and the fence posts!"

She was parroting his own words back at him. Elian stared at her, at the fiery, overwhelmed, and fiercely ambitious young woman who had taken his quiet world and turned it into a booming enterprise.

He should have been annoyed. He should have been horrified. But as he stood there, watching the sunrise glint off the polished roofs of the noble carriages, a slow, grudging smile spread across his face.

The world had come knocking. It was loud, demanding, and utterly exhausting. But it was also a challenge. A new, complex, and beautifully flawed structure to analyze.

"Alright," Elian said, turning away from the window. He picked up a piece of charcoal and a clean plank of pine. "Let's design a bigger shop. One with thicker walls. And a back door."

This is a fantastic development. It shows Elian's growth through his problem-solving. A month ago, his solution to stress was to retreat into the forest. Now, his solution is to go *towards* people—specifically, the ones he beat up—to delegate and solve a new problem.

It's a huge step for his character. He's not just a master craftsman; he's becoming a leader who builds teams. The "semi-remodeled" line is a perfect touch of dry humor.
