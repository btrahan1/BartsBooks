## Chapter 35: A Different Kind of Partnership

The next two weeks were a special kind of hell.

Elian’s workshop, once his silent sanctuary, was now a nexus of organized chaos. It was impossible to get any work done. The front of the shop was Lyra’s domain, a fortress of ledgers and schedules where she deftly managed the unending tide of nobles. The back was a cacophony of construction noise as Joric and his stonemasons worked on the expansion, their cheerful hammering a constant, grinding assault on Elian’s concentration.

And in the middle was Piper, who had hired a lanky, terrified apprentice of her own and was running her production line like a drill sergeant. "Less talking, more sanding! That chair leg isn't going to finish itself!"

Elian was losing his mind. He couldn't hear the wood. He couldn't think. The Iron-Root in his back room had gone completely silent, refusing to speak amidst the racket.

"That's it!" he finally roared one afternoon, throwing down a piece of chalk. "I'm going for a walk!"

He stormed towards the back of the shop, climbed through the hole in the wall that would eventually be a new door, and stalked out into the alley. He needed silence. He needed space.

And for the first time in... forever... Elian needed a drink. A strong one.

He found himself walking towards the docks, the salty air a familiar, bracing scent. His feet, seemingly of their own accord, led him to the one place he knew served ale strong enough to quiet the noise in his head: The Gilded Mug.

He stopped outside. The tavern had been repaired. A new, very sturdy-looking oak table stood where the old one had been. He could tell from the grain it was one of Joric's. Good work.

He wasn't here just for a drink. He had a problem, and the solution, strangely enough, was inside this very tavern. His problem was gold, but his real problem was the commissions that gold required. He couldn't bring himself to build another gaudy cabinet. But Piper couldn't handle all the high-end work alone. He needed another partner. A different kind of craftsman.

*"Time to mend some bridges, and acquire another 'partner'," he thought, as he stood outside the tavern he had recently 'semi-remodeled' with his ax.*

He pushed the door open. The tavern, once loud and boisterous, fell into a sudden, shocked silence. All eyes turned to him. The bartender froze mid-pour.

The former foreman, Silas—now just a patron nursing a cheap ale—saw him and actually shrank back in his booth.

Elian ignored them all. His eyes scanned the room and found his target.

The metalsmith. His neighbor. The old man whose forge Joric's crew had repaired. His name was Kael. He was a master of his craft, but his business was failing, crushed by the larger, more aggressive guilds.

Kael looked up from his drink, his eyes narrowing as Elian approached. He clearly hadn't forgotten Elian's "request" to fix his shop.

Elian slid into the booth opposite the old man. He placed a single, heavy gold crown on the table.

"I have a proposition for you," Elian said, his voice low and direct.

Kael stared at the gold, then back at Elian, his expression a mixture of suspicion and curiosity.

"I'm listening," the metalsmith grumbled.

"My clients," Elian began, "have a... taste for ornamentation. Inlays. Hinges. Drawer pulls. Things of that nature. My work is with wood. I do not 'dabble' in metal."

He pushed the gold coin across the table.

"I need a partner," Elian said. "Someone whose work is as good as my own. Someone who understands the difference between 'functional' and 'good'. I will handle the wood. You will handle the metal. You will have your own bench in my new shop. We will split the profits on any collaborative piece, fifty-fifty."

The old metalsmith stared at the gold, then at Elian. Fifty-fifty. It was an unheard-of offer. It was a lifeline. It was a partnership of equals.

"Why me?" Kael finally asked, his voice a hoarse whisper.

Elian looked around the tavern, at the fearful faces watching them.

"Because," Elian said, a flicker of a smile on his face, "I've already handled your renovations. And I find I have a taste for fixing things that are broken."

That's a fantastic, comedic beat. Elian's reputation now precedes him, but not in the way he intends. He's trying to offer a business proposition, but all Silas hears is the prelude to another "invoice collection." It's a great way to show how his past actions have created an aura of intimidation he now has to work around.

This also shows his brilliant, pragmatic mind at work again: why fight a corrupt system when you can just hire the corrupt guy who knows how to navigate it?
