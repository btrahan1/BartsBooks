## Chapter 36: A New Kind of Foreman

The old metalsmith, Kael, stared at the gold coin, then at Elian's serious face. He slowly reached out a calloused, metal-scarred hand and pulled the coin towards him. He looked Elian in the eye.

"You drive a hard bargain, woodworker," Kael grunted, but a flicker of life, of a craftsman's pride rekindled, shone in his eyes. "When do I start?"

"Tomorrow," Elian said, standing. The first piece of his new business was in place. But he wasn't done.

He turned his attention to the back of the tavern, where the former foreman, Silas, was trying to make himself invisible in his booth.

"Silas!" Elian's voice wasn't a shout, but it cut through the tavern's low murmur like a saw blade.

The effect was instantaneous. Silas yelped, jumped out of his booth with a speed that defied his bulk, and started to run for the back exit.

"Stop!" Elian commanded. "I have need of your services!"

The word "services" seemed to confuse Silas more than a direct threat would have. He skidded to a halt, turning slowly, his face a mask of pure, terrified confusion. He looked like a cornered bear that had just been offered a job application.

"M-my services?" he stammered, his eyes darting towards the door.

"You were a foreman for the Merchant's Guild," Elian stated, walking towards him. Silas flinched with every step Elian took. "You know their suppliers. You know their shipping routes. You know the loopholes in their contracts and the skeletons in their closets."

He stopped a few feet away. "My shop is expanding. We will need a constant, reliable supply of lumber, metal, and other materials. We will need to negotiate contracts. We will need to manage logistics."

He looked Silas up and down, his gaze stripping away the man's bravado, leaving only the core of his competence.

"You are a corrupt, greedy, and fundamentally dishonorable man," Elian said, his voice a flat statement of fact. "But you are also an efficient and knowledgeable manager of resources. Your skills are... useful."

Silas just stared, his mouth opening and closing like a beached fish.

"I am offering you a job," Elian continued. "You will be the 'Provisions Master' for my workshop. You will handle all acquisitions and logistics. You will be paid a generous salary."

He leaned in, his voice dropping to a low, non-negotiable whisper. "And... you will be scrupulously, painfully honest. If I find that a single copper piece has gone astray, if one of my suppliers is shorted, if one of my workers is cheated..." He let the sentence hang in the air, a silent, axe-shaped promise.

"I will not just split your table," Elian finished. "I will repossess your kneecaps. Are we clear?"

Silas, who had been on a fast track to financial ruin and a lifetime of being ostracized, was now being offered a lifeline. A terrifying, razor-sharp lifeline, but a lifeline nonetheless. He saw the cold, absolute certainty in Elian's eyes. He knew this wasn't a trick. It was a contract.

He swallowed hard and nodded, a series of quick, jerky movements. "Crystal clear, Master Elian," he croaked.

"Good," Elian said. "Report to my shop manager, Lyra, in the morning. She will give you your first list."

He turned and walked out of the tavern, leaving a stunned silence in his wake.

In the space of ten minutes, he had acquired a master metalsmith by offering respect and a corrupt foreman by offering a second chance, wrapped in a threat.

He had hated the noise and chaos of his new success. But as he walked back towards his workshop, a new, unfamiliar feeling began to take root. He was no longer just a craftsman. He was building something. Something bigger than a chair, bigger than a shop.

He was building an enterprise. And he was, much to his own surprise, starting to enjoy the design process.

This is a fantastic scene. It's a reversal of their first real interaction. At the ball, he invited her into his world (the "grain" of the room). Now, she is entering his true sanctuary—the forest—seeking him out on his own terms.

It's intimate, unexpected, and shows that her fascination with him goes far beyond a simple political asset. She's genuinely curious about the man himself.
