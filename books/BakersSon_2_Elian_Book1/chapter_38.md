## Chapter 37: The Queen's Walkabout

A month passed. The new workshop rose from the ground, a masterpiece of Joric's stonework and Elian's timber framing. Kael, the metalsmith, had set up his forge, the rhythmic clang of his hammer a new, surprisingly welcome addition to the shop's music. Silas, under Lyra's watchful eye, had proven to be a ruthlessly efficient Provisions Master, leveraging his old contacts to secure materials at prices that made Piper grin with avaricious delight.

The Knot & Burl was no longer a shop; it was a guild in its own right. A powerhouse of craft and commerce.

And Elian was suffocating.

The demands were endless. Designs for Kael's metalwork. Consultations with Joric on roof trusses. Arguments with Piper over the cost of Silver-Linden versus the profit margin. He hadn't had a quiet conversation with a piece of wood in weeks.

One morning, he simply walked out the back door, past the bustling construction, and into the forest. He didn't tell anyone where he was going. He just needed to walk. He needed to remember the feel of damp earth under his boots, the scent of pine, the silent, ancient language of the trees. He roamed the woods, seeking harmony.

He was deep in the old growth, his hand resting on the bark of a massive, sleeping oak, when a voice, calm and melodic, broke the silence.

"Lost, Master Elian?"

Elian froze. He turned slowly.

Queen Isolde sat astride a striking grey gelding, not twenty feet away. She was not dressed in royal gowns, but in a simple, elegant riding habit of dark green. There were no guards, no entourage. She was utterly, completely alone.

Elian was so shocked he forgot all protocol. "How did you find me here, of all places?" he asked. "I would have come if you had summoned me."

"I know," the Queen said, a small smile on her face. She swung down from the horse with a practiced grace. "But I didn't want to 'summon' you. That is for subjects and functionaries. I wanted to just talk. Man to woman. If you have the time?"

He stared at her. The most powerful person in the kingdom had tracked him down in the middle of a forest just to "talk."

"My time is my own, Your Majesty," he finally said, bowing his head slightly.

"Good," she said, looping the horse's reins over a low-hanging branch. "Because I find myself in need of a 'walkabout', as you call it. And I am told you are an excellent guide."

She looked around at the towering trees, at the dappled sunlight filtering through the canopy. "You come here to listen, don't you? To the wood."

"It is quieter than the city," Elian admitted.

"Teach me," she said, her voice a simple, direct plea. "You showed me the grain of my own ballroom. Show me the grain of this."

It was an invitation he couldn't refuse. Not from a Queen, but from a student. He had become a master to a healer, a boss to a pragmatist, a leader to a crew of craftsmen. Now, it seemed, he was to be a teacher to a Queen.

"The first lesson," he said, his voice returning to its familiar, gruffly instructional tone, "is to be silent. The wood will not speak if you are shouting."

He began to walk, deeper into the forest. The Queen fell into step beside him, her royal bearing replaced by the quiet, attentive posture of a willing apprentice. The world of politics and commerce and taxes faded away, leaving only the two of them, and the ancient, whispering language of the trees.

That is a masterful reversal of roles. He is no longer just her subject or even her "interesting artisan." He is using his unique senses—his ability to read the "grain" of things—to diagnose a problem within *her*. He's offering his services not as a woodworker, but as a consultant, a confidant, a healer of a different sort.

This moves their relationship into a truly intimate and unprecedented territory.
