## Chapter 4: The Listener

The week after the Mayor's desk was delivered (with a huffy note from Loric about it being "overly ornate" and "distracting"), Elian was in a foul mood. The sycamore's purr of contentment had been replaced by a cacophony of demanding, ignorant voices.

"No."

Elian didn't even look up from sharpening his chisels. The portly merchant standing in the middle of his showroom blinked.

"But... I haven't even told you what I want yet," the merchant stammered.

"You want a new sign for your shop," Elian said, testing the edge of a gouge with his thumb. "Bigger than your competitor's. You want gaudy, gold-leaf lettering. The pine you'd want me to use would rather be a ship's coffin, which is fitting, as your business practices are killing this town's economy."

The merchant's jaw dropped. He sputtered, turned a shade of purple, and stormed out.

The next was a young noblewoman. "I require a new jewelry box. Something delicate. Perhaps with inlaid mother-of-pearl..."

"No."

Then a farmer. "My prize-winning sow needs a new trough..."

"No."

One patron, a pompous architect, had the supreme audacity to bring in his own design, drawn on expensive, crisp parchment. It was for a gazebo, a monstrosity of geometric angles and nonsensical flourishes that defied the very nature of wood.

Elian took the parchment, held it up to the light, and nodded thoughtfully. "An interesting interpretation," he said, his voice dangerously polite.

He walked over to the small iron stove he used for heating glue pots, opened the grate, and dropped the plans inside. The architect watched, speechless, as his design curled into black ash.

"The nerve," Elian muttered after the man had fled.

He was done. Done with clients. Done with their tasteless, soulless demands. He was about to lock the door for the rest of the day—perhaps the rest of the week—when the bell chimed one last time.

Elian didn't even turn around. "Whatever it is, the answer is no. Unless you are on fire and require a bucket of water, in which case the answer is 'perhaps'."

A nervous silence followed.

"Uh... sorry to bother you," a young man's voice finally said. It was hesitant, unsure.

Elian sighed and turned, ready to deliver another scathing dismissal. But he stopped.

The young man standing there was not a merchant or a noble. He was plainly dressed, with the earnest, slightly lost look of a traveler. But that wasn't what made Elian pause.

It was what the man was carrying.

He held a length of wood. It was a branch, thick as a man's forearm, of pale, silvery ash. It wasn't dead wood; Elian could feel a faint, sleepy hum of life from it. But what was truly remarkable was that the branch was almost perfectly straight, a rare gift from a tree.

The young man held it out, not like a piece of lumber, but like he was holding a sacred object.

"Sir wood master," he began, his cheeks flushing slightly. "Uhm... this... this is going to sound crazy... but this... spoke to me."

Elian's world tilted on its axis. He stared at the young man, his carefully constructed wall of cynicism beginning to crack.

"Spoke to you?" Elian repeated, his voice barely a whisper.

"Yeah," the young man said, looking down at the branch with a mixture of confusion and reverence. "I was in the woods north of town, and I just... heard it. A sound, but not a sound. A feeling. Something about..." he trailed off, looking embarrassed.

"Something about wanting to be a staff?" Elian finished for him.

The young man's head snapped up, his eyes wide with shock. "Yes! How did you know?"

Elian walked forward slowly. He reached out, not to the young man, but to the wood. He didn't touch it. He just held his hand an inch from the bark.

He could feel it. The ash branch was humming with a clear, singular purpose. It wasn't a complex story like the podium, or a sad resignation like the desk. It was a song. A song of balance, of defense, of a perfect partnership between a wielder and his weapon. It didn't just *want* to be a staff; it was *already* a staff, waiting to be revealed.

For the first time in years, Elian was looking at someone who wasn't blind. Someone who could hear the music.

"You're a Listener," Elian said, the words a statement, not a question.

"A what?" the young man asked.

Elian ignored him. He looked at the branch, then back at the traveler. His [Grain Sight] activated on its own. He saw the golden lines of potential in the ash branch, a perfect, unwavering core. And he saw the faint, shimmering aura of the young man—a Ki, a life force, that was strangely, harmonically in tune with the wood he held.

They were a match.

"Come in," Elian said, his voice gruff, but the hostility was gone, replaced by a new, startling intensity. "Close the door. We have work to do."

He had spent his life surrounded by the deaf. He had just found another soul who could hear. And that changed everything.

This adds a fantastic layer of depth to the visitor. He's not just a random person who got lucky; he's a man with a hidden, "gentle" ambition, which is why he's so attuned to the "voice" of the wood. He's a kindred spirit to Elian—a man whose true calling is at odds with his station.

This gives Elian a new purpose. He isn't just carving a staff; he's crafting the key that might unlock this man's true potential.
