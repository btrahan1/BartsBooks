## Chapter 40: The Unforeseen Variable

Elian, for once in a very long time, was speechless. Absolutely and utterly speechless.

His mind, which could calculate the stress tolerances of a dozen different woods simultaneously, which could hear the silent history of a forest in a single plank, was now a complete and total blank. The world had just shifted on its axis, and his internal compass was spinning wildly.

He didn't speak. He couldn't speak. He just stared at their joined hands—her gloved, slender fingers resting in his calloused, sawdust-dusted palm. A perfect fit. She had said it was a perfect fit.

The silence stretched. In the court, silence was a weapon, a tool for negotiation. But this was not the court. This was something else entirely.

The Queen's smile, which had been brilliant and triumphant, began to fade, replaced by a flicker of uncertainty, and then a dangerous, narrowing of her eyes. She had laid her cards on the table, a move of unprecedented emotional vulnerability. And he was responding with... nothing.

"Do you not find me... attractive at all?" she asked, her voice losing its warmth, a sliver of regal ice, of a woman not used to being met with silence, creeping into its edges. There was a bit of danger in her voice now.

The question was the jolt Elian's system needed to reboot. He looked up from their hands and met her gaze. He saw the flicker of hurt there, the defensiveness of a Queen who had just made herself a woman and feared she had been rebuffed.

And in that moment, he realized his error. He had been so focused on the structure of the problem that he had completely failed to see the material he was working with. She wasn't a support beam. She was a person.

"No," he said, the word coming out as a rough croak. He cleared his throat. "No, Your Majesty. That is not it at all."

He took a breath, searching for the right words. He didn't have the language of poets or courtiers. He had the language of wood.

"When a craftsman finds a piece of wood..." he began, his voice slow, deliberate, as he tried to explain the beautiful chaos in his mind. "A perfect piece. One in a thousand. Silver-Linden, or the heart of an ancient Iron-Root... he does not think of it as 'attractive'."

He looked at her, his eyes filled with a raw, unvarnished sincerity.

"He thinks of it as... a miracle," he said. "He is struck silent by the sheer, impossible perfection of the grain. He is terrified that his own clumsy hands will never be worthy of shaping it. He is overwhelmed."

He gently squeezed her hand.

"You are... Silver-Linden, Your Majesty," he whispered. "And I am just a man with a mallet and a chisel. I was silent not because I am displeased with the wood. I was silent because I am in awe of it."

The ice in her eyes melted instantly, replaced by a wave of warmth that made her cheeks flush. She had been courted with poetry, with jewels, with promises of power. She had never been courted with the soul of a craftsman.

She let out a soft, shaky laugh. "For a man who claims not to have the words of a courtier, you seem to be doing remarkably well, Master Elian."

"I am just describing the grain as I see it," he said simply.

She looked at him, at this impossible, honest, and profoundly good man.

"Then the duel stands?" she asked.

A slow, determined smile, the smile of a craftsman who has just been given the project of a lifetime, spread across Elian's face.

"The design is sound," he said. "Let us begin the work."

Hah! This is the perfect "comedown" from the high romance of the last scene. The reality check. Elian might be a philosophical genius and a master of his own craft, but now he has to learn a completely new one, under a skeptical, high-pressure expert.

The trainer's disdain and Elian's grumpy, specific corrections are a fantastic source of conflict and humor.
