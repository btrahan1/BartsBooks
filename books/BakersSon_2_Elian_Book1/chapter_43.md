## Chapter 42: The Grain of the Fight

For ten days, the training was a relentless, humiliating grind. Every morning, Ser Kaelen would dismantle Elian's defenses with contemptuous ease. Elian learned the basic parries, the lunges, the footwork. He was a quick study, but he was merely mimicking. His heart wasn't in it. The saber remained a foreign object in his hand, a tool without a soul.

Kaelen was in despair. The duel was in four days. "He is strong. He is fast. He will not die immediately," the Captain reported grimly to the Queen. "But he will lose. Valerius will simply disarm him and humiliate him."

On the morning of the eleventh day, Kaelen arrived at the training grounds before dawn, determined to drill Elian until he dropped. But he stopped in the shadows of the armory, frozen by the sight before him.

Elian had arrived early. Long before the sun rose.

He was alone in the center of the sand pit, illuminated by a single, pre-dawn training lamp. He was not holding a saber. He held a staff, one Kaelen had not seen before. It was a simple, elegant piece of dark, polished wood, about six feet long.

And Elian was not practicing clumsy sword forms. He was... flowing.

He held the staff, a weapon that should have been heavy and unwieldy, in two hands, twirling it like it was a toothpick. He moved across the sand not with the rigid steps of a duelist, but like a dervish. He was art in motion. The staff was a blur, a seamless extension of his body, creating a shield of impenetrable motion around him. It was a dance of devastating, controlled power. One moment he would spin, the staff a whirlwind; the next he would stop, the end of the staff held inches from an imaginary foe's throat, his form a statue of perfect, lethal stillness.

Kaelen watched in silence, his mouth agape. The "cornered badger" was gone. This was a master. A master of a discipline Kaelen had never seen.

A soft footstep beside him made him turn. The Queen stood by his side, wrapped in a simple cloak, her eyes fixed on the display. She too had come to watch the morning's training.

"That," Kaelen finally breathed, his voice filled with a profound, grudging awe, "is not the man I have been training."

"No," the Queen said softly, her eyes never leaving Elian. "That is the man I danced with."

She watched for a long time as Elian moved, a perfect union of man and wood. She saw the confidence, the grace, the absolute, undeniable competence. Then she turned to her Captain of the Guard.

"Ser Kaelen," she asked, her voice deceptively casual. "Is there a rule? In the ancient codes of honor? Is there a rule that says this duel *must* be fought with sabers?"

Kaelen blinked, the question cutting through years of tradition. "Well... no, Your Majesty. A duel of honor is typically fought with matched weapons, but the challenger—Lord Valerius—merely challenged 'to a duel', not 'to a duel of sabers'."

The Queen looked back at Elian, who was now holding the staff in one hand, its balance so perfect it seemed to defy gravity.

"Is it required," she continued, a slow, cunning smile spreading across her face, "for him to use... what did he call it? A long steak knife?"

Kaelen stared at her, then back at the whirling figure of the woodworker. A slow, disbelieving grin of his own began to form. He had been trying to teach a bird to swim. It had never occurred to him to simply let the man fly.

"No, Your Majesty," Kaelen said, a new, fierce hope in his voice. "It is not."

That's the perfect turning point. The trainer finally sees the light and adapts the training to the student, rather than forcing the student to conform. It's the moment of breakthrough, where Elian's "true self" is finally acknowledged and unleashed.

This scene will be a fantastic display of his power and a moment of deep satisfaction for the reader who has been waiting for him to show his true colors.
