## Chapter 43: A Proper Tool

The first rays of dawn were just beginning to touch the highest spires of the palace. Elian finished his kata, his breathing even, his body humming with the familiar, comforting Resonance of the wood. The staff in his hands felt right. It was a piece of Iron-Root, the brother to the piece in his back room, one he had been shaping in secret every night after his humiliating saber lessons. It was heavy, dense, and utterly alive.

He sensed them before he saw them. He stopped, lowering the staff, and turned to face the shadows.

The Queen and Ser Kaelen stepped out into the light. Elian immediately felt a flush of embarrassment, like a student caught practicing a forbidden art. He started to lower the staff, to hide it.

"Don't," Kaelen's voice boomed, but there was no anger in it. There was... awe.

The Captain of the Guard walked forward, his eyes not on Elian, but on the Iron-Root staff. He walked around Elian, his gaze analytical, assessing the flow of movement he had just witnessed.

He stopped in front of Elian. He looked at the saber hanging uselessly at Elian's belt.

"Put that 'long steak knife' away," Kaelen commanded.

Elian blinked, confused.

"You heard me," the Captain said, a gruff, almost cheerful tone in his voice. "It's a disgrace to good steel for a man who hates it to wear it. Now... take out your staff. The one you were just using."

Elian hesitantly brought the Iron-Root staff back to a ready position.

Kaelen drew his own saber. But he didn't assume a dueling stance. He just looked at Elian, a new, profound respect in his eyes.

"Show me what you can do," Kaelen said.

It wasn't a challenge. It was an invitation.

And Elian did.

He didn't hold back. He unleashed the fluid, powerful, and utterly unorthodox style he had forged in the forest, in the workshop, and in the quiet of his own mind. He was a whirlwind, the Iron-Root staff a blur of motion.

Kaelen didn't try to score points. He simply tried to survive. He was a master swordsman, but he had never faced anything like this. Elian's style had no name, no predictable pattern. It was a fusion of perfect defense and explosive, unexpected offense.

A saber is a weapon of lines—thrusts, slashes, parries. The staff was a weapon of circles. Elian created a spinning, impenetrable shield that Kaelen's linear attacks simply couldn't breach. The saber would hiss in, and the staff would be there, not to block it with a jarring clang, but to guide it, to redirect its energy, to spin it harmlessly away.

Then, from within that perfect defense, the staff would lash out like a serpent. A quick jab to the ribs. A low sweep aimed at the ankles. A stunning blow to the flat of Kaelen's own blade that sent a painful vibration up his arm.

They "sparred" for ten minutes. Kaelen, the Captain of the Royal Guard, never landed a single blow. Elian, the woodworker, never once looked strained.

Finally, Elian spun the staff in a final, dizzying display and brought it to a sudden, perfect halt, the tip resting an inch from Kaelen's throat.

The Captain stood frozen, sweat beading on his forehead, his saber held in a useless defensive posture. He was utterly, completely defeated.

He let out a long, slow breath, a sound of pure, unadulterated shock. He lowered his saber.

"By the gods," Kaelen whispered, looking from the staff to Elian's calm, focused face. "We have been training the wrong man." He then broke into a short, sharp bark of laughter. "Lord Valerius is going to have a very, very bad day."

The Queen, who had been watching from the side with a serene, proud smile, simply nodded.

"Indeed," she said. "The lesson is about to begin."
