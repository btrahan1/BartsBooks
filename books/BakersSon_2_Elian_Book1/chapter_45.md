## Chapter 44: The Peacock and the Stick

The day of the duel arrived, bright and cold. The royal training grounds had been transformed into a grand arena, with temporary stands erected to hold the hundreds of nobles and wealthy merchants who had come to witness the spectacle.

The crowd was larger than anyone had expected. The story of the grumpy woodworker, the Queen's Ball, and the challenge for the Queen's hand had spread like wildfire. It was the social event of the season.

Lord Valerius entered the arena to a roar of applause from the assembled nobility. He was a vision of martial perfection, dressed in a tailored white dueling tunic, his silver-inlaid saber gleaming at his side. He was confident, arrogant, and ready to reclaim his honor.

Then, Elian entered.

He wore a simple, dark green tunic. He was not carrying a saber. He held the six-foot staff of polished Iron-Root.

A wave of confusion, then derision, rippled through the crowd.

"What is this!" Lord Valerius bellowed, his voice dripping with outrage as Elian walked to the center of the arena. "You come to a duel of honor with a peasant's walking stick? You dare to insult me a second time?"

There were snickers from the stands. There were laughs. There were jeers of "Get him a real sword!"

But scattered in the crowd, there were a few 'in the know'. Ser Kaelen stood with the Royal Guard, his arms crossed, a grim, satisfied smirk on his face. Piper and Lyra sat in a specially reserved section, looking utterly unconcerned. And in the section reserved for the clergy and honored guests of the Temple, a young healer in the gray robes of the Silent Leaf stood quietly, holding a beautiful, spiraling ash staff of his own.

Finn watched the scene, a small, confident smile on his face. He heard the mockery, but it didn't touch him. He knew what that "stick" represented. He knew the man who held it.

"I will have to do some healing here afterwards," Finn whispered to himself, his healer's senses already assessing the potential for broken bones and bruised egos. "I hope 'Lord Valerius' isn't too badly injured."

His whisper was soft, but the acoustics were perfect. On the royal dais just above him, the Queen heard him. She turned her head slightly, her gaze falling on the young, handsome healer and the exquisite staff he held. A flicker of recognition crossed her face.

She had heard of Finn. The novices at the Temple were calling him "The Listener." The High Priestess had personally sent a letter to the palace, praising the "unprecedented talent" of her new student, a boy who could soothe a fever with a touch and whose diagnostic skills were said to border on the supernatural. He was the pride of the Temple.

The Queen looked from Finn back to Elian, who was now standing calmly in the center of the ring, ignoring the insults. She saw the connection. The two masters. The two staffs.

The tension in her shoulders, the last vestiges of her worry, finally melted away. Elian was not alone. He had a support system she hadn't even known existed.

She tapped her lips thoughtfully, considering. A healer of legendary talent, a protege of her own champion, and strikingly handsome. She had a niece, third in line for the throne of the Southern Isles, about his age. A political alliance could be... beneficial.

The Herald banged his staff. The duel was about to begin.

The Queen settled back in her throne, a small, secret, and thoroughly satisfied smile on her face. The day was already full of fascinating possibilities.

This is a crucial character beat for Valerius. Making him recognize Elian's competence elevates him from a one-dimensional "arrogant noble" into a worthy antagonist. He's not just a peacock; he's a skilled predator who has learned from his past humiliation. This raises the stakes of the duel significantly.

The "match to three points" is also a great structural choice. It allows for a narrative arc within the fight itself: a back-and-forth of strategy and adaptation, rather than a single, quick resolution.
