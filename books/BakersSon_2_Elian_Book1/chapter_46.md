## Chapter 45: The First Point

The Herald's voice echoed across the silent arena. "The duel of honor is joined! The terms are to three points! A point is scored by a clean strike to the torso, a disarm, or a yield. Let the combatants begin!"

Lord Valerius was arrogant, pompous, and utterly confident, but he was no fool. The public humiliation at the tournament had burned a valuable, painful lesson into his soul. He would not underestimate this woodworker again. For all his bluster about the "stick," he recognized competence. And the man who stood before him, calm and rooted as an ancient oak, was competent. Of that, there was no doubt.

He would take this seriously. Very seriously. The match was to three points, and he would secure his first point quickly and decisively.

Valerius did not charge. He did not twirl. He flowed forward, his saber held in a low, conservative guard, the tip tracing small, testing circles in the air. He was a picture of classical, disciplined swordsmanship.

Elian stood his ground, the Iron-Root staff held in a simple, two-handed grip before him. He was a wall.

Valerius began to circle, his footwork silent and precise. He was testing Elian's defenses, looking for an opening, a flicker of hesitation. Elian mirrored him, turning smoothly, the staff always between them. It was a chess match of silent, coiled tension.

The crowd watched, mesmerized. This was not the brawl they had expected. This was a duel between two masters.

Valerius saw his opening. It was a feint within a feint. He lunged, a classic thrust aimed at Elian's shoulder. As Elian moved to block, Valerius, with a flick of his wrist so fast it was almost invisible, changed the angle of his blade, redirecting it downward in a sweeping slash aimed at Elian's exposed ribs. It was a brilliant, textbook maneuver.

Elian was ready for the thrust. He was not ready for the slash.

He couldn't bring the heavy staff down in time to block. Instead, his instincts took over. He spun, turning his body with the slash to mitigate the damage, but the saber's edge was too fast.

*Zzzzip.*

The blade sliced cleanly through his velvet tunic, leaving a long, shallow, but undeniably bloody line across his side.

Elian stumbled back a step, a sharp hiss of pain escaping his lips.

The referee's voice rang out. "A strike! First point to Lord Valerius!"

A roar of approval went up from the noble section. Valerius did not celebrate. He simply stepped back, his face a mask of cold focus, and returned to his opening stance. He had proven his point. He was faster. He was the superior duelist.

Elian stood, his hand pressed against his side. The cut wasn't deep, but it stung. He looked at Valerius. He had been so focused on defense, on creating his impenetrable circle, that he had been completely outmaneuvered. He had been playing the swordsman's game of lines and angles.

And he had lost the first exchange.

He looked down at his staff. The Iron-Root was humming, not with anger, but with a deep, resonant impatience. It wanted to work. It wanted to move.

Elian took a deep breath. He had been reacting. He had been waiting. It was time to stop playing the swordsman's game. It was time to make the swordsman play his.

He changed his grip, lowering the staff. He let his own stance relax, becoming less of a wall and more of a coiled spring.

The smile returned to his face. Not the cold, grim smile of the debt collector. This was the confident, almost cheerful smile of a master craftsman who had just identified a flaw in the design and knew exactly how to fix it.

The first point was a lesson. The real duel was about to begin.
