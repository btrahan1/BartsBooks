## Chapter 46: The Golden Bird

The referee's call echoed across the arena. "First point to Lord Valerius! The combatants will return to their starting positions!"

Valerius gave a short, formal bow and stepped back, his confidence now cemented. He had drawn first blood. He had proven his superiority.

Elian stood, his hand still pressed to his side. The cut was a clean, stinging line. It wasn't debilitating, but it was a distraction, a flaw in his own form.

Before the referee could signal the start of the next round, a new voice, calm and clear, called out from the stands.

"One moment, please."

All heads turned. It was Finn. He stood from his seat in the Temple's section, his ash staff held loosely in one hand.

"As per the ancient rules of honorable dueling," Finn's voice carried with a quiet authority that belied his age, "any injuries are to be healed between rounds, to ensure the contest is one of skill, not of endurance against wounds."

The referee paused, looking towards the royal dais for guidance. This was an archaic rule, rarely invoked.

The Queen gave a single, almost imperceptible nod.

Finn did not walk down to the arena. He simply raised his staff, *Jasmine*, and tapped its base once on the stone floor. He whispered a single word, a name that was also a command.

He flicked the staff upward.

A breathtaking thing happened. A bird, seemingly woven from pure, golden light, erupted from the tip of the staff. It was not a real bird, but a construct of pure healing energy. It chirped once, a sound like a tiny, perfect bell, and then swooped down from the stands.

The crowd gasped, watching the golden bird descend. It flew in graceful circles around Elian a few times, a silent, shimmering dance of pure magic. Then, it dove.

It didn't just touch the wound; it vanished *into* the slash on Elian's side.

Elian felt a wave of warmth, like honey and sunlight, spread through his body. The stinging pain vanished instantly. He looked down. The cut in his tunic was still there, but beneath it, his skin was clean, unmarked, and completely healed. Not even a scar remained.

The golden bird was gone. The wound was gone.

A stunned, reverent silence fell over the entire arena. They had not just seen healing magic. They had seen an art form.

Valerius stared, his focused composure finally cracking. He had fought hard for that first point, for that small advantage. And in a flash of golden light, it had been completely and utterly erased.

From her throne, the Queen watched, a slow, deeply satisfied smile spreading across her face. *Yes*, she thought. *He will do just fine.*

Elian straightened up, no longer favoring his side. He gave a short, grateful nod in Finn's direction. Then he turned his attention back to his opponent.

The score was 1-0. But the battlefield had just been reset. And Lord Valerius, for the first time, looked uncertain. The woodworker wasn't just a skilled fighter. He was a man with miracles at his back.

This is the perfect turning point for the duel. The first round was Valerius's statement of technical skill. Now, this round needs to be Elian's response, demonstrating his adaptability and unorthodox mastery. The disarm is the ultimate reversal, proving that Elian's "stick" is more than a match for the nobleman's "steak knife."
