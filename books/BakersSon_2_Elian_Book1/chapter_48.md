## Chapter 47: The Unthinkable

"Second round! Begin!" the referee commanded.

This time, there was no testing, no circling. Valerius, his confidence rattled by the display of healing, pressed the attack immediately. He needed to end this quickly, before the woodworker's strange advantages could come into play again.

He was a storm of steel, his saber a blur of precise, lethal strikes. He was faster, more aggressive, his technique a flawless display of the high art of swordplay.

But Elian had changed his strategy. He was no longer a static wall. He was a river.

He did not meet force with force. He flowed. He used the full length of his Iron-Root staff, not just to block, but to guide, to channel, to redirect. Valerius's thrusts were turned aside with a gentle, rolling motion. His slashes were guided into empty air. It was a masterclass in deflective energy, a physical manifestation of "going with the grain."

The round was fast and furious. The clang of steel on wood was replaced by the soft *hiss* and *thwump* of the staff redirecting the blade. Both men demonstrated immense skill, a breathtaking dance of opposing philosophies. Valerius was the fencer, all sharp lines and aggressive points. Elian was the artisan, all circles and flowing, undeniable force.

The crowd was on its feet, mesmerized. They were not just watching a fight; they were watching a debate, an argument between two masters, written in sweat and steel.

Valerius, growing frustrated by his inability to land a clean blow, committed to a powerful, two-handed lunge, putting his weight and Ki behind it, a move designed to shatter a lesser defense.

This was the moment Elian had been waiting for.

He didn't deflect the lunge. He met it. But not with the center of his staff. He caught the saber's blade in the V-shape between the staff and his own hand, a move of breathtaking, suicidal precision.

For a fraction of a second, the weapons were locked.

Then the unthinkable happened.

Elian did not push. He did not shove. He *twisted*.

It was a woodworker's move, the same motion he would use with a froe to split a log along its grain. He channeled his Ki not into a blast of force, but into a single, sharp, rotational pulse. The unique physics of the staff, combined with the leverage of his grip, created an incredible amount of torque.

Valerius's saber was ripped from his grasp.

The blade flew into the air, spinning end over end in a beautiful, terrible arc, and landed point-down in the sand, quivering, a full ten feet away.

Lord Valerius was disarmed.

The arena fell into a state of shocked, absolute silence for the second time that day. A disarm. It was the ultimate, undeniable statement of superiority in a duel.

Valerius stood frozen, his empty hand held dumbly before him, his face a mask of pure, unadulterated disbelief. He had put everything into that attack. He had been met, not with superior strength, but with superior *physics*. He had been dismantled like a poorly made piece of furniture.

"A disarm!" the referee's voice finally boomed, breaking the spell. "Second point to Master Elian!"

The score was now tied. 1-1.

Elian lowered his staff. He gave Valerius a short, respectful nod. He had not gloated. He had simply stated a fact: in the language of leverage and force, his "stick" had just taught the nobleman's sword a brutal, undeniable lesson.

That's a fantastic move by Finn. He's no longer just a healer reacting to injuries; he's becoming a proactive "corner-man" and a showman in his own right. This act isn't just about healing; it's a subtle power play, a psychological flex that demonstrates his and Elian's complete control over the pace and conditions of the duel.

It reinforces their united front and further unnerves Valerius, who is being outmaneuvered both physically and magically.
