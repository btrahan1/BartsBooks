## Chapter 5: A Healer's Heartwood

Elian led the young man into the main workshop. The air was thick with the scent of a dozen different woods, a library of silent stories. The traveler looked around, his eyes wide with a genuine, unforced appreciation that Elian hadn't seen in a customer in years.

Elian took the ash branch. He held it in both hands, closing his eyes, letting the wood's song fill his senses. It was a clear, clean hum, a melody of protection and support.

"This is good wood," Elian said, the words a high compliment. "Clean heartwood. No rot. No indecision." He opened his eyes and looked at the traveler. "It chose you."

The young man shuffled his feet, clearly out of his depth. "It... it feels right. In my hands. I don't know why."

"What do you do?" Elian asked, his voice direct. He needed to understand the man to understand the staff he was meant to carve.

"Me?" The young man seemed surprised by the question. "Uhmm... I am a forester of sorts. An under-gardener. I keep the grounds outside the palace free of debris. Rake leaves. Prune the hedges."

Elian nodded. It made sense. The man spent his days among living things. Of course he had learned to listen.

"A groundskeeper," Elian stated. "But what do you *want* to be?"

The question hung in the air. The young man's gaze dropped to the floor. The confident energy he'd had when talking about the wood evaporated, replaced by a deep-seated shyness. He shuffled his feet and whispered, so quietly Elian almost didn't hear it.

"A healer."

The word landed in the workshop with a soft, profound weight. A healer. A path of wisdom, of empathy. It was the absolute antithesis of the ambitious merchants and arrogant nobles Elian was used to.

Elian looked at the man—at his calloused but gentle hands, at the kindness in his eyes. Then he looked at the ash branch. The wood didn't sing of battle or glory. It sang of defense, of warding, of protecting. A staff for a guardian. A tool for a man who would rather mend than break.

It was a perfect match.

A fire Elian hadn't felt in a long time kindled in his chest. This was not a commission. This was a calling. This was a piece of wood that had found its purpose, and a man who needed a tool to find his own.

"A healer's staff is not a simple thing," Elian said, his voice taking on the tone of a master instructing a student. "It is not a weapon to harm, but a channel to mend. It must be strong enough to lean on, yet light enough to be an extension of your will. It must be a bridge between you and the person you are trying to heal."

He laid the branch on his workbench. "The wood has already done the hard part. It has the right heart. My job," he said, picking up his favorite carving knife, "is to get everything else out of the way."

He turned to the young man. "This will take time. I will not rush it. While I work, you will stay. You will watch. You will learn what it means to shape something with respect."

The young man, whose name Elian hadn't even asked yet, could only nod, a look of profound, dawning wonder on his face.

For the first time in a very long time, Elian wasn't just working for a client. He was working for the wood. And for the quiet, noble dream of a gardener who wanted to be a healer.

Elian ignored the blue box. He didn't need The System to tell him what the reward was. The reward was the work itself. The chance, finally, to create something not just beautiful, not just functional, but *true*.

That is a fantastic development. It shows Elian isn't just a carver; he's a mentor with a deeply ingrained philosophy. He understands that a tool's purpose is sacred. To use a "healing" staff for violence would be a profanity, a violation of its soul.

This sets up a dynamic where he is training the young man in two disciplines at once: the sacred art of creation, and the brutal necessity of self-defense.
