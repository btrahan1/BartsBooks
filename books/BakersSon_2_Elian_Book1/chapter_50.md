## Chapter 49: The Grain of Desperation

The final round began. The energy was different. Valerius, stripped of his arrogance, was now fighting with the focused desperation of a cornered predator. Elian, calm and refreshed, was a mountain of quiet confidence.

They met in the center of the arena, not with a clash, but with a fluid exchange of blows. It was the most spectacular display of martial prowess the court had ever seen. Valerius's saber was a blur of silver light, a storm of perfectly executed lunges and parries. Elian's staff was a spinning vortex of dark wood, a perfect circle of defense that turned every attack aside.

For minutes, they were perfectly matched. It was a stalemate of masters. Valerius could not breach Elian's defense. Elian, respecting his opponent's incredible skill, could find no clean opening to end the fight honorably.

Then, Valerius's desperation boiled over.

During a close exchange, as their weapons locked for a brief moment, Valerius did something unthinkable in a duel of honor. He kicked.

He kicked not at Elian's body, but at the sand at his feet. A cloud of grit and dust flew up, directly into Elian's face.

Elian recoiled instinctively, momentarily blinded, his perfect defense faltering for a single, crucial second.

It was all the opening Valerius needed.

He didn't hesitate. He lunged, not with a grand, powerful strike, but with a lightning-quick riposte, the tip of his saber striking Elian's sword arm. It wasn't a deep cut, but it was clean. It was a point.

A collective, horrified gasp went through the arena. The referee, stunned by the dishonorable act, stood frozen.

Elian stumbled back, wiping the sand from his eyes, a thin line of blood welling on his forearm. He had lost.

The Queen was in shock, her hand flying to her mouth. Piper and Lyra were on their feet, shouting in protest. Finn looked on, his face a mask of disbelief.

The crowd was silent. There was no cheer from the noble section. They had not just witnessed a victory; they had witnessed a profound breach of their code.

Lord Valerius stood panting in the center of the ring, his saber held loosely. The triumph he should have felt was absent, replaced by the hollow, bitter taste of a victory won through shame. He looked at his father in the high box.

The Duke of Corvan was on his feet, his face a thundercloud of pure, unadulterated fury. His son had not just lost his honor; he had disgraced their entire House in front of the Queen and the whole world.

"Silence!" the Duke's voice boomed across the arena, magically amplified, crushing the rising murmurs of the crowd. He wasn't addressing the crowd. He was addressing his son.

The referee finally found his voice. "A... a strike. The final point... to Lord Valerius." The words sounded hollow, a technicality in the face of a moral defeat.

Lord Valerius was the victor. He had won the duel. He had won the right to the Queen's hand.

And in doing so, he had lost everything.

That is an incredible display of power and character from the Duke. He's not just a political rival anymore; he's a man of absolute, rigid honor, even when it means publicly shaming his own son. This act completely changes the political landscape and his relationship with Elian and the Queen.

This is a fantastic twist that resets the stakes and puts all the pressure back on Valerius to redeem himself, and on Elian to win in a way that is truly undeniable.
