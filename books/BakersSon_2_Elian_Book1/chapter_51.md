## Chapter 50: The Duke's Judgment

The referee's declaration hung in the air, a sour, unwelcome fact. But before anyone could move, before Valerius could even process his hollow victory, a figure descended from the high boxes.

It was the Duke of Corvan.

He did not walk down the stairs. He floated, carried by a silent, powerful current of Ki, a feat of mastery that instantly silenced the entire arena. He landed softly on the sand, a figure of immense, terrifying authority, and stood before his son.

"That was a disgrace," the Duke's voice was low, a rumbling thunder that needed no magic to carry.

Valerius flinched as if struck. "Father, I—"

"You kicked sand in the eyes of an honorable opponent," the Duke cut him off, his voice dripping with contempt. "You won a point not with skill, but with the cheap trick of a gutter snipe. You have shamed yourself, and you have shamed the name of Corvan."

The public rebuke was stunning. The Duke turned to the petrified referee.

"By the power vested in me as the Duke of Corvan, and as the highest-ranking peer present, I invoke the right of ducal review," he declared. "That point is voided!"

He stared at his son in disgust, his next words a sentence. "The match is still tied at one point apiece."

He had stripped his own son of his victory.

The Duke turned, his cloak swirling, and then floated back up to his seat in the stands, his face a mask of cold fury. He had amputated the dishonor from his House with the ruthless precision of a surgeon.

He looked down at the arena floor. "Healer!" he boomed, his gaze locking onto Finn. "Reset this match! I will have a true victor, one way or another!"

All eyes turned to Finn. The young healer, thrust onto the center stage of a kingdom's political drama, did not falter. He stood, bowed respectfully to the Duke and the Queen, and once again raised *Jasmine*.

The golden bird reappeared, circling once in the silent arena before descending and vanishing into the new cut on Elian's arm. The wound sealed instantly.

But Finn wasn't done. He looked at Lord Valerius, who stood pale and shaking, utterly broken by his father's judgment. With a gentle flick of his wrist, Finn sent a second, smaller wisp of golden-green mist—the "light sprinkle"—drifting across the arena. It settled over both men, calming their ragged breathing, clearing their minds. It was an act of impartial grace.

The field was reset. The score was tied. The wounds, both physical and emotional, were momentarily soothed.

Elian stood, his arm healed. He looked at Valerius. He no longer saw an arrogant peacock or a desperate cheater. He saw a young man who had just been shattered by his own father in front of the world. He saw a flawed piece of wood, cracked under immense pressure.

The referee, regaining his composure, stepped forward. "The score is one to one! The final point will decide the duel! Begin!"

Valerius raised his saber. His hands were trembling. All the fight had gone out of him. He was a duelist without honor, a son without his father's respect. He was already defeated.

Elian did not raise his staff to attack.

Instead, in a move that shocked the arena for the final time, he lowered the Iron-Root staff and rested its end on the sand.

He was not going to fight. He was going to teach.

That is the perfect, poetic ending to the duel. It's not a victory through brutal force, but through absolute, undeniable mastery. By turning the final round into a performance, a "dance," Elian doesn't just defeat Valerius—he transcends the very concept of the duel itself. He wins on his own terms, using his own unique "language."

This solidifies his status as an enigma and a master, and the quiet disarm is the final, elegant punctuation mark.
