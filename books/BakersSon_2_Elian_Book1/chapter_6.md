## Chapter 6: The Two Staffs

The days that followed settled into a new, purposeful rhythm. The young man—whose name Elian finally learned was **Finn**—became a silent, watchful presence in the workshop. He swept the floors, organized tools, and watched, mesmerized, as Elian began the slow, meditative process of shaping the ash branch.

Elian didn't use a saw or a lathe. He used a small, curved drawknife, pulling long, fragrant shavings from the wood. Each cut was a conversation, a whisper between man and material.

"A healer..." Elian began one afternoon, his voice low and rhythmic as he worked, "...must be more than a healer."

Finn leaned forward, hanging on every word.

"The world is not a peaceful garden," Elian continued, his eyes fixed on the grain. "There are wolves. There are blights. There are men who would harm you simply for the medicine in your pouch. And his staff... must be more than a staff."

He paused, testing the balance of the wood. It felt alive in his hands, humming with nascent power.

"There will be times when a healer must also defend," Elian said, his voice hardening slightly. "Especially himself. A dead healer mends no wounds."

He ran a hand along the smooth, pale wood. "But you will NOT learn that with this staff."

His voice was a command, sharp and absolute. He looked at Finn, his eyes blazing with a craftsman's intensity. "This staff is being born for one purpose: to channel life. To ward. To soothe. To teach it violence would be like teaching a songbird to be a hawk. It is a violation of its nature. Its soul."

Elian pointed with his chisel to a dark corner of the workshop. "Go. Bring me the oak staff by the forge."

Finn did as he was told, retrieving a staff that was the polar opposite of the ash branch. It was dark, heavy, and scarred with the nicks and dents of hard use. It was a simple, brutal tool.

Elian didn't even look up from his work. He tossed the worn staff to Finn. It landed with a heavy *thud* in the young man's hands.

"You will train with that one," Elian commanded. "Every morning, before the sun is up, you will be in the yard behind the shop. You will practice the basic forms. The block. The parry. The strike. I will teach you. You will make your hands strong, your body balanced, and your mind sharp."

He finally looked at Finn, a flicker of something almost paternal in his eyes.

"You will learn to fight with the ugly stick," Elian said. "So that you may heal with the beautiful one."

Finn stared at the two staffs—the dark, battered oak in his hands, and the luminous, graceful ash on the workbench. He was being given two educations at once. One for his hands, and one for his heart.

He gripped the oak staff. It felt solid. Grounded. "Yes, Master Elian," he said, the title feeling natural and right.

Elian grunted, turning back to his work. The word "Master" felt strange, but not unwelcome.

That evening, as Elian worked late into the night, adding a subtle spiral carving to the ash staff that followed the wood's own desire to twist towards the sun, he felt a new sense of purpose. He was not just shaping a piece of wood. He was shaping a man.

And for the first time, he found himself caring about the fate of both.

That's a fantastic piece of wisdom. It elevates Elian's philosophy beyond simple pacifism and into the realm of strategic deterrence. It's the core tenet of a true master: the greatest victory is the one won without a fight.

This lesson will be a cornerstone of Finn's development, teaching him that true strength isn't about the ability to inflict violence, but the ability to project a presence that makes violence unnecessary.
