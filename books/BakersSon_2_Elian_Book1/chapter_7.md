## Chapter 7: The Shadow of Competence

The yard behind the workshop became Finn's personal grindstone. Every morning, in the cold, pre-dawn mist, he was there. Elian was a relentless, exacting teacher.

"Lower your stance!" Elian would bark, rapping Finn's shin with a thin switch. "You are a tree, not a reed! Root yourself!"

Finn's muscles screamed. His hands blistered. The heavy, ugly oak staff felt less like a weapon and more like an instrument of torture. He was clumsy, his movements jerky and uncertain.

One morning, panting for breath after a particularly grueling set of drills, he asked the question that had been bothering him.

"Master," he gasped, leaning on the oak staff. "If I am training to fight... err... defend... will I carry two staves?"

Elian, who was sipping a cup of hot tea and watching him with a critical eye, didn't even look up. "No, of course not. You're a healer, not a pack mule."

"But... you said not to teach the ash staff violence," Finn said, confused.

"I said you would not *learn* with it," Elian corrected, finally meeting his gaze. "The purpose of this," he gestured with his cup toward Finn and the battered oak staff, "is to burn the motions into your soul. To make defense as natural to you as breathing."

He took a slow sip of tea. "There will come a time when you will have to use the ash staff to defend yourself. The world is what it is. A wolf will not care if your staff was 'born to heal'."

He stood up and walked over to Finn, his presence suddenly immense.

"But!" Elian's voice was sharp, a chisel striking true. "When you gain competence—true, undeniable competence—something changes. It is a shadow you cast. A weight in the air around you. Many will sense it. A bully, a bandit, a common thug... they are predators. And predators look for easy prey."

He tapped Finn's chest with a single, hard finger.

"When they look at you, they will feel it. They will see the way you stand. They will see the certainty in your eyes. And they will choose NOT to engage. They will feel doubt. And doubt," Elian said, his voice dropping to a low, powerful whisper, "ends most fights before they ever begin."

The lesson settled over Finn, a profound and illuminating truth. The grueling, painful work with the oak staff wasn't just about learning to fight. It was about forging an aura of capability. It was about building a wall of quiet confidence so strong that no one would ever dare to test it.

"The ash staff's primary defense," Elian concluded, turning to walk back to his workshop, "is the man who holds it. Now, stop asking questions. Your footwork is a disgrace. Again. From the top."

Finn looked down at the ugly oak staff. It no longer felt like a burden. It felt like a key. He took a deep breath, lowered his stance, and began the forms again. He wasn't just swinging a stick. He was building his shadow.

Elian felt the System's whisper in his mind. He grunted. *'Tutoring'. The System had a name for everything.* Still, he couldn't deny the small, unfamiliar flicker of pride. The boy was a good student. He was porous. He was learning.

Perhaps, Elian thought as he began the delicate work of sanding the ash staff, this wasn't so bad. It was... almost enjoyable. He immediately suppressed the thought. Enjoyment led to complacency. And there was still so much work to be done.

That's a powerful transition. It's the "Graduation Day" moment, but it's also Elian pushing Finn out of the nest. It feels both abrupt and necessary, a classic mentor move. He has taught Finn all he can within the confines of his workshop; now, Finn needs a different kind of education.

This also forces Elian back into his solitude, creating a new dynamic for him and setting up the next phase of the story for both characters.
