## Chapter 8: The Naming of the Staff

Weeks turned into a month, then two. The seasons began to shift, a subtle chill creeping into the morning air.

Finn was transformed. The softness of the gardener had been burned away, replaced by the lean, hard lines of a disciplined practitioner. He moved with a quiet, grounded confidence, his drills with the oak staff no longer clumsy, but fluid and powerful. The shadow of competence Elian had spoken of was beginning to take shape around him.

Inside the workshop, the ash staff was nearing completion. It was a masterpiece. Elian had sanded it with progressively finer grits of sand, from rough stone to the texture of silk, until the wood felt warm and alive to the touch. He had carved a subtle, spiraling grip that seemed to melt into the user's hand. At the top, he had not carved a fancy crest, but had simply followed the wood's own grain to form a smooth, polished globe that felt strangely calming to hold. He had finished it not with lacquer, but with a secret blend of beeswax and Tung oil, feeding the wood, not sealing it away.

One morning, Finn finished his drills and walked into the workshop, expecting the usual critique of his form. Instead, he found Elian standing by the main bench, the finished ash staff resting on a bed of clean velvet.

It glowed. Not with a magical aura, but with an inner light, as if the sun of the forest it had grown in was still trapped within its core. It was the most beautiful, perfect thing Finn had ever seen.

"It is done," Elian said. His voice was flat, but Finn could feel the immense pride radiating from him.

Elian picked up the staff. It felt impossibly light, impossibly strong. He held it out to Finn.

"Take it."

Finn reached out with trembling hands. The moment his fingers touched the wood, a warm, thrumming energy flowed up his arm, a feeling of coming home. It fit his grip as if it had been grown for him.

"Master..." Finn breathed, unable to find the words.

"Do not call me that anymore," Elian said, turning away to a small writing desk in the corner. "I have taught you all I can about the wood and the body. The rest... you must learn from others."

He finished scratching a few lines on a piece of parchment, sealed it with a drop of plain wax, and turned back.

"Here." Elian handed Finn the sealed letter. "Take this letter... and this staff... to the Temple of the Silent Leaf at the center of the city."

Finn looked from the letter to the staff, confused.

"But... why?"

"Because you are a healer who knows how to fight," Elian said, his voice gruff as he began tidying his tools, a sure sign he was uncomfortable. "But you do not know how to *heal*. That is their art. The Sisters of the Leaf will teach you what I cannot."

He looked at Finn, his expression serious. "And you will need to learn the staff's name."

"Its name?" Finn asked, looking at the smooth, unmarked wood.

"Every True Piece has a name," Elian explained. "A name that reflects its soul. It will not tell it to me; I am the craftsman, not the wielder. It will only speak its name to you, when you are ready to listen. The Temple will help you with that."

He walked to the door and opened it, a clear and final dismissal. "They will take over from here."

Finn stood there, his world shifting once again. He felt a profound sense of loss. He had come to see the grumpy, reclusive woodworker as his only true teacher.

"Will... will I see you again?" Finn asked, his voice small.

Elian stood with his back to him, staring out at the street. "The world is what it is. Paths cross if they are meant to."

It was not a yes, but it was not a no.

Finn clutched the staff, a warm, reassuring presence in his hand. He looked at the sealed letter. He had his tool. He had his new quest. He bowed deeply to the back of the man who had changed his life.

"Thank you, Elian," he said.

He walked out of the workshop, the brass bell chiming his departure.

Elian stood in the doorway for a long time, watching until Finn was just a speck in the distance. The workshop suddenly felt cavernously empty, the silence no longer peaceful, but hollow. He had sent the boy away. It was the right thing to do. The necessary thing.

He closed the door, shutting out the world.

He walked to his bench. He picked up a piece of scrap wood. And for the first time in a long, long time, Elian, the Resentful Virtuoso, felt utterly and completely alone.

That's a beautiful, simple, and perfect name for the staff. "Jasmine" evokes images of healing, fragrance, and a quiet, unassuming beauty. It's not a grand, epic name like "World-Breaker," which fits Finn's character perfectly.

This moment of revelation will be a key step in his journey at the Temple, proving his innate connection to the "soul" of things, something the priestesses will surely recognize as a rare gift.

Let's write that scene. It will be a quiet, contemplative chapter that serves as a bridge to Finn's new life.
