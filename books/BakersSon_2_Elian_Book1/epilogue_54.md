## Epilogue: A New Grain

If the line outside The Knot & Burl was long before, it was insane now.

Victory in the duel had not just made Elian famous; it had made him a legend. Nobles, merchants, and commoners alike flocked to his shop, not just for a piece of his craft, but for a piece of the story. They wanted a chair made by the Queen's Champion. They wanted a table from the man who had humbled a Duke's son.

The workshop, even with its new expansion, was besieged.

"Enough!" Lyra's voice, usually a gentle stream, was now a firm, commanding river. She stood on a small stool at the shop's entrance, addressing the chaotic, jostling crowd of patrons. "This is a workshop, not a fish market!"

She pointed a finger at Piper, who was trying to take three commissions at once. "Piper, have your apprentice start making chits. Numbered tokens. We will distribute them and service five clients per day, by appointment only. Everyone else, go home! You will be notified by royal messenger when it is your turn to be granted a consultation!"

The crowd grumbled, but the mention of "royal messenger" and the sheer, unshakeable authority in Lyra's voice had its effect. The line began to disperse, clutching their precious, newly-made appointment chits.

Lyra stepped down, pressing her fingers to her temples. She looked around the chaotic shop, then at Piper, who was already barking orders at her apprentice.

"And where is Elian!" Lyra asked, her voice a mixture of exasperation and weary affection. "We are drowning in commissions, and our master craftsman has vanished!"

But Elian was not lost. He was, for the first time in a long time, exactly where he was supposed to be.

In the quiet, pre-dawn mist of the Queen's private garden, two figures moved in slow, perfect synchronicity. Elian, with his dark Iron-Root staff, and Lord Valerius, with a simple, unadorned practice staff of ash, flowed through the forms.

Elian was no longer just a craftsman. He was a teacher. He was teaching Valerius not just how to fight, but how to stand. How to find his center. How to listen to the grain not of the wood, but of himself. The arrogance had been stripped from the young lord, replaced by a quiet, hungry humility. He was a good student.

From her balcony overlooking the garden, Queen Isolde watched them, a cup of morning tea warming her hands. She saw the quiet respect between the two former rivals. She saw the balance Elian was bringing not just to a troubled young man, but to the very heart of her court.

She had won a champion, a partner, and a political solution all in one. But watching them now, she knew she had gained something far more profound. She was witnessing the building of a new, stronger foundation for her kingdom, forged not of steel or stone, but of the quiet, unshakeable integrity of a single, grumpy woodworker.

"I have been blessed," she whispered to the morning sun.

And in the garden below, the work continued. The slow, patient, and deeply satisfying work of finding the true grain, and shaping a better world.
