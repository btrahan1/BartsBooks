## Epilogue 1: The Porcelain Queen

The seasons began their slow turn towards winter. The new, expanded wing of The Knot & Burl was complete, a beautiful and brutally efficient structure of stone and timber. The workshop had found its rhythm, a humming engine of commerce and craft, managed with Lyra's grace and Piper's fire.

Elian, however, was not at peace.

From her private balcony, Queen Isolde watched the two figures in the garden below. The pre-dawn mist swirled around them as they moved through their forms, the clean *thwack* of wood on wood the only sound. Elian and Valerius. Teacher and student. There was no animosity in their movements, only the shared rhythm of a lesson. It was a scene of perfect, hard-won harmony.

The Queen sighed, a heavy, mournful sound that was utterly out of place.

"Why so blue?"

The deep, rumbling voice made her turn. The Duke of Corvan stood behind her, having approached with the silent tread of a lifelong politician. He followed her gaze down to the garden.

The Queen continued staring below. "He's just too damn honorable," she said, her voice a low murmur of pure frustration. "He treats me like porcelain. A priceless vase to be admired from a distance, but never touched for fear of breaking it."

"He is indeed too honorable," the Duke replied, a note of grim amusement in his tone. He leaned against the balustrade beside her. "My son tells me the man's entire philosophy is built on finding the 'perfect structural balance'. It seems he has not yet found it in his own heart."

"He wants to wait on the marriage," she continued, the frustration bubbling over. "A year. Maybe more. He considers himself too 'lowly'... a common craftsman. Yet, he won't accept a title! I offered him a Barony, and he looked at me as if I'd suggested he build a chair out of rotten wood!" Her voice dropped to a near-whisper. "And he won't advance beyond words and hand-holding."

The Duke was silent for a moment, processing the full, intricate, and deeply human flaw in the woodworker's grand design.

"Ah," he said finally, a world of understanding in the single syllable. "I see."

He looked down at the two men finishing their practice. He saw Elian, the man who had faced down his son's rage and his own ducal fury without flinching. The man who had negotiated with bandits, the system, and the Queen herself. And he was being defeated by his own rigid code of honor.

"Perhaps," the Duke said, a slow, cunning grin spreading across his face, "it is time for a discussion. Man to man."

The Queen looked at him, a flicker of hope in her eyes. "You would do that?"

"Your Majesty," the Duke said, his grin widening into something wolfish and conspiratorial. "Your happiness is now a matter of state security. It is my solemn duty to intervene." He looked down at the garden. "Besides, I believe it is time someone explained to our Master Elian that while he may be a master of wood, he knows absolutely nothing about the architecture of a woman's heart."

That is a magnificent scene. It's a masterclass in political maneuvering, delivered with the weight of a kingmaker. The Duke isn't just offering a title; he's framing it as a duty, a structural necessity for the kingdom that Elian, with his obsession with sound structures, cannot possibly refuse.

The final line is the checkmate. It's an impeccable, inescapable piece of logic that corners Elian beautifully.
