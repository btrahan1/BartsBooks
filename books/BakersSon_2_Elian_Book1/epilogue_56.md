## Epilogue 2: The Language of Progress

A formal invitation, sealed with the hawk crest of House Corvan, was not something one refused.

Elian now stood on the Duke's private balcony, high in the imposing stone spire of Corvan Keep. The view was breathtaking, a panoramic vista of the entire city, from the gleaming spires of the palace to the smoky haze of the docks.

The Duke stood beside him, a glass of amber liquid in his hand.

"What do you see?" the Duke asked, his voice a low rumble.

Elian's eyes scanned the sprawling metropolis. He saw the tangled mess of streets, the haphazard construction, the inefficient flow of traffic. He saw a thousand flawed joints and stressed support beams.

"I see chaos in motion," Elian replied honestly.

The Duke took a slow sip. "Ah. But I see progress. I see new roads being paved." He gestured subtly toward the district where The Knot & Burl was located. "I see craftsmen being paid a fair wage. I see a newfound spirit of... civic pride." He turned his sharp, analytical gaze on Elian. "I wonder where that came from."

"I am sure it has been there all along," Elian said, deflecting the credit. "It just needed a proper foundation."

The Duke cleared his throat, the pleasantries over. "The people," he began, his voice taking on a new weight, "see the Queen. They see the nobility. They see a gap they can never cross. What they need is a bridge. They need a Marquess."

Elian was silent.

"They need to see one of their own rise above," the Duke continued, his voice a powerful, persuasive current. "Not by birth, but by merit. They need someone with honor, with skill, with a commoner's grit and a craftsman's integrity, to be a beacon. A symbol that the structure of our kingdom is not rotten. Will you be that man?"

The idea was abhorrent to Elian. A title. A rank. It was a fancy lacquer hiding the true grain of a man. "But... but I am a woodworker," he stammered. "A commoner."

"No buts!" the Duke's voice was sharp as a chisel. "You have already refused a title from the Queen, and in doing so, you have stalled the single most important matter of state: the royal succession. The Queen needs a consort. She needs an heir. And if you won't accept an appointment from her, out of some misplaced sense of humility, then you will accept it from me, as payment for a debt of honor."

The Duke took a step closer, his presence immense. He delivered the final, inescapable blow.

"After all," he said, a wolfish, triumphant grin spreading across his face, "the personal trainer of my son and heir cannot be a commoner, now can he? It would be... structurally unsound."

Elian was trapped. Utterly, completely trapped in a cage of flawless, impeccable logic. The Duke had used his own language, his own philosophy, against him. To refuse now would be to admit that his own deeply held principles were flawed.

He looked out at the city, at the "chaos in motion." He thought of the Queen, a pillar of strength bearing an impossible weight. He thought of Valerius, a young man slowly, painstakingly rebuilding his own foundation.

He was no longer just a craftsman. He was a part of the structure now, whether he liked it or not.

He let out a long, slow sigh of surrender.

"Where," the Marquess-to-be asked wearily, "do I sign?"

This is a beautiful, heartwarming snapshot that serves as the perfect final epilogue. It's a "happily ever after" that feels earned, tying up all the character arcs we've watched grow and develop. It’s the final, perfectly fitted joint on the masterpiece we've been building.
