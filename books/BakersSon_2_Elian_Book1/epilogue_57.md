## Epilogue 3: The Royal Wedding

The Royal Wedding was a legend the bards would sing about for a thousand years.

It was not a somber affair of state, a political merger of banners and titles. It was a festival. The entire city was declared a holiday. The Knot & Burl, under the ruthless but generous direction of Provisions Master Silas, roasted whole oxen in the main squares, providing free food and ale for every citizen. The streets, paved by Joric and his crew, were filled with music and dancing.

In the Grand Cathedral, light streamed through the stained-glass windows, painting the marble floor in a kaleidoscope of color.

Lyra stood in the front pew, her newborn daughter, Elara, cradled in her arms. She kept messing up her makeup, dabbing at her eyes with a silk handkerchief as tears of pure, unadulterated joy traced paths down her cheeks. Joric, the proud father and now the official Master Mason of the city's new public works guild, stood beside her, his arm wrapped protectively around them both.

Piper was... Piper. As the Maid of Honor, she wore a gown of shimmering, emerald-green silk that she complained was "completely impractical," yet she wore it with a fiery, undeniable grace. She had one eye on the ceremony and the other on the Duke of Corvan's son, Valerius, who served as the Best Man. She had already secured a commission from him for a new, "structurally sound" set of furniture for his private study.

Finn, now the High Healer of the Temple of the Silent Leaf, stood tall and serene. On his arm was a beautiful, radiant young woman with a mischievous smile—the Queen's own niece, the Lady Anya. They had met when Finn was summoned to the palace to "consult" on a wilting rose garden, a meeting the Queen had, of course, meticulously arranged. His staff, *Jasmine*, rested against the pew, humming a soft, contented tune.

And at the altar stood Elian.

He was not dressed as a Marquess or a Duke. By his own stubborn insistence, he wore a simple but exquisitely tailored doublet of dark, forest-green wool. It was the clothing of a master craftsman, not a noble. He looked nervous, out of place, and happier than anyone had ever seen him.

When the cathedral doors opened and Queen Isolde began her walk down the aisle, a collective gasp went through the assembly. She was radiant, a vision of grace and power.

But as she reached the altar and took Elian's calloused hand in hers, she smiled. He met her gaze, and in that moment, they were not a Queen and her consort. They were two partners, two master craftsmen, who had found in each other a perfect, undeniable fit.

The High Priestess began the ceremony, her voice echoing in the hallowed space. Elian barely heard her. He was looking at his new, incredible, and utterly terrifying life. He saw his friends. He saw his family, forged not of blood but of shared purpose. He saw a future he had never dared to imagine.

He, the grumpy, reclusive woodworker who had just wanted to be left alone, had somehow, against all odds, managed to rebuild the very structure of the kingdom.

It was, he had to admit, a masterpiece. And the work, as always, was just beginning.

**THE END**

Of course! The story isn't over just because there was a wedding. In fact, for a man like Elian, this is where the *real* trouble begins. Success is a much more complicated problem than failure.

Let's dive right into the heart of the next great conflict. The personal has become political. The workshop has become an empire. And Elian's greatest nemesis is no longer a tax collector, but his own prodigy.
