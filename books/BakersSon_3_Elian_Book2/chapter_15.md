## Chapter 15: The Conclave of Flavors

Elian arrived at "The Daily Rise" the next day at noon, flanked by Lyra and Piper. He had expected a simple table with a few of Master Corbin's best creations.

He had underestimated the power of a good story.

The bakery was closed to the public. The windows were steamy, the air inside thick with an almost overwhelming symphony of aromas—yeast, roasting meat, simmering fruit, hops, and a dozen other scents he couldn't even name.

And it was not only the Baker that showed up.

Master Corbin stood by a massive, groaning table, a proud, proprietary look on his face. But beside him stood others.

A woman with arms as thick as small logs and a cheerful, ruddy face—**Hanna, the Master Brewer** of 'The Foaming Flagon'. Before her were casks and bottles of ales, stouts, and meads of every conceivable color.

A man as round as he was tall, with a magnificent mustache and an impossibly white apron—**Chef Antoine, the Royal Chef** himself, who had apparently slipped away from the palace kitchens. He stood behind a collection of steaming tureens and covered silver platters.

There were others, too. A cheesemonger with a wheel of aged gouda that smelled of the high mountain pastures. A confectioner with a dizzying array of spun-sugar sculptures. They had all heard the story—the tale of the woodworker who demanded not just a product, but a soul. And they had come to answer the call.

They were all ready. They had their own stories to tell.

"Master Elian," Corbin said, his voice a low, rumbling welcome. "It seems your challenge has... resonated."

Elian was, for once, taken aback. He had expected a monologue. He had stumbled into a conclave.

"Let us begin," Chef Antoine declared, his voice a rich, hearty baritone. He lifted the lid from a silver platter. "Behold! 'The Hunter's Rest' pie. The venison is from the Royal Preserve, from a stag who lived a long and noble life. The crust is made with lard from a wild boar, and the herbs... the herbs tell the story of the forest floor."

Elian took a small piece. The flavor was explosive—rich, dark, and complex. He could taste the forest. It was not just a pie; it was an epic poem.

"My turn!" Hanna the Brewer boomed. She poured a dark, creamy stout into a mug. "This is my 'Anvil's Kiss' stout. Brewed with barley roasted over coals from Kael the Smith's own forge. You can taste the smoke, the strength, the honest labor of the smithy in every drop."

Elian took a sip. It was incredible. It tasted of smoke, steel, and satisfaction.

One by one, they presented their work. Corbin's "Sunrise Sourdough," its crust the color of a dawn sky, its crumb telling the story of a patient, three-day rise. The cheesemonger's "Mountain's Memory" cheese. The confectioner's "Dragon's Tear" candies.

It was not a tasting. It was a sharing of souls. These were artisans who, like him, had been forced to cater to the dull, functional tastes of the masses. Today, they had been given permission to create art.

Lyra was, of course, furiously taking notes, her mind a whirlwind of menus, booth placements, and pricing strategies. Piper was tasting everything, her eyes alight with the sheer, marketable genius of it all.

Elian stood in the center of it all, a silent judge. He tasted, he listened, he nodded.

Finally, when the last story had been told, he looked at the assembled masters of flavor.

"You have all done well," he said, his voice filled with a rare, undisguised admiration. "You have all remembered that a meal can be more than just sustenance."

He turned to Corbin, to Hanna, to Antoine.

"You are no longer just bakers, brewers, and chefs," he declared. "You are the Culinary Guild of the Festival of Wonders."

He then looked at Lyra. "Find them the best booths in the plaza," he commanded. "Right next to the main stage. The heart of the festival must be its heart."

A cheer went up from the assembled artisans. They had not just found a place at the festival. They had found their champion. They had found their voice. And they were, all of them, ready to sing.
