## Chapter 16: The Soul of the Stage

The news of the "Conclave of Flavors" spread through the city like a delicious aroma. The festival was no longer just an artisan fair; it was becoming a multi-sensory cultural event of unprecedented scale.

The Queen, sitting in on a planning meeting in Elian's now-crowded workshop, listened as Lyra detailed the new Culinary Guild's logistical needs. She looked at the charts, the maps, the complex web of supply and demand. And she saw a flaw in the design.

"This is all wonderful," the Queen said, her voice cutting through the business-like discussion. "You have filled their eyes with beauty and their bellies with soul. But what of their hearts? What of their spirits?"

Elian, Piper, and Lyra looked at her, confused.

"A festival is not just a market, Master Elian," she continued, a familiar, shrewd glint in her eyes. "It is a performance. People need more than just things to buy. They need things to *see*. To *hear*."

She stood, her regal presence instantly commanding the room. She might not know the difference between a mortise and a tenon, but she knew people. She knew the rhythm of a crowd, the grain of a celebration. She had her own contributions to make.

"We need entertainment!" she declared, her voice ringing with the passion of a born showman. "We need Bards to sing the stories of these creations! We need dancers to fill the plazas with life and motion! We need... performers! Storytellers! Puppet plays for the children!"

She turned to her royal Chamberlain, who had been standing silently in the corner. "Send out the notice! Post it in every tavern, every town square, every playhouse. Auditions are tomorrow, on the main stage in the Great Plaza!"

The idea was a bolt of lightning, instantly illuminating the last dark corner of their plan. A festival of wonders needed wondrous performers.

As the Queen laid out her vision for the entertainment, the Duke of Corvan, who had also joined the meeting, listened intently. He watched the Queen's passion, the artisans' excitement. Then, his gaze drifted to his son, Valerius, who was standing quietly in the back, observing, listening.

The young lord had been a constant, humble presence at Elian's side for weeks. The arrogance was gone, replaced by a quiet, focused discipline. He was healing. He was rebuilding.

The Duke rubbed his chin, looking at his son. He saw not just a duelist, but a young man with a deep, classical education. A man who knew the great epic poems by heart, who had been trained in oratory since he was a child. A storyteller.

An idea began to form in the Duke's mind. A bold, audacious idea. A way for his son to not just attend the festival, but to become a part of its very soul. A way for House Corvan to contribute not gold or influence, but something far more valuable: its own story of redemption.

While the Queen was planning a stage for the kingdom, the Duke was planning a stage for his son. The Festival of Wonders was about to get its most unlikely, and most compelling, performer.

This is a fantastic turn for Valerius. He's found his own voice, his own "craft." He's not just a student anymore; he's a contributor, applying his unique noble background and martial training to the festival. He's translating Elian's philosophy of "artistry in craft" into "artistry in combat."

The fumbling with the alliteration is a wonderful, humanizing touch. It shows his genuine excitement and that he's still growing into this new role.
