## Chapter 17: A Gallantry of Guardsmanship

The auditions were a spectacular success. The Queen's call had been answered by every talented, hopeful, and delightfully strange performer in the kingdom. The main stage in the Great Plaza was a whirlwind of music, magic, and motion.

Elian, who had been dragged along to "consult on the stage design," found himself unexpectedly captivated. He watched a puppeteer bring a block of wood to life with such skill that he had to grudgingly admit the man understood the "True Form" of the marionette.

But the true surprise came from Lord Valerius.

He had approached the Queen and Elian after the first day of auditions, his eyes shining with an idea, a passion that was entirely his own.

"Your Majesty, Master Elian," he had said, bowing. "The festival celebrates the art of the hand. The art of the voice. But we are forgetting the art of the body in motion. The art of defense."

He took a breath, his confidence growing. "A demonstration of the martial arts!" Valerius was on a roll of his own. "A celebration of the discipline and skill that protects this kingdom!"

The Queen and Elian exchanged a surprised look. This was not the sullen student; this was a visionary.

"We will line the main street, the new 'Craftsman's Way', with a double column of our Royal Guard!" he declared, his arms painting a picture in the air. "In their polished silver armor, holding their banners! They will be magnificent! A living frame for the festival itself!"

He was just getting started. "And a tournament! In the old dueling grounds! But not one of competition, not for a prize. A display of grace! A demonstration of different forms—the Northern Axe style, the Southern Spear, the Unarmed Arts of the Silent Leaf Temple!"

He paced, energized, searching for the perfect phrase. "We will call it... 'A Gentlemen's Gallantry of Guardsmanship and... Gaudiness'?" He winced. "No. 'Grandioseness'?" He shook his head, frustrated but undeterred. "I'll work on the name. But the idea! A festival of skill, not of bloodshed!"

The Queen was beaming. Valerius had found his purpose. He wasn't just following Elian's lead; he was creating his own contribution, using his own unique skills and perspective. He was leading.

"An excellent proposal, Lord Valerius," the Queen said, her voice filled with genuine pride. "You will be in charge of organizing it. Take whatever resources you need."

Valerius bowed, his face lit with a joy Elian had never seen on him. He had been given a commission, a project that was his and his alone.

Later that day, Elian was in his workshop, putting the finishing touches on a small, exquisitely carved sign for the festival entrance. Valerius entered, a scroll of parchment in his hand.

"Master Elian," he said, "I have the schedule for the demonstrations. I was hoping... there is a slot for a 'Master of the Staff'. I would be honored if you would agree to perform."

Elian looked up from his work. He saw the respect in Valerius's eyes. He saw the partnership being offered.

"I am a woodworker, my Lord," Elian grumbled. "Not a performer."

But there was no heat in his words.

"I will consider it," he added.

It was the closest he could get to saying yes. And for Valerius, it was more than enough. The festival now had a soul, a heart, a voice, and a guardian. It was almost ready.

That is a fantastic cinematic moment. It's the perfect way to reintroduce the antagonists and show them, through their own eyes, the sheer scale and spirit of what Elian and his alliance have built. The "heartbeat" is a brilliant sensory detail that communicates the life and soul of the festival in a way that goes beyond mere visuals.

This scene sets the stage perfectly for the final confrontation—not a battle of swords, but a clash of ideologies.
