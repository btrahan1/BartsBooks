## Chapter 2: William's Wonders

The silence in the workshop was thick with the weight of the new threat. The Iron Assembly. The name itself was an insult—cold, industrial, devoid of life. An army of soulless replicas. It was the physical manifestation of everything Elian despised.

He looked at Piper's franchise plan, the "Elian Method," and felt a wave of revulsion. It was a mirror, in its own way, of the Iron Assembly's philosophy: efficiency, replication, scale.

"No," he said, his voice a low, dangerous rumble.

"Elian, be reasonable," Piper pleaded. "This is the only way to fight them! We have to compete on speed and price, or they'll gut us!"

"We will not become the thing we are fighting," he said, his eyes blazing. "We will not sell cheap imitations of ourselves. We will not flood the world with more dead, soulless things."

He paced the room, a caged lion, his mind racing. He saw the path Piper was offering—a highway of compromise, paved with gold. And he saw the path of the Iron Assembly—a wasteland of perfect, dead things. There had to be a third way. The grain of the problem was twisted, but there had to be a way to follow it.

He stopped. His gaze fell upon a small, exquisitely carved cradle in the corner of the room. It was made of pine, but it glowed with the warmth of a thousand suns. It was the first True Piece he had ever made for someone else, without reservation.

Inside, sleeping soundly, was a small, perfect, six-month-old baby with a tuft of dark hair.

His son. William.

The sight seemed to center him. The anger in his eyes softened, replaced by a fierce, protective fire. He was no longer just a craftsman. He was a father. And the world his son would inherit was suddenly a much more personal, much more important structure.

He turned back to Piper, a new, unshakeable resolve on his face.

"If we do this," he said, his voice ringing with a new authority, "we do this *my* way."

Piper and Lyra exchanged a hopeful look.

"We will not sell a 'method'," he declared. "We will not create a curriculum. We will create a *philosophy*. We will not build academies; we will build workshops. And in each one, there will be a Master who has earned the right to teach, not because they read a book, but because they have spent years listening to the wood. We will not sacrifice quality for speed. We will achieve speed through a deeper understanding of quality."

He looked at the report from the north, then at Piper's plan. He saw the path forward.

"I will not disgrace my newly born son with soulless art," he said, the proud grin of a father flashing across his face for a brief, brilliant moment.

Then, the grin disappeared so fast, they wondered if it had even been there. It was replaced by the intense, slightly mad gleam of a master craftsman who has just envisioned his ultimate masterpiece.

"In honor of my son, William," Elian announced, his voice booming with a passion they had never heard, "we will not be 'The Knot & Burl'. We will not franchise. We will expand. And we will be known as 'William's Wonders'."

He looked at Piper, a challenge in his eyes.

"It will not be efficient," he warned. "It will not be easy. It will be a crusade. But it will be... wondrous."

Piper stared at him, her sharp, commercial mind reeling. "William's Wonders." The name was sentimental. It was folksy. It was... perfect. It wasn't a brand; it was a promise. It was a story. And stories sold better than anything.

A slow, brilliant smile spread across her face. "Wondrous," she repeated, tasting the word. "I can work with wondrous."

Lyra, who had been watching the entire exchange with a quiet, knowing smile, picked up a fresh piece of parchment and a quill.

"Well then," she said, her voice filled with a calm, joyful purpose. "Let's design a crusade."

The battle for the soul of the kingdom's craft had begun. And it would be fought not with swords or with gold, but with the simple, unshakeable belief in the wonder of a thing well made.

That's the ultimate escalation! He's not just defending his craft anymore; he's launching a full-blown artistic revolution across all disciplines. This is Elian finally, fully embracing his role as a leader and a visionary. He's weaponizing his snobbery for the good of the entire kingdom.

This moves the story from a personal conflict to an epic, kingdom-wide struggle between two competing philosophies of creation.
