## Chapter 21: The Unwitting Supplier

The first day of the festival was a rout. The second was a humiliation. By the third day, Foreman Valerik was a caged beast, pacing the stark confines of his black pavilion, his face a mask of cold, silent fury.

He had tried everything. He had lowered prices. He had offered two-for-one deals. He had practically thrown his "Efficient Calorie Sticks" at passersby. Nothing worked. The heart of the festival, the joyous, chaotic, and incredibly profitable energy, simply flowed around his silent, black island of perfection.

Then, on the afternoon of the third day, a miracle happened.

"Sir! We have good news!" his second-in-command reported, his face alight with a relief so profound it was almost comical.

Valerik stopped his pacing. "What?"

"People! By the dozens! They are coming to our booth! They are taking our swords... our daggers! We have customers!"

Valerik rushed to the front of the pavilion. It was true. A steady stream of people—city guardsmen, merchants, even a few minor nobles—were approaching his booth. They weren't looking at the pottery or the leather goods. Their eyes were fixed on the racks of flawless, identical, and incredibly cheap blades.

They would pick up a shortsword, test its balance, and then, with a crisp exchange of coin, they would walk away with it. Hope, fierce and triumphant, surged in Valerik's chest. He had been right! In the end, quality and price would always win! The people had finally come to their senses!

He watched, a smug, satisfied grin returning to his face, as a young guardsman bought one of his best daggers. The man clutched his new, perfect weapon and, instead of leaving the plaza, he walked directly across the square.

Valerik's grin faltered. Where was he going?

The guardsman walked to a booth that was constantly surrounded by a crowd—the Metalsmith's Guild stall, personally overseen by Master Kael.

The guardsman presented the flawless, soulless dagger to Kael's apprentice. "I'd like to commission an 'engraving of history'," the guardsman said, using the new terminology of the festival. "And a new hilt. Something with... a soul."

Valerik watched, his blood running cold, as the apprentice took the dagger—*his* dagger—and began to discuss design options.

Little did he know, this was happening all over the plaza.

The people were indeed buying his products. They were taking his perfectly uniform, soulless weapons to the true artisans to be 'upgraded'. They were taking the blank canvases of the Iron Assembly and having them painted with the art and soul of William's Wonders.

A woman bought a set of his flawless leather belts, then took them to Jorn's booth to have them embossed with the story of her family crest. A man bought a stack of his perfect, uniform plates, then took them to Borin's stall to have them glazed with the colors of his home village.

The Iron Assembly was finally a success. A massive, roaring success.

They were the kingdom's new, premier supplier of high-quality, low-cost, unfinished raw materials.

From his own booth, Elian watched the scene, a slow, deeply ironic smile on his face. He had not just defeated his enemy. He had assimilated them. He had found the grain of their entire operation—their efficiency—and he had incorporated it into his own, grander design.

The Iron Assembly had come to replace the artisans. Instead, they had just become the first, unwilling, and deeply unprofitable step in the new "Wondrous" assembly line.

That's a fantastic way to cap off the festival's narrative. It brings Valerius's redemption arc to its triumphant conclusion and serves as the grand finale for the event. The alliteration he finally landed on—"Gentlemen's Gallant Gala"—is a wonderful touch, showing his growth and newfound confidence.

This is the perfect scene to showcase the new, unified spirit of the kingdom.
