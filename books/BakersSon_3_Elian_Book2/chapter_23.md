## Chapter 23: The Exclamation Point

The demonstration ended. The roar of the crowd was a physical force, a wave of pure joy and appreciation. Elian and Valerius stood in the center of the ring, bowing to the thunderous applause.

It was a moment of perfect, triumphant unity.

And a single, sneering voice chose that moment to shatter it.

"A pretty dance. Nothing more."

The voice cut through the cheers, sharp and ugly. A figure stepped out from the shadows of the competitor's gate. It was Foreman Valerik of the Iron Assembly. He was flanked by two of his largest, most brutish-looking guards. His face was a mask of cold, humiliated fury.

"I challenge you, 'Master' Elian!" Valerik's voice dripped with contempt. The silence in the arena was now deafening. "You and your 'artsy-fartsy' craft is no match for True Steel. No more dances. No more tricks. A duel. To the yield. Here and now."

He drew a sword. It was an Iron Assembly blade—flawless, black, and utterly soulless. It was a piece of industrial death.

The festive atmosphere evaporated, replaced by a cold, sharp tension. The crowd murmured, shocked by the open aggression. The Royal Guard tensed, their hands moving to their weapons.

Elian just sighed. He had hoped the man would have simply accepted his defeat and left. He started to step forward, ready to end this final, tedious piece of business.

But before Elian could respond, a hand clamped down on his shoulder.

It was Lord Valerius.

He stepped in front of Elian, shielding him. "I've got this, Master Elian," he whispered, his voice a low, dangerous hum.

He looked from the sneering foreman to Elian, a flicker of his old, arrogant fire now burning in his eyes, but this time it was forged with a new, cold steel of honor.

"You are the host of this festival," Valerius said, his voice just for Elian. "Its heart. You should not debase yourself by answering the challenge of this... manufacturer. It would be undignified."

He then turned to face Valerik, his posture shifting, becoming the proud, unassailable nobleman he was born to be.

"But to me," Valerius's voice now rang out across the silent arena, a formal declaration of war, "this is not just a challenge to my teacher. This is an insult to my Kingdom, at the Kingdom's own Festival. A breach of the Queen's peace."

He held out a hand, and a squire rushed forward, presenting him with his own silver-inlaid saber.

Valerius took the sword. He looked at Elian, a sharp, brilliant, and utterly confident grin on his face.

"You have thrown a wonderful party, Master Elian," he said. "Allow me to put the exclamation point on it."

He strode to the center of the ring to face the foreman, a champion not just of a Queen, but of a new, wondrous ideal. The student had now become the guardian.

That is a spectacular moment. The reveal of the twin sabers is a fantastic visual and a powerful symbol of his newfound "balance." It's the perfect fusion of his old style (flawless swordsmanship) and Elian's philosophy.

And the line, "his 'kind of humble... ish' student," is absolutely perfect. It's funny, it's true to his character, and it shows he hasn't lost his noble swagger—he's just aiming it in the right direction now.
