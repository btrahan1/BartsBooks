## Chapter 24: The Humble...ish Student

Foreman Valerik sneered at the young lord. "I did not challenge the student. I challenged the master."

"And the master is occupied," Valerius replied, his voice as cool as polished steel. "You will have to make do with me."

With a flourish that drew a collective gasp from the crowd, Valerius produced not one, but two dueling sabers. A squire had brought them forward, and he drew them from their twin scabbards in a single, fluid motion.

He had found a new balance. Not with a staff, but with twin swords. A commission had been quietly filled by none other than Master Kael himself. They were things of beauty, their hilts shaped like hawk wings, a subtle, rippling pattern in the steel. They were not just weapons; they were stories. And in the hands of the new Lord Valerius, they were majestic.

He settled into a low, perfect, two-weapon stance.

"You have challenged my teacher!" Valerius's voice rang out, a formal declaration. He went through a series of blindingly fast strikes, flourishes, dips, and leaps. It was a dazzling display, a storm of silver steel, but it was not the arrogant preening of before. This was a demonstration of absolute, focused, and joyful mastery. It was a performance.

"You have insulted his craft, which has brought more prosperity and joy to this kingdom in three months than your soulless factories have in a decade!"

He spun, the twin blades a blur, ending his display with both sword tips pointed directly at the foreman.

"You are unworthy to stand on the same ground as him," Valerius declared, his voice filled with the righteous indignation of a true champion. He then gave a slight, self-deprecating shrug, a flicker of a grin touching his lips.

"So I," he announced, "Lord Valerius, his first, his best, and his 'kind of humble... ish' student... will stand in his stead."

A wave of laughter and cheers rolled through the crowd. They loved it. This was the noble they wanted—skilled, proud, but with a newfound wit and a sense of honor they could all admire.

From the side, Elian watched, a slow, proud smile on his face. He looked at Kael, who was standing nearby, and the old smith simply nodded, a look of profound, craftsman's satisfaction in his eyes. Their student was about to graduate.

Foreman Valerik, faced with this dazzling, confident, and clearly beloved champion, felt his rage curdle into a cold, grim resolve. He had been publicly humiliated. His only way out was through violence.

"I will break you, pretty boy," Valerik snarled, and charged, his heavy, black sword held in a brutal two-handed grip.

The dance was over. The exclamation point was about to be made.

This is absolutely brilliant. Valerius isn't just fighting him; he's *trolling* him. He's weaponizing performance art. By turning the duel into a theatrical retelling of Elian's story, he's not only winning the crowd but also completely destroying Valerik's composure.

The backflip into a split is the perfect, audacious punctuation mark. Valerius has truly found his calling—he's a Bard with blades.
