## Chapter 26: The Final Lesson

The playfulness was gone. The performance was over. What was left was the cold, hard math of a master swordsman facing a brute.

Valerius was merciless.

Foreman Valerik was strong, but he was a brawler. His technique was crude, his movements predictable. Against the whirlwind of Valerius's twin blades, he stood no chance.

But Valerius wasn't trying to kill him. He was trying to teach him. He wasn't kidding when he said his master had been dishonored by this... cretin. And the lesson would be delivered with the sharp, stinging flat of a blade.

He systematically dismantled him, piece by piece, but never drew blood.

A flick of the wrist, and Valerik's sword belt was sliced clean, his scabbard clattering to the ground. *Smack!* The flat of a blade caught him on the shoulder, numbing the arm. Another dance, and the straps holding his gauntlets were severed. *Smack!* A blow to the back of the knee forced him to a kneeling position.

Valerik was disarmed, disabled, and utterly humiliated, all without receiving a single cut.

Valerius stood over the kneeling, panting foreman. He looked down, not with the heat of victory, but with the cold, diagnostic eye of his teacher.

"Your balance is all wrong," Lord Valerius said, the words echoing Elian's own lesson to him. "In more ways than one."

He sheathed one of his sabers. He then extended his hand, not to help the foreman up, but in an echo of the offer that had changed his own life.

"I will be in the Royal Gardens before the sun rises," Valerius announced, his voice ringing with a new, quiet authority. "We practice the forms. Show yourself, if you want to learn."

He didn't wait for an answer. The offer was the final, devastating blow—an act of mercy more humiliating than any wound.

With that, he turned his back on the defeated foreman. He looked to the stands, to the Queen, to the Duke, and to Elian, and gave a deep, respectful bow.

Then, with a final, brilliant flash of his new personality, he launched himself into a series of flawless, exuberant cartwheels, all the way out of the arena.

The crowd erupted in a roar that was part cheer, part laughter, part pure, unadulterated adoration.

The Festival of Wonders had its champion. The kingdom had its new hero. And Elian's first, and most difficult, student had just graduated with the highest honors. The circle was complete.
