## Chapter 3: The Artisan's Alliance

The energy in the workshop was electric. The decision had been made. "William's Wonders" was no longer a dream; it was a blueprint. But as they began to lay out the plans—for new workshops, for finding and training new masters—Elian’s vision began to expand at a terrifying, exhilarating rate.

He looked at the report from the north again. The Iron Assembly wasn't just making furniture. The letter mentioned cheap, flawless cutlery. Mass-produced leather goods. Uniformly woven cloth.

They weren't just a rival guild. They were a rival philosophy. A blight that threatened to spread across every craft, replacing soul with soulless efficiency.

"This is not a problem of wood," Elian said, his voice a low, dangerous rumble. He paced the floor, his mind seeing the larger structure, the interconnected grain of the entire kingdom's economy. "The Iron Assembly is a rot that will spread. Today it is my craft. Tomorrow, it will be the metalsmiths. Then the weavers. Then the potters."

He stopped and looked at Piper and Lyra, his eyes burning with a righteous, craftsman's fury.

"And why limit ourselves to wood?" he demanded. "I may not know iron. I may not know cloth. I may not know leather. But I know *quality*! I know craftsmanship. I know *art*!"

He stabbed a finger at the map on the table.

"We cannot fight this rot with a single chisel," he declared. "We need a full set of tools. We need an alliance."

He turned to Piper, his voice ringing with the authority of a king giving a command.

"Piper! Gather the guild leaders. The Stonemasons. The Metalsmiths. The Weavers. The Potters. All of them. Use the Queen's seal if you have to. Bring them here. It's time to teach the 'masses' what real art means!"

Piper's eyes went wide, the sheer scale of his ambition taking her breath away. This was no longer about a franchise. This was about forging an economic and artistic coalition that would span the entire kingdom. It was audacious. It was impossible. And it was the most brilliant idea she had ever heard.

"They won't come," she said, her mind already working through the political obstacles. "The old masters are proud. They won't listen to a woodworker."

"They will listen," Elian countered, "when I show them the enemy. And when I offer them a solution."

He strode to his workbench and picked up a piece of simple, honest pine. "We will show them the difference between a thing that is made, and a thing that is *born*."

He looked from Piper's fiery ambition to Lyra's calm strength. He had his team. He had his purpose.

"The Iron Assembly wants to sell the kingdom cheap, dead things," he said, his voice dropping to a near whisper, filled with a cold, unshakeable resolve. "We are going to sell them life. And we will see which is the better bargain."

***

One week later, the newly expanded main hall of "William's Wonders" was filled with a tense, skeptical collection of the most powerful artisans in the city. There was Kael, the metalsmith, his arms crossed, his expression wary. There was Joric, the stonemason, standing tall and confident. There was a wizened old woman who was the head of the Weaver's Guild, and a barrel-chested man who ran the Potter's Collective.

They had come out of curiosity, or out of respect for the Queen's seal Piper had used. But they were not convinced.

Elian stood before them. He held no charts, no ledgers. He simply held two objects in his hands.

In one hand, he held a simple, wooden bird, carved with a few, swift, perfect cuts. It was alive. You could almost see it about to take flight.

In the other hand, he held a metal gear, provided by a spy Piper had sent north. It was from the Iron Assembly. It was flawless, its teeth perfectly uniform, its surface unnaturally smooth. It was dead.

"This," Elian said, holding up the gear, "is our enemy. It is the death of our craft."

He then held up the bird.

"And this," he said, his voice ringing with the passion of a true believer, "is our answer. This is life. This is wonder. And this is the future we will build together."

The masters were silent, their eyes fixed on the two objects. The battle for the soul of a kingdom had officially been joined.

That's a bold and incredibly risky move. To call out the most respected master of another craft and publicly critique his life's work is the ultimate display of Elian's newfound, absolute confidence. He's not just suggesting an alliance; he's demanding they all rise to his level of artistic integrity.

This is the moment he either unifies them all or gets laughed out of his own workshop. It's a fantastic high-stakes gamble.
