## Chapter 4: The Soulless Stick

The guild masters stared at the wooden bird and the metal gear, a tense, skeptical silence filling the hall. They understood the threat of mass production, but the idea of an alliance, led by a woodworker of all people, was a bitter pill to swallow.

Elian could feel their resistance, a wall of pride and tradition. He needed to break through it. He needed to make this personal.

His eyes scanned the room and landed on the most formidable figure present: Master Kael, the metalsmith. Elian's new partner, but still the stubborn, respected head of his own ancient guild.

"Master Kael!" Elian's voice boomed, startling the old smith. "Come up here. And bring your sword."

Kael, his face a mask of confusion and slight irritation, did as he was asked. He unbuckled the sheathed longsword from his belt—a weapon he had forged himself thirty years ago—and walked to the front of the hall.

"Show me your sword," Elian commanded.

Kael drew the blade. It was a magnificent weapon, a testament to a lifetime of skill. The steel was flawless, the balance perfect, the edge still razor-sharp. A low murmur of appreciation went through the assembled masters. It was, by any measure, a masterpiece of the smithing art.

Elian did not look impressed. He didn't even touch it. He just looked at it.

"This," Elian declared to the stunned room, "is a metal stick."

Kael's face flushed with anger. "That 'stick' has seen three battles and has never lost its edge!"

"I have no doubt it is functional," Elian said, his voice cutting and precise. "It is used to 'stick' things. To cut. To kill. But it has no soul! It has no purpose beyond its function!"

He gestured to the sword. "This blade tells no story. The fuller is perfectly straight, but it does not speak of the river of steel it was born from. The crossguard is strong, but it does not whisper of the lives it is meant to protect. It is a tool of brutal efficiency."

He looked Kael in the eye, then swept his gaze over the other masters.

"Sure," he said, his voice dripping with a craftsman's disdain, "we could sell this to soldiers. They need functional tools. But what about nobility? What about other craftsmen? What about a connoisseur who understands that a sword can be more than just a weapon? It can be a legend. It can be a work of art."

He turned back to Kael's sword, his voice dropping. "This blade is a missed opportunity. It is a disgrace to the art you profess to master."

The hall was utterly silent. No one had ever spoken to the legendary Kael Ironforge like this. The old smith's hand was clenched on the hilt of his sword, his knuckles white with rage. He looked ready to strike Elian down.

But Elian didn't flinch. He wasn't insulting the man; he was challenging the artist.

"The Iron Assembly," Elian said, his voice now a low, dangerous whisper, "is already making flawless, soulless metal sticks. They will soon make them better and cheaper than you can. If you continue to compete on function alone, you will lose."

He picked up the wooden bird from his own workbench.

"But they cannot do this," he said, holding it up. "They cannot give a thing a soul. That is our advantage. That is the weapon they have no defense against."

He looked at Kael. "Imagine this sword, Master Ironforge. But imagine if the pommel was carved in the likeness of the first king of this city. Imagine if the blade had a subtle, wave-like pattern etched into the steel, telling the story of the sea battles he won. Imagine if, when you held it, you didn't just feel its weight, but the weight of its history."

He paused, letting the vision settle. "That is a sword a Queen would carry. That is a sword that would hang in a Duke's hall for a thousand years. That is a sword the Iron Assembly can never, ever make."

Kael stared at Elian, his rage slowly being replaced by a dawning, shocking revelation. The woodworker wasn't insulting his skill. He was showing him a new, higher mountain to climb. He was offering him not just a way to survive, but a way to become a legend.

The old smith slowly, thoughtfully, sheathed his sword. The sound of the steel sliding home was the only noise in the room.

"Show me," Kael said, his voice a low, humbled rumble. "Show me how."

This is perfect. It's a brilliant "And another thing!" moment. Elian isn't just winning them over one by one; he's establishing his authority as the master teacher, the cross-discipline virtuoso. He's building his "Artisan's Alliance" not through diplomacy, but through a series of public, brutal, and ultimately inspiring artistic critiques.

The way he pivots immediately to the Potter is a great show of momentum and absolute confidence.
