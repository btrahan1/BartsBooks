## Chapter 7: The Homework

The week that followed was a strange, humbling, and ultimately transformative experience for the guild masters. They spent their mornings in the garden, in silent contemplation of a rotating collection of natural objects—a smooth river stone, a complex seashell, a fallen bird's nest. Elian would say very little, offering only the occasional, cryptic question: *"Does the stone resist the water, or does it guide it?"*

Their afternoons were spent back in their own workshops, trying to apply these strange, philosophical lessons to their familiar crafts. They were all struggling, their hands, so used to confident, decisive action, now hesitant.

On the final day of the "class," Elian had the masters back at his workshop. The air was thick with a mixture of frustration, curiosity, and a reluctant, burgeoning respect.

"Now," Elian said, standing before them. The potted fern was gone. In its place was a simple, sturdy workbench.

"For the past week, you have been students. My students. But that ends today." He looked from face to face. "I cannot teach you your own crafts. I don't know a rivet from a leather tunic. I don't know a glaze from a dye. But *you* do. You know your materials. You know your tools."

He paced before them, a master craftsman laying out the blueprint for their next, far more difficult, project.

"I want you to contemplate what you've learned. The grain. The flow. The story. The True Form. I want you to discuss it amongst yourselves. Argue. Debate. I want Kael the smith to explain the concept of 'metal fatigue' to Elara the weaver. I want Borin the potter to describe the 'memory' of clay to a leatherworker who has only ever known the skin of a dead thing."

He stopped and looked at them, his gaze sharp and commanding. "You will break down the walls between your guilds. You will learn to speak each other's languages. And then," he said, a slow, challenging smile on his face, "you will have homework."

A low grumble went through the assembled masters. Homework? They were not apprentices.

"Your assignment is this," Elian continued, ignoring their protests. "You will work together, in teams of three—a smith, a weaver, and a woodworker, perhaps. Or a potter, a tanner, and a jeweler. You will create a single piece. An object that incorporates all of your crafts, not as separate parts bolted together, but as a single, harmonious whole."

He held up his hand, silencing the rising objections.

"The Iron Assembly creates products. You will create heirlooms. They create dead things. You will create wonders. This is not just an exercise. This is a prototype. This is the foundation of our new Alliance."

He stood back, his arms crossed. The challenge had been issued. It was audacious, difficult, and completely unprecedented.

He looked at the faces before him—skeptical, intrigued, and undeniably inspired.

"Any questions?" Elian asked.

That is a brilliant pivot. He sees their paralysis and, like a master craftsman, realizes he's trying to get them to do complex joinery before they've re-learned how to properly hold the chisel.

This new assignment is deeply personal and insightful. It forces them to confront their own pasts, measure their growth, and rediscover the *love* for their craft that likely got buried under years of running a business. It's a lesson in introspection, not just creation.
