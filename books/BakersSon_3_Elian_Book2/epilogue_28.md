## Epilogue Two: The Stirring Hoard

Six months later, the city was a hive of renewal. The slums were gone, replaced by neat, sturdy stone houses and clean, paved streets. Scaffolding crisscrossed the skyline as new schools and guild halls rose from the ground. The "William's Wonders Fund" was a river of gold, flowing into every corner of the kingdom, nurturing a renaissance of art, craft, and hope.

But a shadow was creeping in from the north.

Duke Corvan stood beside the Queen on a high parapet of the palace, watching the newly formed "People's Guard" drill in the courtyard below. It was a volunteer force, armed with weapons from Kael's forge and shields from Elian's workshop, their training overseen by Lord Valerius. They were a testament to the city's new spirit.

"So you have heard?" the Duke's voice was a low, grim rumble, cutting through the crisp autumn air.

"Yes," the Queen replied, her gaze distant, fixed on the northern horizon. "The reports from the border scouts are... consistent. The rumors are true. The Blight is spreading. The Hoard is stirring."

The Iron Assembly had been a problem of philosophy. This was a problem of extinction. An ancient, nameless evil was waking in the blighted lands, and its hunger would not be sated with stories or soul.

"Will we be ready?" the Duke asked, the question heavy with the weight of the lives it represented.

"No," the Queen said softly, her voice stripped of all its usual fire, leaving only a stark, brutal honesty. "Our armies are strong, but this is a different kind of war. A war against a rot that consumes the very soul of the land. We can build walls, but we cannot wall out despair. For this, we will need more than soldiers. We will need a miracle."

A long silence settled between them, the distant sounds of the hopeful, rebuilding city a fragile counterpoint to the unspoken dread.

The Duke slowly ran a hand through his short, graying beard, a thoughtful, calculating glint returning to his eyes. He looked toward the sprawling, magnificent new structure that was the grand workshop of William's Wonders, its chimneys puffing cheerful plumes of smoke into the sky.

"Good thing we know a miracle worker, yes?"

This is a fantastic, deeply philosophical, and character-driven start to Book Three. The stakes have shifted from political and economic to existential and spiritual. Elian's greatest strength—his connection to the "soul" of things—has now become his greatest vulnerability when faced with something utterly soulless.

Finn's evolution from humble student to wise counselor is a beautiful payoff, and his final line is the perfect, respectful challenge that sets the stage for the entire book.
