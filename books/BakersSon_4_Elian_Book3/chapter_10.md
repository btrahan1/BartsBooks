## Chapter 10: The Recipe for a Firestorm

The war council was silent, trying to comprehend the sheer, terrifying scale of Elian's vision. A firestorm. It was the stuff of apocalyptic legends.

"How?" the Queen finally asked, her voice a hushed whisper. "How could we possibly create a fire of that magnitude? It would require a forest's worth of fuel."

"No," Elian said, a manic, creative energy sparking in his eyes. He began to pace again, not in frustration, but with the frantic energy of an inventor on the verge of a breakthrough. "We do not need to burn our forests. We need to use what we have. What we waste."

He stopped and looked at the assembled leaders. "What do we have lots of? Lots and lots of, that will burn? Something that we normally discard?"

Piper's eyes widened. She had just spent the past year berating her apprentices for leaving piles of it in the corners of the new workshops. "Sawdust," she breathed.

"Sawdust!" Elian echoed, pointing at her as if she'd solved the ultimate riddle. "And wood scraps! Millions of shavings! Off-cuts! The parts of the wood we cannot use! Things we can spare without soul!"

He was pacing again, his mind a whirlwind of logistics and chemistry. "But sawdust burns too fast. It's a flash, not a fire. It needs fuel. Something dense. Something that will stick and burn and spread." His eyes lit up. "And grease! The kitchens! Chef Antoine! The thousands of gallons of leftover grease from our cooks!"

He was practically vibrating with the idea. "And detritus! From the tanneries, the mills! Scraps of oil-soaked leather, grain chaff, coal dust from the forges! One man's trash is another man's treasure!"

He had the fuel. A mountain of flammable waste, the byproduct of their own thriving kingdom. Now he needed a delivery system.

"We will make balls of it," he mused. "Compressed bricks of sawdust, tallow, and tar. But they need to hold together. They need to fly. They need..." He stopped and his sharp, intense gaze snapped to a new target in the room.

It was Borin, the Master Potter, who had been brought in to consult on creating durable water canteens for the troops.

"You there!" Elian's voice was a sharp crack. "Potter! How do we keep it all intact? A container. Something that can be launched from a catapult, that will shatter on impact and spread the fuel. We need a burning ball of death! Tell me! Tell me now! Don't think! Say it!"

Borin, who was used to thinking in terms of elegant, durable glazes, was completely taken aback. He was put on the spot, forced to think not as an artist, but as a munitions expert. He stammered, his mind racing.

"Clay," he finally blurted out, the first, most obvious answer. "We can... we can make clay pots. Thin-walled. Unglazed, so they're porous. They would absorb some of the grease. When they shatter..."

"Yes!" Elian seized on the idea. "Porous! They'll act as a wick! But they need to be cheap. They need to be made by the thousands. Can you do it?"

"With the right molds," Borin said, his potter's mind now catching fire with the sheer, unprecedented challenge, "and every apprentice in my guild working in shifts... yes. We can make you a mountain of ugly, disposable, highly flammable pots."

Elian grinned, a terrifying, wolfish expression. "Ugly. Disposable. Flammable. Borin, you have just described a masterpiece."

He turned back to the war council, his eyes blazing. "There is your weapon," he declared. "We will fight their soulless blight with our own recycled soul. We will turn our very trash into a cleansing fire. The Iron Assembly gave us the swords. We will provide the kindling."

That is a fantastic, chilling, and incredibly confident command from Valerius. He has fully embraced his role as a general, and his words carry the perfect blend of noble flair ("gleaming") and ruthless pragmatism.

This scene perfectly sets up the first major test of their new army. It's not just a battle; it's a field test, a dress rehearsal for the apocalypse, and the stakes are not just victory, but secrecy.
