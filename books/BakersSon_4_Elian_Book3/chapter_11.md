## Chapter 11: The Rehearsal

The wind was a cold, sharp knife coming down from the blighted northern peaks. It carried a faint, sickly-sweet scent of decay and wrongness.

Below, in the desolate, rocky plains that marked the edge of the borderlands, it moved. A sea of mismatched armor, twisted bodies, and hateful, glowing eyes. It was not an army; it was a tide of corrupted flesh. This was the forward scouting force of the Hoard, a legion ten thousand strong, sent to test the kingdom's defenses.

On the high ridge overlooking the plain, the kingdom's new army was arrayed. It was not a grand line of battle. It was a fluid, living thing, a collection of dozens of small, ten-man units, melting into the rocks and shadows.

Lord Valerius stood at the forefront, his twin sabers at his belt. Elian was beside him, a silent observer. With them were the Duke, the Queen's generals, and the newly minted Lieutenants.

"This is our first battle," Valerius said, his voice a low, confident hum that carried over the wind. "It is our testing ground. Below us is the forward scouting army of the Hoard. They think they are the hunters." A sharp, dangerous glint appeared in his eyes. "We will use them as 'practice'."

He turned to the assembled commanders. His orders were crisp, precise, and utterly ruthless.

"The infantry Fists will form the anvil, deep in the valley. They will not engage. They will flow, they will harass, they will draw the enemy in. The cavalry Saws," he looked at Ser Petyr, "will be our scalpels. You will cut off any attempts to flank. You will isolate their large beasts."

His gaze then swept across the ridges, where Kael the Archer and his "Endless Arrow" units were hidden, silent as ghosts. "But the day belongs to the archers. This is a test of the 'School of Fish' doctrine. You are the sea. Drown them."

He drew one of his sabers and pointed it down at the unsuspecting legion. "Let none escape. This is a rehearsal. The Hoard must learn nothing of our new tactics. They will report back only silence and death. The time for them to fear us will come later, once we have perfected our Dance of Death."

He sheathed his blade. The time for his own performance was not now. Today, he was the conductor.

"Form up!" he commanded.

On the ridges and in the valley below, the army of Wonders began to move. It was not the clank and shuffle of a traditional army. It was the silent, fluid, and terrifyingly coordinated movement of a single, massive predator preparing to strike.

Elian stood, his hand resting on a small, clay pot filled with a foul-smelling mixture of sawdust and grease. They had brought a hundred of the "fire pots," a small-scale test. He watched as his philosophy, his art, was about to be put to the ultimate test. He had designed a beautiful, deadly machine.

Now, it was time to see if it would work.

That series of short, brutal, declarative sentences is incredibly powerful. It perfectly captures the grim reality of what their beautiful, artistic philosophy becomes when applied to the battlefield. It's a stark, chilling, and incredibly effective way to describe the battle's outcome.

Let's use that as the core of the battle scene, showing the terrible beauty and efficiency of what they've created.
