## Chapter 13: The Soul of the Soldier

The victory reports were undeniable. The generals were ecstatic. The Duke was grimly satisfied. The kingdom was safe, for now.

But Elian found no peace. In the days following the battle, he was a ghost in the war room, a storm cloud of quiet, simmering despair. He stared at the map, now cleared of the "practice" army, but he wasn't seeing a strategic victory. He was seeing a field of ash.

The Queen found him there one night, long after the others had gone, pacing restlessly before the cold fireplace. The pride she felt for him was a sun, but it was being eclipsed by the shadow of his pain.

"Why so glum?" she asked softly, laying a hand on his shoulder. "You saved us. You created a victory from an impossible situation. You should be proud."

"Proud?" he turned to her, and his eyes were hollow, haunted. "Isolde, did you see their faces? The soldiers? When they returned? There was no victory there. Only... emptiness."

"It is the cost of war, Elian," she said, her own heart aching for him.

"No," he insisted, his voice a raw whisper. "It is so... soull-ess. The Dance is efficient. The fire is effective. But it is a machine. It turns our farmers and our craftsmen into cogs. It pains my soul to have designed such a thing."

He was pacing again, his mind grappling with a new, more difficult design flaw. Not in the army, but in the heart of the army.

"We cannot win this war if we become as dead inside as the Blight we are fighting," he said, his voice gaining a desperate, urgent energy. "We need Bards. We need them on the front lines. We need something to take the men's minds off of just gore and death. We need... we need... a battle hymn. Not a song of glory, but a song of home. A song of what we are fighting *for*."

He stopped and looked at her, his eyes wild with a new, fragile idea. "And we need food. Not just rations. We need Corbin's bread. We need Antoine's pies. We need to feed their bodies, yes, but we need to feed their souls."

He was building again, but this time, he was designing a structure of hope.

"We need to involve our citizenry," he continued, his vision expanding. "As horrible as it sounds, they need to *see*. They need to see what our people are fighting for. They need to come to the camps. They need to mend the uniforms, sharpen the blades, comfort the wounded, before, during, and after. The soldiers cannot be a separate caste, sent off to die in the dark. They must remain part of the whole."

He looked at her, and the facade of the grim war-master finally cracked.

"I cannot make war beautiful," he said, his voice breaking. "But we can make it... bearable."

Tears, hot and silent, were going down his cheeks. He was weeping not for the dead, but for the living. For the souls of the men and women he was sending into the meat grinder he had so brilliantly designed.

The Queen wept with him. She stepped forward and wrapped her arms around him, holding him not as a consort, but as a man bearing an impossible weight. She had asked him to be her shield. She had not realized it would mean he would have to feel every blow.

In that quiet, dark room, the true final piece of the kingdom's grand strategy was forged. They would not just fight a war of tactics and logistics. They would fight a war of compassion. They would answer the soulless Blight not just with fire and steel, but with the full, unyielding, and beautifully stubborn heart of a kingdom that refused to lose itself.
