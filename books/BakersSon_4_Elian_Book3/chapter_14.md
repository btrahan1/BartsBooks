## Chapter 14: The Heart of the Army

The command went out from the Queen, but the words were Elian's. It was not a call to arms, but a call to service. An invitation for the citizenry to join the war effort, not as soldiers, but as the soul of the army.

The war camp, once a grim, functional city of tents and steel, was transformed.

The citizens didn't just respond; they stepped up.

A convoy of wagons arrived, not with weapons, but with wonders. Corbin the Baker and his guild established a massive field bakery, the scent of fresh, soulful bread chasing the smell of fear from the air. Chef Antoine's cooks set up simmering cauldrons of "Hunter's Rest" stew, feeding every soldier a meal that tasted of home.

The people came. Weavers arrived to mend torn tunics, their stitches a quiet prayer for the wearer's safety. Blacksmiths set up grinding wheels to sharpen blades, their rhythmic work a steady, reassuring percussion. Elderly women sat in circles, knitting warm socks and telling stories to young soldiers who were barely more than boys.

Bards, once relegated to taverns, now walked the camp, their songs not of glorious, bloody battles, but of quiet heroism, of the villages being protected, of the families waiting for their return. They sang the kingdom's new, unofficial battle hymn, a simple, haunting melody about the strength of the grain and the heart of the wood.

The soldiers, who had been grim-faced and isolated in their duty, began to change. They talked with the citizens. They were brought food. They were told how proud the kingdom was of them. They were no longer just a number, a cog in a war machine. They were brothers, sons, and fathers, connected directly to the people they were sworn to protect.

The mood of the entire army... shifted.

The grim, hollow-eyed emptiness Elian had feared was replaced by a quiet, unshakeable resolve. The soldiers' drills became sharper, their movements in the Dance of Death more fluid, not from fear of their commanders, but from a deep, shared sense of purpose. They were not just fighting against the Blight; they were fighting *for* the smiling baker who handed them a warm roll, for the old woman who mended their cloak.

Pity be the Hoard.

For they were not only facing soldiers. They were not just facing an army.

They were facing a nation. A single, living entity, its heart beating with the rhythm of a thousand workshops, its soul nourished by the simple, profound act of a shared meal.

Mobilized. Unified. Wondrous.

They stood no chance.

***

In the heart of the blighted lands, the Dark Scion Malakor felt the shift. He had expected to face an army of steel and fear. He could break steel. He thrived on fear.

But his spies brought back confusing, terrified reports. They spoke of an army that sang. They spoke of soldiers who smiled. They spoke of a morale so pure, so unbreakable, that it was like a wall of light pushing back against his encroaching darkness.

He did not understand it. It was a variable that did not exist in his cold, soulless calculus of power.

And for the first time in his long, blighted existence, he felt a flicker of something he had thought long dead.

He felt doubt.

This is a spectacular, multi-layered "I'll take him" moment. It's a fantastic sequence that showcases the growth of all the key characters.

*   **Valerius's Sacrifice:** His immediate willingness to step forward shows his complete transformation into a true, selfless leader.
*   **Finn's Arrival:** Finn stepping in front of *both* Valerius and Elian is a powerful statement. He's no longer the student. He's a master in his own right, and he's here to protect his teachers and his kingdom.
*   **The Battle Hymn:** Calling for the music isn't just for morale; it's a tactical move. He's using the "soul" of the army as his own personal buff, a weapon against the soulless.

This is a perfect setup for a truly epic confrontation.
