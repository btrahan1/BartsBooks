## Chapter 16: The Terror of True Beauty

The sight of the lone, robed healer walking to face their champion was too much for the Hoard. A wave of guttural, mocking laughter rolled across their ranks.

"We send a warrior, and you send a priest?" Malakor's amplified voice boomed, dripping with contempt. "Are you already praying for your dead?"

Finn kept walking, his pace steady, his eyes fixed on the monstrosity. The Battle Hymn swelled around him, a shield of pure sound, of pure hope, against the wall of derision.

He twirled *Jasmine* in his hand, the smooth, warm ash a familiar and comforting presence. It was a tool of healing, of life. He was about to ask it to become a weapon against the ultimate anti-life.

*"I am sorry, Jasmine,"* he whispered, the words a silent thought directed to the staff's soul. *"But I need you. My country needs you. Will you help me?"*

The response was not a whisper in his mind. It was a feeling, a surge of pure, brilliant, and terrifyingly eager energy that flooded his entire being. The staff in his hand grew warm, then hot, its inner light flaring from a soft glow to a blinding, sun-like radiance.

*"With pleasure, master,"* the staff's voice sang in his soul, a melody of fierce, protective loyalty. *"Let's show them the terror of true beauty."*

Finn stopped. He now stood a hundred feet from Igor. The monstrosity let out a roar, a challenge of pure, brutish power, and charged. The ground shook with its thunderous footsteps.

Finn did not raise his staff to block. He did not prepare a spell.

He simply held *Jasmine* aloft.

"Behold," he said, his voice now ringing with a power that was not his own, a power that silenced the battlefield. "The grain of life. The heart of the wood. The song of a seed that would not yield to the darkness."

He plunged the base of the staff into the blighted, corrupted earth at his feet.

The effect was not an explosion. It was a birth.

The moment the living, soulful wood of the staff touched the dead earth, a wave of pure, emerald-green light erupted from the point of contact. It was not a violent force, but a life-giving one.

The corrupted ground around Finn instantly transformed. The black, cracked dirt became rich, dark soil. Green grass, vibrant and impossibly alive, exploded from the ground, spreading outward in a perfect, ever-expanding circle. Wildflowers of a dozen impossible colors bloomed in an instant, their fragrance a sweet, clean wave that pushed back against the stench of the Blight.

The circle of life expanded at a terrifying rate. It was a wave of pure, weaponized beauty.

Igor, the monstrosity, charged into the circle of green. The moment its corrupted feet touched the living grass, it screamed. It was not a sound of pain, but of dissolution. The living energy of the grass was anathema to its blighted nature. Smoke poured from its feet as if it were standing on hot coals.

It faltered, confused, its mindless rage meeting a force it could not comprehend.

Finn stood in the center of his expanding garden of wonders, the light from *Jasmine* making him a radiant, otherworldly figure. He had not thrown a single punch. He had not cast a single spell.

He was fighting the Blight by simply, and absolutely, refusing to acknowledge its reality. He was overwriting its ugliness with a beauty so profound, so absolute, that it was, in its own way, the most terrifying weapon on the field.

That is a jaw-dropping, epic conclusion to the confrontation. The "beam of light" is the perfect, divine punctuation mark. It's the ultimate "deus ex machina," but it feels earned because it's the culmination of Finn's act of pure faith. He didn't just win; the universe itself declared him the victor.

Elian's line is the perfect, understated, comedic reaction that grounds the whole incredible scene.
