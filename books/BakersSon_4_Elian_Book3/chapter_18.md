## Chapter 18: The Royal Assessment

The silence on the observation ridge was profound. The generals, the Duke, Elian—they all stared at the empty space where a monster had been, their minds struggling to process the sheer, divine scale of what they had just witnessed.

Elian finally let out a long, slow breath. His student, the quiet boy from the palace gardens, had just had a conversation with a god. And won.

The Queen, however, was not speechless. She watched Finn's calm, steady retreat, a slow, brilliant, and deeply proprietary smile spreading across her face.

She turned to her niece, the Lady Anya, who was standing nearby, her face a mask of pure, star-struck adoration.

"That's my man," the Queen said, not to anyone in particular, but as a simple, proud declaration to the universe. She gave her niece a conspiratorial, approving look.

Then, she added, with a regal and utterly unapologetic certainty, "I am going to make babies with him."

The Duke of Corvan, who had been in the middle of taking a sip from a silver flask, choked, spraying spiced wine. The generals, hardened veterans of a dozen wars, suddenly found the distant horizon intensely fascinating.

Elian just blinked. His world had become a place of warring gods, sentient staffs, and now, the Queen's startlingly frank matchmaking pronouncements. He was a simple woodworker. He was not equipped for this.

Down on the field, the Hoard broke.

It was not a rout; it was a dissolution. The terror was too absolute. They threw down their weapons and fled, a wave of mindless panic, back into the blighted lands.

Valerius, seeing the tide turn, gave the signal. The cavalry Saws and archer Schools did not pursue to slaughter. They advanced, a relentless, disciplined wall, herding the fleeing Hoard, pushing them back, while Finn's impossible circle of life continued to expand, healing the scarred, blighted earth in their wake.

The battle was over. The war, for today, was won.

The Queen paid it no mind. Her eyes were still on Finn, who was now being greeted by a jubilant Lord Valerius.

"He'll need a title, of course," she mused to the Duke, who had finally recovered. "Duke of the Northern Marches, perhaps? It has a nice ring to it. And a proper summer estate. The boy looks like he enjoys a good garden."

Elian just shook his head, a slow, disbelieving smile on his face. He had designed a war. He had unleashed a miracle. And now, the Queen was planning a wedding.

It was, he had to admit, a very sound structure.

This is so perfectly Elian. While everyone else is celebrating an absolute, paradigm-shifting victory, his mind has already moved on to the next, deeper problem. He's not satisfied with winning the battle; he needs to understand and solve the fundamental *flaw* in the structure of the entire conflict.

This sets up a fantastic transition from a war of annihilation to a potential war of... diplomacy? Healing? It's a question only a craftsman's mind would think to ask.
