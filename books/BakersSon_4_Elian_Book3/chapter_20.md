## Chapter 20: The Unlikely Envoy

The journey into the Blighted Lands was a descent into a silent, gray hell. The vibrant green of Finn's miracle faded, replaced by a landscape of twisted, petrified trees, black, dusty soil, and a sky the color of a fresh bruise. The air was cold, and it carried a profound, soul-deep silence—the silence of a world where not even insects dared to chirp.

They came under a flag of truce, a simple white banner carried by Lord Valerius. The envoy was small, a deliberate statement of peace, not of force. Just three figures, walking into the heart of a dead land.

Elian, his craftsman's senses overwhelmed by the sheer, perfect ugliness of the place.
Valerius, his nobleman's honor and swordsman's courage his only shield.
And Finn, his healer's soul aching with the palpable, silent scream of the wounded land.

They were met not by an army, but by a single figure, waiting for them before a throne of twisted, dead wood. Malakor, the Blighted Scion. He was ancient, his form a withered husk, his eyes two burning coals of pure, cold hate.

"Have you lost your minds?" Malakor's voice was a dry, rasping sound, like dead leaves skittering across gravestones. "You dare come here? To my heartland?"

He was incensed, but he at least understood the flag of truce. He looked at the three figures, his gaze dismissing Valerius as a mere soldier, lingering on Elian with a flicker of professional curiosity—one master of a craft assessing another.

Then his eyes fell on Finn.

And the ancient, all-powerful Blighted Scion took an involuntary step backward.

His immortal composure shattered. The hate in his eyes was momentarily eclipsed by a raw, primal, and utterly unmistakable fear.

"Demon!" he hissed, his voice a choked whisper, pointing a trembling, skeletal finger at the young healer.

Elian and Valerius exchanged a look of stunned confusion. Demon? He was looking at Finn, the gentlest, purest soul in the entire kingdom.

But Malakor was not seeing a gentle healer. He was seeing the architect of his army's terror. He was seeing a being who had wielded a power that was the absolute, existential antithesis of his own. In his world of decay, of entropy, of the slow, inevitable slide into nothingness, Finn's act of spontaneous, explosive, and absolute creation was the ultimate blasphemy. It was not divine; it was a perversion of the natural order of death.

"He... he *unmade* Igor," Malakor whispered, the memory of the purifying beam of light a fresh terror in his ancient mind. "That is not the work of mortals. That is not the work of gods. What are you?"

Finn did not answer the question. He simply looked at the Blighted Scion, his healer's eyes seeing not a monster, but a patient. He saw the rot, the decay, the centuries of pain that had twisted a soul into this shape.

"We did not come here to fight you," Finn said, his voice calm and clear, a stark contrast to Malakor's rasp. "We came to ask you a question."

Elian stepped forward, his gaze sweeping over the dead land. "This land," he said, his voice a low rumble. "It is in pain. You are its master. Why do you allow it to suffer?"

Malakor stared at them, his fear momentarily forgotten, replaced by a profound, ancient confusion. They had come not to threaten him, not to challenge him, but to... diagnose him?

This was not an envoy he had prepared for.

That dialogue is pure Elian. It's blunt, analytical, and completely strips away Malakor's mystique. He's not treating him like a Dark Lord; he's treating him like a man with a chronic, debilitating condition who has simply forgotten what it feels like to be healthy.

It's the ultimate craftsman's diagnosis: "Your foundation is rotten, and you don't even notice it anymore." This is a fantastic way to approach the "final boss" not with a sword, but with a question and a dose of brutal, compassionate honesty.
