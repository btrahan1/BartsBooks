## Chapter 21: The Chronic Condition

Malakor's fear and confusion hardened back into the familiar comfort of rage. "Pain?" he rasped, a dry, rattling laugh escaping his withered chest. "I am the master of this land. Its pain is my power. The Hoard knows no pain! We bring the pain!"

Elian just shook his head, his expression not of a warrior facing a foe, but of a master craftsman looking at a hopelessly warped piece of wood.

"You are in pain," he stated again, the words not an accusation, but a simple, observable fact.

"The Hoard knows no—"

"Seriously?" Elian cut him off, his voice laced with a genuine, almost frustrated, incredulity. "I am in pain just *standing here*. My boots feel it. The air tastes of it. It is a constant, grinding ache in the soul of the world. And you are its epicenter."

He took a step closer, his gaze sharp and diagnostic. "Tell me... truthfully... do you not feel it? The screaming of the rocks? The agony of the dust? Or have you become so used to it, so marinated in this endless misery, that you just accept it as normal?"

Malakor was silent. The question was a chisel, tapping at a crack in his ancient, carefully constructed armor of hate.

"We call it the Blight," Elian continued, gesturing to the dead, twisted landscape around them. "A disease. A rot. You just call it 'home'."

He let out a short, mirthless laugh.

"No wonder you're pissed off all the time," he said.

The blunt, almost casual diagnosis was more disarming than any threat. Elian wasn't speaking to the Blighted Scion, the World-Ender. He was speaking to a man living in a house with a foundation that was screaming, and telling him that, no, that constant, soul-crushing noise was not, in fact, normal.

Valerius and Finn watched in stunned silence. This was not diplomacy. This was an intervention.

"What... what do you want?" Malakor finally hissed, the question a confession of his own confusion.

"We want to fix it," Elian said simply. "We want to heal the land. Which, it seems, means we have to start by healing you."

Finn stepped forward, his presence a calm, gentle counterpoint to Elian's bluntness. "The pain you wield is not your own," the healer said softly. "It is the land's. You have taken its agony into yourself, and in doing so, you have become a conduit for its suffering. You are not its master; you are its prisoner."

Malakor stared at the two men—the craftsman who saw his pain as a structural flaw, and the healer who saw it as a treatable disease. For centuries, he had been a god of death, a figure of terror.

And for the first time, in the heart of his own dead kingdom, he felt like a patient. A very, very sick patient. And these two strange, impossible men had just offered him a diagnosis.

That line is fantastic. It's so perfectly Finn—understated, gentle, and just a little bit cheeky. "This might tickle a little bit" is the healer's equivalent of a doctor saying "you'll feel a small pinch," but on a cosmic, world-altering scale.

It's the perfect way to transition from diagnosis to treatment, and it beautifully showcases Finn's quiet, almost playful confidence in his own immense power. This will be a moment of profound shock for Malakor.
