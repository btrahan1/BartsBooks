## Chapter 24: The Craftsman's Burden

The moment Malakor's hand touched Finn's, the transfer began. Finn closed his eyes, his healer's senses reaching out to the ancient, festering wound in the Scion's soul. He prepared to draw the pain, to channel the millennia of agony into himself and disperse it.

But he was too slow.

"No."

The word was a low, guttural command from Elian. Before anyone could react, he stepped forward and placed his own hands over Finn's and Malakor's joined ones.

He was not a healer. He did not know how to soothe or disperse. He knew how to take a broken thing, feel its flaws, and force it back into alignment.

He closed his eyes and did the only thing he knew how to do. He took the burden.

Elian screamed.

It was not a sound of pain; it was a sound of pure, unadulterated reality crashing down on him. A thousand years of the land's screaming agony, a pain that had warped a god-like being into a monster, flooded into him in a single, tidal wave of pure despair.

He saw it all. He felt it all. The ancient, magical cataclysm that had scarred the land. The slow, creeping death of every living thing. The hopelessness. The rage. The unending, grinding ache of a world dying.

It was a force that could shatter mountains. It was a flaw so profound it threatened to unmake his very soul.

"Elian!" the Queen's voice was a distant, terrified cry from a world away.

He fell to his knees, his body convulsing, the raw, undiluted essence of the Blight trying to tear him apart from the inside. It was killing him. He was a craftsman, and he had just tried to absorb the flaw of the entire world.

He was losing. The darkness was too much. His own soul, his own grain, was beginning to splinter.

And then, he heard a new voice in his mind. Not the screaming of the land, but a calm, clear, and impossibly strong whisper.

*"I am here, Master."*

It was Jasmine. Finn's staff. Or rather, the soul of the wood within it. The connection they had forged was a two-way street.

A wave of pure, clean, life-giving energy flowed from the staff, through Finn, and into Elian. It was not a tidal wave like the pain. It was a cool, steady river, a current of pure, unyielding life. It did not fight the pain. It surrounded it. It contained it.

*"The flaw is too great to be mended at once,"* Jasmine's voice whispered in his mind. *"It must be shaped. Contained. You are the craftsman. We are the tool. Hold on. Just a little longer."*

Elian gritted his teeth, his knuckles white. He was no longer just a man. He was a crucible. The screaming agony of the Blight on one side, the pure, serene life-force of the staff on the other. He was the point of balance between two cosmic forces.

He took the pain. He took the raw, chaotic, screaming energy of the Blight. And with the help of the staff's pure, living soul, he began to do what he had always done.

He began to give it shape. He began to give it form. He began to carve a story out of the pain.

On the outside, he was just a man, kneeling on the dead ground, screaming. But on the inside, a master craftsman was at work, beginning the most difficult, and most important, project of his entire life.

That is a fantastic and deeply ironic reversal. Malakor, the ancient being who reveled in pain, is now desperate and frantic for the man who took his burden. He's not just "healed"; he's been fundamentally changed. The absence of the pain he's known for a millennium has left him feeling empty, powerless, and surprisingly... guilty.

This is a powerful demonstration of the story's core theme: Elian doesn't just fix things; he changes their very nature.
