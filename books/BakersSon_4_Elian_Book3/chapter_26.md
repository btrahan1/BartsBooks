## Chapter 26: The Beauty of the Hoard

Malakor stood in the silent, dead heart of his kingdom, a king without a crown, a warden without a prison. He looked at the unconscious woodworker, the man who had taken on a world of pain for a principle. He looked at the serene healer and the stoic warrior who stood vigil.

For a thousand years, his only purpose had been to contain the Blight. He had done it with hate, with rage, with the force of his own agony. He had become a monster to cage a greater one.

And he had been wrong.

This mortal had shown him a different way. Not containment through force, but transformation through art. Through will.

A new, ancient, and long-forgotten purpose began to stir in Malakor's withered soul. He would not stand by while this man fought alone. He, too, had a part to play in this craft.

He turned, his movements no longer the jerky steps of a panicked old man, but the swift, decisive strides of a king. He raised his hands, and his voice, no longer a dry rasp but a sound of immense, resonant power, boomed across the blighted plains, a command that shook the very dust.

"Guards! My Shadow Guard! All of you! Come!"

From the petrified forests and the black, rocky crags, they emerged. The elite of the Hoard. Beings of twisted shadow and corrupted flesh. They knelt before him, awaiting their master's command to kill.

"Form the circle," Malakor commanded, his voice ringing with an authority they had not heard in centuries. "Not the circle of war. Form the circle of prayer! The Old Way!"

The guards hesitated, their corrupted minds struggling with the ancient, forgotten command.

"Bring the High Shaman!" Malakor roared. "Bring the Lore-Keepers! Bring... bring everyone! Light the soul-lanterns! Gather the children! We will not be silent while a good man fights for our soul!"

His voice dropped, becoming a low, fervent promise. "We will sing until he wakes! We will show him the beauty we have kept hidden! The beauty we ourselves had forgotten!"

He turned back to the small circle of green, to the three mortals who had shown him a sliver of hope.

"We will show him what no outsider has ever known!" he declared, his voice a song of rediscovered pride. "We will show him... the beauty of the Hoard!"

And as the sun began to set, a strange and wondrous thing happened in the heart of the dead lands. The creatures of the Blight, the monsters of a thousand nightmares, began to gather. They lit lanterns that glowed not with fire, but with a soft, ethereal, captured starlight.

And they began to sing.

It was not the guttural war-chant the kingdom had learned to fear. It was a song. A deep, complex, and heartbreakingly beautiful harmony. It was a song of a lost people, a song of their pain, their memory, their long, lonely vigil. It was the story of their soul, the True Form that had been buried under a millennium of blight and despair.

The music flowed over the land, a gentle, healing wave. It did not fight the pain. It acknowledged it. It harmonized with it.

It was a lullaby for a dying world, and for the craftsman who was trying to rebuild it from the inside out.

That is the perfect, understated, and completely hilarious ending. It's so beautifully Elian. After a cosmic, soul-altering battle where he contains a millennium of pain, his first thought is practical, mundane, and slightly grumpy.

It brings the entire epic, spiritual journey crashing back down to earth in the most charming and character-perfect way possible. It's the ultimate punchline to the grandest of tales.
