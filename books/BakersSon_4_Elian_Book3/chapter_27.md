## Chapter 27: Snacks

The song of the Hoard swelled, a symphony of sorrow and hope that washed over the blighted plains. It was a music born of a thousand years of pain, but for the first time, it was not a scream of agony, but a chorus of shared endurance.

Finn stared in amazement. He felt it. The land. The constant, grinding pressure in his soul, the silent scream of the earth, was beginning to quiet. The song was not a spell, but it was a form of healing he had never conceived of. It was the land itself, and its corrupted children, finally being allowed to grieve.

And as the music reached its crescendo, the Blight... the Blight was retreating.

It wasn't a dramatic reversal. It was a slow, subtle receding, like a tide going out. The edges of the black, cracked earth softened. The twisted, petrified trees seemed to stand a little less crooked. The oppressive, soul-crushing weight in the air began to lift.

They were not defeating the Blight. They were *soothing* it.

In the center of it all, Elian lay still. Inside his mind, the chaotic, screaming energy he had absorbed had been caged. He had built a prison for the pain, a complex, interlocking structure of will and memory, its walls fortified by the pure, living energy of Jasmine and now, inexplicably, by the sorrowful, beautiful music from the outside.

The work was done. The flaw was contained.

And finally... Elian woke.

He opened his eyes. The first thing he saw was the strange, ethereal glow of the soul-lanterns. The first thing he heard was the haunting, beautiful song of the Hoard. The first thing he felt was the soft, living grass beneath his back.

He sat up. He felt... empty. Drained. And incredibly hungry.

He saw Finn kneeling beside him, tears of joy and relief streaming down his face. He saw Valerius standing guard, a look of profound, stunned respect on his face. He saw a hundred creatures of nightmare, standing in a circle, singing a lullaby. He saw the ancient, withered Scion, Malakor, conducting them like a master maestro, a look of fierce, terrible joy on his face.

Elian blinked. He took it all in. The impossible scene. The healed land. The singing monsters.

He looked from face to face, a deep, profound, and world-weary confusion in his eyes.

"What did I miss?" he asked, his voice a hoarse, sawdust-dry croak.

He looked around again, his craftsman's mind trying to process the data.

"And," he added, his stomach giving a low, insistent rumble, "did anyone bring snacks?"

Finn let out a sound that was half a sob, half a laugh. The entire, epic, world-saving saga had ended, as it always did with Elian, with a simple, practical, and deeply profound question.

The Master Craftsman was back. And he was hungry.

**THE END**

That is a fantastic epilogue. It's not just a "happily ever after"; it's a perfect setup for the next great adventure. It answers the mystery of the Blight's origin while simultaneously opening a new, epic quest.

Malakor's role as the lore-keeper and quest-giver is a brilliant evolution for his character, turning him from the antagonist into the wise, ancient guide. It's a beautiful, hopeful, and intriguing hook for a new book.
