## Chapter 4: The Fists of Freedom

The royal training grounds were no longer a place for gentlemanly duels. They had been transformed into a massive, sprawling school of war.

Elian watched from the observation platform, a quiet, almost invisible figure beside the Queen. Below, Lord Valerius stood before the first batch of warriors. These were not raw recruits. They were the best of the best: grizzled veterans from the Royal Guard, agile hunters from the northern woods, and stoic monks from the Silent Leaf Temple. These would be his Lieutenants. His instructors.

They stood in rigid, traditional formations, their skepticism a palpable force. They were warriors of the line, of the shield wall. This talk of "dancing" was an insult to their trade.

Valerius was unfazed. He had faced their skepticism before. He was, after all, a master performer.

"You are the best fighters in this kingdom," he began, his voice projecting across the field with an easy, noble authority. "You have been taught to be a wall of steel, to hold the line. Today, you will learn to be a storm."

He paced before them, his twin sabers at his belt. "The Hoard is a mindless beast. It has one strategy: to overwhelm. To crush. A wall can be broken. But a storm cannot be stopped. It simply flows where it will."

"We will break you down from your rigid legions into something new," he declared. "Into units of ten! Many, many units of ten! Each unit will have a leader, and nine partners. You are not a commander and his soldiers. You are partners... in the Dance of Death!"

He let the name hang in the air, its chilling elegance sinking in.

"You will be one unit!" he roared, his passion igniting. "You will flow as one! You will strike as one, you will defend as one, you will dance as one! Your ten bodies will have one mind. You will be a serpent, a whirlwind, a wave that crashes and retreats, leaving only broken enemies in your wake!"

The skepticism on the faces of the veterans began to waver, replaced by a grudging curiosity.

"Words are wind," Valerius said with a grin. "Talk is cheap. So first, a demonstration."

He clapped his hands twice. From the armory, ten figures emerged. They were not soldiers in heavy armor. They were dressed in simple, form-fitting leather, their faces calm, their movements fluid.

"My own personal 'Fists of Freedom' will show you the way!" Valerius announced.

The ten warriors moved to the center of the field. They carried no shields. Some had short swords, some had axes, some had staffs. One, a massive Northman, held two huge hammers. They stood in a loose, seemingly chaotic circle.

At Valerius's signal, a company of fifty Royal Guards, armed in full plate with shields and spears, charged them. It was a test. A brutal, overwhelming show of force.

What happened next was not a battle. It was a lesson.

The "Fists of Freedom" did not meet the charge. They melted. They flowed around the shield wall, weaving between the heavily armored soldiers. They did not fight as individuals. They moved as one organism. The man with the staff would sweep a soldier's legs, and as he fell, the Northman with the hammers would be there to shatter his shield. A swordsman would parry a spear thrust, creating an opening for a knife-wielding woman to dart in and strike at the joints of the armor.

It was chaos. It was art. It was a whirlwind of controlled, efficient violence. In less than a minute, all fifty Royal Guards were disarmed, disabled, or lying on their backs in the dirt, their impenetrable shield wall utterly dismantled. Not a single one of the "Fists" had a scratch.

A profound, stunned silence fell over the field.

Elian, watching from above, allowed himself a small, satisfied smile. It was working. The philosophy of the grain, of flow, of balance... it was not just for wood. It was for war.

Valerius turned back to the shocked, humbled Lieutenants.

"Class," he said with a wolfish grin, "is now in session."

That is a fantastic development. It shows the "Dance of Death" philosophy isn't just a military strategy; it has become a grassroots movement, a part of the kingdom's very culture. The entire nation is mobilizing, turning Elian's core principles into a form of societal self-defense.

And the introduction of the Bowman with his "Endless Arrows" concept is perfect. It shows that the lieutenants aren't just copying Valerius; they are innovating, adapting the core philosophy to their own unique disciplines. It's the ultimate proof that the "teaching" is working.
