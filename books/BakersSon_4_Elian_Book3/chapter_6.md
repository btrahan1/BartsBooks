## Chapter 6: The School of Fish

Lord Valerius was ecstatic. He brought the report of the "Endless Arrows" to the war council, his voice ringing with triumph. The generals were stunned, the Duke was impressed, and even the Queen allowed herself a rare, hopeful smile. It was a breakthrough, a tactical masterstroke.

Elian, who had been listening silently from his corner of the room, was not smiling. He stood, walked to the front, and looked at the detailed diagrams Valerius had drawn.

"That is decent," Elian commented.

The room, which had been buzzing with excitement, fell silent. "Decent?" Valerius repeated, a flicker of his old, wounded pride in his voice. "Master Elian, it is revolutionary!"

"It is a good start," Elian corrected, his tone that of a master craftsman inspecting an apprentice's first successful dovetail joint. "It is a good machine. But it is not yet art."

He tapped the diagram. "They are too static. They stand in one place and spin. They are a turret. A very effective turret, but a turret nonetheless. The Hoard will adapt. It will send its fastest, most monstrous beasts to break the circle. And then your 'river of death' will be overrun."

He looked from Valerius to the assembled generals. "They need to Dance. They need to flow. They are all firing from the same spot, a single, predictable point on the battlefield."

He picked up a piece of charcoal and, on a clean spot on the map, began to sketch. He didn't draw a circle. He drew a fluid, S-shaped line.

"They need to flow," he said, his voice a low, intense hum as the vision took shape in his mind, "like a school of fish."

The generals leaned in, confused.

"A school of fish does not stand still," Elian explained, his charcoal stick a blur of motion. "It moves as one. It turns, it dives, it scatters and reforms. The individual fish is weak, but the school is a single, massive, unpredictable organism."

He began to draw arrows, showing the archers' movements. "Your 'Endless Arrow' units will not be stationary. They will be in constant, fluid motion across the battlefield. They will advance, firing as they go. They will retreat, laying down a carpet of death behind them. They will flow around our infantry units, providing cover. They will part like water to let a cavalry charge through, then close behind them."

He stepped back from the map. He had not drawn a battle plan. He had drawn a living ecosystem of war. The infantry were the rocks. The cavalry were the sharks. And the archers... the archers were the current, the very water in which the battle was fought.

"They will not just be a river of death," Elian concluded, his eyes burning with the force of his vision. "They will be the entire ocean. An ocean the Hoard will drown in."

A profound, stunned silence fell over the war room. They had been thinking in terms of tactics. Elian was thinking in terms of philosophy. He had taken a brilliant idea and elevated it to the level of genius.

Valerius stared at the map, at the flowing, beautiful, and utterly lethal lines. The pride he had felt in his own student's innovation was replaced by a deep, humbling awe.

He bowed his head to his teacher. "Of course," he whispered. "A school of fish."

The kingdom was not just preparing a new weapon. It was preparing a new world view. And the Blight was about to learn a very hard lesson about the difference between a puddle and the sea.

His direct, demanding approach to the First Officer is pure Elian. He doesn't have time for pleasantries or military protocol; he needs raw data to solve a design problem. It's a fantastic setup for another moment of brilliant, out-of-the-box thinking.
