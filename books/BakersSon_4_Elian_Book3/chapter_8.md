## Chapter 8: The Interlocking Joinery of War

The war council stared at the map, at Elian's revolutionary, saw-toothed design for the cavalry. The Duke of Corvan, a traditionalist at heart, was the first to find the flaw.

"It is a brilliant theory, Master Elian," the Duke conceded. "But it is also a suicidal one. A small, probing unit that punches through the enemy line will be instantly surrounded and annihilated. They will be cut off, alone, and without support."

"Only if they are alone," Elian countered, a gleam in his eye. "Their support will not be behind them. It will be above them. Around them. Everywhere."

He turned his gaze back to the young First Officer, Ser Petyr.

"You will not just be training with your lancers," Elian commanded. "You will work with our Archers. With Master Kael's 'Endless Arrow' units."

He gestured to the map, bringing the two disparate parts of his design together. "The School of Fish will be your support. They are no longer a static defense or a simple offensive weapon. They are your eyes, your shield, and your sword."

He traced a line with the charcoal. "Your probing cavalry units will not charge blindly into the enemy mass. The archers will advance with you, their constant, flowing movement screening your own. They are the water; you are the shark that swims within it."

"When your first wave finds the weak point in the Hoard's line," he continued, his voice low and intense, "and they punch through, the archers' purpose will shift. They will not follow you in. They will collapse *around* the breach you have created, their 'Endless Arrows' creating a wall of death, a circle of pure denial that prevents the enemy from closing the gap."

The vision was breathtaking in its scope and its terrifying elegance.

"The enemy will have a choice," Elian explained. "They can turn to face the cavalry unit that is now wreaking havoc in their rear, and in doing so, expose their backs to the unending storm of arrows. Or, they can maintain their line against the arrows and allow your lancers to cut them apart from within. It is a perfect, inescapable trap."

He looked at Ser Petyr. "The archers will give you the time you need. Time to strike, to wheel, to charge the flanks, and then to retreat back through the safe corridor they have created for you."

He stepped back from the map. The design was complete. The infantry Fists were the solid, unshakeable foundation. The cavalry Saws were the tools of relentless, aggressive deconstruction. And the archer Schools were the living, flowing medium that connected and protected them all.

It was a perfect piece of interlocking joinery. An army built like a masterpiece of furniture, with no single weak point, every part supporting the others in a system of flawless, deadly harmony.

Ser Petyr, the First Officer of the Royal Lancers, stood in stunned silence. He had been trained to be a hammer. He had just been handed the blueprint to become a god of war.

"My men and I..." he finally said, his voice thick with a profound, dawning awe, "...will begin training with the archers at once."

The kingdom was no longer just preparing an army. It was preparing a revolution in the very art of war itself. And its architect was a grumpy woodworker who had simply decided to build a better structure.

This is the perfect escalation. Elian, the ultimate protector of "life" (in the wood), is now forced to confront the necessity of using the ultimate, chaotic, and life-consuming element: fire.

It's a fantastic moral and strategic dilemma for his character. He's solved the puzzle of conventional warfare, but he knows it's not enough. Now he has to venture into something that goes against his very nature as a craftsman of living material.

And Piper and Lyra's commentary is the perfect Greek chorus, highlighting the terrifying and awe-inspiring nature of his creative fury.
