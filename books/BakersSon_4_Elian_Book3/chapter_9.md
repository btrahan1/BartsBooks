## Chapter 9: The Gates of Hell

The training was relentless. The new doctrines—the Dance of Death, the School of Fish, the Ripsaw Charge—were being drilled into the army day and night. The kingdom's military was being transformed into a single, beautiful, and lethal organism.

But Elian was not satisfied.

He stood in the war room, staring at the map. The red stain of the Blight seemed to mock him. His strategies were elegant, efficient. They would save lives. But they would not be enough.

"It's still not enough," he said, his voice a low, frustrated rumble. He began to pace. "This will be a war of attrition. The Hoard is a tide of bodies. We can be a master swordsman, but a swordsman will eventually tire if he has to fight the entire ocean. I will not sacrifice our troops in a battle of numbers."

The Queen and the Duke exchanged a worried look. His design was flawless. What more could he want?

"I know everyone won't be coming home," Elian continued, his voice dropping, filled with the heavy weight of the lives he now felt responsible for. "But I want as few as possible coming home on their shields. We need something more. Something... unique. A force multiplier. A weapon that doesn't just kill, but demoralizes."

He stopped pacing. A strange, frighteningly intense light began to burn in his eyes.

Piper, who had just entered the room with a stack of supply ledgers, saw the look and stopped dead in her tracks. She grabbed Lyra's arm.

"Uh oh," Piper whispered. "He has that master craftsman look about him again."

"The what?" the Duke asked, overhearing her.

"The look of someone who is about to invent something revolutionary," Piper explained, her voice a mixture of awe and terror. "The look of someone who is about to open the gates of hell, and pity anyone who stands in his way."

Elian was no longer seeing the map. He was seeing the battlefield in his mind. He saw the mindless, surging tide of the Ho-ard. He saw their numbers, their brute force.

"We need something they fear," he mused, his voice a low, dangerous whisper. "More than war. More than swords. More than arrows. We need something primal. Something that consumes. Something that purifies."

His eyes widened, the final, terrifying piece of the design clicking into place.

"We need..." he said, turning to face the war council, his face alight with a terrible, brilliant idea. "...we need fire."

"Fire?" a general scoffed. "Master Elian, we have fire arrows. We have mages who can throw fireballs. It is a standard tool of war."

"No," Elian said, shaking his head. "Not a tool. A system. A delivery mechanism for pure, unadulterated, industrial-scale chaos."

He strode to the map and swept a hand over the vast, empty plains south of the blighted border. "This is where we will meet them. And this," he said, his voice dropping to a near hiss, "is where we will burn them."

He wasn't talking about a few flaming arrows. He was talking about a firestorm. He was talking about turning the entire battlefield into a weapon. And in his eyes, the greatest craftsman in the kingdom was already designing the most terrible and wondrous wonder of them all.

That is a fantastic, manic burst of creative energy from Elian. He's not just thinking; he's *composing* a recipe for destruction out of the most mundane, overlooked materials. It's a perfect reflection of his craftsman's ethos: waste nothing, and find the "True Form" of even the most humble ingredient.

The way he puts the Potter on the spot is a great callback to his teaching methods, demanding an instinctive, practical answer rather than a calculated one.
