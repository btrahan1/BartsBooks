## Epilogue One: The Dragon's Tear

Two weeks later, the war camp was being dismantled, not in retreat, but in peace. A tentative, but genuine, truce had been forged. The Hoard, under Malakor's new, wise leadership, had become the guardians of the Blighted Lands, their ancient songs now a tool of slow, steady healing.

Elian sat in the Queen's command tent, a mug of hot tea in his hands. He was quiet, introspective. The "cage" he had built in his soul was stable, the contained agony of the Blight now a silent, humming weight within him. He was no longer just a woodworker; he was the warden of an ancient pain. And it had given him a new kind of sight.

"It has a name," he said, breaking the comfortable silence. The Queen, Finn, and Valerius turned to look at him. "The pain. The Blight. It's not a thing. It's a... being."

He closed his eyes, accessing the memory of the agony he had absorbed. "Her name is Aragor. She was once a great dragon. The mother of this land. But she is trapped. In agony. She spoke to me, in the cage. She showed me a vision."

He looked at them, his eyes filled with the weight of an ancient sorrow. "A vision of an egg."

Malakor, who had been invited to the council as the new envoy of the North, stopped his pacing. He turned, his burning eyes wide with a shocking, dawning recognition.

"I know this story," he whispered, the words a ghost from a forgotten age. "It is an old story, from before the Blight. A child's tale. The story of the Dragon Mother and her last egg. Her egg was stolen, long ago, by the Shadow Lords of the Under-Realm."

Elian nodded slowly. "She knows where it is," he confirmed. "In the deepest, darkest chasm of the world. But she is too old, too weak to retrieve it. She no longer has the power. The Blight... it is not a curse she cast. It is her grief. Her tears have corrupted the land for a thousand years."

The pieces of the puzzle clicked into place, revealing a picture not of malice, but of profound, cosmic heartbreak. They had not been fighting a war against a dark god; they had been standing in the middle of a mother's unending cry of sorrow.

Malakor, the Blighted Scion, the being who had once been their greatest enemy, now looked into the eyes of the trio, one by one. The craftsman, the healer, the warrior. The balance.

"The songs can soothe her," Malakor said, his voice filled with a new, desperate hope. "But they cannot stop the tears. Only the return of her child can do that."

He stood before them, no longer a king or a monster, but a humble petitioner, speaking on behalf of his land, his people, and the grieving mother of the world.

"Will you help us?" he asked, his voice a low, earnest plea. "Will you journey into the dark and retrieve her last hope? Will you help us spawn the next dragon?"

The quest was laid bare. Not to slay a monster, but to rescue a child. Not to end a war, but to heal a family. It was a craftsman's work, a healer's work, a warrior's work.

It was a job for the Wonders of the world.

This is a powerful and poignant final image for the book. It beautifully contrasts the large-scale societal healing with Elian's personal, internal focus. While the kingdom is celebrating peace and unity, he's already grappling with the next, even greater challenge.

It's a perfect cliffhanger that doesn't rely on a cheap trick, but on the sheer, intimidating force of Elian's character and the mystery of his next "design."
