### Chapter 1: The Queen's Workshop

Six months had passed since the heroes had departed on their impossible quest into the Under-Realm. No word had come back. Faith was the only currency they had.

But the kingdom did not wait. It built.

Queen Isolde stood not in her throne room, but in a vast, noisy, and beautifully chaotic new building on the city's outskirts: The Royal Academy of Wondrous Arts. It was a university, a guildhall, and a factory all in one, the thrumming heart of the new kingdom.

"Report," she said, her voice cutting cleanly through the din of hammering and sawing. She was noticeably pregnant now, her hand resting on her belly, but her eyes held the sharp, analytical focus of her husband.

Piper, now Guild-Mistress of the entire Artisan's Alliance and Dean of the new Academy, strode beside her. "Production is up twenty percent across all guilds. Our first class of apprentices has graduated. Joric's masons have finished the new aqueduct system for the lower city. Corbin's 'bread-ships' are now supplying the entire northern coast. We are, to put it mildly, drowning in prosperity."

"And the Ostengard problem?" the Queen asked.

"The Iron Assembly has been trying to undercut our prices in the border towns," Piper reported, a wolfish grin on her face. "It's not working. People would rather pay double for a chair that 'sings' than get a free one that feels dead."

They arrived at their destination: a new, heavily guarded wing of the Academy. The sign above the door did not say "Woodworking" or "Smithing." It said: **Department of Applied Efficiency.**

"The Queen has been taking notes," Piper murmured to Lyra, who had joined them, her ledgers in hand.

Inside, it looked like a vision of the Iron Assembly's hell. It was clean, sterile, and organized with ruthless, geometric precision. And at the center of it all, overseeing a complex assembly line, was Foreman Valerik.

He was a changed man. The arrogance was gone, replaced by the fervent zeal of a convert. He was no longer a conqueror; he was the High Priest of a new kind of creation.

"Your Majesty," he said, bowing low.

"Show me," the Queen commanded.

Valerik gestured to the assembly line. At one end, raw, soulless Iron Assembly blades were brought in by the crate. They were then passed down a line of artisans. One would etch a subtle, flowing pattern into the blade. The next would fit a hilt of warm, hand-carved rowan wood. The next would stitch a leather grip, embossed with a simple, meaningful design.

"We are not creating from scratch," the Queen explained, channeling her "inner Elian." "That is the work of a master. We are... adding the soul. We take the flawless, dead body from the Iron Assembly, and we give it a heart. It is efficient, but it is not soulless."

She had not destroyed her rival. She had repurposed them. She had turned the Iron Assembly into the kingdom's primary supplier of high-quality blanks, creating a massive, profitable import business while simultaneously fueling her own artisan economy. It was a move of such brilliant, ruthless pragmatism that it had made the Duke of Corvan laugh for a solid five minutes.

As they watched, a small commotion at the far end of the hall caught their attention. A toddler, no older than two, had slipped away from the royal nursery. It was Prince William.

He wasn't crying or lost. He was toddling with a determined purpose towards a pile of discarded wood scraps. He stopped, picked up a small, twisted piece of pine, and held it to his ear.

He giggled, a sound of pure, delighted discovery.

"It wants to be a duck," he said, his voice the clear, simple bell of a child. He then looked at the silent, watching adults, his eyes—his father's eyes—holding a spark of ancient, profound wisdom.

"It's sad," the little prince added. "Its grain is lonely."

Lyra and Piper exchanged a look of pure, unadulterated shock.

The Queen just smiled, a deep, motherly pride swelling in her heart. Elian was gone, but his legacy was growing in more ways than one. The kingdom was in good hands.

But as she looked out the window, towards the distant, dark mountains of the south that hid the entrance to the Under-Realm, a flicker of worry touched her heart. The kingdom was strong. But the foundation of it all, the man who had taught them to see the soul in all things, was walking in a land that had none.
