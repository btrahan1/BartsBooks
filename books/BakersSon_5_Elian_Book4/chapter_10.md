### Chapter 10: The Tears of the Mother

They emerged from the cave into the twilight of the Blighted Lands. But they didn't stop there. Malakor led them, his ancient legs finding a new strength, to the highest peak of the Sorrow Mountains.

There, curled around the jagged summit, was Aragor.

She was colossal. Her scales were the color of ash and old bone. Her eyes were clouded with millennia of grief. Her tears, a constant, toxic stream, flowed down the mountain, feeding the Blight below. She was not a monster; she was a monument to loss.

Finn stepped forward, the pouch heavy on his shoulder. He walked up to the great snout of the dragon.

"Mother," he whispered. "We brought him home."

He opened the pouch. He lifted the Egg.

Aragor's clouded eyes shifted. She smelled it. The scent of her own blood. Her own hope.

She let out a sound that broke Elian's heart—a whimper, small and fragile, coming from a throat built for roaring.

She lowered her massive head. She nudged the egg gently.

And then, she cried.

But these were not the toxic tears of grief. They were clear, shimmering tears of pure relief. As they hit the ground, they didn't poison the earth; they healed it. Instantly.

Wildflowers bloomed in the snow. The gray rock turned to gold. A wave of purification swept down the mountain, far faster and more powerful than anything Finn could have conjured. The Blight was not just retreating; it was being washed away by a mother's joy.

Then, a crack appeared on the Egg.

*CRACK.*

A tiny snout poked out. A chirp.

A baby dragon, no bigger than a dog, stumbled out of the shell. It was not black like the egg, or gray like its mother. It was a vibrant, iridescent green. The color of new life. The color of the first leaf Elian had shown the masters in the garden.

Aragor curled around her child, her purr vibrating through the mountain, through the ground, through the soles of their boots.

Elian watched, his arm around Valerius, Malakor weeping openly beside him.

"Now that," Elian said, his voice thick with emotion, "is a masterpiece."

The grain of the world had been realigned. The knot had been untied. The structure was sound.

And somewhere, far to the south, in a workshop filled with sawdust and laughter, a little boy named William was playing with a wooden duck, and suddenly, for no reason at all, he started to clap.

**THE END**

That's the perfect comedic call-back to end the series. It brings us right back to the human, slightly chaotic reality of the characters we love. After saving the world, dragons, and the soul of a kingdom, Valerius still has to explain his "creative interpretation" of Elian's philosophy to the man himself.
