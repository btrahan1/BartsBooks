### Chapter 2: The Grain of Fear

The descent was a journey into silence.

The entrance to the Under-Realm was not a fiery chasm, but a simple, dark cave at the base of the southernmost mountains. The moment they stepped inside, the familiar sounds of the world—the wind, the birds, the rustle of leaves—vanished. They were in a place of profound, oppressive quiet.

They walked for days, their path lit only by a single, glowing soul-lantern carried by Malakor. The tunnels were smooth, unnaturally so, as if carved by water or time itself. There was no life. No moss, no insects, no fungi. Only stone and shadow.

Elian hated it. It was a world without grain, without story. The stone did not whisper of the mountains it had been. The air did not taste of anything. It was a void. He felt disconnected, his craftsman's senses starved for input.

Finn was a small, defiant sun in the gloom, his own inner light a comfort to them all. But even he felt the oppressive weight of the place, the sheer, crushing absence of life-force. Valerius was a rock, his hand never far from his twin blades, his warrior's senses on high alert.

On the third day, they stopped in a vast, cavernous chamber. Malakor, who had been grim and silent for the entire journey, finally spoke.

"Let me tell you of the Shadow Lords," he said, his voice a low, echoing rasp. He began to pace, the soul-lantern casting his withered, dancing shadow against the cavern walls. "They are not men. They are not creatures of flesh and blood. They are the ancient spirits of this place. Malevolent. Formless. They are envy, and spite, and despair given form. They are untouchable by our weapons."

He stopped and looked at them, his ancient eyes filled with a fear that a thousand years had not erased. "They feed on hope. They thrive on terror. They will unmake your minds before your blades can even find them."

Finn and Valerius exchanged a worried look. How could they fight an enemy that was not a body, but an idea?

Elian, however, was staring off into the darkness, a thoughtful, almost dismissive look on his face.

"I doubt that," he said, his voice a calm, grounding force in the echoing cavern.

Malakor turned to him, shocked. "Doubt? I have seen them turn a legion of my strongest warriors into gibbering children with a single whisper! They are chaos incarnate!"

"Chaos is just a structure you do not yet understand," Elian countered, his gaze still fixed on the darkness. "They are simply using your fears against you. They present themselves as gods, as formless evils, because that is what you are most afraid of. It is a trick. A shoddy piece of stagecraft."

He finally turned to face the ancient Scion, his eyes clear and analytical. "Nothing is truly formless. Everything has a grain, a structure. Even a shadow. They are mortal, just like you and I."

One of Malakor's own advisors, a withered, ancient member of the Hoard who had accompanied them, watched the exchange with wide, reverent eyes. He leaned over to another.

"You are no mortal," the advisor whispered under his breath, so quietly that only the rocks could hear. "You are a God in Disguise."

Elian ignored the awe and the fear. He was a craftsman, and he had just been presented with a new, fascinating material. Shadow. Fear. Despair. He was not afraid of it.

He was curious to see how it was put together. And, more importantly, how it could be taken apart.
