## Chapter 3: WWED

While Elian was descending into the earth, Queen Isolde was descending into the city's underbelly.

She stood with Lord Valerius, looking through the heavy iron bars of the Royal Prison's exercise yard. It was a place of gray stone, gray uniforms, and gray, hopeless faces. Few were exercising; most were just lying about, listless and defeated.

"Why are we here again?" Lord Valerius asked, a note of distaste in his voice. He adjusted the fine silk of his doublet.

"Because," the Queen said, her eyes scanning the yard with a familiar, analytical focus, "we have a wasted resource. And you know how Elian hates wasted resources."

"They're prisoners," Valerius countered. "Thieves, brawlers, debtors. By definition, they *are* wasted resources."

The Queen turned to him, a mischievous, challenging glint in her eye. "Really? WWED?"

"Wed? What?" Valerius was confused. "Are we discussing my marriage prospects now? Because my father has a list—"

"No," the Queen interrupted, a small smile playing on her lips. "W. W. E. D. What Would Elian Do?"

Valerius paused. He thought for a long moment. He pictured his grumpy, pragmatic, and infuriatingly effective teacher. He imagined Elian looking at this yard full of idle, hopeless men.

"What would Elian do?" he mused. He let out a short, cynical laugh. "He'd do something completely mad. Something stupid. Like this."

He turned to the prison warden. "Guard! Open the gate!"

"My Lord?" the warden stammered. "It is not advisable—"

"Do it," Valerius commanded, his voice now ringing with a performer's authority. "Do it now."

The heavy gate creaked open. Valerius sauntered into the yard, the prisoners squinting and looking his way, their dull curiosity piqued by the sudden appearance of the flamboyant nobleman.

He pointed at a scrawny pickpocket. "You there! Come here! Yes, you!"

The man shuffled forward nervously.

"What is your name?" Valerius began, then immediately waved a dismissive hand. "No, stop. It doesn't matter." He leaned in. "Why are you here?"

"Well I... uhm... I was caught lifting a—"

"Stop!" Valerius cut him off dramatically. "I'll tell you why you are here. You are here because you got caught!"

"Well, uhm, yes," the man said, confused.

The other prisoners were slowly gathering, keeping a safe distance, watching the bizarre spectacle.

"You there! Big guy!" Valerius pointed to a mountain of a man with arms like tree trunks, a man who had killed two people in a tavern brawl. "Come here!"

The mountain stepped forward, a dangerous look in his eyes.

"Hit me," Valerius said.

The man blinked.

"I said, hit me!" Valerius commanded.

And the man did. He threw a massive, looping punch. Valerius swayed back, the fist passing an inch from his nose, and then stepped in, delivering a sharp, open-handed slap to the man's cheek. The sound echoed in the silent yard.

"You call that a strike?" Valerius scoffed, his voice dripping with theatrical disdain. "Look at your balance! Your footwork is a disgrace! You hit like a little girl!"

He turned his back on the stunned giant and strolled across the yard, addressing the entire, baffled prison population.

"You are ALL here because you have no balance!" he declared, his voice ringing with the passion of a preacher. "In your skills! In your choices! In your very souls! In more ways than one!"

He finished his performance, supremely satisfied with his imitation of Elian's teaching style. He turned and walked back towards the gate where the Queen was waiting.

"And then," he said to her in a low, mocking tone, "he'd say something profound, and they'd all miraculously fall to their knees... begging to be taught, or something equally ridiculous like that."

The Queen just smirked, pointing a finger behind him.

"You mean like that?"

Valerius turned. Every single prisoner in the yard, from the scrawny pickpocket to the mountain of a man, was on their knees. They were not mocking. They were not laughing. They were staring at him with a look of raw, desperate, and utterly sincere hope.

That line is the perfect, comedic, and slightly terrified capstone to the scene. It's the moment Valerius realizes his "performance" has had real, tangible, and deeply inconvenient consequences. He was trying to make a sarcastic point, and he accidentally started a revolution.

It's a fantastic punchline that also serves as a brilliant hook for his new, unexpected character arc.
