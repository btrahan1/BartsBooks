## Chapter 4: The Warden of Balance

Valerius stared.

The sea of gray-clad prisoners, kneeling in the dust, looked up at him with an unnerving, unified reverence. He had intended to mock Elian's methods. He had put on a play, a parody of his teacher's blunt, insightful style. He had expected them to jeer, to attack, to do anything but *this*.

He looked from the kneeling prisoners to the Queen, who was leaning against the iron bars of the gate, her arms crossed, a look of pure, unadulterated amusement on her face. Her smirk was practically an audible sound.

"Well damn, Valerius," she said, her voice a low, teasing murmur. "What have you gotten yourself into?"

He had walked in here to prove a point about Elian's absurdity. He had, instead, become the reluctant messiah of the Royal Prison.

The mountain of a man he had slapped spoke, his voice a low, gravelly rumble of pure sincerity. "Teach us," he said. "Teach us... that balance thing."

"Yeah," the scrawny pickpocket chimed in, his eyes alight with a hope he hadn't felt in years. "Teach us how to not get caught!"

Valerius's mind reeled. What was he supposed to do now? He couldn't just walk away. The Queen was watching. His own honor, the very thing he had so publicly reclaimed, was now at stake. He had, in his attempt to be clever, accidentally issued a challenge to the entire prison population. And they had accepted.

He thought, *What Would Elian Do?*

Elian wouldn't run. He wouldn't make excuses. He would see this not as a problem, but as an opportunity. A source of raw, untapped material.

Valerius let out a long, slow sigh of a man accepting his fate. He turned to face his new, captive audience. He clapped his hands, the sound sharp and authoritative.

"Alright!" he announced, his voice shifting from the theatrical performer to the grim drill instructor. "You want to learn balance? You want to stop being the clumsy fools who get caught? Fine. The lessons begin now."

He pointed to the scrawny pickpocket. "You! Your problem is not your hands; it's your feet. You shuffle. You are a walking announcement of your own guilt. You will spend the next month walking this yard, heel to toe, with a bucket of water on your head. And you will not spill a single drop."

He turned to the mountain. "And you! You have the strength of an ox and the grace of a drunken mule. You will learn the 'Dance of the Leaf', the most basic of the Silent Temple's forms. And you will practice it until you can move without making a sound."

He began to walk the yard, his mind, now fully engaged in the problem, assigning tasks with a terrifying, Elian-like clarity.

"You will learn discipline," he declared to the kneeling men. "You will learn control. You will learn that true strength is not in the fist, but in the foundation. You will become not better criminals, but better men."

He stopped and looked at the warden, who was watching the entire scene with his jaw on the floor.

"Warden," Valerius commanded. "Double their rations. And get me a shipment of wooden practice staffs. A hundred of them. Put it on my House's account."

He turned and strode back towards the gate, leaving a yard full of stunned but hopeful prisoners in his wake.

He stopped before the Queen, a weary but resolute look on his face. He had stumbled into this, but he would see it through.

"It seems, Your Majesty," he said with a sigh, "that I have a new class of students."

The Queen's smile was no longer teasing. It was filled with a deep, profound pride. "It seems, my Lord," she replied, "that you have just founded the kingdom's first 'School of Second Chances'."

Valerius, the Peacock Lord, had just become the Warden of Balance. And his most difficult lesson was just beginning.

That is a fantastic return to the "A-Plot" in the Under-Realm. Elian's pragmatic, craftsman-like approach to a supernatural problem is the perfect contrast to Malakor's ancient fear. "How do you capture a spirit?" is a valid question, and Elian's smile promises a solution that is both ingenious and likely involves some form of overly-complex carpentry.

It's the perfect setup for the first real encounter in the dark.
