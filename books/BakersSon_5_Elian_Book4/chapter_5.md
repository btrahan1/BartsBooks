## Chapter 5: The Spirit Trap

The entrance to the main cavern of the Shadow Lords loomed before them, a gaping maw of absolute darkness. From within, eerie, disembodied sounds drifted out—whispers that sounded like forgotten regrets, low moans that vibrated in the chest.

The group stood at the threshold. Malakor was visibly trembling, his ancient fear warring with his newfound hope. Finn was serene, his staff *Jasmine* casting a small circle of comforting light. Valerius (or rather, the Captain of the Guard who had accompanied them, as Valerius was busy teaching pickpockets to walk straight) stood with his hand on his sword, tense and ready.

"We need intelligence," Elian mused, his voice echoing slightly in the stillness. He was not looking at the cave with fear, but with the calculating eye of a hunter. "We cannot walk into their home blind. We need to know the layout. Their numbers. Their weakness."

He turned to the group. "We need to capture one."

Malakor's head snapped around. "Capture one?" he asked incredulously, his raspy voice climbing an octave. "These are Shadow Spirits, woodworker! They are immaterial! They can pass through stone! They can freeze your blood with a touch! How do you capture a spirit?"

Elian just smiled. It was the same smile he wore when he figured out how to make a desk purr.

"A spirit is just energy with an attitude," Elian said, unshouldering his heavy pack. "Energy has rules. It has a grain. It flows. And if it flows," he said, pulling out a set of delicate, silver-inlaid carving tools and a block of peculiarly dense, pale wood, "it can be guided. It can be trapped."

He sat down on a flat rock and began to carve.

"This," he explained as his knife peeled away a shaving, "is Ghost-Ash. It grows in the misty valleys of the Sorrow Peaks. The wood is porous to spirit energy. It absorbs it. It *likes* it."

He worked quickly, carving not a box, but a complex, interlocking puzzle—a labyrinth carved into the wood itself.

"The trick," he continued, "is to make the trap more interesting than the freedom. Spirits are curious. They are drawn to complexity. To emotion."

He finished the carving. It was a small, intricate totem, shaped like a lantern, but hollow and filled with twisting, internal channels.

He then turned to Finn. "I need bait," he said. "I need a memory. A happy one. A bright one. Something that a creature made of despair cannot resist."

Finn nodded. He placed his hand on the wooden trap. He closed his eyes and summoned a memory of pure joy—the day the golden bird healed Elian, the feeling of absolute, divine purpose. He poured that feeling into the wood. The Ghost-Ash began to glow with a faint, warm, golden light.

"Perfect," Elian said.

He walked to the edge of the darkness. He placed the trap on the ground.

"Now," Elian whispered. "We wait."

They retreated into the shadows. Minutes passed. Then, a form coalesced from the darkness of the cavern. It was a Shadow Spirit—a swirling vortex of cold, gray mist with hollow eyes and a mouth that was a silent scream. It drifted forward, drawn by the impossible warmth radiating from the small wooden object.

It circled the trap, wary but hungry. The light was irresistible. It reached out a tendril of mist. The wood didn't repel it; it welcomed it. The spirit flowed closer, sensing the joy within, a feast it hadn't tasted in eons.

With a sudden, greedy rush, the spirit poured itself into the wooden lantern, seeking to consume the light.

*Click.*

Elian didn't touch it. The trap was mechanical, triggered by the weight of the spiritual energy itself. A tiny, spring-loaded latch made of Iron-Root snapped shut, sealing the complex internal pathways.

The spirit was inside. The lantern rattled, hissed, and then went still, the pale wood now pulsing with a captured, furious gray light.

Elian walked over and picked it up. He tapped the wood.

"Now then," he said to the trapped spirit, his voice calm and conversational. "Let's have a chat about floor plans."

Malakor stared, his jaw practically on the floor. The woodworker hadn't used a spell. He hadn't used a holy relic. He had built a better mousetrap. And he had caught a ghost.

That's perfect. Elian builds the trap, but he knows his limitations. He's the engineer; Finn is the diplomat (or interrogator). The "Chop chop!" adds that signature Elian impatience that keeps the scene grounded and funny despite the supernatural stakes.
