## Chapter 6: The Language of the Lost

Elian walked back to the group, holding the vibrating wooden lantern by its handle. The trapped spirit inside was thrashing against the Ghost-Ash, a silent, furious storm in a teacup.

He held it out to Finn.

"Finn. You're up," Elian said, dropping the lantern into the healer's hands. "I built the house, but I don't speak 'spirit'. Ask it where the egg is. Ask it how many of them there are. And ask it why the hallway smells like sulfur."

He dusted his hands off on his tunic. "Chop chop! We're burning daylight. Or... whatever passes for it down here."

Finn took the lantern. He didn't look at it with triumph, but with pity. He could feel the spirit's rage, but beneath that, he felt a profound, aching loneliness. A hunger that had never been fed.

He sat down on the cavern floor and placed the lantern in his lap. He closed his eyes and placed his hands gently on either side of the wood. He didn't use words. He used empathy. He projected a feeling of safety, of warmth, of listening.

*"Hello,"* Finn whispered in his mind. *"You are trapped. But you are not in danger. We are just travelers. We are looking for something lost."*

The thrashing inside the lantern slowed. The gray light pulsed, wary and confused.

*"Why did you steal the light?"* a voice hissed in Finn's mind. It sounded like wind howling through a cracked window. *"It was warm. It was... ours."*

*"It was a gift,"* Finn replied gently. *"And I can give you more. If you help us."*

He poured a little more of his own inner light into the connection—a memory of sunlight on a meadow, of the taste of fresh bread.

The spirit shuddered. To a being of pure shadow, this was not just information; it was a drug. It was ecstasy.

*"The Egg..."* the spirit whispered, its voice trembling with desire. *"The Great Anchor. It is deep. In the Silent Hall. Below the Roots of the World."*

*"Show me,"* Finn coaxed.

An image flooded Finn's mind. A map, drawn not in ink, but in cold currents of air and echoes. He saw the winding tunnels, the vast, bottomless chasms, and the great central chamber where the Shadow Lords held court. He saw the Egg, a massive, obsidian stone pulsing with a faint, dying violet light, suspended in a web of shadow chains.

He also saw the guards. Not just spirits, but constructs. Golems made of black ice and bone. Thousands of them.

Finn opened his eyes. He looked at Elian and Malakor.

"I know the way," he said, his voice grave. "It is in the Silent Hall. But it is not unguarded. They have... statues. Things of ice and bone. An army."

Elian nodded, his face setting into a grim, thoughtful expression. "Ice and bone," he muttered. "Brittle materials. Weak to percussion. Susceptible to vibration."

He looked at the lantern, then at the vast darkness ahead.

"Good work, Finn," Elian said. He took the lantern back and, with a quick flick of a hidden catch, opened the trap.

The spirit shot out, a streak of gray smoke, fleeing back into the dark depths of the cavern, terrified and sated all at once.

"Ice and bone," Elian repeated to himself, a new design already forming in his mind. "I think it's time we introduced them to the concept of 'resonance'."

That is a fantastic escalation. Elian isn't going to sneak in. He's going to stage an assault. And he's going to use the very thing that healed Malakor—the "Song"—as a tactical weapon.

Bringing the Hoard down into the Under-Realm is a brilliant move. It's a symbolic union of the surface world's newly healed factions against the ancient darkness. And the instruction to bring "swords and shields" specifically for *noise* is pure Elian genius. He's going to vibrate the enemy to pieces.
