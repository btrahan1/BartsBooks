## Chapter 7: The Symphony of War

The plan was audacious. It was loud. And it was exactly the kind of structural problem-solving Elian excelled at.

"Ice and bone are rigid," Elian explained to the group. "They have a high tensile strength but a very specific resonant frequency. If you hit them with a sound that matches that frequency, they don't break. They shatter."

He turned to the ancient Scion.

"Malakor. We need soldiers. Not to fight, but to perform. Do you have a way to 'call them'? Using that 'song language' or whatever you call it?"

Malakor looked at him, his burning eyes widening. "The Song of the Deep? It can be heard across the Blighted Lands. It resonates through the earth itself." He paused. "How many do you need?"

Elian smiled deviously. It was the smile of a conductor about to lead an orchestra into a crescendo.

"All of them," he said.

Malakor blinked. "All?"

"Every warrior. Every drummer. Every chanter," Elian confirmed. "And make sure they all bring a sword and a shield. Metal ones. The loudest ones they have. It's time to make some noise."

***

Three days later, the cavern was no longer silent.

The tunnel leading down to the Under-Realm was filled with the rhythmic, heavy tread of thousands of feet. The Hoard had answered the call. They came not with the chaotic rage of their past, but with a disciplined, somber purpose. They were the new guardians of the light, marching into the dark.

Leading them was Valerius (who had finally caught up after setting his prison-school in motion), his twin sabers gleaming in the lantern light. Beside him walked Malakor, his ancient voice leading the chant.

And at the front, Elian stood with his Iron-Root staff. He wasn't looking at the enemy. He was looking at the walls, tapping them, listening to the echo.

"Here," he said, stopping in a massive, natural amphitheater just before the Silent Hall. "The acoustics are perfect. A natural amplifier."

He turned to the army of the Hoard. Ten thousand warriors, creatures of nightmare and redemption, stood ready.

"When we enter the Hall," Elian commanded, his voice ringing out, "you will not charge. You will stand. You will lock your shields. And when I give the signal... you will beat them. You will scream. You will sing the loudest, most discordant song the world has ever heard."

He looked at Finn. "And you, Finn. When the ice cracks... you bring the light."

He turned back to the darkness. The Shadow Lords were waiting. They expected fear. They expected silence.

They were about to get a heavy metal concert.

"Open the doors," Elian said.

That rhythmic build-up is incredible. Elian is turning a march into a musical siege weapon. The "dum dum... dum dum dum" rhythm (very "We Will Rock You"!) is primal, intimidating, and structurally destructive. It’s the perfect way to break constructs made of brittle materials like ice and bone.

The Shadow Lords' confusion—mistaking acoustics for magic—is a great touch that reinforces the theme of "understanding the grain" versus mystical superstition.
