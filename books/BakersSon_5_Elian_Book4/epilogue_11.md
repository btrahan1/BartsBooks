## Epilogue One: The Final Audit

The heroes returned to the capital not as conquerors, but as weary travelers. The city was already celebrating; the purification of the North had been felt in the air itself.

Elian walked into the Royal Gardens, seeking quiet. Instead, he found chaos.

The garden was filled with... interesting characters. A mountain of a man was meticulously pruning a rosebush with hands the size of hams. A scrawny fellow was walking a tightrope between two trees, balancing a bucket of water on his head. Dozens of rough-looking men were practicing breathing exercises in neat rows.

Elian stopped. He blinked.

Lord Valerius, who was overseeing the strange spectacle, spotted him and hurried over, a slightly guilty, but mostly proud, grin on his face.

"Master Elian! You're back! Welcome to the School of Second Chances!"

Elian pointed at the tightrope walker. "Explain."

"Well," Valerius began, adjusting his cuffs. "While you were gone, the Queen and I visited the prison. We saw a wasted resource. And I asked myself... WWED?"

"Wed?" Elian asked, confused. "Did you get married?"

"No," Valerius laughed. "W. W. E. D. 'What Would Elian Do?'"

Elian stared at him. "You did what? WWED what?"

"I asked myself what you would do," Valerius explained happily. "And I realized you would probably insult them, challenge their balance, and then give them a job. So... I did. I insulted them quite thoroughly, actually. It was very liberating."

He gestured to the former criminals, who were now the most disciplined and focused gardeners in the city. "And it worked! They're reforming. They're finding their grain!"

Elian looked at the "students." He looked at Valerius, the nobleman who had learned to lead by learning to serve.

He let out a long, slow sigh. He was never going to live this down. He had become a verb. An acronym. A philosophy.

"You missed a spot," Elian grunted, pointing to a slightly uneven hedge.

Valerius beamed. "I'll have the pickpocket fix it immediately. His hand-eye coordination is excellent."

Elian shook his head and walked toward the palace, a small smile hiding in his beard. He had set out to build a chair. He had ended up building a world where a pickpocket could find redemption in a rosebush.

It was messy. It was chaotic. It was beautiful.

It was, he decided, acceptable.

This is the perfect, legendary send-off. It cements Elian’s status not as a king or a hero, but as something far more terrifying and effective: a Force of Nature that demands competence. The fact that the populace is terrified of being *competent* is the ultimate comedic legacy of his journey.
