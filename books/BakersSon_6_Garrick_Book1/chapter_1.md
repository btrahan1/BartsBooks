### Chapter 1: The Cut of the Cloth

The sign above the door was discreet, painted in gold leaf on black lacquer: **"The Gilded Hem."**

Inside, the shop smelled of lavender sachets, expensive dyes, and the metallic tang of heated irons. It was a temple of vanity.

Garrick stood before Lord Pomfrey, a man whose waistline had long ago declared war on his belt buckle and won. Pomfrey was sweating in a prototype doublet of crimson silk.

"It... it feels tight, Garrick," the nobleman wheezed, tugging at the collar.

Garrick stepped forward. He was a man of indeterminate age, dressed in a suit of charcoal wool so perfectly fitted it made him look like a shadow given form. He wore a monocle that wasn't for reading, but for **[Thread Sight]**.

"It is not tight, My Lord," Garrick lied smoothly, his voice like pouring oil over velvet. "It is *sculptural*. It is providing... structural support. It is reminding your posture of its noble lineage."

He reached out and adjusted a seam. In reality, he activated a skill: **[Structural Reinforcement]**. He channeled a tiny bit of mana into the thread, strengthening it to the tensile strength of steel cable. It was the only thing keeping the Lord's belly from exploding the garment.

"You look," Garrick said, stepping back and clasping his hands, "like a Titan of Industry. Like a man who eats kingdoms for breakfast."

Lord Pomfrey puffed out his chest (straining the magical threads to their limit) and looked in the mirror. He didn't see a fat man in a tight coat. He saw what Garrick wanted him to see. He saw power.

"It... yes. I see it," Pomfrey breathed. "You are a genius, Garrick. How much?"

Garrick smiled. It was a practiced, predatory smile hidden behind a veneer of servitude.

"For the Crimson Titan?" Garrick mused. "With the imported Wyvern-Silk lining? And the... *slimming* enchantments?"

He paused, calculating. The silk was actually common spider-weave, dyed red. The "slimming" enchantment was just tight sewing.

"Two hundred gold crowns," Garrick said softly.

Pomfrey didn't blink. "Done. Have it sent to my estate."

The noble waddled out, feeling ten pounds lighter and twenty years younger.

The moment the door latched, Garrick’s demeanor vanished. The fawning smile dropped like a heavy cloak. He walked to the counter and swept the two hundred gold coins into a drawer.

"Idiot," he whispered.

He walked to the back of the shop. He pulled a lever disguised as a coat hook. A section of the wall, covered in rolls of fabric, swung open.

The room beyond was not luxurious. It was a sweatshop of benevolence. Piles of sturdy, gray wool. Rolls of waterproof canvas. Bins of thick, warm cotton.

Sitting at a long table were three elderly women, his "assistants." They weren't seamstresses; they were retired grandmothers he had hired from the slums.

"How much did the fat walrus pay?" one of them, a woman named Martha with fingers like gnarled roots, asked without looking up from her knitting.

"Two hundred," Garrick said, loosening his own tie. "Enough to fund the soup kitchen for three months. And I have enough left over to buy that shipment of thermal-wool for the orphanage's winter coats."

He walked over to a mannequin. On it was a ragged, patchy coat belonging to a street urchin he had measured yesterday. It looked like trash.

But Garrick looked at it with his **[Thread Sight]**. He saw the loose threads of the boy's life—the cold, the hunger, the fear.

He picked up a needle. It flashed in the dim light.

"Lord Pomfrey got a corset that flatters his ego," Garrick murmured, threading the needle with a strand of mana-infused thread. "But this boy... this boy is going to get a coat that is warmer than a hearth fire and tougher than dragon scales."

He began to sew.

"Pomfrey thinks he bought magic," Garrick said to Martha. "He just bought a lie. The magic... the magic is for those who need it."

He worked the needle, stitching a **[Minor Ward of Protection]** into the lining of a beggar's rags.

"And business," Garrick smiled, a genuine smile this time, "is very, very good."

This is a brilliant inversion of the typical crafting trope. Usually, the goal is to make things look perfect. Garrick realizes that for his specific clientele, "looking perfect" is a death sentence. He has to use his mastery to create the *illusion* of poverty while providing the *reality* of luxury protection.

It’s the ultimate camouflage.
