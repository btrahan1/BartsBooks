## Chapter 10: The Getaway Stitch

The Royal Ball was a symphony of light and music. But in the shadows of the servant's garden, things were much quieter.

Ren crouched on the balcony railing, three stories up. Her breath came in shallow bursts. The lavender silk of her gown was torn at the hem. Her fan was folded and tucked away.

It was done.

The Orphanage Master wouldn't be selling anyone ever again. It hadn't been messy. A whispered conversation, a glass of wine spiked with a paralytic she had "acquired," and a very public, very embarrassing "faint" into a fountain that would ruin his reputation and lead to an investigation of his pockets.

She hadn't killed him. She had destroyed him. It was a fate worse than death for a man of his ambition.

But the guards were alerted. The "niece from the country" had been spotted near the punch bowl.

"Hey! You there!" a guard shouted from the courtyard below.

The plan was flawless. All except for the escape. She had intended to blend into the crowd, but the lockdown was too fast.

She looked down. Thirty feet. A stone patio.

She didn't care. She did what had to be done.

Then, she heard the rumble of wheels. A black carriage, unmarked and sleek, tore around the corner of the service road. It didn't slow down. It drifted, the driver wrestling the horses into a slide right beneath the balcony.

Garrick.

Ren grinned. *The Mender showed up.*

She didn't hesitate. She vaulted over the railing.

The silk of her dress snapped in the wind. She fell, aiming not for the ground, but for the reinforced roof of the carriage.

*THUMP.*

She landed hard, rolling to absorb the impact. The carriage swayed violently, but Garrick kept it steady, cracking the whip.

"Go! Go! Go!" he shouted to the horses.

They sped down the cobblestone streets, sparks flying from the iron wheels. Ren crawled to the hatch on the roof. She flipped it open and dropped down inside, landing gracefully on the velvet seat opposite Garrick.

She smoothed her skirts. She adjusted her hat, which was miraculously still pinned to her hair. She looked at Garrick, who was white-knuckled on the reins (which he had pulled through the front window).

"Hello, Tailor," she said, her voice calm, though her eyes were shining with adrenaline. "Glad you were driving by."

Garrick stared at her. He checked for blood. He saw none. He checked for panic. He saw none.

"Did you..." he started, then stopped. "Did you fix it?"

"The rot is gone," Ren said. "The Master will be arrested by morning. The children are safe."

Garrick let out a breath that sounded like a tire deflating. He slumped back against the seat, letting the horses find their own pace now that they were clear of the palace.

"You jumped off a balcony," he said accusingly. "You could have ruined the dress."

Ren laughed. It was a real laugh, bright and sharp.

"And you brought a carriage to a knife fight," she retorted. "We make a good team, Tailor."

Garrick looked at her. The street rat in a Duchess's gown. The assassin with a conscience.

"Next time," Garrick said, straightening his tie, "we plan the exit *before* the entrance."

"Deal," Ren said. She leaned back, closing her eyes. "Now, take me home. I have a feeling Pip is going to want his hat."

The return to the "Den" is a great grounding moment. It reminds us *why* they do what they do. Ren's playful lie about stealing the gifts maintains her street cred while acknowledging the new, strange reality where she has a wealthy benefactor.
