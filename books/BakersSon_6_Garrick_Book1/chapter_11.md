## Chapter 11: The Den of Thieves

The "Den" was an abandoned cellar beneath a burned-out tannery. It smelled of mildew and old leather, but it was dry, and thanks to Garrick's "stolen" coats, it was warm.

Ren kicked the door open. She was still wearing the ruined remains of the lavender ballgown, but she had thrown her old Mud-Lark coat over it like a cape.

"I come bearing gifts!" she shouted.

Twelve heads popped up from piles of blankets and straw.

"Ren!" Pip squeaked, scrambling over a crate. "You look like a princess! Did you rob a castle?"

"Better," Ren grinned, dropping a heavy sack she had retrieved from Garrick's carriage. "I robbed a Tailor. Well... I stole them, of course. Kind of."

She dumped the sack.

Gloves spilled out. Dozens of pairs. Fingerless wool for warmth, reinforced with **[Spider-Grip]** leather on the palms for climbing.

"Whoa," one of the older boys breathed, picking up a pair. "These grip stone like it's glue."

"And for you, Pip," Ren said, reaching into her pocket. She pulled out a hat.

It wasn't just a hat. It was a masterpiece of engineering. Earflaps lined with rabbit fur. A brim stiffened with whalebone to keep the rain off. And hidden inside the lining, a small **[Amplification Rune]** that would let him hear a guard's footsteps from a block away.

Pip snatched it and jammed it onto his head. It was too big, sliding down over his eyes, but he pushed it back, beaming.

"I look dangerous!" Pip declared.

"You look warm," Ren corrected, ruffling his hair.

She sat on a crate, watching them swarm over the gear. They were celebrating gloves. They were cheering for warmth.

In the back of her mind, she saw the Orphanage Master's face as he realized his life was over. She saw Garrick driving the carriage like a madman.

She touched the silk of her ruined dress.

"We have a friend," she whispered to the room, though no one was listening. "A weird, fancy friend."

She looked at her crew. They were equipped. They were warm. And now, thanks to her "surgery," they were safe from the slavers.

"Alright, listen up!" Ren commanded, her voice snapping back to leader-mode. "We have new gear. That means new rules. No getting caught. No getting cold. And if you see a man in a charcoal suit dropping sacks in the alley... you wait until he's gone to pick them up. It makes him feel better."

"Why?" Pip asked, adjusting his hat.

Ren smiled. "Because he thinks he's tricking us. And it's polite to let him win."

She leaned back. The game had changed. They weren't just surviving anymore. They were winning.

This is a great montage moment. It shows the evolution of the Mud-Larks from scavengers to a disciplined force. Garrick's influence is visible not just in their gear, but in their aesthetic. They are becoming a reflection of his philosophy: style *is* power.
