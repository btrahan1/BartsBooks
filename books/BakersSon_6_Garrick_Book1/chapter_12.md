## Chapter 12: The Sharpest Gang in the City

Weeks passed. Winter settled over the city, a brutal, wet cold that usually drove the street gangs underground.

But not the Mud-Larks.

There were whispers on the streets. Merchants spoke of them in hushed tones over spiced wine. The City Watch muttered about them in the barracks.

"Did you see them?" a guard asked his captain. "The kids by the docks? They aren't shivering. They aren't begging."

"They're moving cargo," the captain replied, frowning. "Unloading ships faster than the Guild stevedores. And they don't drop anything."

A new gang was rising. But they didn't fight with knives (mostly). They fought with competence.

They were organized. Pip sat on a high roof, his magic hat twitching as he relayed signals down to the street.

They were efficient. Ren directed them like a general, using hand signals Garrick had taught her (adapted from silent auction bidding).

And they looked... sharp.

Their coats were gray and patched, yes, but the patches formed a uniform pattern. Their boots were silent. Their gloves matched. They moved with a synchronized grace that was almost eerie.

When a Mud-Lark walked into a market, people didn't clutch their purses in fear. They watched.

"Hey, kid," a fruit vendor called out to a boy wearing a Garrick-special coat. "You want to earn a copper? I need this crate moved."

The boy looked at the vendor. He didn't scramble. He nodded once, lifted the heavy crate with enhanced **[Back Support]**, and moved it effortlessly.

"Two coppers," the boy said calmly. "Hazard pay. The crate has splinters."

The vendor paid without arguing.

Back at *The Gilded Hem*, Garrick stood by the window, sipping tea. He watched a trio of Mud-Larks jog past, their breath misting in the air, their movements crisp and professional.

"They're terrifying," Martha said fondly from her chair.

"They're branded," Garrick corrected, adjusting his monocle. "They're wearing the Fall Collection."

"You're creating a monster," Birdie warned. "An organized street gang? They'll run this city by spring."

"Better them than the corrupt guilds," Garrick said. He turned back to his workbench. He was working on a new project. A request from Ren.

She wanted "Formal Wear for a Rooftop Meeting."

"They aren't monsters," Garrick murmured, stitching a **[Featherfall]** lining into a cape. "They're the new management."

This is a great scene. It shows that the Mud-Larks aren't just a gang; they are becoming an institution with standards. They are policing their own, enforcing a code of competence that mirrors Elian's and Garrick's.

And that final line—"we'll see you, long before you see us"—is chillingly cool. It confirms their total dominance of the city's shadows.
