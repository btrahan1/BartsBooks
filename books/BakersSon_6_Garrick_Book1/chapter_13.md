## Chapter 13: Quality Control

The marketplace was crowded. A young pickpocket, new to the city and desperate, bumped into a wealthy merchant. It was a clumsy bump. His hand lingered too long. The purse strings snagged.

The merchant shouted. "Thief!"

The boy panicked and ran. He ducked into an alley, his heart pounding, clutching the few silver coins he had managed to grab.

He stopped, panting, thinking he was safe.

A shadow detached itself from the wall. Then another. And another.

Three figures stood before him. They wore gray coats that seemed to blur in the dim light. They wore gloves that gripped the brick walls like spiders.

The leader, a girl with sharp eyes (Ren's lieutenant, a girl named Kestrel), stepped forward. She didn't look angry. She looked disappointed.

"You there!" she barked. "That was the worst pickpocket attempt I've ever seen! You telegraphed the bump! You used your thumb instead of your index finger! You practically introduced yourself!"

The boy trembled. "I... I needed the money."

"We all need money," Kestrel snapped. "But we don't insult the craft."

She held out her hand. "Give it back."

"What?"

"The purse. Give it back. You don't even deserve what you stole. It was shoddy work."

The boy, terrified by these strange, professional children, handed over the purse.

"I'm going to return this," Kestrel said. "I'll tell the merchant you dropped it. We have a reputation to maintain."

She looked the boy up and down. He was skinny. Shivering. His shoes were cardboard.

"You have potential," she decided. "You're fast. Just stupid."

She pointed a gloved finger at him.

"Come see us tonight at the docks. Warehouse 4. We're holding tryouts."

The boy blinked. "How... how will I find you?"

Kestrel smiled. It was the smile of a predator who knows the jungle better than the prey.

"Don't worry," she said, fading back into the shadows with her crew. "We'll see you, long before you see us."

The boy stood alone in the alley. He had just been robbed of his robbery. But for the first time in his life, he didn't feel lost. He felt like he had just been invited to an interview.

And he was going to be early.

This is the crucial pivot point. Ren realizes that success brings scaling problems. A gang of thieves is sustainable; an army of highly competent operatives needs structure, legitimacy, and a bigger plan. She's ready to evolve from a gang leader to something more like a Guild Mistress or a Spymaster.

It's time for the Mender and the Fixer to have another strategy meeting.
