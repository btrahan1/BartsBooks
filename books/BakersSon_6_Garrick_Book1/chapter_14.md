## Chapter 14: Scaling Up

The Den was crowded. Too crowded.

Ren sat on her crate "throne," watching the chaos. There were thirty kids now. Thirty mouths to feed. Thirty coats to mend. They were successful, yes. They controlled the docks. They had the respect of the market vendors.

But they were still just kids in an alley.

"Ren," Pip said, tugging on her sleeve. "We're out of soup."

"Ren," Kestrel reported. "The City Watch is asking questions. They want to know who 'The Grey Ghosts' are."

"Ren," another boy said. "I found a ledger in a warehouse. It has numbers I don't understand, but the merchant looked really scared when he lost it."

Ren rubbed her temples. Success was exhausting.

They were growing. And this bothered Ren... immensely.

If they stayed in the shadows, eventually the light would find them. A bigger gang would come. Or the corrupt guards would raid them. Or a noble would decide they were a nuisance and burn the tannery down.

"We need to up our game," she whispered to herself.

Being "just thieves" wasn't enough. Thieves got caught. Thieves got hanged.

But they had skills. They had intel. They had the ledger the boy found—which she suspected proved tax evasion by a major Guild.

There were problems in this city that "just thieves" couldn't solve. Problems of law, of politics, of legitimacy.

She looked at her crew. They were sharp. They were loyal. They deserved better than a basement.

"Pip," she said, standing up. "Get your hat. We're going uptown."

"To rob?" Pip asked hopefully.

"No," Ren said, adjusting her stolen-mended coat. "To negotiate. It's time to talk to the Tailor."

She needed more than coats now. She needed a business model. And Garrick was the only man she knew who could turn a lie into a luxury brand.

"We're going to stop being a gang," she announced to the room. "And start being a Service."

This is perfect. "Delivery Service" is the ultimate euphemism. It implies logistics, which is boring and respectable, but in Ren's world, they "deliver" justice, information, and consequences.

Garrick's confusion turning into realization will be fun to write. He's created a monster, and now the monster wants a uniform upgrade.
