## Chapter 15: The Grey Service

Garrick was fitting a Duke for a "hunting ensemble" (designed for a man who had never hunted anything more dangerous than a roasted quail) when the back door chime sounded. A specific rhythm. Three shorts, one long.

He excused himself and entered the back room.

Ren was sitting on his cutting table, eating an apple she hadn't paid for.

"You look tired," Garrick observed. "Success weighing heavy?"

"Success is crowded," Ren said. "We're expanding. We have new recruits. We have intel. We have leverage."

She took a bite of the apple.

"We need a rebrand," she said. "The 'gray rags' look was good for winter. It said 'pity us.' But spring is coming. We don't want pity anymore. We want respect. We want access."

"You want what?" Garrick asked, intrigued. "Spring uniforms?"

"Something cleaner," Ren said. "Crisp. Professional. Like those couriers the Royal Palace uses, but... sharper. Darker. I want people to see us and think 'official business,' not 'street rat.'"

Garrick leaned against the wall, crossing his arms. "What are you planning? A delivery service?"

Ren grinned. It was the shark grin.

"No," she said. "A different kind of service. But you could call it a 'delivery service'... of sorts."

She hopped off the table.

"We deliver messages people don't want to hear," she explained. "We deliver lost items to their owners—for a fee. We deliver 'evidence' to the magistrate when a merchant cheats on his taxes. We deliver... clarity."

Garrick stared at her. She was proposing a private intelligence agency run by orphans. It was insane. It was dangerous.

It was exactly the kind of structural disruption Elian would have loved.

"The Grey Service," Garrick mused. "Sounds... legitimate."

"Legitimate enough to rent a real office," Ren said. "Above ground. With a sign."

Garrick walked to his fabric rack. He pulled down a bolt of lightweight, slate-blue canvas. It was tough, breathable, and looked very official.

"Double-breasted jackets," he muttered, the design already forming in his mind. "Brass buttons—enchanted to tarnish if poison is near. Caps with hidden pockets for notes. And satchels. Lots of satchels. A person with a satchel is never questioned. They always look like they're supposed to be there."

He looked at Ren.

"This is going to cost you," he warned. "This isn't charity. This is a corporate uniform."

Ren reached into her boot. She pulled out a heavy, jingling pouch. She tossed it to him.

"We had a good month," she said. "The 'tax recovery' business is booming."

Garrick caught the pouch. It was heavy.

"Start measuring," Ren said. "We have a city to run."

That's the perfect description. It lives in the gray area—the *Garrick* area. It's aggressive competence masquerading as civic duty. They aren't extorting businesses; they are "insuring continuity of operations."

This is the birth of the **Grey Service** as a third power in the city, balancing the Nobility and the Guilds.
