## Chapter 16: The Gray Area

By the first thaw of spring, the city had changed.

It wasn't a dramatic revolution. There were no riots. There were just... fewer problems.

A merchant who usually lost 10% of his stock to theft suddenly found his losses dropping to zero. Why? Because he paid a small monthly fee to **The Grey Service** for "logistical oversight."

A noble who cheated his servants found a polite, slate-blue uniformed courier at his door, handing him a dossier detailing his indiscretions, along with a suggested "donation" to the Widow's Fund.

It wasn't exactly 'mafia' protection. They didn't break legs if you didn't pay. They just... stopped helping. And suddenly, your crates got lost. Your carriage wheel broke. Your secrets got loud.

It wasn't exactly 'bribery'. The money went into the Service, funding the office, the uniforms, the food, and the school Ren had set up in the back of the warehouse.

It was legitimate... sort of.

If you looked at it from the right angle (specifically, Garrick's angle), it was simply the free market correcting itself.

Ren sat in her new office. It was a small room above a bakery (rented legally). She wore her new uniform: a slate-blue jacket with silver piping, perfectly tailored by Garrick.

Pip burst in. He was wearing a miniature version of the uniform, looking like a very dangerous bellhop.

"Boss," Pip said. "The City Watch Captain is downstairs. He wants to talk."

Ren didn't panic. She adjusted her cuffs.

"Send him up," she said. "And offer him tea. We're a respectable business now."

The Captain entered. He was a gruesome man named Brutus, used to bullying gangs. He looked at the clean office. He looked at Ren's uniform. He looked at the ledger on her desk.

"You're the... 'Director'?" Brutus scoffed. "You're a kid."

"I am a service provider," Ren corrected calmly. "And my agents tell me you're having trouble with the smuggling ring in the East District. They're using the sewer tunnels."

Brutus blinked. "How do you know that?"

"We know the tunnels better than the rats," Ren said. She pushed a piece of paper across the desk. "Here are the entrance points. And the times of their shifts."

Brutus stared at the paper. "Why give me this?"

"Because smugglers bring in cheap, shoddy goods," Ren said, echoing Garrick's disdain. "Bad for business. Bad for the city."

She leaned back. "The Grey Service supports law and order, Captain. As long as law and order supports us."

Brutus looked at the paper. He looked at the girl. He realized he wasn't dealing with a criminal. He was dealing with a politician.

"Legitimate," Brutus muttered, taking the paper. "Sort of."

"Exactly," Ren smiled. "Sort of."

As the Captain left, Ren looked out the window. She saw her agents moving through the streets, blue specks in the crowd, delivering messages, moving goods, watching everything.

Garrick had stitched the uniforms. But she had stitched the city.

The Mender and the Fixer. They were quite a team.
