## Chapter 2: The Dirty Waltz

The back room looked less like a tailor's shop and more like a riot.

Garrick stood before a table piled high with small, sturdy wool coats destined for the Mud-Lark Gang—a group of orphans who scavenged the riverbanks at low tide. He picked one up. The stitching was flawless. The wool was thick and waterproofed with a high-grade alchemical wax. The buttons were reinforced with bone.

It looked magnificent.

"No," Garrick murmured, tapping his lips. "No, no, no."

Martha looked up from her knitting. "What's wrong? It's the warmest thing you've ever made. It has **[Thermal Regulation +5]**."

"That's the problem," Garrick said, frowning. "Look at it. It screams 'New.' It screams 'Quality.' If a Mud-Lark wears this down to the docks, he won't make it past the first alley. A bigger thug will beat him and steal it. Or worse, the City Watch will assume he stole it from a merchant's son and throw him in the clink."

He tossed the coat back onto the pile.

"They look too nice," he declared. "We need to rough them up a bit. Rub some dirt on them. We need them to look like they've been pulled out of a gutter, digested by a goat, and then worn for three years."

He walked over to a shelf and grabbed a jar labeled **'Urban Grime - Grade A'**. It was a special dye he had concocted himself—a mixture of charcoal, coffee grounds, and a harmless alchemical pigment that permanently stained fabric a depressing shade of brownish-gray.

He sprinkled the powder over the pristine pile of coats.

"Throw them on the ground," Garrick commanded.

Martha and the other two assistants, Birdie and Agnes, looked at each other, grinning. This was their favorite part. They swept the pile of masterwork coats onto the dusty floorboards.

Garrick picked up his loot—the heavy bag of gold from Lord Pomfrey—and started to play it like a tambourine, shaking it rhythmically. *Ching-a-ching-ching.*

"Dance, ladies!" Garrick shouted, his voice filled with a manic, infectious joy. "Dance for the poor!"

The three old women cackled and began to stomp. They shuffled, they hopped, they did a seated jig with their heavy work boots right on top of the fabric. They ground the dust and the dye into the wool. They twisted the sleeves. They scuffed the buttons.

Garrick joined in, his polished boots sliding across the coats. He wasn't just stomping; he was applying **[Distressing]**. He knew exactly where a coat would naturally wear—the elbows, the cuffs, the collar. He kicked and twisted, breaking the "memory" of the new fabric, forcing it to relax, to look tired and worn.

It was a chaotic, dusty party in the back of the most exclusive shop in the city.

After ten minutes of vigorous dancing, Garrick held up a hand. "Halt!"

He reached down and picked up the coat he had inspected earlier.

It was transformed. It looked shapeless, stained, and utterly pathetic. It looked like a rag that had been used to wipe down a horse.

But Garrick activated his **[Thread Sight]**.

Underneath the artificial grime, the structural integrity was 100%. The thermal wards were humming. The pockets were actually *larger* on the inside (a trick of spatial folding he was experimenting with).

**[Item: The Mud-Lark's Skin]**
**[Class: Rare (Disguised as Junk)]**
**[Defense: High]**
**[Durability: High]**
**[Special Effect: 'Nothing to See Here'. Passersby will unconsciously avert their gaze, assuming the wearer is of no value.]**

"Perfect," Garrick whispered, patting a 'stain' that was actually a reinforced patch over the heart. "They'll be invisible. And they'll be warm."

He tossed the coat to Martha. "Pack them up. Delivery is tonight. The tide is low at midnight."

He dusted off his impeccable charcoal suit, checked his monocle, and smoothed his hair.

"Now," he said, turning back toward the secret door. "I believe Lady Vanderal is coming in for a fitting at two. She wants a dress that says 'I am richer than God but very sad about it.' I'm thinking... midnight blue velvet with a corset that restricts breathing just enough to induce a delicate faint."

He winked at Martha.

"I'm going to charge her three hundred."

This is a fantastic wrinkle. The orphans have pride. They have a code. They won't accept charity because it makes them feel weak, but they'll happily "steal" a coat because it proves they are clever.

Garrick understands his clients perfectly. He isn't just a tailor; he's a theatrical director staging a heist where the "victim" wants to lose.
