## Chapter 3: The Honorable Theft

The docks at midnight smelled of brine and bad decisions.

Garrick stood in the shadow of a warehouse, dressed not in his fine suit, but in the guise of a careless merchant. He wore a slightly oversized coat (padded for protection), a hat pulled low, and he carried a large, burlap sack that was conspicuously overflowing with "old rags."

Martha and Birdie were hidden nearby, behind a stack of crates, acting as his lookouts.

"They're coming," Martha whispered, her voice carrying on the wind. "The Mud-Larks. Twelve o'clock."

Garrick adjusted his stance. He needed to look distracted. Vulnerable. Like a man who had had too much to drink and was taking a shortcut he shouldn't have.

He stumbled slightly, letting the sack slip from his shoulder. It hit the ground with a soft *thump*, spilling a few of the "dirty" coats onto the cobblestones.

"Curse this... *hic*... darkness," Garrick muttered loudly, fumbling with the sack.

From the shadows of the pier, a dozen pairs of eyes watched him. The Mud-Larks. They were a scrawny, fierce bunch, led by a boy named Pip (no relation to the woodworker's apprentice, though they shared a spiritual affinity for chaos).

Pip signaled his crew. A drunk merchant. Dropping his goods. It was too easy. It was a gift from the streets.

Garrick sensed them moving. He didn't use magic; he used his tailor's intuition. He felt the shift in the air pressure as bodies moved silently through the dark.

*Closer,* he thought. *Come on. Don't be shy.*

He bent down to pick up the coats, deliberately exposing his back. "Just... gotta get these... useless old rags to the... incinerator," he grumbled. "Worthless junk."

That was the hook. If they thought it was trash, they might ignore it. But if they thought *he* thought it was trash, but *they* could see value in it? That was a victory.

Pip moved. He was fast. He darted out from behind a barrel, his bare feet silent on the stones. He grabbed the corner of the sack.

"Yoink!" Pip whispered.

Garrick did his part. He yelped, spun around, and made a clumsy grab for the boy, deliberately missing by a foot. "Hey! You little rat! That's my... garbage!"

"Finders keepers, old man!" Pip crowed. He whistled, and the rest of the gang swarmed. They grabbed the coats, snatching them up with the practiced speed of piranhas.

"Get away!" Garrick shouted, flailing his arms theatrically. "Those are mine! I was going to... use them for... dog bedding!"

"Your dog sleeps cold tonight!" one of the girls shouted back, pulling on one of the enchanted coats. She zipped it up. Her eyes widened. The warmth hit her instantly, a magical embrace against the sea chill. "Whoa. It's... it's really warm!"

"It's magic trash!" another boy yelled, delighted.

They didn't stop to question it. They had won. They had outsmarted a grown-up. They had "liberated" supplies.

Pip grabbed the last coat and gave Garrick a mocking salute. "Thanks for the donation, grandpa!"

They scattered, vanishing back into the shadows of the pier, their laughter echoing in the night.

Garrick stood there, panting, his hands on his knees. He watched them go. He saw the way the coats fit them—perfectly, thanks to his **[Auto-Size Adjustment]** enchantments. He saw their posture straighten as the warmth seeped into their bones. They weren't shivering anymore.

He straightened up, dusting off his disguise. He adjusted his hat.

"Grandpa," he muttered, feigning offense. "I'm thirty-five."

Martha stepped out from the shadows, grinning. "Hook, line, and sinker. They think they're the kings of the dock now."

"Good," Garrick said softly. "Let them feel like kings. It's the only crown they'll ever get."

He looked at the empty alley where the children had disappeared.

"Now," he said, turning back toward the city. "I believe I have a date with a very wealthy, very sad woman who needs a corset that hugs her like a mother."

"You're a con artist, Garrick," Birdie cackled.

"I prefer 'Redistribution Specialist'," Garrick corrected, straightening his tie. "And the night is young. Let's go make some money."

This is the perfect way to reel in Lady Vanderal. Garrick is using the oldest trick in the luxury book: **Exclusivity via Refusal**. By saying he *can't* do it, he makes her want it infinitely more. He challenges her vanity and her wallet simultaneously.

It establishes him as the one in control. He isn't serving her; he's *evaluating* her.
