## Chapter 4: The Miracle of Midnight Velvet

The afternoon sun filtered through the silk drapes of *The Gilded Hem*. Lady Vanderal stood on the dais, surrounded by mirrors. She was a woman of fading beauty and increasing desperation, her husband having recently taken an interest in younger, more vibrant distractions at court.

She wanted a weapon. She wanted a dress that would make her the center of the universe.

Garrick circled her, his monocle glinting. He tapped his chin. He frowned. He let out a long, slow sigh of professional doubt.

"I don't know..." Garrick tsked, shaking his head. "What you are asking for... honestly. To capture the essence of 'Tragic Beauty' while maintaining a silhouette of 'Untouchable Power'? It is... ambitious."

He walked to his fabric wall and touched a bolt of velvet so dark it seemed to absorb the light around it. "I am not sure I can do it. Whose eye is it you want to catch again?"

"Everyone's," Lady Vanderal whispered, her voice trembling. "I want them to look at me and weep for what they cannot have."

Garrick turned back to her. He looked at her with a critical, almost pitying eye.

"Hmm..." he murmured. "This is going to be very expensive. The materials alone... We would need Midnight Velvet from the Void-Weavers. We would need to crush sapphires into the dye for the bodice. We would need a corset structure made of... well, let's just say it's rare."

He stepped closer, invading her personal space just enough to be conspiratorial.

"No..." he said, backing away, his shoulders slumping in defeat. "Sorry. I cannot take the commission. You are asking for a transformation. I am a tailor... not a miracle worker."

In the back room, watching through a peephole, Martha and Birdie had to stuff knuckles in their mouths to hide their grins. *What a ham!*

Lady Vanderal looked stricken. The rejection hit her harder than any price tag could. If the great Garrick said it was impossible, then it truly was. And that made her want it with a fierce, burning hunger.

"Name your price," she said, her voice hard. "I don't care. Double it. Triple it. Just... make me a miracle."

Garrick paused. He looked at her, letting the silence stretch until it was almost uncomfortable. He seemed to be wrestling with his artistic integrity.

"Triple?" he asked softly.

"Triple," she confirmed.

Garrick let out a breath he had been holding. He walked back to the dais. He placed his hands on her shoulders, looking her in the eye in the mirror.

"Very well," he said, his voice grave. "But you must do exactly as I say. The fittings will be grueling. The fabric is... temperamental. It feeds on confidence. If you waver, the dress will fail."

"I will not waver," she vowed.

"Then we begin," Garrick said. He pulled a tape measure from his pocket. It snapped like a whip.

"Three hundred gold became nine hundred," he thought to himself, his face a mask of solemn concentration.

*Nine hundred gold.* That wasn't just coats. That was a new roof for the orphanage. That was a shipment of medical supplies for the Free Clinic. That was a bribe for the magistrate to look the other way when the Mud-Larks "found" a crate of apples.

"Chin up, My Lady," Garrick commanded. "We are going to make them weep."

Oh, that wink! It suggests she knows the game. She knows she's being overcharged, and she *doesn't care* because the result is worth every copper. She isn't buying fabric; she's buying a nuclear weapon for her social war.

This elevates Garrick from a con artist to a luxury service provider. He gives them exactly what they need, even if what they need is a delusion made of silk.
