## Chapter 5: The Weaponization of Silk

For nine hundred gold, Garrick didn't just sew. He performed alchemy.

He broke out the magic. He dug deep into his private stash of materials—the ones he usually reserved for his "charity" work, but refined for high society. He used **[Shadow-Thread]** to create seams that vanished into darkness, slimming the waist not just physically, but visually. He crushed actual, low-grade mana crystals into the velvet dust, so the dress didn't just shimmer; it *pulsed* with a faint, hypnotic aura.

He spared no expense! Well... if you call one hundred gold in expenses for a nine hundred gold commission... sparing.

The corset wasn't just bone and canvas. He used **[Tension Redistribution]**. It took the physical strain of the tight lacing and dispersed it, so Lady Vanderal felt like she was being hugged by a cloud, even as her waist was cinched to an impossible dimension.

When the final fitting arrived, the shop was silent.

Lady Vanderal stood before the triple mirror. She was virtually in tears. She didn't look like a fading wife. She looked... devastating.

The dress was a void of midnight blue that swallowed the light and gave it back as a dangerous allure. Her posture was regal, enforced by the magic in the seams. Her eyes, framed by the dark fabric, looked predatory.

She would not just attend court. She would crush them.

She turned to Garrick. The desperation was gone, replaced by the terrifying confidence of a woman who knows she is the most dangerous thing in the room.

She snapped her fingers. Two footmen struggled into the shop carrying a small, heavy chest.

"One thousand gold," she said, her voice cool and imperious. "Just like we agreed."

Garrick didn't correct her math. He knew better. The extra hundred was a tip. A bribe. A thank you note written in bullion.

"You are too generous, My Lady," Garrick bowed, his face a perfect mask of humility.

Lady Vanderal stepped off the dais. She walked past him, and as she did, she leaned in.

She winked.

She actually winked.

"Good day, Tailor," she purred. "I have an upcoming engagement to attend."

Her grin was quite terrifying. It was the grin of a shark entering a seal pool.

She swept out of the shop, her entourage scrambling to keep up.

Garrick stood there for a moment, the heavy chest of gold at his feet. He felt a strange mixture of pride and fear. He had done his job too well. He had unleashed a monster.

"Well," Martha said, emerging from the back room and eyeing the chest. "That's a lot of soup."

"That's not just soup, Martha," Garrick said, popping the latch on the chest. The gold glittered, a fortune in cold, hard currency. "That's a new wing for the clinic. That's a bribe for the Watch Captain to ignore the Mud-Larks for a year. That's..."

He paused, a new thought occurring to him.

"...that's seed money."

He looked at the open door, where Lady Vanderal had exited to wage war on her social circle.

"If I can sell confidence to a noble for a thousand gold," he mused, "imagine what I could sell to a King."

But first, he had a delivery to make. The Mud-Larks needed boots. And for that, he needed leather that looked like trash but walked like air.

"Birdie!" he shouted. "Get the sandpaper! We're making masterwork boots look like garbage!"

We absolutely do.

This has a completely different energy from the previous three stories, and that's its strength.

*   **Bran (Baker)** was pure-hearted and physically strong.
*   **Tim (Martial Artist)** was pragmatic and invisible.
*   **Elian (Woodworker)** was grumpy, philosophical, and artistic.
*   **Garrick (Tailor)** is **Machiavellian, slick, and theatrical.**

**Why this works:**
1.  **The "Robin Hood" Economy:** The mechanism of overcharging the rich (who *want* to be overcharged because it validates their status) to fund high-tech gear for the poor is brilliant. It’s a sustainable model of heroism.
2.  **The "Reverse Crafting":** Spending time making masterwork items look like trash is a hilarious and unique twist on the LitRPG crafting trope. Usually, it's about making things shiny. Garrick creates "Stealth Wealth."
3.  **The Clientele:** Lady Vanderal is a great archetype. The customers aren't just annoyances (like Elian's); they are *weapons* Garrick is arming. He is essentially an arms dealer for social warfare.
4.  **The Aesthetic:** The contrast between the lavender-scented front shop and the chaotic "dance party" back room is fantastic.

**Where it could go:**
*   **The Conflict:** The City Watch or a rival "High End" tailor who is actually a dark mage.
*   **The Escalation:** He eventually has to dress the King or Queen, perhaps disguising a ruler as a beggar or vice versa.
*   **The "Mud-Lark" Army:** The street kids he's equipping eventually become his eyes and ears, a spy network dressed in magical rags.

This has legs. It’s got style, wit, and a very clever protagonist who solves problems with psychology and thread rather than a sword. It’s definitely a worthy addition to your crafting saga!

This is the perfect evolution. It moves Garrick from a "benevolent trickster" to a "strategic partner." It also introduces a character who can puncture his ego, which is essential for a character as slick as he is.

Let’s introduce the Queen of the Mud-Larks.
