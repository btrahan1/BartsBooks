## Chapter 6: The Cobbler’s "Mistake"

A week later, the moon was obscured by heavy clouds. Perfect weather for a heist.

Garrick was ready for Act Two. This time, the props were boots. Twelve pairs of them. He had spent the afternoon crafting them from supple calfskin, enchanted with **[Silence]** and **[Sure-Step]**. They were boots that could run across wet slate without slipping and sprint down a hallway without a whisper.

Then, he and Birdie had taken sandpaper, a rasp, and a bucket of dock-mud to them. Now, they looked like stiff, cracked, reject leather that had been left out in the rain.

Garrick adjusted his disguise—a different hat, a fake beard, and a limp. He dragged the sack down a narrow alleyway known as "The Rat Run," a favorite shortcut for the Mud-Larks.

He reached the designated spot. He groaned theatrically.

"Oh, my aching back," Garrick complained to the empty air. "This sack of... *worthless*... boots is too heavy. Maybe I'll just rest my eyes for a moment..."

He leaned against the wall, sliding down until he was sitting. He closed his eyes, feigning sleep, the sack loosely held in his hand. He waited for the pitter-patter of little feet. He waited for the "Yoink."

He waited.

And waited.

A minute passed. Then two. The alley was silent.

Garrick cracked one eye open. Nothing.

He frowned. Had he miscalculated the route? Was his acting too subtle? He let out a loud, snorting snore and twitched his leg, kicking the sack open so the "ruined" boots spilled out invitingly.

*Clap. Clap. Clap.*

It was the slow, rhythmic sound of applause.

Garrick froze. He opened both eyes.

Standing directly in front of him, leaning casually against a crate, was a girl. She looked to be about sixteen, wiry and sharp-featured. She was wearing one of his coats—the "Mud-Lark Skin." It fit her perfectly, the magical tailoring adjusting to her frame. She had accessorized it with a belt made of rope and a terrifyingly large knife.

"Bravo," she said, her voice dry and rasping like sand on stone. "The limp was a nice touch. But you overplayed the snore. A real drunk wheezes more."

Garrick dropped the act. He sat up straight, dusting off his disguise. There was no point in playing the fool when the audience had read the script.

"You're not Pip," Garrick noted.

"Pip is eight," the girl said. "He thinks you're a magic idiot who drops treasure. I'm **Ren**. And I know that nobody drops masterwork leather by accident twice in a week."

She stepped forward, picking up one of the boots. She ignored the mud and the scratches. She flexed the sole. She ran her thumb along the stitching.

"Dark-thread binding," she analyzed, her eyes narrowing. "Reinforced arch support. And..." She tapped the heel on the stone. It made no sound. "Silencing wards. This is high-end work. You're the Tailor from the Gilded Hem."

Garrick stared at her. He felt a sudden, sharp spike of respect. She hadn't just seen through the disguise; she had analyzed the craft.

"You have a good eye, Ren," Garrick said, standing up and ditching the fake limp. "And yes. I am."

Ren crossed her arms. "So here's the question, Tailor. Why is a man who charges nobles a thousand gold for a dress playing Santa Claus in a dirty alley?"

"Redistribution," Garrick said smoothly. "The nobles pay for the vanity. You pay with... opportunity cost."

"We're not charity cases," Ren snapped, a flash of fierce pride in her eyes. "We don't take handouts. We take what we can get because we're smarter and faster than the marks."

"And you are," Garrick agreed. "That's why I'm here. I don't want to give you charity. I want to give you equipment."

Ren looked at the boots, then back at him. She was calculating. She was a merchant of the streets, just as he was a merchant of the salon.

"You're arming us," she realized. "Coats that hide us. Boots that quiet us. You're building an army."

"I'm building a network," Garrick corrected. "I live in the high world, Ren. I see the tops of the heads of the powerful. But you? You see their feet. You see where they go at night. You hear what they say in the alleys."

He kicked the sack of boots toward her.

"The boots are yours," Garrick said. "Payment for... future considerations. Information. Rumors. The word on the street."

Ren looked at the sack. She looked at her own worn-out shoes, held together with twine. She looked at Garrick.

She grinned. It was a sharp, dangerous grin that mirrored his own.

"You want eyes and ears," she said. "We can do that. But the price isn't just boots."

"Oh?" Garrick raised an eyebrow. "Negotiating? I like you already."

"We need gloves," Ren said, ticking off points on her fingers. "Winter is coming. Fingerless, but warm. Grip-enchanted, for climbing. And Pip needs a hat. He has sensitive ears."

Garrick laughed. It was a genuine sound of delight. He had come to trick some children and found a counterpart.

"Done," Garrick said. "Gloves and a hat. Next week. Same time."

Ren grabbed the sack of boots. She swung it over her shoulder effortlessly.

"Pleasure doing business with you, Tailor," she said. She turned to leave, then paused. "Oh, and work on the accent. You sound like a noble trying to sound like a drunk. Drop the vowels more."

She vanished into the shadows, silent as a ghost in her new coat.

Garrick stood alone in the alley. He adjusted his monocle.

"The Mud-Lark Army," he whispered to himself.

He had expected a group of ragtag scavengers. He had found a highly organized, fiercely proud guild of spies. And he had just become their exclusive outfitter.

"Martha is going to love this," he muttered, and headed home to draft a pattern for climbing gloves.

This is a brilliant development. Ren isn't just a street rat; she's a chameleon. She's beating Garrick at his own game of appearances. This raises the question: where did she get the clothes? Did she steal them? Did she make them? Or does she have a secret life?

It immediately shifts the power dynamic. He’s the tailor, but she just walked into his domain looking like she owns the place.
