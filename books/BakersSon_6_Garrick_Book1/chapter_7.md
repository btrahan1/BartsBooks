## Chapter 7: The Chameleon

The bell chimed. It wasn't the tentative tinkle of a nervous client, nor the imperious ring of a noble. It was a sharp, confident sound.

Garrick was measuring a bolt of emerald silk for a Duchess who wanted to look "forest-adjacent." He looked up, annoyed at the interruption. He looked through the glass of the front door.

His jaw dropped.

Standing there was a young woman. She wore a walking dress of pale dove-gray wool, cut in the latest fashion. The stitching was precise. The fit was impeccable. She wore a hat with a modest but elegant feather, and her gloves were of soft, white kidskin. She looked like the daughter of a wealthy merchant, or perhaps a minor baroness.

But the eyes were the same. Sharp. Calculating. Street-smart.

It was Ren.

Garrick blinked. He looked closer. The posture was different—straight-backed, refined. But the smirk? That was unmistakable.

He hurried to the door and unlocked it.

Ren sauntered inside. She didn't scurry like a rat; she glided. She ran a gloved hand over a display of velvet, her touch light and critical.

"A bit heavy on the nap," she commented, her voice completely changed. Gone was the rasping street slang. Her tone was polished, elevated, with just the right amount of bored privilege. "But the color is adequate."

"Ren?" Garrick hissed, looking around to make sure no other clients were present. "What... how... where did you get those clothes?"

She turned to him, raising an elegant eyebrow. "A lady never reveals her sources, Tailor."

"Did you steal them?" Garrick asked, his mind racing. "If you stole them from a client, the magical signature will—"

"Please," Ren scoffed, dropping the noble accent for a split second. "I didn't steal them. I *assembled* them."

She spun in a slow circle.

"The dress was a discard from the Lady Margrave's maid. A tear in the hem. I mended it. The hat was found in a gutter, crushed. I steamed it back to shape. The gloves?" She wiggled her fingers. "I found one in the East Market and the other in the West. It took me three months to find a match."

Garrick stared. He used his **[Thread Sight]**.

She was telling the truth. The outfit was a Frankenstein's monster of cast-offs and trash. But the mending... the mending was exquisite. It wasn't magical; it was purely mechanical, but it was done with a precision that bordered on genius. She had darned the wool so perfectly the weave looked unbroken.

"You can sew," Garrick said, stunned.

"A girl has to have skills," Ren shrugged, adjusting her feather. "The streets are cold. If you can't fix a coat, you freeze."

She leaned against the counter, her noble persona slipping back into place like a well-oiled mask.

"I'm not here for a fitting," she said. "I'm here for a consultation. We have a... situation. And I need to access the Royal Ball."

Garrick laughed. "The Ball? Ren, I can dress you like a Duchess, but you don't have an invitation. And you don't have a name."

"I have something better," Ren said. She reached into her elegant reticule (which Garrick realized was made from an old boot) and pulled out a crumpled, stained piece of parchment.

"I have the seating chart," she said. "And I know that Baroness Finch is allergic to shellfish, and her husband is currently sleeping with her maid. I'm going to be her 'distant niece from the country'."

Garrick looked at the chart. It was the real deal. High-level intel.

"Why?" Garrick asked. "What's at the Ball?"

Ren's eyes hardened. The street rat peeked through the lady's face.

"The man who runs the orphanage," she whispered. "He's selling the kids to the mines in the north. He's making a deal tonight with a mining magnate. I'm going to stop him."

Garrick felt a cold chill. The orphanage he had been funding. The coats he had sent.

"He's taking my money," Garrick realized, "and selling the children I'm trying to save."

Ren nodded. "Exactly. So, Tailor. You want to play dress-up? Or do you want to help me ruin a slave trader?"

Garrick looked at her. He looked at the masterful mending on her dress. He looked at the fire in her eyes.

"Martha!" he shouted toward the back room. "Get the kit! We have a rush job. We need to turn a street rat into a Countess in two hours."

He turned back to Ren, a savage grin on his face.

"And I have just the accessory for you," he said. "A fan. With a hidden blade."

This adds a fantastic layer of grit. Ren is challenging Garrick's "Robin Hood" fantasy. He thinks throwing money and coats at the problem solves it. She knows the rot goes deeper.

This scene establishes Ren as the **Cynic** to Garrick's **Optimist**. He believes in style; she believes in survival.
