## Chapter 8: The Needle and the Knife

The back room was a whirlwind of activity. Martha, Birdie, and Agnes swarmed around Ren like dervishes, tape measures flying, pins glinting in the lantern light.

They were building a new identity. The "Frankenstein" dress was impressive, but it wouldn't pass close inspection at a Royal Ball. Garrick was creating something new. A gown of pale lavender silk, embroidered with silver thread that acted as a mild **[Confusion Ward]**—it made people's eyes slide off the wearer's face, making her harder to recognize later.

Ren stood stock still on the pedestal, her arms raised. She didn't flinch as the pins grazed her skin. She had endured worse.

"There's more going on in this city than hunger and cold, Tailor," she said quietly, staring at her reflection in the dusty mirror.

Garrick was on his knees, hemming the skirt. "I know," he murmured. "Corruption. Greed. That's why I overcharge them."

"No," Ren corrected. "Not just greed. *Cruelty*."

She looked down at him. "You give us coats. You give the orphanage money. It makes you feel good. It makes you feel like you're fixing things."

"I *am* fixing things," Garrick said, a little defensively. "Those coats kept Pip from freezing last week."

"And tomorrow, the Orphanage Master might sell Pip for ten gold to dig coal until his lungs turn black," Ren said flatly.

Garrick stopped sewing. The needle hovered over the silk.

"You have a kind heart, Tailor," Ren said, and it wasn't a compliment. It was a warning. "But a kind heart won't get done... what needs getting done. You hear me?"

"What needs getting done?" Garrick asked, looking up.

"Surgery," Ren said. "You're applying bandages to a cancer. Tonight? Tonight we cut it out."

She wasn't talking about legal justice. She wasn't talking about exposing the man. She was talking about something permanent.

Garrick stood up. He looked at the girl—no, the young woman—standing before him. She looked like a princess. She spoke like an assassin.

"I don't kill," Garrick said firmly. "I stitch. I mend."

"I know," Ren said. She reached into her sash and pulled out the fan he had promised her. She snapped it open. The hidden blade, thin as a needle and sharp as a razor, caught the light.

"That's why you have me," she said. "You stitch the wound. I'll remove the rot."

Garrick stared at the blade. He thought of the children. He thought of the man selling them. He thought of his "ethical" stealing and his "harmless" cons.

He realized he had been playing a game. Ren was fighting a war.

He took a deep breath. He didn't confiscate the fan. He didn't lecture her.

He reached out and adjusted the lace on her shoulder.

"If you're going to do it," Garrick said softly, "make sure you don't get blood on the silk. It stains terribly."

Ren smiled. It was the sharpest thing in the room.

"I'm a professional, Tailor. I never make a mess."

This is a crucial moment of reflection. Garrick is at a crossroads. He's been comfortable being a "Robin Hood" from a distance, but now he's an accomplice to something darker. Martha's wisdom here is key—she grounds him and validates Ren's capability.

The distinction between "Mender" (reactive) and "Fixer" (proactive/permanent) is the central theme of this arc.
