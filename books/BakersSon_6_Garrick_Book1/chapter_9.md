## Chapter 9: The Mender and the Fixer

The shop was quiet. Ren was gone, swallowed by the night and the city's elite.

Garrick paced the room. He had never been more nervous. Not even when he had sold a vest made of rat-pelt to a Duke claiming it was "Rare Sewer-Mink."

"What have I gotten myself into?" he muttered, running a hand through his hair. "She's a child. A street rat. And I just sent her into a den of vipers with a knife."

He stopped at the window, staring out at the gas-lit street. "What if she gets hurt? What if she gets caught? I'm an accessory to... whatever she's planning."

Martha shuffled over and pressed a cup of hot tea into his shaking hands. "Drink," she commanded. "You're spiraling."

Garrick took the tea. "Martha, she had a blade in her fan. She talked about 'surgery'."

"I know," Martha said calmly, sitting back down at her knitting. "I sharpened it for her."

Garrick choked on his tea. "You what?"

"I've seen that look before," Martha said, her needles clicking a steady rhythm. "Not in you. You're soft, Garrick. You like things neat. Seams straight. Ledgers balanced."

She looked up, her old eyes hard and knowing.

"But that girl? She knows what she's doing. How? What's her story? I don't know. Maybe she was a soldier's brat. Maybe she had to learn the hard way. But I've never seen such resolve."

Martha pointed a needle at him. "She will get it done. And it's probably the first of many things she will 'get done'... with our help. Your help, Master Garrick."

"I didn't sign up for assassinations," Garrick whispered. "I signed up for coats."

"She asked the right question," Martha said softly. "Are you a mender... or are you a fixer?"

Garrick stared into his tea.

A Mender patches the hole. He sews it up, hides the tear, makes it functional again. But the fabric is still weak. The stress remains.

A Fixer? A Fixer reinforces the structure. Sometimes, a Fixer has to cut away the ruined cloth to save the garment.

"The Orphanage Master," Garrick said. "He's the rot."

"And if he isn't removed," Martha said, "no amount of coats will save those children."

Garrick set the cup down. He walked to his work table. He picked up his shears. They were heavy, sharp, capable of cutting through leather, canvas, and silk. They were tools of creation, but they were also blades.

"If she fails," Garrick said, his voice steadying, "she'll need an exit strategy."

"She will," Martha agreed.

"I have a carriage," Garrick said. "It's painted black. Discreet. I can be at the servant's entrance in twenty minutes."

"Take the crossbow," Martha suggested, reaching under her chair and pulling out a heavy, repeating crossbow he didn't know she had. "Just in case."

Garrick looked at the weapon. He looked at the old woman. He realized he didn't know his employees nearly as well as he thought.

"Fine," Garrick said, grabbing his coat. "I'm going. Not to kill. But to make sure my... associate... gets home."

He was still a mender. But tonight, he was going to mend a very dangerous situation.

This is the perfect cinematic escape. Garrick steps up just in time, transforming from a passive worrier into the getaway driver. Ren's nonchalance ("Glad you were driving by") perfectly contrasts with the adrenaline of the moment. It cements their partnership—he handles the logistics; she handles the "surgery."
