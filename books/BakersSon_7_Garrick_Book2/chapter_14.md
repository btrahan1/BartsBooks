## Chapter 12: The Second Jump

Ren sprinted up the stairs, her lungs burning. The Shadow Suit was starting to fade, its magic exhausted by the proximity to the Blight. She was becoming visible.

"There she is!" a guard shouted. A crossbow bolt whizzed past her ear, embedding itself in the plaster.

She reached the third-floor landing. Dead end. Just a long hallway and a massive, stained-glass window depicting the triumph of Artifice over Nature.

"Fitting," she panted.

She could hear the heavy boots of the Golems thundering up the stairs behind her.

She glanced around frantically. The window!

And... was that a familiar sound? The distinct, rhythmic clatter of iron-shod wheels on cobblestones, taken at a speed that was definitely illegal?

She heard the crack of a whip.

"Garrick," she breathed.

She didn't hesitate. She didn't look back.

"See you around, losers!" she yelled at the approaching guards.

She dashed. She leaped.

*CRASH.*

The stained glass exploded outward in a shower of colored shards. Ren fell into the cool night air.

Below her, the black carriage was drifting around the corner, sparks flying from the wheels. Garrick was standing up in the driver's seat, looking up, his face a mask of calculated panic.

"Aim for the velvet!" he screamed.

Ren tucked and rolled. She hit the roof of the carriage. It groaned under the impact, but held. Garrick had reinforced it since the last time.

She scrambled to the hatch and dropped inside.

"Go!" she shouted up to him.

"Going!" Garrick yelled back. The carriage surged forward, leaving the confused guards staring at a broken window and a cloud of dust.

Ren collapsed onto the seat. She was bruised, battered, and her suit was smoking slightly.

But in her hand, she clutched the mirror-disc. It had recorded everything. The machine. The rock. The Guildmaster.

She looked at the empty seat across from her. There was a small basket. Inside was a thermos of hot tea and a fresh, warm bun.

A note was pinned to it. *For the road. - Martha.*

Ren smiled. She took a bite of the bun. It tasted like victory.

"Delivery complete," she whispered.

The war for the city was about to begin. And the Grey Service just fired the first shot.

This is unbelievable. It's the perfect escalation of the William joke. He isn't just mimicking Elian anymore; he's *weaponizing* the persona. The image of a toddler pacing like a drill sergeant, scolding grown men for being "unbalanced," is the funniest thing in the entire series.

And Elian's reaction—going from righteous fury to baffled pride—is the perfect cherry on top.
