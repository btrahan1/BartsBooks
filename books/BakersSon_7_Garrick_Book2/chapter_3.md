### Chapter 1: The Healer’s Habit

The bell of *The Gilded Hem* chimed. It was a soft, polite sound, distinctly different from the demanding ring of a noble or the sharp, coded rap of the Grey Service.

Garrick looked up from a pile of receipts. Business was booming, the taxes were delightfully low thanks to a certain Royal Decree, and he was currently calculating the markup on "imported nebula-silk" (which was actually just cotton treated with phosphorescent algae).

Standing in the doorway was a man who radiated a calm so profound it felt like a physical drop in temperature. He wore simple, slate-gray robes that were impeccably clean but tragically cut—boxy, lifeless, and utterly devoid of style. In his hand, he held a staff of pale ash that seemed to glow with an inner pearlescent light.

Garrick adjusted his monocle. He recognized the man instantly. Everyone did. It was **Finn**, the High Healer of the Silent Leaf, the man who had turned a battlefield into a garden and made a dragon cry.

"High Healer," Garrick said, stepping out from behind the counter and offering a bow that was respectful but not fawning. "You bring a great deal of tranquility into my shop. It’s making the velvet nervous."

Finn smiled. It was the smile of a man who had stared down a monster and offered it a tissue. "Master Garrick. I am told you are the man to see when one needs... practical miracles."

"I specialize in the impossible," Garrick agreed smoothly. "Usually involving corsets and egos. But for a man of the cloth? I assume you don't need to look thinner."

Finn stepped further inside. Behind him trailed a teenager, a novice of the Temple. She looked overwhelmed, clutching a satchel of herbs like a shield.

But it was her clothes that made Garrick wince physically.

She was wearing the standard-issue novice robes of the Silent Leaf. They were a drab, shapeless sack of gray wool. The hem was too long, dragging on the floor. The sleeves were too wide, threatening to knock over displays. The shoulders slumped.

It wasn't clothing; it was an apology made of fabric.

"This is **Maya**," Finn said gently, placing a hand on her shoulder. "My new apprentice. She has... significant potential. But she is having trouble with the uniform."

Maya looked up, her face flushing. "I keep tripping," she whispered. "And the sleeves dip into the mixing bowls when I make poultices."

Garrick walked around the counter. He circled the poor girl like a shark circling a swimmer, his monocle glinting.

"Tripping is an understatement," Garrick declared. "That garment is a safety hazard. It is a burlap sack that has given up on life."

He looked at Finn. "You want me to dress a healer. You want functionality. You want mobility. You want..." He looked at the staff in Finn's hand. The wood seemed to hum at him. Garrick narrowed his eyes. "...and I suppose the stick has an opinion too?"

Finn chuckled. "Jasmine usually does. She thinks Maya deserves something with 'flow'."

Garrick sighed, the sound of a master artist burdened by the lack of aesthetic standards in the world.

"Flow," Garrick muttered. "I can do flow. But it's going to cost you. Not gold," he added quickly, seeing Finn's humble expression. "I have enough gold."

He looked at the novice, seeing not just the bad robe, but the potential underneath. He used his **[Thread Sight]**. He saw lines of vibrant, green energy radiating from her—healing magic, wild and untamed, currently being suffocated by the heavy wool.

"I want a favor," Garrick said. "I have a... associate. A young man named Pip. He runs a very active organization. He gets into scrapes. I want him to have access to the Temple's healing services. No questions asked. Day or night."

Finn looked at Garrick. He saw the shadow of the Grey Service behind the tailor's request.

"The Temple is open to all," Finn said. " But for Pip... we can keep a special cot ready."

"Excellent," Garrick clapped his hands. "Martha! Get the tape measure! We are burning this gray sack immediately. We need something in a breathable linen, reinforced elbows, and for the love of the gods, a hemline that doesn't sweep the floor!"
