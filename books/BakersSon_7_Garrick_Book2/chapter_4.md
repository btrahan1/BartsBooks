## Chapter 2: The Living Habit

Two days later, Maya stood before the triple mirror.

The outfit wasn't breathtaking. It wasn't the midnight velvet of Lady Vanderal, nor the slate-blue authority of the Grey Service uniforms. It was a soft, understated sage-gray. To the casual observer, it looked like a simple tunic and trousers, perhaps made of high-quality linen.

Healers weren't supposed to be breathtaking. They were supposed to be present.

But to Maya, it felt like she was wearing armor made of clouds.

"Move," Garrick commanded, circling her. "Reach for a high shelf. Kneel as if tending a patient. Run."

Maya obeyed. She reached up; the sleeves didn't pull at the shoulder. She knelt; the fabric at the knees didn't bunch or pinch. She spun; the tunic flared slightly but settled instantly back into place.

It fit her like a glove. Better than a glove. It felt less like clothing and more like a second skin that anticipated her movements.

"It... it doesn't get in the way," Maya breathed, looking at her arms. The sleeves were tapered, ending in snug cuffs that wouldn't drag in a mixing bowl, yet they didn't constrict her wrists.

"Of course it doesn't," Garrick sniffed, adjusting a hem by a millimeter. "Fabric should be a partner, not an obstacle."

Finn, leaning on *Jasmine*, watched with keen eyes. He didn't look at the cut or the fashion. He looked at the mana.

"You used Living Thread," Finn observed, his voice tinged with surprise. "That is... extravagant."

"It is necessary," Garrick corrected. He tapped the shoulder seam. "She is young. She will grow. A standard uniform would be too tight in six months. This?"

He tugged on the fabric. It stretched elastically, then snapped back without a wrinkle.

"This is **[Adaptive Weave]**. It will grow with her. It will always fit her so. If she gains muscle from grinding herbs, the shoulders will broaden. If she grows taller, the hem will lengthen. It is a garment for a lifetime."

He walked around to her side and pointed to the pockets. There were many, hidden in the folds of the tunic, but they laid perfectly flat.

"And these," Garrick said, sliding a heavy mortar and pestle into a pocket that shouldn't have been able to hold it without sagging, "are reinforced with **[Void-Stitch]**. You can carry ten pounds of gear, but it will weigh five ounces against your belt."

Maya reached into the pocket. It was deep, spacious, and impossibly light.

"And finally," Garrick said, picking up a vial of dark, staining ink. Before Maya could react, he splashed it onto her sleeve.

Maya gasped.

The ink hit the fabric and beaded up like water on a hot skillet. It rolled off, dripping onto the floor, leaving the sage-gray linen spotless.

"**[Repulsion Ward]**," Garrick explained. "Blood, bile, mud, potion spills. It repels them all. A healer should not look like a butcher after a day's work."

Finn looked at the outfit. It was a masterpiece of utility disguised as modesty. It was exactly what Elian would have done with wood—finding the true purpose of the object and elevating it.

*Jasmine*, the ash staff, hummed a low, approving note that vibrated the floorboards.

"She likes it," Finn smiled.

"She has good taste," Garrick said, wiping the ink off the floor with a rag. "Now, shoo. You have healing to do. And tell Pip that if he gets stabbed again, he is to go to you immediately, not try to stitch it himself with a fishing hook."

Maya looked at herself in the mirror one last time. She didn't look like a clumsy novice anymore. She looked competent. She looked ready.

"Thank you, Master Garrick," she said, bowing low.

"Don't thank me," Garrick said, turning back to his ledger. "Just don't get shot. It ruins the weave."

This is the perfect way to close the scene. It brings the Grey Service back into the picture immediately, reinforcing the connection between the characters and showing that Garrick isn't the only one who can deliver surprises. It's a nice callback to the "delivery service" theme.
