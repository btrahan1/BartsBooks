## Chapter 3: The Ghostly Delivery

Finn watched Maya walk out of the shop, her stride confident and free for the first time in months. He turned back to thank the tailor again, but Garrick was already engrossed in a debate with Martha about the proper thread count for a Duke’s handkerchief.

"We are square, High Healer," Garrick said without looking up. "The transaction is complete."

Finn grinned, shaking his head. The man was as prickly as a pincushion, but his work was undeniable. He turned to leave, his hand on the door latch.

Then he froze.

Resting on a small display table near the door—a table that had been empty when they walked in—was a bag. It wasn't a fancy silk pouch or a leather satchel. It was a sturdy, slate-blue canvas bag with the discreet silver piping of the Grey Service.

There was a note pinned to it.

Finn picked it up. The bag clinked softly. Inside were vials. Dozens of them. Rare alchemical reagents, purified salts, and a bundle of dried *Night-Bloom*, a herb that only grew in the dangerous alleyways of the lower city and was potent for fevers.

He read the note. It was written in a sharp, angular script.

*"Payment for future services rendered. Keep the cot warm. - R."*

Finn looked around. The bell hadn't chimed. The door hadn't opened. He hadn't felt a single soul move past him.

"When..." Finn murmured, looking at Garrick.

Garrick glanced up, saw the bag, and smirked. "I told you. They're efficient. You were busy looking at the dress. Ren's people were busy stocking your inventory."

Finn laughed, a sound of genuine delight. He slung the bag over his shoulder.

"It seems," Finn said, opening the door, "that the city has become a very interesting place."

He walked out into the bustling street, *Jasmine* tapping a happy rhythm on the cobblestones. The healer had his tools. The apprentice had her armor. And somewhere in the shadows, the city's new guardians were watching, ready to deliver whatever was needed, whenever it was needed.

The game was afoot. And everyone was dressed for the occasion.

This is the collision we've been waiting for. Elian, who deals in tangible, honest wood, reacting to the complex, shadowy, and highly political web Finn has walked into. It brings the two storylines crashing together.

Elian is likely working on something massive (maybe rebuilding the Royal Library or the main Cathedral doors), so finding Finn involved in "spy craft" and "street gangs" is going to baffle his straightforward craftsman's brain.
