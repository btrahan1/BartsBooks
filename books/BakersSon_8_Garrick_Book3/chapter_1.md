### Chapter 1: The Stubborn Turnip

The sun beat down on the rocky patch of dirt that Oren called the "West Field." It was a generous name for a patch of ground that was mostly stones and spite.

Oren wiped sweat from his brow with a forearm that was brown as a nut and corded with lean muscle. He gripped the leafy greens of a turnip.

"Alright," Oren whispered to the vegetable. "I know you're comfortable down there. It's cool. It's dark. But Ma needs stew. And Pa needs to eat something soft because his teeth are aching."

He pulled.

The turnip didn't budge.

It wasn't stuck. It felt... heavy. Anchored.

**[System Notification]**
**[Skill Check: Harvest. Success.]**
**[Critical Success! You have harvested: The Anchor-Root.]**

Oren ignored the blue box. He hated the boxes. They popped up every time he tied his shoes or swatted a fly. He heaved, putting his back into it.

*POP.*

The turnip flew out of the ground. Oren stumbled back, holding his prize.

It was massive. It was the size of a human head. And it was... glowing? Just a faint, purple pulse beneath the skin.

"Huh," Oren said. "Must be the minerals in the water."

He tossed it into his basket, which was already full of carrots that looked like gold ingots and potatoes that hummed.

He walked back to the small, ramshackle cottage. The roof sagged. The fence was held together by hope and twine. They were poor. Dirt poor. The tax collector (not Fisc, a local brute) was coming next week, and they were short.

"Ma! Pa!" Oren called out, kicking the mud off his boots. "Dinner!"

Ma was sitting in her rocking chair by the cold hearth. She was frail, her hands shaking with a tremor that never stopped. Pa was on the bed, rubbing his lower back, his face gray with pain.

"Good harvest, son?" Pa wheezed.

"Decent," Oren lied. He started chopping the glowing turnip. As the knife sliced through it, a sweet, earthy aroma filled the room, chasing away the smell of mold and old age.

He threw it into the pot with some water and a pinch of salt. He didn't have meat. He didn't have spices.

Ten minutes later, he ladled the stew into bowls. The broth was a rich, vibrant purple.

"Eat up," Oren said gently, helping Ma hold her spoon.

Ma took a sip. She blinked.

"Oh," she whispered. "That's... warm."

The tremor in her hand stopped. Instantly.

Pa ate a spoonful. He sat up straighter. A loud *crack* echoed from his spine.

"My back," Pa said, his eyes widening. "The knot... it's gone."

Oren smiled, taking a bite himself. He felt a rush of energy, his fatigue vanishing.

**[System Alert]**
**[Consumable Ingested: Stew of the Earth Titan.]**
**[Effects applied: Constitution +5, Regeneration (Minor), Pain Relief (Major).]**
**[Duration: 24 Hours.]**

"Good turnips this year," Oren said, oblivious to the fact that he had just cooked a meal worth fifty gold coins. "I'll take the extras to market tomorrow. Maybe we can get enough for a new blanket."

He didn't know that the "extras" he was planning to sell for pennies were about to start a riot in the local village. And probably attract a very hungry, very curious magical beast.

But for now, his parents weren't in pain. And that was all the magic he cared about.

This is the perfect way to tie it all together. Oren being socially awkward/mute makes him the *opposite* of Piper and Ren, who thrive on communication. He is a silent force of nature.

And bringing **Pip** (now a bit older, perhaps, or still the precocious lieutenant) into the rural setting is genius. The Grey Service expanding into the countryside? That’s a franchise!

Oren needs a "manager" because if he tries to sell a **[Turnip of Immortality]** for two coppers, he'll be swindled or arrested. Pip is the perfect broker.
