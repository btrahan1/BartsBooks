## Chapter 11: The Rat in the Machine

Ren was falling. The metal chute was slick, steep, and dark.

Panic flared. She flailed, trying to find purchase, but the walls were smooth. She was sliding toward whatever hellish machinery lay at the bottom.

Then, a voice in her mind. Not her own. It was gruff, annoyed, and oddly comforting.

*"Balance. Feel the grain. Master the flow."*

It was Elian. Or rather, her memory of him yelling at her while she tried to carve a simple spoon.

Ren shook her head, her hair whipping in the wind of her descent. "Thanks, Mr. Miyagi," she grumbled through gritted teeth. "Real helpful."

But the words stuck. *Feel the grain.*

The chute wasn't perfectly smooth. It was made of metal plates. Riveted together. There were seams.

Ren stopped flailing. She extended her arms and legs, pressing outward. She didn't fight the slide; she felt for the rhythm of the rivets. *Bump. Bump. Bump.*

She timed it. On the next bump, she dug her heels in. The **[Spider-Grip]** on her boots bit into the seam. Her gloves caught the rivet heads.

*SCREEEEEEEEECH.*

Sparks flew as she slowed, her body acting as a brake. Her muscles screamed. The friction heat warmed the Shadow Suit dangerously.

She slammed to a halt, hanging spread-eagled in the shaft, ten feet above a spinning fan blade that would have turned her into confetti.

"Okay," she panted. "Flow mastered. Now for the exit."

She looked around. There was a maintenance hatch to her left. She kicked it open and swung inside, landing in a quiet corridor.

She was alive. She was inside. And she was angry.

"Time to void some warranties," she whispered, drawing her knife.

She crept down the hall. The air grew colder. She turned a corner and found herself in a storage room.

But it wasn't storing parts. It was storing the source.

In the center of the room, suspended in a cage of glass, was a piece of the Blight itself. A fragment of black, weeping rock. It was pulsing, sending waves of sickness through the air.

Ren felt a wave of nausea. The cold touched her heart. The despair of the Blight tried to claw its way into her soul.

*Flash.*

The golden leaf on her chest flared. Finn's blessing. It burned hot, a searing brand of life against the cold death. The nausea vanished. The despair broke.

Ren gasped, clutching her chest. The leaf was gone. The charge was spent.

But she was clear-headed.

"Thanks, Finn," she muttered.

She looked at the Blight fragment. She looked at the fuel lines running from it to the main machine room.

She couldn't destroy the rock. It was too powerful. But she could destroy the connection.

She pulled out a small bag of Garrick's "Urban Grime." It wasn't just dirt; it was conductive, alchemical dust.

She poured it into the fuel intake.

"Enjoy the indigestion," she said.

She turned to run, but the door opened.

Standing there was the Guildmaster. And behind him, two massive Stone Golems.

"You are a persistent little pest," the Guildmaster said, raising his hand. "Kill her."

Ren backed up. She was trapped. No vents. No windows. Just a angry wizard and two tons of angry rock.

She grinned. It was a terrifying grin.

"I'm not a pest," she said, reaching into her pocket. "I'm a delivery service."

She pulled out a small, wooden wooden duck. William's duck.

She threw it.

Not at the wizard. At the Blight rock.

The duck hit the glass cage. *Clack.*

It did nothing. It bounced off.

The Guildmaster laughed. "A toy? That is your weapon?"

Ren shrugged. "Not the duck. The enchantment on the duck."

Elian had carved it. He had poured his love for his son into it. It was a vessel of pure, innocent joy.

When it touched the aura of the Blight rock, the reaction was... violent.

Joy and Despair cannot occupy the same space.

*CRACK.*

The glass shattered. The Blight rock shrieked. A shockwave of chaotic magic exploded outward.

The Guildmaster was thrown back. The Golems stumbled.

Ren didn't wait. She dove through the confusion, sliding between the Golem's legs, sprinting for the door.

"Complaint filed!" she shouted over her shoulder as the room dissolved into magical anarchy.

She ran. The alarm klaxons began to scream. The entire building was waking up.

She needed an exit. And she needed it now.

It's the classic "Jump into the getaway car" trope, but with the specific flavor of this world! It brings Garrick back into the action as the ultimate support character. He's always there when the window breaks.
