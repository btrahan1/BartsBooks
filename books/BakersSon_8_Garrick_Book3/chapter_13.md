## Chapter 13: The Tiny Terror

Ren's evidence was damning. The mirror-disc showed everything. But legal proceedings took time. Elian did not have time. He had rage.

The Artificer's Guild warehouse (a secondary storage facility Ren had identified) was quiet. The smugglers were counting their ill-gotten gains, thinking they were safe.

There was a knock on the warehouse doors. A polite, firm knock.

They looked up nervously.

And then the doors EXPLODED!

Splinters of oak and iron flew like shrapnel. The smugglers dove for cover behind crates.

In the settling dust, a man stepped in. He was not wearing armor. He wore a simple craftsman's apron. But in his hand, he held a massive, iron-wood mallet.

He looked like a man of nightmares. And he was angry. He was very, very angry.

"You would bring the Blight into *MY* city!" Elian roared, his voice shaking the rafters. The sheer force of his presence pinned the men to the floor.

"For what?" he demanded, stalking forward. "For coin?"

He reached into his pocket and grabbed a fistful of gold coins—the profits from a month of William's Wonders. He threw them. They hit the floor like bullets.

"For *this*! You would endanger MY CHILD! FOR THIS!"

The smugglers trembled. They had faced guards. They had faced rival gangs. They had never faced a furious father with a legendary crafting stat.

In the shocked silence, a little figure stepped from behind his Daddy's robes.

Prince William. He was wearing a tiny, perfectly tailored version of Elian's work clothes. And in his hands, he carried a small, polished wooden baton.

He strode forward. He didn't waddle. He marched.

He stopped in front of the cowering leader of the smugglers. He tapped the baton against his palm. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

He bent down, picked up a gold coin, and with surprising arm strength, flung it.

*ZING.*

It hit the leader square in the forehead. "Ow!"

"You there!" William squeaked, pointing the baton. "Stand up! My Daddy is talking to you!"

The leader, a hardened criminal who had killed men for less, looked at the toddler. He looked at the furious giant behind him. He stood up.

"Do it. Do it now!" William commanded.

He continued forward, pacing back and forth in front of the line of incredulous, terrified men. He clasped the baton behind his back with two hands, his chin jutting out.

"You are all unbalanced!" the toddler declared, his voice echoing in the silent warehouse. "Your grain is crooked! Your joinery is weak! In more ways than one!"

He stopped pacing. He gave them one last, withering glare that he had clearly practiced in the mirror.

Then, he turned and strode back to stand in front of his father.

Elian stood there, his mallet lowered, his mouth agape. His rage had been derailed by sheer, surreal bewilderment.

William looked up at him, beaming.

"All yours, Daddy," the little prince chirped. "They are listening."

Elian looked at his son. He looked at the smugglers, who were now more confused than terrified.

He let out a long, slow sigh. He put his mallet away.

"Right," Elian said, rubbing his temples. "I think the scary part is over. Now comes the boring part."

He pointed to the door. "Get out. Leave the city. If I see you again... I'll let him loose on you."

He gestured to William.

The smugglers didn't need to be told twice. They fled, terrified not of the woodworker, but of the tiny, baton-wielding demon who spoke like a Guild Master.

As they ran, Elian picked up his son.

"Unbalanced?" Elian asked. "Really?"

William hugged his neck. "They had bad posture."

Elian chuckled. "Good eye, son. Good eye."

This is the perfect ending for this little arc. William has fully integrated his father's gruff authority with his mother's regal command (and generosity). The "adequate" line is pure Elian, and the immediate pivot to "hide and seek" reminds us he's still just a kid.

It's a heartwarming, hilarious, and satisfying conclusion to the "Toddler Tyrant" saga.
