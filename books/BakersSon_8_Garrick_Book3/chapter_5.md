## Chapter 5: A Playdate with Shadows

Elian stood over the intercepted crate in the back of the Temple. It looked like a standard shipment of rough-hewn timber. But to his senses, it screamed. It was wood that had been tortured, killed, and then hollowed out to hide contraband.

Inside were daggers. Iron Assembly daggers. But they were coated in a dark, oily residue that smelled of the Northern wastes.

"This is not just smuggling," Elian said, his voice cold. "This is an infection vector. Someone is trying to bring the Blight back into the city, hidden in the guise of commerce."

He looked at Finn. "You said the girl found this? The street rat?"

"Ren," Finn corrected. "And yes. Her people intercepted it at the docks."

Elian nodded, his mind already building a new structure, a new plan. He needed eyes where he couldn't go. He needed the chaos he despised to fight the order he hated even more.

"Arrange a meeting," Elian commanded. "With this... what was her name? Rain?"

"Ren," Finn said again.

"Ren. Fine. Arrange it. Tonight. In my workshop. It's neutral ground."

He paused, a thoughtful look crossing his face. He looked at the deadly daggers, then at the dead wood.

"And... bring snacks," he added. "Good ones. From Antoine."

Finn nodded, turning to leave.

"And Finn," Elian called out.

"Yes?"

"Tell the Queen I'm borrowing the Prince."

Finn stopped dead. "You're... what?"

"I'm bringing my son," Elian said, picking up a piece of the dead wood with a pair of tongs. "It is time he got his feet wet, so to speak. He needs to see the grain of the world, not just the polished floors of the palace. He needs to know what a lie smells like."

Finn stared at him. "He's two, Elian."

"He's a quick learner," Elian said dismissively. "Just make sure the snacks are soft. He's teething."

***

That night, the workshop was silent. Elian sat at the Mayor's Desk (the original one, which he had kept for "administrative purposes"), Prince William sitting on his lap, happily gnawing on a wooden duck.

There was a knock at the back door. The secret knock.

Elian waved a hand, and the door swung open.

Ren entered. She was wearing her "Grey Service" uniform, looking every inch the professional spy-mistress. She was flanked by Pip, who looked terrified to be in the legendary workshop, and Kestrel.

Ren stopped. She looked at the Master Craftsman, whose reputation was terrifying. She looked at the baby drooling on the priceless desk.

She looked at the plate of gourmet pastries.

"This," Ren said, taking off her cap, "is not exactly the interrogation I was expecting."

"Sit down," Elian said, his voice gruff but not unkind. "Have a tart. And tell me everything you know about the dead wood."

William looked up, saw the new people, and waved his soggy wooden duck. "Duck!" he proclaimed happily.

Ren blinked. Then, a slow, genuine smile spread across her face.

"Nice duck, kid," she said, sitting down. "Let's talk business."

This is absolutely hilarious. It’s the ultimate payoff for Elian’s specific parenting style. William isn’t just learning to walk; he’s learning to command a workshop. He is mimicking his father's "God of Competence" persona perfectly, but with the squeaky voice of a two-year-old.
