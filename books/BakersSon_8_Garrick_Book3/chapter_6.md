## Chapter 6: The Toddler Tyrant

The room went dead silent.

Elian had been about to speak, to open the negotiations regarding the Blighted contraband. But he was upstaged.

The young prince wiggled free from his father’s grip and landed on the floor with a determined *thump* of his soft leather booties. He stood up, steadying himself on the leg of the desk.

He scanned the room with a gaze that was disturbingly intense for a toddler. His eyes locked onto Pip, who was trying to hide behind Ren’s chair.

William raised a chubby, imperious finger.

"You there!" William squeaked, his voice a perfect, high-pitched mimicry of Elian’s workshop roar.

Pip froze. He had faced angry merchants, corrupt guards, and rival gangs. He had never faced a toddler Prince with an attitude.

"Come," William commanded, pointing to a spot on the rug next to him. "Sit down! Do it. Do it now!"

Pip looked at Ren, panic in his eyes. "Boss?"

Ren was biting her lip so hard it was turning white, trying to contain her laughter. She nodded. "Better do as he says, Pip. He outranks you. By a lot."

Pip shuffled forward, clutching his oversized magic hat nervously. He sat down on the rug, cross-legged, facing the tiny tyrant.

William nodded. "Acceptable."

He waddled forward, closing the distance. He held out his prize possession—the slobber-covered wooden duck. He thrust it into Pip’s hands.

"Hold," William ordered.

Pip held the wet duck with two fingers, looking terrified.

William walked around Pip, inspecting him. He reached out and touched the fabric of Pip’s Grey Service uniform. He frowned. He tugged on a loose thread.

"Sloppy," William pronounced.

Elian let out a long groan and covered his face with his hand. "Oh, gods. I’ve created a monster."

"No," Ren whispered, her shoulders shaking with silent mirth. "You’ve created an heir."

William finished his inspection and returned to Pip’s front. He sat down with a heavy sigh, mirroring exactly the way Elian sat down after a long day. He patted Pip’s knee.

"Good hat," William said seriously. "Big ears."

Pip blinked. A slow grin spread across his face. He took the duck properly. "Thanks, Your Highness. Nice duck. Good... grain."

William beamed. "Duck is good. Now... snacks?"

He looked at Elian, expecting the service to continue.

Elian uncovered his eyes. He looked at his son, who had successfully commandeered the meeting, intimidated the opposition, and secured a playmate, all in under a minute.

"Yes," Elian said, pushing the plate of tarts toward the rug. "Snacks."

He looked at Ren, his expression resigned. "I apologize. He spends too much time listening to me yell at apprentices."

"Don't apologize," Ren said, grabbing a tart for herself. "He's got leadership potential. If the whole 'King' thing doesn't work out, he's got a future in the Service."

The transition back to business is perfect. It acknowledges the humor while re-establishing Elian's authority. He can't let a toddler run the *entire* kingdom just yet.

This sets the stage for the grim reality of the Blighted smuggling ring to intrude on this bizarrely domestic scene.
