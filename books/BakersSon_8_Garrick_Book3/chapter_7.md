## Chapter 7: The Smuggler's Grain

Elian cleared his throat.

"Now... if William doesn't mind..." He glared softly at his son, who was currently trying to feed a raspberry tart to the wooden duck while Pip politely held it.

William looked up, fruit smeared on his chin. He waved a dismissive hand at his father. "Talk. Busy."

Elian sighed deeply. "Right. Thank you, Your Highness."

He turned back to Ren, his demeanor shifting instantly from weary father to the Master of Wondrous Arts. The playfulness vanished from his eyes, replaced by the cold focus of a craftsman inspecting a rot.

"You were saying?" Elian prompted. "About the dead wood. Where did it come from? Who signed for it?"

Ren swallowed her mouthful of tart. Her expression hardened. "It came in on a ship called *The Shadow's Wake*. Unmarked crates. But the paperwork... the paperwork was forged. Good forgeries, too. Royal seals."

She slid a piece of parchment across the desk. "But here's the interesting part. The destination wasn't a warehouse. It was the basement of the Artificer's Guild."

Elian picked up the paper. The Artificer's Guild. The makers of magical trinkets and constructs. They had always been jealous of the Artisan's Alliance. They felt their "high magic" was superior to Elian's "soul craft."

"They're building something," Ren said quietly. "Something big. My scouts heard grinding. Like stone on metal. And they heard... humming. But not like your wood hums. It sounded... sick."

Elian looked at the intercepted dagger on the desk. He touched the oily residue. It burned his fingertip slightly.

"They aren't just smuggling weapons," Elian realized, his voice grim. "They're trying to replicate the Blight. They're trying to create a power source that rivals our own."

He stood up. "The Artificer's Guild is a closed shop. High security. Wards."

"Impenetrable," Ren agreed. "Unless..."

She looked at Pip, who was currently wearing a second, smaller hat William had found under the desk and placed on his head.

"Unless you're small," Ren said. "And unless you have a tailor who can make a suit that walks through walls."

Elian looked at Pip. He looked at William.

"No," Elian said immediately. "Absolutely not. They are children."

"They are the only ones who can fit in the ventilation ducts," Ren countered. "And Garrick just finished a prototype. A 'Shadow-Suit'. It makes the wearer functionally invisible to magical wards."

"I am not sending my son's new best friend into a Blighted workshop," Elian stated.

"Duck?" William asked, sensing the tension.

"No duck," Elian said firmly.

He looked at the map of the city on his wall. He looked at the location of the Artificer's Guild.

"If we are going to do this," Elian said, "we are not sending a child. We are sending a professional."

He looked at Ren.

"How fast can Garrick make that suit in *your* size?"

This is the perfect crossover moment. Ren going to Garrick for a "mission suit" is like James Bond visiting Q, but with more velvet and attitude. Garrick's mumbling shows his process—he's not just making clothes; he's engineering a magical solution.

The time limit on the suit adds tension. It’s a "growing" suit, but in reverse—it's a temporary, volatile masterpiece.
