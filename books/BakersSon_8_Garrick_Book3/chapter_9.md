## Chapter 9: The Blessing of Ash

Ren was a ghost. She moved through the crowded market square, weaving between carts and pedestrians without making a sound. No one looked at her. No one sensed her. The Shadow Suit was working perfectly. She was invincible.

"One moment, please."

The voice was calm, polite, and terrifyingly close.

Ren froze mid-step. She was invisible. She was warded against magic. How?

She turned slowly. Standing by the fountain, leaning on his staff, was Finn. His eyes were closed. He wasn't looking at her. But *Jasmine*, the ash staff, was glowing with a faint, pulsing light, and the tip was pointed directly at her chest.

"How did you see me?" Ren whispered, pulling back her hood slightly to reveal her face.

Finn opened his eyes. They were warm and kind. "I didn't," he admitted. "It was Jasmine. She senses... intent. And she says your intent is heavy."

He walked towards her. "You are going into a place of sickness. Of dead wood and blighted steel. Garrick has hidden your body, but your soul is still exposed. The Blight can touch you through the cracks in your fear."

He stopped a foot away. He raised the staff.

"She has a gift for you," Finn said softly. "An enchantment, of sorts. Not of concealment, but of protection."

He looked at her, his expression serious.

"Please stand absolutely still."

Ren, the girl who defied dukes and negotiated with kings, felt a sudden urge to obey. She stood rigid.

Finn tapped the tip of the staff against the center of her chest, right over her heart.

*"Be safe,"* he whispered.

A warmth flooded through the black silk of the suit. It wasn't heat; it was life. A golden leaf pattern, glowing faintly, spread from the point of contact, weaving itself into the fabric of the suit. It pulsed once, syncing with her heartbeat, and then faded from sight.

"What did you do?" Ren breathed, touching her chest.

"A Ward of the Green," Finn explained. "If the Blight tries to touch you... if it tries to corrupt you... the leaf will take the hit. It will burn away to save you."

He stepped back.

"It is a single charge," he warned. "Use it wisely. And Ren?"

"Yeah?"

"Bring the evidence back," Finn said, his voice hardening. "Elian is ready to break things. But he needs a target."

Ren pulled her hood back up, vanishing from sight.

"Consider it delivered," the empty air whispered.

She vaulted over a wall and was gone, a ghost armed with a knife, a suit of shadows, and the blessing of a tree. The Artificer's Guild didn't stand a chance.

This interior monologue is perfect. It adds tension and personality to the infiltration. Even a hardened street operator like Ren has moments of doubt when squeezing into a ventilation duct designed for air, not people. "Suck it up, buttercup" is a great modern-feeling idiom that fits her tough persona perfectly.
