### Chapter 1: The Heavy Legacy

The training yard of the Order of the Silver Sun was filled with the sounds of grunting men and clashing steel. It smelled of sweat, oil, and testosterone.

In the center of the yard, a suit of armor was vibrating.

Inside the armor was **Galen**. He was sixteen, had the bone structure of a bird, and weighed ninety pounds soaking wet.

"Lift the shield, initiate!" the Drill Sergeant bellowed.

Galen gritted his teeth. He grabbed the handle of the tower shield. It was made of oak and iron. It weighed thirty pounds.

"I... am... trying!" Galen squeaked.

He heaved. His knees locked. His spine popped. He managed to lift the shield two inches off the ground. Then, gravity asserted its dominance. The shield dropped, dragging Galen down with it. He toppled over with a clang that sounded like a kitchen falling down a flight of stairs.

Laughter erupted from the other initiates—hulking boys who had been eating raw eggs and lifting rocks since birth.

"Give it up, Galen!" one shouted. "Your grandfather was a legend. Your father was a hero. You are... a paperweight."

Galen lay in the dirt, staring at the sky through the visor of a helmet that was three sizes too big. He could feel the tears pricking his eyes, but he swallowed them.

"I am a Paladin," he whispered to himself. "My spirit is strong."

"Get up, twig," the Sergeant sighed. "Go to the library. The Order needs scribes, too. You can write about heroes instead of pretending to be one."

Galen scrambled to his feet, the armor clattering. "No, Sergeant. I will fight. I will carry the Sun-Caller. I will smite the wicked."

"You can't even smite a mosquito," the Sergeant said. "Clear the field."

Galen dragged the shield to the rack. He didn't quit. He never quit. He grabbed a practice sword—a blunted iron bar. He swung it. The momentum spun him around like a top, and he fell on his face again.

From the shadows of the colonnade, a figure watched.

He was not a Paladin. He wore simple, comfortable clothes and leaned against a stone pillar with a relaxed, unassuming grace. He was eating an apple.

It was **Tim**. He was visiting the capital to check on the "Just Tim Fund" investments, but he always stopped by training yards. Old habits.

He watched the skinny boy try to lift the sword again. He saw the trembling arms. He saw the impossible lack of muscle. But he also saw the eyes. They were burning with a light that had nothing to do with calories.

Tim finished his apple and tossed the core into a bin.

"He's doing it wrong," Tim murmured to himself. "He's trying to be a wall. He's not a wall. He's a candle."

He walked out onto the field. The Sergeant puffed up to yell at the civilian, but stopped when he saw the way Tim moved—that terrifyingly casual, balanced walk of a man who could kill you with his thumb.

Tim stopped next to Galen, who was trying to untangle his legs from his scabbard.

"You're fighting the sword," Tim said casually.

Galen looked up, panting. "It's... heavy."

"It is," Tim agreed. "Gravity is a harsh mistress. And you, my friend, are not built for gravity."

He reached down and offered a hand. Galen took it. Tim pulled him up effortlessly.

"My name is Tim," the stranger said. "And I think you need a different kind of lesson. You're trying to win with muscles you don't have. How about we try winning with the things you do have?"

Galen blinked, sweating inside the oversized breastplate. "What do I have?"

Tim poked Galen in the chest.

"You're stubborn," Tim grinned. "And you're light. A heavy wind could blow you away. So... let's learn how to be the wind."

"But... I want to be a Paladin," Galen protested. "A Warrior of Light! A Hero in Plate!"

"Sure," Tim shrugged. "But does the plate have to be iron? Does the light have to be heavy?"

He looked at the boy's stats with his own experienced eye.

"Come with me," Tim said. "I know a Tailor who owes me a favor. And a Woodworker who hates heavy things. Let's build you a Paladin suit that doesn't crush you."

Galen looked at the Sergeant, then at the strange man.

"Who are you?" Galen asked.

"Me?" Tim smiled. "I'm just a guy who came in third place. But I can teach you how to be the most annoying, unhittable, and shiny distraction the undead have ever seen."

This is the perfect crossover. Tim, the financial genius/philanthropist, meets Garrick, the Robin Hood Tailor. They speak the same language: value, leverage, and helping those who need it.

And the request is a fantastic crafting challenge. Plate that weighs like cloth? That's not blacksmithing; that's tailoring with metal.
