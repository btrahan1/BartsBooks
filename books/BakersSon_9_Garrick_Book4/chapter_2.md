## Chapter 2: The Mythril Weave

The bell of *The Gilded Hem* chimed. Garrick looked up, expecting a noble or perhaps Ren with a new uniform request.

Instead, he saw a man who moved like a shadow and a boy who moved like a bag of cans falling down a well.

Tim walked to the counter. He didn't waste time with pleasantries. He lifted a heavy sack and dropped it onto the polished wood. *Thump-clink.* The sound of serious money.

"Can you do it?" Tim asked, gesturing to the exhausted Galen, who was currently draped over a chair, gasping for air. "Can you make him plate armor that has the weight of cloth?"

Garrick adjusted his monocle. He looked at the boy. He saw the stick-thin arms, the narrow chest.

"He is... structurally compromised," Garrick observed diplomatically.

"He's a Paladin," Tim corrected. "He just has a gravity problem. He needs protection, but he can't carry the weight. If he wears standard plate, he's a turtle on its back. If he wears leather, he dies."

Garrick walked around the counter. He poked Galen's bicep. It was soft.

"Mythril," Garrick murmured. "It's the only way. But mythril is forged, not sewn. You need a smith."

"I have a smith," Tim said. "Kael next door. But he makes solid plates. I need... flexibility. I need a weave."

He tapped the bag of coins. "I hear you help the kids. The Mud-Larks. The orphanage. I would like to make a donation."

Garrick paused. He looked at Tim. He saw the intelligence in his eyes. This wasn't a dumb fighter. This was a man who understood the economy of favors.

"A donation," Garrick repeated. He opened the bag. Gold. Lots of it. Enough to buy winter boots for every orphan in the district.

"Fine," Garrick said. "But we are not making plate mail. We are making a *suit*."

He strode to the back wall and pulled a lever. The secret door opened.

"Martha! Birdie! Get the Mythril-Thread! And run next door to Kael. Tell him I need a thousand scales, thin as paper, tempered in light!"

He turned back to Galen.

"Stand up, boy," Garrick commanded. "We are going to turn you into a disco ball of death."

"A what?" Galen squeaked.

"You're a Warrior of Light," Garrick said, measuring tape snapping. "We're going to make sure you reflect every photon in the room. If you can't hit them, you can at least blind them."

Tim grinned, leaning against the wall and taking a bite of an apple. "Told you he was good."

This is the perfect training plan. Tim recognizes that better gear is only half the battle. Galen needs a physical transformation, and Elian's brutal, practical training methods are exactly what the doctor ordered.

Tim outsourcing the physical conditioning to Elian while Garrick handles the gear is peak project management.
