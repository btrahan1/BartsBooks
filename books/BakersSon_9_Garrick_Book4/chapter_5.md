## Chapter 5: The First Rattle

The crypt was old and smelled of dry earth. It was a known "low-level zone" near the city outskirts, usually cleared by apprentices.

Tim stood by the entrance, leaning against a gravestone. Galen stood ten feet away, shimmering in his Mythril-Weave bodysuit. He held his weapon.

It wasn't a sword. Elian had refused to make a generic sword. It was a **[Sun-Mace]**. The handle was white ash (a cutting from Jasmine's tree), and the head was a complex, interlocking geometric shape made of Iron-Root and silver. It was light, balanced, and hummed with a faint, holy resonance.

"That there," Tim pointed to a shambling pile of bones emerging from the dark, "is a skeleton. The lowest of the undead. They come out of this crypt now and again to rattle their teeth at tourists."

The skeleton saw them. Its eye sockets flared with cold blue light. It raised a rusty scimitar and hissed.

"Take your new staff," Tim instructed. "The one Elian made. It is 'special'... Jasmine said so."

Galen gulped. He gripped the mace. His hands didn't shake. The Iron-Bone Milk held true.

"What do I do?" Galen whispered.

"Don't get hit," Tim said. "You're wearing cloth armor, remember? Magic cloth, but still. If that rusty blade touches you, you get tetanus. Tetanus is bad."

The skeleton charged. It was slow, clumsy.

Galen froze for a second.

"Move!" Tim barked.

Galen moved. He didn't block; he pivoted. He used the "Dance of the Leaf" footwork Valerius had taught the prisoners. He flowed around the clumsy swing.

He was behind the skeleton.

"Hit it!" Tim shouted.

Galen swung the mace. He didn't use brute strength. He used the snap of his hips, the kinetic chain.

*CRACK.*

The mace head connected with the skeleton's spine. It didn't just break the bone; the holy resonance in the wood flared.

*FLASH.*

A burst of white light exploded from the mace. The skeleton didn't crumble; it disintegrated. The bones turned to dust instantly.

Galen stood there, blinking spots from his eyes. He looked at the pile of dust. He looked at his glowing mace.

"Whoa," he breathed.

Tim pushed off the gravestone. He walked over and kicked the dust.

"Not bad," Tim said. "A bit sloppy on the follow-through. You over-rotated."

He clapped Galen on the shoulder.

"But you didn't die. That's a solid C-plus."

He pointed into the darkness of the crypt, where more blue eyes were starting to glow.

"Remember," Tim snickered, echoing his own teacher's voice. "Balance! In more ways than one!"

Galen straightened his spine. He raised his shining mace. He wasn't afraid. He was fed. He was armored. He was trained.

"Bring them on," the Lightweight Paladin said. And for the first time, he sounded heavy.

This is the perfect escalation. Finn isn't just a passive observer; he's the "Dungeon Master" of this training session. He knows Galen needs to be pushed, not just tested. It shows Finn's playful side, and his absolute trust in Tim's training and Elian's gear.
