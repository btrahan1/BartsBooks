# Chapter 2: The Decision

The basement was quiet for exactly three seconds before the storm of speculation broke, but for Mihwa, those three seconds were an eternity. She was still feeling the phantom vibration of the commercial’s music in her fingers, a resonance that seemed to mock the sterile, quiet atmosphere of Bart’s parents’ basement. 

"Okay, I am obviously going to play a Bard," Bart announced, breaking the silence with the subtle grace of a sledgehammer hitting a mirror. He stood up, striking a dramatic pose—one foot on a milk crate full of old magazines, hands on his hips. He looked less like a hero and more like he was auditioning for a low-budget Shakespeare-in-the-park production, but the intensity in his eyes was real. "No questions. I don't care if they suck, I am going Bard. I was born for the stage, and if the stage is a digital fantasy world with near-infinite rendering distance, then so be it."

Mihwa watched him, a small, knowing smile tugging at the corners of her mouth. She had seen this version of Bart a thousand times—the one who refused to let reality get in the way of a good moment. It was one of the reasons she had fallen for him in the first place, back when they were just two kids in the high school drama club trying to make sense of a world that felt too small for both of them. 

"Bards don't suck in fifth edition, Bart," Nathan said, rubbing his chin as he looked at the TV screen with the clinical gaze of a man who had spent too much time reading core rulebooks. "But for a long-term FIVR? You're going to be talking. A lot. Even more than usual. Are you sure your vocal cords are ready for a permanent residency?"

"Exactly!" Bart grinned, his face lighting up with a manic sort of glee. He turned to Mihwa, his expression softening but still packed with that signature mischief. "Babe, are you going to go with a druid? I mean, you’ve already got the 'talking to plants' thing down in our backyard. You’ve spent the last three months trying to convince that fern to stop wilting. In Vitreous, it might actually talk back."

Mihwa laughed, leaning back in her creaky folding chair. She looked at her hands, then at the battle map where her druid miniature stood—a shapely figure carved from green resin, currently surrounded by the plastic bones of the dragon’s previous victims. 

For Mihwa, the idea of Vitreous wasn't just about the game. It was about the escape. She worked as an urban planner for a city that was slowly choking on its own concrete, a job that involved more spreadsheets and zoning disputes than actual "planning." She spent her days staring at gray maps and her nights trying to find a patch of sky that wasn't obscured by the orange glow of streetlights. 

If she could actually *feel* the forest? If she could smell the damp earth of a grove that hadn't been paved over to make room for a strip mall? 

"If I can turn into a wolf and not just imagine the transition?" she said, her voice sounding a little steadier than she felt. "If I can actually touch the spirits of the world? Yeah, Bart. I'm going Druid. I want to see what 'full immersion' really means for someone who has spent her life trying to find a bit of green in a world of gray. And with seven to one time dilation? I could spend an entire month in the woods and only miss four days of real-world spreadsheets."

"We're doing this, then?" Tim asked, his voice low and heavy with the weight of the pragmatism that usually kept the party from doing anything too reckless. He looked around the circle, his eyes lingering on the character sheets that were now effectively obsolete. "We're actually talking about... the permanent residency? We’d have to liquidate everything. The house, the cars, the savings. It’s a one-way trip, guys. There’s no 'log out' button for the residency pods."

"It’s a one-way trip into a world where you don't have to worry about property taxes, health insurance premiums, or the slow, inevitable decline of your lumbar support, Tim," David argued. He was already tapping on his phone, his face illuminated by the blue light of a pre-order page. "I'm in. And I'm going Rogue. If the physics engine is half as good as the investor reports claim, I want to see if I can actually hide in a shadow. Not just roll a d20 and hope the DM is feeling generous. Plus, the seven-to-one factor? That’s basically seven lifetimes in one. It’s an optimizer’s dream."

"Of course you're going Rogue," Nathan said with a snort, though there was a hint of admiration in his voice. "You're the only person I know who tries to 'sneak' into a Starbucks just to see if the baristas are paying attention. If you guys are serious... if we’re doing this as a party... then I’m not staying behind to watch the world end in a cubicle. But I’m done DMing. I've spent three years coordinating your schedules and listening to your whining about encounter difficulty. I want to be the one on the front lines for once. I'm going Paladin. Someone has to keep you lunatics alive."

Mihwa looked at Bart. They had talked about the future before—about weddings, about maybe buying a house together, about the life they were supposed to build in this world. But that future had always felt like a script they were forced to read, a series of milestones that they were expected to hit because that was what people did. 

Vitreous offered a different script. A world where their bond wasn't defined by rent payments, but by the shared survival of a dungeon crawl. 

"A Paladin, a Druid, a Rogue, and a Cleric?" Bart looked at Tim. "What about you, buddy? Don't tell me you're going to stay here and keep working on that spreadsheet for the insurance company. You’re the best healer I know, even if you do complain about the price of diamond dust for Revivify."

Tim looked at the screen, where the image of a massive cathedral made of living glass was being shown. "No. I'm not staying behind. If Nathan is the shield, you're going to need someone to patch the holes in our armor—and our souls. I'll stick with Cleric. But I want a god of life this time. No more of that 'god of transitions' nonsense. I’m tired of being the guy who handles the paperwork for the afterlife."

"Deal," Bart said, clapping his hands together with a sound that seemed to snap the tension out of the room. "The Beautiful Bard and his Merry Band of Miscreants. We’re going to be legends. We’re going to be the people they write songs about in the taverns of Vitreous."

Mihwa reached out and took Bart’s hand. His palm was warm, a solid reminder of the life they were about to leave behind. She thought about her apartment, the one with the leaky faucet and the view of the recycling bins. She thought about her parents, who wouldn't understand why she was choosing a "game" over a career. 

But then she thought about the wolf. The possibility of running through a forest that didn't end at a highway. The possibility of being part of something larger than a bureaucratic machine. 

"We’re going to be broke if we don't start the liquidation process tomorrow," David reminded them, though he was finally smiling. "The residency kits are limited. The company is only opening three servers for the first wave of permanents. If we want to drop in on launch day, we need to move fast."

"Then let's move fast," Bart said, picking up his guitar again. He played a bright, triumphant major chord that filled the basement. "To Vitreous!"

The excitement was infectious, but for Mihwa, it was also bittersweet. She spent the next few hours not focused on the mechanics—though David was already lecturing them on the benefits of 7:1 time dilation—but on the people around her. These were the only things she was taking with her. Everything else—the clothes, the memories of her childhood home, the very physical structure of her body—was being traded for a digital avatar. 

"One thing though," Mihwa said, looking Bart in the eye as the others started discussing the merits of different Elf sub-races. "If you're the leader, you have to promise not to try and seduce every dragon we meet. I want a peaceful forest, Bart, not a harem of fire-breathing lizards."

Bart gasped, clutching his chest with theatrical agony. "Mihwa! I am a professional. I only seduce the ones with hoard-worthy treasure. You know my rates."

The basement filled with laughter, the weight of their momentous decision lightened by the bond of a decade of gaming. Mihwa realized then that Vitreous wasn't just a game to them anymore. It was a commitment. A collective leap of faith into a world where they could finally be the people they had been pretending to be for years. 

As she walked up the stairs to Bart’s parents’ kitchen to get more soda, she looked out the window. The city was a grid of lights, a cage made of sparks. She closed her eyes and tried to imagine the scent of the forest, the feeling of paws on the ground, and the sound of Bart’s music echoing through the trees. 

Tomorrow, they would start giving it all away. But tonight, they were still the heroes of their own small world. A world that was about to turn seven times faster.
