# Chapter 4: The Shadow

David didn't look up from his phone immediately. He was lean, with sharp features and a habit of tapping his foot in a rhythm that only he could hear—a human metronome for a mind that never quite stopped calculating. 

"All right! Dastardly David!" Bart’s voice cut through David’s mental simulation with the subtlety of a brass fanfare. "What kind of rogue do you want to be? Are we talking the 'sneak-thief-who-steals-the-pants-off-a-sleeping-guard' kind, or the 'edgy-masked-assassin-with-too-many-belts' kind?"

David finally looked up, his thumb pausing on the screen. He wore a faint, almost imperceptible smirk—the look of a man who had already seen the end of the conversation before it had even begun. 

"Neither, Bart," David said, his voice as precise as a razor. "Stealing pants is for amateurs, and belts are just additional drag. If the physics engine in Vitreous Online is as granular as the technical white papers suggest, then 'Rogue' isn't just a class. It’s a license to de-map the environment."

Bart blinked, tilting his head. "De-map? David, we're going into a fantasy world, not a server room. Talk to me in Bard-speak. Are you going to be the shadow in the corner or the blade in the back?"

"I'm going to be the error in the equation," David replied, leaning back in his chair. "Look, Nathan is looking at the min-maxing of stats. That's fine for the front lines. But a Rogue? A Rogue’s primary resource isn't Strength or even Dexterity. It's information asymmetry. In a 7:1 immersion, the difference between a successful infiltration and a neural cascade failure is a matter of microseconds and a deep understanding of the occlusion culling."

"Dastardly," Bart muttered, a grin spreading across his face. "See? This is why we call him that. He’s already planning to cheat the game before he’s even made a character."

"It’s not cheating, it's hyper-optimizing," David corrected, though the smirk widened. "If the world is 'Vitreous'—transparent, glass-like—it implies a level of clarity and responsiveness that most games can't handle. If I can understand how the game handles shadows—not as 'dead zones' where the AI ignores you, but as actual ray-traced lighting anomalies—then I can exist in places where the code says I shouldn't be."

He stood up and walked over to Nathan’s whiteboard, taking a red marker and circling the Rogue icon. "I'm going Arcane Trickster, but not for the spells. I want the Mage Hand Legerdemain. If I can manipulate objects at a distance with 7:1 precision, I’m not just picking locks. I’m rearranging the furniture of the world while the enemies are still trying to figure out which direction they're facing. I’m thinking 'The Ghost In The Machine' build."

Mihwa leaned forward, her expression skeptical. "So you’re not even going to fight? You’re just going to... rearrange furniture?"

"In a world where we’re liquidating our lives for permanent residency, Mihwa, the economy is the real boss fight," David said, his tone turning serious. "The three launch servers are going to be a gold rush. The first groups to secure rare resources, trade routes, and high-tier crafting recipes will control the trajectory of the entire digital civilization. I want to be the guy who knows who has what, where they keep it, and how to get it through a three-inch gap in a reinforced iron vault."

"He's going to be a corporate spy in a fantasy world," Nathan said, rubbing his temples. "I should have known. David, please tell me you'll at least use a dagger occasionally to help me keep the goblins off Tim."

"If I've done my job correctly, the goblins won't even know we're in the room until the loot is already gone," David said. "But fine. If a blade is required, it will be a surgical one. No wasted motion. Seven to one dilation means I can turn a one-second opening into a seven-second monologue with my daggers. I’ll be the shadow that moves faster than the light can catch."

Bart laughed, clapping David on the shoulder. "Dastardly David! The man who wants to turn a dungeon crawl into a heist movie. I love it! It fits the 'Merry Band of Miscreants' vibe perfectly. Nathan is the shield, Mihwa is the forest, you’re the silence, and I’m the noise."

"And Tim is the one who keeps the noise from dying," Tim added dryly from the corner.

David looked at the group, and for a fleeting second, the cold calculation in his eyes softened. He was a man who spent his life analyzing risks and rewards, but here, in this basement, he wasn't looking at a spreadsheet. He was looking at the only variables that actually mattered.

"The liquidation starts tomorrow," David said, his voice dropping an octave. "I’ve already outlined a three-phase asset divestment strategy for the group. If we pool our resources into a single 'Residency Fund,' we can bypass the individual processing fees and get a better exchange rate for the initial in-game currency conversion. We can start our lives in Vitreous with a significant head start over the solo players."

"Calculated to the last cent," Bart said, shaking his head in admiration. "David, what would we do without you?"

"You'd probably spend all your launch gold on a really fancy mandolin and starve to death in the first week," David retorted. 

The room filled with the kind of comfortable insults that can only be traded by people who truly trust one another. David felt the familiar hum of a plan coming together—a complex, multi-layered strategy that would carry them across the threshold into a new reality. He didn't care about the 'Aetheria' clones or the generic fantasy tropes. He cared about the mechanics. He cared about the 7:1 ratio. He cared about the fact that for the first time in his life, his ability to see the world as a series of systems would be the very thing that kept his family safe.

"Phase one is the cars," David said, opening a new tab on his phone. "Then the house. We need to be lean, fast, and ready to dive. Because when that clock starts ticking seven times faster, I want us already standing in the shadow of the throne."

Bart raised an imaginary glass. "To the Shadow!"

"To the Shadow," the others echoed. 

David looked at the screen one last time, the image of Vitreous Online glowing like a promise. He didn't just see a game. He saw a landscape of opportunity, a world where the only limit was how much data you could process before the sun went down. And David? David was hungry for the data.
