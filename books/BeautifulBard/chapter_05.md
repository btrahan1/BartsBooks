# Chapter 5: The Pillar

Tim sat at the far end of the table, his fingers tracing the edge of a character sheet that felt like a relic from a past life. Unlike Bart, who flourished in the spotlight, or David, who lived in the data, Tim was the anchor. He was the one who remembered the rations, the one who tracked the light sources, and the one who quietly made sure the party didn't walk into a total party kill (TPK) because they were too busy arguing about the loot.

"Tim. My brother from the same mother!" Bart’s voice was warm, but it carried that edge of older-brother interrogation. He had abandoned his milk crate and was now pacing the basement with the restless energy of a caged lion. "Nathan says you're serious. Dastardly David says the paperwork for your Cleric build is already in the 'Optimized' pile. You are really going Cleric? Are you sure?"

Tim looked up, his expression calm but guarded. "I'm sure, Bart."

"But Tim! You have always LOVED being a mage!" Bart threw his hands up, nearly knocking a stack of rulebooks off a shelf. "You’re the guy who memorizes the fireballs. You're the one who calculates the area of effect for a Thunderwave so it hits the maximum number of goblins without singeing Nathan’s cape. Between me—Barts that can heal, mind you—Mihwa’s druidic magic, and Nathan’s Lay on Hands, don't you feel the Mage calling you? Don't you want to be the one who bends reality until it snaps?"

Tim let out a slow, measured breath. He reached for a soda and took a sip, letting the carbonation settle before he spoke. 

"I love being a Mage, Bart. I love the power, the versatility, and the feeling of having a solution for every problem written in a book. But Vitreous Online isn't a game night where we can just roll up a new character if the healer gets distracted by a shiny treasure chest."

He stood up, his movements less theatrical than Bart’s but filled with a quiet weight. "Seven to one time dilation, Bart. Think about what that actually means. If we dive for permanent residency, we aren't just playing a character. We are living a life. And in that life, there is no 'Reload Last Save' button."

"That's what Nathan is for!" Bart pointed at the Paladin miniature. "He's the shield!"

"Nathan is the shield, yes," Tim agreed, nodding toward their friend who was still staring at the whiteboard. "And David is the shadow. Mihwa is the forest. And you... you’re the soul of this group. You’re the one who keeps us moving. But a soul can’t live in a broken body."

Tim walked over to the TV, which was still showing the looped footage of a crystalline city. "Nathan analyzed the engine. He talked about neural plasticity and physical feedback. But he didn't talk about the strain. If you get hurt in there, and the pain is seven times more persistent than it is here... if you lose a limb, or a friend... that’s not something a Bardic Inspiration can just 'fix.' That’s a trauma that lasts for weeks of subjective time."

He looked at Bart, his eyes reflecting a deep-seated worry that he usually kept hidden. "A Bard can heal, sure. A Druid can patch you up with some leaves and a prayer. A Paladin can give you a boost of divine energy to keep you from falling. but those are secondary skills. They are patches. They are 'in-the-moment' fixes for a party that’s already winning."

"And you want to be the one who fixes us when we're losing?" Bart asked, his voice softening.

"I want to be the Pillar," Tim said. "A dedicated Life Domain Cleric isn't just about hit points, Bart. It’s about the underlying architecture of survival. It’s about being the one person in the group whose only job—whose *entire existence*—is to ensure that the rest of you get to be the heroes you want to be. I want to be the one who handles the paperwork of the afterlife by making sure none of you ever have to fill it out."

Bart looked at his brother, seeing the determination in the tilt of his head. Tim had always been the one to take things more seriously than necessary. When they were kids, Tim was the one who checked the expiration dates on the milk while Bart was trying to see if he could drink the whole gallon in under a minute. 

"You're giving up the Fireballs for us," Bart realized, a rare moment of genuine vulnerability crossing his face. "You're giving up the 'glass cannon' glory to make sure I don't die being an idiot."

"Partly," Tim admitted with a small, crooked smile. "But there’s more to it. In a world lived at 7:1 speed, I want to be the one who holds it all together. I want to be the constant. If everything is moving fast, I want to be the slow, steady heartbeat that keeps you all grounded. Besides, I've spent three years being your brother in the real world. That’s enough training for keeping a high-Charisma idiot alive, don't you think?"

Bart laughed, the tension breaking as he pulled Tim into a one-armed hug. "I guess I can't argue with that logic. But you have to promise me one thing, Tim."

"What's that?"

"Even as a Cleric, you're going to use 'Inflict Wounds' once in a while. I don't want you to be a pacifist. I want a brother who can punch a hole through a zombie's chest with the power of a god."

Tim laughed, a sound that finally seemed to chase the gloom out of the corner he had been inhabiting. "Deal. I'll be the Pillar, but I’ll bring a mace that hits like a meteor. Just to keep the 'glass cannon' spirit alive."

"To the Pillar!" Bart shouted, turning to the rest of the room. 

As the group cheered, Tim felt a sense of relief wash over him. He had made his choice. He was giving up the flashy magic for the heavy armor and the burden of their lives. He was stepping into the role that he had always played, just in a different setting. 

He looked at the commercial one last time. *Vitreous Online*. It was a world of glass, beautiful and fragile. And as the 7:1 clock began to tick, Tim was ready to be the one thing that wouldn't break.
