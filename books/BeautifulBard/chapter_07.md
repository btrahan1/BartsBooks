# Chapter 7: The Line

The Vitreous Online Registration Center North-4 looked less like a government building and more like the aftermath of a miracle. 

It was a sprawling complex of glass and brushed steel surrounded by a sea of humanity that stretched as far as the eye could see. Tens of thousands of people, all clutching their liquidation certificates and medical clearance forms, were huddled in a queue that snaked through the city’s plaza and disappeared around the corner of three separate blocks. The air was a thick soup of nervous chatter, the smell of street food, and the low-frequency hum of thousands of portable fans battling the afternoon heat.

"Where's the sun? It’s 6:00 PM and it feels like we’re standing on the surface of Mercury!" Bart drawled, wiping sweat from his forehead with the back of his hand. He adjusted the strap of his guitar case—the one thing he’d refused to auction off, claiming it was 'narratively essential' for the transition. "I thought you said we were early, Nathan."

Nathan didn’t look up from his tablet. He was pale, his eyes darting across a live data feed of the server latency. "We were early, Bart. We were here at 3:00 PM. The problem is that forty thousand other people were here at 2:00 PM. The system is bottlenecking at the biometric scan."

"We're never going to get in by seven," Mihwa said, her voice small as she looked at the glacial pace of the line. "If we miss the launch window, we might not get into the same starting zone. We could be separated for weeks of subjective time."

"Not happening," David said. 

He was standing a few feet away, leaning against a lamp post with his arms crossed. He wasn't looking at the line. He was looking at the building. Specifically, he was looking at a set of heavy service elevators located near the loading docks on the far west side of the complex. 

"David?" Bart asked, noticing the predatory stillness in his friend’s posture. "You've got that look. The 'I-know-something-you-don't' look. Please tell me you found a teleporter."

"Better," David replied, pushes off the post. He adjusted his collar and checked his watch. "Nathan, what was the name of the subcontractor handling the pod haptics?"

"Neural-Sync Dynamics," Nathan answered automatically. "Why?"

"Look at the loading dock," David pointed. "They're bringing in a shipment of Grade-A cooling fluid. The techs are wearing gray jumpsuits with the NSD logo. And look at the guy at the side entrance. He’s not checking biometrics; he’s checking manifest IDs."

David reached into his back pocket and pulled out a lanyard he’d "borrowed" from his office—a generic security pass that looked official enough if you didn't look too closely. He tucked it into his shirt pocket so only the clip and a sliver of the card were visible. 

"Act natural and follow me," David said, his voice dropping into a tone of quiet, unshakeable authority.

"David, we're going to get arrested," Tim whispered, his face turning a shade of green that matched his future Cleric robes. "This is a Federal Immigration Procedure."

"They're too overwhelmed to arrest anyone, Tim. They just want the line to move," David said, already walking toward the loading dock with a purpose that made the crowds part before him. "Bart, walk behind me. Look like you're bored and important. Mihwa, stay on my left. Nathan, keep that tablet out but look like you're checking a schematic, not a game feed."

Bart grinned, the thrill of the 'heist' sparking in his chest. He straightened his posture, adopting the 'Serious Bart' persona he usually reserved for high-stakes business meetings or particularly difficult auditions. He followed David, his steps echoing the confident stride of a man who belonged exactly where he was going.

As they neared the service entrance, a guard in a tactical vest stepped forward, his hand resting on his belt. "Whoa, whoa. This is a restricted area. The queue is back that way, half a mile."

David didn't even slow down. He held up a hand, palm out, a gesture that commanded a stop. 

"We're the haptic calibration override team for Block J," David said, his voice flat and annoyed, the perfect imitation of a senior engineer who had been awake for forty hours and was currently dealing with a catastrophic failure. "We were supposed to be here four hours ago, but your security detail at Gate 3 didn't have the updated manifests. We have five pods in the VIP suite that are currently throwing a 404 neural-echo error. If those pods aren't calibrated before the 7:00 PM dive, the regional director is going to have a very public, very expensive lawsuit on his hands. Do you want to be the guy who explains why five millionaires had a seizure on launch day?"

The guard blinked, his confidence wavering under the sheer, concentrated 'Dastardly' energy David was radiating. He looked at the clipped pass in David's pocket, then at the group behind him.

Bart stepped forward, giving the guard a look of profound, weary disappointment. "I’m the lead technician," Bart said, his voice booming with a bass-heavy resonance that made the guard flinch. "I have my instruments and my team. Every second we stand here, the neural feedback loop gets tighter. Is your nametag visible? I’d like to know who to put in my report."

"Uh... no, sir. Sorry, sir," the guard said, stepping aside and swiping his card over the reader. The heavy steel door hissed open. "Elevator C will take you straight to the VIP Processing floor. I’ll notify the desk."

"Don't worry about the desk," David said, already stepping through the doorway. "We know the way."

The door clicked shut behind them, cutting off the roar of the crowd. The interior of the center was cool, quiet, and smelled of ozone and expensive cologne.

"Oh my god," Tim exhaled, leaning against the elevator wall as it began to rise. "David, you're a monster. Bart, you're a bigger monster. I think I just committed a felony."

"It’s not a felony if you’re fixing a '404 neural-echo error'," Bart laughed, clapping David on the back. "David, that was beautiful. The regional director? The lawsuit? You’re a natural."

"It’s just data, Bart," David said, though he was clearly enjoying the praise. "The system is built on expectations. I just gave them a more urgent one."

The elevator doors slid open to reveal a plush lounge with minimalist furniture and a row of high-end registration kiosks. There were only a dozen people in the room, all of them looking calm, wealthy, and very, very registered.

A woman in a silk suit looked up from her terminal, her expression one of mild confusion. "The haptic team? I wasn't told—"

"Change of plans," David said, not giving her a chance to finish. "The director wants the internal team to handle the Block J calibration. In the meantime, he’s authorized us to finalize our own biometrics while we wait for the server protocols to refresh. Five-person block, priority Tier 1."

The woman hesitated, her fingers hovering over the keyboard. 

"We're with the *Beautiful Bard* project," Bart added, leaning over the counter with a smile that would have melted a glacier. "It’s a high-profile immersion study. We need a five-person apartment—contiguous units, shared neural-web. We need to stay in sync."

"I... of course," the woman said, her confusion replaced by the desire to be helpful to the 'important' people in front of her. "I have a Five-Pod Executive Suite available in Sector 7. It has a dedicated fiber-uplink and a shared common room for the transition phase."

Bart looked at his friends, his eyes shining with triumph. "Sector 7. That sounds perfect."

Five minutes later, they were holding five silver registration cards. They weren't just players anymore; they were residents. They had bypassed the line, bypassed the heat, and secured a base of operations that most people wouldn't be able to afford after ten years of in-game labor.

As they walked back toward the elevator, Bart stopped and looked at David, then Tim, Nathan, and Mihwa. 

"I've never been prouder," Bart said, his voice thick with genuine emotion. "We haven't even logged in yet, and we’re already winning. We’re going to step into that world together, from the same room, with the best gear and the best team."

"And the best bullshit," Tim added, though he was smiling now as he tucked his silver card into his wallet. 

"The Line is for people who believe the rules are static," Bart said, pressing the 'Down' button. "But we’re the miscreants. We’re the ones who find the gaps. And tonight, at 7:00 PM, we’re going to fill those gaps with music."

Nathan checked the countdown one last time. "Six hours to launch. Let’s get to the apartment. We have pods to prep."

As the elevator descended toward the loading docks, Bart gripped the strap of his guitar case. He could almost hear the music already—the overture to a life lived at 7:1 speed. And with his 'Dastardly' strategist at his side, he knew the ending was going to be legendary.
