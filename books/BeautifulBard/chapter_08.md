# Chapter 8: The Transition

Registration Center Sector 7 didn't feel like a government facility. It felt like the lobby of a hotel that charged by the millisecond and specialized in making the concept of 'material reality' feel gauche. 

The elevators opened onto a corridor of frosted glass and soft, recessed lighting that seemed to pulse in a slow, rhythmic lavender. There was no noise here—no roar of the forty thousand outside, no hum of city traffic. Only the faint, almost musical sound of air filtration systems working at peak efficiency. 

"Wow. This is our new home! It’s beautiful!" Mihwa breathed, her eyes wide as she stepped onto the plush carpet. She brushed a hand against a wall that felt more like silk than stone. "I thought we were going to be in a medical ward."

"VIP Tier 1," David said, leading the way toward the door marked suite 705. He swiped his silver card, and the door slid back with a hushed, pneumatic hiss. "The director doesn't want the elite residents distracted by things like 'hospital smells' or 'visible wires.' They want the transition to be as seamless as possible."

The suite was a minimalist masterpiece. A small common area with a low-slung sofa and a table was surrounded by five individual alcoves. Each alcove contained a Vitreous-brand immersion pod—a sleek, white sarcophagus of carbon-fiber and haptic mesh that looked more like a piece of high-end furniture than a piece of life-support equipment. 

The team immediately spread out to the five pods. There was a frantic, giddy energy as they began the final registration process. Bart climbed onto the edge of his pod, the haptic gel padding shifting beneath him to conform to his body. On the interior lid of the pod, a holographic interface flickered to life, displaying his character sheet, his attribute distribution, and a series of complex medical waivers.

As time passed and everyone was finalizing their picks, the house grew quiet, punctuated only by the soft *boop* and *whir* of the pods syncing with their silver cards. 

"Whoa! Look at this guys. Did you see this?" Nathan’s voice muffled through the shell of his alcove, but the excitement was clear. "There is a spin-up and spin-down sequence in the final immersion protocol. I’m reading the neural-load documentation right now."

"Spin-up?" Bart asked, his finger hovering over the 'Confirm Character' button. "Is that like a loading screen?"

"Not exactly," Nathan said, poking his head out from his pod, his glasses slightly askew. "It’s a phased adjustment for the 7:1 dilation. The system takes a week—subjective time—to 'Spin Up.' Every day during the sequence, it adds +1 to the immersion level. It’s like slowly turning up the volume on reality so your brain doesn't shatter when you hit the full immersion depth."

"And the reverse?" Mihwa asked from two pods down. "It says 'Spin-Down' here too."

"What is this spin-down?!?" Bart asked, glancing at his own interface where a new series of graphs was appearing.

"Every three months," David spoke up, his voice calm and authoritative. He was already lying back in his pod, his eyes tracking a scrolling list of terms of service. "They spin the servers down every three months and we all take a three-day 'evaluation break' in the real world. It’s a mandatory lockout. During that week before the break, the immersion levels subtract by one every day. It brings your brain back to baseline synchronization so you don't forget how to walk or breathe in 'real' gravity."

Bart looked at the graph. A week of slowly waking up into a world seven times more vivid than his own. A week of slowly fading back out into the mundane. In between, twelve weeks of pure, unadulterated existence. 

"That... that makes a lot of sense," Bart said, a slow grin spreading across his face. "And in a way, it’s pretty awesome! It’s like an intermission. A chance to grab a snack and compare notes before the next performance starts."

"It’s a safety valve," Nathan corrected, though he was nodding. "Without it, the psychological drift would be irreversible within a year. The 'Spin-Down' ensures that 'Serious Bart' and 'Bard Bart' remain the same guy."

"I like the 'Spin-Up' best," Mihwa said, her voice sounding dreamy. "A week of watching the world get more real every day. Watching the trees get greener, the wind get warmer. It’s like a long sunrise."

"And a week of sunset every ninety days," David added. "It’s a cycle, people. Just like the markets. We use the breaks to divest any assets we’ve managed to route through the external exchanges and make sure our physical bodies haven't withered into husks."

Tim sat on the edge of his pod, looking at the glowing silver card in his hand. "Seven to one. Three months. That’s nearly two years of life in every cycle. We’re going to be in there for two years before we even see the sun in this room again."

The weight of that realization sat heavy in the room. Two years of adventures. Two years of songs. Two years of keeping one another alive. 

Bart stood up and walked to the center of the common area. He looked at each of them—his brother, his partner, his friends. They were the architects of this new life. They were the ones who had found the back door, the ones who had cheated the line, and the ones who were about to become the noise in a silent world.

"Six hours to registration lock," Bart said, his voice ringing with a newfound clarity. "But for us, the timer has already started. We’re in the suite. We’re in the pods. The overture is over."

He walked back to his pod and lay down, the haptic mesh wrapping around him like a familiar embrace. He looked at the 18 Charisma glowing on his screen—the 'God-tier' start that would be his shield.

"See you on the other side of the spin-up," Bart said. 

"See you there," Nathan replied.

"Stay in the light," Tim muttered.

"Don't get lost," Mihwa whispered.

David didn't say anything. He just tapped a command on his screen, and his pod lid slid shut with a soft, final click.

Bart reached out and pressed the final 'Commit' button. The lid of his pod began to descend, the lavender light fading into a deep, comforting indigo. He felt a faint prickle at the base of his neck—the neural interface making its first, tentative contact.

*Commencing Spin-Up: Day 1 of 7.*
*Immersion Level: 1/10.*
*Welcome to Vitreous Online, Bart.*

The world didn't vanish. It just shifted. The sound of the room faded, replaced by the distant, rhythmic thrum of a heartbeat that wasn't his own. The smell of ozone was replaced by the faint, earthy scent of damp grass and woodsmoke.

Bart closed his eyes and let the resonance take him. The performance had finally begun.
