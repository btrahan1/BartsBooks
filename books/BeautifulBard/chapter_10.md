# Chapter 10: The Party Sheet

The misty courtyard of the Ascension Hall was silent, save for the rhythmic *clack* of David’s practice daggers against the wooden dummies. The Level 1 immersion was still layering in, making the air feel a bit like chilled water, but the group was already beginning to find their rhythm.

Tim, now Timothy Healz, was the first to stop his experimentation. He’d been staring at a translucent blue pane of light that only he seemed to be able to manipulate. His fingers flickered through the air, dragging and dropping icons that looked like ancient vellum scrolls.

"Okay, I've come up with a character party sheet," Tim said, his voice carrying the professional, slightly tired weight of a man who had spent his entire career in accounting and his entire life being the group's conscience. "I've been digging through the shared neural-links while you guys were testing the physics. I think it works like this."

The rest of the group gathered around him. Tim flicked his wrist, and a large, semi-transparent table projected into the air between them. It was a grid of numbers and labels, the raw data of their existence in Vitreous Online.

"We all started with 10 as the base for every stat," Tim explained, pointing to the column headers. "We know Bart min-maxed to get his 18 Charisma. He sacrificed his physicals down to 8s and 9s to bank those points. But the rest of us... we stayed at the 10 baseline during the pre-dive allocation."

"But the table shows higher numbers for most of us," Nathan—The Protector—observed, his massive frame casting a long shadow over the glowing projection. 

"The class bonuses," Tim nodded. "I found the fundamental modifiers in the system logs. Paladins get a flat +1 to Strength and +1 to Wisdom. That puts you at 11s, Nathan. Clerics—that’s me—get a flat +2 to Wisdom. Druids get +1 to Dexterity and +1 to Wisdom. And Rogues get a pure +2 to Dexterity."

"Optimizer’s dream," David—Dastardly Dave—muttered, a small, cold smile touching his lips. "Six weeks for a +2 to a primary attribute in most games would take a thousand hours of grinding. Here, it’s a day one gift."

"It gets more interesting when you look at the derived stats," Tim continued, his finger highlighting the HP and MP columns. "The engine uses a 10-point multiplier. We all get 10 health per point of Constitution. And we all get 10 mana per point of Wisdom."

"Wait," Bart said, squinting at the line marked *Beautiful Bart*. "My mana is 180. But my Wisdom is an 8. If the rule is 10 per Wisdom, I should be sitting at 80."

"That’s your 'Charismatic Casting' talent at work," Tim said, looking at him with a mix of wonder and concern. "The system recognized the talent during the final sync. It switched your Mana pool to a Charisma-based scaling. You get 10 mana per point of Charisma instead of Wisdom. Since you’re at 18, you have almost double the mana pool of anyone else in the party."

"Eighteen mana pools," Bart whispered, his grin widening. "I’m going to be able to talk until the servers die."

"You’re also the only one here with 80 health," Tim reminded him, his voice returning to its 'Pillar' tone. "Nathan and the rest of us are at a solid 100. You’re a glass cannon, Bart. A very loud, very magnetic glass cannon. One bad roll on a Constitution save and you’re a smear on the marble."

"Worth it," Bart declared, throwing his arms out wide. "I’m not here to be durable. I’m here to be undeniable."

"Look at Dave," Mihwa said, pointing to the Rogue's line. "12 Dexterity at Level 1. He’s already hitting the soft-cap for the local training zone's agility checks."

"And look at Nathan's Strength," Tim added. "11 STR gives him the baseline needed to wield the heavy practice mace without the 'Novice Penalty.' If we had stayed at 10s across the board, we’d be struggling with the first-tier gear."

Bart looked at the table, the numbers feeling more real than the stone beneath his feet. It was a map of their strengths, and more importantly, their dependencies. 

- **Beautiful Bart:** 80 HP, 180 MP (18 CHA)
- **The Protector:** 100 HP, 110 MP (11 STR, 11 WIS)
- **Timothy Healz:** 100 HP, 120 MP (12 WIS)
- **Mihwa:** 100 HP, 110 MP (11 DEX, 11 WIS)
- **Dastardly Dave:** 100 HP, 100 MP (12 DEX)

"This is our reality for the next subjective week," Nathan said, his voice deep and solemn. "Day 1 of Spin-Up. We have these stats, we have these handles, and we have a 30% physics drag. I suggest we put these numbers to work. I saw a group of Level 1 'Training Echoes' in the orchard. I think it’s time Timothy Healz tested his first heal, and I tested my first block."

"And I," Bart said, his voice ringing with a quality that made the translucent party sheet ripple like water, "am going to test if 18 Charisma is enough to convince a training echo that it really doesn't want to hit a Paladin. Maybe I can talk it into a existential crisis before Nathan even swings."

Nathan let out a booming laugh that shook the air in the hall. At Level 1 immersion, the acoustics were still a bit flat, but his presence was undeniable. "I’d like to see that, Bart. But if the crisis doesn't take, I’ll be there with the mace. Tim, keep those prayers ready. We’re at 100 health, but a 'watercolor' punch can still leave a digital bruise."

"I'm on it," Tim said, his eyes already glowing with a faint, silvery light as he began to practice the gestures for his basic heal. "I can feel the MP pool. It’s like a cool reservoir at the back of my mind. It’s... intoxicating, honestly. I’ve never played a healer that felt this 'connected'."

"Six days of this," Mihwa said, her voice filled with a mix of anticipation and awe. She was watching the mist in the courtyard, where the shapes of the 'Training Echoes'—shimmering, semi-transparent warriors—were beginning to solidify. "Six days of learning how to be these people. Six days of the sunrise."

"And then," David added, his practice daggers vanishing into his belt with a flicker of movement that seemed too fast for Level 1, "we find out if the 'Beautiful' build survives the first real sunset."

Bart smiled, a wide, confident expression that felt perfectly natural in this body. He stepped through the light of the party sheet, his hand resting on the hilt of the ornamental blade at his hip. He didn't need a sword to win. He had the noise. 

He followed them out into the gardens, his heart beating with the slow, steady rhythm of the 7:1 world. They were no longer just five friends in a living room in North-4. They were a party. They were a legend in the making. And as the Level 1 sun began its long, slow ascent over the misty courtyard, Bart knew one thing for certain.

The stats were only the beginning. The performance was everything.
