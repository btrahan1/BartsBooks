# Chapter 11: Training Echoes

The orchard beyond the Ascension Hall was a masterpiece of restraint. It wasn't a garden in the traditional sense; it was a simulation of growth, a landscape of silvery, semi-transparent trees that looked like they were made of spun moonlight. The grass beneath their feet didn't rustle; it gave off a faint, digital chime with every step, a sensory placeholder for a sound the system would fully render in a later stage of Spin-Up.

"All right, our weapons are lame, our armor practically non-existent, but I'm pretty sure we can't die here," Bart said, his voice carrying with that unnerving Charisma-boosted resonance. He was looking at his own gear—a simple linen tunic and a pair of leather boots that felt like they were made of reinforced paper. "That would be downright embarrassing! Imagine the headlines: 'Beautiful Bard Dies in Tutorial to Passive Orchard Echo'."

He reached back and pulled his starting instrument from the strap on his shoulder. It was a lute, but ‘lame’ was an understatement. The wood looked like reclaimed plywood, and the strings were a dull, nylon-like material that didn't look like they could hold a tune, let alone a magical vibration.

"Look at this lute," Bart muttered, plucking a string. The sound was flat, a Level 1 metallic *thrum*. "I’ve seen better instruments in a toddler’s toy box. But hey, it’s a start."

"Better than a wooden stick," Nathan—The Protector—said, though he was currently holding a sword that looked like it had been carved out of a single piece of gray plastic. He had a shield strapped to his arm—the 'board' to his 'sword'—that was similarly lackluster, lacking any heraldry or ornamentation. "At least they gave me the basics. Sword and board. Very traditional."

David—Dastardly Dave—was already testing the balance of his practice daggers, his movements a blur of dark leather and cold steel. He didn't look bothered by the quality of the gear. To him, it was just a set of variables to be optimized.

"Let's go with Formation 1," Bart commanded, his tone shifting from jocular to authoritative. "We’ve practiced this in a hundred tabletop sessions, but this is the real dive. At least they gave our Protector a sword and board. You're up front, Nate. Do your thing. Be the mountain."

Nathan nodded, his massive form stepping into the light of the orchard. "Formation 1. I’ll hold the aggro. Just remember the drag."

"Dave, you slip in behind for the action you like," Bart continued, pointing a finger toward the shimmering figures of the Training Echoes that were beginning to manifest near a cluster of silver birches. "Wait for the flank. Don't pull the threat too early."

"Understood," David said, his voice already sounding distant as he began to circle the perimeter of the orchard, his silhouette blending into the morning mist.

"Mihwa, not sure what you have yet," Bart said, looking at her. She was holding a gnarled wooden staff that looked like it had been found in a forest fire. "But either put a HoT on Nate, or a damage shield, or root one of the mobs. Give the big guy some breathing room."

Mihwa closed her eyes, her fingers tracing a pattern in the air. "I have 'Verdant Pulse'. It’s a slow-burn heal. I’ll keep it on Nathan once the Echoes engage."

"And Tim," Bart said, turning to his brother. "I won't insult our healer by telling you what to do. You’re the Pillar. Keep us from the 'digital dead-end', yeah?"

Timothy Healz—Tim—just gave a small, weary nod. He was already holding his holy symbol, a simple iron disc, and his eyes were tracking the HP bars that floated in the periphery of his vision. "Just stay in range, Bart. Even an 18 Charisma won't save you if you’re a mile away when the 'watercolor' punch lands."

"And me?" Bart grinned, his fingers dancing over the plywood lute. "I'm gonna play some sick toons on this lute thing I started with. If I’m the noise in the silence, I might as well make it loud."

The Training Echoes—four semi-transparent warriors with glowing blue swords—began to move. Their steps were synchronized, their eyes fixed on the party with a cold, algorithmic intent. They moved with the same 30% drag that the party was feeling, a slow-motion dance of watercolor death.

"Engagement in five," Nathan called out, his shield raised. "Four. Three. Two. One."

The first Echo lunged, its blue sword whistling through the mist. Nathan met it with his shield, the impact producing a hollow, digital *thud*. The force of the blow pushed him back a step, but he didn't break. He swung his plastic-looking sword in a wide arc, catching the Echo across its chest and sending a spray of blue particles into the air.

"And now, for the overture!" Bart shouted.

He struck the first chord of a battle hymn he’d been humming in his head since David first mentioned *Vitreous Online*. It was a driving, rhythmic melody, a song of defiance and momentum. Despite the plywood construction of the lute, the sound that erupted from it was staggering. 

Thanks to the *Charismatic Casting* talent and his 18 Charisma, the music didn't just carry; it pushed. 

A wave of indigo energy rippled out from Bart, washing over the party. He felt his mana pool—that massive 180 reservior—begin to drain at a steady, rhythmic rate.

*Skill Unleashed: Battle Hymn of the Miscreant.*
*Effect: +2 Physical Damage, +5% Movement Speed.*

"Whoa!" Nathan yelled, his sword suddenly glowing with a faint indigo light. His movements became a fraction sharper, a fraction more certain. He slammed his shield into the second Echo, the impact sending it stumbling back into the mist.

David capitalized instantly. He appeared from the shadows behind the staggered Echo, his practice daggers flickering in the pale light. He delivered a flurry of strikes—point, point, point—that carved deep grooves into the Echo’s digital form. 

"Action received," David muttered, his eyes cold and focused.

Mihwa pointed her staff at Nathan. "Verdant Pulse, active!" A soft green glow began to emanate from the Paladin’s feet, stitching together the fraying edges of his tunic where the blue blade had caught him.

Bart kept playing, his fingers a blur over the strings. He wasn't just playing a song; he was conducting a battle. He could feel the connection between them, the neural-link that Tim had pointed out. Every note he struck felt like it was reinforcing the bonds of the party, making Nathan stronger, David faster, and Tim more focused.

"Timothy, eyes up!" Bart shouted over the music. "Another one’s coming in from the left!"

Tim didn't even look. He just extended a hand, and a pulse of silvery light caught Nathan just as a third Echo slammed into his flank. The Paladin’s HP bar, which had dipped for a fraction of a second, snapped back to full.

"Timothy Healz, ladies and gentlemen!" Bart cheered, never missing a beat. "The man who keeps the show going!"

The battle was a slow, deliberate affair, dictated by the Level 1 immersion lock. Every swing had a lead time, every movement a recovery phase. It felt like fighting in a dream, or under ten feet of water. But with Bart’s battle hymn providing the tempo, the party found a synchronization that the Training Echoes couldn't match.

The Echoes were predictable. They were icons of an older age of gaming, limited by the very culling algorithms Nathan had praised. They followed patterns that the miscreants—veterans of a thousand tabletop encounters—could see coming a mile away.

"Final swell!" Bart shouted, striking a powerful, resonant chord. 

The Indigo energy intensified, creating a brief, blinding flash. David delivered a final, devastating blow to the last Echo, causing it to shatter into a million blue pixels that faded into the mist.

The silence that followed was profound. The orchard returned to its quiet, watercolor state. The only sound was the heavy, rhythmic breathing of the group as they leaned on their lame weapons, their hearts finally starting to slow down.

"Formation 1 success," Nathan said, wiping digital sweat from his brow. He looked at his shield, which now had a shallow dent in the center. "Man, even at Level 1, that felt... real. I could feel the impact through the haptic mesh."

"And the music," Mihwa said, looking at Bart with a new kind of wonder. "I could feel it in my chest, Bart. It wasn't just a sound. It was like... a pressure. A good one."

"That was 110% Beautiful Bart," David said, clean his daggers on his tunic. "The mana scaling on that hymn is aggressive, though. You burnt through 40 points in a three-minute engagement."

Bart checked his HUD. 140/180. "Worth every drop. If I can make Nate move that fast at Level 1, imagine what I can do when the immersion hits 10."

"We’re going to be dangerous," Tim said, his voice quiet but certain. He looked at the silver card he’d tucked into his belt. "We’re going to be very dangerous."

Bart slung the plywood lute back over his shoulder and grinned. He looked at the misty exit to the orchard, where the first quests of the starter zone were waiting. 

"Our weapons are still lame," Bart said, stepping forward. "And our armor is still nonexistent. But the noise? The noise is just fine."

"Formation 1?" Nathan asked, already lining up at the lead.

"Formation 1," Bart confirmed. "Let's go show Server One what a real orchestra looks like."

As they walked out of the orchard, the mist began to thin, revealing the first glimpses of a world that was becoming more real with every passing subjective second. And Bart, looking at his friends, knew that the 'Serious Bart' and the 'Beautiful Bart' were finally, completely, in sync.

The performance had only just begun.
