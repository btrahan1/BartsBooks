# Chapter 12: The XP Math

The silver orchard was quiet again, the blue pixels of the Training Echoes having long since dissolved into the morning mist. Bart wiped his brow—though there was no actual sweat yet, just the digital suggestion of exertion—and slung the plywood lute across his back.

"All right, what did we get from that XP-wise?" Bart asked, a wide, expectant grin on his face. "Do we get anything against these training echo-echo-echo-things? Or was that just for the 'sick toons' practice?"

Nathan laughed, leaning on his plastic-looking sword. "I hope there’s something. My shield looks like it went through a blender, and that was just Level 1."

Tim, who had been staring off into the middle distance at his translucent HUD, finally blinked and looked over. "I've found the logs," he said, his voice regaining that analytical edge. "The 'Event History' is recording the data. Those Echoes were tutorial units—basically calibration targets—but they do have a fixed value."

He flicked his wrist, and a new window projected into the air. 

*Combat Log Update:*
*Target: Training Echo (x4)*
*Total Base Experience: 200 XP*

"Fifty a piece," David muttered, leaning against a silver birch. "Lame. In most games, you’d be Level 3 by now if you cleared a tutorial pack that fast."

"Wait, look at the final tally," Tim said, pointing to the bottom of the screen.

*Party Modifier Applied: Vitreous Cohesion Bonus (1.2x)*
*Total Adjusted XP: 240 XP*
*Shared Experience: 48 XP per member.*

"A cohesion bonus?" Mihwa asked, her eyes brightening. "Because we used Formation 1?"

"Exactly," Tim said. "The engine rewards coordination. It looks like a full party of five gets a 20% multiplier to the total XP pool before it splits. It’s a mechanism to stop people from just soloing the starter zones and out-leveling the content. They want us in parties."

"So, 48 XP toward... what?" Bart asked, crossing his arms. "What do the exp tables look like? Did you find that, Tim? I'm guessing 1k exp to level 2, but I'm not sure what these guys set up for us. I hope it’s not some legendary grind."

Tim swiped through another set of menus. "Found it. It’s a 10-point scale for the early tiers. You’re right, Bart. Level 2 requires exactly 1,000 XP. Level 3 is 3,000. Level 4 is 6,000."

"It’s exponential," Nathan observed, his brow furrowing as he did the math in his head. "If the mobs stay at 50 XP, we’re going to need to kill about twenty packs like that just to hit Level 2."

"But the Echoes are the bottom of the barrel," Tim reminded them. "The system says the 'Starter Zone proper'—the world beyond the gate—has mobs with threat ratings. Level 1 wolves, basic skeletons. Those should be worth considerably more."

"And the party penalty?" Bart pressed. "Usually, being in a group means you level slower because the XP is split. But here, the Cohesion Bonus is almost half-covering the split?"

"It’s not perfect," Tim explained. "In a solo run, if you kill a 100 XP wolf, you get 100 XP. In our group, we kill that same wolf, we get 120 XP thanks to the 1.2 multiplier, but we each only walk away with 24. So yes, it’s slower than soloing. But..."

"But soloing at 7:1 immersion is suicide," David finished the sentence, looking at Nathan’s dented shield. "You saw how those Echoes focused the point-man. An 8-CON Bard soloing a pack of Level 1 wolves would be back in the Ascension Hall in thirty seconds. We’re splitting the XP, but we’re multiplying the survival rate."

"And the speed," Bart added. "I can buff four people just as easily as one. If we move fast enough, we more than make up for the 5-way split. It’s about throughput."

"Wait," Mihwa said, her hand hovering over a new icon on her HUD. "Look at the 'Spin-Up calibration bonus'. Since it's Day 1, the system is giving us a flat 20% boost to *all* sensory gains. It’s making our bodies adjust to the stats faster."

"God-tier throughput," Bart grinned. "Forty-eight XP down, nine hundred and fifty-two to go. At this rate, we’ll be Level 2 by the end of Day 3 of Spin-Up."

"And we haven't even seen the town yet," Nathan said, looking toward the large, iron-wrought gate at the end of the orchard. "The Protector is ready. Timothy Healz, how’s your mana?"

"Sitting at 115/120," Tim said. "Mana regen in Level 1 immersion is slow, though. About 1 point every two minutes subjective. We need to be careful with the spamming."

"And our Beautiful Bard?" David asked, looking at Bart.

"140/180," Bart said, checking his own stats. "And I have a Battle Hymn that’s itching for a second verse. Let's get out of this watercolor tutorial. I want to see something with actual textures."

As they walked toward the gate, the 30% drag felt a little less heavy. Maybe they were adjusting, or maybe the Cohesion Bonus was more than just a number on a screen. They were moving as a unit, their reflections in the silver mist overlapping and merging.

They reached the gate—the boundary between the Ascension Hall and the world of *Vitreous Online*. The iron bars were cold and solid, the first thing in the game that didn't feel like a painting.

"Ready?" Nathan asked, his hand on the latch.

"Ready," they said in unison.

The gate creaked open, the sound echoing through the orchard with a raw, mechanical groan. On the other side was a path of winding cobblestone, leading down a hill toward a village of thatched roofs and stone chimneys. It was stillwatercolor, still Level 1, but the scale was immense.

The first subjective day of their new lives was just beginning. And with 48 XP in their pockets and a Cohesion Bonus in their souls, the miscreants of Server One were finally moving beyond the gate.

"Formation 1," Bart whispered, his hand resting on his lute. "Let's go find some wolves."

"And some actual gear," David added, looking at his plastic daggers. "I’m going to lose my mind if I have to backstab another training dummy."

They stepped through the threshhold, the names 'The Protector', 'Timothy Healz', 'Mihwa', 'Dastardly Dave', and 'Beautiful Bart' floating above their heads as they descended toward their first true quest. 

The math was simple. The legend was not.
