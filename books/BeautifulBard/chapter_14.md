# Chapter 14: Oakhaven Outreach

The road to Oakhaven was a gentle downhill slope, the silver cobblestones reflecting the pale, early-morning light of the Spin-Up. The adrenaline from the wolf ambush was still buzzing in their neural links, but the 30% physics drag made the walk feel deliberate, almost meditative.

Bart took the lead, his plywood lute slung back, but he wasn't silent. He began to hum—a low, eerie, yet strangely comforting melody. It didn't have the driving force of the Battle Hymn or the soft, watery resonance of the Recovery Hymn. It was something else—a steady, rhythmic vibration that seemed to harmonize with the very air around them.

Tim, who had been obsessively checking his HUD to monitor the party's slow mana regeneration, suddenly blinked. He stopped walking, his finger hovering over a line of data.

"Wait a second," Tim said, his voice reflecting a mix of confusion and scientific curiosity. "Bart, why is my mana going up? We’re out of combat. The passive regen should be 1 point every two minutes at Level 1 immersion."

Bart stopped humming just long enough to glance over his shoulder, a mischievous glint in his eyes. "That's my Mana Chant, Tim. Hehehehe."

"Mana Chant?" David asked, appearing from the mist like a wraith. "I saw your mana dipping during the walk, but yours is going up too. How are you maintaining a chant while the Recovery Hymn is still on its ten-second cooldown?"

"That's the beauty of it," Bart said, resuming the eerie hum. "I can actually do that while twisting. Muahahahah. It’s a vocalization, not a chord progression on the instrument. As long as I keep the tune in my head and the vibration in my throat, it stacks."

Tim squinted at the live log. "And I don't know if you saw that it's 3 per tick instead of 1. Wait, let me check the math... Six-second intervals. Bart, that’s triple the standard tutorial scaling."

"Eighteen Charisma, baby!" Bart cheered, his voice booming through the watercolor forest. "If you check the logs from the wolf fight, the health from the Recovery Hymn was probably 3 per tick as well. Higher Charisma doesn't just make the songs louder; it makes them more efficient. The engine is rewarding the 'force of personality' by multiplying the output per resonance."

"Triple scaling at Level 1," Nathan—The Protector—muttered, shaking his head. "If you hit Level 10 and keep pumping Charisma, you’re going to be a walking fountain of youth. You’ll be able to keep a whole raid group topped off just by humming."

"I intend to be a walking tsunami of youth," Bart corrected, grinning. "But for now, I’m just making sure we don't arrive in Oakhaven looking like we crawled out of a gutter. Look."

He pointed down the road. The mist was finally lifting, revealing the village of Oakhaven. It wasn't a grand city—yet—but it was solid. The thatched roofs were woven with the same silver-barked wood of the forest, and the stone chimneys were sending up plumes of white smoke that looked like clouds of cotton candy in the Level 1 render.

"Actual textures," David observed, his eyes narrowing as he spotted the first NPCs moving near the village gates. "Look at the gate guard. That’s at least 4K resolution on the gambeson."

They reached the gates, the iron hinges creaking as they approached. A guard in a simple, unadorned breastplate looked up from a clipboard. He was a 'Tutorial Guide' NPC, designed to funnel players into the first few quests.

"Welcome to Oakhaven, travelers," the guard said, his voice sounding a little too scripted, a little too perfect. "Are you here for registration or—"

"We’re here for the best room in the house," Bart interrupted, stepping forward. He didn't just walk; he performed. He adjusted his plywood lute, cleared his throat, and let his 18 Charisma do the heavy lifting before he even spoke a word of intent. 

The guard blinked, his scripted response faltering. He looked at Bart, really *looked* at him, his AI processing the massive social weight of the player in front of him. 

"The... the best room?" the guard stammered. "The village inn is quite modest, sir. It’s mostly for passing merchants."

"Modest is a state of mind," Bart said, leaning against the gatepost with an easy, practiced grace. "And we are anything but passing. We are the architects of Oakhaven’s future. My friend here," he pointed to David, "is a master of logistics. This mountain of a man," he patted Nathan’s shield, "is your new insurance policy. And our healer? He’s the reason your village won't need a cemetery for the next six weeks."

Tim gave a small, embarrassed wave.

"Now," Bart continued, his voice dropping to a warm, conspiratorial tone. "I’m sure a man of your discernment knows that a group of this caliber deserves more than a straw pallet in the common room. Perhaps a private suite? Or the top floor of the tavern? On the 'Legendary Future Discount'?"

The guard checked his clipboard, then looked back at Bart, his AI clearly struggling with the 18 Charisma stat. Usually, players at Level 1 had a 10 social rating, which meant the NPCs gave them basic, perfunctory service. Bart was hitting him with 'Epic-Tier' social pressure before they’d even finished the first subjective hour of the game.

"I... I suppose I could speak to the Innkeeper," the guard said, his tone shifting from bored to eager. "Master Thorne is usually quite strict, but for a group of your... quality... he might be willing to make an exception. Mention that Gerrick at the gate sent you."

"Gerrick," Bart smiled, the expression so genuine and radiant that it probably taxed the system’s facial-mapping servers. "I knew you were a man of vision. We’ll be sure to tell the Bards of the future about your hospitality."

Bart led the group through the gates, his steps light and confident. 

"Social engineering," David whispered, a small, impressed smirk on his face. "You just talked our way into a private room using a 10-second conversation and a smile."

"It’s not just a smile, Dave," Bart said, looking around the village square. There were other players now—mostly soloists or groups of two—walking around in their basic gray tunics, looking lost and overwhelmed by the watercolor world. "It’s 18 Charisma. In *Vitreous Online*, if you sound like a hero, the engine treats you like one. Now, find me the tavern. I want to see if Thorne is as 'strict' as Gerrick says."

They found the *Thirsty Oak* Tavern at the center of the square. It was a sturdy building of stone and timber, with a sign that featured a massive, weeping willow dripping with what looked like golden ale. 

As they stepped inside, the atmosphere changed. The Level 1 immersion was deeper here, the air smelling of roasted meat and sawdust. A heavy-set man with a scarred cheek was wiping down the bar. He looked up, his eyes hard and suspicious. This was Thorne.

"We don't want no trouble," Thorne grunted. "Standard beds are 5 silver. No discounts, no bartering."

Bart didn't even flinch. He walked straight to the bar, unslung his plywood lute, and placed it gently on the wood. He didn't say a word for three seconds, letting the silence—and his aura—fill the room.

"Thorne," Bart finally said, his voice rich and melodic. "Master Gerrick at the gate said you were a man of business. I see he was right. Because a man of business knows that the first group to clear the Oakhaven Orchard in ten minutes flat is a group that’s going to be bringing a lot of gold back to this tavern."

Thorne stopped wiping the bar. He looked at Bart’s 18-Charisma-boosted expression, then at the rest of the group—the massive Paladin, the shadow-like Rogue, the calm Cleric, and the Druid with the glowing staff.

"Ten minutes?" Thorne asked, his voice losing some of its edge. "The Orchard Echoes? Most new blood takes an hour just to calibrate."

"Calibration is for people who don't have a rhythm," Bart said, plucking a single, perfect note on his lute. Even the plywood sounded like crystal. "We have the rhythm. And we want the suite. Three silver a night, plus a tab for the house ale. We’ll pay you back in wolf hides and reputation. Within a week, the *Thirsty Oak* will be the most famous tavern on Server One."

Thorne stared at Bart for a long moment, the 18 Charisma stat working its magic on his AI logic. The cost-benefit analysis was leaning heavily toward the 'Beautiful' side of the equation.

"Suite on the third floor," Thorne finally grunted, tossing a heavy brass key onto the bar. "Three silver. But if you don't bring those hides by sunset, you’re sleeping in the stables. And Gerrick gets a week of cleaning duties for sending me 'architects' instead of gold."

"Deal," Bart said, catching the key. He turned to the group and winked. "Formation 1 is for combat, guys. Formation 2 is for the bill. Everyone, to the suite. We’ve got a long week of Spin-Up ahead, and I plan on doing it in style."

As they climbed the stairs, Tim looked at Bart, a faint smile on his face. "You really are the loudest thing in this game, Bart."

"Loudest and most comfortable," Bart said, unlocking the door to a room that looked surprisingly real, complete with five feathered beds and a window overlooking the village square. "Welcome to Oakhaven, miscreants. Let's see what else we can get for a smile and a song."

The first day was far from over. And Beautiful Bart was just getting warmed up.
