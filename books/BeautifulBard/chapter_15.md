# Chapter 15: The Local Legend

The *Thirsty Oak* was more than just a tavern; it was the heart of Oakhaven. That evening, the common room was a chaotic tapestry of sounds and smells. The scent of roasted pork and stale ale mingled with the sawdust on the floor, and the air was thick with the chatter of NPCs and players alike. Most of the players were hunched over their tables, staring at their HUDs and whispering about quest rewards or the sluggishness of the Level 1 immersion.

Bart sat with the group at a corner table, his plywood lute resting against his chair. They were eating a meal of dense brown bread and a stew that tasted surprisingly like actual beef, though at Level 1, the texture was still a bit like soft rubber.

"The room on the third floor is perfect," Nathan said, taking a long pull from a wooden flagon of ale. "Gerricks wasn't lying. It's the best strategic position in the village. We can see the gate, the square, and the stables from that window."

"And the beds are feathered," Tim added, looking more relaxed than Bart had seen him since the game launch. "I think the 'Neural Feedback' from that wolf fight is finally starting to clear. My MP is at 100%."

"Ours too," David said, glancing at Bart. "The Mana Chant worked. We’re going into tomorrow with a full tank."

Bart didn't say anything for a moment. He was watching the other players. He saw a group of three in the center of the room arguing over a map, their voices rising in frustration. He saw a soloist sitting by the hearth, looking at his plastic-looking daggers with a localized expression of despair.

"This world is too quiet," Bart muttered, his voice carrying that unnerving Charisma-boosted resonance.

"What do you mean?" Mihwa asked, reaching out to touch his hand. "It’s a tavern. It’s loud."

"It’s noise," Bart corrected, standing up. "It’s not music. Everyone here is treating *Vitreous Online* like a spreadsheet with a haptic interface. They’re optimization-engines wearing gray tunics."

He walked to the center of the common room, his movements drawing the eyes of everyone in the room. He didn't just walk; he commanded the space. Without saying a word to Thorne, who was busy behind the bar, Bart grabbed the edge of a heavy oak table and dragged it two feet to the left. He grabbed a second table and slid it into place next to the first, creating a makeshift stage in the dead center of the tavern.

"Bart, what are you doing?" Nathan whispered, half-rising from his seat.

Bart didn't answer. He simply sat in the center of the joined tables, crossing his legs and pulling his plywood lute into his lap. He didn't activate his Battle Hymn. He didn't start the eerie vibration of the Mana Chant. 

He just played.

The first few notes were tentative, a series of bright, clear plucks that sliced through the tavern’s chatter like a hot knife through butter. It was a well-known tune from 'upside'—a melody of nostalgia and hope that every player in the room recognized. 

The chatter died instantly. 

Thorne stopped half-way through pouring a pint. The group of arguing players at the map table froze. Even the NPC servers—AI scripts designed for routine—slowed their movements as the engine’s audio-processing algorithms struggled to categorize the sound. It wasn't a 'skill'. It wasn't a 'buff'. It was just... music.

Bart’s 18 Charisma was working in a way the developers probably hadn't anticipated. It wasn't just scaling the damage of his songs; it was scaling the *impact* of his performance. Every note he struck felt like it was reverberating in the bones of everyone in the room, cutting through the watercolor fog of Level 1 and making the world feel, for a fleeting moment, like Level 10.

After a few minutes of pure instrumental, Bart leaned in and started humming. It was a soft, wordless accompaniment to the lute, a melody of home and the sun and the reality they’d left behind.

Mihwa watched him from the corner table. She saw the way the light from the hearth caught the edges of his plywood lute, turning the cheap wood into something that looked like polished mahogany. She saw the way the players around the room were leaning in, their eyes wide, their HUDs forgotten.

She stood up.

She didn't wait for an invitation. She walked to the makeshift stage and stood next to him. Mihwa wasn't a Bard; she was a Druid. She didn't have the 'Charismatic Casting' talent or the 18-point social aura. But she had a voice that had always been the grounding force in Bart’s life.

As Bart hit the first verse of the chorus, Mihwa started in on the words.

*“Where the silver rivers run, beneath the dying of the sun…”*

Her voice was a perfect counterpoint to his lute—warm, earthy, and filled with a raw sincerity that the game’s audio filters almost couldn't handle. They duo'd the chorus, their voices blending into a harmony that felt like a physical weight in the tavern.

The gathered crowd was awestruck. 

This wasn't 'Vitreous Online'. This wasn't a quest or a grind. This was a piece of the world beyond the pods, brought into the digital heart of Oakhaven. The players weren't looking at their stats anymore. They were looking at each other. They were looking at Bart and Mihwa.

Even Thorne, who had been grunting about 'architects' an hour ago, was leaning against the bar with a look of profound, silent respect.

Bart closed his eyes, his fingers a blur over the plywood strings. In this moment, he wasn't the 'miscreant' or the 'optimizer'. He was what he’d always been: a performer. And as the final note faded into the rafters of the *Thirsty Oak*, the silence that followed was more powerful than any battle hymn he could have ever played.

For a long, breathless moment, no one moved. Then, the tavern erupted. 

It wasn't just clapping; it was a roar of approval that shook the silver-barked walls. Players stood on their chairs. The NPC guide, Gerrick, who had wandered in for a drink, was cheering with a volume that almost seemed a violation of his script.

Bart stood up, slinging the lute back over his shoulder. He looked at Mihwa and winked, then looked at the crowd.

"The Beautiful Bard has arrived," he said, his voice carrying the calm authority of a man who knew exactly what he was worth. "And if you think that was good, wait 'til we hit Level 2."

He stepped off the tables, Mihwa following close behind. As they walked back to their corner table, players were reaching out to touch their tunics, to offer gold, or just to say 'thank you'.

"You did it," Nathan said, his voice almost a whisper as they sat back down. "You just turned this tavern into our territory."

"It’s not about territory, Nate," Bart said, taking a bite of his stew, which now tasted remarkably like a five-star meal. "It’s about making the world worth living in. If we’re going to be here for three months at a time, I don't plan on doing it in silence."

"Reputation gain: +500 with Oakhaven," Tim said, his eyes scanning a new notification on his HUD. "And there’s a new quest notification from Thorne. He wants us to play at the Summer Solstice festival next week."

"Next week?" David asked. "In real time or game time?"

"Game time," Tim said. "Which is about twenty-four hours from now."

Bart grinned, his 18 Charisma shining brighter than the hearth fire. "Tell him we’re available. But the stage is going to need to be bigger. And I want an instrument that isn't made of plywood."

"We’ve established the legend," Mihwa said, leaning her head on his shoulder. "Now comes the hard part."

"The hard part?" Bart laughed. "Mihwa, the hard part was getting Gerricks to let us in the gate. Everything from here is just the encore."

Oakhaven had its Bards before, perhaps. But it had never seen anything like this. And Bart, looking at the stunned faces of the players around him, knew that the 'noise' of *Vitreous Online* was finally starting to sound like a symphony.

The performance had only just begun.
