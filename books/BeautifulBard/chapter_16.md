# Chapter 16: Phase 2 Sync

The sun that rose over Oakhaven the next morning felt... different. It wasn't just higher in the sky; it was brighter. The pale, watercolor blue of the previous day had deepened into a rich cerulean, and the silver-barked trees of the village square were casting shadows that actually looked like shadows, rather than soft gray gradients.

Bart and the group were sitting at their corner table in the *Thirsty Oak*, nursing flagons of lukewarm cider and a plate of fried eggs that finally had the texture of something that had once been inside a shell.

"Formation 2 success," Bart said, tapping the brass room key against the wood of the table. "I checked the tab. Thorne already credited us with a 'Celebrity Deposit'. We’ve got a week of credit before we even have to start thinking about wolf hides."

"It’s not just the credit," David said, his eyes scanning the other players in the room. There were more of them now, but most looked tired, their tunics rumpled and their spirits flagging after a night of tutorial grinding. "It’s the reputation. People are already whispering about the 'Bard and the Druid' who played last night. We’ve carved out a niche."

"And the 7:1 life is starting to feel real," Tim added, checking his HUD. "Subjective time: Day 2. Real time: Hour 24 of the launch."

Suddenly, a sound erupted from the rafters. It wasn't a voice, or music; it was a rhythmic, metallic chime that seemed to resonate through the very floorboards of the tavern. It sounded like an emergency broadcast system, but with a refined, digital clarity that made the watercolor air vibrate.

*BONG. BONG. BONG.*

"What is that?" Mihwa asked, her hand instinctively reaching for her staff.

The chatter in the tavern died instantly. Even Thorne stopped wiping the bar, his head tilted toward the ceiling.

Then, a voice filled the room. It was deep, calm, and utterly authoritative—an AI broadcast that carried the weight of a god.

**"Attention, Citizens of Server One. The initial registration window for Vitreous Online has now reached its conclusion."**

"It’s closing," Nathan whispered, his grip tightening on his flagon. "The first wave is locked in."

**"Registration for this server is now closed for a period of no less than three months IRL. Total confirmed player population: 47,370."**

"Forty-seven thousand," David muttered, doing the math. "That’s a small city. All of us locked in the same haptic cage for the next two years of subjective life."

**"Time Dilation Phase 2 speed-up commencing in sixty seconds. Calibration protocols are shifting from Level One to Level Two."**

"Level Two," Bart said, leaning back in his chair. "He told us this would happen. The 'Watercolor' is about to get a face-lift."

**"Please find a comfortable place to sit or recline for the next few minutes. Sensory synchronization may cause minor equilibrium shifts as the haptic mesh deepens. Welcome to the second day of your new reality."**

"Sixty seconds," Nathan said, already moving to lean back against the wall. "Everyone, sit down. If the physics drag drops and the resolution spikes at the same time, it’s going to feel like the floor just disappeared."

They sat in silence, watching the digital clock in the corner of their vision count down. around them, other players were scrambled to find chairs or leaning against the bar. The NPCs, meanwhile, seemed unaffected, their AI simply pausing in a 'wait' state.

*10... 9... 8...*

"Hold on to your hats," Bart grinned, though his heart was thumping a rhythmic, digital beat in his chest.

*3... 2... 1...*

*Commencing Phase 2 Sync.*

The shift wasn't a sound or a light. It was a *pressure*. 

It felt like a heavy blanket had been lifted off Bart's brain. Suddenly, the 30% physics drag—that thick, underwater feeling that had defined every step—snapped. It didn't disappear, but it lightened to 25%. The 'watercolor' edges of the tavern sharpened with a violent, geometric precision. 

The sawdust on the floor was no longer a yellow blur; he could see individual grains. The wood of the table under his hands suddenly had *grain*—rough, splintery, and wonderfully real. The smell of the roasted pork from the kitchen didn't just drift; it expanded, the complexity of the scent doubling in a single subjective second.

"Whoa," Mihwa gasped, her hand flying to her mouth. "Everything... I can see the dust motes in the light."

"Texture resolution just quadrupled," David whispered, his fingers tracing the edge of his daggers. "And the frame rate... it’s like my eyes just got upgraded to 8K."

Bart stood up, and for the first time, he didn't feel like he was moving through molasses. He felt... agile. Graceful. The 18 Charisma wasn't just a stat anymore; it was an energy he could feel radiating from his skin. 

He looked at his plywood lute. It still looked like plywood, but the grain of the wood was now visible—the imperfections, the knots, the way the nylon strings caught the light. It wasn't a toy anymore. It was an instrument.

"The drag is down," Nathan said, standing up and testing the weight of his shield. "It’s still there, but it’s... manageable. I can move."

"And look at the population," Tim said, pointing to the HUD. The '47,370' was glowing in gold now. "We’re the elite. The first wave. Everyone who didn't make the cut is stuck upside for the next three months."

"That means the resources are fixed," David observed, his strategist brain already churning. "The mobs, the loot, the village expansion. We know exactly how many competitors we have."

Bart walked to the window of the suite—ignoring the stairs as he moved with a new, fluid speed—and looked out over Oakhaven. 

The village was a revelation. In Level 2 immersion, Oakhaven looked like a real place. The thatch was textured, the stone chimneys were weathered, and the sky was a deep, vibrant blue that felt like it had actual depth. The 'Watercolor' was gone, replaced by a world that was becoming more real with every passing second.

"The Summer Solstice is coming," Bart said, his voice carrying through the room with a new, crystal-clear resonance. "And with Phase 2 active, that festival isn't just going to be a quest. It’s going to be the first major event of Server One."

"We need to get to Level 2," Tim reminded them. "If the world just got harder to render, the mobs are going to be harder to kill."

"Ninety-two XP each," Bart said, checking his HUD. "We need 832 more to hit the level cap. And with Phase 2 physics, I bet I can twist three hymns if I really push it."

"Triple twisting at Level 2?" Nathan laughed. "Bart, you’re going to blow a fuse in those pods."

"Let them blow," Bart grinned, slinging his newly-textured lute over his shoulder. "I’ve got a Mana Chant that can handle the heat."

The announcement had ended, but the world was still humming with the energy of the shift. The miscreants of Server One stood together in the morning light, no longer just players in a tutorial, but citizens of a reality that was finally, truly, waking up.

"Formation 1?" Nathan asked, already heading for the door.

"Formation 1," Bart confirmed. "Let's go show Day 2 what we can do."

The first SUBJECTIVE week was only just hitting its stride. And Beautiful Bart was ready for the next layer of the symphony.
