# Chapter 27: The Tavern Initiative

The Oakhaven Arms was the beating, pixelated heart of the settlement. On Day 2 of the global launch, the tavern was a chaotic mess of sweaty players, NPCs complaining about the price of ale, and the persistent, background hum of a thousand private messages. But as Bart’s Beautiful Band crossed the threshold, the volume dropped.

Their 'Pioneer' titles were still glowing. Their gear, though they’d just left it for repairs, was still visually distinctive—Bart’s Star-Field Lute catching the lantern light with a shimmer that looked like a captured galaxy.

"Keep your heads down," Bart whispered, leading them to a private booth in the back, far from the central hearth. "We don't want to be 'holding court' just yet."

They slid into the oversized wooden seats, the haptic feedback of the worn grain feeling unusually comforting after the jagged quartz of the Silver Gate. Mihwa exhaled a long, shaky breath, her HUD showing her 'Mental Fatigue' bar slowly cooling down.

"So," Nathan said, leaning over the table. "You have a plan. And considering that grin on your face, it’s not just about a better room for the night."

Bart looked at his friends—his 'Band'. He saw the exhaustion in Tim’s eyes, the cautious excitement in David’s, and the mock-dread on Mihwa’s. He reached into his inventory and pulled out a spectral scroll, unrolling it on the table.

"A guild?" Tim blurted out, reading the header. "On Day 2? You want to form a guild already!"

"Absolutely," Bart said, his 19 Charisma giving his voice a resonance that made the nearby NPCs glance their way. "I wanted to wait until next week, but the World First titles got me thinking. The system has a hidden reward tier. I’d bet this lute that the 'World First Guild' achievement comes with something the rest of the server won't see for months."

"Like what?" David asked.

"A Guild House Token," Bart whispered. "Permanent property in Oakhaven. Or better, a modular construction permit. While everyone else is sleeping in taverns and pay-to-play inns, we could have a headquarters with private storage, high-fidelity crafting stations, and a place to plan without fifty players watching our every move."

"Bart, look at the bottom of the form," Tim said, pointing to a red exclamation mark. "Requirement: 100 signatures from registered entities. One hundred. We’re a party of five. Even if we recruited everyone in this tavern, we’d barely hit thirty."

Nathan nodded. "Getting a hundred players organized on Day 2 or 3 of a global launch isn't just hard, it’s impossible. Most people don't even know how to use the invite system yet."

Bart just smiled, the starlight from his lute pulsing in rhythm with his amusement. "You're right, Nate. It’s not meant to be completed this early. The developers intended this to be a mid-to-late game milestone. They want people to build social networks over weeks of gameplay."

He paused, leaning in closer, his voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper. "But... who said the signatures have to be *only* players?"

Tim looked at his older brother in shock. He’d spent his life studying the rigid logic of game engines, but Bart... Bart saw the gaps where the logic met the fiction. 

"Wait," Tim said, his brain already racing through the server's database parameters. "The form says 'registered entities'. That’s... that’s the technical term for every persistent object in the world's database."

"Exactly," Bart grinned evilly. "In *Vitreous Online*, the NPCs aren't just background art. They're registered denizens. They have unique IDs, persistent memory, and—crucially—they have the agency to sign legal documents within the game's simulation."

"Oh, that's devious!" Tim exclaimed, his moral compass struggling against his admiration for the exploit. "You’re going to use the NPCs to fill the quota!"

"Think about it," Bart continued. "We have the 'Pioneer' title. We have the 'World First' reputation. And," he tapped his temple, "I have nineteen points of Charisma. To the NPCs of Oakhaven, I’m not just a player. I’m a celebrity. A hero who just saved their orchard Keeper from... whatever it was. If I ask the captain of the guard, the village elders, and the head of the merchant's guild to sign my charter... do you think they’ll say no?"

Mihwa leaned back, a genuine laugh escaping her. "You’re going to build a 'Player Guild' mostly made of NPCs."

"For now," Bart said. "Once the house is built and the token is used, we can phase in actual players as we find them. But being the *first* gives us the house. It gives us the territory. And most importantly, it gives us the narrative control of Oakhaven."

"We’d be the Oakhaven Pioneers," Nathan realized. "The actual authorities of the zone."

"Precisely," Bart said. "So, here’s the play. Tomorrow morning, while our gear is being finished by the smith, we split up. David, you use your stealth to find the 'reputation' NPCs—the ones with the silver borders. Tim, you verify the 'registered entity' ID for every signature we get to make sure we’re not wasting ink. Mihwa, Nathan, you use your Druid and Paladin status to get the local nature and religious leaders on board."

"And you?" Mihwa asked.

"I," Bart said, standing up and slinging the galactic lute, "am going to go find the Mayor. I have a feeling the 'Beautiful Bard' and the 'World First' announcement have already done most of the heavy lifting. I just need to close the deal."

He looked at the tavern, which was now vibrating with the news of the party's presence. He could see players whispering, pointing, and trying to get a look at their HUDs.

"Let's get some rest," Bart said. "Day 3 starts at sunrise. By sunset, I want a key to our own front door."

"This can't be good," Mihwa grinned, but she reached out and put her hand over the guild scroll. "But I'm in. Let's go shopping for signatures!"

Tim just shook his head, a mixture of disbelief and pride on his face. "Brother, if the developers ever find out you did this... they're going to patch the world just for you."

"Then I’d better get the house before the update," Bart winked.

*Guild Status: DRAFTED.*
*Signatures: 5 / 100.*
*Target: THE MAYOR OF OAKHAVEN.*

The 'Tavern Initiative' was officially underway. To the rest of the server, Bart was a lucky player with a good lute. To Bart, he was the architect of a new digital empire. And the foundation was going to be built on silver ink and high-reputation AI.
