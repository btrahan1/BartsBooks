# Chapter 35: The Solstice Gala Begins

The northern hill of Oakhaven no longer looked like a part of the starter zone. It looked like a tear in the sky, a place where the high-fidelity future of *Vitreous Online* had bled into the watercolor present. 

The Pioneer's Hall was draped in indigo silk that shimmered with a constant, digital pearl-essence. Thousands of silver-bark lanterns, hung with surgical precision by the NPC workforce, created a path of light that stretched from the settlement gates all the way to the peak. And at the center of the marble courtyard stood the Stage.

It was an honest-to-God stage. Carved from heart-oak and reinforced with obsidian filigree, it stood six feet high, surrounded by a ring of "Crystal-Sharp" flame-jets that threw no heat but bathed everything in a cool, silver glow.

Bart stood in the center of that stage, staring out at the horizon. "That's an honest to God stage! Wow!"

His voice, usually so resonant and full of effortless "Social Gravity," had a tiny, presque-noticeable quiver in it. 

Tim walked up the steps, clapping his brother on the back with a haptic thud that momentarily disrupted Bart’s celebrity aura. "Don't tell me, that YOU, of all people, are nervous?"

Bart spun around, his indigo-lined cloak swirling in a series of high-definition folds. "What? Me? ME? Nervous?"

He struck a pose, his hand resting on the Star-Field Lute, but the "Aura of Presence" flickered for a millisecond before stabilizing.

Tim raised an eyebrow, his Level 3 Intelligence HUD highlighting the slight dilation in Bart’s digital pupils. "Bart. You’re rubbing your hands together. You only do that when you’re either about to perform a miracle or about to throw up."

Bart let out a huff, his shoulders slumping just an inch. "Uhm, maybe just a little?"

Mihwa came over from the sound-check pedestal, her emerald-silk robes rustling with a sharpness that indicated she was fully sync-calibrated. She wrapped her arms around Bart from behind, hugging him tightly. 

"Nothing to be nervous about, sweetheart," she whispered, her voice warm and grounding. "It's just an audience of 57,000, give or take, right?"

"Fifty-seven... thousand?" Bart choked.

"Well," Tim said, checking the server’s real-time density map. "Forty-seven thousand players on Server One, plus the ten thousand registered NPCs in the reach. Since every single one of them received a mass-invite with a 'High-Reputation' tag... yeah. Most of them are currently on the move toward this hill."

Bart looked down the slope. Even from this height, he could see the torches. Hundreds—no, thousands—of tiny fires were snaking their way up the path. The watercolor forest was being overwhelmed by a sea of player-tags and NPC names.

"This is what Eve meant," Bart whispered, his fingers brushing the filaments of his lute. "A focal point. We’ve turned the entire server toward this hall."

"And you're the main attraction," David said, appearing from the shadows of the obsidian forge. He was wearing his new Masterwork Obsidian armor, looking like a literal piece of the night. "The 'World First' pioneers. The 'Beautiful' band. They’re coming to see if the legends are true."

"No pressure," Nathan added, standing at the edge of the stage with his obsidian-edge shield. "But I’ve stayed at the gate for twenty minutes. There are groups from the 'Iron Fist' guild and 'The Silver Seekers' in the crowd. They aren't here for the elderberry wine, Bart. They're here to see you fail."

Bart took a deep breath. He looked at the stage, then at his party—the team that had gritted their teeth through the Silver Gate and fought through the Alpha Woods to hit Level 3 in record time. 

The "Cooling Effect" of a system entity’s presence suddenly brushed past him. He didn't see Eve, but he felt the warp—the tiny silver tear she had left behind.

"Mihwa," Bart said, his voice finding its resonance again.

"Yes?"

"Check the lanterns. I want them at 110% brightness. If we're going to give them a show, I want them to see it from the other side of the forest."

Mihwa grinned, her eyes glowing with Druidic power. "Consider it done."

"Tim, David—keep an eye on those rival guilds. If they try to disrupt the 'Social Gravity' of the performance, I want them neutralized before they can even draw a weapon."

"Got it," David whispered, vanishing once more.

Bart turned back to the audience—the growing sea of lights at the bottom of the hill. He adjusted the Star-Field Lute, the starlight filaments beginning to hum with the 19-Charisma force of his will.

"Nervous?" Bart said, his half-insane, half-ingenious smile finally locking into place. "Maybe. But you know what they say in the theater, Tim."

"What’s that?"

"The bigger the crowd, the bigger the miracle."

He struck a single, ringing chord. It wasn't the "Summons" from the night before. This was the "Overture"—a sound so bright and sharp it momentarily cleared the watercolor fog for five miles in every direction.

*EVENT START: THE SUMMER SOLSTICE GALA.*
*NPC REPUTATION MULTIPLIER: X2.*
*PLAYER SYNC FEEDBACK: CALIBRATING...*

The first tier of guests—hundreds of players dressed in their best early-game gear, followed by a confused but enthusiastic Mayor Weston—reached the courtyard. They stopped, their eyes widening as they saw the Level 3 pioneers standing on a Phase 3 stage, bathed in the silver light of a hall that shouldn't have existed. 

Bart stepped to the edge of the stage, the Star-Field Lute projecting a nebula of starlight that reached all the way to the roof. 

"Welcome!" Bart’s voice boomed, carrying the full weight of his Level 2 Sync formation. "Welcome to the Pioneer's Hall! Welcome to the first day of the rest of your lives!"

The crowd erupted. The "Beautiful Bard" was back, and he was about to play for 57,000 people. 

The Summer Solstice Gala had officially begun.

*Gala Status: KICKOFF.*
*Attendance: 4,200 (Arrivals continuing).*
*Social Gravity: PEAK.*
*Current Objective: THE OPENING PERFORMANCE.*

Bart looked at Mihwa, then at Tim, and then back at the sea of faces. The nerves were gone, replaced by a white-hot focused adrenaline. He had the world’s attention. Now, he just had to keep it.
