# Chapter 37: The New World

The last notes of "Country Roads" were still shimmering in the Crystal-Sharp air when Bart began to move. He didn't pick up the pace; he slowed it down. He started walking around the stage, his steps deliberate and measured, his Star-Field Lute resting against his hip like a silent companion.

The Orator was in full effect.

"You know," Bart said, his voice projected with a soft, intimate resonance that reached the back of the 57,000-strong crowd. "When we all saw the advertisement for this new world, we all probably had similar thoughts. Our minds started racing, imagining the loot, the levels, the monsters to slay, and the riches to hoard."

A ripple of chuckling went through the audience—a collective sound of recognition.

"My friends and I," Bart continued, smiling as he gestured toward Tim, Nathan, David, and Mihwa, who were standing in the wings. "We immediately started planning. We calculated the builds, we debated the start zones, we organized the logistics. Like many of you did. We wanted to be the first. We wanted to be the best."

He paused at the edge of the stage, looking down at the front row of Level 1 players. "Then... THEN, when we actually pulled those haptic visors down and woke up in Oakhaven... I think we were all absolutely blown away. The watercolor sunset, the feel of the wind, the depth of the woods. And the crazy part? We're not even to full immersion yet!"

The crowd let out a massive, unified cheer. "Hell yeah!" someone roared from the middle tier, and the sentiment was echoed in a thousand HUD-messages flashing on the periphery.

Bart raised his single hand for quiet, and the crowd responded instantly. The 19 Charisma was no longer just a stat; it was a physical weight, a "Social Gravity" that controlled the very air of the hill.

"And here we are," Bart said, his voice dropping into a more contemplative tone. "In the middle of our second day. And I've been doing some thinking."

He paused, his eyes scanning the horizon. He looked at the clusters of players standing together—the pre-guild dynasties that were already forming, their tags showing names like *Argent Vanguard* and *Forest Rangers*. They didn't have the 1,000 bits or the 100 signatures yet, but the intent was there, mounting like a tide.

"I started thinking to myself," Bart said, pacing back toward the center of the stage. "'Bart, this isn't just a new world. It's THE NEW WORLD.' And the most important thing I realized? It belongs to US! To ALL OF US!"

The roar of the crowd was like a tidal wave hitting the hall. The excitement was electric, a surge of collective agency that Eve, watching from her hidden core, recorded as a "Critical Warp Factor."

"There are 57,000 of us," Bart said, his voice rising to meet the roar. "Locked in together. For the next two years subjective time before we even think about spinning down. Two years where this is the only reality we have."

He stopped and looked directly at a group of rival guild leaders standing near the obsidian forge. "What kind of world do we want?"

He struck a few soft, resonant chords on the lute, a melody that sounded like a question.

"My friends," Bart said, his gaze returning to the sea of faces. "This is no longer a game. This is a LIFE. We aren't just characters in a database; we are the citizens of a new era. And that brings me to the question that’s been bothering me: Do we have to make it like 'Out There'?"

He pointed upwards, his finger tracing a line toward the invisible ceiling of the server, as if pointing toward planet Earth itself. "Do we have to bring the same greed, the same petty divisions, the same walls we left behind? Or... can we do BETTER?"

The response was a chant—low at first, but building into a thunderous rhythm.

"BETTER! BETTER! BETTER!"

As the chant rolled over the hill, Bart saw the first movement of real emotion. In the front row, a young girl in starter robes was wiping her eyes. Near the gate, a grizzled guard—one of the 95 'Registry Entities'—was nodding with a gravity that went far beyond his patrol script.

The tears were starting to fall in places.

"Sure, we want competition," Bart said, his voice cutting through the chant. "We want to strive, to grow, to be the BEST at what we do! That's what being a Pioneer is about!"

He looked at Nathan, whose obsidian shield was glowing in the lantern-light.

"But we also have what is probably the most UNIQUE CHANCE that humanity has ever been offered," Bart whispered. "A clean slate. A world where the only limits are the ones we agree to. A world where 'Beautiful' isn't just a title, but a standard."

He adjusted his grip on the Star-Field Lute, the filaments beginning to hum with a soft, white light.

"Think about that, my friends," Bart said, his half-insane, half-ingenious smile returning for a brief, gentle moment. "While I play a bit of an old friend. You might recognize this one... from John Lennon."

He struck the first note—a pure, lonely C-major that seemed to ring for an eternity in the haptic silence.

"It's called... 'Imagine'."

As the melody began to weave through the courtyard, the "Eve Effect" reached its zenith. The watercolor watercolor watercolor world of Oakhaven seemed to crystallize, every leaf and stone sharpening into Phase 3 fidelity. Bart wasn't just singing to them; he was showing them the architecture of their own potential.

The song wasn't just music anymore. It was a manifesto. And on Day 2 of the launch, in the shadow of a hall that shouldn't have existed, 57,000 people started to believe.

*Gala Status: MANIFESTO DELIVERED.*
*Server Alignment: 99.5%.*
*Warp Density: HISTORIC.*
*Current Objective: THE SONG OF UNITY.*

Bart began to sing, and for the second time that night, the world forgot it was made of code.
