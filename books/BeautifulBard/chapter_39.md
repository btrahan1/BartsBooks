# Chapter 39: The Devil Comes to Vitreous

The intermission had done its job. The 57,000 souls on the hill were no longer just a collection of confused players and scripted NPCs; they were a community, buzzed on elderberry wine and the promise of a "New World." The conversations about guilds, trades, and non-aggression pacts were still humming in the air when a familiar, electric vibration rippled through the courtyard.

Bart was retaking the stage.

He move was subtle at first—just a shadow sliding back up the heart-oak steps—but as soon as his indigo cloak caught the silver flame-jets, the "Social Gravity" of the peak snapped back into place. Player conversations died mid-sentence. NPCs turned their heads in perfect, high-fidelity synchronization.

"Is everyone having a good time?" Bart called out, leaning into the Star-Field Lute.

A loud, thunderous "YES!" erupted from the front tier, rolling down the hill like a physical force.

Bart cupped his single hand to his ear, his 19-Charisma grin splitting his face. "I'm sorry, I CAN'T HEAR YOU!"

The resulting scream was so intense it actually caused a temporary "Server-Stress Warning" to flicker in Tim’s HUD. The fifty-seven thousand people roared with an abandon that nearly broke the haptic limit of the zone. The sound was a literal wall of noise, a declaration of presence that silenced the forest for miles.

"Ah," Bart said, his voice dropping back into a comfortable, amplified purr. "That's better!"

He walked to the center of the stage and sat on the edge of the obsidian-filigreed pedestal, strumming his guitar with a casual, almost absentminded grace. He looked out at the sea of faces, his expression musing, almost vulnerable.

"You know," Bart and, "As you all know, I'm just a humble bard."

He paused, the timing perfect. A ripple of snickers and laughter went through the crowd. "Humble" was the last word anyone would use to describe the man who had just materialised a guild hall and unified a server on Day 2.

"No, seriously!" Bart said, his eyes twinkling in the silver light. "I'm no world builder. I’m no grand strategist. I don't have a map with pins in it or a spreadsheet for tax rates."

He looked at Tim, who was currently holding a literal haptic spreadsheet in his hand. More laughter.

"I just like to play a good song, every now and then," Bart said, his voice dropping into a conspiratorial whisper. "Like... this one!"

He didn't just play; he leapt.

Bart exploded from the pedestal, striking a chord so sharp and hot it felt like a flash of lightning. The Star-Field Lute’s filaments didn't just hum; they screamed, the starlight turning into a fiery, golden orange.

"Maybe a few of you have heard of it!" Bart roared, his strings beginning a fast, aggressive bluegrass rhythm that defied the "watercolor" physics of the zone. "It's called... 'The Devil Comes to Vitreous'!"

*SKILL ACTIVATE: BARDIC SHRED (RANK 3 - OVERWHELM).*
*AOE RADIUS: CRITICAL.*

"A tune about a fiddler," Bart yelled over the rising intensity of the music, "who likes to play it HOT!"

The performance was a transformation. Gone was the soulful, philosophical orator who had sung "Imagine." In his place was a musical demon, his fingers moving with a Phase 4 speed that caused "Motion Blur" artifacts to ripple around his hands. 

The song caught the crowd like a wildfire. It was a Vitreous-specific adaptation of a classic, a tale of a system error trying to steal the souls of Oakhaven and being beaten back by a lowly musician with a starlight lute.

*“The Devil came to Vitreous, he was looking for a bug,*
*He was in a bind 'cause he was way behind, and he was looking for a plug!*
*He came across this hill of ours, and he saw a Bard in blue,*
*And he said, ‘Boy, let me tell you what... I’ve got some code for you!’”*

The crowd didn't just cheer; they stomped. Tens of thousands of haptic-boots hit the marble in time with the beat, the vibration so powerful it started a "Seismic Sync Event" that registered on the global server map.

Bart was shredding. He wasn't just playing chords; he was playing *Vitreous Online* itself. Every high note sent a flare of golden data-packets into the sky. Every low growl of the strings caused the silver lanterns to flare with an orange heat. 

The NPCs in the crowd—the 95 signatories—were the most affected. They weren't just singing anymore; they were dancing. Mayor Weston was doing a high-kicking jig with Oakhaven’s Head Guard, their AI scripts completely overwritten by the "Social Gravity" of the performance. They were "Registered Entities" serving as the rhythm section for the world's most dangerous bard.

*"Fire on the Mountain! Run, boy, run!*
*"The Devil's in the Hall with a system-gun!*
*"Mihwa's in the kitchen picking out dough!*
*"Granny, does your haptic-feedback bite? No, child, no!"*

The intensity peaked. Bart was a blur of indigo and gold in the center of the stage, his 19 Charisma projecting a "Zone of Influence" that made every person in the 57,000-strong audience feel like they were the one he was shredding for. 

Tim watched the Mana-Chant bar. Bart was burning through points at a rate that should have bottomed him out in seconds, but the "Social Feedback" from the crowd was pumping Mana back into him just as fast. It was an infinite loop of charisma and energy, a self-sustaining social explosion.

The final crescendo was a sonic boom that literally cleared the clouds from Oakhaven’s sky, revealing the star-field that gave the lute its name.

Bart held the final note, his hand raised high, the lute vibrating with a frequency that made the obsidian forge hum. He was panting, his cloak smoking slightly from the "Visual Fidelity" surcharge he had put on the engine.

The silence that followed lasted for exactly one second. Then the hill exploded in a roar that was loud enough to be heard in the starter zones of Server 2.

"Humble," indeed.

*Gala Status: PERFORMANCE PEAK.*
*Server Excitement: OVERFLOW.*
*NPC Agency: SENTIENT-ADJACENT.*
*Current Objective: THE CHARTER SIGNING.*

Bart looked at his team, his eyes glowing with the white-hot adrenaline of a God-tier performance. He had them. He had all of them. 

"Now," Bart whispered, his voice carrying the weight of a world. "Who wants to sign a contract?"
