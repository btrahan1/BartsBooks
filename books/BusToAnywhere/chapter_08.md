# Chapter 8: The Upgrade

Monday morning, Mac left for Dallas. He drove his Buick LeSabre like he was fleeing a bank robbery, grinning the whole way.

By Tuesday afternoon, Ben realized that the shoebox method of accounting was a bottleneck. He had optimized the physical flow of the store, but the data flow was still analog. He needed silicon.

Pecos didn't have a Best Buy. It had *Miller’s Electronics & Repair*, a narrow storefront sandwiched between a bakery and a bail bondsman.

The bell above the door was digital—a chirping *bing-bong* that sounded out of place. The air inside was cool and smelled of ozone and soldering iron.

Ben stood in front of a display shelf of laptops. There were three models. One was pink. One was a "Gaming Beast" with red LEDs. One was a beige brick that looked like it ran Windows 95.

He scratched his head. He needed a server, or at least a workstation capable of running a SQL database and a POS frontend, not a machine designed for *Minecraft*.

"Can I help you with something?"

The voice came from behind the repair counter.

Ben turned.

She was petite, with dark hair pulled back in a messy bun held together by what looked like a stylus. She wore an oversized Mario Kart t-shirt and thick-rimmed glasses that slid slightly down her nose.

She was cute. Like, librarian cute. But with a soldering burn on her thumb.

"Uhm... well, uh," Ben stammered. The Flow usually helped him with heavy objects, not pretty girls.

She stood up, wiping her hands on a microfiber cloth. "You looking for something for school? Work? We got the Inspirons on sale, but honestly, the hinges are trash."

"No," Ben said, finding his frequency. "I'm running *Abernathy's Feed* for Mac. I need to digitize the inventory. I'm looking for a Point of Sale system, maybe a barcode scanner if you can order one."

Her eyes went wide behind the lenses. She squealed. A genuine, high-pitched noise of excitement.

"You're him!?"

Ben blinked. "Him?"

"You're the one!" She came around the counter, almost tripping over a stack of hard drives. "The one who went all 'Kung Fu' on those assholes from out of town? The guys with the primer-grey truck?"

She started moving her hands, making *hi-yah!* and *whap!* karate chop noises that were terribly executed but incredibly endearing.

"Mrs. Gable told my mom you hit a guy in the 'solar plexus' and he folded like a lawn chair! And that you fixed her ice machine with a toothpick! Is it true?"

Ben smiled, a flush of heat rising up his neck. "It wasn't exactly like that. It was a coffee stirrer, not a toothpick. And it was a Phoenix Eye strike to the floating rib. But, uh, yeah. That was me."

She stared at him like he was a visiting celebrity. "That is so cool. This town has been boring since the oil boom busted in the eighties. I’m Sarah."

"Ben."

"Ben the Kung Fu Manager," she grinned. She adjusted her glasses, pushing them back up her nose. "Okay, Ben. You don’t want those laptops. Total garbage. If you want a real POS system, we need to build you a rig. Do you have a database preference? SQL? Access?"

Ben felt the tension leave his shoulders. He looked at her not as a terrifyingly cute girl, but as a fellow architect of order.

"SQL," Ben said. "And I need it to talk to a barcode scanner and a receipt printer."

Sarah’s smile turned sharp, professional, and dangerous. "Oh, this is going to be fun. Come on back to the salvage rack. I think I have a Dell OptiPlex we can Frankenstein into a server."

She led him into the back room. Ben followed. 

He had walked in looking for a computer. He had a feeling he was going to walk out with a lot more complexity than he bargained for.
