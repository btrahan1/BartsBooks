# Chapter 9: The Consultation Fee

Thirty minutes later, the back room of *Miller’s Electronics* looked like a robot autopsy.

Sarah had pulled a motherboard from a retired dental office PC, a power supply from a gaming rig that had "died of Mountain Dew exposure," and a hard drive that she swore was "enterprise grade" despite the sticker featuring a cartoon panda.

She snapped the RAM sticks into place with a satisfying *click*.

"Okay," she said, wiping a smudge of thermal paste from her cheek. "Hardware is solid. This thing will run a SQL server until the sun expands and swallows the earth. Total cost for parts: seventy-five bucks."

"That's efficient," Ben noted. He liked efficiency.

"But," Sarah said, leaning back against the workbench. She crossed her arms. Her expression shifted from *Tech Wizard* to *Savvy Negotiator*.

"Hmmm..."

She tapped her chin. "Ben, you know, this is going to take a lot of work to get set up and running. The hardware is the easy part. Building the database schema? Importing Mac’s weird shoebox accounting? Configuring the daily backups so you don't lose everything when the power grid hiccups?"

Ben nodded. "I can handle the data entry if you show me the interface."

"It's not just data entry," she countered. "It's architecture. If we don't normalize the tables correctly, you'll end up with duplicate customers every time Mrs. Higgins buys a new cat toy."

She took a step closer. The soldering iron smell was replaced by something sweeter—vanilla, maybe, or old paper.

"I'll probably have to stop by during lunch for several days in a row to help you set it up," she said.

Ben blinked. "I can pay your hourly rate. Miller’s charges sixty an hour, right?"

Sarah shook her head. A small, mischievous smile played on her lips. "No. This is off the books. I'm doing this pro bono for the Town Hero. But..."

She locked eyes with him.

"It's going to cost you."

Ben groaned internally. He checked his mental wallet. He had his weekly thousand, minus expenses. "How much?"

Sarah started counting on her fingers.

"I like Burgers. Tacos. Chicken. Mexican. Chinese."

She ticked them off like line items on an invoice.

"Make sure you have it ordered by Noon when I stop by each day, okay? Take your pick for lunch from those things."

Ben paused. He processed the transaction.
*Cost: ~$15.00 per day x 5 days = $75.00 total.*
*Benefit: Enterprise-grade inventory system + Professional IT support.*
*Hidden Variable: She wants to have lunch with me.*

The realization hit him late, as social cues often did. She wasn't gouging him. She was... scheduling a date. Five dates.

Ben adjusted his stance. "That seems like an equitable exchange."

"Good," Sarah said. She grabbed a keyboard and shoved it into his hands. "Then pick up this monitor. I'll carry the tower. We start tomorrow. I hope you like spicy food, Ben, because if you get me tacos, I expect the green salsa."

Ben followed her out of the shop. He was carrying a heavy CRT monitor and a tangle of cables, but for some reason, his step felt lighter than it had in years.
