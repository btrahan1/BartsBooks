# Chapter 11: The Hidden Architecture

Wednesday was Chinese. *Golden Dragon* takeout. Kung Pao Chicken for him (extra spicy, per her warning), General Tso’s for her.

Sarah arrived at *Abernathy’s* at 11:45 AM. The heat outside was a physical weight, pressing down on the asphalt until it shimmered. Inside, the store was cool and dim, smelling of grain and the faint, sweet scent of molasses.

"Ben?" she called out.

No answer from the front counter. The server she had Frankensteined yesterday was humming happily under the desk, its green LEDs blinking in the dark like watchful eyes.

"Delivery!" she shouted, holding up the plastic bag.

Still nothing.

She heard a sound from the back—a sharp *exhale*, followed by a soft, rhythmic scuffing of shoes on concrete.

She walked down the center aisle, past the pesticides, past the horse tack, toward the open loading bay doors. The sunlight was pouring in from the back lot, creating a stark rectangle of blinding white against the shadows of the warehouse.

She stopped at the edge of the shade. And she dropped the takeout bag.

Ben was there. But he wasn't stacking feed. He wasn't sweeping.

He was moving.

He was facing away from her, toward the open desert beyond the lot. He moved with a fluidity that didn't make sense—slow, agonizingly controlled motions that suddenly snapped into terrifying speed, then dissolved back into stillness.

And he wasn't wearing a shirt.

Sarah felt her brain stutter. It was a syntax error. A 404 Not Found.

In her head, Ben was "The Manager." He was wiry. He was polite. He wore grey t-shirts that hung a little loose on his frame, suggesting he was skinny—maybe even malnourished from living on vending machine snacks.

She was wrong.

Without the shirt, the architecture was visible. He wasn't bodybuilder-bulky; those guys looked like inflatable balloons. Ben looked like he was carved out of teak wood and wire cabling.

Every muscle in his back shifted as he moved. The lats flared like wings when he raised his arms. The deltoids were sharp, defined caps. When he twisted into a low stance, the spinal erectors coiled like snakes under the skin.

He flowed from a soft Tai Chi block into a Tae Kwon Do snapping kick that was so fast Sarah’s eyes couldn't track the apex—only the blur and the *crack* of his pant leg snapping against the air.

He landed silently. He held the pose. Sweat slicked his skin, making him gleam in the harsh light.

"Oh," Sarah whispered. It was a small sound. A sound that had nothing to do with computer science.

Ben spun around.

His chest was heaving slightly, a rhythmic rise and fall. His abs were a rigid grid of definition, tight and functional. He had a scar running along his left ribs—old and faded against the tan skin.

He saw her. He froze. The intense warrior focus vanished, replaced instantly by a flush of embarrassment that turned his ears pink.

"Sarah," he said, breathless. He scrambled for his grey t-shirt, which was hanging on a nearby pallet hook. "I... you're early."

He pulled the shirt on quickly, hiding the evidence, but the afterimage was burned onto her retinas.

Sarah blinked, trying to reboot her speech center.

"I... uh. Yeah. Traffic." (There was no traffic in Pecos).

She pointed a shaking finger at him. "What... what was that?"

Ben tugged the shirt down, smoothing it out as if he could smooth over the moment. "Forms. Keep the joints loose. The inventory shipment was light today, so I had some spare cycles."

"Spare cycles," she repeated. Her voice sounded pitched a little higher than normal.

She remembered the story Mrs. Gable told. *Folded him like a lawn chair.*

She looked at his arms, now covered by the grey cotton. They looked normal again. But she knew what was underneath. It was like finding out the beige office PC was running a Threadripper CPU and an RTX 4090.

"I brought the food," she said, finally managing to sound semi-normal. "Kung Pao. Extra spicy."

Ben walked over. He looked normal Ben again. Polite. calm.

"Thank you," he said. "I'll go wash up."

He walked past her toward the bathroom.

Sarah stood there for a moment in the heat of the loading bay, staring at the empty space where he had been moving. She fanned herself with her hand.

"Okay," she whispered to the empty warehouse. "Hidden features. Noted."
