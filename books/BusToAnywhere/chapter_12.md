# Chapter 12: The Redundancy Protocol

Thursday was Taco Day (green salsa, as mandated).
Friday was Burgers again because *Pecos Pit* had a special.

By Saturday noon, the system was fully operational.

Ben stood next to Sarah at the front counter. The new barcode scanner beeped with a cheerful, affirmative tone as Sarah scanned a bottle of horse shampoo. On the screen, the inventory count decremented by one, the transaction logged to the SQL database, and the receipt printer spat out a crisp white slip.

It was perfect. It was seamless.

It was finished.

Sarah set the scanner down. She looked at the screen, then at the server humming under the desk, then at her empty Dr. Pepper bottle.

"Well," she said, a slight frown creasing her forehead. "I think that's all there is. We imported the last of the customer records this morning."

The silence in the store felt heavy. The project was done. The transaction—tech support for lunch—was complete. Logically, the contract was fulfilled.

Ben looked at the blinking cursor on the screen. He didn't want the contract to be fulfilled.

"Are you sure?" Ben asked, leaning against the counter. He smiled at her—not his polite, customer-service smile, but the real one that reached his eyes. "I mean, we could backup the backups? We haven't configured a tertiary off-site mirror yet."

Sarah looked up at him. She saw the joke, but she also saw the intent behind it. The frown softened into a smile that was a little shy, a little relieved.

"Tertiary backups," she laughed softly. "That’s extremely redundant, Ben."

"Redundancy is safety," he countered. "Two is one, one is none."

Sarah traced the edge of the counter with her finger. She looked around the dusty store, which had been her prison for three years, and then back at the man who had turned it into a puzzle they solved together.

"You know," she said quietly, "lately I don't feel the need to 'Get out of town' quite so much."

The admission hung in the air between them, more significant than any code they had written. She wasn't talking about the business. She was talking about the vector of her own life.

She took a breath and adjusted her glasses. Her "Savvy Negotiator" face came back, but softer this time.

"With that said... tonight."

She paused, gauging him.

"Do you like pool?"

Ben considered the physics of billiards. Angles. Velocity. Spin. Fulcrums. It was geometry in motion.

"I have an appreciation for the geometry," Ben said.

"Good," Sarah said. She grabbed her purse. "Because I'm going to destroy you. meet me at *The Broken Cue* at seven. And Ben?"

"Yes?"

"No grey t-shirt. Wear the blue one I saw in your drawer. It brings out your eyes."

She walked out the door before he could process how she knew what was in his drawer (Mrs. Gable, undoubtedly).

Ben watched her go. He looked at the perfect POS system. He looked at the clock.

Seven o'clock was seven hours away.

He grabbed a broom. He needed to flow.
