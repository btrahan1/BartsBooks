# Chapter 14: Indecision

They walked out of *Boots and Balls* at 9:30 PM.

The desert air had cooled down to a perfect seventy degrees. The sky was a vast, glittering dome of stars that city people only saw in planetariums.

Sarah was quiet. She walked with her hands in her pockets, kicking a small rock along the pavement. The energy she had shown during the game—the competitive fire—had banked down into something softer, more hesitant.

They reached her car, a beat-up Honda Civic filled with computer parts and empty energy drink cans.

"Well," Sarah said. She leaned against the driver's door. She looked at her shoes. "I practiced my 'victory speech' all week, and you didn't even let me use it."

Ben smiled. "You won four games to three. The victory is logged."

"Yeah. I guess."

She looked up at him. She bit her bottom lip. It was a nervous, unconscious gesture. Her eyes darted to his face, then away, then back.

The data was clear.
*Shoulders slumped forward (Reluctance).*
*Prolonged eye contact (Connection).*
*Biting lip (Uncertainty/Desire).*

She didn't want the night to end. But she was the "Tech Girl" and he was "The Manager," and the protocol for what happened next was undefined.

Ben waited. He saw the hesitation. He saw her searching for a variable that would solve the equation.

He decided to provide the constant.

"So," Ben said, his voice low and steady. "Do you have any wine at your place?"

Sarah stopped biting her lip. Her eyes widened, and then crinkled at the corners. The tension evaporated, replaced by a radiant relief.

"I have a bottle of red that's been breathing since Tuesday," she said. "And I have Netflix."

Ben nodded. "That sounds like an optimal configuration."

"Get in," she said, unlocking the car. "But you have to move the box of graphics cards off the passenger seat."

Ben prioritized the graphics cards to the back seat. He got in.

The engine started with a cough, then settled into a hum. As they pulled out of the lot, Ben looked back at the neon sign of the kicking boots.

He had won the pool game (technically lost, but won the objective). He had de-escalated a fight. And now, he was going home with the smartest girl in Pecos.

The flow was good.
