# Chapter 15: The Alibi

When Sarah arrived at *Abernathy’s* the next morning, the bell above the door didn't jingle. It was already open.

And there was a police cruiser parked next to Mac’s old Buick (which was gone, currently en route to Dallas).

Sarah hurried inside, her heart hammering a frantic rhythm against her ribs. The scene she found made her stop short.

Ben was behind the counter, reorganizing a display of cattle ear tags. He looked calm. Serene, even.

Standing opposite him was Sheriff Dean. Dean was a good man, built like a vending machine and wearing a uniform that was pressed to within an inch of its life. He had his notepad out.

"So you admit there was an altercation?" Dean asked.

Ben placed a yellow tag in the bin marked *#401*. "Altercation implies conflict, Sheriff. There was a collision. He spilled his beer. I bought him a replacement."

"Witnesses say he was aggressive."

"He was intoxicated," Ben corrected. "Aggression requires intent. He was just steering poorly."

"And you left the bar at 9:30?"

"Approximately. I didn't check the timestamp."

Dean tapped his pen against the pad. He studied Ben with the weary skepticism of a man who had seen too many 'accidents' in his thirty years on the force.

"What's this all about, Sheriff?"

Sarah stepped forward. Her voice was steady, but her hands were shaking slightly.

Dean turned. He touched the brim of his hat. "Morning, Sarah. Just following up on a report."

He turned back to Ben.

"Well, Hank didn't make it home last night. He was found dead this morning behind the dumpster at *Boots and Balls*. A knife in the back."

Sarah gasped. The sound sucked the air out of the room. Murder. In Pecos.

Dean watched Ben’s face for a reaction. A flinch. A twitch.

Ben didn't flinch. He prioritized the data.
*Hank dead. Knife. Time of death unknown but presumably post-9:30 PM. I am the last known antagonistic contact.*
*Conclusion: I am the suspect.*

"I didn't kill him, Sheriff," Ben said. "Inefficient. I already bought him a beer."

"Buying a man a beer doesn't clear you of stabbing him an hour later," Dean said heavily. "I need to know where you went after you left the bar, Ben. Assuming you *did* leave."

The silence stretched. Ben looked at Dean. He wouldn't lie to a peace officer, but the truth ('I watched Netflix with Sarah') felt... complicated.

"He was with me, Dean."

Sarah stepped up to the counter. She stood next to Ben. She crossed her arms, channeling every ounce of *Miller’s Electronics* authority she possessed.

"He left the bar with me at 9:30. We went to my place. He was there all night."

Dean looked at Sarah. He looked at Ben. He looked back at Sarah. The implication hung in the air, heavy and scandalous. The Pastor's daughter (metaphorically speaking) and the drifter manager.

Sarah didn't blink. She left the unsaid, unsaid.

Ben raised an eyebrow. He looked at Sarah, registering the magnitude of the lie. It wasn't a lie—he *was* there—but the context Dean was assuming was incorrect. They had watched a documentary on bridges and he had fallen asleep on the couch. But Sarah was letting Dean think... *that*.

"Well," Ben said, turning back to Dean. "That's the timeline, Sheriff."

Dean closed his notebook. He visibly relaxed. If Sarah Miller vouched for him—*that* way—then he had an alibi.

"Alright," Dean said. "That's good enough for me. Sorry to bother you, Ben. Just doing the job."

He tipped his hat again. "You guys be careful. Seems like we might have a murderer on the loose."

Dean walked out. The cruiser started up and rolled away.

Ben waited until the sound faded. He turned to Sarah.

"You realized," Ben said softly, "that you just ruined your reputation in this town for the sake of efficiency."

Sarah’s face turned a bright, violent shade of red. She busied herself with the receipt printer.

"I calculated the risk," she muttered. "And... well. I wasn't going to let him arrest my IT guy."
