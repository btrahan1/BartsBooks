# Chapter 16: Investigation Vectors

The store was quiet again, but the air felt charged. The receipt printer hummed on the counter, ignorant of the fact that it had just witnessed perjury.

Sarah leaned against the display of work gloves, her face returning to its normal color, though her ears were still pink.

"Besides," she said, finding her footing again. "You were there all night. Even if nothing happened *that* time..."

She paused, looking at him over the rim of her glasses. A mischievous smile played on her lips.

"...that doesn't mean nothing will happen the next time."

Ben felt the hit. It was a direct impact to his composure. He cleared his throat, adjusting a stack of receipts that were already perfectly aligned.

"You may have a point, Sarah," he said, trying to recover the tactical advantage. "There's a bad guy out there. You need protecting!"

Ben puffed his chest out, striking a pose that was half-Superman, half-security guard. He held it for a full two seconds before the absurdity broke him.

They both laughed—a release of the tension that had tightened the room since Dean walked in.

"Okay, okay, Hero," Sarah giggled. "You can put the chest away."

Ben exhaled, his expression sobering. "Anyway, that's sad about Hank. He didn't deserve that. He was just drunk and angry. That's a human condition, not a capital offense."

He looked out the front window, where the dust from the Sheriff's cruiser had settled back onto the road.

"What do you think of this town's 'police force'?" Ben asked. "Dean seems decent, but... does he strike you as the type who solves homicides? Or the type who breaks up bar fights and catches speeders?"

Sarah frowned. "Dean is a nice guy. He coaches the Little League team. But the last 'major crime' in Pecos was when teenagers stole the cow statue from the dairy Queen three years ago. Dean found it in the football field endzone."

"So, zero experience with forensic analysis or homicide vectors," Ben concluded.

He looked at Sarah. He saw the same spark in her eyes that he had seen when they were building the server. The desire to solve the puzzle. To fix the broken system.

"Do you think they can handle it?" Ben asked, raising an eyebrow. "Or should we... 'investigate'?"

He put emphatic air quotes around the word, grinning.

Sarah pushed off the counter. She walked over to the server, checking the uptime.

"Ben," she said, "I have access to the town’s public records database because I fixed the City Hall router last month. I can pull up Hank’s arrest record, known associates, and last known address in about five minutes."

She looked back at him.

"You have the physical skills. I have the digital access. Dean has a notepad."

Ben nodded. Ideally, the authorities would handle this. But efficiency demanded results.

"Okay," Ben said. "Let's optimize justice."
