# Chapter 17: Outsiders

Sarah's living room was exactly what Ben expected: comfortable, slightly messy, and dominated by technology. There was a server rack in the closet (he could hear the fans), a pile of gaming controllers on the coffee table, and a half-disassembled drone on the bookshelf.

Ben was sitting on the couch. He had insisted on coming over to "verify the perimeter," which Sarah found hilarious but secretly comforting.

She was scrolling through the town's public safety reports on her laptop.

"No domestic disputes reported for Hank in five years," she said. "No gambling debts known. No jealous ex-husbands."

Ben paced the room. "Pecos is a closed system. Everyone knows everyone. Murder requires a level of deviation that doesn't exist here. If Hank had an enemy, you'd know. Mrs. Gable would know."

"Exactly," Sarah said. "People here fight with fists or lawyers. They don't stab people in the back behind a dumpster."

They looked at each other. The data pointed to a single conclusion.

"It has to be outsiders," they said in unison.

Sarah smiled at the synchronization. Ben just nodded.

"There weren't any plausible suspects," Ben continued. "It's a small town, devoid of serial killers. The probability of a random transient violent crime is low... unless..."

Ben snapped his fingers. The sound was sharp in the quiet room.

"That truck!"

"The primer-grey Ford?" Sarah asked. "The guys you... educated?"

"Yes. Those assholes from a few weeks ago. They were thieves. They were humiliated. They were driven out of town."

Ben’s mind raced through the scenario. *Revenge? No, too personal. Opportunity? More likely.*

"They had no plates," Ben said. "They move town to town. If they circled back... maybe they saw a drunk guy in the alley. Maybe they saw an easy mark."

He looked at Sarah.

"Does the report say if he was robbed? Did they find his wallet?"

Sarah typed furiously. "Dean's report is... let's say 'concise'. Hold on. I'm checking the property log from the coroner's intake."

She hit a key. She frowned.

"Ben," she said, looking up. "Property on person: One set of keys. One pocket comb. One pack of gum."

"No wallet?"

"No wallet."

Ben stopped pacing. "Robbery. A robbery gone wrong, or a robbery turned violent. And those guys were already stealing tools. Stealing a wallet from a drunk is a lower exertion threshold."

"So we're looking for the truck," Sarah said.

"We're looking for the truck," Ben agreed. "And this time, I don't think I'm going to let them drive away."
