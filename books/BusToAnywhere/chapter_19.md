# Chapter 19: The Digital Sting

The perimeter was quiet. The feed from camera #3 (Main Street North) showed nothing but a stray cat and a tumbleweed.

Ben and Sarah were in his apartment, which had been converted into a tactical command center. The 27-inch monitor from *Miller’s Electronics* cast a blue glow over the room.

They were eating popcorn. The suspense was high, but the caloric needs were met.

"You really think they'll use it?" Sarah asked, tossing a kernel into her mouth. "If they killed him for it, wouldn't they just take the cash and ditch the plastic?"

"Opportunists are greedy," Ben said, eyes scanning the quad-split screen. "They don't think about digital footprints. They think about a full tank of gas."

*BEEP!*

The sound was sharp and loud, cutting through the silence.

Sarah jumped. Her hand hit the keyboard, sending keys flying.

"A hit!" she shouted, ignoring the scattered hardware. She pulled up the alert window.

"A gas station five miles from here. The Chevron at the junction. Someone just used Hank’s credit card!"

Ben stood up. The calm manager was gone. The Hunter was back.

"Five miles," Ben calculated. "That puts them in town in seven minutes if they drive the limit. Six if they don't."

"Amateurs," Ben muttered. "Using a dead man's card within the same county."

He moved to the bedroom. "Time to suit up."

Sarah turned around in her chair.

Ben was disrobing quickly, shedding the grey t-shirt and jeans. Underneath, he grabbed his "After Hours" gear—black cargo pants, a tight black long-sleeve compression shirt (wicking material, low friction), and soft-soled tactical boots.

It wasn't a Ninja suit, technically. But it was close.

Sarah grinned, watching the transformation.

"Yeah baby," she cheered, wolf-whistling. "Take it off!"

Ben paused mid-change to shoot her a look. He smiled, a dangerous glint in his eye.

"Save it for later," he said.

He slipped an earpiece—a modified Bluetooth set Sarah had hacked for encrypted comms—into his ear.

"I'll be down on Main Street in the shadows," Ben said, checking the fit of his gloves. "Let me know if they enter town and where they stop, as per our plan."

He moved to the window. He didn't use the door. He slipped out onto the fire escape and merged with the darkness.

Sarah spun back to the screens. Her heart was racing.

"Roger that, Batman," she whispered. "Eyes on."
