# Chapter 21: The Judgment

Ben crossed the street. The shadows clung to him until the very last second.

The first guy—the one who had shoved Mac weeks ago—came stumbling out of the store with an armful of beef jerky and a carton of Marlboros.

He didn't see Ben. He felt him.

Ben grabbed the man's collar and belt line. In one fluid motion, he utilized the man's forward momentum against him. He literally flipped him over his back.

*WHAM.*

The man hit the hard concrete sidewalk flat on his back. The sound was sickening—like a slab of wet meat hitting a butcher's block.

Ben wasn't gentle. He didn't release the grip on the man's sleeve. He gave a sharp, calculated tug upward while the body was pinned.

*POP.*

The shoulder dislocated. The man screamed, dropping the jerky in a shower of plastic wrappers.

The second guy—the quiet one—came out of the broken window behind him. He saw his friend down. He saw Ben.

He didn't run. He pulled a knife. A six-inch hunting blade, stained and dull.

He lunged at Ben from behind.

Ben turned swiftly, sensing the displacement of air. He shifted his weight, but the alley was narrow. The blade grazed his ribs, slicing through the tactical shirt and biting into the skin.

It stung a little. A hot, sharp line of pain.

Ben looked at the knife. He looked at the man's eyes—dilated, desperate, violent.

*He used a knife on Hank.*

Ben didn't just block the next thrust. He intercepted it. He caught the man's wrist in a vice grip. He didn't use a pressure point this time. He used torque.

He twisted the arm against the joint, continuing past the point of resistance.

*SNAP.*

He broke the wrist, viscously. It was a sound of finality. The knife clattered to the sidewalk. The man collapsed, cradling his ruined hand, sobbing.

Ben stood over them. He was breathing hard, the adrenaline flooding his system. The cut on his side was bleeding, staining the black shirt darker.

The truck door opened.

The driver—the guy in the backwards cap—stepped out. He saw his two friends on the ground. One with a shoulder hanging loose, one with a wrist bent at a ninety-degree angle.

He held his hands up, palms open.

"Hey!" he stammered, backing away. "I'm just the driver! I didn't do nothing!"

Ben stepped over the man with the broken wrist. He walked toward the driver.

"You said that last time," Ben said, his voice a low growl.

"I... I..."

"No need to drive today," Ben said. He chambered a fist. "So it's NOT your lucky day."

He didn't wait for a rebuttal.
