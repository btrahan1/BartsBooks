# Chapter 23: The Plausible Deniability

Ben moved stiffly around the shop the next day. Every time he lifted a bag of chicken scratch, the stitches Sarah had improvised (butterfly strips and superglue) pulled tight against his ribs.

At 10:00 AM, the bell jingled. It wasn't a customer. It was Sheriff Dean.

Dean walked in slowly, taking off his hat. He looked tired but satisfied.

"Ben," he said, leaning on the counter. "How you doing?"

Ben placed a bag of feed down carefully. He prioritized his breathing. "Just fine, Sheriff. Keeping busy."

Dean looked him up and down. He noted the slight hitch in Ben's movement. He noted the fresh bruise blooming on Ben's jawline (from where he had hit the pavement during the flip).

"Heard there was quite the ruckus last night," Dean said. "Over at the General Store."

"Is that so?" Ben asked, picking up a rag to wipe the counter. "I was... occupied. Sarah and I were watching movies."

Dean nodded slowly. "Movies. Right."

He pulled a pack of gum from his pocket.

"Funny thing," Dean said. "We got a call about a break-in. Got there in three minutes. And what do we find? Three guys—the same guys I've been looking for regarding the Hank Miller homicide—gift-wrapped on the sidewalk."

Dean unwrapped a piece of gum.

"Zip-tied. Hand and foot. One of 'em had a dislocated shoulder. One had a broken wrist. And the driver... well, he was just crying. Kept saying it wasn't his lucky day."

Ben continued wiping the counter. "Sounds like vigorous police work, Sheriff."

"That's the thing," Dean said. "I don't carry zip-ties. I carry cuffs. And I haven't dislocated a shoulder since high school football."

He stared at Ben. The silence stretched, filled only by the hum of the ice machine in the corner (which was running perfectly).

"Whoever did it," Dean said, "knew exactly what they were doing. Efficient. Brutal. But careful. Didn't kill 'em. Just... stopped 'em."

Ben stopped wiping. He met Dean's gaze.

"Maybe the town got lucky, Sheriff. Maybe gravity just took hold."

Dean chuckled. A dry, knowing sound. "Gravity. Yeah. That's a powerful force."

He put his hat back on.

"Coroner confirmed the knife found on the scene matched the wound on Hank. Case is closed. District Attorney is happy. I'm happy."

He walked to the door, then paused.

"You take it easy on that lifting, Ben. Gravity can be hard on the back."

"I will, Sheriff."

Dean walked out. Ben watched him go.

The system was restored. Order had returned to Pecos.

Ben touched his side. It hurt, but it was a good hurt. Measuring the cost of justice against the value of peace.

It was a bargain.
