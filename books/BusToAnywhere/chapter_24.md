# Chapter 24: The Public Domain

The next night, *Boots and Balls* was louder than usual, but the energy was different. It wasn't the jagged, drunken energy of a Saturday. It was the warm, communal buzz of a family reunion.

Ben sat at the end of the bar. He was moving a little slower than usual—the stitches in his side were a constant, itching reminder—but he was feeling better.

He was on his third Shiner Bock. He hadn't paid for a single one.

Every ten minutes or so, someone would walk by—a ranch hand, a mechanic, a teacher—and nod.

"Preciate it, Ben," they’d say. Or just, "Good work."

In the corner, Table 4 was empty except for a framed picture of Hank. It was a bad photo—Hank looking red-faced and surprised at a BBQ—but someone had placed a lit candle and a fresh beer in front of it. Folks stopped by, patted the frame, and whispered a few words. Hank hadn't been a saint, but he was *their* sinner.

The bartender, a guy named Earl (Jerry's cousin, presumably), slid another cold bottle toward Ben.

"I didn't order this," Ben said.

Earl wiped the bar with a rag. "Compliments of that guy down heavily on the end."

Ben looked down the bar. A man in a trucker hat raised his glass.

"Nice Video!" the man called out.

Ben froze. He turned to Earl. "Video?"

Earl chuckled. He pulled his phone out of his apron.

"You ain't seen it? It's got forty thousand views on 'Pecos Justice'. Somebody... probably somebody good with computers... uploaded it this morning."

Earl hit play.

The video was 4K. Crisp. Clear. It was taken from high up, across the street. The angle was perfect.

It showed the truck. The smash. And then, a figure emerging from the shadows. The figure was blurred—a digital mosaic obscuring the face—but the movement was unmistakable.

The flip. *WHAM.*
The arm bar. *SNAP.*
The cool, terrifying walk toward the driver.

Ben watched himself administer the algorithm of violence. It looked different on screen. Less messy. More... cinematic.

"Identity protection," Earl noted, watching the screen. "Face is blurred. But we all know who wears those grey shirts, Ben."

Ben took a sip of the beer. Sarah. She had turned the Panopticon into a PR campaign. She had released the footage into the public domain, turning a vigilant act into a community service announcement.

*Message: Do not mess with this town.*

Ben looked at the reflection of the neon boot in the mirror. He looked at the picture of Hank. He touched the bandage on his ribs.

He raised his bottle to the guy at the end of the bar.

"To the editor," Ben murmured.

And he drank.
