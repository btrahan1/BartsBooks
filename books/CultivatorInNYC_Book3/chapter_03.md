# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 3: The Status Craze

If Han Wei had thought the world was chaotic before he went to the Amazon, he was woefully unprepared for the digital firestorm that followed his speech at Guzman’s bodega.

By the time he’d finished his "King-Sized Mediocre Master Special"—which, to Guzman’s credit, was indeed extra-pickled—the internet hadn't just reacted; it had fundamentally mutated. The word "Status" had become more than just a search term; it had become the new global currency of social validation.

"Master, you might want to see this," Jax said, hovering just above the pile of napkins on the wobbly table. The drone’s projected screen flickered into life, displaying a rapid-fire montage of TikTok clips, Twitter threads, and Reddit posts. 

The first clip was a teenager in a suburban bedroom, his eyes dilated with excitement as he stared into his phone camera. "Yo, guys! I just did the Wei-meditation for like, two minutes? And I said the word? Look!" He pointed his camera at the empty air beside his head, and although the phone couldn't *see* the Qi-reflection, the teen’s frantic description did the work. "Stamina 12! I'm a 12! My mom is only a 9! I’m literally built different!" 

The teen then proceeded to try and sprint through his bedroom door without opening it. The video cut off right as his shoulder made contact with the drywall.

"He’s transitioning into a 'Strength-Focused' build," Jax deadpanned. "And a 'Medical-Debt-Focused' one."

Wei groaned, rubbing his temples. "I told them to find balance, Jax. I didn't tell them to use their heads as battering rams."

"The concept of 'Balance' is currently trending at number forty-two, Master. 'Maxing Stats' is trending at number one."

It wasn't just humans. The memes had already begun, and they were, in Wei’s professional opinion, utterly ridiculous. 

One viral image showed a particularly buff-looking tabby cat sitting on a weight bench. A Photoshopped translucent blue box hovered over its head: *NAME: MR. WHISKERS. STRENGTH: 12. AGILITY: 25. SPIRIT: 0 (BECAUSE HE IS A DEMON).* 

Another featured a mouse caught in a snapshot as it appeared to stay suspended in mid-air for a fraction of a second too long. *SPIRIT 15. HE IS ASCENDING. RUN.*

"Even the animals are mocking us now," Miller said, leaning over to look at the screen. He had his own phone out, his brow furrowed as he stared at his own internal reflection. "I’m an 18 in Strength and a 16 in Stamina. But my Spirit is an 8. Is that why I feel like I'm constantly vibrating?"

"Eight is low for someone with your combat experience," Wei said, glancing at the mercenary. "You’ve spent your life training your body but treating your mind like a tool. You're like a high-performance engine with a tiny fuel tank. You'll burn out."

"Great," Miller muttered. "I'm a Ferrari with the tank of a lawnmower."

As the group watched, the commercialization of the "Era of Essence" began in real-time. The ads were popping up everywhere, flooding every feed and sidebar with predatory promises.

*CROWN HEIGHTS DOJO OF RADIANT LIGHT!* one flashing banner screamed. *LEARN THE 'TRUE' STATUS-BOOSTING KATA! STRENGTH +5 GUARANTEED IN ONE WEEK!*

*DR. ELARA’S QUANTUM-ENLIGHTENMENT PILLS,* another ad claimed, featuring a woman in a lab coat holding a bottle of glowing purple capsules. *ENGINEERED BY SCIENTIFIC CULTIVATORS TO ALIGN YOUR DANTIAN. ONLY $299.99 PER MONTH PER MERIDIAN.*

"Wait, 'Per Meridian'?" Sarah asked, her journalist’s nose wrinkling in disgust. "Human beings have twelve primary meridians. That's three thousand dollars a month just to 'align' yourself. This is a scam."

"Of course it's a scam," Wei said, his voice dripping with ancient exasperation. "You can't buy alignment in a pill. That's like trying to get fit by eating a picture of a salad. And those 'Dojos'... look at that form." He pointed to a video of a 'Master' in Times Square who was leading a group of tourists in what looked like a series of aggressive jazz-ercise moves. "He’s swinging his arms so hard he’s going to dislocate his shoulders before he ever gathers a speck of Qi."

"The 'Golden Dragon Academy of Manhattan' is currently offering a 'Wei-Certified' introductory course," Jax added helpfully. 

Wei nearly choked on his coffee. "Wei-certified? I haven't even met those people! I haven't met *anyone* in Manhattan!"

"They claim they spoke to you in a dream, Master. The video has three million likes."

Wei slumped back into his chair, the "Mediocre Special" suddenly feeling like lead in his stomach. Back at the Azure Cloud Sect, the process of teaching was sacred. It took decades of observation, sweeps of the floor, and endless repetitions of "Basic Fist" before a disciple was even allowed to *think* about their own internal standing. Here, it had taken forty-five minutes for a burger-flipping teenager to decide he was a god and for a dozen grifters to start selling bottle-water "Elixirs."

"Master, search queries for 'How to increase Spirit' have spiked by four thousand percent," Jax reported. "Unfortunately, the top result is a blog post suggesting people 'scream at the moon until it turns blue.'"

"It’s already violet, Jax! What more do they want?"

"They want a shortcut, Wei," Tupi said softly. She was watching the street through the bodega window. A group of teenagers were standing on the corner, all of them staring at nothing with intense focus, their lips moving as they repeated the word "Status" like a mantra. "Back home, we knew that power came from the land and the long sleep of the forest. Here... they think it’s an app they can download."

Wei looked at the kids on the corner. One of them, a girl with bright pink hair, suddenly gave a little gasp. A faint, nearly invisible shimmer of light danced around her fingertips for a split second before vanishing. She looked at her friends, her eyes wide with shock and joy.

"I did it! I saw it! My Agility went up! It was a 10 and now it’s 10.1!"

Her friends cheered, and within seconds, they were all trying to mimic her movements, their bodies jerking with a desperate, uncoordinated hunger for that 0.1 increase. 

Wei watched her. He saw the way she was moving—too fast, too tight. She was burning her own vital essence to power that tiny spark. It wasn't cultivation; it was a slow-motion self-immolation.

"She’s going to be bedridden for a week if she keeps that up," Wei whispered.

"Then you have to tell them, Wei," Sarah said, her voice firm. She pulled out her own tablet, her fingers flying across the screen. "You can’t just give them the mirror and then walk away. You’re the only person on this planet who knows that a 10.1 isn't worth a week of exhaustion."

Wei looked at his hands—calloused, mediocre, and steady. He looked at the bodega, where Guzman was currently trying to "cultivate" a meat-slicer with a terrifying amount of focus. He looked at the world through Jax's projectors, a world that was currently spiraling into a fever of ego, avarice, and genuine, misplaced hope.

He thought about his old Sect Master, a man who had told him he was "too average to ever truly lead." 

*Maybe so,* Wei thought. *But I’m the only average person left who knows where the ground is.*

"Jax," Wei said, standing up.

"Yes, Master?"

"Start a new stream. Not a speech. Not a legend. A lesson."

"Title, Master?"

Wei looked at the girl on the corner, who was currently panting for breath, her face pale but her eyes still fixed on the invisible numbers in the air.

"Title it: 'Why 10 is Good Enough.' And tell them... tell them we're going to start with 'Sweeping the Floor.'"

Jax’s camera-eye whirred. "Master, that is going to be incredibly boring. We will lose at least two million viewers in the first five minutes."

Wei adjusted his tattered robes and picked up a stray broom leaning against the deli counter.

"Good," Wei said, his voice calm and certain. "Those two million weren't going to survive the first dungeon anyway. Let’s see who’s left when we’re done."

He stepped out onto the sidewalk, the violet moon hanging overhead like a silent, watchful eye. He looked at the teenagers, who stopped their frantic gesturing to stare at him. 

"You," Wei said, pointing the broom at the girl with the pink hair. "You’re breathing through your shoulders. Stop it. You’re going to give yourself a headache. If you want to see your Status change, try making sure the sidewalk is clean first."

The girl blinked, confused. "But... but I saw the 10.1! I'm progressing!"

"You're shrinking," Wei countered. "Progress isn't a number. It's the space you can hold without shaking. Now, take the broom. We’ve got a lot of work to do."

As the girl hesitantly took the broom and Jax’s red light began to pulse, Wei took a deep breath of the electric New York air. 

He had intended to retire to his favorite park and eat his sandwich in peace. Instead, he was starting a school. In a city of eight million people, all of whom currently wanted to be the main character.

"This is going to be a lot of work," Wei sighed.

But as he watched the girl take her first, unsteady sweep, he felt a familiar, humble peace settle over him. 

It was time to get to work.
