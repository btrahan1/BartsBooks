# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 5: The Rat Wars

Han Wei sat in the back of Guzman’s bodega, his eyes fixed on the projected screen of Jax’s latest livestream. Outside, the violet moon hung in a sky that was rapidly becoming the most-watched backdrop in human history. Inside, the air smelled of extra-pickles and the sharp, ozone-crackle of a world that was tearing itself apart and stitching itself back together at the same time.

"It’s happening, Master," Jax chirped, the drone’s red light pulsing with a data-heavy frequency. "The hashtag #RatWars has officially surpassed #StatusReveals. I am currently monitoring two hundred thousand simultaneous uploads from the NYC subterranean network alone."

Wei leaned forward, his elbows on the wobbly table. "Show me."

The screen split into a chaotic mosaic of vertical video clips. The first was a group of NYU students in Union Square, armed with nothing but broomsticks and hockey sticks. They were chasing a rat the size of a Cocker Spaniel through the fountain. The rodent’s eyes glowed with a faint, malevolent lavender light, and its teeth left jagged score-marks in the granite of the fountain’s edge. 

The kids were yelling, swinging wildly, and tripping over each other. It was a disaster. One student tripped, and the rat lunged—only to be intercepted by a heavy-set man in a janitor’s uniform who stepped into the frame and delivered a perfectly timed strike with a heavy mop handle.

"Strength 11.2," the janitor panted, looking into his own invisible Status screen as the rat scurried off into a sewer grate. "I can feel the leverage now. It’s like the wood knows where to hit."

The scene shifted. The mosaic blurred into a series of hospital hallways. This was the dark underbelly of the 'Rat Wars.' 

"Emergency rooms are at two hundred percent capacity," Sarah said, her voice tight as she sat across from Wei, her tablet displaying the intake reports from Bellevue and Elmhurst. "The rats aren't just big, Wei. They’re smart. They’re nipping at ankles, dragging people down, and some of them seem to have a localized 'fear-aura' that’s literal Qi-suppression. We’ve got thousands of puncture wounds and twisted ankles."

Wei watched a clip from a triage tent at JFK. It looked like a war zone. Stretchers was lined up under buzzing fluorescent lights that flickered with violet interference. But then, he saw it.

In the center of the frame was a nurse, her scrubs stained with blood and iodine, her face a mask of exhaustion and grim focus. She was wrapping a teenager’s leg. Her hands moved with a speed and precision that was far beyond her normal training. There was a faint, golden shimmer around her fingertips—the sign of a 'Healing Intent' that Wei recognized from the lower-tier Alchemist Sects back home.

"Hold still, Jimmy," the nurse snapped, her voice carrying a resonance that seemed to physically calm the boy. 

Suddenly, she paused. Her eyes widened, focusing on the air in front of her. A small, translucent shimmer appeared—not a blue box, but a ripple of light that only she could truly see, though the ambient Qi allowed the camera to catch the distortion.

"Status," she whispered.

A moment later, a tired, beautiful smile broke across her face. She looked at her fellow nurse, who was struggling to stabilize a man in shock. 

"I'm a Level 3 Nurse!" she shouted, her voice shaking with a mixture of disbelief and pride. "My Spirit score just jumped three points! I can... I can see the circulation! I know where the internal bleeding is!"

She turned back to Jimmy, her hands glowing more brightly. "Don't you worry, honey. I’ve reached the *Flow of Mending*. You’re going to be walking by tomorrow."

The mosaic shifted again, showing a mother in a Queens apartment, her living room barricaded with furniture. She was bandaging her daughter’s bitten hand with strips of a bedsheet. She wasn't a professional. She was just terrified and loving. 

*Status.*

The mother’s eyes lit up. "Strength 9... but Spirit 15? Oh, baby... Mommy’s got the 'Soothing Heart' now. It doesn't hurt as much, does it?"

The little girl shook her head, her tears drying as the mother’s natural Qi, fueled by maternal desperation and focused care, knit the skin back together twice as fast as it should have.

Wei sighed, but it was a sigh of relief, not frustration. "The meritocracy," he whispered. "It isn't just for those who hunt. It's for those who protect. The world is rewarding the healers as much as the warriors."

"The memes are already rebranding them," Jax reported, flicking through a series of viral images. "They’re calling them 'White Mage Moms' and 'Triage Templars.' And look at this."

He pulled up a formal-looking website that had appeared within the last hour: *THE NYC RAT HUNTER’S GUILD (EST. TWO HOURS AGO).* 

It featured a picture of a construction worker, a bodega clerk, and a retired gym teacher, all standing together with their 'sticks' in hand. They were wearing makeshift armor made of thick cardboard and duct tape. 

*JOIN THE VANGUARD,* the text read. *REQUIREMENTS: STRENGTH 11+, AGILITY 10+, AND A WILLINGNESS TO SWEEP THE LINE. PAY: SPIRIT CRYSTALS AND COMMUNITY GRATITUDE. (NO GIMMICKS. NO FAKE PILLS. WEI SAYS WALK THE PATH).*

"They’re organizing," Miller said, a note of professional respect in his voice. "They’re not waiting for the government. They’re not waiting for the CSuite Cultuivators. They're just... taking the trash out."

Wei watched a video of a group of 'Rat Hunters' in the Bronx. They were moving in a tight formation, using their sticks not just to hit, but to guide the rats into a containment zone. They weren't flashy. They weren't flying through the air or shooting fireballs. They were just... balanced. They were using the basics Wei had demonstrated in the park.

One of them—the girl with the pink hair from the corner—was leading the group. Her movements were calm, her breathing rhythmic. She wasn't chasing the 0.1 anymore. She was holding the line.

"She reached Level 1 Hunter," Jax noted. "And her Spirit score settled at 12. She’s no longer bedridden, Master. She’s... she’s flourishing."

Wei stood up, picking up his own staff from the corner. The extra-pickled sandwich was gone, and the weight of the new world was settling comfortably on his shoulders. He looked out the window. The streets were messy, the hospitals were full, and the internet was a madhouse of status updates and rodent combat footage.

But for the first time since the Violet Moon had risen, New York didn't feel like a victim. It felt like a sect. A chaotic, loud, incredibly stubborn sect that was learning to cultivate through sheer, unadulterated grit.

"Is it enough, Master?" Jax asked, the camera-eye tilting curiously. "Millions are still in danger. The 'Dissonance' in the tunnels is growing."

Wei looked at the screen one last time, at the Level 3 Nurse giving a thumbs-up to a room full of smiling, bandaged patients. At the 'Rat Hunters' guarding their neighborhood deli. At the mothers turning their fear into a healing light.

"It’s a start, Jax," Wei said, his voice quiet but full of a growing, mediocre pride. "Back home, the sect elders would say these people are 'low-tier.' They’d say their Qi is muddy and their forms are crude."

He opened the bodega door, the bell chiming a bright, clear note against the sound of a distant ambulance. 

"But I’ve never seen a 'High Mortal' elder bandage a stranger’s leg with that much heart. They’re not just cultivators, Jax. They’re New Yorkers."

He stepped out onto the sidewalk, his staff tapping a steady beat against the concrete. 

"Let’s go to 42nd Street," Wei said. "The Vanguard has the surface. It’s time we showed them what’s waiting in the deep."

As they headed toward the subway, a group of kids ran past, chasing a particularly large rat with a collective roar of "STATUS!" 

Wei didn't even scold them for the bad form. He just watched them go, a small smile on his face. 

The Rat Wars were just the beginning. But the people of Earth had already found their first quest. And they were leveling up the only way that truly mattered: together.
