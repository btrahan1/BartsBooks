# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 6: The Queen of the Deep

The 42nd Street Station, usually a bustling intersection of eight million lives, was now a tomb of concrete and violet shadow. 

Han Wei stepped onto the platform of the S-train, his staff tapping a rhythmic warning against the grime-crusted tile. The air here was unlike the surface. It was stagnant, heavy with the scent of rotting trash and a sharp, stinging acidity that made Wei’s eyes water. The violet mist wasn't just a haze here; it was pooling on the tracks like a physical liquid, swirling around the rusted third rail.

"Master, the interference is reaching critical levels," Jax whispered, the drone’s fans whirring at maximum speed. "I am struggling to maintain the uplink. My sensors are detecting a massive bio-resonance signature directly ahead. It is... significantly larger than a Cocker Spaniel."

"Stay close," Miller said, his voice a low grunt. He was holding a heavy piece of iron pipe he’d scavenged from a construction site, his knuckles white. He’d wrapped the handle in duct tape, a very 'New York' version of a polearm. "I’ve seen some bad things in tunnels, Wei. But this... this doesn't feel like a tunnel anymore. It feels like a throat."

Sarah was behind them, her tablet held high to catch the faint light. She was pale, her fingers trembling as she watched the scrolling data. "The 'Rat Wars' feed is going silent in this sector. Everyone who came down here in the last hour hasn't posted a single thing. It’s a total blackout."

Suddenly, the silence was broken. Not by a screech, but by a sound that was far more terrifying: a wet, rhythmic *thumping*. It was the sound of a heart the size of a bass drum.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

From the darkness of the tunnel, she emerged.

The Rat Queen was a nightmare given form by a planet that had forgotten how to be gentle. She was the size of a city bus, her fur a mangy, matted tapestry of gray and violet. Her eyes weren't just glowing; they were burning pits of lavender fire that seemed to suck the light out of the air. Around her, hundreds of smaller rats—each the size of a large dog—were scuttling in a protective, synchronized swarm.

"Guard Rats," Wei said, his voice steady despite the chill running down his spine. "They’re forming a defensive array. They aren't just hungry; they’re protecting their source."

The Queen let out a screech that wasn't a sound, but a physical wave of pressure. Sarah stumbled back, clutching her ears, as the Qi in the air suddenly spiked, turning into a crushing weight.

"Miller! Left!" Wei shouted.

The swarm lunged. It was a wave of fur and teeth. Miller roared, his iron pipe sweeping through the air in a brutal, efficient arc. He didn't have Wei’s finesse, but he had the 'Status' of a man who had spent twenty years in the field. 

*Strength 19. Stamina 17.*

His pipe slammed into a guard rat, sending it flying across the platform. But for every one he hit, three more took its place. Tupi stepped forward, her eyes closed, her hand touching a tiny patch of moss growing in the crack of a support pillar.

"Great Mother," she whispered. "Feed on the shadow."

Suddenly, the moss exploded into a vibrant, emerald-green growth. It snaked across the floor, tangling the feet of the rats, its roots fueled by Tupi’s Spirit score. It bought them a second, but only a second.

The Queen lunged. She moved with a speed that defied her size, her heavy, clawed feet cracking the concrete of the platform. Wei met her halfway. He didn't use brute force—he couldn't. He was a 'Mortal' cultivator, a man of averages. He used the Queen’s own momentum.

He dived under her strike, his staff tapping her front leg with a pinpoint surge of Qi. It was a 'Vibration Strike,' a basic technique meant to disrupt the flow of energy. The Queen stumbled, her massive head slamming into a support beam, sending a shower of sparks from the dead fluorescent lights.

But as Wei rolled back to his feet, the Queen’s tail whipped around. It was a jagged, hairless whip tipped with a singular, obsidian-black talon.

Wei saw it coming. He twisted, his staff rising to parry, but the talon was too fast. It grazed his shoulder, cutting through the blue cotton of his sect robes and drawing a thin line of blood.

"Wei!" Sarah screamed.

Wei staggered back, his breath hitching. The cut wasn't deep, but he could feel the poison immediately. It wasn't a biological toxin; it was 'Qi-Rot'—corrupted, stagnant energy that began to eat at his meridians from the inside out. His vision flickered. His Status screen—the internal reflection he’d shared with the world—started to glitch, the numbers spinning wildly toward zero.

*Stamina 12... 8... 5...*

"I’ve got him!" Miller shouted, stepping in front of Wei and swinging his pipe like a madman to keep the swarm at bay. "Sarah! Do something!"

Sarah dropped her tablet. She looked at Wei, who was sinking to his knees, his face turning an ash-gray. She looked at the Queen, who was recovered and preparing for a final, crushing lunge. 

In that moment of absolute terror, Sarah didn't think about her career, her followers, or the absurdity of the world. She thought about the nurse in Chapter 5. She thought about the mother in Queens. She thought about the 'Spirit 12' she had seen in her own reflection this morning.

"Find the balance," she whispered, her voice cracking. 

She reached out, her hands hovering over Wei’s wound. She didn't know what she was doing, not really. She just knew that Wei was the center, and without him, the city would fall. She poured everything she had—every bit of her 'Spirit'—into the thought of *clarity*.

"Stop the rot," she commanded.

For a second, nothing happened. Then, a blindingly white light erupted from her palms. It wasn't the violet of the moon or the gold of the bodega; it was the pure, clinical white of a hospital at dawn. The light flooded into Wei’s shoulder, chasing the violet poison out of his veins.

Wei gasped, his lungs suddenly clear. His Status screen stabilized, then surged.

Beside Sarah, a message appeared in the air, so bright it was visible even to Jax’s cameras:

**CLASS EVOLUTION: ARCHETYPE UNLOCKED.**
**NAME: SARAH HIGGINS.**
**NEW CLASS: CLERIC (LVL 1).**
**ABILITY GAINED: PURIFYING WAVE.**

"Wei, get up!" Sarah yelled, her face glowing with a new, quiet authority.

Wei didn't need to be told twice. He felt the surge of Sarah’s healing Qi—pure, honest, and incredibly potent. He stood up, his staff spinning in a blur of motion.

"Final kata," Wei whispered. "The *Storm of the Outer Gate*."

He didn't hit the Queen. He hit the air. He moved his staff in a complex, recursive pattern, drawing all the ambient, chaotic Qi of the station into a singular point. The violet mist swirled into a vortex, centering on the tip of his oak staff.

The Queen lunged, her mouth open in a final, desperate screech.

Wei thrust the staff forward. The vortex of Qi exploded, a concentrated beam of purified energy that tore through the Queen’s chest, exiting out her back and shattering the tiles of the station wall behind her.

The Queen collapsed. She didn't die with a sound; she simply dissolved into a cloud of fine, lavender dust. The guard rats, their source of power gone, shrieked and scattered back into the dark tunnels, their lavender eyes fading to the dull gray of normal rodents.

Silence returned to 42nd Street.

"We... we did it," Miller panted, leaning on his pipe. 

Sarah sagged, the white light in her hands fading, her new 'Cleric' status feeling like a heavy, warm blanket over her soul. Tupi walked over, her moss-covered pillar already starting to wither now that the combat-Adrenaline had faded.

"Master?" Jax whispered. "Look."

In everyone’s vision—and on every screen across the planet—a message began to scroll in letters of liquid gold.

**WORLD FIRST: [RAT QUEEN] DEFEATED.**
**PARTY: THE MEDIOCRE MASTER & THE VANGUARD.**
**REWARD: GLOBAL QI-STABILIZATION (ZONE: NYC).**

The internet didn't just go wild; it shattered. The 'Rat Wars' feed exploded with billions of comments. 'Cleric Sarah' was trending within thirty seconds. 

But then, the golden letters faded, replaced by a cold, pulsing red.

**ALERT: LEVEL 1 THRESHOLD REACHED.**
**PLANETARY TUNING COMPLETE.**
**WARNING: DUNGEONS WILL START SPAWNING IN 30 DAYS.**
**PREPARE YOURSELVES. THE WELL IS DEEP.**

Wei looked at the message, then at his tattered blue robes, and finally at his small, exhausted group of disciples. 

"Thirty days," Wei said, his voice soft in the cavernous quiet of the subway.

"Master?" Tupi asked. "What does it mean?"

Wei looked into the dark tunnels, where the echoes of the Queen’s death were still fading.

"It means," Wei said, "that the rats were just the welcoming committee. It’s time to stop sweeping the floor, and start building some walls."

As they walked back toward the surface, leaving the lavender dust behind, Han Wei knew that the 'Mediocre Era' was over. The 'Dungeon Era' had begun. And thirty days was barely enough time to teach a city how to hold a stick.

But as he looked at Sarah—the world’s first Cleric—he knew they weren't alone anymore.

The school was officially in session.
