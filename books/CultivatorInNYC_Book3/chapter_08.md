# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 8: The Rise of the Guilds

The transition from "Rat Hunt" to "War Prep" happened faster than Han Wei had anticipated. In a city like New York, where every trend was amplified by a million screens and a billion opinions, the "Stick and Robe" era had lasted exactly three days.

Wei was back at the Hall of Supreme Flavor, a half-eaten sandwich sitting cooling on the table, while Sarah and Jax huddled over a flickering holographic projection. The atmosphere in the bodega was different now; the regulars weren't just drinking coffee and complaining about the MTA. They were comparing "Spirit Scores" and discussing the best ways to sharpen a piece of rebar.

"Look at this, Wei!" Sarah said, her voice sharp with a mix of excitement and apprehension. She flicked her hand, and the projection expanded, capturing the attention of everyone in the store. "It’s a new video from a group in New Jersey. They’re calling themselves the 'Dungeon Killers.' It has ten million views in two hours."

As they watched the feed, the screen stabilized on a man standing in what appeared to be a converted high-end fitness studio. He wasn't wearing tattered robes or a 'I Heart NY' t-shirt. He was decked out in a set of heavy, gambeson-style armor made of reinforced canvas and leather, with a series of interlocking steel plates guarding his shoulders and chest.

But it was his face that commanded attention. He looked like a man who had spent the better part of his life staring into a monitor, his eyes sharp and analytical, his posture surprisingly commander-like.

"My fellow citizens," the man began, his voice deep and modulated, "I am known as Krull. You might recognize me as the former top-ranked tank of *Azeroth’s End*. But today, the game has become the world, and I am the Guild Leader of the Dungeon Killers."

He reached behind his back and drew a weapon that made the bodega regulars gasp. It was a massive, two-handed broadsword, the steel blade etched with faint, geometric patterns that Wei recognized as low-grade Qi-conduits. It looked heavy, lethal, and devastatingly professional.

"The 'Mediocre Master' told you to pick up a stick," Krull said, his eyes fixed on the camera. "And he wasn't wrong. For the rats in your kitchen, a stick is fine. For the 'Basics' of breathing, a stick is fine. But I have been a gamer for twenty years. I have led raids through the worst virtual hells the human mind can imagine. And I am telling you—sticks and robes will not be enough when the Instances open in thirty days."

He stepped forward, the weight of his armor clanking with a rhythm of solid, industrialized intent. 

"We are not cultivators from a distant world," Krull continued. "We are New Yorkers. We are Jerseyites. We are technologists and builders. We have branches forming now in every borough. We have professional blacksmiths who have spent the last forty-eight hours converting the '3x Meritocracy' into the finest armor and weapons Earth has seen in a century. We have Bowyers making composite bows that can pierce an armored hide at two hundred yards. We are recruiting anyone who wants to defend our planet, not just as students, but as soldiers."

Krull raised his sword, the edge shimmering with a faint, disciplined white light. 

"The dungeons are coming. The 'Overflow' is a threat we cannot ignore. If you want to sit in a park and do breathing exercises, by all means, stay with the Master. But if you want to kill what’s in the dark before it kills your family... join us. The Dungeon Killers are the Vanguard of the New World."

The video cut to black, replaced by a recruitment link and a countdown timer synced to the one the world had seen at 42nd Street.

The silence in the bodega was palpable. Even Guzman had stopped slicing turkey, his eyes fixed on the spot where the projection had been. 

"Damn," Miller whispered, a professional glint in his eye. "He’s got standard-issue gear. And that sword... that’s not a piece of pipe, Wei. That’s been forged with Qi-stabilized forging techniques. I recognize those runes. They’re based on the ones the Scientific Cultivators were playing with."

Sarah looked at Wei, her expression worried. "Wei? He’s basically calling you obsolete. He’s telling people that your way is too slow for the 'Dungeon Era.'"

Wei sat back, his fingers tracing the edge of his patched blue sleeve. He didn't look angry. If anything, he looked... relieved.

"He's not wrong," Wei sighed, the sound a mix of exhaustion and a strange, humble pride. "He’s not wrong at all."

"What do you mean?" Tupi asked, her hand resting on her moss-totem.

"I am an Outer Disciple," Wei said, looking at his small group. "I know the basics. I know how to flow, and I know how to keep a heart from stopping. But I was never a general. I was never a blacksmith. Back in the Azure Cloud Sect, the soldiers—the true Martial Vanguard—didn't fight with brooms. They fought with spirit-steel and formations that could move mountains."

He looked at the screen where Krull’s stern face was still etched in the memory of the air.

"New York needs people who know how to build walls," Wei continued. "It needs people who know how to forge. Krull isn't a cultivator in the traditional sense, but he has the mind for it. He understands the 'Game' of the world—the strategy, the logistics, the brutal necessity of gear."

"But he’s splitting the world, Wei," Sarah countered. "It’s becomes 'The Mediocre Master' vs 'The Dungeon Killers.' People like teams. They like choosing sides."

"Let them choose," Wei said, standing up and picking up his staff. "If they want to be soldiers, they should go to Krull. If they want to be 'balanced,' they should stay here. Because when those dungeons open, we are going to need both. We are going to need more like him."

He walked to the window, looking out at the street. Already, he could see the change. A group of men were working at a nearby auto-shop, but they weren't fixing cars. They were using an industrial welder and a pile of scrap steel to fashion what looked like large, heavy shields. They were moving with the 3x speed, their hammers striking the metal with a rhythmic, thunderous cadence.

"They're taking his advice," Wei noted. "They’re building the armor. The Scientific Cultivators are providing the runes, and the 'Gamers' are providing the tactics."

"And what are we providing?" Jax asked, his camera-eye tilting up at Wei.

"The heart," Wei said quietly. "A set of armor can stop a tooth, Jax. But if the person inside the armor breaks under the Dissonance, the armor is just a fancy coffin. Krull will give them the swords. I’m going to make sure they’re still humans when they put them down."

He turned back to the bodega. "Guzman! I need more Spirit Rice and about fifty more staves. If the 'Dungeon Killers' are recruiting, then the 'Wei-Training Hall' needs to get serious. We aren't just sweeping anymore. We’re preparing for a raid."

As the bodega erupted into a flurry of renewed activity—Guzman shouting orders to his nephews, Miller sharpening his pipe with a fresh vigor, and Sarah checking the 'Cleric' forums for new healing techniques—Wei felt the familiar, heavy pulse of the world.

The "Dungeon Era" was indeed here. It was loud, it was violent, and it was being led by a man who treated reality like an MMO. 

*Azeroth’s End,* Wei thought, a small, tired smile on his lips. *I wonder if they have better pickles in that game.*

He walked out onto the sidewalk, the violet moon still hanging in the sky. He could hear the sound of hammers across the city. The guilds were rising. The world was arming itself. 

And for the first time in his long, mediocre life, Han Wei felt like he wasn't just a failure. He was the foundation. 

"Good luck, Krull," Wei whispered into the electric New York night. "I hope your blacksmiths are as good as you say. Because thirty days is a very short time to learn how to carry a sword you don't understand."

He set his jaw and began his nightly forms, the blue aura around him surging with a new, protective intensity. The war was coming. And the 'Mediocre Master' was going to make sure the city had a soul left to defend.
