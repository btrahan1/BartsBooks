# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 9: The Point of History

The violet mist over Manhattan seemed to pulse with a new, metallic tension. The sound of hammers from the burgeoning forge-halls of the 'Dungeon Killers' had become a constant, rhythmic backdrop to the city's heartbeat, but today, Central Park was quiet. 

Han Wei stood on the Sheep Meadow stage, the morning dew still dampening the grass. Beside him, a simple, weathered broom leaned against a support post. Jax was hovering higher than usual today, his wide-angle lens capturing not just Wei, but the thousands of people who had shown up with their own sticks and cardboard shields, all of them looking a little self-conscious after Krull’s "Dungeon Killer" broadcast.

"Yesterday," Wei began, his voice rolling across the meadow like a gentle tide, "you heard a man speak of spirit-steel and heavy armor. He spoke of the need for soldiers."

He picked up the broom. 

"Krull is a general. He looks at the battlefield and sees the need for walls. He is right to do so. But a wall is useless if the person behind it doesn't know why it was built."

Wei held the broom horizontal. With a sudden, surgical snap of his wrist, he broke the straw head off the handle. The wood splintered with a sharp *crack*, leaving a jagged, uneven end on the long oak shaft. 

"Man's first weapon was a rock," Wei said, his fingers tracing the rough break. "In your stories, a brother used it to kill his brother. It was a weapon of desperation, of proximity. It was the birth of violence, but it was a clumsy birth."

He closed his eyes. A faint, cerulean glow began to gather around his fingertips—not the explosive white of Sarah’s healing or the lavender fire of the Rat Queen, but a steady, focused aura. He gripped the jagged end of the broomstick and began to slide his hand along the wood.

Under his touch, the wood didn't just splinter; it *compressed*. The cellulose fibers groaned as they were rewritten by Wei’s intent. The jagged mess smoothed out, elongating and tapering into a wickedly sharp, needle-like point. It wasn't just carved; it was forged. The tip of the oak glowed with a dull, blue heat before settling into a finish that looked more like polished stone than wood.

"But when fighting beasts," Wei continued, holding up the now-lethal spear, "a rock is not a great weapon. A beast has longer teeth, sharper claws, and a faster reflex. To a beast, a man with a rock is just a very slow, very loud snack."

He thrust the spear forward, the tip whistling through the thin air.

"And so, man picked up a stick. He created distance. He learned that he didn't have to be stronger than the beast if he could simply be further away from its teeth than it was from his point. A stick can pierce hide if the will behind it is sharp enough. Man invented the spear, and from that point on, the beasts of the ancient world trembled. The spear made us the masters of the wild."

Wei began to move. It wasn't the slow, expansive flow of the previous day’s kata. It was a series of sharp, linear strikes—thrusts, parries, and quick, low stabs. The spear moved like a living thing, an extension of his own reach.

"The spear is a simple weapon," Wei said, his breath timed perfectly with every strike. "It is a weapon of the common man. It doesn't require the months of training a sword demands, nor the massive Strength score needed to swing Krull’s broadsword. But it is devastating. A single spearman can hold a hallway. A group of spearmen... they can hold a world."

He stopped, the tip of his spear resting precisely a hair’s breadth from Jax’s lens.

"You have a story in this world," Wei said, a small, knowing smile touching his lips. "A story of three hundred men. You made a movie of it—lots of yelling, lots of slow-motion blood. But the truth of that story wasn't in their muscles or their shouting. It was in their formation."

He gestured to Miller and Sarah, who stepped forward holding two of the heavy, scrap-steel shields they’d helped the local shop owners fashion earlier that morning. 

"Miller, Sarah, lock."

They stepped together, the shields overlapping with a heavy, metallic *clank*. They formed a wall of steel, their eyes visible just over the top edges. Wei stepped behind them, sliding his spear through the small gap between the shields.

"This is the *Phalanx of the Outer Gate*," Wei said. "A spear and a shield. Alone, you are a target. Together, you are an island. If Krull gives you the sword, he gives you the power to kill a monster. If I give you the spear and the person next to you, I give you the power to survive the dungeon."

He leaned into the formation, his shoulder against Miller’s back, his spear-point steady.

"The Rat Queen fell because we worked as one," Wei stated. "Sarah found the balance between her fear and her heart. Miller found the distance between his pipe and the swarm. That is the lesson for today."

"Pair up!" Wei commanded, his voice rising in a sharp, military snap. "If you have a stick, sharpen your intent. If you have a friend, become their shield. We are not just training our bodies for the Status screen. We are training our city to be a wall that breathes."

Across the meadow, the thousands began to move. It was awkward at first—people bumping into each other, shields clashing inconsistently, broomsticks being waved like batons. But then, it began to settle. The sound of "Status" being whispered filled the air, not as a cry for ego, but as a check of resources.

*Strength 10. Stamina 11. Spirit 9.*

"Look at them," Sarah whispered, her eyes glowing with her Cleric light. "They’re not just chasing numbers anymore. They’re looking at each other."

"They have to," Wei said. "Because in twenty-eight days, a 'World First' announcement won't matter if there’s no one left to read it."

He stepped off the stage and walked into the crowd, correcting a grip here, adjusting a stance there. He wasn't the most powerful cultivator on Earth—Krull probably had him beat in raw physical stats by now, and the Scientific Cultivators certainly had more 'Knowledge'—but Han Wei knew the weight of a spear. He knew the comfort of a shield. And he knew that New York didn't need a god.

It needed a phalanx.

"Master," Jax whispered as he followed. "The viewership is holding steady at forty-five million. And interestingly, the most-used emoji in the chat right now is a spear. Also, a historical society in Greece just sent a donation of ten thousand 'Spirit-Enhanced' olives. They say, 'For the New Spartans.'"

Wei laughed, a genuine, warm sound. "Tell them we’ll take the olives. But tell them we’re not Spartans. We’re just New Yorkers who are tired of being eaten by rats."

As the morning sun bleached the violet out of the sky, the Sheep Meadow was no longer just a park. It was a training ground. A sprawling, chaotic, incredibly loud school where the 'Basics' were being forged into a new kind of modern legend.

"Distance," Wei repeated to a group of nervous-looking accountants. "Distance is your friend. The beast has to come to you. Let the point do the work."

The accountant nodded, his grip on his sharpened mop-handle tightening, his Status screen flickering with a subtle, 3x gain in *Focus*. 

Wei smiled. It was a start. A mediocre, simple, incredibly stubborn start. 

And as the hammers of the 'Dungeon Killers' continued to ring in the distance, Han Wei knew that when the first Instance opened, the world wouldn't find a city in panic. It would find a city standing shoulder-to-shoulder, spear-points leveled, waiting for the dark.

"Phalanx," Wei whispered, his own spear held steady. "Welcome to the new world."
