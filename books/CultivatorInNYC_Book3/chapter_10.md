# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 10: The Great Extermination

The viral spirit of Sparta had colonized New York City faster than the violet mist itself. 

#ForSparta and #ForNYC were trending across every platform, a digital war cry that had turned the city's collective fear into a disciplined, if somewhat amateur, military machine. The sewers of Manhattan, once the exclusive domain of urban legends and the Rat Queen’s fading influence, were now the front lines of what the internet was calling 'The Great Extermination.'

Han Wei sat in the back of the Hall of Supreme Flavor, watching a grid of twelve different livestreams on his tablet. Jax was hovering over his shoulder, adding a real-time data overlay that tracked the movements of the various 'adventurer' groups scouring the subterranean tunnels.

"It’s no longer a battle, Master," Jax chirped, his red eye blinking with excitement. "It has become a systematic clearing operation. Look at Sector 7—the Lower East Side."

On the screen, a group of twenty people were moving through a large interceptor tunnel. They weren't just a mob with sticks anymore. They were a unit. 

At the front were the spearmen—mostly construction workers and off-duty security guards. They held their sharpened broomsticks and rebar-spears in a tight row, their scrap-steel shields overlapping perfectly. They moved with a slow, heavy rhythm, their boots splashing in the shallow water as one.

"Contact!" the leader shouted, a man with a *Strength 14* badge pinned to his tactical vest.

A dozen rats, their eyes glowing lavender, lunged from the shadows of a side-pipe. But they didn't hit a wall of meat; they hit a wall of points. The spearmen didn't break formation. They simply stepped into a low lunge, their spears flashing out in synchronized thrusts. The rats were impaled before they could even get within biting distance.

"Man’s first weapon," Wei whispered, a ghost of a smile on his lips. "They’re learning the distance."

But it didn't stop there. Behind the wall of spears was a second line of bowmen—mostly former archers from the city’s archery clubs and hobbyists who had spent the last forty-eight hours making high-tension composite bows from recycled plastics and Qi-stabilized wood. Their arrows hissed through the air over the heads of the spearmen, picking off the larger rats at the back of the swarm with lethal precision.

And behind the bowmen, standing in a protective pocket of space, was a young woman in a NYU sweatshirt. Her eyes were closed, her brow furrowed in intense concentration. 

"Flicker," she whispered.

A tiny, marble-sized orb of blue energy formed between her fingertips—a mana bolt. It wasn't the roaring dragon of a high-tier cultivator or the purifying wave of Sarah’s Cleric class. It was weak, its light flickering like a dying bulb. But she pointed her finger, and the bolt shot forward, striking a particularly resistant rat in the eye with a sharp *zip*.

The rat didn't explode, but it staggered, blinded long enough for a spearman to finish it.

"Mana bolts," Sarah noted, sitting beside Wei. She was wearing her own 'Cleric' insignia now—a white cross over a blue circle. "They’re starting to unlock the *Arcane Spark* archetype. It’s inefficient, but it’s effective. It gives them a range that doesn't rely on ammunition."

"It’s the scientific method applied to the soul," Wei said, nodding. "They’re figuring out the 'Standardized Bolt.' It’s the first step toward true Essence manipulation."

The video feed showed one of the spearmen taking a grazing bite to the arm. He didn't panic. He simply shouted, "FALLING BACK!" and stepped out of the line. The person behind him immediately stepped up, closing the gap with their shield before the rats could exploit it.

Nearby, a medic in a reflective vest—a 'Nurse' class—immediately began wrapping the wound. Her hands glowed with that familiar, golden Intent. 

"Stay steady, Mike," she said. "The rot hasn't set. I’ve caught it."

The man was bandaged and ready to rotate back in within two minutes. It was working. The chaos of the first few days had been replaced by a grim, industrial proficiency.

"But look at the other side of the park, Master," Jax said, switching the main feed to the Upper West Side. 

This was the 'Dungeon Killer' territory. Here, the tactics were different. There were no broomsticks or rebar spears. Instead, there were the ironclad swordsmen Krull had promised. They were wearing full plate-and-chain mail, some of it looking like it had been pulled straight from a museum and reinforced with modern welding.

They didn't move in a phalanx. They moved in 'Combat Cells.' Three swordsmen, one heavy-shield bearer, and a designated 'Executer.' They were faster, more aggressive. They didn't wait for the rats; they hunted them. They used their heavy broadswords to cleave through the swarms in a whirlwind of steel and grit.

"They’re making more progress in terms of raw numbers," Miller noted, coming in from the morning training session, his pipe resting on his shoulder. "Krull’s boys are clearing a thousand rats an hour. They’re calling it 'Grinding.' They treat the sewers like a level-up zone."

"Hopefully it will be enough," Wei said, his gaze fixed on the violet mist outside. "The rats are the surface. The 'Instances' will be deeper, and the 'Overflow' won't just be animals."

"Master, the internet is already debating which side is better," Jax said, showing a series of viral polls. "The 'Spartan Phalanx' vs 'The Iron Vanguard.' People like the spears because they’re 'For the People,' but they’re buying Krull’s gear because it looks 'Pro.'"

"It’s not a competition," Wei sighed. "But New Yorkers will make it one. At least it keeps them training."

He stood up, looking at his own small group. Miller, the heavy-hitter. Sarah, the Cleric. Tupi, the Spirit-Natural. And Jax, the Digital Sage. They were a bizarre sect, a 'Mediocre Array' that would have been laughed out of any mountain temple in his home world.

But here? In a city that was learning to fight its own shadows with broomsticks and scrap metal? They were the blueprints.

"Master, the 42nd Street 'Deep Scan' is complete," Jax reported, his voice dropping to a whisper. "The 'Dissonance' is stabilizing. The first Dungeon is taking shape under Grand Central. And it’s not a rat nest."

Wei’s expression sharpened. "What is it?"

"My sensors are picking up a structural shift," Jax said. "A pattern of 'Regal Intent.' It’s not biological, Master. It’s... architectural. It’s like the terminal is rebuilding itself into something else."

"The Gilded Labyrinth," Wei muttered, remembering the name from his own internal resonance. "It’s starting early."

He picked up his staff, the blue aura around him humming with a new, urgent frequency. 

"The Extermination is a good start," Wei said, "but it’s a distraction. The rats were trying to keep us out of the sewers because they knew the real threat was already rooting."

He turned to his team. "Miller, tell the 'Spartan' groups to stay in the upper levels. They’re effective against swarms, but they’re not ready for a dimensional fold. Sarah, I need you to coordinate with the medics in the guilds. We need a 'Clearance Protocol' for when the first delvers come out of the Instance."

"And us?" Miller asked.

Wei looked at the blue point of his broomstick-spear. 

"We’re going to Grand Central," Wei said. "Before the 'Dungeon Killers' try to tank a Labyrinth they don't understand, and before the city finds out what an 'Instance Boss' really looks like."

As they stepped out of the bodega, the cry of "FOR SPARTA! FOR NYC!" echoed from a nearby subway entrance. It was a sound of hope, of defiance. 

But as Han Wei looked toward the golden roof of Grand Central Terminal, he knew that the 'Extermination' was over. The 'Delving' was about to begin. 

And in a world of spears and iron mail, the only thing that would matter in the Labyrinth was who had the balance to step into the dark without losing their soul.

"Let’s go," Wei said. "The Labyrinth is calling."
