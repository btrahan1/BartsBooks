# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 12: The Trial Run

The amber light of the Gilded Labyrinth didn't just illuminate; it felt like it was reading their thoughts. 

As Han Wei stepped through the threshold, the sounds of Grand Central—the humming Qi, Krull’s clanking armor, even Jax’s whirring fans—cut off as if a door had been slammed shut. They were standing in a corridor that looked like it had been carved from a single, massive block of citrine. The air smelled of ozone, old books, and a strange, metallic tang that Sarah immediately identified as "Wrong."

"Master, the Dissonance is... vibrant," Jax whispered, his red eye pulsing with a frantic cadence. "My internal compass is spinning. We aren't in Manhattan anymore. We’re in a derivation."

"Stay in the diamond," Wei commanded, his voice low and resonant. He held his staff vertical, the blue aura around it casting long, steady shadows against the glowing walls. "Miller, watch the shadows on the ceiling. Tupi, how's the fern?"

Tupi looked down at the potted plant in her sling. The leaves were vibrant, but they were twitching, swaying toward their left. "It’s leaning toward the 'Weight,' Master. There’s something heavy in the next room."

They moved forward, their boots making no sound on the translucent floor. They hadn't gone fifty yards before the first 'mobs' emerged.

They weren't rats. They were *Shards*—constructs made of the same citrine-like material as the walls, shaped into the jagged forms of hunting hounds. They didn't growl; they made a sound like glass grinding on glass.

"Contact!" Miller barked, his iron pipe coming off his shoulder. 

The Shards lunged with a terrifying, jerky speed. The three Spartans immediately locked their shields, the metal clanging against the crystalline hides of the beasts. It wasn't an easy fight. The spears of the Spartans, so effective against soft-bodied rats, skidded off the Shards' facets unless the thrust was perfectly perpendicular.

"Aim for the joints!" Wei shouted, his staff blurring as he stepped outside the formation to parry a Shard that had tried to leap over the shield wall. He didn't shatter it; he simply tapped it, redirecting its force into the wall.

Sarah stepped forward, her hands glowing with a soft, restorative light. "Miller, your Stamina is dropping! You’re fighting the air as much as the beasts!"

Indeed, the air itself was exhausting. Every breath felt like inhaling fine sand. 

After ten minutes of brutal, grinding combat, the four Shards lay shattered on the floor, their forms dissolving into fine, lavender dust that smelled faintly of static.

"Is that... it?" Miller panted, leaning on his pipe. He was sweating, his face flushed. "Those things were... solid."

"Those were just the antibodies," Wei said, his eyes scanning the corridor ahead. "The Labyrinth hasn't even noticed we’re here yet. That was just a reflex."

They continued deeper, the architecture becoming more ornate, more 'Regal.' The corridor opened into a vast chamber that resembled the main terminal, but the arches were made of white bone and the ceiling was a swirling vortex of molten gold.

Suddenly, Wei stopped. His skin pricked with a sudden, violent heat. 

"STAY BACK!" he roared.

He didn't wait for them to move. He slammed his staff into the floor and threw his arms wide. The blue aura around him didn't just glow; it expanded, forming a shimmering, dome-like shield that encapsulated the entire group just as the floor tiles beneath them flipped.

A roar of white-hot flame erupted from the vents below. It wasn't a normal fire; it was 'Corrupted Essence'—a liquid heat that burned with a screaming, high-pitched frequency. It slammed into Wei’s shield with the force of a tidal wave.

Wei’s teeth were gritted, his face turning a deep, pained red as he held the barrier. The heat was so intense that Sarah’s NYU sweatshirt began to singe, and Tupi’s fern let out a visible puff of steam.

"Hold... the heart!" Wei gasped, his knees buckling under the pressure of the flame.

After five agonizing seconds, the vents slammed shut. The flame vanished, leaving the chamber in a shimmering, heat-distorted haze.

Wei collapsed his shield and slumped against his staff, his breath coming in ragged gasps. His robes were charred at the edges, and his eyebrows were singed, but he was alive.

"Master!" Sarah cried, running to him. She didn't wait for permission; she poured a Purifying Wave into his chest, her hands shaking. "You... you blocked it. All of it."

"It was a trap," Miller whispered, looking at the floor tiles that had reset themselves as if nothing had happened. "If you hadn't seen that, Wei... we’d be charcoal."

Wei took a long, shaky breath and stood up. He looked around the silent, gilded chamber with a new, grim clarity. 

"We leave," Wei said. "Now."

"But Master—" Tupi started.

"Now," Wei repeated. "We have the feeling of it. Any deeper, and I won't be able to hold the next one."

The exit trek was just as tense, but they finally burst out of the amber gateway into the main concourse of Grand Central. The sound of the city hit them like a physical blow—the sirens, the shouting, the wonderful, mundane noise of New York.

They were singed, soot-streaked, and smelling of burnt hair. Krull and his Dungeon Killers were still there, watching them as they stumbled out. 

"Back so soon?" Krull asked, though there was no mockery in his voice. He saw the state of Wei’s robes. "Let me guess. The Fire Room?"

"The Flame of Corrupted Essence," Wei corrected, his voice steadying. He looked at Krull, then at the thousands of people watching the live feed through Jax’s lens.

"It is as I feared," Wei said, his voice carrying through the terminal. "The Gilded Labyrinth is a deathtrap for the unprepared. The Qi inside is not just dense; it is corrupted. It will eat your mind before it eats your body."

He looked at Sarah, then at the crowd.

"Most groups will need more than just soldiers," Wei continued. "To survive a place like that, you will need a 'Rogue' of sorts—someone with a high enough Spirit and Agility to detect the traps before they trigger. And you will need Clerics or Paladins—not just to heal, but to purify the very air you breathe. Without a way to deal with the Corrupted Qi, your heart will fail long before your armor does."

Krull narrowed his eyes, his hands tightening on his broadsword. "Rogues and Paladins. You’re talking about specialized classes, Wei. Most people haven't even unlocked their Status yet."

"Then they should stay out," Wei said bluntly. "But there is hope. Each exposure, each room survived, helps you resist the corruption. Your body learns the 'flavor' of the Labyrinth’s malice. This is a marathon, Krull. Not a sprint. If you try to power-level through the center in one go, you will become part of the citrine walls."

He looked at his own group—tired, singed, but their eyes were wide with the kind of hard-won experience that no park-kata could provide.

"We go home," Wei said. "We wash, we rest, and we train for the specific. The 'Basics' got us through the door. But it will be our specialized 'Hearts' that get us to the center."

As they walked out of the terminal, the internet was already ablaze with the news. 'The Fire Trap' was trending. People were already arguing over who had the 'Rogue' potential. A group of parkour enthusiasts in Brooklyn were already claiming they were the world's first 'Rogues.'

Wei didn't care about the trends. He just felt the singed edge of his sleeve and the steady, slightly faster beat of his own heart. 

The Labyrinth was a monster, yes. But it was a teacher too. And today, New York had learned its first hard lesson about what it really meant to 'Instance.'

"Master," Jax whispered as they reached the fresh air of 42nd Street. "The Scientific Cultivators are waiting at the bodega. They say their 'Essence Engines' just blew a gasket, and they heard you know how to handle 'Trash Grade' fuel."

Wei smiled through the soot on his face. "Of course they are. Tell them to wait. I need a shower and a very large pickle first."

As they headed toward the subway, Han Wei looked back at the golden glow of the terminal. The Gilded Labyrinth was waiting. The thirty days were still counting down. But for the first time, Earth wasn't just guessing. 

They knew exactly how hot the fire could be.
