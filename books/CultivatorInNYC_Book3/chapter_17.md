# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 17: The Haunted Training

The plum-colored sky over Manhattan was no longer a symbol of doom. To the people of New York, it had become a theatrical spotlight, illuminating a city that was rapidly turning its most eccentric hobbies into a wartime industry.

Han Wei stood in the back of the warehouse in Long Island City, his eyes fixed on the massive projection screen Jax had patched into the global feed. The hashtag #RunMyDungeon wasn't just trending; it was a digital wildfire, consuming every scrap of bandwidth the 'Scientific Cultivators' had been able to stabilize.

"Master! Look at this!" Jax’s voice was a frantic, synthesized chirp. "It’s not just the Rogues anymore. The 'Perfect Groups' are signing up for private slots!"

Wei leaned forward, his soot-streaked face reflecting the flickering light of a video from New Jersey. A man with a megaphone, standing in front of a massive, barn-like structure, was addresssing a group of nervous-looking adventurers.

"Welcome to 'Blood-Moon Manor'!" the man shouted. "In October, we’re the number one haunted house in the Tri-State area. But today? Today we are a sanctioned Level 1 Training Instance! We’ve got retractable traps, hidden pressure plates, and thirty-five professional 'Stalkers' wearing Qi-reactive makeup! If you can reach the 'Grand Prize' in the attic without losing your 'Health-Ribbon,' your entry fee is refunded!"

Wei watched as a 'Perfect Group'—two spearmen, a rogue in a hoodie, and a frantic-looking teen with a glowing NYU sweatshirt—disappeared into the dark maw of the manor.

"Haunted house builders," Wei murmured, a slow, appreciative smile spreading across his face. "Of course. They spend their lives studying how to scare people, how to funnel them into traps, and how to hide in plain sight. They’ve been practicing for the Gilded Labyrinth for decades."

"And look at this one, Master," Jax said, switching to a feed from Coney Island. 

The iconic House of Mirrors had been completely overhauled. Instead of simple glass and distorted reflections, the interior was now a maze of 'Dissonance-Panels' provided by Thorne’s team. 

"Step right up!" a man in a striped vest cried out. "The Labyrinth of Infinite Reflections! We’ve got mercury-simulators, refraction-traps, and we’ve even hired the guy who built the 'Labyrinth' set for that eighties movie! If you can find the center without shattering your sanity, you’re ready for the 42nd Street Gate!"

Wei watched a group of 'Vanguard' soldiers from Krull’s guild—clinking in their heavy plate mail—immediately get separated by a series of shifting mirrors. Their heavy swords were useless against their own reflections, and their 'Rogue' was frantically tapping on a glass wall that looked identical to every other wall in the room.

"NYC never ceases to amaze me," Wei said, shaking his head. "In my world, a training ground was a sacred site, built by masters over centuries of meditation. Here? You just hire the guys who build the Halloween sets and the carnival barkers."

"It’s efficient, Master," Sarah said, joining him. She was carrying a tray of coffee, her 'Cleric' aura glowing with a soft, steady warmth. "The haunted house actors are getting a 'Spirit-Boost' from the fear and excitement of the adventurers. They’re practically unlocking a 'Stalker' or 'Illusionist' sub-class. And the adventurers? They’re learning that a dungeon isn't just about swinging a sword. It’s about not panicking when the lights go out."

Miller walked in, a massive grin on his face. He was carrying a newspaper—one of the few still being printed on Qi-stabilized presses. "Wei, you see the headlines? 'The Bronx Scabbard'—a group of retired metal-workers—have built a series of 'Mechanical Rats' for the Phalanxes to practice on. They use recycled batteries and magnets. If the spear doesn't hit the 'Core,' the rat just bounces back."

Wei laughed, a deep, resonant sound. "Guerrilla-Cultivation. They are building a world out of spare parts and carnival tricks."

He walked over to the back of the warehouse, where Rogue Spider was checking a set of 'Magi-Tech picks' Thorne had just delivered.

"Spider," Wei called out. "Have you seen the Coney Island feed?"

The man with the web tattoo looked up, a competitive glint in his eye. "The House of Mirrors? Yeah. Krull’s boys are currently stuck in the 'Third Reflection.' They’ve been in there for two hours and they’re still arguing over which way is North."

"Go there tonight," Wei commanded. "But don't go as an adventurer. Go as a 'Shadow.' Navigate the maze without touching a single mirror. And when you reach the center, leave a 'GOTTEN' note on the Barkar’s podium."

Spider grinned, his silvery aura flickering with excitement. "Consider it done, Master. I’ve always wanted to see if I could out-trick a carny."

"And Sarah," Wei turned to the young woman. "Coordinate with the haunted house owners. Tell them to add a 'Sanitizing' element to their traps. If they can simulate the Corrupted Essence with light-frequency, we can train the Clerics to purify the air before the healers are even needed."

"On it," Sarah said, her fingers already flying across her tablet. "I’ll call it the 'Lavender Frequency.' It’ll smell like soap and keep the 'mobs' from getting too aggressive."

Han Wei walked out onto the balcony of the warehouse, looking toward the Manhattan skyline. The violet mist was thin today, and the plum-colored sun was setting behind the Empire State Building. 

He could feel the city breathing. It was a chaotic, loud, and incredibly creative breath. 

Two weeks. 

In fourteen days, the Well would overflow. The Gilded Labyrinth would open its final gate, and dozen of other 'Instances' would tear through the fabric of reality across the five boroughs. 

But as Wei looked at a nearby rooftop, where a group of 'Perfect Group' hopefuls were practicing their coordination around a makeshift 'fire-pit' built by a local welder, he felt a strange, new confidence.

In his old world, he was a failure because he couldn't master the perfect forms of the past. 

But here? In a world that turned 'House of Mirrors' into training grounds and 'Haunted Houses' into dungeons? 

His 'Mediocre' basics were exactly the foundation this beautiful, crazy city needed to build its own future.

"New York," Wei whispered, the words a blessing and a promise. "If you can survive a Coney Island summer, you can survive a Labyrinth."

He picked up his staff, the blue aura around it humming with a steady, anticipatory frequency. He began a new kata—one that incorporated the jerky, unpredictable rhythm of the city’s heart. 

He wasn't just training himself anymore. He was training with the city. 

"Jax," Wei said, his staff blurring into a series of defensive arcs. "Tell the 'Scientific Cultivators' I want a progress report on the 'Trash Grade' stones. We’re going to need them to power those 'Mechanical Rats' for the Bronx Phalanxes."

"Understood, Master," Jax chirped. "The 'Magi-Tech Development' channel is humming. Thorne says he’s found a way to use old subway tokens as 'Essence Fuses.' He’s calling it the 'Legacy Hack'."

Wei smiled, his staff coming to a sharp, silent halt. 

"Perfect. The old world protecting the new. Let’s get to work."

As the first lights of the city began to flicker on—some orange-gold, some the new, vibrant cerulean—the world of Earth became a little less like a victim and a little more like a player. 

The #RunMyDungeon movement was just the beginning. The city was building its own 'Instance.' And when the monsters finally arrived, they were going to find out that New Yorkers had been practicing for 'The End' every Halloween since 1970.

"Showtime," Wei muttered, looking at the distant, golden glow of Grand Central. "Let's see who’s really ready to perform."
