# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 19: The Raid Group

The Jacob K. Javits Convention Center, once a place for auto shows and comic-cons, had been transformed into something the internet had dubbed 'Hell on Earth.' 

The 1.8 million square feet of glass and steel had been partitioned into a nightmare of shifting walls, strobe-lit corridors, and Magi-Tech traps. But it wasn't the architecture that made it terrifying; it was the 'Mobs.' Hundreds of volunteers—dressed in padding and carrying Qi-neutralized batons—were currently acting as rat-swarms, Shards, and mercury-gargoyles.

In the center of the main hall, Han Wei stood on a raised platform, his eyes closed, listening to the synchronized breathing of the twenty people below him. 

"Again!" Wei’s voice was a whip-crack, resonating with a frequency that bypassed the ears and hit the core.

Below him, the Raid Group moved as a single, multi-faceted organism. They were divided into three six-man teams, each a 'Perfect Group' unto itself, with Han Wei and Sarah acting as the 'Floaters'—the mobile elements that filled the gaps and provided the ultimate stabilization.

Team Alpha, led by Miller, was the anvil. They consisted of Miller, two of the heaviest Spartans with re-enforced shields, a Rogue for trap-sensing, an Archer for suppression, and a Medic from the downtown trauma center. They moved with a slow, crushing weight, Miller’s iron pipe clearing a path that the others occupied instantly.

Team Beta was the scalpel, led by Rogue Spider. He had two Spartans for protection, but his team focused on mobility—two light Archers and an Arcane Spark from NYU who could now throw 'Flicker-Nets' to tangle the mobs. They moved through the shadows of the Javits Center like smoke, appearing only to strike and then vanishing before the 'mobs' could retaliate.

Team Gamma was the experimental unit, led by Tupi. She had her spirit-fern in its sling, her hand constantly brushing its leaves to sense the 'Intent-Flow' of the room. Her team included two Spartans, another Rogue, an Archer, and a second Arcane Spark. They didn't prioritize force or stealth; they prioritized 'Resonance.' They moved where the Qi was thin, avoiding traps before they even saw them.

"Contact! West Flank!" Miller roared.

A 'Swell' of fifty volunteers—acting as a rat-swarm—burst from behind a stack of shipping containers. 

Team Alpha didn't even slow down. Miller stepped forward, his pipe letting out a low, resonant hum as he slammed it into the floor. The 'vibration'—a neutralized wave of Wei’s own Intent—staggered the swarm. Instantly, the Spartans locked their shields, and the Archer unleashed a volley of padded arrows that 'tagged' the leading rats.

"Alpha holding!" Miller shouted.

"Beta flanking!" Spider’s voice came from the rafters. 

Two seconds later, a series of Flicker-Nets descended from the ceiling, tangling the rear of the swarm. The Arcane Spark didn't just throw bolts; she threw 'Static-Waves' that made the volunteers' hair stand on end, a harmless but effective deterrent.

"Gamma, bridge the gap!" Wei commanded from his platform.

Tupi’s team moved into the center of the hall, their Rogue disarming a 'Fire-Trap' (a harmless smoke-machine tuned to high-frequency) with a single, precise click of his picks. Tupi raised her hand, and the spirit-fern glowed with a soft, green light that seemed to calm the chaotic 'Dissonance' of the simulated dungeon.

"Floaters in!" Wei shouted.

He didn't walk down the stairs. He became a blur of blue light, appearing in the center of the fray. He didn't hit anyone; he simply moved between the 'mobs' and the group, his staff creating a series of soft, repelling pulses that guided the teams into a tighter, more efficient formation.

Sarah was right behind him, her hands glowing with a soft, golden aura. She didn't heal wounds today—there were none to heal. Instead, she 'Purified' the simulated-exhaustion of the teams, her presence acting like a fresh breeze in the stale, sweaty air of the center.

"Settle! Form the Phalanx of the Outer Gate!" Wei barked.

The three teams merged. Alpha formed the front wall, Beta took the flanks, and Gamma protected the rear. They were a twenty-man circle of steel, intent, and magic.

The fifty volunteers slammed into the circle and bounced off like water hitting a rock. The coordination was absolute. When a Spartan in Team Alpha shifted to catch his breath, a Rogue from Beta immediately stepped into the gap, distracting the 'mobs' until the shield was reset.

"Clear!" Miller bellowed as the last volunteer 'rat' was tagged and sat down.

Wei stood in the center of the huddle, his breathing perfectly calm while the others were gasping for air. 

"Systematic," Wei said, nodding his approval. "That was the North Sector simulation. Total time: four minutes. Casualties: zero. Intent-Waste: minimal."

He looked at each of them—the best of the best. New York’s first True Raiders. Miller, Sarah, Tupi, Spider, the Spartans, the Archers, the Sparks. 

"It was perfect," Miller panted, a wide grin on his soot-streaked face. "Wei, I’ve been playing RPGs for twenty years, but this... feeling the shield-thud and the mana-spark in my ears... it’s better than any screen."

"It’s real, Miller," Wei said quietly. "And in three days, it won't be volunteers with padding. It will be the Gilded Labyrinth. And the Level 4 'Regal' mobs won't sit down when they’re tagged."

He walked the circle, touching each team leader on the shoulder. 

"You have the 'Perfect Groups.' You have the 'Raid Structure.' And most importantly, you have each other’s Intent. You are no longer twenty individuals. You are the 'Sect of the Five Boroughs'."

"Master," Jax chirped, hovering over the group. "The Javits Center 'Sim-Session' has reached its conclusion. The volunteers are asking if they can get 'Participation Credits' toward their own Status levels."

Wei laughed. "Tell them to report to Sarah. She will give them a 'Breath-Stabilization' blessing. It will help them unlock their own Statuses faster."

As the teams began to break down their gear, checking their Magi-Tech fuses and sharpening their re-enforced spears, Han Wei looked at the massive, empty hall. Three days. Seventy-two hours until the 250 nodes ignited and the Gilded Labyrinth became the focal point of Earth’s transformation.

"We go home," Wei said. "We sleep for twelve hours. No training. No live-streaming. Just rest. Your hearts need the silence before the storm."

Spider looked at the vault door they had just bypassed. "Master, do you think Krull’s boys are doing the same? I heard they’re trying to 'brute-force' a Level 2 node in the Bronx today."

"Krull is training his way," Wei said. "We are training ours. The Labyrinth will be the final judge. But tonight, we don't worry about Krull. We worry about our own balance."

As the Raid Group filtered out of the Javits Center—walking into a city that was now lit by two hundred and fifty pillars of emerald, indigo, and gold—they didn't look like heroes from a movie. They looked like specialized urban professionals. 

The Rogue with his silenced boots. The Spartan with his scrap-steel shield. The Arcane Spark with her flickering fingertips.

They were the first generation of Delvers. And as Han Wei watched them disappear into the night, he felt the heavy, thrumming resonance of the Gilded Labyrinth calling to him. 

"Seventy-two hours," Wei whispered, his staff tapping a rhythmic count on the pavement. "Let’s see if New York is ready to clear the floor."

The preparation was over. The 'Perfect Group' was forged. And the Level 4 Instance was waiting for its masters.
