# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 23: The Engine Room Goblins

The descent from the first floor didn't feel like moving down a staircase. It felt like sinking into a heavy, oily marsh that happened to be made of shadows and glass. 

As the Raid Group reached the bottom of the emerald glass spiral, the air changed. The ozone of the clockwork hall was gone, replaced by the thick, suffocating scent of diesel, hot iron, and ancient, subterranean grime. 

Han Wei stepped onto the floor of the second level, and his boots immediately slid an inch. The surface wasn't obsidian here; it was rusted iron plate, slick with a black, viscous residue that hummed with a low-frequency malice.

"Woah," Miller whispered, his iron pipe glowing a dim, wary red. "Is this even the same dungeon? It looks like... well, it looks like the bowels of the 7 trains if they hadn't been cleaned since 1920."

The environment was a dizzying maze of steam-pipes, coal-chutes, and vibrating boilers that stretched upward into a ceiling dripping with black condensation. The golden pillars of light from the floor above were gone, replaced by the flickering, orange-red glow of furnace-mouths that seemed to breathe with a heavy, wet rhythm.

"It’s a different harmonic," Sarah said, her tablet screen covered in digital grease. "The first floor was the 'Brain'—the logic and the timing. This floor... this is the 'Heart'. The raw, dirty energy that keeps the Labyrinth alive. The Scientific Cultivators are calling it the 'Engine Room'."

Jax hovered close to Wei, his camera lens clicking as it struggled to adjust to the low light. "Master, the viewers are confused. They were expecting more 'Gilded' and they got 'Steam-Punk Nightmare'. The chat is full of people asking if we just walked into a sewer."

"It’s not a sewer," Wei said, his eyes scanning the pipe-work. "It’s the Dross. Every dungeon has a stomach, Jax. This is where the Labyrinth digests the intent it pulls from the city."

Suddenly, a high-pitched, chattering sound echoed through the iron corridors. It wasn't the metallic whine of the Sentinels. It was organic. Rasping. Competitive.

"Movement! Two o'clock, in the steam-vents!" Spider hissed, his Rogues immediately fading into the shadows of a massive boiler.

From a cluster of rusted pipes, a dozen small, hunched figures emerged. They were three feet tall, with spindly, oily limbs and skin that looked like it had been patched together from discarded subway maps and wet soot. Their eyes were glowing orange embers, and they carried 'weapons' made of sharpened rebar and rusted pipe-wrenches.

"Are those..." Tupi squinted, her spirit-fern curled tight in its sling. "Are those... goblins?"

The creatures snarled, their mouths full of jagged, charcoal-teeth. They moved with a jerky, frantic energy, leaping between the pipes with the agility of rats.

"Goblins," Miller muttered, hefting his pipe. "Of course. If you’re going to have a dungeon, you’ve gotta have goblins. New York goblins."

"They aren't biological," Wei noted, his aura flaring in a warning pulse. "They are 'Dross-Goblins'. Spirit-constructs spawned from the collective grime and frustration of the city's tunnels. They are the leftovers of the Well."

The goblins didn't wait for an introduction. With a collective shriek, they lunged.

"Alpha, Phalanx! Keep your feet!" Miller roared.

But the iron floor was too slick. As the first wave of goblins slammed into the shields, the Spartans slid backward, their boots losing purchase on the oily residue. The goblins didn't hit the shields; they climbed them, their greasy claws finding purchase in the dents and scratches of the scrap-steel.

"Get 'em off! Get 'em off!" one of the Spartans shouted, swinging a short-sword at a goblin that was trying to bite through his helmet.

"Beta, suppression! Use the vents!" Wei commanded.

Spider and his Rogues appeared from the shadows, their Magi-Tech daggers flashing. But the goblins were like mercury—they didn't stay still long enough to be struck, and when they were hit, they burst into clouds of soot that blinded the delvers.

"Gamma, Sarah! The floor!" Wei shouted, parrying three rebar-spears with a single rotation of his staff.

Sarah realized the problem instantly. "The oil! It’s 'Frictionless Dissonance'! Tupi, help me!"

Tupi reached into her sling, her fingers brushing the fern. "Intent... Growth... CLEANSE!"

She didn't summon fire. She summoned 'Moss'. 

A wave of vibrant, emerald-green moss erupted from the floor under Team Alpha’s feet. It wasn't just decorative; the moss had a rough, velcro-like texture that bit into the iron plates and provided instant, solid traction. 

With their feet finally anchored, the Spartans surged forward. Miller’s iron pipe swept out in a massive overhead strike, crushing two goblins into piles of black ash.

"Sarah, the Anchor!" Miller panted.

Sarah slammed the Magi-Tech Anchor into the center of the moss-patch. "Purifying Field... ACTIVE!"

The golden light didn't melt the goblins, but it 'dried' them. The oily residue on their skin turned to brittle charcoal, their movements slowing as their 'greasy' intent was purified into something solid and clumsy.

"Now! Beta, take them!" Spider roared.

With the goblins slowed and the delvers anchored, the battle turned into a slaughter. The Archers picked off the goblins in the rafters, while the Rogues methodically cleared the floor. For every 'Dross-Goblin' that vanished, a small, black 'Dross-Shard' fell to the floor—a low-grade spirit stone that hummed with a heavy, industrial heat.

"Clear!" Miller shouted, wiping soot from his visor. 

He looked around at the Engine Room. The moss-patch Tupi had created was the only clean spot in an otherwise filthy nightmare. 

"Twenty targets neutralized," Jax announced, his overlay showing the group's stats. "Spirit levels took a hit due to the 'Oily Miasma', but health is stable. Master, the chat is losing it. Someone just dubbed them 'The Grease-Goblins of Grand Central'."

Wei walked to the edge of the moss, looking at one of the Dross-Shards. He picked it up with a piece of cloth, sensing the raw, unrefined power within. 

"They are weak alone," Wei said, "but the Labyrinth is using them to slow us down. This floor isn't a puzzle. It’s a trek. The 'Engine Room' is built to exhaust our spirit before we reach the Core."

"Wei, the heat is rising," Sarah noted, pointing to the massive boilers that were now glowing a dull, angry red. "The 'Governor' of this floor has noticed the loss of its scavengers. The steam-pressure is reaching Level 3 critical."

From the depths of the labyrinth of pipes, a deep, metallic thud echoed. It sounded like a massive piston striking an anvil. 

*Thud.*

*Thud.*

"That's not a goblin," Miller said, his iron pipe glowing brighter in response to the heat. 

"That’s the 'Steam-Construct'," Wei said, his aura tightening. "And it’s pulling the Dross from across the entire floor to form its body. Spider, find us a path that isn't straight through the furnace. Tupi, keep that moss growing—we’re going to need every inch of traction we can get."

As the Raid Group began to move deeper into the soot-choked maze of Level 2, Han Wei felt the shift in the city's intent. The first floor was about the 'Grand Central' that the world saw—the clockwork, the gold, the prestige.

But this floor? This was the Grand Central that the workers knew. The grime, the heat, the relentless, grinding machinery that kept the world moving while everyone else looked at the ceiling.

"Master," Jax whispered as they navigated a narrow walkway over a pit of boiling oil. "The world is silent. They’re realized this isn't just a fantasy adventure anymore. It’s... it’s a job."

"Cultivation is always a job, Jax," Wei said, his staff tapping the iron plates. "Now, tell the 'Scientific Cultivators' to analyze these Dross-Shards. If we can refine them, they’ll be the perfect fuel for Thorne's new 'Magi-Tech Engines'."

"Understood, Master," Jax chirped. "The 'Dross-Economy' has officially begun."

Wei led them forward, his blue aura a lonely spark of light in the heart of the great, oily machine. The Goblins were just the scouts. The real engine was still waiting to be vented.
