# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 24: The Shaman of the Dross

The temperature in the 'Heart of the Engine Room' had climbed past the point of endurance. The air was a shimmering haze of superheated steam and vaporized grease, making the outlines of the massive boilers look like they were melting. 

In the center of the chamber, standing atop a mound of discarded Dross Shards and shattered porcelain insulators, was the source of the heat. 

The Goblinoid Shaman was nearly five feet tall, its body a grotesque collage of rusted copper wires and soot-stained subway upholstery. In its right hand, it clutched a staff made of a twisted Third Rail, the end still sparking with a malevolent, violet electricity. It wore a necklace of 'found objects'—discolored pennies, broken lightbulbs, and what looked like a dried-up pizza crust that had somehow achieved spiritual resonance.

"Master, the thermal readings are through the roof!" Jax shouted over the roar of the venting steam. "The Shaman is using the 'Overheat' of the Labyrinth to create an 'Aura of Exhaustion'. My sensors are struggling to stay calibrated!"

Han Wei stood at the edge of Tupi’s moss-patch, his blue aura forming a thin, shimmering barrier against the heat. "Miller, report!"

"Alpha is holding, but the shields are too hot to touch!" Miller grunted, his iron pipe glowing a brilliant, dangerous white. "The Spartans are taking 'Status-Depletion' every five seconds. We can't stay in the open!"

The Shaman raised its Third Rail staff, and the violet sparks jumped to the surrounding boilers. With a sound like a thousand screeching brakes, the steam-pipes began to twist and reshape themselves, forming a series of lunging, metallic serpents.

"STEAM-STRIKE! DEFEND!" Wei commanded.

"Beta, find the exhaust vents! We need to vent this pressure!" Spider’s voice came from somewhere near the ceiling, though he was almost invisible in the haze.

Team Beta Rogues scrambled across the overhead conduits, their Magi-Tech daggers carving through the iron lattices. They weren't fighting the Shaman; they were fighting the room. For every valve they opened, a geyser of white steam erupted, temporarily dropping the local temperature.

"Gamma, Sarah! Counter-Resonance!" Tupi called out.

Team Gamma moved to the center of the hall, their Arcane Sparks throwing short-range 'Static-Dampers' that pulled the violet electricity away from the Shaman. Sarah slammed her Anchor into the moss. 

"Purifying Field... REFRIGERATION!" Sarah cried, her voice strained. She was pouring every scrap of her Spirit into the Anchor, forcing the golden light to carry the quality of a winter morning in Central Park.

A ten-foot bubble of cool, crisp air formed around the Anchor. Team Alpha retreated into it, their gasping Spartans slumping against their shields as their health-bars stabilized.

The Shaman let out a rasping, wet growl—the sound of bubbles in a clogged drain. It raised its staff again, and the mound of Dross Shards beneath it began to swirl. The black shards flew into the air, caught in a mini-tornado of oily soot.

"SHARD-STORM! CLOAK!" Wei roared.

He became a whirlwind of cerulean light. He didn't use his staff to hit the shards; he used it to create a 'Centrifugal Void'. He spun the oak staff so fast it created a vacuum, pulling the black shards into a tight, harmless spiral around him.

"Beta, the vents are open! NOW!" Spider shouted.

With the pressure vented, the orange glow of the boilers dimmed slightly. The 'Aura of Exhaustion' wavered.

"ALPHA, CHARGE!" Wei commanded.

Miller didn't wait. He surged out of the cool-zone, his glowing pipe trailing a wake of steam. He didn't go for the Shaman’s head; he went for the mound of shards. 

"SUBWAY SMASH!" Miller bellowed, slamming his pipe into the foundation of the Shaman’s throne.

The impact shattered the rusted insulators. The Shaman lost its footing, and the Third Rail staff clattered against the iron floor, discharging a massive arc of violet light that momentarily blinded everyone in the room.

"GO! SPIDER! NOW!" Wei shouted.

Spider dropped from the ceiling like a falling shadow. He appeared behind the Shaman, his Magi-Tech dagger glowing with a sharp, silvery frequency. He didn't stab the Shaman; he 'cut' the thread of violet Intent that connected the creature to the boilers.

The Shaman let out a high-pitched scream—a sound like a balloon popping—and its body of wires and upholstery began to unravel.

"Sarah, the Purifying Wave! Finish it!" Tupi cried.

Sarah didn't even stand up. She reached out and touched the floor of the moss-patch, sending one final, concentrated pulse of gold through the roots of the moss. 

The golden light raced up the mound, through Miller’s pipe, and slammed into the unraveling Shaman. 

The creature didn't shatter; it 'evaporated'. The soot, the grease, and the wires turned into a cloud of harmless grey ash that was immediately sucked into the open exhaust vents.

The roar of the boilers died down to a low, rhythmic hum. The orange glow faded back into a dull, industrial amber. 

In the center of the ash-cloud, a single, glowing purple object remained: a 'Refined Dross Shard', shaped like a subway token.

"Floor Two Mini-Boss... PURIFIED!" Jax announced, his overlay flashing with a series of high-level achievements. "Raid Group Status: Alpha at forty percent, Beta at sixty percent, Gamma at fifty percent. We have cleared 'The Shaman of the Dross'!"

"That is 'Intent', Sarah," Wei said, nodding. "You didn't use a spell. You used a memory to shape the Well."

As the last of the ash settled, Tupi pointed to the base of the shattered insulator-mound. "Master, look! The resonance isn't just in the core."

A small, ornate chest—carved from the same obsidian as the first floor's gears but inlaid with shimmering blue glass—had manifested in the space where the Shaman's throne once stood. It didn't look like a product of the Engine Room's grime; it looked like a rewarded piece of the Labyrinth's own architecture.

Spider approached it cautiously, his picks out, but then he stepped back. "It's not locked, Master. It's... it's waiting."

Wei nodded to Sarah. "This floor was cleared by your cooling-intent. It is only right that you open it."

Sarah stepped forward, her hands still trembling from the effort of the Anchor. She touched the lid, and the obsidian chest hissed open, releasing a cloud of cold, lavender-scented vapor.

Inside lay a staff. 

It was made of a white, porcelain-like material that seemed to glow with an internal, subterranean light. The head of the staff was shaped like a stylized anchor, but instead of iron, it was made of a clear, emerald glass that contained a single, rotating gear from the first floor.

"The Staff of the Anchor," Wei murmured, sensing the Intent. "It is a Level 3 resonance-conduit. It will take the strain off your body, Sarah. It will ground your purification directly into the Labyrinth's own logic."

Sarah picked it up, and the moment her fingers touched the white porcelain, her soot-stained clothes seemed to brighten. The cooling-aura she had struggled to maintain now flowed effortlessly from the emerald glass.

"It feels... solid," Sarah whispered, a new confidence in her eyes. "Like I don't have to hold the world together by myself anymore."

"You never did," Wei said, smiling. "But now the world is helping you back."

Spider emerged from the shadows, his hands covered in grease. "Master, the exhaust vents led to a secondary shaft. It’s not bone and glass this time. It’s... it’s a series of service-tunnels lit by a strange, metallic blue light. But the air... it smells like copper and ozone."

Wei looked at the token in his hand. "Level 3 is next. The 'Vermin Engine'. According to the Northern Heavens map, that’s where the Labyrinth’s mechanical hunger meets the city’s oldest pests. It will be faster, sharper, and full of different kind of tricks."

As the Raid Group gathered their gear, the moss Tupi had grown began to wither, its work done. They stood in the center of the silent Engine Room, twenty survivors who were starting to look like veterans. 

"Ten minute rest," Wei commanded. "Then, we enter the Blue."
