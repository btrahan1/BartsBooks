# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 28: The Gilded Warm-up

The golden gates of Grand Central didn't just stand open; they seemed to pulse with a renewed, hungry vibration. As the forty-man raid group stepped through the threshold, a wave of familiar ozone and clockwork ticking washed over them.

But something was wrong. Or, from Han Wei’s perspective, something was exactly right.

"Everything respawned?" Jax asked, his drone-body hovering in disbelief as he scanned the entrance hall. The brass gears that had been shattered during the first entry were spinning again. The 'Clockwork Sentinels' they had methodically dismantled were standing in their designated alcoves, their bronze bodies gleaming as if they had just been polished. "Wei, look at the readings! The first floor is at one hundred percent integrity! It’s like we were never here!"

The 'Dungeon Killers' shifted their heavy vanguard shields, the metal clanging against the marble floor. Behind their visors, eyes widened with a mixture of confusion and growing irritation.

"We cleared this!" one of Krull’s lieutenants grunted, hefting a massive spirit-steel hammer. "We fought through three floors of this junk! You’re telling me we have to do it all over again?"

Krull looked at the Sentinels, his own 'Vanguard' Greatsword resting on his shoulder. He narrowed his eyes, a low growl of anticipation vibrating in his chest. "Let's get warmed up!"

Han Wei stood at the head of the formation, his blue aura extended in a wide, calm circle that encompassed both his disciples and the Dungeon Killers. He didn't look frustrated. In fact, he looked pleased.

"Good!" Wei answered, his voice carrying through the vaulted hall with a resonant, steady authority. "This is exactly what we wanted!"

"Exactly what we wanted?" Miller asked, leaning on his iron pipe, though he already had a knowing grin on his face. "Master, I think the coffee is still talking for you."

"No, Miller," Wei said, his gaze sweeping the forty warriors. "Think. We spent the night at the Javits Center practicing with wooden sticks and theory. We practiced the 'Sync-Stab' and the 'Vanguard-Leanto.' But that was a simulation. This... this is a gift from the Well."

He looked at Krull. "We have twenty people who know how to dodge and twenty people who know how to stand. We have a combined resonance that has never been tested in live-fire. If we walked straight to the Level 4 Heart now, we would be forty strangers fighting one boss. By the time we reach the third floor again, we will be a Sect."

Krull’s grin returned, sharper and more predatory. He slammed his Greatsword into the floor, the red Qi of his 'Internal Fire' flashing across the runes Wei had etched into the blade. "Live-fire training it is, then. First floor Sentinels... they're just training dummies with better armor."

"Alpha-Vanguard, take the center!" Wei commanded, his staff pointing to the first cluster of Sentinels.

"Dungeon Killers, you heard the man!" Krull roared. "Let’s show these clockwork toys what New York steel looks like! SHIELD WALL!"

The battle for the first floor was no longer a struggle for survival; it was a rhythmic, high-efficiency exercise in mass-coordination. 

The Sentinels lunged, but instead of the Spartans having to dodge and parry, they simply stepped back. Four of Krull’s Vanguard-heavyweights surged forward, their massive tower-shields locking together in a phalanx that was five times thicker than anything Wei’s group could have produced.

*CLANG.*

The Sentinels' bronze spears hit the spirit-steel shields and simply bounced, the impact absorbed by forty combined feet of mass and grounded Intent. 

"Now! Beta-Sync!" Wei commanded.

From the gaps between the heavy shields, Spider and his Rogues appeared like shadows. They didn't just strike; they used the Vanguard-Scouts from Krull’s group to provide a 'Force-Boost.' A Scout would hurl a blunt hammer at the Sentinel’s head, and while the construct’s sensors calculated the impact, a Rogue would slide a Magi-Tech dagger into the mercury-joint.

"Clear!" Miller shouted. 

In the time it had taken the original twenty-man group to clear the first room, the forty-man raid had cleared the entire entrance hall.

"Floor One, Room One cleared in three minutes!" Jax announced, his global feed showing a series of high-speed comparisons. "Raid Group Status: One hundred percent health across the board. Spirit levels: Ninety-eight percent. Coordination Factor: Rising rapidly!"

The descent through the first floor became a blitz. The 'Keeper of the Cog,' the mini-boss that had nearly broken them the day before, didn't last five minutes. With Krull’s heavy-hitters pinning its mallet and Wei’s disciples striking the resonant weak-points, the obsidian construct shattered with a pathetic, dying grind.

As they reached the entrance to the 'Engine Room' on the second floor, the group was no longer forty individuals. They were moving in a perfect, rotating circle—a 'Sect-Wheel' that absorbed every impact and returned it with ten times the force.

"Engine Room Goblins!" Spider chirped, noticing the movement in the pipes.

"Don't even stop!" Krull roared. "Vanguard-Charge! Flatten 'em!"

They didn't wait for Sarah's moss this time. The Vanguard simply used their heavy, spiked boots and their weight to crush the oily floor into submission. The goblins, expecting a nimble group they could slide around, were instead met with a literal wall of screaming steel that didn't care about the oil.

"They're not even fighting us!" Tupi looked as the Dross-Goblins were simply swept aside by the vanguard shields. "They're just... moving through the grime."

"Speed through dross," Wei noted, his staff parrying a lone pipe-wrench with a casual flick. "That is the 40-man advantage. We don't have to dodge the dirt when we can simply out-mass it."

By the time they reached the 'Vermin Engine' on Level 3, the spirit of the group was electric. The QI-infused coffee had worn off, replaced by the far more potent drug of absolute tactical dominance. 

The SteamPunk rats emerged from the chrome corridors, their whirring tails and steam-pumps ready for another high-speed ambush.

"Wait for it," Miller whispered, his iron pipe glowing a steady, confident red. "Wait for the Anchor..."

Sarah, holding the Staff of the Anchor, didn't need to close her eyes for long. She tapped the porcelain staff on the floor and a pulse of cooling-authority raced out, not as a desperate wave, but as a surgical strike that specifically targeted the rats' steam-joints.

"Now! EXTERMINATE!" Krull roared.

The fight was a symphony of precision and power. The rats would lunge, find the Vanguard shields, and then be instantly crushed by the Spartans' spears or the Scouts' hammers. For every 'Vermin' that fell, a 'Refined Dross Shard' was collected by the Gamma-Medics, who were already using them to recharge the group's spirit-stones on the fly.

"We're back," Wei whispered as they reached the liquid-gold doors of the Boss Room. 

They stood at the threshold again. But this time, they weren't battered or exhausted. They were standing tall, their auras merged into a massive, pulsing dome of blue and red light that made the liquid-gold glass of the doors feel... manageable.

"Jax, status?" Wei asked, his staff resting on the chrome floor.

"Raid Group Status: Ninety-five percent health, eighty percent spirit. Casualties: ZERO. We have cleared the first three floors in record time," Jax reported, his voice ringing with triumph. "The world is watching. We are at the Heart. And this time... we aren't looking for a seat. We're looking for the Core."

Krull walked up to the liquid-gold doors and spat on the floor. "Alright, Wei. You were right. The warm-up was good. My guys are loose. Your guys are anchored. Now... let's see what this 'Regal' thing looks like up close."

Wei looked at the doors, his blue eyes reflecting the shifting versions of the city within the glass. He felt the Level 4 intent pulsing behind it—the 'Regal' consciousness that believed it was the true ruler of New York.

"The 4th Harmonic," Wei whispered. "It is the sound of the city's deepest memory. It will try to convince you that you don't belong here. It will try to make you believe that the Labyrinth is the only reality."

He looked at his forty warriors. 

"Don't listen," Wei commanded. "New York is yours. The Well is yours. And this morning... we take the Gilded Heart."

He raised his staff and slammed the base into the chrome plates. The blue-red resonance hit the liquid-gold doors, and with a sound like a thousand harps shattering, the glass began to dissolve.

"THE FORTY... ADVANCE!" Wei roared.

The raid entered the final chamber. The Gilded Warm-up was over. The 4th Harmonic had begun.
