# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 29: The Gilded Throne

The liquid-gold doors didn't just open; they dissolved into a shimmering mist of pure spirit-energy, revealing a chamber that made the previous three floors look like a junkyard.

It was a throne room of impossible proportions. The walls were made of white marble veined with pulsing circuits of sapphire light. The ceiling was a vast, rotating map of the New York City subway system, but instead of trains, it tracked the flow of Qi throughout the five boroughs. 

And at the far end of the hall, seated upon a throne made of interlocked obsidian pistons and silver gears, sat the master of the Gilded Labyrinth.

"A throne room?" Jax asked, his camera lens zooming in on the figure. "And on it, sat its king. What sewer is complete without a king? That's not very original, Wei."

"It's not about originality, Jax," Wei whispered, his staff held high as the forty-man raid fanned out into their integrated formation. "It's about the 'Structure of Power.' The Labyrinth has reached into the city's subconscious and found the oldest archetype we have."

But as they drew closer, the 'King' proved himself to be anything but a cliche. 

He was a nightmare of steampunk engineering, standing nearly fifteen feet tall even while seated. His torso was a massive, glowing boiler encased in ornate, spirit-refined gold. His head was a rotating carousel of three faces—one stoic, one weeping, one laughing—all made of polished ivory. 

But it was his arms that made the delvers freeze. He had eight of them, thick and powerful, made of a fusion of bronze plating and hydraulic lines. Each hand gripped a different weapon: a massive obsidian hammer, a glowing blue plasma-spear, a serrated saw-blade, a heavy spirit-steel shield, a long-range bow of white bone, a double-headed axe, a vibrating sonic-mace, and an eight-foot-long executioner's sword.

"The Gilded King," Wei breathed, his aura tightening.

In front of the throne, an army of 'Regal' constructs stood in silent, perfect rows. They weren't the clunky Sentinels from the first floor. They were sleek, humanoid warriors in sapphire-and-gold armor, their eyes glowing with a cold, refined intelligence. There were at least a hundred of them.

"Well, well, well," the King stated, his voice a deep, resonant series of metallic clicks that coalesced into a perfect, albeit robotic, baritone. The vibration of his voice was so strong it caused the spirit-stones in the delvers' pockets to hum. 

His eyes—six of them, arranged in a hexagonal pattern on his central face—locked onto Han Wei.

"The 'Best' of New York City?" the King asked, his laugh sounding like a handful of coins being dropped on a marble floor. "A collection of scrap-iron warriors and a single spark of the Northern Heavens."

Krull stepped forward, his 'Vanguard' Greatsword glowing a fierce, defiant red. "Scrap-iron? I’ve flattened your entire dungeon to get here, you oversized toaster!"

The King didn't even look at Krull. His focus remained entirely on Wei. "You're not from around here, I can tell. You carry the scent of a world that understood the 'Well' before it had names. You are an anomaly in my system."

The King raised his eight arms in a slow, theatrical gesture. "I'll tell you what, anomaly. I won't enter the fight until you do. A King has no business with the 'Dross' until their Master proves his worth. Let's see how your minions do against my minions, yes?"

He snapped two of his ivory fingers. 

The hundred regal constructs surged forward in one silent, terrifying movement. They didn't roar. They didn't scream. They simply 'advanced' with the inevitability of a tidal wave.

"Wei! The numbers!" Miller shouted, his iron pipe glowing white-hot as he braced his shield. "They've got us two-to-one, and they look like they actually know how to fight!"

"Alpha-Vanguard, LOCK!" Han Wei roared, his voice cutting through the King's metallic laughter. "This is what we trained for! Don't look at the King! Look at the line!"

The 'Minion Battle' erupted in a storm of sapphire light and industrial steel. 

The regal constructs hit the forty-man shield wall with a force that made the marble floor crack. For the first time, Krull’s Vanguard-warriors were actually pushed back, their heavy tower-shields groaning as the King's'minions' used high-frequency spirit-blades to slice through the spirit-steel.

"Vanguard, LEAN!" Krull bellowed, throwing his own mass into the center of the phalanx. "Spartans, THRUST!"

The integration held. As the Vanguard took the initial, crushing impact, the Spartans used the gaps to deliver precision strikes to the constructs' joints. Sarah, standing in the center with her Staff of the Anchor, was no longer just 'cooling' the air; she was 'Anchor-Binding' the constructs, sending threads of green-gold light into the floor to lock the minions' feet in place.

"Beta-Scouts, take the flanks!" Spider commanded.

The Rogues and Scouts leaped above the fray, using the sapphire circuits on the walls as footing. They dropped onto the constructs from above, their combined daggers and hammers shattering the 'Regal' armor in bursts of violet sparks.

"It's working!" Jax shouted to the world, his feed showing a chaotic but controlled mosh-pit of high-level cultivation. "The Forty are holding! They're actually clearing the King’s guard!"

But for every construct they shattered, the King simply clicked his metallic tongue, and another would step from the shadows of the throne. It was a war of attrition, and the Raid Group’s spirit levels were beginning to dip into the yellow.

"We need to move the line forward!" Miller panted, his armor dented and smoking. "We're stuck in the door!"

"Not yet," Wei said, his eyes fixed on the King. He hadn't moved. He stood at the very back of the formation, his staff held vertical, his blue aura a silent, watchful flame. "He's waiting for me to break. He's waiting for me to step in so he can unleash the '4th Harmonic'."

The King leaned back on his throne, his eight arms shifting restlessly. "Your minions have spirit, Anomaly. They fight with the desperation of the dying. But my army is the Labyrinth itself. I have ten thousand years of 'grime' to turn into 'gold'."

"New York doesn't have ten thousand years," Wei said, his voice a calm anchor in the center of the storm. "We have the 'Now.' And the 'Now' is a lot harder than your logic can handle."

Sarah let out a cry of effort as she sent a massive 'Total Stasis' wave through the front line, freezing a dozen constructs in their tracks. "Wei! I'm at twenty percent! We can't hold the 'Wait' much longer!"

The Forty began to buckle. The sheer pressure of the endless constructs was finally grinding down their reserves. Krull was down on one knee, his Greatsword buried in the marble to keep himself upright. Miller’s shield was cracked in half.

The King stood up. The movement was slow, fluid, and terrifyingly silent. The hydraulic lines in his eight arms hissed with golden steam.

"It seems your minions are spent," the King said, raising the plasma-spear and the executioner's sword. "The balance has tipped. Now... show me the 'Master' of New York."

"Everything we've got!" Miller shouted, trying to stand.

"No," Wei said, stepping through the center of the ragged phalanx. 

As he passed his students, his blue aura didn't just touch them; it 're-filled' them. A wave of cool, stabilizing intent washed over the Forty, their health-bars flickering back into the green as Wei shared his own, immense reservoir of spirit.

Wei stepped onto the open marble floor, ten feet away from the first line of constructs. He didn't raise his staff. He didn't flare his aura. He just stood there, a mediocre-looking man in a simple tracksuit, holding an oak stick.

"The minions have done their part," Wei said to the King. "Now... it's just you and me."

The King tilted his head, his laughing face rotating to the front. "Very well, Anomaly. Let us see if New York is worth its weight in gold."

The 4th Harmonic peaked. The sapphire circuits on the walls turned a violent, blood-red. 

Wei looked back at his forty warriors, who were now standing tall again, their spirits anchored by his presence.

"Watch," Wei commanded. "And learn. This is how you disarm a King."

The final battle had truly begun.
