# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 30: Seven to Go

The air in the Gilded Throne room didn't just vibrate; it seemed to crystallize. The crimson light of the 4th Harmonic was so intense it stained the white marble floors like spilled wine. 

Jax, his drone-chassis hovering at a safe distance, hovered close to Sarah, his camera lens clicking rapidly. "Uh oh," he whispered, his audio-interface dropping to a low, conspiratorial frequency. "I know that look. That was the look he had in the Amazon!"

Han Wei was no longer just a man in a tracking suit. He was moving forward with a grace that transcended mere athleticism. He twirled his oak staff in a slow, hypnotic circle, the wood leaving trails of sapphire light in the air as if the atmosphere itself had become a canvas.

Sarah gripped her Staff of the Anchor, her eyes wide as she watched her master. "Wei's been holding back," she whispered, a realization hitting her with the force of a spiritual breakthrough. "He wasn't just being 'mediocre.' He's been holding back so *we* could grow stronger. He wanted us to find our own balance before he showed us the depth of his."

Wei stopped ten paces from the King. A soft blue glow began to emanate from his skin, not a flare of aggressive power, but a steady, tranquil luminescence that seemed to push back the red chaos of the room. He shifted his weight, his feet finding the natural ley-lines of the marble floor, and began the first movements of the Wei Chi forms. 

"Whenever you're ready, Tin Man," Wei said, his voice a calm ripple in a stormy sea.

The Gilded King didn't answer with words. He answered with a mechanical roar that shatereered the sapphire circuits in the walls. All eight of his arms blurred into motion.

The first part of the battle was a symphony of fierce, high-frequency collisions. The King lunged, his eight arms delivering a coordinated assault that should have been impossible to defend against. The obsidian hammer struck from the left, the plasma-spear from the right, and the serrated saw-blade swept at Wei’s knees.

But Wei didn't block. He flowed.

He moved likes a leaf caught in a gale, his body twisting and pivoting in ways that defied traditional physics. He used the 'Wei Chi' principle of minimal resistance, letting the King’s own massive momentum carry the weapons harmlessly past him. For every strike the King made, Wei was a fraction of an inch away, his staff tapping the ground to maintain a perfect, shifting center.

"He's playing with him!" Krull shouted, leaning on his Greatsword as he watched the duel. "Look at the footwork! The King is swinging half a ton of gear, and Wei is just... walking!"

The King grew frustrated. The hydraulic lines in his arms hissed with golden steam, and his three faces began to rotate at dizzying speeds. He raised all eight weapons for a simultaneous 'Crushing Harmonic' strike.

Wei didn't move away this time. He moved *in*.

He stepped into the 'eye' of the King’s storm, his blue aura flaring into a concentrated point at the tip of his staff. He didn't aim for the King’s torso; he aimed for the 'Shoulder-Pivot' of the arm holding the obsidian hammer.

With a sound like a lightning strike, Wei’s staff connected. The 'Intent' behind the blow was so dense it ignored the bronze plating entirely, vibrating straight into the mercury-core of the joint.

*CRACK.*

The King let out a shriek—a high-pitched, electronic scream that made the delvers drop to their knees. The massive obsidian-hammer arm was severed cleanly at the shoulder, falling to the marble floor with a bone-shaking thud.

The King staggered, his ivory faces spinning into a mask of pure, laughing malice. He slammed his seven remaining weapons into the floor.

The ground erupted. 

The white marble shattered as dozens of new rifts opened, vomiting forth a fresh wave of 'Dross Goblins' and 'SteamPunk Rats,' but these were different—they were infused with the red light of the 4th Harmonic, their eyes burning with a suicidal rage.

"The minions of the King are restless!" the Gilded King roared, his robotic voice now distorted by the loss of his arm. "If you will not fall to my blade, you will be buried by my city!"

Wei didn't even look back at the erupting rifts. He kept his eyes locked on the King, his blue aura stabilizing once more.

"The Thirty-Nine can handle the rift raft," Wei said, his voice carrying back to his team with the weight of an absolute command. "You guys handle the adds! Miller, Krull, secure the flanks! Sarah, keep the Anchor steady!"

He twirled his staff again, the sapphire light now tinged with a sharp, silver edge.

"One arm down," Wei whispered, a small, dangerous smile touching his lips. "Seven to go."

Miller roared, hefting his iron pipe as he led the Vanguard into the new swarm of adds. Krull laughed, his Greatsword carving a path through the first wave of rats. "You heard him! Keep the trash off the Master!"

The Throne Room became a two-tiered war. In the center, the forty-man raid fought a desperate, grinding battle against a sea of corrupted vermin. And in the very heart of the chaos, Han Wei danced with the Gilded King, a lone spark of blue light against the dying sun of the Labyrinth.

"Show me the rest, Tin Man," Wei said, stepping forward into a new form. "I’m just getting started."

The King lunged again, but the rhythm had changed. The Labyrinth was no longer just a dungeon; it was a challenge. And for the first time in ten thousand years, it was afraid.
