# Cultivator in NYC: Book 3 - Chapter 31: The Halfway Point

The 4th Harmonic had transformed the Throne Room into a kaleidoscopic nightmare of red and sapphire pressure. While the Forty waged a desperate, high-speed war against the endless rifts of corrupted vermin, Han Wei remained locked in the eye of the storm.

The Gilded King was no longer laughing. His remaining seven arms were a blur of coordinated violence, the plasma-spear and the executioner’s sword weaving a web of light that should have been impossible to penetrate. 

Wei moved with the fluid, deceptive simplicity of the Wei Chi forms. He wasn't just dodging; he was 'threading the needle,' his body passing between the King’s strikes with the precision of a master clockmaker. 

"Look at the transition!" Sarah shouted over the roar of a SteamPunk rat's exhaust. She slammed her Staff of the Anchor into the floor, freezing a dozen adds to clear a path for the Vanguard. "Wei is using the King’s own hydraulic rhythm against him!"

Wei saw the opening. The King’s sonic-mace arm overextended, its vibration creating a momentary ripple in the atmospheric pressure. Wei stepped inside the vibration, his oak staff glowing with a concentrated, silver-blue light.

With a sharp *crack*, he struck the secondary elbow-joint. The silver-and-gold plating buckled, the hydraulic lines bursting in a spray of iridescent coolant.

Two down.

But the King was adapting. As Wei’s staff retracted, the executioner's sword arm swung in a massive, horizontal arc that Wei couldn't completely avoid. He twisted mid-air, shifting his center of gravity to take the hit on his thigh rather than his torso, but the impact was still seismic.

Wei was sent skidding across the marble floor, his boots carving deep grooves in the stone. He came to a stop twenty feet away, a sharp, metallic tang in his mouth.

"Wei!" Miller roared, his iron pipe smashing through a brass drone as he tried to surge toward his master. 

Wei raised a hand, his face tight but his aura steady. He stood up slowly, his left leg dragging slightly. A distinct limp had entered his stride, but he didn't look defeated. He looked... focused. He shrugged his shoulders, a dull thud echoing as he reset his center, and a soft blue pulse of Intent washed over the leg, stabilize the bone and muscle enough to continue.

"I'm fine," Wei said, his voice a calm anchor in the chaos. "Keep the rifts clear. He’s starting to leak."

The King lunged again, sensing the weakness in Wei’s step. But the limp was a deception—a 'broken' rhythm that confused the King’s mechanical sensors. As the plasma-spear thrust forward, Wei didn't dodge left or right. He 'collapsed' into the limp, sliding under the spear-head and striking upward with the full weight of his internal fire.

The sapphire head of his staff caught the shoulder-pivot of the serrated saw-blade arm. 

Three down.

Wei didn't stop. He used the momentum of the strike to pivot on his healthy leg, his staff coming around in a wide, whistling circle that slammed into the elbow of the bow-arm.

Four down. 

The King let out a sound that wasn't a roar or a shriek. It was a deep, resonating groan that shook the very foundations of the Labyrinth. He stepped back, the four severed limbs lying in a circle of obsidian and copper on the floor. 

The three faces on his ivory head stopped rotating. All three—the stoic, the weeping, and the laughing—turned to face Wei simultaneously, their features melting and merging into a single, expressionless mask of polished gold.

"Half-ton of scrap-iron, you said?" the King asked, his voice no longer clicking. It was a single, pure frequency that made the delvers' ears bleed. 

The King’s torso began to expand, the golden boiler in his chest glowing with the intensity of a dying sun. The three remaining arms—the spear, the sword, and the shield—merged into a single, massive limb that occupied his entire right side, while his left side shifted into a series of pulsing, obsidian cannons.

"So," the King whispered, the air around him beginning to liquefy. "I see you are not lacking some skill. I guess it's time for me to get serious!"

The 4th Harmonic shifted. The red light vanished, replaced by a blinding, absolute white that felt like the surface of a star. 

Wei stood at the center of the white-out, his limp still visible, his blue aura a small, defiant spark in the desert of light. He raised his staff, the oak wood now glowing with a solid, silver Intent that felt older than the city itself.

"Finally," Wei said, a smile pulling at his lips.

"Jax!" Sarah screamed, her voice barely audible over the rising hum. "What’s the status?!"

"Critical!" Jax shouted back, his own sensors smoking. "Spirit levels in the room are reaching Level 4.5! The Labyrinth is no longer just a dungeon! It’s... it’s a Reactor!"

Krull slammed his Greatsword into the ground, his eyes wide as he watched the King's'Serious' form. "Back off! Everyone, back behind the Vanguard! The Master is about to go deep!"

The Forty retreated behind the wall of spirit-steel, their combined shields forming a final, desperate dome as the white light consumed everything.

In the center of the star, the Gilded King raised his single, massive blade.

"One arm down, seven to go?" the King asked, his voice a tidal wave of golden pressure. "No, Anomaly. Now... there is only the Core."

Wei stood his ground, the silver light of his staff cutting through the white void like a razor. 

"Let's see your Core then, Tin Man," Wei said.

The first strike of the 'Serious' phase was not a swing. It was a world-ending vibration.
