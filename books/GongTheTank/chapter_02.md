# Chapter 2: The Soft Shield

By age eight, Gong had grown. He was not tall, but he was wide. While other boys were growing up like bamboo shoots—thin and flexible—Gong was growing like a tree stump.

It was a humid afternoon near the sericulture sheds. The smell of boiling silkworm cocoons hung heavy in the air. Gong was sitting on a crate, eating a raw turnip with the rhythmic *crunch-crunch-crunch* of a machine, when he heard the noise.

"Give it back!"

It was a girl’s voice. High, sharp, and scared.

Gong stopped chewing. Use of that tone usually meant trouble. Momma said, *"If you see trouble, you steer clear, unless it's trouble finding someone smaller than you."*

Gong swallowed the turnip chunk whole and stood up. He trundled around the corner of the shed.

Three boys from the village—older, faster, and meaner—had cornered Meilin. Meilin was the silk-weaver’s daughter. She was holding a small, embroidered pouch behind her back. The boys were laughing. The leader, a rat-faced kid named Jin who had already started learning the 'Iron Fist' technique from his uncle, shoved Meilin. She stumbled into the dirt.

"Momma says you shouldn't hit girls," Gong said.

His voice wasn't loud. It sounded like two rocks grinding together.

The three boys spun around. They saw Gong. They didn't look scared. Why would they? Gong was the kid who got headbutted by goats.

"Get lost, Stump," Jin sneered. "This isn't your business."

"She's a girl," Gong repeated, as if stating a law of physics. "You can't hit her."

"I can hit whoever I want," Jin said, and to prove his point, he raised his hand toward Meilin again.

Gong moved.
It wasn't a dash. It was a lurch. He simply placed his body between Jin's hand and Meilin's face.

*SMACK.*

Jin’s palm connected with Gong’s cheek. It sounded like slapping a side of beef. Gong’s head turned slightly. He blinked.

"Run away, Meilin," Gong said. He didn't look back at her. He was watching Jin's other hand.

"Don't ignore me!" Jin shouted. He punched Gong in the stomach.
Gong grunted. He took a step back, but he didn't fall.

Meilin scrambled up and ran. The other two bullies tried to chase her.
Gong reached out. His left hand grabbed the collar of Bully #2. His right hand grabbed the sash of Bully #3.
He jerked them back. They crashed into him.

"Let go, you oaf!"
"Get him!"

For the next twenty minutes, the alley behind the silk shed became a flurry of violence. Or rather, a flurry of violence landing on a stationary object.
Jin knew some martial arts. He landed kicks to Gong’s ribs. He punched Gong’s nose. The other two boys rained blows on Gong’s back and arms.

Gong did not fight back. He possessed no technique to hit three moving targets. He simply tucked his chin, covered his vital organs with his thick arms, and stood there.
Every time they tried to run past him to chase Meilin, he would lurch in front of them, or grab a wrist, or simply fall on them.

He took a beating that would have hospitalized a normal man, let alone a boy. His nose bled. His left eye swelled shut. His bruises developed bruises.

Finally, a particularly vicious kick to the back of the knee buckled his leg. Gong went down. He tried to push himself up, but his arms shook. They folded. He collapsed into the dust, wheezing.

"He's done," Jin panted, wiping sweat from his own forehead. "Stupid Stump. Probably brain damaged."
"Meilin's gone," the other boy spat, kicking Gong one last time in the hip. "Waste of time."

Exhausted from the effort of beating him up, the three bullies wandered off, nursing sore knuckles.

Gong lay in the dirt for a long time. The sun began to set, turning the sky a bruised purple that matched his face.
Eventually, he heard footsteps. Heavy ones. Momma.

She didn't scream. She didn't cry. She knelt down and gently wiped the blood from his lip with her apron.
"Gong," she sighed. "What happened?"

Gong opened his good eye. He tested his jaw. It clicked, but it worked.
"Jin... was hitting Meilin."

Momma looked at his battered face. "And you fought Jin? And his friends?"
"I didn't fight," Gong corrected. His voice was thick. "I just... stayed."

"Does it hurt?"
"Yes."
"Was it worth it?"

Gong thought about it. He imagined Meilin crying. Then he imagined Meilin running away, safe.
He touched his swollen eye. It throbbed. But the pain inside was just pain. It wasn't shame.

"Better they hit me," Gong rasped, "than hit her."

Momma picked him up. She was strong, but he was getting heavy. She carried him home, his beat-up head resting on her shoulder.
"You're a good boy, Gong," she whispered. "A hard head, but a good heart."

Gong fell asleep before they reached the front door. He slept for two days. When he woke up, he ate four bowls of rice and went back to the sericulture shed to finish his turnip.
