# Chapter 5: The Chew Toy

Three nights later, Brother Han decided they didn't need a bonfire.
"We are close to the Sect lands," he reasoned. "The aura of the Iron Mountain keeps weaker beasts away. Save your strength."

He was wrong.
The wolves that hunted near the Iron Mountain were not weak. They were Spirit Wolves—creatures with fur like steel wire and jaws that could snap a spear shaft. And they were hungry.

The attack came at the darkest hour, just before dawn. Use silence.
First, a scream. Then, the sound of tearing canvas.

Gong woke up when a wolf the size of a pony crashed through the tent next to him.
Chaos erupted. Boys were screaming, scrambling for weapons they didn't know how to use. Brother Han was shouting orders, his sword flashing in the moonlight as he engaged the Alpha.

"Form a circle! Back to back!" Han yelled. "Protect the supplies! Protect Xiao!"

Xiao was the group's healer recruit—a quiet girl of twelve who had joined them at the last village. She had a rare talent for wood-element healing but zero combat ability. She was currently freezing in terror near the pickled plum wagon.

Three wolves separated from the pack. They had spotted the smallest, softest target. They loped toward Xiao, drool dripping from their maws.

Gong was closer to the wagon than the circle.
He didn't have a weapon. He had a rule. *Protects girls.*

He lurched. He didn't have time to grab them. He only had time to get in the way.
He threw himself in front of Xiao just as the first wolf lunged.

*CRUNCH.*

The wolf’s jaws clamped onto Gong’s left forearm. It felt like a bear trap closing. The teeth sank deep, hitting bone.
Gong grunted. "Get back, Xiao."

The second wolf hit him from the right, biting into his thigh. The impact knocked him to one knee.
The third wolf went for his throat. Gong tucked his chin, forcing the beast to bite his shoulder instead.

"Gong!" Xiao screamed. Her hands glowed with a soft, green light. She didn't run. She reached out and touched Gong’s back.
*Verdant Mending.*

The pain in Gong’s arm flared, then dulled. The flesh tried to knit together, even as the wolf tore it apart.
It became a gruesome tug-of-war. The wolves were dealing damage. Xiao was pouring health back in. And Gong was the vessel for both.

He was a statue of meat.
The wolf on his arm shook its head violently, trying to rip the limb off. Gong set his feet. He grabbed the wolf’s scruff with his free hand (which was currently being chewed by the third wolf) and held on.

"Don't worry," Gong wheezed, blood running down his tunic. "I got them."

"You don't got them!" Xiao cried, tears streaming down her face. "They're eating you!"

"I'm... keeping them... busy."

The stalemate lasted for a horrifying minute. The wolves couldn't bypass Gong because he was holding onto them. They couldn't kill him because Xiao was pumping Qi into his veins. But Gong couldn't fight back. He was just a buffet that refused to close.

The wolves, frustrated and frenzied, began to drag him.
With three of them pulling, they slowly hauled Gong away from the wagon. His boots furrowed the dirt. Use gravity.
Xiao stumbled after him, keeping her hand on his back to maintain the spell.

"Let go, Gong! Let go!" Xiao sobbed.

"Can't," Gong grunted. "If I let go... they bite you."

He was being dragged toward the treeline. The pain was distant now, a dull roar. He felt lightheaded.
*Just hold on,* he told himself. *Momma said finish the job.*

Suddenly, a flash of silver light cut the air.
*SHING. SHING.*

The wolf on his left arm went limp, its head separated from its body.
Brother Han stood there, chest heaving, sword dripping dark blood. He moved with a speed Gong couldn't comprehend. He spun and decapitated the second wolf. The third one released Gong’s shoulder and bolted into the darkness.

Gong collapsed.
He didn't fall gracefully. He simply tipped over like a felled tree.
He lay on his back, staring at the stars. His left arm was a ruin. His leg was a mess. His shoulder was chewed meat.

Xiao collapsed beside him, her hands glowing brighter, pouring every ounce of her remaining energy into his wounds.
"You idiot," she wept. "You big, stupid idiot."

Brother Han looked down at them. He wiped his sword. He looked at the dead spirit wolves—beasts that usually required a team of iron-rank disciples to handle.
He looked at Gong, who was already snoring softly as the healing magic put him into a recovery coma.

"Well," Han muttered, sheathing his blade. "He certainly holds aggro."

Gong didn't hear him. He was dreaming of turnips.
