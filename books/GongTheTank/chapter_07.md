# Chapter 7: The Stone Appraisal

The morning after the climb, the successful recruits were gathered in the Hall of Assessment. It was a grand room filled with incense smoke and the quiet scratching of scribes' brushes.

 Elder Fang, a man with a beard so long it was tucked into his belt, presided over the ceremony. In front of him stood the Appraisal Stone—a massive chunk of spirit jade carved into the shape of a dragon.

"Step forward," Elder Fang droned. "Place your hand. Circulate your Qi. The stone does not lie."

Pip went first. The stone glowed a respectable blue (Wind affinity). The scribes nodded. "Outer Disciple. Agile Wind Division."
Xiao went next. The stone glowed soft green. "Healer Corps. Wood Division."

Then it was Gong's turn.
He trundled up to the podium. The stone looked fragile compared to him.
"Hand," Elder Fang said, looking bored.

Gong placed his meat-slab of a hand on the dragon's head. He tried to "circulate" his Qi. He wasn't entirely sure what that meant, so he just squeezed.

*First Test: Spiritual Aptitude (Color)*
The stone flickered. It didn't turn Blue or Green or Red. It turned a dull, muddy brown. The color of wet dirt.
"Earth affinity," Fang muttered. "Low grade. Very low grade."
Scribe: *Aptitude: Poor.*

*Second Test: Agility (Pulse Rate)*
The stone usually pulsed with light to measure reaction speed.
It didn't pulse. It just... sat there.
"Is it broken?" a scribe asked.
"No," Fang said, squinting. "He's just... thinking about it."
A solid ten seconds later, the stone emitted a single, sad *bloop*.
Scribe: *Agility: Non-existent.*

*Third Test: Strength (Intensity)*
"Push," Fang commanded.
Gong pushed.
He didn't use Qi. He used his shoulders. He used the muscles built from hauling trees and holding roofs.
The dull brown light suddenly flared. It became blindingly bright, not from spectral energy, but from sheer pressure. The jade began to vibrate.
*SCREEE.*
A hairline fracture appeared on the dragon's snout.
"Enough!" Fang shouted, slapping Gong's hand away. "You'll break the artifact!"
The scribes whispered furiously.
Scribe: *Strength: Abnormal. Exceeds standard calibration.*

*Final Test: Qi Density*
Elder Fang placed his own hand on Gong's wrist to feel his meridians. He frowned. Then he frowned deeper. He tapped Gong's arm.
"Circulate," Fang ordered. "Move the energy to your palm."

"I'm trying," Gong grunted. His face turned red with effort.
Inside Gong's body, his Qi was not a flowing river. It was not a gentle breeze.
It was wet concrete. It was sludge. It sat in his dantian like a lead weight. It refused to budge.

"Incredible," Fang whispered, but not in a good way. "I have never seen Qi this... stagnant. It's so dense it's practically solid. You will never be able to cast a spell. By the time you summoned a fireball, the war would be over."

Fang wiped his hands on a cloth, as if Gong's sluggishness might be contagious.
"Verdict," Fang announced.
"Strength: High. Constitution: High. Everything else: Tragic."

He looked at Gong.
"You have no future as a Cultivator. You cannot fly swords. You cannot cast hexes. You are, effectively, a moving rock."

Gong nodded. "Okay."

"However," Fang continued, "The sect needs rocks. Specifically, rocks that can carry heavy things and don't complain."

He grabbed a wooden token from the 'rejected' pile, stamped it with a heavy seal, and tossed it to Gong.
"Labor Division. Report to the Beast Pens. You'll be mucking out the Spirit Rhinos. Try not to let them trample you."

Gong caught the token. He bowed. "Thank you, Elder. I like rhinos."

"Of course you do," Fang sighed. "Next!"
