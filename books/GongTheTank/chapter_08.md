# Chapter 8: Kindred Spirits

The Beast Pens were located at the base of the Iron Mountain, far away from the polished jade halls of the main sect. It was a place of mud, straw, and smells that would make a weaker man's eyes water.

To Gong, it smelled like home.

He trundled down the dirt path, his wooden token clutched in his hand. The "Labour Division" was mostly older men who had failed to progress in their cultivation but had nowhere else to go. They looked tired and defeated.

Gong did not look defeated. He looked curious.

Brother Zhu, the foreman of the pens, was a squat man with a permanent scowl and a missing ear. He looked at Gong’s massive frame, then at the token.

"Mucking duty," Zhu grunted. "Pen 4. The Spirit Rhinos. They’re temperamental. If they charge, don't run. You're too slow. Just... try to fall somewhere soft."

"Okay," Gong said.

He walked to Pen 4. It was a large enclosure reinforced with ironwood beams as thick as tree trunks. Inside were three massive creatures.

Spirit Rhinos were not like normal rhinos. They were etched with natural rock-like plating that shimmered with weak earth Qi. They were grumpy, territorial, and heavy.

Gong opened the gate.
The nearest rhino, a scarred bull with a horn the size of a plowshare, snorted. It pawed the ground, shaking the earth. *THUMP. THUMP.*

Most recruits would have backed away.
Gong walked forward. He looked at the rhino. The rhino looked at him.

Gong saw a creature that was built of heavy bones and thick skin. A creature that wasn't fast, wasn't flashy, but could walk through a wall if it wanted to.
He felt a strange sense of recognition.

"Hello," Gong said.

The rhino lowered its head, preparing to charge.
Gong didn't flinch. He just stood there, radiating a profound lack of aggression. He wasn't challenging the beast. He was just... present. Indestructible and calm.

The rhino hesitated. Animals were good at sensing intent. It sensed no threat from this two-legged boulder. It sensed only... rock.

Gong reached into his tunic and pulled out a withered turnip he had saved from breakfast. Ideally, one would offer spirit grass, but Gong worked with what he had.
He held it out flat on his massive palm.

The rhino sniffed. It took the turnip. *Crunch.*
Then it nudged Gong’s hand with its wet snout, asking for more.

"That's all," Gong said seriously. "I have to work now."

He picked up a shovel—a heavy iron tool that looked like a toy in his hands—and began to scoop the manure. The rhinos ignored him. Actually, that wasn't true. They accepted him. By the time Brother Zhu came to check on him an hour later, Gong was scrubbing the bull's flank with a stiff brush. The rhino was leaning into it, making a low rumbling sound like a purring earthquake.

"Huh," Zhu muttered. "Usually they try to gore the fresh ones."

***

That evening, Gong sat on a log outside the Labour barracks, watching the sun set behind the peaks. He was tired, but it was a good tired. The kind that came from honest work.

"Gong!"

He turned to see Xiao jogging down the path. She wore the light green robes of a novice healer now, clean and crisp. She looked out of place among the mud and muck of the pens.

"Xiao," Gong said, a smile cracking his face.

She stopped in front of him, catching her breath. "I heard. They sent you to the pens? After the appraisal?"

"Yes. Spirit Rhinos."

"That's awful," she said, her face twisting in sympathy. "You saved us. You held off three wolves! And they make you a... a stable boy?"

"I like it," Gong said. "The rhinos are good. They are heavy. Like me."

Xiao stared at him, searching for sarcasm. Finding none, her shoulders slumped. "You really are simple, aren't you?"

"Maybe," Gong shrugged.

Xiao sat down on the log beside him. She was small next to him, a sapling next to a boulder.
"I got into the Inner Sect," she said quietly. "Elder Willow took me as a personal disciple. Because of my 'pure wood spirit roots'."

"That is good," Gong said. "You will learn big heals."

"I... I wanted to say thank you," Xiao said. She fidgeted with her sleeve. "I wouldn't be here if you hadn't held them. Everyone else ran. You didn't."

She reached into her pouch and pulled out a small, glass vial. Inside was a pill that glowed with a soft golden light.
"I stole this from the supply cache," she whispered conspiratorially. "Bone Knitting Pill. High grade. Not that you need it now, you're already healed. But keep it. Just in case."

Gong took the vial. It was tiny in his hand. "Thank you."

"I probably won't be able to come down here often," Xiao said, looking up at the distant peaks where the Inner Sect disciples lived. "They say we have to sever ties with the mortal world to cultivate properly."

"Okay."

"But I won't forget," she said fiercely. "We're friends, Gong. If you ever get hurt... if a rhino steps on you... you send a message to the Hall of Medicine. You ask for Xiao. I'll come."

Gong looked at her. He saw the same fierce determination she had when she poured her Qi into him while the wolves chewed his arm.
"Okay," he said. "If I get hurt."

Xiao stood up and brushed off her robes. She looked at him one last time, a mix of pity and admiration in her eyes. "Don't let them treat you like dirt, Gong. You're worth more than dirt."

"Dirt is good," Gong said earnestly. "It grows turnips."

Xiao laughed. It was a wet, teary sound. "Goodbye, Gong."

"Bye, Xiao."

He watched her walk back up the path, toward the clouds and the flying swords and the immortals. Then he looked back at the pen, where his new friends were chewing cud in the twilight.

He had a job. He had a friend. He had a turnip for later.
Gong was content.
