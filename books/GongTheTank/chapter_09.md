# Chapter 9: Heavy Breathing

Time in the Beast Pens moved differently than in the rest of the sect. Up on the mountain, time was measured in cultivation breakthroughs and pill furnaces. Down in the mud, time was measured in shovel loads.

Gong's routine was simple. He liked simple.

Dawn: Wake up before the sun. The Rhinos liked to eat early.
Morning: Haul feed. The Spirit Rhinos ate a mixture of iron-grass and rock-tubers. The bags weighed three hundred pounds each. The other workers used a cart with a rusted wheel. Gong just carried two bags, one on each shoulder. It was faster.

Midday: Mucking. This was the part the others hated. Gong didn't mind. It was just moving matter from one place to another. He used the rhythm. Scoop. Lift. Twist. Throw. *Thump.*

Afternoon: Grooming. The Rhinos needed their rocky plates scrubbed to prevent moss buildup. Gong used a wire brush. He found the perfect spot behind the ears that made their legs twitch.

Evening: Dinner. Rice and pickled vegetables. Sometimes a turnip if he was lucky.

Night: Sleep. Dream of nothing.

It had been a month. Gong had gotten stronger. Not faster—never faster—but denser. Carrying the feed bags had made his legs like tree trunks. Scrubbing the plates had made his grip even more iron-like.

But the most interesting part of his day was the Nap.

Every afternoon, after eating the iron-grass, the Rhinos would lie down in the mud. They would close their eyes and breathe.

Gong watched them.
He noticed something strange about their breathing.
It wasn't just air going in and out.
When they inhaled, the heavy, earth-aspected Qi in the ground seemed to *vibrate* toward them. It didn't flow like water. It rumbled like a landslide.
When they exhaled, the Qi didn't leave. It settled. It sank deep into their bones, adding another microscopic layer of density to their armor.

They weren't circulating Qi through meridians like the disciples upstairs. They weren't spinning it. They were *packing* it.
Compressing it.
Like stomping dirt to make a floor.

Gong sat on his log, mimicking them.
He closed his eyes. He took a breath.
*Imagine the Qi is mud.*
He pulled it in. He didn't try to spin it. He tried to swallow it. He tried to push it down into his stomach and pack it tight.

*Heavy.*
*Heavy is good.*
*Heavy is hard to move.*

He felt a tiny, dense thrum in his gut. A vibration. *Bum-bum-bum.* Just like the Rhinos.
He wasn't cultivating. He was sedimenting.

"Hey, Big Gong!"

Gong opened his eyes. It was Brother Zhu.
"Stop sleeping. We need to check the females in Pen 6. They've been acting weird."

Gong stood up. The heavy feeling remained in his gut, like he had swallowed a lead ball. It felt comforting.
"Okay."

Pen 6 held the females. They were usually calmer than the bulls, but today they were agitated. They were circling one particular rhino—a smaller female named "Pebble" by Gong.
Pebble was refusing to eat. She was standing in the corner, shifting her weight from foot to foot. She let out a low, distressed moan.

"She's sick," Zhu said, scratching his chin. "Probably ate a bad tuber. If she doesn't eat by tomorrow, we'll have to call the beast-healers. And they charge the department a fortune."

Gong walked into the pen. The other females parted for him. They knew him. He was the scratcher-of-ears. The bringer-of-turnips.
He approached Pebble.
She didn't back away. She leaned her heavy head against his chest.

Gong put his hand on her side.
He felt the vibration. *Bum-bum-bum.*
But there was something else. A second vibration.
It was faint, frantic, and very small.
*bip-bip-bip.*

It was hidden deep beneath her rock plates, masked by her own heavy energy. But Gong felt it. He knew vibrations. He knew weight.
He felt a tiny, extra weight inside her.

He looked at Pebble's eyes. They weren't sick. They were scared. And proud.

"She is not sick," Gong said.

Zhu frowned. "What do you know? Look at her. She's off her feed."

"She is not sick," Gong repeated. He patted Pebble's flank gently. "She is two."

"Two? What are you babbling about?"

"Two Rhinos," Gong said. "One big. One very small."

Zhu's eyes widened. He stared at Pebble, then at Gong. "You mean... she's pregnant? Impossible. We haven't had a breeding cycle in three years. The conditions have been too poor."

Gong didn't know about cycles or conditions. He just knew what his hand felt.
"Baby Rhino," Gong confirmed. "Eating for two. But she doesn't like the iron-grass. It's too hard for the baby."

He remembered his momma when she was heavy with his little sister. She didn't want hard bread. She wanted soft pottage.
Gong walked to the feed room. He ignored the iron-grass. He found a sack of softer, sweet-potato-like tubers reserved for the elders' mounts.
He brought a handful back.

Pebble sniffed. Then, she ate. Voraciously.

Zhu watched, his mouth slightly open. "Well," he muttered. "I'll be damned."

He looked at Gong with a new expression. Not just as a laborer, but as something else.
"Good eye, Gong. Good eye."

Gong just smiled and scratched Pebble behind the ear. He felt the heavy Qi in his own gut settle, satisfied. He had learned a breathing trick. And he was going to be an uncle.
