# Chapter 10: The Sound of Iron

Pregnancy, Gong discovered, was a universal constant. It made living things hungry, cranky, and particular.

Pebble was no longer satisfied with simple iron-grass or even the sweet tubers. She wanted variety. Specifically, she wanted *spicy* things.

Gong spent his spare time scavenging. He walked the perimeter of the Beast Pens, looking for Fire-Root. It was a nasty, stinging plant that most animals avoided because it burned the tongue. Pebble ate it by the bushel.
He found a patch near the disciples' rubbish heap. He dug it up, washed it in the stream until his hands tingled, and brought it to her.

She crunched it happily, tears streaming from her Rhino eyes, then burped a small puff of smoke.
Gong patted her flank. "Good burn," he said.

He spoke to the bump often.
"Hello little rock," he would whisper while brushing Pebble's side. "It is warm in there. Stay warm. Eat the spicy root."
He felt the tiny heartbeat, the *vibration* that was distinct from the mother's deep rumble. It felt feisty. It felt stubborn.
It felt like a... Maggie.
"Maggie," Gong decided. "Good name. Strong."

One afternoon, Brother Zhu sent Gong on a delivery run.
"Take this cart of manure to the training grounds," Zhu ordered. "The Earth Division uses it to fertilize their strength-training gourds. Don't linger. The inner disciples don't like smelling us."

Gong nodded. He picked up the cart handles. The cart was heavy, laden with the density of Spirit Rhino waste. Gong didn't mind. He liked the weight. It gave him traction.

He trundled up the path, leaving the valley of the pens and entering the lower tiers of the sect proper.
The training grounds were vast. Disciples in pristine white robes were practicing forms. Swords flashed. Fireballs sizzled. Wind blades cut the air.

Gong ignored them. They were too fast. Too floaty.
He delivered the cart to the garden shed, tipped it over, and prepared to leave.

Then he heard it.

*CLANG.*

It wasn't the sharp *shing* of a sword. It wasn't the *fwoosh* of a spell.
It was a deep, dull, resonant sound. The sound of something unstoppable hitting something immovable.

*DOOOOOM.*

Gong stopped. The vibration traveled through the ground, up his boots, and settled in his bones. It felt... correct.
He followed the sound.
In a secluded corner of the training yard, a lone disciple was practicing. He wasn't dancing around. He was standing perfectly still.
He held a large, square slab of black iron.
Another disciple was attacking him with a heavy mace.

*SMASH.*
The mace hit the iron slab.
The disciple behind the slab didn't move. He didn't dodge. He just braced his shoulder and absorbed the impact.
*DOOOOOM.*

The attacker struck again and again. The defender just stood there, breathing rhythmically. Grounding the force.
"Again," the defender grunted.

Gong watched, mesmerized.
It was beautiful.
No running away. No jumping. No fancy tricks.
Just... *No.*
The iron slab said *No*.

"What is that?" Gong asked a passing gardener.
The gardener looked at Gong, then at the pair. "That? That's just shield training. Boring stuff. Only the talentless ones use shields. Real cultivators rely on speed and barriers."

"Shield," Gong repeated. The word tasted heavy. Like a good potato.

He watched the disciple again.
If Gong had a shield... he wouldn't have to catch the wolves with his bare arms. He wouldn't have to be chewed on.
He could just put the wall between the bad thing and the soft thing (Xiao).
Less healing. More protecting.
Safety.

He looked at his own hands. They were wide. Strong. Made for holding.
He imagined holding a big piece of iron.
He imagined a rhino charging him, and him simply standing there, meeting the charge.
*DOOOOOM.*

Gong smiled. It was the first time he had seen a weapon that made sense to him.
"Shield," he whispered again.
He turned and walked back down the hill. He had to find more Fire-Root for Maggie. But his mind was buzzing with a new thought.

He needed a door. Or a wall. Or something very, very flat and heavy.
He needed to become a wall.
