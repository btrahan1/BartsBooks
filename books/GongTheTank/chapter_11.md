# Chapter 11: The Ugly Door

Gong needed a wall. Since he couldn't carry a wall, he decided to make one.

The "scrap heap" behind the Beast Pens was a treasure trove of broken things. Shattered wagon wheels, rusted plowshares, and planks of ironwood that were too knotted to be used for construction.
Gong spent the morning salvaging.
He found a heavy, square hatch from an old grain silo. It was made of iron-banded oak, about three inches thick. It was heavy.
*Good base,* he thought.

He reinforced it. He found a broken axle made of low-grade steel and bent it (using a convenient rock and his own body weight) to frame the wood. He hammered large, rusted nails through the bands.
For the handle, he used a thick leather strap from a broken harness, bolting it to the back with screws the size of carrots.

The result was hideous. It looked like a door that had been beaten up by a castle and then thrown away. It was splintery, rusted, and uneven.
Gong lifted it. It weighed as much as a small person.
He slid his arm through the strap. It fit snugly.
"Shield," Gong declared.

Now he needed a hammer.

He walked to Pen 5.
Pen 5 was the home of Bertha.
Bertha was not like the other females. She was larger, older, and perpetually annoyed. Her skin was the color of dark slate, and her horn was blunt from ramming things she didn't like (which was most things).
Even Brother Zhu avoided Pen 5.

Gong opened the gate.
Bertha was chewing on a dry stump. She stopped and glared at him. She let out a snort that kicked up a cloud of dust.

"Hey, Bertha," Gong said cheerfully. "How are you feeling today? Not too cranky I hope!"

Bertha narrowed her beady eyes. She scraped her hoof against the ground. *Scritch. Scritch.*

"I know, I know," Gong said, walking to the center of the muddy pen. He adjusted the ugly door on his left arm. "But, if you are cranky... I wouldn't mind if you helped me a little."

He planted his feet. He bent his knees, sinking into the stance he had watched the Rhinos use. He took a breath, packing the air into his gut. *Sedimenting.*
He held the shield up.

"If you want to bang your head against me a few times, feel free!"

Bertha didn't need an invitation. She needed a target.
She lowered her head. The muscles in her shoulders bunched up like rolling boulders.
She charged.

*THUD-THUD-THUD-THUD.*

The ground shook. A normal man would have run. A sensible cultivator would have dodged.
Gong watched her come. He didn't close his eyes. He leaned into the shield.

*Impact.*

**CRUNCH.**

The sound was not like the bell-toll in the training yard. It was wetter. Crunchier.
Bertha hit the shield with the force of a falling house.
Gong slid backward. His boots tore through the mud, digging two deep trenches. He slid one yard. Two yards. Three yards.

But he didn't fall.
And the shield... mostly held. The oak splintered. The iron rim bent. But it stayed between him and the horn.

Bertha pulled back, shaking her head. She looked confused. Usually, things broke. Or went flying. This thing just... slid.
She looked at Gong.
Gong looked at the shield. There was a massive dent in the center. His arm felt numb, vibrating with a dull, happy ache.

"Good hit, Bertha," Gong praised, his voice shaking slightly from the adrenaline. "Very solid."

Bertha snorted again. She looked at the shield, then at Gong. She seemed to decide that this new game was acceptable.
She backed up for another go.

"Okay," Gong said, resetting his feet. "One more. Then turnips."

He raised the Ugly Door. It wasn't pretty. It wasn't a spirit artifact. But it worked.
For the first time, Gong wasn't just enduring. He was *blocking*.
