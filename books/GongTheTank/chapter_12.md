# Chapter 12: The Angle

The month that followed was a blur of mud, sweat, and splinters.

Gong’s life settled into a rhythm that was heavier than before, but also more satisfying.
He worked. He hauled the feed, he mucked the pens, he scrubbed the hides.
He visited Pebble and Maggie. He brought them spicy roots and whispered to the belly. Pebble was getting huge, her rock-plates stretching to accommodate the life inside. Gong made sure she had the softest straw.

And then, he fought Bertha.

Every evening, before the sun went down, Gong would enter Pen 5.
Bertha would be waiting. She seemed to look forward to it. In a world where everything broke when she hit it, Gong was the one toy that didn't break.
Well, he didn't break *completely*.

The Ugly Door, however, suffered.
In the first week, Bertha smashed the oak planks to kindling.
Gong spent the night rebuilding it. He found a sheet of warped copper in the smithy's discard pile and nailed it over the wood.
*Heavier,* he thought. *Good.*

In the second week, the copper crumpled like paper.
Gong hammered it flat. Then he added a layer of leather padding between the wood and the metal to absorb the shock. He had noticed that the vibration hurt his bones more than the impact itself.

In the third week, he realized something important.
He was standing in the mud, shield raised. Bertha charged.
Usually, Gong stood square. He met the force like a wall. *Flat.*
*CRUNCH.*
It always pushed him back three yards. It always made his teeth rattle.

But this time, his foot slipped.
He didn't fall, but his shoulder dipped. The shield turned. It wasn't flat anymore. It was angled.
Bertha hit.
*SCREEE-THUMP.*

She didn't stop dead. Her horn hit the copper, slid off to the side, and she stumbled past him, crashing into the mud wall of the pen.
Gong didn't slide back. He just pivoted.
He stood there, blinking.
He looked at his shield. The dent was a long scratch, not a crater.
He looked at his arm. It didn't hurt.

"Huh," Gong said.

He thought about the roof of the barn. The rain didn't hit it flat. It hit it and slid off. If the roof was flat, the water would sit and rot the wood. The slant made the heavy water light.

He looked at Bertha, who was shaking the mud off her head and looking very offended.
"Again?" Gong offered.

She charged.
This time, Gong didn't try to be a wall. He tried to be a roof.
He stepped slightly to the side. He held the shield at an angle.
*Slant.*

Bertha hit. She slid off the copper facing with a screech of friction. Gong felt the impact, but he guided it away, letting her own momentum carry her past him.
He barely moved his feet.

It was less work. It was smarter.
Gong smiled.
He spent the rest of the week practicing the Slant. He experimented. Forty-five degrees was good. Too much angle and she missed the shield entirely and hit him (painful). Too little angle and it was a flat hit (also painful).
He had to find the sweet spot.

By the end of the month, the Ugly Door was a patchwork of three different metals, five types of wood, and innumerable nails. It was hideous. It was heavy.
But Gong knew every dent.
And he knew that if he held it just right, he didn't have to be stronger than the rhino. He just had to be... smoother.

"You are tricky," he told Bertha as he gave her a conciliatory turnip.
She chewed it aggressively, eyeing the shield. She respected the Ugly Door. It was the only thing in the pens as stubborn as she was.
