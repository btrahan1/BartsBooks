# Chapter 13: Two Hands

The following week, Gong was back at the edge of the training grounds. He was looking for scrap metal to reinforce the Ugly Door, which was starting to look less like a door and more like a metal salad.

"You there! Big guy! Come here!"

Gong looked up.
Standing in the center of the ring was the disciple with the mace. Up close, he was lean but corded with muscle, sweating profusely. He looked frustrated.
"Me?" Gong pointed to himself.
"Yes, you. The one who looks like a shaved bear."

Gong walked over. He recognized the disciple. This was the one who hit the shield guy.
"Hello," Gong said.

"I need a partner," the disciple snapped. He gestured to a pile of training gear. "Bran broke his arm yesterday. Blocked wrong. Weak bones."
He looked Gong up and down, squeezing Gong's bicep. It was like squeezing a tree branch.
"You look sturdy. Grab a weapon and a shield and let me beat on you. One copper coin per minute."

Gong’s eyes widened. A copper coin? He could buy a lot of turnips with that. Maybe even the premium ones from the import market.
"Okay."

Gong walked to the weapon rack.
There were wooden swords, blunted spears, and heavy iron maces.
And there were shields.
Gong picked up a square tower shield. It felt good. Heavy.
Then he looked at the maces. He didn't like them. They were for hitting. He wasn't good at hitting.
He looked at the other shields. There was a round buckler. A kite shield. And another square tower shield.

Gong put the mace back.
He picked up the second tower shield.
He strapped one to his left arm. He strapped the other to his right arm.
He stood there, basically a box with a head sticking out.

The disciple stared at him. "What are you doing?"
"Ready," Gong said.
"Where is your weapon?"
"Here," Gong raised his left shield. "And here." He raised his right shield.

The disciple rubbed his temples. "You can't fight with two shields. That's... that's stupid. How are you going to hit me?"
"I don't want to hit you," Gong explained. "You want to hit me. That is the job."

The disciple blinked. "Are you ambidextrous?"

Gong stared at him. It was a long word.
"Ambidextrous," the disciple repeated slowly. "Can you use both hands equally well?"

Gong looked at his left hand (shield). Then his right hand (shield).
"I have two hands," Gong stated comfortably. "I use them both. Just like everybody else."

The disciple let out a sharp laugh. It sounded like a bark. "Fine. Whatever. Just don't cry when I smash your fingers. I'm Brother Cai, by the way."
"Gong."
"Ready, Gong? I'm not holding back."

Cai spun his mace. It whistled in the air.
Gong sank into his stance. He brought the two shields together in front of him, overlapping them slightly. He wasn't a wall anymore. He was a fortress.
*Sediment.*

*CRACK.*

Cai didn't hold back. He swung an overhead smash that would have shattered a normal recruit's collarbone.
It hit the double-shield.
Gong’s knees bent. The ground cracked under his boots.
But the shields held.
And because he was using two arms to support the weight, the impact was distributed. It traveled down his spine, through his legs, and into the earth.

*DOOOOOM.*

Cai paused. His hands vibrated from the recoil.
"Solid," Cai muttered. "Very solid."
He swung again. A side swipe.
Gong moved his left shield to intercept, angling it. *Slant.*
The mace skittered off.

Cai grew faster. He unleashed a flurry of blows. High, low, side, diagonal.
Gong was slow. He couldn't dance. But he didn't need to dance. He just needed to close the door.
Left door. Right door. Double door.
He moved with a robotic, heavy efficiency. *Thud. Clang. Thump. DOOM.*

Five minutes passed.
Cai was panting. Sweat dripped from his nose.
Gong was still standing. He was breathing heavy, yes, but he wasn't hurt. He looked... patient. Like a rock waiting for the ocean to get tired.

Cai lowered his mace. He looked at Gong with a mix of exhaustion and delight.
"You," Cai wheezed, "are a freak of nature."

"Thank you," Gong said politely. "Five minutes. Five coppers?"

Cai laughed, wheezing. He reached into his pouch and threw a whole silver coin at Gong.
"Keep the change. Same time tomorrow?"

Gong caught the silver. It was heavy. It was beautiful.
"Okay," Gong agreed. "Same time."

He walked away, carrying the coin. He had a job. He had a friend (Xiao). He had a niece (Maggie). And now, he had a career.
Professional Wall.
