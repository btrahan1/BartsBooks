# Chapter 14: Sorry!

It started with Brother Cai. It didn't end with him.

Word spread quickly in the lower training courts. "There's a rock down by the east wall," they whispered. "You can hit it as hard as you want, and it doesn't break. And it doesn't complain."

By the second week, Gong had a line.
It was mostly outer disciples—young men and women desperate to test their new techniques but afraid of hurting each other (or getting hurt).
Gong charged one copper coin per minute. It was a standardized rate now.

He fought swords. *Clang.*
He fought spears. *Thump.*
He fought a girl with a chain-whip that wrapped around his shields. *Rattle.*

At first, he just stood there. The Fortress. Two shields locked together, enduring the storm.
But as the days went by, Gong realized something.
Every weapon had a rhythm.
The sword was *swish-swish-stab.*
The spear was *poke-poke-sweep.*
The mace was *swing-recover-swing.*

He started to move. Just a little.
When the spear poked, he didn't just block. He nudged his left shield forward, catching the tip and sliding it away.
When the mace swung, he stepped into it, meeting the force before it could build up maximum speed.

"He's getting... annoying," one disciple grumbled, wiping sweat from his brow. "I can't get a clean hit."
"Good," Gong said from behind his iron wall. "Clean hits hurt."

Then came Brother Mo.
Brother Mo was large. Not Gong-large, but big for a cultivator. He used a heavy two-handed practice sword, a "Greatsword" made of dense ironwood.
"I'm going to break your guard," Mo announced. He put down three silver coins. "Three minutes. No holding back."

Gong nodded. "Okay."

Mo charged. He didn't use finesse. He used momentum. He spun like a top, the greatsword acting as a flywheel.
*WHAM.*
Gong took the hit on both shields. He slid back a foot. his boots carving the dirt.
*WHAM.*
Another hit. He slid another foot.

Mo was overextended. He brought the sword up for a massive overhead cleave. He left his chest wide open.
Gong saw the opening.
He didn't think "Attack." He didn't think "Counter."
He just thought, *If I move forward, the sword can't come down.*

So he moved.
He stepped in, deep, invading Mo's swinging space.
And as he stepped, he turned his body. The motion whipped his right shield around in a tight arc.
He didn't mean to hit Mo. he just meant to put the shield in the way.

But Mo was rushing forward. Gong was rushing forward.
The edge of Gong's right shield caught Mo square in the chest.

*THWACK.*

It wasn't a cut. It wasn't a pierce. It was a collision.
Mo's feet left the ground. He flew backward, air leaving his lungs in a sharp *whoosh*. He landed on his back, skidding across the dirt, his greatsword clattering away.

Silence fell over the training corner.
The other disciples stared.
Gong stared.
He looked at his shield. He looked at Mo, who was wheezing like a deflated bellows.

Gong dropped his shields and ran over.
"Oh no," Gong said, his face filled with genuine panic. "I am so sorry!"

He knelt beside Mo, his massive hands hovering anxiously. "Did I break you? I didn't mean to break you. It was an accident. I just... turned."

Mo gasped, sucking in air. He sat up, rubbing his chest. He looked at Gong with wide eyes.
"You... you knocked me down," Mo wheezed. "I was in Earth Stance. I'm immovable."

"I moved you," Gong said apologetically. "I will give the silver back."

"No," Mo said. He started to laugh. It was a painful, wheezing sound. "No. That was... amazing. You didn't just block. You... slammed."

"Shield Slam," a watcher whispered. "He used a Shield Slam."

Gong looked at his hands.
Shield Slam.
It sounded violent. But... it was effective. Mo stopped attacking because he was on the ground.
If the bad thing is on the ground, it can't bite Xiao.

"I am sorry," Gong said again. "Are you okay?"

"I'm fine," Mo grinned, getting to his feet wobbly. "But next time? Charge me extra for that. That was a lesson."

Gong blinked.
He picked up his shields.
A lesson.
He could charge extra for hitting people?
This cultivation world was very strange. But Gong was starting to like it.
