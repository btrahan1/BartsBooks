# Chapter 15: The Adjustment

Gong didn't think of himself as a warrior. He was a service provider.
His service was "being hit."
And like any good worker, he wanted to provide the best service possible.

If he just stood there like a wall, the fast disciples got bored. Their swords bounced off, and they learned nothing.
If he just slammed everyone, the weak disciples got hurt. They stopped coming (and stopped paying).

He needed to... fit.
He needed to Adjust.

It happened with Sister Lin. She used a rapier—a thin, needle-like sword. She was fast, but she had no weight behind her strikes.
She would poke his shield. *Ting. Ting.*
Gong stood still.
"This is useless," Lin complained, lowering her sword. "It's like fighting a statue. I'm not getting any better."

Gong thought about it.
Momma said, "If the soup is too thick, add water. If the soup is too thin, add potato."
Sister Lin's fighting was... thin.
She needed resistance, but not a wall. She needed a moving target.

"Okay," Gong said. "I will Adjust."

When she thrust again, Gong didn't just block. He moved his shield slightly *away*, forcing her to extend her arm further.
She missed. She stumbled.
"Hey!"

"You didn't reach," Gong stated. "Reach further."

She tried again. She lunged deeper.
This time, she hit the shield. But because she was overextended, her balance was off.
Gong gently nudged her with the edge of his shield. Just a tap.
She fell over.

She sat in the dust, looking angry. Then, she looked thoughtful.
"I... I overcommitted," she whispered. "My stance was too wide."

"Yes," Gong agreed helpfully. "You fell down. Don't fall down."

She stood up, eyes fierce. "Again."

For the next hour, Gong Adjusted.
For the heavy hammer users, he became soft—deflecting their blows so they stumbled from their own momentum.
For the erratic dagger users, he became wide—using his shields to cut off their escape routes, forcing them to face him head-on.

He wasn't trying to teach them swordplay. He didn't know swordplay.
He was just trying to be the hardest thing to hit in the specific way they needed to hit something.

"You're doing it wrong," he told a young spearman who kept hitting the thickest part of the shield. "The rivets are hard. Your hands vibrate. Hit the wood part."
"But I'm trying to pierce the iron!"
"You are not strong enough," Gong said honestly. "Start with the wood. Build up to the iron."

The spearman flushed red. But he aimed for the wood. And his strikes got faster.

By the end of the month, the "Gong Corner" of the training ground was the busiest spot in the sect.
Disciples lined up. Not just to beat on the big guy, but to see what he would do. To see if they could hit him without falling over. To see if they could make him move.

Brother Cai watched from the sidelines, leaning on his mace.
"Look at them," Cai chuckled. "They think they're training him. But he's training them."

Gong didn't know about training.
He just knew that when he Adjusted, the customers stayed longer. They fought harder. They paid more.
And at night, when he went back to the pens, he practiced Adjusting with Bertha.
When she charged, he didn't just slant. He adjusted the angle of the slant based on her speed.
If she was fast, steep angle.
If she was slow, shallow angle.

"Good job, me," Gong whispered to himself as he repaired the Ugly Door for the hundredth time. "Employee of the month."
