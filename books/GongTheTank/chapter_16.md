# Chapter 16: The Bond

The storm came in the night, but the real storm was inside Pen 6.

Pebble was in labor. And it was going wrong.
Spirit Rhinos were creatures of earth. They were stubborn, durable, and slow. But birth required movement. It required flow.
Pebble was too heavy. Her Qi was locking up.

Brother Zhu was frantic. "The beast-healers are busy with the Elder's Sky-Crane! They said they can't come for a 'livestock issue'!"
He looked at the moaning rhino, then at Gong.
"She's going to die, Gong. The calf is stuck. Her energy is turning to stone."

Gong knelt in the mud. He hadn't left the pen in two days. He hadn't eaten. He hadn't slept.
He had his hand on Pebble's flank.
He felt the blockage. It was like a dam of solidified mud.
"No," Gong said. "She will not die."

"Gong, there's nothing we can do—"

"Shh," Gong silenced the foreman.
He closed his eyes.
He didn't know medical cultivation. He didn't know meridians.
But he knew weight. He knew how to pack dirt. And he knew how to *move* dirt.

*Sediment.*
He poured his own heavy, slow Qi into Pebble.
Usually, this would be a bad idea. Mixing Qi could cause an explosion.
But Gong's Qi wasn't aggressive. It was just... there. It was a stabilizer.
He didn't try to force the calf out. He tried to support the mother. He built a scaffold of energy under her, lifting the weight so she could focus on pushing.

"Push, Pebble," he whispered. "I have the weight. You push."

Hours passed. The rain lashed down, turning the pen into a swamp.
Gong didn't move. He was a statue of focus. His face was pale, his reserves draining into the rhino.
He felt the tiny heartbeat inside—Maggie—panicking.
"It's okay, Maggie," he murmured. "Uncle is here. Uncle is a wall."

Then, a shift.
The dam broke. Not with a crash, but with a slide.
Pebble let out a roar that shook the beams of the barn.
Gong felt a sudden release of pressure.

And there, in the straw, wet and shivering and grey as a storm cloud, was Maggie.
She was small (for a rhino), about the size of a large dog. She had clumsy legs and a tiny nub of a horn.
She sneezed.

Pebble collapsed, exhausted but alive. She nuzzled the calf.
Gong slumped back against the wooden fence. He felt hollowed out. But he was smiling.
"Hello, Maggie."

The calf wobbled to its feet. She ignored her mother. She walked straight to Gong.
She sniffed his hand. Then she headbutted his knee. A tiny *thump*.
She licked the mud off his knuckles.

Suddenly, the air in the pen chimed.
It wasn't a sound. It was a feeling. A golden ripple expanded from where Maggie touched Gong.
A voice, clear and mechanical, spoke in Gong's head. It was the same voice that spoke from the Appraisal Stone, but softer.

*[Notice: Spirit Beast Bond Established]*
*[Host: Gong]*
*[Beast: Iron-Hide Rhinoceros (Variant)]*
*[Bond Grade: Soul-Linked]*

Gong blinked. The golden light faded, leaving a faint, shimmering tether connecting his chest to Maggie's.
He looked at Brother Zhu, who was standing there with his mouth open.
He looked at the crowd of other laborers who had gathered by the fence to watch the birth.

"What is a bonded pet?" Gong asked out loud.

Discussion exploded.
The whispering was furious.
"Did you see the light?"
"A bond? With a labor disciple?"
"Who has a pet rhino?"
"That's inner sect stuff! You need a Beast Taming Medallion!"
"He just... did it. He just sat there and did it."

Brother Zhu walked forward slowly. He looked at Maggie, who was now curling up on Gong's feet to sleep.
"Gong," Zhu whispered. " You... you just bonded a Spirit Beast. That's... people pay thousands of spirit stones for a scroll to learn how to do that."

Gong scratched Maggie behind her tiny, soft ears.
"She is not a beast," Gong said. "She is Maggie. And she is cold."
He took off his heavy outer tunic and draped it over the calf.
"Bonded," Gong repeated, tasting the word. "Does that mean keep?"

Zhu laughed, a hysterical, incredulous sound. "Yes, Gong. It means keep. Forever."

"Good," Gong said. "I like keeps."
