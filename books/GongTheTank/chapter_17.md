# Chapter 17: The Bottle

The economy of the lower training grounds collapsed. Well, not collapsed, but it certainly slowed down.
The "Gong Corner" was empty.
Brother Cai arrived with his mace, ready to pay his weekly silver, and found only empty dirt. Brother Mo arrived with his greatsword, eager to practice not falling down, and found nothing.

"Where is the Wall?" Cai asked.
"Maybe he quit?" Mo suggested.
"He can't quit," Cai grumbled. "He's the only one who can take my Dragon-Tooth Strike without dying."

So, they went looking.
It wasn't hard to find him. They just followed the smell of hay and the sound of cooing.

When the group of inner disciples arrived at the Beast Pens, they stopped dead.
They expected to see Gong hauling manure. They expected to see him wrestling beasts.

What they saw was Gong sitting on his log. He was holding a glass bottle filled with a milky white liquid (a special formula of crushed spirit-herbs and goat milk).
In his lap, wrapped in a blanket that looked suspiciously like a disciple's outer robe, was a grey, wrinkly creature.
Maggie was suckling greedily, her tiny ears wiggling with every gulp.

Gong looked up. He saw the gathered warriors—men and women who could split boulders and summon fire.
"Shh," Gong whispered. "She is eating."

The disciples stood there, awkward in their armor.
"Is that... a rhino?" Cai whispered.
"It's so... small," Mo added.

Gong burped the baby. It was a surprisingly loud *BELCH*.
"Good girl," Gong praised softly.

Just then, Xiao bustled out of the barn. She was wearing her pristine green healer's robes, but the hem was stained with mud. She carried a basket of herbs.
"Gong, I checked Pebble," she said, ignoring the crowd of stunned disciples. "Her infection is gone. The Spirit Bond is helping her recover faster than I expected. You sharing your vitality with her really did the trick."

She stopped and looked at the audience.
"Oh. Hello, Brothers. Sisters."
She recognized some of them. Brother Cai was famous. Brother Mo was a rising star.
"Are you here for the rhinos?" she asked sweetly.

"We... came for Gong," Cai stammered. "We want to hit him."

Gong looked up from the bottle. "Not today. Paternity leave."

"Paternity...?" Cai looked at the rhino calf. "Gong, please tell me you aren't the father."

"I am the Uncle," Gong corrected. "And the Bond."
He lifted Maggie up. She let out a squeak.
"This is Maggie. She is new."

The tension in the air, usually thick with martial intent, dissolved.
One of the female disciples, the one with the chain whip, melted. "Oh, look at her nose! It's so squishy!"
She stepped forward. "Can I...?"

"Wash hands first," Gong ordered sternly. "No dirty fighting hands on the baby."

The deadly cultivator immediately ran to the water trough and scrubbed her hands.

For the next hour, the deadliest disciples of the Iron Mountain Sect stood around a mud pit, watching a giant man feed a baby beast.
Xiao moved among them, checking their pulses out of habit.
"You're stressed, Brother Cai," she noted. "You should hold the baby. It lowers blood pressure."

"I will not hold the—"
Gong thrust the sleeping calf into Cai's arms.
Cai froze. He held Maggie like she was a bomb made of glass.
Maggie uncurled and yawned, revealing gums with no teeth. She snuggled into Cai's silk chest armor.
Cai looked down. His face, usually twisted in a scowl of concentration, relaxed.
"Huh," Cai whispered. "She's... heavy."

"Density," Gong nodded. "Good stock."

Eventually, the sun began to set.
"I will be back tomorrow," Gong promised them. "I have to buy more milk formula. It is expensive."

Cai carefully handed Maggie back. He reached into his pouch and dropped a heavy bag of silver into Gong's lap.
"Pre-payment," Cai muttered. "For the next ten sessions. Buy the good milk."

He turned to leave, trying to regain his martial dignity.
"And Gong?"
"Yes?"
"Bring the kid. She... balances the Qi."

Gong smiled. "Okay."
He looked at Xiao, who was beaming.
They were a strange family here in the mud. A healer, a tank, a mother, and a baby.
But it worked. It was solid.
