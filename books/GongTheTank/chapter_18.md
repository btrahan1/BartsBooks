# Chapter 18: The Manager

Maggie grew like a weed. A very heavy, armored weed.
Within a month, she was no longer a wobbly calf but a sturdy tank-puppy. Her skin had hardened into proper slate-grey plates, and her horn was a respectable nub.

She accompanied Gong everywhere.
When Gong stood in his corner, shields raised, Maggie sat behind him in the "Safe Zone," chewing on a premium fire-root that a disciple had brought as a tribute.
Between rounds, sweating warriors would line up not just to pay Gong, but to pet the rhino.
"Who's a good stone-crusher?" Brother Mo cooed, scratching Maggie's belly. "You are! Yes, you are!"
Maggie snorted contentedly. She was spoiled rotten.

But something else was happening. The disciples were getting *good*.
Brother Mo wasn't falling over anymore. Sister Lin's strikes had weight. Brother Cai had learned to conserve his stamina.
The "Gong Corner" wasn't just a side-show; it was producing results.

High above, in the Observation Pavilion, the Sect Elders took notice.
"The outer disciples' win rate has increased by 40% this month," Elder Iron-Heart noted, stroking his beard. "Specifically, the ones who train near the dung-heap. Why?"
"There is a helper," another elder reported. "A labor disciple with two shields. He.. corrects them."
"A laborer teaching cultivators?" Iron-Heart frowned. "Test him. Send Feng."

***

The next day, the crowd at Gong's corner parted.
A young man walked through. He wore the azure robes of the Core Disciples—one step below the Elders. He carried a long, wrapped spear on his back.
He didn't swagger. He walked with a quiet, efficient grace.
"I am Feng," he said, bowing slightly to Gong. "I have heard of your... services."

The crowd went silent. Feng was famous. The "Piercing Wind." He was known for his precision. He didn't break walls; he found the cracks in them.

Gong bowed back. "I am Gong. This is Maggie."
Maggie burped.

"I wish to test this defense," Feng said. "I am not here to bully. I am here to learn."
He reached for his spear.

Suddenly, a mace blocked his path.
Brother Cai stepped forward. He looked terrified—Feng outranked him significantly—but he stood his ground.
"Hold on, Brother Feng," Cai said, his voice cracking only slightly.

Feng raised an eyebrow. "Yes?"

"Gong is... fully booked," Cai lied. "And his rates have changed for high-level consultation."
Cai puffed out his chest. "I represent his interests. As his... Manager."

Gong blinked. "Manager?"
"Shh," Cai hissed. "I'm negotiating."
He turned back to Feng. "If you want a session, it's five silver. Up front. And you have to bring a fruit for the rhino."

The crowd gasped. Consorting with a laborer was one thing; extorting a Core Disciple was another.
Feng stared at Cai. Then at Gong. Then at Maggie, who was looking at him expectantly.

Feng smiled. It was a genuine smile.
"Professional," Feng noted. "I like it."
He produced five silver coins and a pristine Sun-Pear from his spatial ring. He tossed the coins to Cai and the pear to Maggie.

"Acceptable?" Feng asked.

"Pleasure doing business," Cai exhaled, looking like he might faint from relief. "Gong, you're up. Don't die."

Gong stepped forward. He raised his shields—the Ugly Door and its twin.
Feng unwrapped his spear. The tip gleamed with cold light.
"Ready," Gong said.

Feng didn't charge. He flowed. The spear moved like liquid mercury. *Thrust. Retract. Thrust.*
Gong moved. *Block. Slant. Adjust.*
He didn't try to stop the spear; he guided it.
*Ting. Ting. Slide.*

Feng's eyes narrowed in focus. He increased the speed.
Gong increased the angle.
For ten minutes, they danced. A storm of wind against a mountain of stone.
Feng found a crack—a gap between the shields. He thrust.
Gong *twisted*, closing the gap with his shoulder, trapping the spear tip for a microsecond.
Feng yanked it back, surprised.

"Good," Feng breathed. "Very good."
He stopped. He wasn't winded, but he was glowing with the joy of a puzzle solved.
"You force me to be accurate," Feng said. "If I am one inch off, you catch me. Most opponents just try to kill me. You try to... fix me."

"You are very sharp," Gong complimented. "My shields are worried."

Feng laughed. He bowed deeply—a bow of equals.
"I will be back, Manager Cai," Feng called out. "Keep my slot open."

Cai, clutching the silver, beamed. "VIP status, Brother Feng! Anytime!"

Gong sat down on his log. Maggie crunched her Sun-Pear.
Gong looked at Cai. "Manager is good job?"
"The best, buddy," Cai grinned. "The best."
