# Chapter 20: The Heavy Core

Gong woke up to the smell of medicinal herbs and the sound of arguing.
He was lying on a cot in the field tent. Maggie was asleep on his feet, acting as a very heavy blanket.

"...impossible density," a voice was whispering. "I tried to inject Qi to scan it, and my probe just... bent."

Gong opened one eye.
The tent was crowded. Xiao was there, looking exhausted but relieved. Brother Cai and Brother Feng were hovering in the corner.
But the space around his bed was dominated by three old men in elaborate robes—Elders.
And a new healer, a man with silver needles floating around his head.

"He's awake," Xiao announced. She moved quickly to his side. "Gong? How is your stomach?"

"Still full," Gong grunted, sitting up. The cot creaked alarmingly. "Like I ate a cannonball."

"You essentially did," the floating-needle healer muttered. "Lie back down. We are trying to classify your Core."
He turned to the Elders.
"Look at the resonance. It shines with the dull brown of a Low-Grade Copper Foundation. That is the lowest tier of success. Usually, this means the cultivator has weak potential and impure Qi."

Elder Fang—the same elder who had called Gong a "moving rock" months ago—stroked his long beard. "So, he barely passed? A Copper Core. As expected of a laborer."

"Wait," the healer corrected. "It *looks* like Copper. But look at the mass."
He placed a spirit scale on Gong's chest. The needle slammed to the maximum reading instantly. *CLINK.*
"It weighs more than Platinum," the healer said, his voice trembling. "It appears to be Copper, but it feels like... Iron. Condensed, pressurized Iron."

"A Copper Core with the density of a star-metal alloy?" Fang frowned. "That makes no sense. It took him longer than the other recruits to establish it, which suggests low talent..."

"But that's just it!" another Elder argued. "He's not a disciple! He has no training! He hasn't touched a cultivation manual in his life! How did he form a Foundation—even a weird one—by shoveling manure?"

The tent went silent. The question hung in the air.
How did a boy who couldn't circulate Qi, who had been rejected by every master, suddenly achieve the first major step of immortality?

From the back of the tent, a wisened voice spoke up.
"What do you think he has been doing for the last six months?"

It was Elder Iron-Heart, the head of the Combat Hall. He stepped forward, his eyes sharp.
"You say he has no training?" Iron-Heart pointed at Gong. "Look at his arms. Look at his skin."
He gestured to the bruises that were already fading.
"He has taken thousands of strikes. Thousands of impacts. Every time he was hit, he didn't just block. He absorbed."

Iron-Heart looked at Gong with a strange respect.
"Normal disciples cultivate by sitting in a room and breathing air. They take the energy in gently. It is safe. It is efficient."
He paused.
"This boy cultivated by having energy *beaten* into him. He didn't spin his Qi. He compacted it. Layer by layer. Hit by hit. Like forging a sword, but he is the metal."

He turned to Elder Fang.
"You called him a rock, Fang. You were right. He is a sedimentary rock. He didn't grow. He accumulated."

Gong blinked. He didn't understand the metaphors.
"Does this mean I'm sick?" Gong asked.

Iron-Heart laughed. "No, boy. It means you are heavy. And you are solid."
He looked at the other Elders.
"Foundation Established. Grade: Anomaly. I say we let him keep doing what he's doing. It seems to be working."

"But he needs a manual!" Fang sputtered. "He needs a technique!"

"He has a technique," Iron-Heart smiled. "It's called 'Work'."
He turned to Gong.
"Rest up, disciple. You have a new shift starting soon. The world is getting more dangerous, and we might need a wall that doesn't break."

Gong nodded. "Okay. Can I have a turnip now?"

Iron-Heart reached into his own sleeve and pulled out a Spirit-Ginseng worth ten years of cultivation. He tossed it to Gong.
"Have a big turnip."
