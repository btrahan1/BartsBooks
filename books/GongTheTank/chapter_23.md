# Chapter 23: The Rolling Stone

Floor 2 was quieter than Floor 1. And darker.
The bioluminescent mushrooms were gone, replaced by jagged stalactites and deep shadows.

"Trap check," Pip whispered, halting the group with a raised fist.
He crept forward, his eyes glowing with a faint wind-enchantment.
"Tripwire," he pointed to a thin silver thread stretched across the path. "And a pressure plate. It's primitive."

"Can you disarm it?" Lin asked, her hand on her sword hilt.
"Please," Pip scoffed. "I was top of my class in Mechanics."
He pulled out a set of delicate tools. He knelt by the wall, fiddling with a hidden mechanism.
"Just... cut... this..."

*CLICK.*

The sound echoed like a gunshot.
Pip froze. "Uh oh."

A low rumble started from the darkness ahead. The floor began to vibrate.
"Run!" Zhi screamed. "It's a boulder trap!"
Pip scrambled back, pale as a ghost. "I turned it the wrong way!"

Out of the gloom, a massive sphere of stone—easily ten feet tall—came thundering down the corridor. It scraped the walls, shooting sparks. It was moving fast enough to crush bone to paste.

The party scrambled. Lin and Zhi pressed themselves into a small alcove. Pip dove behind a rock.
But the corridor was narrow. The boulder filled it.
Except for the middle.

Gong didn't run. He stepped forward.
"Gong! Move!" Xiao shrieked.

"No room," Gong said calmely. "I have it."
He widened his stance. Deep Horse Stance. He channeled his Qi into his boots, rooting himself into the cavern floor.
*Sediment.*
He became part of the mountain.

The boulder roared closer.
Gong didn't try to stop it dead—that would be stupid. Physics was physics.
He raised his left shield, angling it sharply. *The Slant.*
He braced his shoulder.

*CRASH.*
The impact was like being hit by a train. Gong's boots gouged deep furrows in the stone. Smoke rose from his shield.
But he didn't break.
He *guided*.
With a grunt of exertion, he shoved the boulder to the right. It deflected off his shield, slammed into the wall with a deafening screech, and ground to a halt, wedged tight.

dust settled.
Gong stood up, shaking stone chips off his shoulders.
"Trap disarmed," he said.

Pip peered out from his rock. "You... you just parried a rock. A really big rock."
"It was heavy," Gong admitted. "Good practice."

They moved on, shaken but alive.
The next challenge wasn't a trap. It was the ceiling.
"Screech!"
From the high shadows, shapes descended. Gloom-Bats. Large, leathery, and fast.
There were dozens of them.
They didn't land. They swooped, clawing at Zhi's face and tangling in Lin's hair.

"Get off!" Zhi flailed, his fire spells missing the erratic targets.
"I can't hit them!" Lin growled, slashing at empty air. "They're too high!"

Gong waved his shields. "Come here!"
The bats ignored him. They knew the big metal thing couldn't fly. They focused on the soft, squishy mages.

Gong frowned. He couldn't jump that high. He couldn't throw his shields (he needed them).
But he could be loud.

"Hey!" Gong shouted.
He slammed the shields together. *CLANG.*
The sound wave rippled through the cavern.
The bats shrieked, their sonar disrupted. They turned their ugly, furry heads toward Gong.

"YOU! DATE! ME!" Gong yelled.
"Face!" Xiao corrected from the back. "Face you!"
"FACE ME!" Gong corrected. *CLANG. CLANG.*

The noise was irritating. It was a challenge.
The swarm turned as one. They abandoned the mages and dove at the source of the headache.
A funnel of leathery wings descended on Gong.

"Now!" Gong shouted, hunkering down under his shields. "They are grouped!"

Zhi grinned. "Finally."
He raised his staff. *Cone of Flame.*
A sheet of fire washed over Gong, incinerating the bats that swarmed him.
Gong felt the heat—it was warm, like a cozy blanket—but his stone skin ignored the burn.
Lin dashed in, her rapier a blur, piercing the few bats that survived the fire.

In seconds, the floor was covered in ash and bat wings.
Gong stood up, brushing soot off his nose.
"Good team," he nodded. "Zhi makes good fire."

Zhi puffed out his chest. "I do, don't I?"

They walked toward the heavy iron doors at the end of the hall. The Boss Room.
"Ready?" Gong asked.
"Ready," the team said. And for the first time, they sounded like they meant it.
