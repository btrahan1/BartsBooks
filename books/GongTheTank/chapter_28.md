# Chapter 28: No Shields

The Silver Bastion's training ground was pristine. White stone tiles, polished weapons racks, and magical barriers to contain stray energy.
Compared to the muddy Beast Pens, it felt like a palace.

Gong stood ready. He had polished his two tower shields until they gleamed (mostly).
"I am ready, Teacher Gaul," Gong said, slamming his shields together. *Clang.*

Gaul stood in the center of the arena, holding a thin wooden practice sword. He wasn't wearing his armor today, just a simple white tunic that showed off arms like tree trunks.
"Good," Gaul nodded. "It is time to begin! First, get rid of your shields!"

Gong froze. He blinked.
"My... shields?"

"YES!" Gaul pointed to the far corner of the arena. "Place them in the corner. Stare at them if you must. But you will not touch them."

Gong clutched the handles tighter. "But... I am the Wall. A wall without bricks is just air."

"A wall that cannot move is a target," Gaul countered. "You rely on the metal too much. You take hits you should dodge. You absorb force you should deflect. To truly master the Shield, you must first learn how not to get hit *without* it."

Gong looked at his shields. He looked at the corner.
It felt like being asked to take off his skin.
Slowly, painfully, he walked over and propped them up. He patted them gently.
"I will be back," he whispered to the steel.

He walked back to the center. He felt light. Too light. He felt naked. Wobbly.
"I am ready," Gong said, holding his empty hands up in awkward fists.

"Good," Gaul grinned. "Now. Don't let me hit you."

*Thwack.*
The wooden sword smacked Gong in the ribs.
"Ow," Gong grunted. He hadn't even seen it move.

"Too slow!" Gaul shouted. "Move your feet! Don't root yourself!"
*Thwack.* (Shoulder).
*Thwack.* (Thigh).

Gong tried to move. But his instinct—honed by thousands of hours of rhino-mucking and disciple-bashing—was to *brace*.
Every time Gaul swung, Gong's body automatically dropped into a horse stance. He tensed his muscles. He prepared to take the hit.
And he took it. perfectly.

"NO!" Gaul yelled, hitting him again. "Do NOT sediment! Do NOT pack! FLOW!"

"I don't flow!" Gong argued, rubbing a welt on his arm. "I settle!"

"You are a stone in a river!" Gaul lectured, dancing around him with frustrating grace. "The water goes around the stone! Be the water!"

"But I am the stone!"
*Thwack.*

For an hour, it continued.
Gong looked like he had been put in a sack with a dozen angry badgers. He was covered in bruises.
He was panting, not from exertion, but from the mental strain of trying to override his own core.
He simply could not dodge. If something came at him, he wanted to hit it back or block it. Moving *away* felt wrong. It felt like failure.

Gaul stopped. He leaned on his wooden sword, wiping sweat from his brow.
He looked at Gong, who was currently standing in a perfect, immovable stance, ready to be hit again.

"By the Ancestors," Gaul muttered. "Your instincts are... remarkably consistent."

"Thank you," Gong said politely.

"That wasn't a compliment!" Gaul sighed. "You have zero evasion. None. Your body literally refuses to not get hit."

He looked at the bruises on Gong, which were already fading thanks to the boy's ridiculous constitution.
"This might take longer than I thought," Gaul admitted.
He rubbed his chin.
"To teach a rock to float... we need a different approach."
