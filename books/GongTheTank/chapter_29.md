# Chapter 29: The Chicken

The next morning, Gong arrived early. He was hungry.
Gaul had told him to skip breakfast. "Empty stomach, sharp eyes," the Paladin had said.

Gaul was waiting in the arena. He held a small wooden crate.
From inside came a distinct *cluck*.

"Is that a chicken?" Gong asked, his stomach rumbling.
"Yes!" Gaul beamed. He opened the crate.

A creature hopped out. It looked like a normal chicken, but its feathers were a blur of red and gold, and its eyes gleamed with unnatural intelligence.
It scratched the stone floor, sending sparks flying.
A Spirit Chicken. Known for running at 40 miles per hour and changing direction in mid-air.

"Your breakfast," Gaul announced.
He pointed at the bird.
"Your job is to catch it. You can eat when you do."

Gong looked at the chicken. The chicken looked at Gong. It tilted its head.
"Catch... and cook?" Gong clarified.
"Catch, cook, eat. Whatever you want. But no weapons. No shields. Just hands."

Gaul checked the sundial on the wall.
"I have a meeting with the Sect Master. I'll be back in four hours."
He slapped Gong on the back.
"Good luck. Try not to starve."

And then he left.

Gong stood alone with the chicken.
"Here, chick-chick," Gong said softly.
He took a step forward.
*ZIP.*
The chicken was gone. It was now on the other side of the arena, pecking at an imaginary worm.

Gong narrowed his eyes.
He charged.
*Thud-thud-thud.*
He was fast for a big guy. He closed the distance in seconds. He lunged.
*FLAP.*
The chicken didn't run away. It ran *up*. It bounced off Gong's knee, fluttered over his shoulder, and landed behind him.
*Cluck.*

Gong turned. He dove.
*SWISH.*
The chicken side-stepped.

For the first hour, it was funny. A giant boy diving onto stone tiles, missing a bird that weighed three pounds.
For the second hour, it was frustrating. Gong was sweating. He was scraped. And he was really, really hungry.
For the third hour, it was war.

Gong tried strategy. He tried to herd it into a corner.
But the arena was round. There were no corners.
He tried to ambush it. But he was as stealthy as a falling tree.
He tried to "Taunt" it.
*CLAP.* "FACE ME!"
The chicken just ignored him. It wasn't a monster. It was poultry. It had no pride to wound.

By the fourth hour, Gong was lying face down on the cool stone.
The chicken was perched on his butt, preening its feathers.

The doors opened. Gaul walked in.
He looked at the sweaty, defeated disciple. He looked to the triumphant bird.
"Hungry?" Gaul asked.

"Yes," Gong muffled into the floor.

"Good," Gaul nodded. "Hunger is a teacher. Tomorrow, we try again. But maybe... try moving your feet before your hands."
He tossed a bag of grain to the chicken.
"See you at dawn, Gong. Still hungry."
