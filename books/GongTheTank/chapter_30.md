# Chapter 30: The Audience

Day Two.
Word had spread. "The Wall is fighting a Chicken."
It was the kind of rumor that emptied dining halls.

When Gong arrived at the arena, dragging his feet from hunger, the stands were not empty.
Brother Cai was there, eating a pork bun (Gong's stomach growled loudly).
Brother Mo was there. Sister Lin. Even Brother Feng, the Core Disciple, had stopped by to watch.

Gaul stood in the center, arms crossed.
"Begin," he said simply.

The chicken, looking well-rested and smug, scratched the floor.
Gong sighed. "Please. Just one leg. You don't need both."
He lunged.

*HO PAI!*
The chicken fluttered over his head.

"Come on, Gong!" Brother Cai shouted, spraying crumbs. "You can do it!"
"Move your feet!" Brother Mo yelled. "Don't plant them! Glide!"
"Bend your knees!" Sister Lin advised. "Lower your center of gravity!"
"Use your hips when you turn!" almost everyone shouted at once.

Gong tried. He really did.
He bent his knees. He moved his hips.
He looked like a dancing bear trying to catch a mosquito.
*Crash.*
He slammed into the barrier. The chicken pecked his ear.

"Left! It's going left!"
"Cut it off!"
"Dive!"

The advice rained down. It was well-meaning, but useless.
Gong wasn't a duelist. He wasn't a scout. He was a tank. His body knew how to stop things, not chase them.
Every time he tried to switch directions, his momentum fought him. He skidded. He stumbled.
He spent four hours providing high-quality entertainment for the entire sect.

At sunset, Gaul whistled.
"Time."

Gong collapsed. He didn't even have the energy to groan.
The crowd dispersed, shaking their heads.
"He's got no talent for speed," one whispered.
"Shame. He's a good Wall, but he's useless without it."

Gong lay there, listening to them leave.
He was empty. His stomach was twisting in knots.
"Tomorrow," Gaul said, tossing grain to the bird.
"Tomorrow," Gong whispered.

***

High above, in the Observation Pavilion.
The Elders were drinking Spirit Wine. They watched the Paladin walk out of the arena.

"Brutal," Elder Willow murmured. "He hasn't eaten in two days. Do you think he will catch it?"
She looked at Gaul, who had joined them on the balcony.

The Paladin snorted, pouring himself a large goblet of wine.
"Of course not!"
He took a long drink.
"That is a Golden-Step Pheasant. It can dodge a Grandmaster. Gong has the agility of a tectonic plate. He could chase it for a hundred years and never touch a feather."

Elder Iron-Heart frowned. "Then why? Is this cruelty?"

"It is a test," Gaul said, wiping his mouth. "Not of speed. But of will."
He pointed down at the empty arena, where Gong had dragged himself away.
"Most disciples would have quit yesterday. They would have complained. They would have cheated and used Qi."
Gaul smiled, a sharp, dangerous smile.
"Gong just keeps trying. The challenge isn't to catch the bird. The challenge is to see when he'll give up."
He looked at the Elders.
"I'm betting he starves first."
