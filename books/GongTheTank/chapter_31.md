# Chapter 31: Be The Chicken

Night had fallen over the Iron Mountain Sect. The crickets were chirping. The wind was rustling the bamboo.
And Gong’s stomach was roaring like a trapped beast.

He sat on his log outside the beast pens, staring at the moon.
He hadn’t eaten in two days.
For a boy who defined himself by his mass, this was torture. He felt light. Windy.

Xiao found him there. She was carrying a basket.
The smell hit him first. Sweet red bean paste. Steamed dough. Warmth.
Sticky buns. His favorite.

"Gong," Xiao whispered, sitting beside him. "You look terrible."

"I am empty," Gong agreed hoarsely. "Like a drum."

"Eat," she said, holding out a bun. It was fluffy and perfect. "Gaul isn't here. He won't know."

Gong looked at the bun. His mouth watered. His hand twitched.
But he didn't take it.
"No."

Xiao’s eyes widened. "Gong, you're starving. It's just a test. It's not worth dying over."

"It is," Gong said, his voice surprisingly steady. "Momma said, 'If you do a job, do it right.' My job is to catch the chicken. If I eat, I didn't do the job."
He looked at her, his eyes hollow but burning with a strange intensity.
"Xiao... you are smart. You passed the tests. How can I catch that chicken?"

Xiao looked at her friend. She saw the bruises on his knees from diving onto stone. She saw the exhaustion. But she also saw the stubbornness that made him the Wall.
She lowered the bun eventually.
"Gong," she said softly. "You keep trying to catch it like a wolf catches a rabbit. You chase. You predict."

"It is fast," Gong argued.

"It is," she agreed. "Faster than you. You can't beat it with speed. You have to understand it."
She stood up, brushing crumbs from her lap.
"The chicken doesn't think about 'left' or 'right'. It just flows. It reacts."

She put a hand on his shoulder. A wave of warm, green energy flowed into him—not food, but comfort. She could feel his hunger pangs vibrating through her own core. It made her heart ache.
"Be the chicken, Gong," she whispered. "Don't chase it. Be it."

She turned to leave, picking up the basket. She couldn't watch him starve. It hurt too much.
"I'll leave the buns by the door," she choked out. "In case you change your mind."
Then she hurried away into the darkness.

Gong sat alone.
He looked at the moon.
"Be the chicken," he muttered.
He closed his eyes.
What is a chicken?
Small. Fast.
Scared? No, the Spirit Chicken wasn't scared. It was... annoying. It was free.

"Be the chicken," Gong repeated.
His stomach grumbled.
"Be the hungry chicken."
