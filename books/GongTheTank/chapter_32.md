# Chapter 32: The Touch

The sun hadn't even crested the Iron Mountain when Gong walked into the arena.
Gaul was already there, feeding the chicken.
"You're early," the Paladin noted.
"I'm hungry," Gong replied. His voice was low, rasping. His eyes were locked on the bird.

The Golden-Step Pheasant looked up. It ruffled its feathers. It remembered this big, slow human.
Gong didn't charge this time. He stepped into the ring.
He lowered his center of gravity.
"Let's dance, little bird," Gong whispered. "Let's dance."

He moved.
Not a lunge. A slide.
The chicken hopped left. Gong didn't chase. He stepped left, cutting off the angle.
The chicken looked surprised. It fluttered right.
Gong was already moving right.

*Be the chicken.*
*The chicken likes space. It hates corners. It flows to the open air.*

For four hours, they circled.
Gong was still too slow. The bird was supernatural. But Gong wasn't crashing into walls anymore. He was herding.
Around noon, Gaul stood up.
"Time," Gaul called out. "That's four hours. Rest."

Gong didn't stop. He didn't even look at Gaul.
"No," Gong grunted, sweat pouring down his face. "I can keep going."

Gaul paused. "Gong, you haven't eaten in three days. You will collapse."
"I am the Wall," Gong said, spinning to block the chicken's escape route. "Walls don't collapse."

Gaul sat back down. He pulled out a communication talisman.
"Elders," he whispered into it. "You might want to see this."

By hour six, the arena was full again. The word had spread: *The Wall is still going.*
By hour seven, the crowd was silent. No one was shouting advice.
They were watching a test of endurance that defied logic.
Gong was stumbling. His lips were cracked. But his eyes were terrifyingly focused.
He was reading the bird.
*Tail twitch left -> Jump right.*
*Head dip -> Sprint.*

Hour eight. The sun was touching the horizon.
The chicken was tired. It had never been chased this long. It was used to bursts of speed, not a marathon.
It made a mistake.
It fluttered toward the center—the largest space—but it hesitated.

Gong saw it.
"Be the chicken," he roared.
He didn't run. He launched himself.
It wasn't a graceful dive. It was a calculated collision.
The chicken squawked and engaged its flash-step, blurring out of existence.

But not fast enough.
Gong's thick, calloused fingers slashed through the air.
*Brush.*

He hit the ground hard, sliding ten feet on his chest.
He lay there, gasping.
In his hand, caught between his thumb and forefinger, was a single, shimmering golden feather.

The chicken landed on the far wall, looking ruffled and offended. It let out an indignant *SQUAWK*.

The crowd gasped.
Brother Cai dropped his mace.
"He... he touched it?"

High above, the wine goblet slipped from Elder Fang's hand and shattered on the floor.
"Impossible," Fang whispered. "That is a Golden-Step. No Foundation cultivator has ever touched one without movement arts."

Elder Iron-Heart leaned over the balcony, a grin spreading across his face.
"He didn't use speed," Iron-Heart laughed. "He used pressure. He just kept walking until the bird ran out of world."

In the arena, Gong rolled over. He held up the feather.
"Gaul," he wheezed. "Does a feather count as a drumstick?"
