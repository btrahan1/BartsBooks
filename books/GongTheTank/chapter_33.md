# Chapter 33: The Feast

The Sect Kitchens were usually loud, chaotic places.
Tonight, they were oddly quiet.
Or rather, the noise was hushed.

In the center of the main dining hall, tables had been pushed together.
Sitting alone at this makeshift banquet table was Gong.
In front of him were:
- Six baskets of sticky buns.
- A roast pig.
- Three ducks.
- A vat of congee.
- And a mountain of pickled vegetables.

Surrounding him, standing on benches, leaning against pillars, and peeking from the kitchen doors, was... everyone.
The crowd that had watched him fail for two days was now watching him succeed at the one thing he was truly a Grandmaster at: Eating.

*Chomp. Slurp. Swallow.*
Gong worked through the food with the rhythmic efficiency of a woodchipper. He didn't rush. He didn't make a mess. He just... processed.

"He touched it?" a junior disciple whispered near the door.
"I saw it," Brother Cai confirmed, his eyes wide. "He didn't use Qi. He didn't use a movement technique. He just... was there."
"Is that even possible?"
"Gaul says it's impossible. But he has the feather."

Gong reached for a duck. He stripped the meat from the bone in one clean motion.
"I heard he actually plucked a feather!"
"Plucked? I heard he grabbed the tail and the bird shed it to escape!"
"I heard he ate the bird," someone lied.
"No," Gong said, pausing mid-chew. "Bird was too fast. Only feather."
He swallowed.
"Feather was dry. Not tasty."

The crowd laughed. It was a release of tension. The legend of the "Unmovable Wall" was becoming something else. Something stranger.

A cook came out with another tray. Steamed dumplings.
"More?" the cook asked, looking terrified and impressed.
"Please," Gong nodded politely. "I missed breakfast. And lunch. And dinner. times three."

"How much can one person eat?" a female disciple whispered, looking at Gong's flat stomach (which was somehow still flat, thanks to his hyper-dense muscle structure).
"He is building his foundation," Brother Feng said from the back of the room. The Core Disciple was leaning against a wall, smiling. "Every brick needs mortar."

Gong wiped his mouth with a napkin. He looked at the empty platters.
He looked at the crowd.
"Thank you," he said earnestly. "The food is good. But..."
He looked around.
"Where is the chicken? I think we are friends now."

The room erupted in laughter again.
Gong smiled. He was full. He had done the job.
And he had a souvenir.
Tucked behind his ear, gleaming in the lantern light, was a single, golden feather.
