# Chapter 35: The Drop

It had been a month.
Autumn had turned to early Winter. The air was crisp.
Gong was running laps.
*Thud. Thud. Thud.*
Every step was heavy. He wore iron bands on his wrists, his ankles, and a vest of lead plates. One hundred pounds of "Training Armor."
He had slept in them. He had eaten in them. He had even used the latrine in them (which was a challenge in itself).

Gaul leaned against the arena wall, watching his student churn the earth.
"He doesn't complain," Gaul noted. "He just sweats."

Elder Willow stood beside him, wrapped in a fur-lined cloak.
"So, today is the day?" she asked.

"Yes," Gaul replied. He checked the hourglass. "He's maxxed out. Any more weight and his knees will turn to powder."
He looked at the Healer Elder.
"This was your idea, Willow. It's time to see if it was worth it. I still say you can't teach a rock to fly."

"I don't want him to fly," Willow smiled mysteriously. "I want him to not get hit."

Gaul walked into the center of the arena.
"Gong! Halt!"

Gong skidded to a stop. He was panting, steam billowing from his chest.
"Teacher?"

"Review time," Gaul said. "How do you feel?"
"Heavy," Gong admitted. "Shoulders hurt."

"Good. That means it worked."
Gaul pointed to the ground.
"Gong, remove the weights. Let's see if you can actually run now."

Gong blinked. "Take... off?"
He had grown used to the crushing pressure. It felt like a part of him.
Slowly, he undid the clasps.
*CLANG.* The wrist bands hit the stone.
*THUD.* The ankle weights cracked a tile.
*CRUNCH.* The vest landed like a dead body.

Gong stood up.
He gasped.
He felt... wrong.
He felt like a balloon. He took a step and almost floated. He looked at his hands; they moved before he even finished the thought.
"I am... empty," Gong whispered.

"Run," Gaul commanded. "Sprint to the wall."

Gong looked at the wall, fifty yards away.
He leaned forward. His muscles, tuned to push 300 pounds of mass, fired with full force.
*BOOM.*
Dirt exploded where he stood.
He didn't run. He *launched*.
It wasn't elegant. It wasn't the graceful flicker of a Golden-Step Pheasant.
It was a cannonball.

Gong's eyes widened. The wall was coming way too fast.
"Stop!" he told his legs.
His legs said, *We don't know how.*

*CRASH.*
Gong hit the stone wall. He didn't bounce off. He went *through* it.
Dust and rubble rained down.
Gong stood in the hole, blinking, covered in plaster.
"Oops," he said.

Elder Willow clapped her hands politely. "Oh my. That is... acceleration."

Gaul stared at the hole.
"He still can't dodge," Gaul muttered. "But he can certainly close the gap."
He grinned.
"If you can't avoid the attack... just tackle the attacker before they swing. Very efficient."
