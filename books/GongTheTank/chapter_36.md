# Chapter 36: The Dance

Gong felt exposed.
He wasn't naked, but he might as well have been.
He was standing in the Sect's Ballroom—a place of polished marble and chandeliers that he didn't even know existed.
He was wearing a white silk outfit. It was tight. It had ruffles. It felt like something a fencer would wear if they lost a bet.
His boots—his beloved heavy iron boots—were gone, replaced by thin leather slippers.

"I feel... squishy," Gong muttered, wiggling his toes.

The double doors opened.
A breeze seemed to enter the room, carrying the scent of jasmine.
A woman walked in. No, she didn't walk. She flowed.
She wore a dress of pale blue chiffon that swirled around her like petals in a windstorm. Every step was silent. She barely seemed to touch the floor.

She stopped in front of Gong, looking him up and down. Her eyes were sharp, evaluating his posture, his center of mass, his utter lack of rhythm.

"My name is Elina," she said, her voice like a silver bell. "I am here to teach you to dance."

Gong stood dumbstruck.
He looked at his massive hands. He looked at the delicate woman.
"Dance?" Gong asked. "Walls can't dance!"

"Everything dances," Elina corrected. " The wind dances. The water dances. Even the mountain dances when the earth shakes."
She circled him.
"Gaul says you move like a landslide. My job is to turn that landslide into a... controlled descent."
She extended a hand.
"Take my hand. We will start with the Waltz. Three steps. One-two-three. One-two-three."

Gong took her hand. It felt like holding a butterfly. He was terrified he would crush it.
"One," Elina commanded.
Gong stepped.
*THUD.*
The floorboards groaned. Elina winced.
"Lighter," she whispered. "Two."
Gong tried to be light. He ended up hopping.
*STOMP.*

It went on.
Gong stepped on her toes (she dodged).
Gong spun and nearly took out a decorative vase with his elbow.
Gong tried to dip her and almost threw her across the room.

After the first hour, Elina stopped.
She walked over to the side of the room where a few junior disciples (her assistants) were watching in horror.
"Cancel my calendar," Elina said, massaging her temples.
"For today, Mistress?" an assistant asked.
"For the next week," Elina sighed, looking at the sweating, apologetic fortress in the center of her ballroom. "This is going to take a while."
