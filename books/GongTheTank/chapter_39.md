# Chapter 39: The Show

"We should charge admission!" Brother Cai shouted, spraying crumbs of a lotus cake. "This is awesome!"

The training arena was packed. It wasn't just fellow disciples anymore; even some of the junior instructors had stopped by to watch.
In the center, a blur of bronze and leather was currently assaulting a large, miserable-looking wall.

*CRACK. THUD. SLAM.*

Vala laughed. It was a terrifying sound.
She vaulted off Gong's raised shield, twisted in mid-air, and kicked him in the helmet with both feet.
Gong stumbled back, his boots leaving deep furrows in the stone. He didn't fall. He just shook his head like a dog drying itself.

"GOOD!" Vala roared, landing gracefully. "Again!"

The Elders watched from the balcony.
"She is enjoying this too much," Elder Willow noted. "Is he... okay?"

"Look at him," Gaul pointed. "He's not bleeding. He's not winded. He's just... annoyed."

Vala wasn't kidding. She was having a blast.
She had spent years holding back. Most men were too weak. They bruised. They broke. They whined about "safety" and "restraint."
But not Gong.
She could hit him with a full-power shield bash that would shatter a tree, and he would just grunt and say, "Sturdy."
She could sweep his legs, and he would bounce.

He was bad at the Amazon style. Truly bad.
At the beginning of the week, he was terrible. He tried to block everything and kept getting tripped.
Now, he was just bad.
He managed to deflect a strike. He managed to shuffle out of the way of a kick.
He was aiming for "decent."

"Use the spear, Gong!" Vala shouted, engaging him again. "It has a pointy end! Stick me with it!"
Gong thrust the spear. It was a slow, heavy poke.
Vala batted it aside with her buckler and slapped him in the ribs with her spear shaft.
"Faster! Like a snake! Not like a broom!"

Gong grunted, absorbing the blow.
"I am trying," he wheezed. "Snake is hard. Broom is easier."

Vala grinned, her eyes wild.
"Then I will beat the broom until it becomes a snake!"
She charged again.
The crowd cheered.
"Get him, Vala!"
"Hold the door, Gong!"

Gong sighed inside his helmet.
He missed the chicken. The chicken didn't hit back.
But he planted his feet. He raised his little dinner plate.
"I am ready," he said.
And for the first time, he shifted his weight. Just a little. Preparing not just to take the hit, but to roll with it.
Vala saw it. Her grin got wider.
"Glorious," she whispered.
