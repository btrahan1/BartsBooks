# Chapter 46: The Routine

Gong's days had become a bizarre series of disconnected events, held together by sweat and the promise of meals.

Morning began before the sun.
He was back at the Beast Pens, but his duties had changed. He didn't muck as much—Brother Cai had hired juniors for that. Instead, Gong spent his mornings grooming Maggie, who had grown significantly and now demanded belly rubs with the force of a small bulldozer. It was the only peaceful part of his day.

Late morning was The Jog.
A "light" 10km run around the Sect perimeter. He didn't wear the weights anymore, but he kept the pace high, his "Shuffle" eating up the miles with efficient, terrifying consistency.

Afternoon was The Drill.
Four hours at the target range. Just him, his spear, and the regenerating pink blob.
*Thwip-Thwip-Thwip.* He worked on the triple-strike until his arms felt like jelly and his vision blurred.

Late afternoon was The Dance.
Elina was gone, or hiding, so Gong practiced with volunteers.
"Volunteers" was a strong word. Usually, they were disciples who had lost a bet.
"One, two, OUCH, three," was the rhythm of the ballroom. Gong went through partners faster than he went through shoes.

But the evenings... the evenings were for business.
The line outside the training arena was constant. Gong's reputation as the "Unbreakable Toy" had spread.
Customers paid for the privilege of venting their frustrations on him.
"I want to practice my sword forms!" one would say. Gong would pick up his tower shields.
"I want to test my new hammer!" another would shout. Gong would brace.
"Fight me with a stick!" a drunken Elder demanded once. Gong picked up a broom.

He used everything.
Sometimes he had a spear and shield.
Sometimes he dual-wielded shields.
Sometimes he was bare-handed, practicing grappling (which mostly involved hugging people until they passed out).
He even tried comical weapons at the request of eccentricity-seeking clients. A bench. A large ham from the kitchen. A sapling.

He became a mirror. Whatever the customer needed, Gong became.
He didn't win. He rarely tried to. He just survived.
And in surviving, he learned. He learned the reach of a whip, the arc of a flail, the deceptive speed of a dagger.

By the time he collapsed onto his straw mat at the Beast Pens, usually past midnight, he was too tired to dream.
Sleep was rare. Sleep was a luxury.
But as he closed his eyes, his muscles twitching with the memory of a thousand impacts, Gong smiled.
He was very, very sturdy.
