# Chapter 48: The Questions

The next morning, the 10km run was... different.
Usually, it was just Gong and the rhythm of his own heavy breathing.
Today, it was Gong, heavy breathing, and a constant stream of questions.

"Why do you run like that? Is it efficient? Why are your boots iron? Do you like the noise? Have you ever seen a Spirit Crane? My uncle says they eat fish, but I read they eat clouds. Which is it? Why is the sky blue? Is it blue outside the Sect?"

Mia ran beside him.
She wasn't wearing running gear. She was still in her silk robes. She held her fan in one hand.
And yet, she wasn't sweating. She wasn't panting.
While Gong thundered along like a runaway siege tower, she floated. Her feet barely seemed to touch the dirt. She moved with the same uncanny lightness as Elina, but less performative. More... natural.

"Sky is blue because... sky," Gong wheezed, answering the only question he felt qualified for.
"Fascinating," Mia noted, tapping her chin with her fan. "You are very simple. I like it. Everyone else talks in riddles."

She seemed to be about his age, maybe a year younger. But she felt ancient. She spoke with the vocabulary of an Elder, yet asked questions a toddler would ask. It was disorienting.
"Have you been outside?" Gong asked, between breaths.
"No," Mia said simply. "Uncle says it is dangerous. Only books. Books are safe."
She looked at a passing butterfly with intense wonder.
"That is a Monarch," she identified. "Its wings are toxic to frogs. Have you eaten a frog?"
"Yes," Gong admitted. "Tastes like chicken."
"What is a chicken?"

Gong stumbled. "You... don't know chicken?"
"I have read about them. Golden-Step Pheasants. Fire-Combs. But a 'chicken' chicken? No."
Gong felt a wave of pity. A life without chicken was no life at all.

Later, at the training range, the dynamic changed.
Gong resumed his war against the pink regenerating blob. *Thwip-Thwip-Thwip.* He was getting better at the Drill.
Mia sat on a rock, watching him with those dark, sharp eyes. She didn't ask questions now. She studied.
"Your elbow drifts," she commented after an hour.
Gong paused. "It does?"
"Yes. On the third strike. You drop it. That is why the angle changes."

Gong corrected his elbow. The next strike was straighter.
"Oh," he said. "Thanks."

During his water break, Mia stood up.
"May I?" she asked, pointing to an open space.
"Free country," Gong shrugged.
Mia opened her fan.
She didn't fight the air. She danced with it.
She moved through a series of forms—fluid, circular, hypnotic. It looked like the waterbending techniques he had seen in books, but mixed with something sharper. Every time the fan snapped open, the air cracked.
*Snap. Whoosh. Snap.*

Gong watched, mesmerized.
She wasn't weak. She wasn't fragile.
She was terrified of the world, yes. But she clearly knew how to break it.
