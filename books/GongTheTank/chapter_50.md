# Chapter 50: The Project

The tea room was quiet. Incense curled in the air, smelling of sandalwood and secrets.

Elder Iron-Heart sipped his tea. It was bitter. He liked it.
Across the low table sat Elder Mist-Walker, Mia's uncle. He looked as insubstantial as his name suggested, but his eyes were sharp as flint.

"So," Mist-Walker broke the silence. "How is our little project?"

Iron-Heart raised an eyebrow. "Which one? The boy with the core of lead, or the girl with the heart of glass?"

"Both of them," Mist-Walker smiled thinly. "They are... entangled, are they not?"

"They are," Iron-Heart grunted. "Your niece is currently teaching my disciple how to perform the 'Courtship of the Spirit Crane.' It is... painful to watch. He looks like a boulder trying to fly."

"But he is trying," Mist-Walker countered. "And Mia? Is she safe?"

"She is shadowed by a mountain," Iron-Heart assured him. "Gong takes his duties seriously. He sleeps outside her door. He tastes her food (and asks for seconds). He glares at any boy who looks at her too long."

Mist-Walker nodded, satisfied.
"Good. She needs that. She has lived in a tower of books for too long. She knows the theory of the world, but not the weight of it. Gong provides... weight."

"And she provides movement," Iron-Heart admitted. "Gong is stagnant. Hiding behind his shields. Your niece forces him to step. To turn. To adapt."

He set his cup down.
"The Wall and the Wind," Iron-Heart mused. "An unlikely pair."

"But effective," Mist-Walker concluded. "If they do not kill each other first."

"There is that," Iron-Heart agreed. "Apparently she tried to teach him the 'Butterfly Spin' yesterday and he threw up."

"Progress," Mist-Walker chuckled. "Progress."

He put his cup down. The humor vanished from his face.
"However," he said. "Domesticity breeds complacency. They are getting too comfortable."

"Agreed," Iron-Heart nodded.

"Set up the encounter," Mist-Walker commanded. "You have hired the thugs?"

"Yes," Iron-Heart replied. "Mercenaries from the Lower City. They are... expendable. They think they are robbing a rich merchant's daughter."

Mist-Walker faltered for a second. His hand tightened on his fan until the knuckles turned white.
"Will she be in danger?"

"Yes," Iron-Heart said brutally. "Real danger. Swords do not care about bloodlines. Anything could happen."

Mist-Walker closed his eyes. He took a long, slow breath, steadying his Qi.
"Good. It is necessary."
He stood up to leave, blending back into the shadows of the room.
"Have the healer nearby," he whispered. "Just in case."
