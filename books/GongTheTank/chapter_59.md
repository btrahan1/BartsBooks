# Chapter 59: The Assessment

The incense in the tea room had burned down to ash, but neither Elder moved to replace it.
On the table lay the crystal recording of the dungeon run. The image of Gong diving in front of the purple curse played on a loop, suspended in the glass.

"That was..." Elder Iron-Heart broke the silence, swirling the dregs of his tea. "...decent."

Elder Mist-Walker leaned forward, his eyes fixed on the frozen image of the large boy shielding his niece.
"Show me again," he commanded.

Iron-Heart tapped the crystal.
Gong launched himself. Explosive acceleration. Zero hesitation. The curse hit. The boy fell. The mountain rose.
"He did not calculate," Mist-Walker noted softly. "He simply... acted."

"That is his nature," Iron-Heart grunted. "He is simple. Simple things are hard to break."

Another elder, an old man with robes embroidered with star-charts, snorted from the corner shadows.
"If not for the curse, they probably could have finished.Floor Five is just traps and a loot room. They had the momentum."
He pointed a bony finger at the crystal.
"Where did that come from? A Curse of Withering? On the Fourth Floor? That is a Floor Ten mechanic."

Iron-Heart frowned. "It was... anomalous. Bad luck."
He pulled a scrolled report from his sleeve.
"The Chieftain had been upgraded. A variant. It wasn't in the reports from the Scout Hall. Sometimes the dungeon simply... evolves."

"Bad luck," Mist-Walker repeated. "Or a sign."
He looked at the image of his niece, safe behind the wall of steel.
"Regardless of the luck," Mist-Walker stood up, smoothing his robes. "The test is concluded."
He looked at Iron-Heart.
"He will do. He stood when others would have fled. He bled so she would not."

Iron-Heart nodded, finally extinguishing the image in the crystal.
"Yes. He will do."
He allowed himself a rare, grim smile.
"The Tournament will be interesting this year."
