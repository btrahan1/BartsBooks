# Chapter 61: The Mascot

The "Elite Training Hall" was different from the muddy courtyard of the Beast Pens. The floor was polished hardwood. The weapons rack was stocked with high-quality steel. There was even a magically cooled pitcher of water in the corner.

Lin, Zhi, Mia, and Sister Xiao were already stretching.
"Where is the wall?" Zhi asked, creating a small fireball and extinguishing it. "He is late."

"He said he had to pick up his gear," Xiao said, adjusting her robes. "And... something else."

The double doors groaned open.
Gong stepped through. He was wearing his full kit—the Elven Chain shimmering under his heavy leathers, the tower shield on his back, the round shield on his left arm, the spear in his right. He looked formidable. He looked ready for war.

Then, something trotted out from behind his legs.

It was gray. It was round. It was wearing a very small, very poorly made leather hat.

"Is that..." Lin lowered her rapier. "Is that a rhino?"

"This is Maggie," Gong introduced, patting the creature's flank. Maggie let out a soft *huff* and nudged Gong's thigh, looking for a treat. She had grown. She now stood waist-high to Gong, her skin thickening into distinct, armor-like plates. A single, blunt nub of a horn was beginning to push through her nose.

"Why is there a beast in the Elite Hall?" Zhi demanded. "This is for disciples. Not... livestock."

"She is not livestock," Gong said, offended. "She is my bonded partner. And she is training."

"Training?" Mia tilted her head, fascinated. "What style does she use?"

"The Rock Style," Gong said confidently. "She stands there. It is very effective."

Zhi rubbed his temples. "Gong, we are preparing to fight the best disciples from across the region. We need precision. We need focus. We do not need a barn animal wandering around tripping us."

"She won't trip you," Gong promised. "Unless you are clumsy."

"She can't stay," Zhi insisted. "Take her back to the pens."

Sister Xiao stepped forward. She knelt down. Maggie immediately abandoned Gong and trotted over to the healer. The rhino sniffed Xiao's hand, recognized the scent of the person who had healed her mother, and let out a happy squeak.
"Oh, look at her," Xiao cooed, scratching Maggie behind the ears. "She's wearing a hat."

"It is a helmet," Gong corrected. "I made it from a boot."

"She stays," Xiao declared, looking up at Zhi with a glare that brooked no argument. Healers were usually gentle, but every disciple knew you didn't upset the person who decided if your arm grew back crooked or straight.

Zhi sighed. "Fine. But if she messes up the formation..."

***

They began with a standard defensive drill.
Gong took point. Mia was on the flank. Lin and Zhi were the attackers, simulating an enemy rush. Xiao was the objective to be protected.

"Ready?" Lin called out.
"Ready," Gong grunted, raising his shields.

Lin blurred forward, her rapier darting like a snake. Zhi laid down suppressing fire—small, stinging bolts of mana aimed at Gong's feet.
Gong stepped into the 'Gatekeeper' stance, deflecting the bolts with his round shield while tracking Lin with the tower shield.

Mia darted in and out, using her fan to harass Lin.

It was going well. Gong was holding the line.
Then, Maggie decided she was bored.

She had been sitting in the corner, chewing on a wooden practice sword she had found. But seeing the commotion around Xiao—her favorite person—she decided to investigate.
Mid-drill, the rhino trotted right into the center of the formation.

"Watch out!" Zhi yelled, halting his cast.

Maggie ignored him. She walked past Gong, walked past Mia, and plopped herself down directly on Sister Xiao's feet. She let out a heavy sigh, closing her eyes.

"Maggie!" Gong scolded. " tactical positioning!"

"She's... actually, hold on," Lin said. She tried to step around Gong to get a clear line of sight on Xiao.
She couldn't.
Maggie was a solid, three-hundred-pound lump of Spirit Beast occupying the exact space Lin needed to lunge.

"I can't get a collection angle," Lin frowned. "The rhino is blocking the lower quadrant. If I lunge high, Gong blocks it. If I lunge low, I hit the mascot."

"Don't hit the mascot!" Xiao warned, patting Maggie's armored back.

Zhi tried to circle around the other side.
Maggie, sensing movement, didn't get up. She just lazily rotated her body while lying down, effectively swiveling her armored rear end to face Zhi.

"She is... creating cover," Mia observed, her eyes wide. "Dynamic, low-level cover."

Gong nodded as if he had planned this entirely.
"See?" he said. "Terrain."

"She's sitting on the healer!" Zhi argued.

"The healer is safe," Gong pointed out. "Can you hit Xiao?"

Zhi raised his hand, then lowered it. "No. The beast is in the way. I'd have to waste a heavy spell to move her."

"And if you waste a heavy spell on the mascot," Mia added, snapping her fan shut, "then Gong smashes you."

There was a moment of silence as the team considered this.
A tank who blocked the front. A mobile, armored rock that sat on the healer. It was annoying. It was chaotic.
And it was effective.

"Fine," Zhi grumbled, holstering his wand. "She can stay. But get her a better helmet. That boot looks ridiculous."

Gong beamed. He reached into his pouch and pulled out a dried turnip.
"Good girl, Maggie. Good terrain."

Maggie crunched the turnip loud enough to echo in the hall.
The heavy training for the Grand Tournament had officially begun.
