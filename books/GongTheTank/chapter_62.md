# Chapter 62: The Business

Two days later, the training hall wasn't empty. It was besieged.

Zhi looked at the line of disciples stretching out the door and down the hallway. "What is happening? Are we being invaded?"

"No," Brother Cai said, stepping out from behind a small, collapsible table he had set up near the entrance. He was wearing a silk robe that looked far too expensive for a gym, and he was clicking rapidly on an abacus. "You are open for business."

"Business?" Gong asked, tightening his shield straps. Maggie was already asleep on Sister Xiao’s feet.

"You need practice partners," Cai explained, not looking up from his calculations. "They need to test themselves against the 'Dungeon Breakers.' It is a simple supply and demand curve."

He pointed to a sign he had painted:
**THE WALL EXPERIENCE**
*30 Minutes: 5 Silver*
*Heal Included (Courtesy of Sister Xiao)*
*No Refunds for Broken Bones*

"Five silver?" Mia scoffed. "That's robbery. Who would pay five silver just to spar?"

"Me," a burly disciple at the front of the line grunted. He slapped five silver coins onto the table. "I'm tired of hitting straw dummies. I want to see if I can move the Mountain."

Cai swept the coins into a pouch with a practiced motion. "Next! Team 'Red Tiger', you are up in slot one. You have thirty minutes. If the Rhino sits on you, that counts as a technical defeat."

***

The sparring began.
It was chaotic. It was intense. It was profitable.

The first team, "Red Tiger," was a group of Axe-wielders. They specialized in brute force.
"Break the formation!" their leader shouted, charging straight at Gong.

Gong didn't budge. He dropped into his stance, the Elven Chain absorbing the shockwaves, his tower shield acting as a literal wall. When they tried to flank him, Mia was there, her fan snapping like a whip to disrupt their rhythm. When they tried to ignore Gong and rush the backline, Maggie simply stood up and blocked the path, lazily chewing on a turnip while axes bounced off her hide.

Thirty minutes later, the Red Tiger team was exhausted, bruised, and smiling.
"That was... effective," the leader panted, shaking Gong's hand. "I've never hit anything that hard before. My wrists are killing me. Same time tomorrow?"

"If you have the silver," Cai called out from his table. "Slot two! 'The Whispering Blades'. You're up!"

The Whispering Blades were assassins. They moved like smoke.
They bypassed Gong entirely, aiming for Sister Xiao.
"Got you!" one of them hissed, reaching for the healer.

*SNORT.*
Maggie, who had been pretending to sleep, swung her head. Her blunt horn connected with the assassin's hip. He went flying across the room, crashing into the weapons rack.

"Terrain hazard," Gong reminded them politely.

By noon, the pile of silver on Cai's table was a small mountain.
By evening, it was a large mountain.

Gong wiped sweat from his forehead. He was tired, but it was a good tired. He had blocked hammers, swords, daggers, and fireballs. He had adjusted to a dozen different styles in a single day.

"How much?" Zhi asked, looking at the money with greed in his eyes. He had spent a lot of mana today.

"After my 10% management fee," Cai said magnanimously, "and the venue rental bribe... I mean, 'donation' to the janitor... you each clear fifty silver."

"Fifty?" Lin's jaw dropped. "That's a month's stipend."

"We're rich," Mia laughed.

"We are hungry," Gong corrected. He looked at Cai. "Can this buy meat?"

"Gong," Cai grinned, tossing a heavy pouch to him. "This can buy the whole cow."

***

Up on the balcony, unnoticed in the shadows, Elder Iron-Heart and the Sect Leader watched the spectacle.

"Mercenary behavior," the Sect Leader noted, though he didn't sound angry.

"Pragmatic behavior," Iron-Heart countered. "They are training against every style in the sect. They are learning to adapt. And the rest of the disciples are getting a reality check on what a real defense looks like."

He watched as Gong helped a winded opponent to his feet, offered him a sip of water, and then immediately politely asked if he wanted to pay for another round.

"The boy is simple," Iron-Heart mused. "But he understands value. He is not just building a team. He is building an economy."

"Let them keep the money," the Sect Leader decided, turning to leave. "If they are going to represent us, I don't want them starving."

"With Gong?" Iron-Heart snorted. "Starvation is the only enemy he fears."
