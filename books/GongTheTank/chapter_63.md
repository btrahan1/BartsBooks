# Chapter 63: The Horn

Three months of constant combat had changed the team.
Gong was sharper, his shield movements less like a wall and more like a fortress gate—opening and closing with terrifying precision.
Mia was a blur of silk and steel, her fan claiming knuckles and knees with ruthless efficiency.
Lin and Zhi had learned to cast and thrust without looking, trusting that the "Wall" would keep them safe.

But the biggest change wasn't the disciples.
It was the mascot.

"Kill the healer!"
The shout came from the captain of 'Team Iron-Jaw', a group of hammer-wielding outer disciples who had pooled their money for a shot at the 'Dungeon Breakers'.

It was the standard strategy. In every sect tournament, in every skirmish, the rule was simple: If you want to win, you take out the support first.
Three of them broke off from Gong, ignoring his shield bash. They sprinted past Mia, eating a wind-blade to the shoulder just to close the gap.

They reached the backline.
Sister Xiao stood there, calm, her hands glowing with green light as she topped up Gong's stamina.
She didn't flinch. She didn't run.
She just patted the gray mountain at her feet.

"Maggie," Xiao whispered. "Company."

Maggie, who had been napping (or pretending to), opened one eye.
She stood up.
It wasn't a scramble. It was a tectonic shift. One moment she was a rug, the next she was a barricade.

"Move the beast!" the hammer captain yelled, winding up a massive overhead swing. "Shatter Strike!"

The hammer came down. It was a blow meant to crack boulders.
Maggie didn't dodge. She lowered her head.
And then, it happened.

Her blunt nub of a horn—usually the color of dusty slate—began to hum. A deep, amber light pulsed from deep within the keratin. It wasn't a flash; it was a steady, heavy glow, like molten rock cooling in the dark.

*THOOM.*

The hammer hit the horn.
The hammer bounced.
The captain didn't just stumble; he was repelled. A shockwave of amber energy ripple out from Maggie, knocking all three attackers onto their backsides.

Silence fell over the training hall.
Even Gong stopped fighting, holding a confused disciple in a headlock.

"Did..." Zhi lowered his wand. "Did the rhino just cast a barrier spell?"

Maggie huffed. The glow faded as the threat retreated. She looked at the stunned captain, then turned around and sat back down on Xiao's feet, letting out a contented sigh.

"She countered a Core-strength blow," Lin whispered, staring. "With her face."

Sister Xiao was beaming. She knelt down and wrapped her arms around Maggie's thick neck, burying her face in the leathery folds. "Who's a good girl? Who's the best guardian?"

Maggie squeaked, her tail wagging furiously.

"The horn," Mia observed, walking over to inspect it. "It's accumulating Earth Qi. She isn't just absorbing impact anymore. She's rejecting it."

"She is a flashlight," Gong said, walking over with the disciple still in his headlock (he had forgotten to let go). "A very sturdy flashlight."

"It's a Guardian manifestation," Elder Mist-Walker said. He had stepped out from the shadows, his eyes locked on the beast. "Spirit Rhinos are usually passive. But this one... she has bonded with a Tank and protects a Healer. She is evolving to match her environment."

He looked at Gong.
"She is no longer just a pet, Disciple Gong. She is a piece of wargear."

Gong looked at Maggie, who was currently drooling on Xiao’s expensive robes.
"She is Maggie," Gong said simply. "And she is hungry."

The Elder sighed, but the ghost of a smile touched his lips. "Yes. I suppose she is. Feed her. The Tournament begins tomorrow."
