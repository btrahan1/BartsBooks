# Chapter 65: The Soloists

Gong and Maggie sat in the stands, sharing a bag of roasted peanuts. Gong crunched the shells. Maggie crunched the bag.
"Watch closely," Gong instructed, pointing a shelled peanut at the arena below. "This is how fast people fight."

Down in Ring 4, Zhi was screaming.
"Stay still!" the Fire Mage yelled, hurling a fireball that missed his opponent—a nimble shadow-stepper—by inches.
Without Gong to hold the target in place, Zhi was forced to aim at a moving target. He was sweating. His mana reserves were dropping faster than he liked.
Normally, he would just unleash a cone of flame behind Gong's shield. But now, if he missed, he got punched in the face.
He finally caught the shadow-stepper with a desperate, wide-area *Blast Wave* that knocked them both out of the ring.
"Winner by Ring Out: Zhi!"
Zhi lay on the grass, panting as if he had run a marathon. "I hate cardio," he wheezed.

***

In Ring 2, Lin was putting on a clinic of swordsmanship. Her rapier was a blur of silver light. Her opponent, a heavy saber user, couldn't touch her.
But he could pressure her.
Every time Lin lunged, she had to immediately retreat to avoid the counter-swing. There was no "Wall" to step behind to reset her stance. There was no "Safe Zone."
She won, eventually. She slipped past a clumsy guard and delivered a precise thrust to the saber user's throat, stopping just short of drawing blood.
"Yield!" she commanded.
He yielded.
Lin sheathed her rapier. Her hand was shaking slightly. She touched her cheek—there was a thin, red line where the wind of the saber had cut her.
"Too close," she muttered. She missed the shadow of the tower shield.

***

In Ring 7, Mia was dancing.
She was fighting a brute from the Hammer Hall. She was running circles around him, her fan snapping his ears, his nose, his knuckles.
It was humiliating for him.
But it was terrifying for her.
She was the "Wind" of the team. But wind needs a mountain to blow against. Without Gong as her anchor, she felt untethered. If she made one mistake, one slip, there was no one to catch the blow.
She finally tripped him, using her sash to tangle his ankles. As he fell, she placed her fan against his neck.
"Winner: Mia!"
She didn't celebrate. She practically sprinted out of the ring, looking for a familiar gray shape.

***

They met up in the locker room tunnel.
They were not the triumphant conquerors Gong had been. They looked like survivors.
Zhi was chugging a mana potion like it was water. Lin was applying a plaster to her cheek. Mia was pacing.

"That was awful," Zhi declared, wiping his mouth. "I had to aim. Actually aim."

"I had to parry," Lin complained. "Do you know how much heavier a saber is than a rapier? My wrist is sprained."

Mia just sighed, leaning against the wall. "It felt... empty. Like dancing without music."

Gong walked in, Maggie trotting at his heels. He looked fresh, happy, and full of peanuts.
"Good fights," he praised them. "You all won."

"We struggled," Lin corrected him. "We're top-tier disciples, Gong. We shouldn't struggle in the prelims."

"You struggled," Gong said wisely, "because you are fingers."
He held up his hand, wiggling his thick sausage-like fingers.
"Fingers are weak alone. They break."
Then, he slowly curled his hand into a fist. A fist the size of a small ham.
"But together," Gong said, "they are a rock."

Zhi stared at the fist. "Did you come up with that?"

"No," Gong admitted. "Momma said it. Usually before she hit a stubborn nail."

Mia smiled, the tension finally leaving her shoulders. She walked over and patted Maggie's head.
"Your Momma was a wise woman, Gong."

"She was," Gong agreed. "Tonight, we eat. Tomorrow, we fight as a fist."
