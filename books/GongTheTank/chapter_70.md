# Chapter 70: The Filter

The higher brackets of the Individual Tournament were known as "The Filter."
It was where talent met hard counters. It was where the specialists were weeded out by the masters.

Zhi was the first to fall.
He faced a Water Mage from the River Valley Sect.
"Burn!" Zhi screamed, launching a torrent of fire.
"No," the Water Mage said calmly, raising a wall of water.
Steam hissed. Zhi cast again. And again.
Without a tank to distract the mage, Zhi was forced into a direct elemental clash. Fire against Water.
He lost. He walked out of the ring soaking wet and shivering. "I hate water," he grumbled. "It puts out the fun."

***

Lin was next.
She faced a disciple wielding a "Meteor Hammer"—a massive iron ball on a chain.
Lin was faster. She landed three strikes for every swing of the hammer.
But the hammer only needed to land once.
*CRACK.*
Lin parried perfectly. But her rapier, fine steel as it was, couldn't handle the kinetic energy. The blade snapped. The shockwave sent her tumbling into the dirt.
"Yield," she said, looking at the broken pieces of her weapon. She realized then how much she relied on Gong to absorb those shield-shattering blows.

***

Mia lasted the longest.
She fought a Shadow Dancer—a disciple who specialized in the same fan-and-sash style she used, but with ten years more experience.
It was a beautiful fight. A whirlwind of silk and steel.
But Mia was a "Utility" fighter. She was trained to disrupt, to harass, to peal. In a duel, she lacked the finishing blow.
She was eventually cornered and disarmed.
"Good dance," the Shadow Dancer complimented her. "But you kept looking over your shoulder for a wall that wasn't there."

***

Then, it was Gong’s turn.
His opponent was "Flash" Jiao. A speedster who moved so fast he left afterimages.
"You can't hit what you can't catch!" Jiao taunted, blurring around Gong.
*Zip. Zip. Zip.*
He struck Gong's back, his legs, his helmet.
*Ping. Ting. Bonk.*
Gong stood in the center of the ring, turning slowly like a turret. He didn't chase. He didn't swipe. He just waited.
Ten minutes passed. Jiao began to slow down. Breathing became ragged.
Fifteen minutes. Jiao stumbled.
Gong stepped.
It wasn't a martial art. It was a hug.
Gong opened his arms and simply enclosed the exhausted speedster in a bear hug.
"Gotcha," Gong said.
He squeezed. Gently. Just enough to compress the air out of Jiao's lungs.
"Yield?" Gong asked politely.
"Y-yield!" Jiao squeaked, his face turning blue.

"Winner: Gong!"

The team met in the tunnel again.
Three losers. One winner.
"So," Zhi said, wringing water out of his robe. "We're all out. Except the Wall."
"Because the Wall doesn't have a bad matchup," Lin noted, nursing her wrist. "He just... ignores the matchup."

Gong walked up to them, Maggie trotting behind him with Jiao's sash in her mouth (a souvenir).
"Did you see?" Gong asked happily. "I caught the fast one. I used the 'Hug' technique."

Mia laughed, though there was a hint of sadness in it. "Yes, Gong. You were great. You're the only one left."

"In the singles," Gong corrected. "Tomorrow, we are a fist again."
He handed Lin a bag of peanuts.
"Eat. You look skinny."
