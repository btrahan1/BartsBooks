# Chapter 71: The Prediction

The tea in the Elder's box was gone. Replaced by a jug of stronger wine.

"Zhi's defeat," Elder Mist-Walker noted, reading the report. "Expected."

"Agreed," Elder Iron-Heart grunted. "Fire without control is just a candle in the wind. He met water. He died."

"Lin's defeat," Mist-Walker continued. "Expected. Her blade is fast but brittle. She met iron. She shattered."

"And Mia," Iron-Heart added. "She relies on distraction. In a duel, there is nothing to distract from. She was cornered."

They sat in silence for a moment, looking at the empty arena.
The team was flawed. They knew this. That was the point of the project.
"And Gong?" Mist-Walker asked.

"Agreed," Iron-Heart smiled. "He is a fortress. It does not matter if you attack a fortress with water, or iron, or speed. It is still a fortress. He simply outlasted them."

"He is in the Individual Finals," Mist-Walker mused. "And the Team Finals."

"A rare double," Iron-Heart admitted. "Who are the opponents?"

Mist-Walker unrolled a gold-leafed scroll. Even the paper looked arrogant.
"Team Golden Dragon," he read. "Led by Prince Jin."

Iron-Heart whistled. "The Royal Scion? I thought he was training in the Capital."
"He returned for the tournament. To 'inspire the commoners'."
Mist-Walker tapped the scroll.
"They are not like the Vipers or the Obsidian team. They do not have a gimmick. They have... excellence."

"Explain," Iron-Heart demanded.

"Five members," Mist-Walker listed. "Jin (Sword Saint style). A Light Elementalist. A Heavy Lancer. A Battle-Bard. And a dedicated Paladin."
"A balanced party," Iron-Heart frowned. "Classic composition. High stats. Best gear money can buy."

"And they are undefeated," Mist-Walker added. "Average match time: Two minutes. They simply overpower their opponents with superior technique and cultivation base."

"Gong faces Jin in the Individual Final first," Iron-Heart noted. "Then the Team Final in the afternoon."

"Do we offer advice?" Mist-Walker asked. "Gaul wants to tell them about Jin's 'Heavenly Strike' technique."

Iron-Heart shook his head. He poured another cup of wine.
"No. Let them figure it out. Gong has faced strength before. He has faced speed. He has faced poison."
He looked down at the sand, where a lone frantic servant was still trying to sweep up the rubble from Gong's siege attack.
"He has never faced Perfection," Iron-Heart said. "It will be good for him to see that even gold can dent."
