# Chapter 72: The Handshake

The sun was high. The sand was hot.
And the two fighters in the center of the arena had been at it for an hour.

Prince Jin was a whirlwind of gold and white. His sword technique, the "Thousand Petal Lotus," was breathtaking. He struck from every angle, his blade moving faster than the eye could follow.
*Clang. Ting. Ping. Clang.*
Gong was a statue of iron and leather. He pivoted on his heel, his tower shield blocking the heavy strikes, his round shield deflecting the light ones. He didn't attack. He couldn't. Every time he thrust his spear, Jin was already gone, dancing out of range.

The crowd, usually bloodthirsty, had gone silent. They were watching a masterclass in opposition.
Total Defense against Total Offense.

Jin landed a combo that would have severed the head of a lesser tank. His blade sparked against Gong's helmet.
Gong didn't flinch. He just grunted and adjusted his stance.

Jin backed away, breathing hard. sweat was beading on his noble forehead.
"You," Jin said, panting, "are very... sturdy."

"You," Gong replied, wiping sweat from his eyes, "are very... mosquito."

Jin laughed. It was a genuine laugh, bright and sharp. "I have struck you four thousand times."

"I have blocked four thousand times," Gong corrected.

"I cannot break your guard without exhausting my core," Jin admitted. He looked at the sun. "And we have a team match in two hours."

"I cannot catch you without dropping my shield," Gong admitted. "And if I drop my shield, you poke me."

"A dilemma," Jin noted. He sheathed his sword. The sharp *click* echoed in the silent arena.
"A draw?"

The crowd gasped. A draw in the finals? It was unheard of. Usually, they fought until one couldn't stand.
Gong looked at the Prince. He saw not arrogance, but calculation. And respect.
Gong lowered his shield. Maggie, who had fallen asleep in the shade of the tower shield ten minutes ago, woke up with a snort.

"A draw," Gong agreed. "I need lunch."

Jin walked forward. He didn't bow like a noble to a peasant. He stuck out his hand.
Gong took it. His glove was rough, stained with oil and leather. Jin's hand was manicured and soft.
They shook.

"Save your strength, Wall," Jin warned, his eyes gleaming. "My team does not have my limitations. In the group match, we will not be so polite."

"Bring them," Gong said, patting Maggie's head. "My team is also... not polite."

The Judge, recovering from his shock, raised both flags.
"Result: Draw! By Mutual Consent!"

The applause started slowly, then grew. It wasn't the roar of bloodlust. It was the applause of appreciation. They had seen two immovable forces recognize each other.
As they walked off the field, Iron-Heart, up in his box, nodded.
"Good," the Elder said. "He made a friend. Or at least, a rival who won't try to poison his tea."
