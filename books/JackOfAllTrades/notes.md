This is a great pivot point. We've established Jack as the ultimate
"High-Functioning Generalist." He is the glue holding his neighborhood
together.

To challenge a Generalist, you need a problem that **Specialists cannot
solve**.

The central theme of the book seems to be **Versatility vs. Rigid
Specialization**. The conflict should stem from a situation where the
experts are paralyzed because they can only see their 10% of the
problem, but Jack can see the whole 80% picture.

Here are four high-level concepts for the central conflict/plot:

### []{#anchor}Option 1: The "Silo" Crisis (Corporate/Industrial Espionage)

**The Setup:** An eccentric billionaire (or perhaps a desperate CEO of a
local failing tech/manufacturing giant) hears about Jack. They have a
prototype or a project that is failing disastrously. **The Problem:**
The company has the best engineers, the best coders, and the best
marketers in the world (the 100% specialists). But the engineers hate
the coders, and the marketers don't understand physics. The project is
bleeding money. **The Conflict:** Jack is hired as a "consultant." He
walks into a hostile environment where PhDs look down on him. **Jack's
Role:** He realizes the problem isn't the tech; it's the *synthesis*.
The cooling system (plumbing) interferes with the wifi (IT), which ruins
the user experience (psychology). **The Climax:** Sabotage is
discovered. Jack has to use mechanics, coding, social engineering, and
maybe a little lock-picking to save the project and expose the mole.

### []{#anchor}Option 2: The "Perfect" Storm (Disaster/Survival)

**The Setup:** A freak weather event or infrastructure failure cuts
Maplewood off from the grid. No power, no cell service, roads blocked.
**The Problem:** The modern world relies on supply chains and experts.
Without the internet, the "smart" systems fail. The doctors can't
operate without power; the city officials are paralyzed without guidance
from the state. **The Conflict:** Order begins to break down. A rigid,
bureaucratic HOA president or city official tries to enforce rules that
don't make sense in a crisis, hoarding resources for "official use."
**Jack's Role:** Jack becomes the de facto leader. He builds water
filtration systems (chemistry/plumbing), sets up a ham radio network
(electronics), fixes generators (mechanics), and mediates disputes
(sociology). **The Synthesis:** He has to rig a solution to save
someone's life (maybe Maya or Mrs. Higgins) that uses car parts, medical
supplies, and garden tools.

### []{#anchor}Option 3: The David vs. Goliath (Legal/Community)

**The Setup:** A massive conglomerate tries to seize the land where
"Ernest's Auto," "The Daily Grind," and Mrs. Higgins' house stand to
build a sterile "Smart City" complex. **The Problem:** They have an army
of lawyers and specialized surveyors. They find "violations" in every
building to force people out. **The Conflict:** The neighbors are giving
up. They can't afford to fight. **Jack's Role:** Jack reads the fine
print (contract law). He checks the surveyor's math (trigonometry). He
digs into the history of the land (archaeology/library sciences). He
realizes the conglomerate's own environmental study is flawed based on a
specific species of beetle he noticed in Mrs. Higgins' garden
(entomology). **The Synthesis:** Jack represents the neighborhood in a
town hall against a Harvard lawyer, dismantling their case not with one
silver bullet, but with a "death by a thousand cuts" attack using 20
different disciplines.

### []{#anchor}Option 4: The Cold Case (Mystery)

**The Setup:** An old friend of Jack's is accused of a crime they didn't
commit (maybe theft of high-end equipment or digital currency). The
police have "perfect" evidence. **The Problem:** The detectives are
looking at the forensics, but missing the context. The cyber-crime unit
is looking at the code, but missing the physical breach. **The
Conflict:** Jack has to clear his friend's name before the trial. He has
to investigate the crime scene. **Jack's Role:** He notices the wear
pattern on a lock (locksmithing). He recognizes a specific chemical
smell (chemistry). He understands the accounting trick used to hide the
money (finance). **The Synthesis:** Jack breaks into the real criminal's
secure facility using a combination of hacking, climbing, and social
engineering to plant a trap.

My Recommendation:

I lean toward **Option 3 (David vs. Goliath)** or **Option 1 (The Silo
Crisis)**.

- **Option 3** keeps it grounded in the neighborhood we just built. It
  allows us to use Ernest, Maya, and Mrs. Higgins as supporting
  characters with stakes in the game. It proves that a "Man About Town"
  is more valuable than a corporation.
- **Option 1** is fun because it takes Jack *out* of his comfort zone
  and puts him in a shiny, sterile world where he offends everyone with
  his pragmatism, yet is the only one who can actually get things done.
