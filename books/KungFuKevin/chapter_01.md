# Chapter 1: The Basement Warrior


The air in the basement smelled of damp concrete, stale pepperoni pizza, and raw, unbridled testosterone.

Kevin stood before the full-length mirror he'd duct-taped to the back of the furnace door. He adjusted his headband, a strip of red cloth ripped from an old t-shirt, and narrowed his eyes until they were dangerous slits. He was nineteen years old, weighed one hundred and forty pounds soaking wet, and he was a lethal weapon.

At least, that's what he told the mirror.

"You think you can take me?" Kevin whispered to his reflection, his voice dropping an octave to mimic the gravelly tone of a man who had seen too many underground death matches. "You think you have the speed? The *ferocity*?"

The reflection didn't answer, mostly because it was Kevin, but also because it knew better than to provoke the sleeping dragon.

Kevin snapped into motion. He threw a right jab that stopped six inches short of the glass, followed by a left hook that swung wide enough to hug a refrigerator.

"Hwah! Ki-Ya!"

He spun, his yellow track pants swishing violently. These were genuine nylon, ordered from eBay, replicas of the ones Bruce Lee wore in *Game of Death*. They were a size too small, riding up uncomfortably in the crotch, but Kevin accepted this. The path of the warrior was paved with discomfort.

Kevin's "dojo" was a sanctuary of martial arts history. Posters covered the wood-paneled walls: Chuck Norris holding a machine gun, Jackie Chan hanging off a clock tower, and Van Damme doing the splits between two Volvo trucks. These were his senseis. His masters. He had watched every movie in his collection at least fifty times, analyzing the frame rates, the wirework, and the facial expressions.

He had never taken a single class. Classes were for people who needed structure. Kevin was an intuitive genius. A Ronin of the suburbs.

"Focus, Kevin," he muttered. "The Iron Lotus style requires absolute stillness."

He froze, standing on one leg, his arms splayed out like a drunk flamingo. He held the pose for three seconds before his left calf began to tremble.

*An opponent approaches from the rear!* his mind screamed.

Kevin dropped the leg and spun around, executing a move he called the "Reverse Tornado Chop." In his mind, it was a blur of lethal motion. In reality, he looked like a man trying to slap a mosquito off his own back.

His hand connected with the string of the single lightbulb illuminating the basement. The bulb swung wildly, casting erratic shadows that danced like ninjas against the washing machine.

"Ha! Missed me!" Kevin taunted the shadows. He brought his right thumb up, brushed the side of his nose, and then extended his hand, fingers curling inward in the universal sign for *Come get some.*

It was time for the finisher. The move he had been practicing for weeks. The Flying Guillotine.

He eyed the heavy bag hanging from the support beam. It was actually a laundry bag filled with dirty towels and old National Geographics, but to Kevin, it was the Grandmaster of the Black Dragon Clan.

"Your reign of terror ends now," Kevin declared.

He took a running start. He leaped.

"KIIIIII-YAAAAA!"

Mid-air, Kevin realized a fundamental error in his calculations. He had forgotten about the puddle of condensation leaking from the dehumidifier. As his cheap canvas kung fu slipper hit the wet concrete, friction abandoned him.

Kevin didn't fly. He became a projectile.

He sailed past the laundry bag, flailing his arms like a bird shot out of the sky. He crashed shoulder-first into the home's main breaker panel on the far wall.

There was a sound like a frying egg, magnified a thousand times.
*ZZZ-CRACK!*

Blue electricity arced from the metal box, jumping onto Kevin's zipper. His vision went white. His body seized up, locking him into a pose that looked suspiciously like the Crane Kick.

*This is it,* Kevin thought, his brain vibrating. *I've unlocked my Chi!
The power... it's over 9000!*

The basement dissolved. The smell of pepperoni was replaced by the scent of ozone and burning pine. The sensation of wet concrete beneath his feet vanished, replaced by cold, hard stone.

With a final, thunderous *BOOM*, the light faded.

Kevin stumbled, coughing, waving his hands to clear the smoke. He was dizzy, but he felt alive. He checked his limbs. Still attached. He checked his hair. Still fantastic.

"Man," Kevin wheezed, straightening his headband. "I really gotta fix that wiring. But that adrenaline rush? Totally worth it."

He opened his eyes.

He was not in his basement.

He was standing on a circular platform of white marble, suspended high above a sea of clouds. Jagged, impossible mountain peaks pierced the mist in the distance, floating in the air as if gravity was merely a suggestion.

Surrounding the platform were bleachers carved from jade, filled with hundreds of people wearing flowing silk robes in shades of azure and white. They stared at him in stunned silence.

In front of Kevin stood two men.

One was an old man with a beard so long it was tucked into his belt. He floated, actually *floated*, three inches off the ground.

The other was a handsome, arrogant-looking youth holding a sword that glowed with a soft blue light. He looked at Kevin with a mixture of confusion and disgust.

Kevin blinked. He looked at the floating mountains. He looked at the glowing sword. He looked at the crowd.

"Whoa," Kevin whispered, a grin spreading across his face. "I must have hit my head harder than I thought. This is the most vivid hallucination ever."

He looked at the arrogant youth with the sword. The guy looked like every villain from every wuxia movie Kevin had ever pirated.

The youth stepped forward, pointing the glowing blade at Kevin's chest. "Who are you, intruder? You dare interrupt the Sacred Ascension Tournament of the Azure Cloud Sect? How did you bypass the defensive formation?"

The voice was melodramatic. Perfect.

Kevin chuckled. He knew this script. This was the part where the underdog hero arrives to shake up the establishment.

He didn't answer immediately. Instead, he widened his stance, bouncing lightly on the balls of his feet. He brushed his nose with his thumb, sniffing loudly, and then extended his right arm, curling his fingers.

"The name's Kevin," he announced, his voice echoing across the silent peak. "Kung Fu Kevin. And I don't know what a defensive formation is, buddy, but you're holding that prop sword all wrong."

The crowd gasped.

The youth's face turned red. "Prop... sword? Courting death!"

"Yeah, yeah, 'Courting death,' I've heard that one," Kevin said, hopping side to side like a boxer. "Come on, pretty boy. Let's see your style. Is it Monkey? Crane? Or are you more of a Crouching Tiger type?"

Kevin unleashed a high-pitched, warbling scream.

"WOOOO-AAAAH!"

The youth flinched, his grip on the sword faltering. The Cultivators in the stands murmured in horror.

*Did you hear that?* one Elder whispered to another. *That scream... it
contained no Qi, yet it shook my very soul. Is this the legendary Lion's Roar technique?*

*He wears strange, tight yellow garbs,* another observed. *Perhaps he is
a Grandmaster from the Southern Golden Bee Sect?*

Kevin grinned. He had them intimidated. He didn't know where he was, or why the air tasted like blueberries, but he knew one thing.

It was time to dance.

"Make your move," Kevin said, striking a pose he saw on a movie poster once. "But be warned... my hands are registered as lethal weapons."
