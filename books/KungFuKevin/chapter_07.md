# Chapter 7: Tony Hawk's Pro Cultivator

"Babe, I'm only nineteen!" Kevin protested, adjusting his headband as they walked out of the Obsidian Palace. "I ain't no Elder. That sounds like I need a walker and a bingo membership. But whatever floats your boat."

He looked up at Lin Xue, who was gracefully gliding a few inches off the ground to avoid the dust.

"Let's go check out my new digs," Kevin said, rubbing his hands together. "And hey, I'm gonna need my own flying sword, you know? Riding pillion is cool and all, but a hero needs his own wheels. Or... blades. You get it."

Lin Xue stopped. She looked at him with an expression that was becoming her default state: profound exhaustion mixed with scientific curiosity.

"Kevin," she said slowly, testing the foreign name on her tongue. "Flying swords are not... 'wheels'. They are an extension of one's soul. They require a Gold Core cultivation base to levitate, and years of mental refinement to control. You have no Qi. You cannot fly."

Kevin scoffed. "Details, shmetails. I didn't have a license when I drove my dad's El Camino, either. Just get me a sword. I'll figure out the clutch."

Lin Xue sighed. She reached into her spatial ring,a concept Kevin had dismissed as "magic pockets", and pulled out a rusty, chipped iron sword. It was a training blade for children, heavy and dull.

"This is 'Old Iron'," she said, handing it to him. "It has no spirit. It is heavy. If you can lift it, you can keep it."

Kevin grabbed the hilt. It weighed about forty pounds.

"Oof," Kevin grunted, his knees buckling slightly. He quickly corrected his posture, leaning casually on the sword like it was a cane. "Solid. Heavy duty. I like the patina. It's got that 'post-apocalyptic chic' vibe."

He patted the rusty flat of the blade. "I shall call you... **The Board.**"

"The Board?" Lin Xue asked. "A strange name for a weapon."

"It's not a weapon, babe. It's transportation."

Kevin walked to the edge of the cliff overlooking the misty abyss. Shattered Moon Peak was visible in the distance, a lonely, jagged spire of rock connected to the main sect by a flimsy-looking chain bridge.

"Alright, System," Kevin whispered. "Don't let me down. I watched *Back to the Future Part II* every day for a year."

Kevin threw the heavy iron sword off the cliff.

Lin Xue watched it fall. "You threw it away?"

"Wait for it..." Kevin said.

He sprinted.

"Ki-Ya!"

Kevin leaped off the cliff after the sword.

Lin Xue screamed. "You fool!"

She prepared to dive after him, but she stopped.

Mid-air, Kevin caught up to the falling sword. But he didn't grab the handle. He landed on the flat of the blade with both feet, crouching low, arms out for balance.

In a normal world, both Kevin and the sword would continue to plummet to a splatty death. But Kevin had assumed the **Skater Stance**.

**\[SKILL ACTIVATED: GRIND THE HEAVENS\]** **\[SUB-SKILL: OLLIE OF THE
UNBOUND WIND\]**

The sword jerked. It didn't fall. It began to *glide*.

Blue sparks erupted from under the rusty metal as it surfed on the friction of the air itself.

"Woooo-hoooo!" Kevin screamed, carving a hard left turn through a cloud bank. "Gnarly!"

Lin Xue's mouth fell open. *He... he is standing on the blade? Without Qi to bind his feet? He is using pure balance to ride the wind currents! This is the legendary* '*Cloud-Treading Step*' *lost for ten thousand years!*

"Keep up, Buttercup!" Kevin yelled over his shoulder.

He kicked the back of the sword, forcing the front up. The sword responded, shooting upward. He was literally surfing the sky.

Kevin approached the Shattered Moon Peak. He saw the roof of a dilapidated pagoda.

"Target acquired!" Kevin shouted. "Preparing for landing! Do a kickflip!"

He stomped on the tail of the sword. The heavy iron blade flipped in the air under his feet. Kevin caught it again with his sneakers, *CLANG*, and slammed into the roof of his new home.

He skidded across the ancient tiles, shredding moss and scaring a flock of spirit cranes, before grinding to a halt at the edge of the roof.

He hopped off, caught the sword by the hilt as it flipped up, and struck a pose.

"Stuck the landing," Kevin panted, his heart beating out of his chest. "Ten out of ten from the Russian judges."

Lin Xue landed softly beside him a moment later. She stared at the rusty sword, then at Kevin's cheap canvas shoes.

"You..." she breathed. "You treated a spirit tool like a... a toy. You trampled upon it. And yet, it obeyed you."

Kevin shrugged, sheathing the heavy sword through his belt loop (which immediately sagged under the weight). "It's all in the ankles, Lin. All in the ankles."

He looked around at his new estate.

Shattered Moon Peak was... a fixer-upper. The main pagoda had missing tiles. The courtyard was overgrown with purple weeds that hissed when you looked at them. The windows were covered in spiderwebs thick enough to catch a cat.

"Whoa," Kevin said, looking at the eerie, fog-covered ruins. "Look at this aesthetic. It's so goth. It's got that 'Haunted Mansion' vibe. I love it."

He walked up to the rotting wooden doors of the main hall.

"Home sweet home," Kevin said. He turned to Lin Xue. "Alright, first order of business. We need to clear this yard. Do you guys have a lawnmower? Or maybe a goat?"

Lin Xue looked at the purple weeds. "Those are Ghost-Bite Vines. They drink the blood of the living."

Kevin laughed, slapping her on the shoulder. "You and your metaphors! 'Drink the blood.' Good one. Seriously though, grab a sickle. We're doing a montage."
