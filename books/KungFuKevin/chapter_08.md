# Chapter 8: The Great Weed Whacker Massacre

"Drink the blood of the living?" Kevin laughed, wiping sweat from his forehead with the back of his wrist. "Hah! I prefer Miller Lite. Less filling, tastes great. But whatever. I'm thirsty!"

He looked at the courtyard. The purple vines were writhing. A flower the size of a basketball snapped its jaws open, revealing rows of needle-like teeth.

"Man, these dandelions are aggressive," Kevin muttered. "Must be the soil pH."

He unbuckled 'The Board' from his waist. The rusty iron sword thudded into the dirt.

"Lin, grab a rake. I'm going to do some trimming."

Lin Xue stood frozen near the gate. "Kevin... those are not dandelions. That is a Crimson Maw. It can chew through Spirit Armor. You must not approach it without a suppression talisman!"

"Suppression talisman? Is that like Roundup?" Kevin cracked his knuckles. "Nah, too many chemicals. I'm going organic."

He cranked his neck. *Crack. Crack.*

**\[SYSTEM PLAYLIST:** "**PUSH IT TO THE LIMIT**"** - SCARFACE OST\]**

Kevin grabbed the hilt of the heavy iron sword with both hands. He didn't lift it like a sword; he held it low, like a weed whacker or a scythe.

"Fore!" Kevin yelled.

He spun.

The Crimson Maw lunged. It aimed for Kevin's jugular, moving with the speed of a striking cobra.

At that exact moment, Kevin slipped on a patch of moss.

His body dipped low, his center of gravity shifting. To Lin Xue, it looked like a masterful "Drunken Immortal" dodge. The giant flower snapped at empty air where Kevin's head had been a microsecond before.

Kevin, recovering his balance, swung the heavy sword around in a clumsy, horizontal arc.

"Take a little off the top!"

THWACK.

The blunt, rusty edge of the sword smashed into the stem of the man-eating flower. It didn't slice cleanly; it pulverized the plant fiber with sheer blunt force trauma. The flower head flew across the courtyard and landed in a birdbath.

The vines hissed a high-pitched shriek of pain.

"What is that noise?" Kevin complained, swinging again. "It sounds like a tea kettle. You guys really need to check the plumbing up here."

He began to work his way through the garden. To him, it was manual labor. He was hacking away at overgrown brush.

To the sentient, demonic flora of Shattered Moon Peak, it was the Apocalypse.

A Ghost-Bite Vine tried to wrap around his ankle. Kevin, thinking he stepped in gum, stomped his foot down hard. *CRUNCH.* The vine shattered.

A spore-shooting mushroom fired toxic darts at him. Kevin paused to sneeze ("Bless you," he told himself), ducking the darts entirely. He then swatted the mushroom with the flat of his blade. "Bad mushroom! No soup for you!"

Lin Xue watched, her hand covering her mouth.

*He treats the flora of the Forbidden Zone like... like unruly pets,*
she thought. *He does not use Qi to cut them. He uses... disdain. He beats them into submission with raw iron and bad jokes.*

Within ten minutes, the courtyard was a massacre of green sludge and purple pulp.

Kevin stood in the center of the carnage, leaning on his sword, chest heaving.

"Phew," he exhaled. "Cardio. That's what I'm talking about." He looked at the pile of dead monster plants. "Great compost, though. We can grow some killer tomatoes here next season."

He turned to Lin Xue. "Okay. Now about that drink. I'm parched."

Lin Xue hesitated. She reached into her robe and produced a small, jade gourd sealed with a talisman.

"We do not have... 'Miller Lite'," she said cautiously. "This is *Fire Spirit Nectar*. It is brewed from volcanic peppers and dragon blood. It is meant to be sipped by Elders to refine their Yang energy. A single drop can burn a mortal's throat to ash."

Kevin's eyes lit up. "Dragon blood? Is that an IPA? Sounds hoppy. Gimme."

He snatched the gourd before she could protest. He popped the talisman off like a bottle cap.

"Cheers, babe."

He tilted his head back and chugged.

Glug. Glug. Glug.

Lin Xue waited for him to explode. She waited for the fire to consume him from the inside out. She prepared a healing spell.

Kevin drained the gourd. He wiped his mouth.

His face turned bright red. Steam shot out of his ears with a cartoonish
*TOOT* sound.

"Whoa!" Kevin wheezed, his voice rasping. "That's got a kick! What is that, 90 proof? Tastes like cinnamon and... gasoline."

He belched. A small puff of actual fire came out of his mouth and singed a nearby curtain.

**\[SYSTEM ALERT: TOXICITY DETECTED.\]** **\[ANALYZING SUBSTANCE: DRAGON
FIRE POISON.\]** **\[CONVERTING TO:** "**LIQUID COURAGE**"**\]**
**\[BUFF APPLIED: NO PAIN RECEPTORS FOR 4 HOURS.\]**

Kevin grinned, swaying slightly. "Good stuff. Smooth finish. Got any pretzels?"

Lin Xue stared at the empty gourd. *He drank a month's supply of Yang energy. And he just... burped fire?*

"You are..." she struggled for the words. "You are a bottomless pit."

"I get that a lot," Kevin said, stumbling toward the pagoda doors. "Now, let's check out the crib. I wanna see where I'm gonna put my recliner."

He kicked the doors open.

Inside, the hall was grand, dark, and filled with ancient statues of demons and gods. It was terrifying.

"Okay," Kevin said, looking around, hands on his hips. "It's a bit gloomy. Needs more neon. Maybe a lava lamp over there by the spooky statue. And we definitely need to move that altar. It's blocking the flow."

He pointed to a sacred altar made of human bones.

"That's gotta go," Kevin said decisively. "That's where the TV is gonna live."

"TV?" Lin Xue asked, following him in, terrified of what he would do next.

"Yeah. The idiot box. Speaking of which..." Kevin tapped the side of his head. "Hey System! Where's my loot? I got a 'Basement Dweller' title, right? Does that come with furniture?"

**\[SYSTEM RESPONSE:\]** **\[ACTIVATING** "**MAN CAVE**" **PROTOCOL.\]**
**\[ITEM GENERATION: BEAN BAG CHAIR (LEGENDARY GRADE)\]**

There was a flash of light.

In the center of the ancient hall, right next to a 5,000-year-old incense burner, a neon-green bean bag chair appeared with a wet *flump* sound.

Kevin's eyes filled with tears of joy.

"Beautiful," he whispered. He threw himself onto the bean bag. It crunched loudly.

"Lin," he said, sinking into the Styrofoam pellets. "Welcome to the Dojo. Shoes off at the door."
