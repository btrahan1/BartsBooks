# Chapter 28: The Iceman Cometh

Kevin stood on the balcony of the **Dojo of Dopeness**, sipping his morning protein shake (raw eggs and Spirit Ginseng, it tasted like dirt, but the gains were undeniable).

Below him, the sect was buzzing. Disciples were doing tai chi while eating leftover pizza. A group of initiates was trying to replicate his "Moonwalk" near the fountain.

"It's a good life," Kevin murmured, adjusting his silk robe. "Real life."

He watched a Spirit Crane fly by carrying a bucket of wings. He smiled. He wasn't looking for a way home anymore. Why would he? Back home, he was an assistant manager who had to wear a nametag. Here, he was the
**Pizza Lord**.

But as he scanned his domain, his gaze drifted up to the **Peak of Solitary Reflection**. It was a jagged, miserable-looking rock floating higher than the rest, wreathed in storm clouds. That was where Young Master Chen had been banished to "think about his failures" after the pizza-peel incident.

Kevin frowned behind his aviators.

"Can't leave an enemy at your back," Kevin whispered, quoting a line from a gritty war movie he couldn't quite remember. "That's how you get stabbed in the third act."

He set his goblet down.

"System," Kevin said. "Queue up the soundtrack. Something tense but hopeful. Think *Top Gun* volleyball scene, but with more angst."

**\[SYSTEM MUSIC QUEUE:** "**PLAYING WITH THE BOYS**"** - KENNY
LOGGINS\]**

Kevin grabbed the *Radical Shredder*.

"Time to bridge the gap."

The Peak of Solitary Reflection was wet, cold, and miserable.

Young Master Chen sat in a cave, shirtless, punching a wall of solid granite. His knuckles were bloody. His hair was a mess. His eyes were burning with hatred.

*Punch.* "I will kill him." *Punch.* "I will destroy his dough."
*Punch.* "I will..."

A low humming sound interrupted his mantra. Then, a neon blue light flooded the damp cave.

Chen spun around, Qi flaring. "Who dares?!"

Kevin floated into the cave on his board, hovering a few inches above the puddles. He looked out of place in his dragon-scale vest and sunglasses, radiating pure, unadulterated swagger.

"Sup, Turbo," Kevin said, leaning casually against the cave wall.

Chen's face twisted. "You! You dare come here? To mock me? To finish the job?"

He raised a fist wreathed in fire. "I will incinerate you where you stand!"

"Whoa, pump the brakes, Goose," Kevin said, holding up a hand. He didn't flinch. He didn't draw his nunchucks. He just stood there, cool as a cucumber in a freezer.

"I ain't here to fight. We did that. I won. You ate the pepperoni. It's canon now."

Chen trembled with rage. "Then why are you here?"

Kevin hopped off his board. He walked slowly toward Chen, ignoring the killing intent radiating off the boy.

"I'm here because I realized something, Chen. Every great man needs a rival."

Chen blinked. "A... rival?"

"Yeah," Kevin said, pacing around the confused cultivator. "Look at the greats. Rocky had Apollo. Batman has the Joker. Goku has Vegeta. Without a rival pushing them, they get soft. They get lazy."

Kevin stopped and pointed a finger at Chen's chest.

"I look at the rest of these disciples, and you know what I see? NPCs. Background characters. They eat my pizza, they cheer my name, but they don't *challenge* me."

Kevin lowered his sunglasses, looking Chen in the eye.

"But you? You got fire, kid. Literally. You hate me so much it's actually kind of impressive. That kind of passion? You can't teach that."

Chen lowered his fist slightly. "You... you think I am worthy of being your rival?"

"Worthy?" Kevin scoffed. "Buddy, you're the only one in this whole zip code who can take a punch from me and keep standing. You're the
**Iceman** to my **Maverick**."

"Ice... man?" Chen asked, confused. "But my cultivation is Fire."

"It's a metaphor, Chen! Work with me!" Kevin grabbed Chen's shoulder.

"Listen. You can stay up here, punching rocks, hating me, and eventually turn into a boring, generic villain who gets defeated in Chapter 5. OR..."

Kevin pulled a small, flat object from his pocket. It was a pair of
**Obsidian Aviators**, identical to his own, but with gold rims.

"Or," Kevin said softly. "You can get on board. You can stop trying to destroy me, and start trying to *outdo* me. You push me, I push you. We rise together. Iron sharpens iron."

Chen looked at the sunglasses. He looked at Kevin.

His entire worldview was crumbling. He had been raised to believe that defeat meant death or dishonor. But this man... this strange, yellow-pants-wearing lunatic... was offering him a third path.

"You wish for me to... become stronger? So I can defeat you?" Chen asked slowly.

"I wish for you to *try*," Kevin grinned. "Because if I beat a weakling, I look weak. But if I beat a Legend? Then I'm a God."

Kevin held out the glasses.

"So, what do you say? You wanna be a side character? Or do you wanna be the Co-Star?"

Chen stared at the glasses. His hand trembled. Slowly, he reached out. He took the aviators.

He put them on.

The world darkened, but the glare of the sun vanished. He looked cooler. He *felt* cooler.

**\[SYSTEM ALERT: RIVALRY UPDATED.\]** **\[YOUNG MASTER CHEN STATUS:
FRENEMY.\]** **\[NEW ATTRIBUTE UNLOCKED FOR CHEN:** "**THE GRUDGING RESPECT**"**.\]**

Chen stood up straighter. He wiped the blood from his knuckles.

"I will take your offer, Kevin," Chen said, his voice low and serious. "But know this. I will not rest until I am faster, stronger, and... cooler than you."

"That's the spirit!" Kevin cheered, slapping him on the back.

Kevin held out his hand.

"You can be my wingman any time."

Chen looked at the hand. He didn't know what a 'wingman' was, but he understood the gesture. It was a warrior's pact.

He clasped Kevin's hand firmly.

"I do not have wings," Chen said seriously. "But... I will watch your back."

"Good," Kevin said, letting go. "Now, get cleaned up. We got work to do. I need a Security Chief for the Cinema, and those Enforcers of yours are too soft. I need someone scary."

Kevin jumped back on his board.

"Meet me at the Dojo in an hour. And Chen?"

Chen looked up.

"Bring your fire. The popcorn machine is acting up again."

Kevin kicked the board and zoomed out of the cave, leaving a trail of blue light.

Chen stood alone in the cave. He touched the gold-rimmed glasses on his face. He looked at his fist.

For the first time in weeks, he didn't feel shame. He felt... a challenge.

"The Iceman," Chen whispered, testing the name. He ignited a small flame in his palm, watching it reflect in the obsidian lenses.

"I like it."
