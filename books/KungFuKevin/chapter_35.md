# Chapter 35: The Filibuster of Fury

The **Editor**, a being of pure data and annoyance, floated ominously above the clouds. Its pixelated face scowled.

"**AUDIT IN PROGRESS,**" it droned. "**YOUR LOGIC IS INVALID. PREPARE FOR FORMATTING.**"

It raised a hand, and the very air around Kevin began to pixelate, turning into blocky, low-resolution static.

"Hold up!" Kevin shouted, holding up a hand in the universal 'stop' gesture. "Timeout! Freeze frame!"

He kicked his board forward, floating right up to the chrome entity. He leaned in, invading its personal space, pulling his sunglasses down to the tip of his nose.

"Listen up... Mr. IRS," Kevin said, his voice dripping with condescension. "Let me 'splain something to you. You think you're the boss because you got a clipboard and a shiny suit? Please. I know how this works. You're middle management. You're a cog."

Kevin poked the Editor in the chest. It felt like poking a TV screen.

"You actually work for me, you dig? Without me, there's no story. No conflict. No views. I am the Content Creator. You're just the Moderator."

The Editor paused. "**CONTENT... CREATOR?**"

"Exactly!" Kevin pressed on, pacing back and forth on his board. "And another thing... taxation without representation... that's a serious no-no. Did I vote for you? Did *they* vote for you?"

He gestured to Lin Xue and Chen, who were watching with a mixture of terror and confusion.

"I did not cast a ballot," Chen confirmed helpfully, steam hissing from his ears.

"See?" Kevin spread his arms. "This is a tyranny! And did I mention the appeals process?"

Kevin whistled long and low.

"Man, that could take decades in a place like this. You gotta file the forms in triplicate. You gotta get them notarized by a Golden Core Elder. You gotta wait for the celestial alignment. By the time you delete me, the sequel will already be out on Blu-ray!"

The Editor's glow flickered. It seemed to be processing. "**ADMINISTRATIVE DELAYS... ARE STATISTICALLY PROBABLE.**"

"Probable? It's guaranteed!" Kevin grinned. He reached into his pocket. Not for a weapon. But for a prop.

He pulled out three envelopes.

"So..." Kevin said smoothly. "Have you heard of the game called... 'Let's Make a Deal'?"

"**A GAME OF CHANCE?**" The Editor asked.

"No," Kevin corrected. "A game of *opportunity*."

He held up the envelopes.

"Envelope Number One," Kevin tapped it. "Contains a full audit report of
*your* performance. I bet you've missed a few plot holes in other
dimensions, haven't you? Wouldn't want Corporate to find out."

The Editor stiffened.

"Envelope Number Two," Kevin continued. "Contains a lifetime supply of
**Thunder Pizza** coupons. Delivered to any dimension. Hot and fresh."

"And Envelope Number Three..."

Kevin ripped it open. It was empty.

"Is the Mystery Box!" Kevin shouted. "It could be anything! It could be a boat! It could be a new plot twist that raises your ratings through the roof!"

Kevin leaned in close.

"So, what's it gonna be, chrome-dome? Do you want to spend the next ten thousand years filling out paperwork to delete a guy who sells spicy chicken wings? Or do you want to be a hero? Do you want to be the guy who discovered the next big genre?"

"**GENRE...**" The Editor mused. "**CULINARY-CULTIVATION-CYBERPUNK... IT IS TESTING WELL IN THE DEMOGRAPHICS.**"

"It's trending, baby!" Kevin shouted. "We're viral!"

The Editor lowered its stylus. The pixelation around them faded.

"**VERY WELL,**" The Editor said. "**I WILL CLASSIFY THIS AS A** '**SPIN-OFF**'**. BUT BE WARNED, USER KEVIN. IF THE RATINGS DROP... I WILL RETURN.**"

"Fair enough," Kevin said. He tossed Envelope Number Two (the coupons) to the entity. "Don't eat the crust all at once. It's got a kick."

The Editor vanished into the rift. The sky returned to normal. The sun came out.

Kevin exhaled a breath he didn't know he was holding. He collapsed onto his board.

"Phew," Kevin wiped sweat from his brow. "That was close. I was running out of metaphors."

Lin Xue flew up to him. She sheathed her sword.

"You defeated a cosmic entity... with bureaucracy and coupons?" she asked, stunned.

"The pen is mightier than the sword, Lin," Kevin said, putting his sunglasses back on. "Especially when the pen is used to sign a liability waiver."

He looked at Chen.

"Iceman, you good?"

Chen was staring at the spot where the rift had been.

"You..." Chen whispered. "You negotiated with the Heavens. You bribed the Gods."

Chen slowly started to smile. A wide, terrifying, steamy smile.

"Teach me," Chen said. "Teach me the Dao of the Deal."

Kevin laughed.

"Class is in session, boys and girls. But first..."

He pointed toward home.

"Who wants tacos?"
