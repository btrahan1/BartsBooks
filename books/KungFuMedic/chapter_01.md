# Chapter 1: Introduction

He stood up in the aisle, his six-foot-two frame coiled like a spring. He snapped into a horse stance, his hands held up in a classic praying mantis style.

"Every time, Bruce. Every single time," a weary voice sighed from the row behind him.

Sal, a man who had been a medic since pagers were considered high-tech, hauled himself out of his seat. He had a graying mustache that had seen too many 3 a.m. chili dogs and a permanent look of resignation.

"Let him get it out of his system, Sal," said Maya, hopping into the aisle with a grin. She was younger, sharper, and viewed Bruce with the kind of exasperated affection one has for a very large, very clumsy golden retriever. "It's better than him trying the Drunken Master style while driving."

Bruce ignored them. He was in the zone. He took a slow, deliberate step forward. His mouth moved silently, a beat of poorly-dubbed celluloid passing before the words came out. "Your technique is sloppy. You have dishonored my school!"

He threw a crisp, shadow-boxed punch, followed by a spinning kick that was surprisingly agile. It was also dangerously close to taking out a cardboard standee of a cartoon popcorn box.

"Okay, Master Po, let's go. We have a pre-shift check," Sal grumbled, nudging Bruce toward the exit.

"A warrior is always prepared," Bruce said sagely, walking backward down the hallway. "He does not check his gear. He *is* the gear."

"Right. Tell that to the rig checklist," Maya chirped, holding the door for them.

The morning sun hit them as they entered the parking lot. Bruce immediately saw it not as a stretch of cracked asphalt, but as a rooftop training ground, the final battleground from a dozen different films. He saw their ambulance, Unit 73, parked near the edge of the lot.

"Behold!" Bruce bellowed, pointing a dramatic finger. "The Batmobile!"

"It's a Ford E-350, Bruce," Sal said, jingling the keys. "It smells like bleach and regret."

Bruce ran a hand lovingly along the side of the ambulance, patting the faded emblem. "This chariot has seen many battles, Sal. It has earned our respect." He then proceeded to do a series of high-knee warm-ups, his reflection warping in the side mirror.

Maya was already opening the passenger door, pulling out the rig checklist clipboard when the radio crackled to life, the dispatcher's voice cutting through the morning calm.

*"Unit 73, available for a call?"*

Sal grabbed the radio from his belt clip. "73 is available."

*"Copy, 73. Respond to a priority one, robbery in progress at the
Kwik-E-Mart, corner of 12th and Elm. Use caution. Suspect is reported to be armed."*

The mood shifted in an instant. Maya's cheerful demeanor hardened into focus. Sal's eyes narrowed, his posture straightening. The years of routine and training took over.

For Bruce, it was something else entirely. His warm-ups stopped. A slow grin spread across his face. He looked from Sal to Maya, his eyes wide with destiny.

"The city... it calls for a hero."

Sal just sighed, pinching the bridge of his nose. "Just get in the back, Bruce. And for the love of God, don't try to catch any bullets with your teeth."

"No promises," Bruce said with a wink, sliding the heavy side door open and hopping inside. As he slammed it shut, his partners could hear his muffled, triumphant yell from inside.

"Let's. Get. DANGEROUS!"

Of course. Let's get this show on the road.
