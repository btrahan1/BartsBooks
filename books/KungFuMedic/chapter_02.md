# Chapter 2: Code Three and a Movie Monologue

The ambulance didn't so much pull out of the parking lot as it escaped. The engine roared with a ferocity Sal was certain wasn't factory standard, and the tires chirped as Bruce swung the big vehicle onto the main street, a perfect blend of precision and madness. In the back, Maya was immediately thrown against a supply cabinet.

"Seatbelt!" Sal barked from the passenger seat, his hands already gripping the dashboard and the "oh-shit" handle above the door in a white-knuckled embrace.

"Belts are for the weak!" Bruce's voice boomed from the driver's seat, though it wasn't directed at Sal. It was a proclamation to the world. He flicked on the sirens and lights, and the world dissolved into a strobing, wailing tunnel of noise and motion.

This was Bruce's temple. His cockpit. His dojo on wheels.

"In a city of shadows, where crime lurks around every corner," Bruce began his narration, his voice low and gravelly like a movie trailer voice-over. "One team dares to answer the call. They are the thin, white and red line between order and chaos."

He spun the wheel, threading the massive ambulance between a city bus and a terrified-looking man in a Prius with the grace of a gazelle. In the back, Maya was securing the jump bag with a bungee cord, her face pale.

"He's doing the voice again, isn't he?" she yelled over the siren's scream.

"Just breathe through your nose, kid," Sal shouted back, his eyes squeezed shut. "It helps with the nausea."

"The Beast roars down the asphalt canyons!" Bruce continued, oblivious. "Its war cry scatters the metal sheep of the morning commute. Each red light is a test, a crimson guardian daring us to fail. But we do not fail. For justice has a destination... and we are its chariot!"

He took a corner faster than a vehicle of that size had any right to, the gurney in the back rattling violently in its restraints. Maya, who had been trying to check the oxygen tank levels, slid a full three feet before bracing herself against the wall.

"Bruce, I swear to God, if you dislodge the primary O2 tank again, I'm sedating you myself!" she screamed.

"A warrior's path is fraught with peril!" Bruce declared, patting the dashboard affectionately. "Fear not, my comrades! The Beast knows the way. It smells the fear, the desperation... and the cheap, delicious coffee of the Kwik-E-Mart."

Sal risked opening one eye. "He's not wrong about the coffee. It's surprisingly decent."

"Focus, Sal!" Maya yelled, finally getting the jump bag secured.

Ahead, the traffic was gridlocked. A sea of brake lights. For a normal driver, it was the end of the line. For Bruce, it was an invitation. With a wild grin, he swerved onto the wide shoulder, sending a shower of gravel against the side of a minivan.

"They thought they could cage the tiger," Bruce whispered, his eyes gleaming with intensity. "But the tiger knows no cage."

"That was a family of five, Bruce!" Sal yelped, his voice cracking. "I think I saw a baby!"

"A baby who will one day tell the tale of how he saw heroes in action!"

And then, just as suddenly as it began, it was over.

Bruce swung the ambulance into the Kwik-E-Mart parking lot, the vehicle gliding into a perfect, diagonal stop that expertly blocked two other cars while providing a clear line to the front door. The tires didn't screech; they sighed. The engine didn't sputter; it went silent with a click.

The sudden quiet was more jarring than the sirens. Through the windshield, they could see it. Two police cruisers were already on scene, officers crouched behind their doors. The glass front door of the convenience store was shattered.

Bruce killed the lights. The frantic, strobing colors vanished, leaving only the stark reality of the morning sun glinting off the broken glass. He took a deep, centering breath, his entire demeanor shifting from maniacal driver to... well, a different kind of maniac.

He turned to Sal, his face a mask of grim determination.

"It's showtime."
