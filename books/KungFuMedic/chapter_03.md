# Chapter 3: What's the 411?

The driver's side door of the ambulance flew open with a bang. But Bruce Johnson didn't just get out. He emerged. In one fluid, practiced motion, he swung his body out, kept low to the ground, and performed a textbook tactical roll behind the cover of the open door, coming up on one knee like a SWAT team breacher.

Sal and Maya simply stepped out of the passenger and side doors, respectively.

"Was that really necessary?" Maya muttered, pulling a heavy-looking trauma bag from the back.

"You never know where the sniper's nest is," Bruce whispered dramatically from his crouch. "Stay frosty."

Sal just sighed, the sound now a familiar part of their daily symphony. "Stay here, Bruce. We stage until the police clear the scene. That's the protocol."

But Bruce was already moving. He saw the two officers crouched behind the door of their cruiser, weapons drawn. He saw the tension, the focus. He recognized his cue. With his shoulders squared and a stride brimming with unearned authority, he walked directly toward the police line. He didn't jog; he strode with the deliberate pace of a man who owned the scene.

He stopped just beside the senior officer, a grizzled sergeant with "Miller" on his nameplate, and clapped a hand on the man's shoulder.

"Okay, boys. Nice job," Bruce said, his voice a low, commanding rumble. "Give me the 411."

The younger officer, Davis, flinched at the sudden contact, nearly swinging his pistol around. Sergeant Miller just slowly turned his head, his tired eyes tracing a path from the hand on his shoulder up to Bruce's intensely serious face. His expression was one of pure, unfiltered bewilderment.

"Who in the blue hell are you?" Miller asked, his voice flat.

Sal trotted up, holding his hands out in a placating gesture. "He's with us, Sergeant. Paramedic. Brain-to-mouth filter is on backorder. Please, continue."

Miller gave Bruce one last, long stare before turning his attention back to the store. "One shooter, male, appears agitated. Armed with a handgun. He's got the clerk as a hostage behind the checkout counter. We have no clean line of sight."

Bruce nodded slowly, stroking his chin as if processing complex battlefield intelligence. He gazed at the shattered front door, his eyes distant.

"So," he began, his voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper. "We have a lone wolf operative. A desperate man with nothing to lose, pushed to the edge by a society that no longer had a place for him. A ghost."

Miller and Davis exchanged a confused glance.

"He's taken an innocent," Bruce continued, his gaze intensifying. "The very soul of this city, held in the balance. And he's chosen his kill box with tactical precision. A classic CQC scenario. Close Quarters Combat. He holds all the cards in this deadly game."

He paused, turning to lock eyes with Sergeant Miller. "This isn't about the money in the register, is it, Sergeant? This is about sending a message."

Miller just stared, his mouth slightly agape. He looked at Sal and mouthed the words, *Is he okay?*

Before Sal could offer another apology, a sharp, terrified scream erupted from inside the Kwik-E-Mart, cutting through the morning air and shattering the bizarre calm.

Here we go. It's time for our hero to shine, much to everyone else's horror.
