# Chapter 4: The Serpentine Protocol

The scream acted on Bruce like a starter pistol. All semblance of protocol, reason, and self-preservation evaporated, replaced by pure, unadulterated action-hero instinct. His body coiled.

"There's no time, we have to act now!" he hissed, his voice a low, urgent growl. "Innocent lives are at stake!"

"Bruce, NO!" Sal yelled, lunging forward as if to physically restrain him. "STAND DOWN! That is an order!"

But it was like trying to stop a tidal wave with a bucket.

"The time for talk is over," Bruce declared to no one in particular. "It's time... for action."

He launched himself forward. Instead of running in a straight line across the twenty yards of open pavement, he opted for a more... scenic route. He planted a hand on the hood of Sergeant Miller's cruiser, vaulting over it in a surprisingly clean arc. He landed on the other side in a perfect three-point stance, his head snapping up to assess the "battlefield."

"What the hell is he doing?" Officer Davis sputtered, his pistol wavering for a second.

From the crouch, Bruce exploded into a forward roll across the asphalt, coming to his feet just beside a concrete trash receptacle. He pressed his back against it, peering around the edge as if expecting a hail of gunfire.

"He's gonna get himself killed!" Maya breathed, her hand flying to her mouth.

"Serpentine, Bruce! Serpentine!" Sal screamed, a desperate, last-ditch effort to impart some kind of tactical advice, which Bruce immediately misinterpreted as a brilliant suggestion.

A wild grin lit up Bruce's face. He burst from cover, running in a frantic, zig-zagging pattern across the parking lot. He punctuated the maneuver with a completely unnecessary, yet shockingly fluid, cartwheel.

"I think I'm in love," Davis whispered in awe. Sergeant Miller shot him a look that could curdle milk.

The final leg of his journey was a low, skidding slide on his knees, bringing him to a stop right beside the shattered glass door of the Kwik-E-Mart. He was a whirlwind of motion, a ballet of bad ideas executed with breathtaking athleticism. The entire spectacle took less than ten seconds.

He pressed himself flat against the wall next to the entrance, hidden from the view of anyone inside. He was now the point man. The tip of the spear. He took a deep, centering breath, then turned his head back towards the police.

He held up a clenched fist. He then pointed two fingers at his own eyes, then jabbed them emphatically towards the door. *I have eyes on the situation.*

He followed this by holding up three fingers, then making a circular motion, before drawing a thumb across his throat. *Three hostiles, circle and neutralize.*

He finished with a complex flurry of gestures that involved tapping his helmetless head, flapping his arms like a bird, and then giving two thumbs-up. *I will create a distraction using the element of surprise, then we go in.*

Back behind the cruiser, the two officers and two paramedics stared in stunned silence.

Sergeant Miller lowered his weapon slightly and turned to Sal, his face a perfect mask of weary confusion.

"Tell me," the sergeant said, his voice dangerously calm. "Is that sign language for 'I have a death wish'?"

Here we go. The point of no return.
