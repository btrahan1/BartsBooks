# Chapter 6: The Tooth of the Dragon

Time slowed to a crawl, the frames clicking by one by one, just like in the final reel of a Shaw Brothers classic. The black circle of the gun's muzzle seemed to expand, becoming a dark tunnel into eternity. For another man, this would be a moment of terror. For Bruce Leeroy The Magnificent, it was a moment of pure, crystalline clarity.

There was no fear. There was only destiny.

His eyes, which had been filled with a righteous fire, now widened into saucers. This was it. The ultimate test. The technique whispered about in only the most secret of dojos, a move so legendary, so impossible, that only one in a generation would dare attempt it. The Bullet Catch.

He had 'trained' for this. He had practiced in his mirror, using a Nerf gun and foam darts, his timing nearly perfected. This was just a larger, faster dart.

He shifted his weight, sinking lower into a wide, powerful horse stance. His feet scraped against the grimy linoleum, rooting him to the very center of the earth. His hands, which had been held in a generic combat pose, now came up to frame his face, fingers splayed like a lion's claws.

His mouth began to move. Silently, his lips twitched, forming words a half-beat before the sound could escape, a perfect, self-made kung fu dub.

Then, the sound came, a bellow that echoed off the shelves of potato chips and beef jerky.

"YOU COURT DEATH!"

The gunman's finger tightened on the trigger. He flinched, a spasm of rage and confusion.

From behind the counter, the cashier rose up, the Louisville Slugger held high over his shoulder. His face was a mask of primal terror and adrenaline.

It all happened in a single, explosive instant.

A deafening CRACK! filled the small store as the gun fired, a muzzle flash of orange and white blooming in the air.

At the exact same moment, a sickening THWACK! echoed as the heavy ash bat connected squarely with the side of the gunman's head.

The gunman's body went limp, his eyes rolling back as he crumpled to the floor like a puppet with its strings cut. The bullet, its path already set, flew true.

The cashier, breathing in ragged, hysterical gasps, was not finished. He raised the bat again.

THWACK!

And again.

THWACK!

And one more time, for good measure.

THWACK!
