# Chapter 7: The Problem with Physics

The world became a film running at one-quarter speed.

The blossom of fire at the end of the pistol's barrel was beautiful, a silent, expanding flower of orange and white. Bruce saw it not as a threat, but as a challenge. He saw the tiny projectile, a spec of copper and lead, emerge from that bloom. It wasn't a bullet. It was a test.

He saw the cashier rising behind the gunman, the baseball bat swinging in a graceful, desperate arc. A secondary hero, providing support in the final fight. It was perfect.

But his focus was singular. The tiny, angry hornet of lead spinning through the air towards him.

Time, for Bruce, stretched into an eternity. He had a thousand moments to think, to act, to achieve the impossible. His body moved with a grace he didn't know he possessed. It was the grace of instinct, of a million daydreams made manifest.

He saw the bullet's trajectory. It was heading right for his heart. A fatal shot.

*Unacceptable.*

He dipped his head, a micro-movement, a subtle shift that would have been invisible to the naked eye in real-time. He aligned his mouth with the bullet's path. He opened his jaw, just wide enough. He had practiced this. The spacing had to be perfect. Too wide, and it would fly past. Too narrow, and it would shatter his teeth on impact.

He could feel the air compressing in front of the projectile. He could almost read the manufacturer's stamp on its spinning surface.

His jaw snapped shut.

There was a sharp, metallic CLINK!

For one glorious, infinitesimal fraction of a second, he had done it. The technique of the masters. He had caught the bullet. He could feel the pressure of it, wedged perfectly between his upper and lower incisors. A wave of pure, unadulterated triumph washed over him. He was a legend. He was the hero.

And then, physics, the universe's undefeated champion, had its say.

A bullet is not a static object. It is a vessel of immense kinetic energy, a messenger that refuses to be stopped by something as quaint as dental enamel.

The bullet didn't stop.

The energy did not vanish. It transferred. A shockwave of unbelievable force erupted from the point of contact. The triumphant *clink* was instantly consumed by a soundless, internal explosion of pressure.

His head was not knocked back; it was violently snapped back, as if by an invisible giant. The world in front of him ceased to be a picture. It became a violent, blurry smear of color and light. He registered the solid *thwack* of the bat hitting the gunman, but it sounded like it was coming from a million miles away, at the end of a long tunnel.

He wasn't standing anymore. He was falling, but he couldn't feel his body. The linoleum floor rushed up to meet him, but he felt no impact.

His last coherent thought was not one of fear, or pain, or regret. It was one of honest, critical self-assessment.

*So... close. Need to work on... grip strength...*

The world dissolved. The bright, fluorescent lights of the Kwik-E-Mart, the flashing red and blue from the police cars outside, the concerned face of the cashier---it all faded into a deep, silent black.

And then, the blackness was pierced. A single point of light appeared in the center of his vision. It did not flicker; it grew, steadily, brilliantly. It wasn't a harsh, artificial light. It was warm, golden, and utterly absolute, consuming the darkness, consuming everything.

Bruce Johnson, Ambulance Driver, stared directly into the magnificent, blinding light.

Interlude: The Soul in Cubicle 7G

The Multiversal System was a monument to infinite, soul-crushing bureaucracy. It existed in a dimension of pure data, which to its beleaguered employees, looked a lot like a vast, grey office space under the unforgiving hum of fluorescent nebulae.

In cubicle 7G, a being named Zorp rubbed his three temples in a slow, circular motion. Zorp was a mid-level Soul Classification Monitor, a job that combined the existential weight of judging the dead with the mind-numbing tedium of data entry. His desk was littered with cosmic-energy drink cans and the faint, shimmering residue of a thousand closed case files.

A soft, insistent chime pinged in his cerebral cortex. Another file. Zorp sighed, the sound like grinding gravel. He straightened his slightly-too-tight celestial collar and opened the notification.

`INCOMING SOUL. DESIGNATION: HEROIC DEATH.`

Zorp perked up slightly. Heroic Deaths were usually straightforward. Gallant knights, self-sacrificing soldiers, firefighters rushing into a blaze. They had good stats, clear motivations. Easy to process.

He pulled up the soul's data packet. `SUBJECT: JOHNSON, BRUCE.` A holographic window materialized, displaying the key metrics.

`[CRITERIA MET: VOLUNTARY INTERVENTION IN LETHAL-FORCE EVENT. ACTION DIRECTLY RESULTED IN SAVING OF NON-COMBATANT LIFE. MOTIVATION: ALTRUISTIC (Probable)].`

"See? Simple," Zorp murmured to himself, already reaching for the "Standard Warrior Reincarnation" macro. But then, as per protocol, he initiated the Final Moments Playback. His headache, which had been a dull throb, began to pulse with a malevolent rhythm.

He watched the scene unfold in a detached, third-person view. He saw the subject's "Serpentine Protocol," the bizarre hand signals, the grand, unrequested entrance.

"Oh, for nebula's sake," Zorp groaned, slumping in his chair. "It's one of *those*."

He watched the monologue, the system automatically cross-referencing the litany of titles with a billion cultural databases and coming up with nothing but error messages. He watched the subject assume a stance and shout a challenge. Then he zoomed in on the final, fatal moment.

The system's analysis appeared in a sidebar. `[TECHNIQUE ATTEMPTED: UNKNOWN. CLASSIFICATION PENDING. NEAREST ANALOGY: ORAL PROJECTILE CAPTURE.]`

Zorp's head hit his desk with a soft, metaphysical thud. He'd been doing this job for three millennia, and "Oral Projectile Capture" was a new, and deeply stupid, low.

The problem now was classification. He pulled up the soul's base stats, and the headache flared into a full-blown migraine.

`STR: 11` `DEX: 12` `CON: 11` `INT: 8` `WIS: 7` `LCK: 6`

"Utterly, spectacularly mediocre," Zorp muttered. But then, the System's auto-sorter flashed a helpful suggestion, based on the soul's previous life data.

`[PAST OCCUPATION: FIELD MEDIC / AMBULANCE DRIVER]` `[SUGGESTED CLASS: HEALER]`

Zorp stared at the suggestion, then at the Wisdom stat of 7, then back at the suggestion. He actually checked the system's core programming to see if a bug had been reported.

`[ERROR: MINIMUM WISDOM FOR HEALER CLASS: 10. PLEASE RE-EVALUATE.]`

"You don't say," Zorp said to the uncaring system. He couldn't make him a Healer. The stats were too low to make him a decent Warrior or Rogue. He was a cosmic edge case, a piece of paperwork that didn't fit in any of the standard boxes. Zorp's only goal now was to get this file off his desk.

He scrolled through the list of available entry-level positions in reincarnation-ready realms. He needed something with no stat requirements. Something nobody wanted. His eyes landed on a requisition order from a high-fantasy Wuxia world known for its constant, bloody conflicts.

`[REALM: IRON-BLOOD (SECTOR 7G). STATUS: HIGH-ATTRITION. POSITIONS AVAILABLE.]`

He scrolled down the list. `Swordsman (Recruit). Archer (Recruit). Alchemist's Apprentice.` Then, at the very bottom, he found it. A position so menial, so utterly devoid of glory, it was perpetually unfilled.

He typed in the new designation, a faint, cruel smile on his face.

`CLASS ASSIGNMENT: ASSISTANT TO THE ASSISTANT FIELD MEDIC.`

He added a final, cautionary note to the file. `[NOTE: SUBJECT POSSESSES DANGEROUS LEVELS OF UNWARRANTED SELF-CONFIDENCE. ON-THE-JOB LEARNING IS THE ONLY VIABLE PATH. SUPERVISION IS NOT RECOMMENDED, IT IS CRUCIAL.]`

With a final, decisive keystroke, he hit the "Approve & Transmigrate" button. The file for Bruce Johnson vanished from his queue.

Zorp leaned back in his chair, the pulsing in his temples finally subsiding. "Not my problem anymore," he sighed, reaching for a can of lukewarm Cosmic Cola. "Good luck with that one, Iron-Blood Realm."
