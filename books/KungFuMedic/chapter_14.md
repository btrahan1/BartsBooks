# Chapter 14: The Laws of Chow Time

Lin led BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent through the grand stone archway, leaving the humid, quiet solitude of the Hall of Rebirth behind. The change was immediate. They stepped into a vast, bustling common area that felt like a cross between a military barracks and a monastery.

The air was filled with the din of a hundred conversations, the clang of steel from a distant training yard, and the unmistakable, savory smell of roasting meat. Warriors in various states of training gear moved with purpose, acolytes like herself hurried along on errands, and stern-faced instructors observed everything with critical eyes.

"This is the Hub," Lin explained, raising her voice to be heard over the noise. She began walking, expecting him to follow. "The dormitories are down that corridor. Men's on the left, women's on the right. Training yards are through the far doors. The infirmary, where you'll be assigned, is on the second level..."

She continued her well-rehearsed orientation speech, pointing out landmarks and explaining the daily schedule. It was a monologue she'd given a hundred times, and her mind was only half on it. The other half was still trying to process the sheer, magnificent absurdity of the man trailing behind her. He was, without a doubt, the strangest soul she had ever processed.

"...and a final word of warning," she said, turning around to emphasize her point. "The Sect Master does not tolerate insubordination, so it is crucial that you show respect to your superiors, which, for the time being, is pretty much everyo---"

She stopped. The space behind her was empty. He was gone.

A jolt of pure panic shot through her. She had lost her charge---a level 1 soul with the IQ of a rock and the self-preservation instincts of a moth---in the middle of the busiest part of the facility. He could be anywhere. He could be challenging a master swordsman to a duel. He could be trying to "fix" a complex magical artifact with a percussive slap.

Her eyes darted around the hall, frantically scanning the crowd. She half-expected to see a commotion, a crowd gathering around some new, unfolding disaster. But she saw nothing out of the ordinary. Then her eyes followed the scent of food.

And there he was.

Standing patiently in the long, winding line for the cafeteria, tucked between a hulking barbarian type and a wiry-looking archer. He wasn't causing trouble. He wasn't monologuing. He was just looking at the bubbling stew pots with an expression of intense, primal hunger.

Lin marched over to him, her face a storm cloud of exasperation. She tapped him firmly on the shoulder.

"What are you doing?" she demanded in a harsh whisper. "I was in the middle of giving you the tour!"

Bruce turned, his expression not one of guilt, but of simple, happy recognition. "Oh, hey Lin! You want me to save you a spot?" He then leaned in conspiratorially. "I'm gonna get the big ladle of that brown stuff. It looks epic."

"You're not supposed to be in here! This is for warriors who have completed their morning training regimen!"

Bruce just patted his flat, muscular stomach, which let out a low, rumbling growl as if on cue.

"Man!" he said, his voice full of awe. "That resurrection thing sure burns up some calories. I'm starving!"
