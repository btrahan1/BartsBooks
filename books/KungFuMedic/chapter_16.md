# Chapter 16: The Legend of the Kwik-E-Mart

The truce lasted for approximately ninety seconds. That was how long it took Lin to get a hunk of black bread, a wedge of hard cheese, and return to where she had left BruceLeeroy. She scanned the long, noisy tables, expecting to find him eating alone, perhaps shoveling stew into his mouth with his bare hands.

What she found was far worse.

He was indeed at a table, but he was not alone. He was the sun, and a small solar system of burly, battle-hardened warriors had formed an orbit around him. No less than six of them---three scarred swordsmen, a mountain of a man with a battle-axe resting by his feet, and two archers---were leaning in, their own meals forgotten, completely captivated.

And BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent, as she now forced herself to think of him, was holding court.

He shoveled a spoonful of stew into his mouth, chewed twice, and swallowed before continuing his grand narrative, using the spoon as a prop to gesture wildly.

"...so the leader of the Iron Skull Gang---a vicious brute named 'El Macho'---he looks at me, and I look at him," Bruce narrated, his voice low and dramatic. "He's got the clerk, see? A civilian. An innocent. And he's got this... this hand-cannon. A fire-spitter that launches tiny metal demons of death!"

The warriors around the table murmured in awe. "Metal demons..." one of the swordsmen whispered, his eyes wide.

"He screams, 'You're a dead man, Magnificent!' and unleashes the demon!" Bruce slammed his spoon on the table for emphasis, sloshing stew onto the wood. "But he was too slow. My senses, honed by a thousand battles, saw it coming. I moved like the wind, a shadow in the storm. I snatched the metal demon right out of the air!"

Lin stopped dead in her tracks about ten feet from the table. The dull, familiar throb began to return behind her eyes.

"I held it there, between my thumb and forefinger," Bruce continued, demonstrating with a crumb of bread. "The little demon was angry. It was trying to escape. I could feel its power. But my will... my will was stronger. I crushed it into dust with a single flex of my chi!"

The man with the battle-axe let out a low whistle of appreciation.

"With the demon defeated, I disarmed him with a spinning crane kick, rescued the clerk, and neutralized the entire Iron Skull Gang---all twelve of them---using nothing but a bag of stale pretzels and the art of Dim Mak, the Death Touch."

Lin's headache was back with a vengeance. She listened to this utterly fabricated, physically impossible account of his demise and felt a sense of cosmic despair. He hadn't just embellished the story. He had rewritten it from the ground up, casting himself as a demigod in a tale that likely involved him tripping over his own feet.

She marched the final few steps to the table and slammed the bread and cheese down next to his bowl with a loud thud that made everyone jump.

"That," she said, her voice tight with strained patience, "is the most ridiculous story I have ever heard."

Bruce looked up, his face shining with the glory of his own tale, and gave her a brilliant grin. "I know, right? It was epic!"
