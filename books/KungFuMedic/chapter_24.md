# Chapter 24: Doctor Magnificent, I Presume

Lin, now resigned to her fate as a handler for this force of nature, pointed a weary finger at a cot in the corner. A little girl, no older than six, lay quietly, her face pale and streaked with tears. A crude wooden splint was bound to her forearm.

Bruce strode over with the immense authority of a chief surgeon on his morning rounds. He stopped at the foot of the cot and turned to Lin, who had followed him like a reluctant shadow.

"All right, you," he said, his voice a low, professional murmur. "Give me the 411 on this patient. She looks like she's in pain. We can't have that, now can we?"

"Her name is Mei," Lin whispered, keeping her voice down. "She fell from the apple tree behind the kitchens. Her arm is broken. A senior acolyte put on the splint and a pain-dulling poultice, but we haven't been able to set the bone yet. She's too scared."

Bruce nodded, his face a mask of deep contemplation, as if processing complex medical data. "A high-impact, gravity-induced trauma. Multiple fractures. Got it."

He then moved to the side of the cot, and his entire demeanor shifted. The gruff, all-business "doctor" persona melted away, replaced by a gentle, disarming warmth. He crouched down so he was eye-level with the little girl, a feat that made him look like a folded-up giant.

He crossed his eyes, puffed out his cheeks, and wiggled his ears.

"Hey there, sweetheart," he whispered, his voice soft and goofy. "The name's Doctor Magnificent. Can you tell me where the pain is?"

The little girl, Mei, who had been staring at her arm with wide, fearful eyes, looked up at him. A small, watery giggle escaped her lips, the first sound she'd made in an hour. Through her tears, she shyly pointed a finger on her good hand towards the splint.

"My arm," she sniffled. "It hurts a lot."

The funny face on Bruce vanished, replaced by a look of sincere compassion. He reached out and gently patted her good hand.

"I know it does," he said, his voice full of a genuine kindness that surprised even Lin. "But don't you worry about a thing. We're gonna get you fixed right up. You're gonna be climbing trees again in no time."

He gave her one last, reassuring smile before standing up, a determined glint in his eye. He cracked his knuckles with a series of loud, ominous pops.

"Okay," he announced to Lin. "I'm going in."

This is Bruce at his absolute best: a perfect storm of unwarranted confidence, genuine kindness, and terrible ideas.
