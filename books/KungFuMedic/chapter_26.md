# Chapter 26: The Cost of a Miracle

Instead of "three," Bruce's voice exploded into his now-familiar battle cry.

"BY THE POWER OF GREYSKULL!"

In the same instant, he gave the little girl's arm a short, sharp, expertly-applied tug. There was a faint, sickening *click* as the bones slid back into alignment, a sound that made Lin flinch. He then immediately slapped his other hand onto the girl's forearm, right over the break.

A flash of blue light erupted from his palm. It wasn't the tiny, apologetic flicker from before. Fueled by his single, precious extra point of Wisdom, this flash was brighter, more substantial. It enveloped the girl's entire forearm for a split second, a brilliant cerulean glow that made the shadows in the room dance.

Mei, who had tensed for a scream of pain, let out a small gasp of surprise instead. The pain that she had been bracing for never came. In fact, the deep, throbbing ache that had been her constant companion for hours simply... vanished.

The blue light faded. Bruce removed his hand.

Lin stared, her mouth agape. The crude splint had fallen away, its bindings loosened. Beneath it, Mei's arm was perfect. There was no swelling. No bruising. Not even a mark to show where the break had been. The girl cautiously flexed her fingers, then her wrist, her eyes growing wider with every movement.

"It... it doesn't hurt anymore," Mei whispered in pure, unadulterated awe. She looked at Bruce as if he were a god.

Bruce rose to his full height, a look of profound, weary satisfaction on his face. He had stared into the face of a compound fracture and emerged victorious. He turned his back on the patient, his work once again complete.

"See?" he said, his voice a testament to his own brilliance. "Professional."

He took one confident, heroic step away from the cot.

He took a second, slightly more wobbly step.

And, as if bound by some cosmic law of slapstick, he pitched forward and face-planted onto the cold stone floor for the third time in a single day.

As the darkness swirled around him, a triumphant final thought echoed in his mind, accompanied by the familiar, shimmering blue text.

`[MAJOR COMBAT ACTION SUCCESSFUL: BROKEN BONE HEALED]` `SKILL 'LESSER MINOR HEAL' HAS GAINED EXPERIENCE.` `WISDOM STAT INCREASED BY 1.` `CHARISMA STAT INCREASED BY 1.`

*Two for two...* he thought, a delirious, unconscious grin on his face.
*I'm on a roll...*

The return trip to consciousness was becoming a familiar journey for Bruce. This time, the fog cleared faster. It was less like swimming up from the depths of the ocean and more like surfacing from a pleasantly deep nap. Four hours. A new personal best.

He sat up, swinging his legs over the side of the cot. He felt a thrum of energy, the satisfying hum of a full mana bar. He looked around and saw Lin sitting in a chair beside his cot, her head in her hands. She looked up as he moved.

"How is she?" Bruce asked, his voice clear and immediate. His first thought was of his patient.

Lin managed a tired smile. "She's fine, Bruce. She's just fine. She's in the cafeteria right now, showing everyone her 'magic arm' and telling them all about the benevolent Goddess GreySkull."

A look of deep satisfaction settled on Bruce's face. "Good. Mission accomplished." He started to stand up.

Lin shot out of her chair, placing a hand on his chest. "No! Whoa, hold on. You've been unconscious for four hours. You have to rest. Please?" The last word was a desperate plea. "For me? Just... rest?"

Bruce looked at her hand on his chest, then into her worried eyes. He sighed, a deep, world-weary sound, and gently removed her hand.

"Rest?" he scoffed, as if she'd suggested something truly offensive. "Lin, rest is a luxury we cannot afford. Rest is the enemy of progress, the cousin of defeat. While I rest, injustice flourishes! While I sleep, bones remain unset!"

He stood up, this time with a solid stability that hadn't been there before. He began to pace in the small space beside the cot, a caged tiger ready for action.

"My patients need me!" he declared, gesturing to the quiet ward around them. "This city... this realm... it needs a hero to stand watch. It needs a guardian who does not tire, who does not falter!"

He stopped pacing and looked at his hands, flexing them as if feeling the raw power coursing through his veins.

"The power is flowing through me," he said, his voice a low, intense whisper. "I am at full strength... for at least... the next few minutes."

Lin barely had time to process his speech before he was in motion. But he didn't just walk out of the room. With a smooth, inexplicable grace, Bruce Leeroy TheMagnificent turned and began to moonwalk backward through the doorway, his feet gliding effortlessly over the stone floor.

He slid into the next room, a slightly larger area with a half-dozen cots, each occupied by a child with a minor but miserable ailment. He spun around, his arms outstretched in a grand V.

"I heard there were some kids in here that could use a little..." he paused, wiggling his eyebrows, "...Midas Touch!"

He surveyed the room, his eyes twinkling. The children, who had been lying in bored, listless silence, all sat up, their eyes wide with a mixture of confusion and fascination.

"I am..." he declared, his voice dropping to a dramatic whisper before rising to a crescendo, "...BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent!"

To punctuate this, he planted his hands on the floor and kicked his legs up into a perfect, unwavering handstand. He held it for three full seconds before snapping his legs down and popping back to his feet in a single, fluid motion.

"I am the Purveyor of Promise!" he continued, striding between the cots. "The Ambassador of Awesome! The Sultan of Salve!"

He came to a stop in the very center of the room, clapping his hands together with a loud crack. A broad, benevolent, and slightly unhinged smile spread across his face, a complete and sudden shift in persona.

"Now..." he said, his voice now a jolly, booming baritone. "Who has been a really good boy or girl this year? Because Santa Claus has come... to town!"

He let out a hearty "Ho ho ho!" that bounced off the walls, leaving the children in stunned, captivated silence and Lin, standing in the doorway, wondering if it was too late to ask for a reassignment to the Dragon Fodder division.

Bruce's "Santa" persona zeroed in on the first patient. A young boy of about eight sat holding a damp cloth to his forehead, a thin line of dried blood tracing a path down his temple. Bruce strode over, his hands clasped behind his back in a gesture of magnanimous authority.

"You there!" he boomed, pointing a finger. "I see a nasty gash on your forehead. Didn't your mommy teach you to watch where you are walking?"

He stopped abruptly, his body snapping into a classic martial arts stance, his hands held like claws. His face became a mask of intense focus. Then, in a surprisingly accurate, gravelly impersonation, he whispered, "*Boards... don't hit back.*"

Before anyone could process the bizarre non-sequitur, he dropped to the floor. In a move that defied all medical and military decorum, he began to perform a perfect, undulating "Worm" dance move, his body rippling across the stone floor until he was right beside the boy's cot.

He sprang up from the floor in a single, fluid motion, his grin never faltering. With no pause for ceremony, he placed his hand gently on the boy's head.

"By the Power Invested in me by Our Goddess, Lady GreySkull," he intoned with the solemnity of a high priest, "I pronounce you... HEALED!"

A blue flash, brighter and more confident than before, pulsed from his hand. The gash on the boy's forehead vanished, the skin knitting together, leaving no trace it had ever been there.

The energy drain hit Bruce like a physical blow. He staggered backward, his knees buckling. For a terrifying second, it looked like he was going down for a fourth face-plant. But this time, he caught himself. He wavered on his feet, his body swaying, but with a supreme effort of will, he remained standing. He was breathing heavily, but he was upright.

He looked at his hands, a slightly disappointed look on his face as he mentally checked his internal status.

"Hmm... no bonus points that time," he muttered to himself, a flicker of the gamer peeking through the heroic facade.

He quickly shook off the disappointment, his dazzling smile snapping back into place as he addressed his small, captivated audience.

"No worries!" he announced, striking a final, triumphant pose. "I'll be here all week!"

The sight of Bruce swaying on his feet, clearly running on fumes but still posturing for the children, was the final straw for Lin. Her role as a timid guide was over; she was now a full-time wrangler.

She marched over and grabbed his arm, her grip surprisingly firm. "Okay, BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent," she said, her voice a low but unyielding command. "It's after midnight, and these kids need to sleep!"

She physically turned him towards the doorway, her hand still clamped on his bicep to prevent any further outbreaks of performance art.

"Now let's get you fed, and a bit of honey water to restore your energy, and then you need to get to sleep as well!"

Bruce looked down at her, a flicker of protest in his eyes, but it was quickly extinguished by the profound, bone-deep weariness that was finally setting in. He had healed three children, leveled up twice, and face-planted twice. It had been a full day.

"Fine," he conceded with a dramatic sigh, his shoulders slumping slightly. "I am a bit tired."

But he couldn't just leave. An audience required a proper exit. He stopped at the doorway and turned back to face the children, who were all watching him with wide, sleepy eyes, clutching their blankets. He straightened up, his posture shifting, his face becoming a stoic, emotionless mask.

Then, in a deep, guttural, and flawlessly accurate Austrian accent he had mastered over years of dedicated practice, he delivered the sacred words.

"I'll be baaack."

With that final, solemn promise hanging in the air, he allowed the very tired "pretty lady" to lead him away into the quiet, torch-lit corridors of the Hub.

### Interlude: The Anomaly in Sector 7G

A sharp, dissonant chime echoed through the grey, data-stream-lined corridor of the Bureau of Reincarnation. It was an alert tone reserved for high-level system paradoxes, the kind of sound that made monitors spill their lukewarm cosmic coffee.

In Cubicle 7G, Zorp was just about to log off for his designated sleep cycle when the chime blared directly in his cerebral cortex. A Supervisor---a tall, crystalline being with four unblinking eyes and a posture of stern efficiency---materialized at the edge of his cubicle.

"Zorp," Supervisor K'tharr's voice was the sound of cracking ice. "My console is showing a string of Level-Omega anomalies originating from your sector. All tied to a single soul unit."

Zorp felt his three stomachs clench. "Anomalies, Supervisor? Everything on my end has been processed according to standard parameters."

Supervisor K'tharr's upper two eyes narrowed. "Don't play coy with me, Zorp. The flags are undeniable. Unsanctioned Designation Change. Spontaneous Skill Generation outside of class parameters. And multiple instances of Non-Standard Stat Progression. The algorithm is having a meltdown."

She leaned closer, her four eyes focusing on him with unnerving intensity.

"So, what seems to be the problem? Is that the 'bullet catcher'?"

Zorp flinched. She remembered. "Subject 734, yes," he said, quickly pulling up the file. A holographic window appeared, already flashing with a dozen bright red warning labels. "I assigned him to a low-impact, non-essential role as per the 'Cosmic Edge Case' protocol."

"Low impact?" K'tharr scoffed. "Zorp, in the last ten hours, this 'low impact' soul has forced The System to invent a new, non-existent healing spell, awarded himself two points of Wisdom for draining his own mana pool to unconsciousness, and..." she paused, tapping her crystalline chin as a new alert popped up on the file. "And... he just gained a point of Charisma. Tell me, Zorp. What heroic, charismatic act did he perform to earn this?"

Zorp zoomed in on the data log, his face a mask of weary disbelief. He watched a quick, data-driven playback: Subject 734 heals a child, stands up, makes a confident declaration, and immediately face-plants on the floor.

"He... healed a child with a broken bone, Supervisor," Zorp reported dutifully.

"And the Charisma point was awarded for the healing?"

Zorp scanned the log again, his shoulders slumping. "No, Supervisor. According to the emotional resonance metrics, the Charisma point was awarded for the face-plant. The children found it...'endearingly pathetic'."

Supervisor K'tharr was silent for a long, terrifying moment. Her four eyes blinked, once, in perfect, unnerving unison. The System, the great, logical, all-knowing machine of reincarnation, had just awarded a stat point based on the comedic value of slapstick.

"This soul isn't just an edge case," she finally said, her voice dangerously quiet. "It's a bug. A virus in the code of reality."

She reached out a sharp, crystalline finger and tapped a single command on Zorp's console. The file for BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent was suddenly encased in a shimmering, pulsating gold border.

`FILE STATUS: SPECIAL OBSERVATION. LEVEL: MAXIMUM.`

"This file is now your sole priority, Zorp," Supervisor K'tharr stated, her voice leaving no room for argument. "You will monitor Subject 734's every action. You will log every anomaly. And you will find out if he is a bug to be patched, or something far, far worse."

She vanished as quickly as she appeared, leaving Zorp alone in his cubicle with the blinking, golden file of the man who was, single-handedly, breaking the universe one skinned knee at a time. Zorp sighed and transmatted a fresh, extra-large can of Cosmic Cola to his desk. It was going to be a long millennium.
