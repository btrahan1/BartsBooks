# Chapter 27: The Magnificent Problem

The office of Master Elara, Head of the Healing Hand Sect's infirmary, was a place of serene, scholarly order. Scrolls of anatomical charts lined the walls, the air smelled of old parchment and calming incense, and a single, perfectly pruned bonsai tree sat in the center of a polished mahogany table.

Today, the serenity was a lie.

Master Elara sat at the head of the table, his fingers steepled, his brow furrowed in a look of profound consternation. Across from him sat Matron Hwei, the infirmary's chief administrator, a stern woman whose entire life was dedicated to rules, schedules, and the proper filing of requisitions. A very tired, very nervous Acolyte Lin stood before them, shifting her weight from foot to foot.

"So let me summarize," Matron Hwei said, her voice as crisp and dry as old paper. "A single, undocumented soul arrives this morning. In the space of one day, he has introduced a nonexistent goddess into the pantheon of the children's ward, performed three unsanctioned healing procedures via percussive unconsciousness, and has, by your account, Acolyte Lin, face-planted no less than three times."

"Twice, Matron," Lin corrected meekly. "The third time he... he wavered."

Matron Hwei shot her a withering glare. "The point stands. He is a chaotic, undisciplined, and frankly, insane presence in a place of order and healing. We should cast him out."

Master Elara let out a long, slow sigh. "I wish it were that simple, Matron. I made a tour of the children's ward an hour ago. It was... transformed. They aren't crying. They're telling stories. They're practicing 'The Wink of the Magnificent' on each other. One of them asked me when the next 'Sermon of Santa Claus' would be."

He looked at Hwei, his eyes heavy. "Well, we can't fire him. The kids would riot."

"This is a hospital, not a festival!" Hwei protested. "We can't make policy based on the whims of children!"

"We also can't ignore results," Elara countered, his voice firm. "Lin, the girl with the broken arm. I examined her myself. It was a clean break. The bone is not just set; it is fully mended. There is no trace of the injury. I have never seen a healing so total, so... absolute. His methods are madness, but the magic is real."

A long silence filled the room. Matron Hwei tapped a long, manicured finger on the table.

"Fine," she conceded. "We cannot remove him. But his current title is an embarrassment. 'Assistant to the Assistant.' He is the laughingstock of the acolytes."

"I agree," said Elara. "He can't be an assistant to an assistant anymore. He has proven he can heal. Therefore, it is only logical. Let's promote him to Assistant."

Hwei's eyebrows shot up. "Promote the maniac? After one day?"

Elara turned his gaze to Lin. "Acolyte, you performed a final inspection, did you not? What is his current Wisdom attribute?"

Lin swallowed hard. "It is... nine, Master."

The news landed like a stone in a quiet pond. "Nine?" Hwei whispered, her administrative composure finally cracking. "He has raised his Wisdom by two points in a single day?"

"Indeed," Elara confirmed. "Unprecedented. Unheard of. But undeniable. A Wisdom of nine is more than sufficient for a junior acolyte's duties. I guess we could do that."

He looked from Hwei to Lin, his decision made. "It is settled. We will promote him. It gives him a modicum of legitimacy and places him directly in the chain of command. My chain of command."

He then fixed his gaze on Lin, a glimmer of pity in his old eyes.

"Also," he said, his voice leaving no room for negotiation, "let's permanently assign Lin as his 'guide.' They seem to get along well enough. She will be responsible for his training, his duties, and... his containment."

Lin felt her stomach drop. It was official. This was her life now.

"Go and inform Assistant TheMagnificent of his promotion," Elara commanded, a deep weariness in his voice. "And Lin? Try to ensure he understands that even 'Assistants' are expected to remain conscious for a majority of their shift."
