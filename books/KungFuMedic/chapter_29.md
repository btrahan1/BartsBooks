# Chapter 29: The Magnificent's First Day

The next morning, armed with a new sense of purpose and a very official-looking (and mostly empty) clipboard, Lin marched toward the children's ward. Her mission: to instill a modicum of discipline in her new charge. She would outline his duties, explain the proper procedures for requesting healing supplies, and set a firm schedule. Today would be the first day of an orderly, productive new reality.

The reality she walked into involved a man in a makeshift Santa Claus costume.

"Ho ho ho! Merry Christmas!"

BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent stood in the center of the room, a vision of baffling, festive cheer. He was wearing a red uniform that looked suspiciously like a healer's blanket that had been crudely cut and re-stitched. A pillow was clearly stuffed inside, giving him a jolly paunch. A silly, conical hat, also made of red fabric, sat askew on his head, and a ridiculously fake beard made of what looked like torn linen strips was tied around his ears.

*Where did he get that uniform?* Lin thought, her mind grinding to a
halt. *We don't even have red uniforms!*

"I come bearing gifts!" Bruce boomed, reaching into a burlap sack at his feet. He began moving from cot to cot, handing out... origami?

He presented a small paper crane to Mei, who squealed with delight. He gave a lopsided frog to the boy whose forehead he had healed. Each child received a small, meticulously folded paper animal.

*When did he make those?* Lin wondered, her plans for an orderly day
dissolving into smoke. *He must have been up all night.*

He moved with a boundless, joyful energy, his booming laugh echoing in the ward. The children, who were usually quiet and sullen in the mornings, were all sitting up, their faces bright with excitement. The entire atmosphere of the room had been transformed from one of sickness to one of pure, unadulterated holiday cheer.

Lin stood in the doorway, clipboard hanging limply at her side, utterly defeated before the day had even begun. Her new charge, the official Assistant Field Medic, had decided that his primary duty was not medicine, but morale. And, infuriatingly, he was incredibly good at it.

That first official day as "Assistant Field Medic" set a pattern that was as bizarre as it was effective. The Santa Claus costume was eventually discarded (mostly because Matron Hwei saw it and nearly had an aneurysm), but the core routine remained.

Bruce would make his rounds, a whirlwind of goofy faces, bad impersonations, and terrible jokes. He'd find a child with a cut, a bruise, or a nasty cough. He would then strike a pose, yell an incantation pulled from the depths of his 80s-addled brain, and unleash a flash of blue light.

The first few times, the energy drain was severe. He'd heal, waver, and immediately stumble over to the bench where Lin sat waiting with a waterskin of honey-sweetened water, a remedy she'd discovered was the quickest way to restore his energy. He'd gulp it down, rest for ten minutes while telling the kids a wildly inaccurate story about his "adventures," and then get back up to do it all over again.

Heal. Waver. Drink honey water. Tell a story. Rinse. Repeat.

He learned to pace himself, a concept so foreign to his nature that it was a miracle he grasped it at all. He started with the most serious cases and worked his way down. With each healing, he grew a little more stable, the wavering became less pronounced. He was a machine fueled by sugar water and an unshakable desire to help, a bizarre, beautiful engine of compassion and chaos.

By the end of the day, as the sun set outside, only one patient was left---a little girl with a persistent fever. He walked over to her cot, his movements now slow and deliberate, the day's efforts clearly having taken their toll.

He placed a hand on her forehead. "By the final, flickering flame of justice," he whispered, his voice hoarse, "be... healed."

A soft, gentle pulse of blue light washed over the girl. Her shivering stopped, and her breathing evened out into a peaceful sleep.

And with that, Bruce's own energy reserves finally hit zero. His eyes rolled back, and he collapsed, not in a dramatic face-plant this time, but in a slow, boneless crumple, landing in a heap on the floor.

As he lay there, a final, familiar blue screen flickered in his unconscious mind.

`[DAILY QUEST COMPLETE: HEAL THE CHILDREN'S WARD]` `SKILL 'LESSER MINOR HEAL' HAS GAINED SIGNIFICANT EXPERIENCE.` `INTELLIGENCE STAT INCREASED BY 1.` `CHARISMA STAT INCREASED BY 1.`

"Totally worth it," he mumbled into the stone floor, the words barely audible. A second later, a loud, rattling snore filled the now-quiet room. He hadn't gained any Wisdom, but he had learned something new: the value of pacing himself. And every child in the ward was sleeping soundly, dreaming of a strange, magnificent man who fought sickness with bad jokes and the power of a goddess named GreySkull.
