# Chapter 31: The Library-Dojo

The Hall of Tomes was the polar opposite of the bustling Hub or the chaotic infirmary. It was a sanctuary of silence, a cavernous, circular room lined from floor to ceiling with thousands of scrolls and leather-bound books. The air smelled of dust and ancient knowledge. A few elderly scholars sat at massive wooden tables, so still they could have been mistaken for statues.

Lin put a finger to her lips. "We have to be very quiet in here, Bruce," she whispered, her voice barely a breath.

Bruce nodded, his face a mask of intense seriousness. He began walking on the tips of his toes, each step a comically exaggerated, silent placement of the foot, as if he were a spy infiltrating an enemy fortress.

Lin led him to a secluded alcove dedicated to medicinal texts. She carefully pulled a large, heavy scroll from a cubbyhole and unrolled it on a small, empty table. It was a beautifully detailed anatomical chart of the human body, complete with renderings of the skeletal structure, major organs, and the intricate, glowing lines of the meridian system.

Bruce leaned over the table, his eyes wide with fascination. "Whoa. Cool."

"Okay," Lin whispered, pointing a slender finger at the drawing of the skeleton. "This is the foundation. The skeletal system. It gives the body its structure."

Bruce squinted at the chart, his brow furrowed in deep thought. "Right. So... that's the battle cage."

Lin blinked. "The... what?"

"The battle cage," Bruce repeated, tapping the ribcage on the chart with his finger. "It protects the power core." He then pointed to the heart.

"That's... not what it's called," Lin said, trying to keep her voice down. "It's the ribcage, and that's the heart. It pumps blood."

"Right, the fuel line for the battle cage," Bruce said, nodding sagely, completely unconcerned with her correction. He seemed genuinely proud of himself for figuring it out. He then leaned in closer, a look of revelation dawning on his face.

"Okay, Sensei," he whispered, his enthusiasm bubbling over. "I'm not much of a reader, so... let's start with books with pictures. We look at some body parts, and you 'splain them to me, k?"

And 'splain she did.

For the next two hours, Lin patiently guided Bruce through the basics of the human form. She pointed to bones, traced the paths of major muscles, and outlined the locations of vital organs. And, much to her absolute amazement, Bruce listened. He didn't interrupt with a bad joke, he didn't bust out into a random set of pushups, he didn't try to give the anatomical drawing a cool nickname. He was a model student, his eyes locked on the scroll, his brow furrowed in a look of intense concentration. She had no idea if he was actually learning or retaining any of it, but he was, for the first time since he'd arrived, completely and utterly focused.

If she only knew what was going through Bruce's mind.

Lin pointed a delicate finger at the two parallel bones in the forearm. "Okay, this one is the Ulna, and this one is the Radius. They are crucial for the arm's rotation and strength."

Bruce nodded slowly, his face a mask of scholarly seriousness.

*Okay,* the voice in his head said, a voice that sounded suspiciously
like a movie trailer narrator. *Ulna. Radius. Too many syllables. We'll call that* '*Small Arm-Bone*' *and* '*Big Arm-Bone.*' *Simple. Tactical. Next.*

Lin moved her finger up to the large, powerful bone in the thigh. "This is the Femur. It is the strongest bone in the body."

*The Kicking Piston,* Bruce's mind supplied instantly. *Target for
immobilization.*

On and on it went. As Lin provided the proper, medically accurate terminology, Bruce was busy categorizing everything in his mind... the BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent way.

The Lungs became the "Stamina Tanks." The Stomach was the "Fuel Processor." The Spine, the central column of the nervous system, was the "Main Power Conduit." And the Brain, the seat of all thought and reason, was, of course, "The Cockpit."

From the outside, Lin saw a student diligently absorbing knowledge. On the inside, Bruce was constructing a flawless, action-hero schematic of the human body. He wasn't learning medicine; he was learning a targeting system, a tactical map for his new "healing powers." When his first lesson in the library-dojo was over, both student and sensei felt a profound sense of accomplishment, though for entirely different reasons.

They had spent another hour on the major muscle groups (or, in Bruce's mind, the "Punching Cords," "Kicking Cables," and "Lifting Levers") when his finger darted out and tapped a different part of the scroll. It was a secondary diagram, an overlay showing a complex web of thin, glowing lines that crisscrossed the entire body, intersecting at various bright points.

"Hey, wait, what are those?" he asked, his voice losing its goofy edge and gaining a note of genuine, focused curiosity. He was pointing to the Meridians. "Those look like... some sort of magic lines?"

Lin looked up, a little startled. She was so used to pointing things out for him that she was surprised he had noticed such a subtle, complex system on his own. A flicker of pride, the kind a teacher feels when a student asks a truly insightful question, sparked within her.

"You're not wrong," she whispered, a small, genuine smile on her face. "They *are* a kind of magic line. We call them Meridians."

She leaned over the scroll, her tone shifting from that of a basic anatomy instructor to one of reverence. "Think of the body as a great big field. The bones are the rocks, the muscles are the soil. The Meridians... they are the rivers. They carry the body's essential energy---we call it Qi---to every part of the field to nourish it."

Bruce stared at the glowing lines, completely captivated.

"When a person gets sick or injured," Lin continued, tracing a major meridian with her finger, "it's like a dam has been built in the river. A blockage. The Qi can't flow properly, and the part of the body downstream begins to wither. True healing isn't just about mending the bone or the skin; it's about breaking the dam and letting the river flow freely again."

The look on Bruce's face was one of pure, unadulterated revelation. It was the face of a man who had been playing a video game his entire life and had just discovered the button for the super-move. The disparate pieces of his new reality---the healing, the blue flashes, the passing out---all slammed together in his mind, forming a single, coherent, and incredibly cool picture.

"So..." he said slowly, his voice full of awe as he connected the dots. "The magic lines are the body's internal power grid. And the Qi... that's the juice."

He looked up from the scroll, his eyes locking with hers, burning with a new, terrifying intensity.

"So when I yell about GreySkull and blast someone," he asked, the final piece clicking into place, "what I'm *really* doing... is using my juice to jumpstart their power grid?"

Lin stared at him, her mouth slightly agape. It was the most crude, simplistic, and spiritually bankrupt summary of a sacred and ancient art she had ever heard.

And it was, to her absolute horror, completely and totally correct.

The connection he made was like a lightning strike to his brain. The vague, magical hand-waving of his "power" suddenly had a blueprint. A schematic. Rules. And if there were rules, he could learn them. And if he could learn them, he could become the best.

"This changes *everything*!" he whisper-shouted, his voice a cannonball of excitement in the quiet library. He shot up from his crouched position, his hands slapping the table with a loud crack that made a scholar three alcoves over jump.

He grabbed Lin by the shoulders, his eyes wide and wild, burning with the fire of a thousand suns. The goofy, lovable idiot was gone, replaced by a man possessed by a singular, obsessive purpose.

"Lin, quick! Tell me!" he said, the words tumbling out of him in a frantic, desperate rush. "How do they work? Do you poke them? Do you massage them? How many are there? Twelve? A hundred? Where do they go? Do they end at the fingers? Where did they come from? Were we born with them? Are they always the same size, or do they get bigger if you work out? And finally..."

He leaned in so close their noses were almost touching, his voice dropping to the most intense, important question he could possibly ask.

"...I have some, right?"

Lin was completely overwhelmed, pinned in place by his sudden, explosive onslaught of curiosity. She felt like a dam that had just been hit by a tidal wave. She had spent the last two days trying to get him to focus on a single concept for more than five seconds, and now she had accidentally unlocked an unstoppable torrent of questions she was completely unprepared to answer.

"Yes! Yes, of course you have them!" she finally stammered, trying to pry his hands off her shoulders. "Everyone has them! And slow down! One question at a time!"

But it was too late. She had shown the caveman how to make fire, and now all he wanted to do was burn down the forest to see how it worked.

The barrage of questions hung in the air, echoing in the silent library. Lin, still caught in his grip, was about to try and answer the first one when she saw his face change.

The frantic, wild-eyed energy vanished. The intense focus on her dissolved. Bruce's hands loosened their grip on her shoulders, falling limp to his sides. His eyes unfocused, his jaw went slightly slack, and he stared off into the middle distance, looking straight through a bookshelf as if it wasn't there. He was completely still, a statue in the midst of a profound, internal reboot.

He wasn't seeing the library anymore. In his mind's eye, the anatomical chart was a three-dimensional, holographic wireframe. He saw the "power grid" of the Meridians, saw the "juice" of the Qi flowing through it. He saw the blockages---the injuries---as busted circuits. And he saw his own power, the blue flash, not as a random blast, but as a targeted jolt of energy, a divine defibrillator for the soul's electrical system. It was the most complex, most beautiful, most awesome thing he had ever conceived.
