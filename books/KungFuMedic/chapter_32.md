# Chapter 32: The First Lesson

A familiar, shimmering blue screen flickered into existence in the quiet, processing space of his mind.

`[CRITICAL KNOWLEDGE ACQUIRED: QI & MERIDIAN THEORY (BASIC)]` `[A NEW PATH OF UNDERSTANDING HAS OPENED]` `INTELLIGENCE STAT INCREASED BY 1.` `CURRENT INTELLIGENCE: 9`

Bruce blinked once, slowly. The wireframe model in his mind vanished, and the dusty library snapped back into focus. He looked down at Lin, his eyes sharp and clear with a newfound, terrifying clarity.

"So," he said, his voice a low murmur of pure revelation. "It's like... spiritual acupuncture... with my fists."

Lin, still reeling from the "spiritual acupuncture with my fists" comment, decided the best course of action was to redirect. If he was this interested, she would use the momentum. She carefully rolled up the anatomical scroll, a silent admission that the practical lesson was over, and moved to a nearby bookshelf.

"Your... 'jumpstarting'... is only one path of healing," she said, trying to steer the conversation back to something resembling established doctrine. "There are two main schools of thought in the Healing Hand Sect. Two pillars that support all our work."

She pulled two thick, heavy tomes from the shelf. They were ancient, bound in cracked leather, the titles stamped in faded gold leaf. She placed them side-by-side on the table with a heavy thud.

Bruce leaned over, his newfound intellectual curiosity still burning brightly. He squinted at the titles, his lips moving silently as he painstakingly sounded out the archaic script.

"Okay..." he said slowly, pointing a thick finger at the first book. "This right here says... uhmmm... 'Med-i-ca-tion'?"

"Meditation," Lin corrected gently. "The path of inner harmony. It is the art of calming the mind and spirit, looking inward to cultivate and purify one's own Qi. It is slow, patient, and requires immense discipline. By understanding the self, one can heal the self."

Bruce nodded, his expression unreadable. He then slid his finger over to the second, slightly thicker volume.

"...And this one says... 'Cul-ti-va-tion'?"

"Cultivation," Lin affirmed, a little more pride in her voice now. "The path of outer strength. It is the art of absorbing Qi from the world around us---from the air, from the earth, from spiritual herbs and beasts. By drawing in the power of the world, one can temper the body, making it stronger, faster, and more resilient to injury."

Bruce stared at the two books, the two pillars of healing, the two paths to power. Meditation. Cultivation. The way of the mind and the way of the body. He looked from one to the other, a thoughtful, calculating look on his face. He was, for the first time, being presented with a choice. A choice of how to "level up."

A fire ignited in Bruce's eyes. He saw the two books not as texts to be studied, but as instruction manuals for his next upgrade. He looked at Lin, his face alight with a raw, desperate eagerness that was almost startling in its intensity.

"Teach me!" he pleaded, his voice a low, urgent whisper that was somehow louder than a shout. "Please!"

Lin was completely taken aback. Just a few hours ago, she'd had to trick him into coming to the library. Now he was begging her to teach him the most profound and difficult arts known to their sect. The sudden reversal gave her a kind of intellectual whiplash. But his plea was quickly followed by a pang of shame and inadequacy.

"Bruce... I..." she stammered, her gaze falling to the floor. "I can't. I'm just a junior acolyte. I'm a field medic, not a master. I can barely meditate myself, much less cultivate! That... that takes a lifetime of dedication. It's for the elders, the true masters."

The fire in Bruce's eyes dimmed, extinguished by her words. The hopeful, eager expression on his face collapsed into one of pure, crushing disappointment. He looked crestfallen, like a child who had just been told that his favorite hero wasn't real.

"Oh," was all he said.

The silence in the alcove was heavy. He had come to his Sensei for the secret of the universe, and his Sensei had just told him she didn't have it.

Then, just as quickly as the disappointment had come, it was replaced by something else: a familiar, stubborn, unshakeable resolve. He looked up from the books, his jaw set in a hard, determined line.

"Fine!" he declared, his voice ringing with defiance. "Then I will teach myself!"

And with that, his "tactical knowledge-enhancement session" was officially over. Before Lin could even respond, he took a step back, crouched, and launched himself into a perfect, soaring backflip, landing silently on the balls of his feet. He immediately dropped to the floor and banged out twenty lightning-fast pushups, a grunt of pure determination escaping his lips with each one.

He sprang back to his feet and, without a single look back, turned and ran out of the library, his footsteps echoing in the sacred silence. He left behind a collection of scattered scrolls, two very heavy books, and a completely flabbergasted Lin who could only stare after him, wondering what fresh chaos she had just unwittingly unleashed upon the world.
