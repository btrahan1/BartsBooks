# Chapter 34: The Fragile Bubble

The fragile, almost-sacred bubble of clumsy meditation was shattered by a voice sharp with indignation.

"What is the meaning of this! What are you doing?"

A young man strode into the courtyard, his chest puffed out with the self-importance of a new uniform. He was a martial artist lower inductee, barely older than Lin, but he carried himself as if he were a general. His grey training robes were immaculate, his hands clasped behind his back in a posture of stern authority.

He marched right up to the edge of the circle of children, his face a mask of contempt. "This ground is sacred! It is for the training of warriors, not for... for whatever this ridiculous noise is!"

The children flinched, their chanting faltering. The delicate energy of the moment dissipated like smoke.

But Bruce did not move. He did not open his eyes. He remained perfectly still in his cross-legged pose, the devil-horn mudras unwavering. The only thing that changed was the slow, deliberate twitching of his mouth, his lips forming silent words a half-beat ahead of the sound, a perfect, self-dubbed prelude to a confrontation.

Then, one of his eyes, his left eye, slowly creaked open, pinning the inductee with a gaze of pure, condescending power.

"Be gone, peasant," Bruce's voice was a low, dangerous rumble that cut through the courtyard's sudden silence. He didn't shout; he didn't have to. The words dripped with the weight of a thousand kung fu masters.

"You court death."

The inductee, who had clearly been expecting a meek apology, was completely unprepared for the response. He stood there, mouth agape, ready to deliver another scolding.

He never got the chance.

He flew backward into the air, tucked, and executed one perfect backflip, then a second, his body a blur of motion. He landed ten feet away from where he started, his descent silent, his body sinking into a low, three-point hero crouch, one hand touching the ground, his eyes burning with a righteous fire.

"You have interrupted the sacred chant of the tiny warriors!" he snarled, his voice a low, gravelly growl.

His mouth twitched silently for a moment, the prelude to a classic line.

"I am Itoya Manegro!" he bellowed, the completely fabricated name sounding both ridiculous and terrifyingly confident. "You have dishonored my school! PREPARE TO DIE!"

The lower inductee, who had likely never been in a real fight in his life and whose authority was built entirely on a freshly-issued uniform, took one look at the half-naked, wild-eyed man crouched before him like a predator. He saw the sheer, unadulterated madness in Bruce's eyes. He didn't respond. He didn't draw a weapon.

He let out a small, terrified squeak, turned, and fled the courtyard at a dead sprint, his dreams of being a stern disciplinarian shattered.

Bruce held his dramatic crouch for a long, satisfying moment, watching the inductee disappear. He then smoothly rose to his feet, the intimidating persona melting away as if it had never been there. He turned back to the wide-eyed children, a warm, gentle smile returning to his face.

"Now, children," he said, his voice calm and pleasant again as he walked back to his spot and sat down. "Where were we? Ah, yes. The main battery."
