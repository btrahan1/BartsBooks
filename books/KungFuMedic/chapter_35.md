# Chapter 35: The Magnificent Promotion

For a long, silent moment, Lin just stared.

The inductee was gone, a memory of fleeting, misplaced authority. The children were looking at Bruce, not with fear or confusion, but with pure, unadulterated adoration. He was their champion. He had protected their sacred chant.

Lin stared some more.

Every rule in her head, every lesson she had ever learned about discipline, respect, and the proper order of things, was screaming at her. A part of her that respected the centuries of tradition was horrified. This was chaos. This was sacrilege.

But another, newer part of her, a part that had seen him heal a broken arm with a catchphrase and stand up for forgotten children, was... impressed. Deeply impressed.

He wasn't just a fool. He was a force of nature. He didn't bend the rules; he acted as if they didn't apply to him at all. And in his wake, frightened children became brave, and self-important bullies ran away with their tails between their legs. He had taken a profound spiritual concept, wrapped it in a layer of absolute nonsense, and somehow, managed to keep the core of it pure: Find your strength. Protect your own.

A slow smile, the first truly genuine, unguarded smile she'd had all day, touched Lin's lips. The headache was gone. The exasperation was gone. All that was left was a sense of profound, liberating absurdity.

She took a breath. Then, with a quiet determination that went completely unnoticed by the circle, she walked over.

She found a small, empty spot between Mei and the boy with the newly-healed forehead. She sat down, tucked her legs into a cross-legged position, and straightened her back, mirroring Bruce's posture. She looked at his ridiculous devil-horn hand gesture, shook her head once, and then, with a sigh that let go of a lifetime of rigid propriety, she formed the silly gesture with her own hands.

She took a deep breath.

"Ahhhmmm..." she chanted, her clear voice joining the clumsy, enthusiastic chorus.

High above the courtyard, concealed by the shadows of a carved stone balcony, stood two figures. Their robes were of a finer cut and a deeper, more profound grey than those below. They watched the bizarre scene unfold, their presence completely unnoticed by the assembly of chanters.

One was Master Tenjin, the Head Trainer of the Sect's martial disciples. He was a man carved from granite and discipline, his face a roadmap of a hundred battles, his posture as unbending as the mountains themselves.

The other was the Sect Leader, a man known only as The First Elder. He was older, his long, white beard betraying his age, but his eyes were sharp and clear, missing nothing.

The Sect Leader spoke first, a low chuckle rumbling in his chest as the inductee fled the courtyard.

"Did you see that, Tenjin? The boy ran like he'd seen a ghost."

The Trainer, Master Tenjin, was not amused. His lips were a thin, hard line. "Good people are hard to find these days, First Elder. That boy had potential, but no spine. A flaw I shall enjoy correcting."

"Perhaps," the First Elder said, his gaze never leaving the circle below. His eyes were fixed on the strange, shirtless man who had so effortlessly routed one of Tenjin's students without throwing a single punch.

He stroked his long beard, a thoughtful, calculating look on his face.

"So, Tenjin," the Sect Leader mused, his voice a low, intrigued murmur. "What do you make of this... healer... who can intimidate a warrior with nothing but a ridiculous name and a shout?"

Master Tenjin's gaze remained fixed on the courtyard, his analytical mind processing the scene. "His stats are... unusual. The reports from the Hall of Rebirth were flagged for review. He is built like a warrior, albeit a low-level one, but his designation is an Assistant Field Medic, according to his soul slate."

"Interesting," the First Elder murmured, the word carrying a weight far beyond simple curiosity. He continued to watch Bruce, who was now leading his small flock in a round of particularly enthusiastic breathing exercises. "A warrior's build, a healer's title, and a charlatan's theatrics. He is a contradiction."

"He is an undisciplined fool who got lucky," Tenjin countered, his voice flat. "The inductee was weak. A true disciple would have broken him in half for such a display of disrespect."

"Perhaps," the First Elder conceded, a faint, unreadable smile playing on his lips. "Or perhaps there is more to him than meets the eye. Raw power without discipline is chaos, but chaos, properly channeled, can be a weapon."

He turned his gaze from the courtyard and looked at his Head Trainer, his eyes sharp and decisive.

"Find out more about him," the First Elder commanded. "His origins, his training---what little he seems to possess---everything. I want a full report by sunrise."

"As you wish, First Elder."

"And Tenjin," the Sect Leader added, his voice dropping slightly, a glint of steel in his tone. "Arrange... an 'incident.' A training mishap. Something plausible. I'd like to see how this magnificent healer handles himself with someone who doesn't run."

A slow, cruel smile crept onto Master Tenjin's lips for the first time that day. He gave a short, sharp bow.

"It would be my pleasure, First Elder."
