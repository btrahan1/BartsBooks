# Chapter 36: The Magnificent Incident

The next afternoon found Bruce in the same spot in the courtyard, leading another meditation session. The circle had grown. A few more curious children and even two shy, off-duty laundry workers had joined the outer rim of the circle, clumsily mimicking the "Ahhhmmm" chant. Lin sat with them, a look of serene resignation on her face.

The peace was broken by the crunch of boots on gravel. A new figure strode into the courtyard, moving with the deliberate, rolling gait of a brawler. He was a senior disciple, his grey robes tighter, his arms thick with muscle. A wooden training sword was slung across his back. He had a sneer permanently etched onto his face. He stopped a few feet from the circle, his shadow falling over Bruce.

"You there," the disciple's voice was a loud, arrogant bark. "BruceLeeroy! I am Jian. I come to trade pointers! A friendly match!" The word "friendly" was laced with undisguised venom.

The chanting stopped. The children looked up, startled and scared.

Lin's eyes snapped open, and she was on her feet in an instant. She recognized Jian; he was one of Master Tenjin's top students, a notorious bully known for his heavy hands and cruel sense of humor. This was no friendly match. This was an execution.

She opened her mouth to object, to tell Jian to leave them alone, but Bruce held up a single, calming hand, not even looking at her. He then looked at Jian, and a slow, easy smile spread across his face.

"Bruce," Lin said, her voice a low, urgent whisper. "Let's go. It's not worth it. He's trying to hurt you."

Bruce ignored her. He stood up slowly, deliberately, uncoiling from his seated position with a surprising grace. He turned to his small, frightened audience.

"Children," he said, his voice calm and warm, all traces of the brewing conflict gone. "That's all for today. I heard there are extra-sweet honey cookies in the Field Medic tent! Go on, now!"

A collective "Yay!" erupted from the children. Forgetting their fear in the face of a promised treat, they scrambled to their feet and ran off toward the infirmary, leaving the courtyard empty save for the three of them.

Only when the last child was out of sight did Bruce turn his full attention back to the swaggering man before him. The easy smile remained, but his eyes were sharp, calculating.

"I'm sorry," he said, his voice polite and dangerously calm. "I didn't catch your name?"

The disciple's sneer widened into a smirk of contempt. "The name is Jian. As in, 'sword.' The instrument I'm going to beat some respect into you with."

Bruce tilted his head, a look of exaggerated confusion on his face. He put a hand to his ear. "Oh, wait," he said, his voice full of mock sincerity. "Jane, you said? Sorry about that. I guess your mother was disappointed you weren't a girl?"

The air in the courtyard went from tense to ice-cold.

Lin let out a small, strangled gasp. Her blood ran cold. She had been trying to de-escalate, to save Bruce from a beating. And Bruce had just responded by pouring a barrel of oil onto a raging fire.

Jian's face, which had been pale with arrogance, turned a deep, blotchy red. The smirk vanished, replaced by a snarl that twisted his features into a mask of pure fury. His hands, which had been resting loosely at his sides, clenched into white-knuckled fists.

"You..." Jian hissed, the word a venomous whisper. "You are going to regret that."

Just as Jian took a half-step forward, ready to lunge, Bruce held up a hand. Not in a fighting stance, not in a gesture of surrender, but a simple, open-palmed motion that seemed to suck all the momentum out of the air.

"Trade pointers, you say?" Bruce asked, his voice now devoid of any mockery or theatricality. It was level, quiet, and unnervingly calm. "I am not familiar with that term in this context. Please... explain."

The effect was immediate and profound. Jian, who had been a coiled spring of rage, ready to unleash a beating, stopped dead. His anger was a physical force seeking a physical response, and Bruce had just denied him one. The request for clarification was so unexpected, so completely out of sync with the tone of the confrontation, that it left Jian momentarily baffled.

"Explain?" Jian stammered, his momentum broken. "It... it means we fight! I show you my skills, you show me yours!"

Lin just stared at Bruce, her heart hammering in her chest. This was not the man she knew. The goofy, lovable idiot who monologued about being a "Janitor of Jolts" was gone. The earnest, compassionate healer who made funny faces at children was gone. The manic, over-the-top showman who did the worm and moonwalked was gone.

The man standing before Jian was someone else entirely. His posture was relaxed but rooted, his eyes sharp and analytical. There was a cold, quiet stillness about him that she had never seen before. It wasn't the vacant stare of a fool; it was the focused calm of a predator. He was using his words not as jokes, but as weapons, dissecting Jian's aggression with a surgeon's precision.

For the first time since he'd arrived, BruceLeeroy The Magnificent was not playing a character. And it was absolutely terrifying.

Bruce nodded slowly, a look of serene understanding on his face, as if Jian had just explained a complex but fascinating philosophical concept.

"Ah, I see," he said, his voice still unnervingly calm. "We have a similar game where I'm from. A contest of skill and discipline."

He took a half-step forward, closing the distance slightly, his hands held loosely at his sides. "It's called... 'who can hit the softest.'"

Jian's red-faced fury morphed into pure, slack-jawed confusion. "What? Hit the... softest? That's the stupidest thing I've ever heard."

"On the contrary," Bruce countered smoothly. "Any brute can swing for the fences. Any fool can try to break a rock with their fist. But to possess great power, and to be able to deliver it with such control that it feels like a gentle breeze... that is the mark of a true master. The goal," he explained, his eyes locking onto Jian's, "is to see who can hit the other person as soft as they possibly can. It is a test of supreme control."

He gestured an open hand towards Jian, a welcoming, inviting motion.

"This will be our first skill test," Bruce declared, his voice leaving no room for argument. "As the challenger, you shall have the honor. You go first."

Lin stared, her mind struggling to keep up. He wasn't just de-escalating anymore. He was rewriting the rules of the entire confrontation. He had taken Jian's challenge of violence and twisted it into a bizarre, esoteric contest of discipline---a contest that, by its very nature, made aggression look clumsy and foolish. He was using Jian's own ego against him, backing him into a corner where refusing the "game" would look like an admission that he lacked control. It was the most brilliant, insane piece of psychological warfare she had ever witnessed.

Jian's chest puffed out with pride. He had been challenged in his strongest suit---control---and had, in his mind, flawlessly demonstrated his superiority.

"Fine," he sneered. "I am certain that I can hit softer than you. I am a master of control. See if you can hit softer than this!"

He strode forward, closing the distance until he stood directly in front of Bruce. His fist shot out, a blur of motion so fast the air hissed. It looked like a killing blow, a strike meant to cave in a man's sternum. But in the final, infinitesimal inch, his momentum vanished. All the force bled away into nothing. The knuckles of his fist came to rest on Bruce's chest with the gentle, almost imperceptible pressure of a falling feather.

He pulled his hand back with a flourish. "There," he said, his voice dripping with condescending triumph. "See if you can beat that."

Bruce smiled. It was not his goofy, lovable grin. It was a thin, flat, cold smile.

He settled into the deepest, most picture-perfect karate stance Lin had ever seen, a low, rooted horse stance that looked like it had been copied directly from a master's scroll. He took a long, slow breath in through his nose, his entire body seeming to swell with energy. He then breathed out, a short, sharp hiss of air escaping his lips. For a fleeting moment, a faint, almost invisible blue shimmer, the same color as his healing flashes, seemed to coalesce around his right fist.

He took one step forward and threw a single, perfect, straight punch aimed at Jian's center of mass. It was not a wild swing. It was a piston of controlled, disciplined force.

The moment his fist connected with Jian's chest, a sickening, wet *THUD* echoed across the silent courtyard.

It was not a loud crack. It was the dense, heavy sound of an immense amount of kinetic energy being transferred into a solid object.

Jian's eyes went wide with a look of pure, uncomprehending shock. His feet lifted off the ground. He didn't just fall; he was launched backward, flying through the air for a good ten feet before landing in a sprawling, boneless heap near the edge of the training circle. Unconscious.

A collective gasp went through the handful of onlookers who had been watching from the Hub's doorways. Lin stood frozen, her hand over her mouth, her mind completely blank with shock.

Bruce slowly retracted his fist, shaking it out as if he'd gotten a cramp. He stood up from his stance, the cold, focused expression melting away, replaced by a look of mild, detached disappointment. He looked down at his own fist, then at the unconscious form of Jian across the courtyard.

He let out a small sigh. "Oops," he stated calmly to the silent air. "You win."

He then turned, gave a brief, unreadable glance at the horrified Lin, and simply walked away, his job done.
