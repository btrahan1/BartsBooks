# Chapter 37: The First Lesson

High on the balcony, the two figures stood in stunned silence. Master Tenjin's jaw, for the first time in thirty years, was not clenched in a firm line, but hung slightly, disgracefully open.

The First Elder slowly stroked his beard, a single, dangerous glint of profound interest in his ancient eyes.

Down in the courtyard, Bruce walked past Lin without a word, his face a calm, unreadable mask. The goofy, pop-culture-spouting himbo she knew was nowhere to be seen. The compassionate, funny-face-making healer was gone. The cold, terrifyingly focused warrior who had just appeared was now also gone.

The person who walked past her was a complete and total mystery.

Lin just stared as he went by. She didn't call out his name. She didn't know what to say. Her mind was a whirlwind of confusion, fear, and something else... something she couldn't quite name.

All she knew was the sound of her own heart, a frantic, powerful rhythm in her ears.

Thump.

Thump.

Thump.

It was no longer just the thumping of fear or shock. It was the thumping of a world that had been turned completely upside down, and of a woman who was realizing the magnificent fool she'd been assigned to guide might just be the most dangerous, and fascinating, person she had ever met.

Dawn broke, cool and grey. Lin found Bruce not in the training yard, but exactly where he was supposed to be: the infirmary. He was not, however, performing his usual morning routine of chaotic morale-boosting. He was sitting quietly on a stool beside a cot, holding the frail, wrinkled hand of a very old woman who was struggling for each rattling breath.

The quiet was shattered by the sharp, authoritative rap of a staff on the stone floor. A tall, severe-looking senior disciple stood in the doorway, his robes immaculate, a ceremonial scroll held in one hand. It was Disciple Kai, one of the First Elder's personal attendants.

"Assistant Field Medic TheMagnificent," Kai announced, his voice ringing with officious importance, managing to make the name sound like an insult. "You have been summoned. The First Elder awaits you in the Chamber of the Inner Gate for your Test."

Bruce didn't even look up. "No."

The word was not defiant. It was not angry. It was a simple, flat statement of fact, delivered with the same tone one might use to state that the sky is blue.

Kai's posture stiffened. "No? What do you mean, 'no'?" he sputtered, taking a step into the room. "You have been summoned by the Sect Leader, you buffoon! You will come now!"

Bruce slowly raised his head, his gaze leaving the elderly woman's face for the first time. He looked at the furious disciple, and his expression was not one of fear or defiance, but of simple, profound annoyance, as if Kai were a fly buzzing in his ear.

"I'm busy," Bruce said, his voice low and even. "Go away." He turned his attention back to his patient.

"What?! Did you just..." Kai was so apoplectic he was lost for words. Lin, who had been organizing supplies in the corner, rushed forward, her face pale with terror.

"Bruce, you can't!" she hissed. "This is the First Elder! You have to go!"

Bruce ignored her. He looked back at the sputtering Disciple Kai, his eyes now hard as flint. "Listen carefully," he said, his voice dangerously calm. "This woman can't breathe. My job is to help her. If you want me to do this 'test thingee,' you will go back to your boss, and you will have a healer---a REAL healer, one of your masters---assigned to take my place. When she is being cared for, then I will come."

He then completely dismissed the attendant, turning his full attention back to the elderly woman. He gently propped another pillow behind her back.

"Now," he said softly to her, his voice full of a warmth that had been absent a moment before. "Go away. I'm busy."

Disciple Kai stood frozen in the doorway of the infirmary, his face a swirling vortex of fury, disbelief, and humiliation. He had been dismissed. Dismissed like a child. By an Assistant Field Medic.

He turned without another word, his back rigid, and stormed away, his staff striking the stone floor with furious, ringing cracks. He marched directly from the infirmary to the Chamber of the Inner Gate, a hallowed, high-ceilinged room where Master Tenjin and the First Elder were waiting.

Kai burst into the chamber, foregoing all ceremony. "First Elder! Master Tenjin! The healer... he refuses!"

Tenjin, who was inspecting a rack of ceremonial weapons, spun around. "Refuses? No one refuses the First Elder!"

"He said... he said 'no'," Kai reported, his voice tight with outrage. "He said he was 'busy' with a patient and told me to go away!"

"He said *what*?" Tenjin's voice was a low, dangerous growl. The temperature in the room seemed to drop several degrees.

"But that is not all, Master," Kai continued, his indignation boiling over. "He demanded... he demanded that you, First Elder, send a Master Healer to take his place! He said he would not come until his patient was being cared for by a 'real' healer!"

Tenjin's face went from anger to pure, murderous rage. "He *demanded* what?!?!" The words echoed in the stone chamber. "The arrogance! The insubordination! First Elder, allow me to drag him here myself. I will break every bone in his..."

"Enough, Tenjin."

The Sect Leader's voice was quiet, but it cut through the room with an authority that silenced Tenjin instantly. The First Elder had not moved. He stood, hands clasped behind his back, his expression unreadable. He had listened to Kai's entire report without a flicker of emotion.

For a long, heavy moment, he was silent. Then, he spoke, his words not directed at Tenjin or Kai, but to the room at large.

"So... the healer, who was promoted for healing, refuses a summons to a test of power... in order to continue healing."

He turned his head slowly, his ancient eyes locking onto the sputtering Disciple Kai.

"Go to the infirmary," the First Elder commanded, his voice calm and clear. "Tell Master Elara that he is to attend to the elderly woman in the west wing. Personally. Tell him it is by my direct order."

Kai just stared, confused. "But... the healer?"

The First Elder turned away from them both, his back now to the room.

"I will speak to the Temple Leader."

And with that, he turned and walked out of the Chamber, leaving a stunned Master Tenjin and a thoroughly bewildered attendant in his wake. He wasn't going to punish the healer for his defiance. He was going to grant his demand.
