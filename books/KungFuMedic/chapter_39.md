# Chapter 39: The Magnificent Awakening

The moment Master Sonara finished her initial treatment and declared the elderly woman stable, Bruce felt the crushing weight of a day's exhaustion hit him like a physical blow. He mumbled a thank you, stumbled to the nearest empty cot, and was snoring before his head hit the pillow.

He awoke hours later, not with a groggy slowness, but with the explosive energy of a supernova. The +1 to Wisdom had done more than just open a new path; it had supercharged his entire system. He felt... magnificent.

"All right, all right, all right!"

His voice boomed through the quiet infirmary. Lin, who had been quietly re-stocking bandages, jumped, dropping a whole armful. She looked up just in time to see Bruce launch himself off the cot. He didn't just get up; he sprang vertically into the air, tucked backward into a full gainer, and landed on his feet with the silent grace of a cat.

He immediately dropped to the floor, his body a blur of motion as he hammered out thirty perfect pushups in about fifteen seconds. He then sprang back to his feet, not even breathing heavily.

He saw Lin staring at him, her face a mask of pure, unadulterated shock. He winked.

"You. Lin. Sweetheart," he said, his voice a smooth, confident purr. "How you doin'?"

He sauntered over, a completely unearned and infuriatingly charming swagger in his step, and smiled broadly. The tired, defeated man of yesterday was gone, replaced by this... this pinnacle of over-caffeinated, pop-culture-fueled confidence.

"So," he said, leaning against a supply cabinet, crossing his arms over his chest. "Tell me what you know about this 'test thingee.' I never did all that well on tests in school. Especially multiple choice. Always hated the little bubbles."

Lin just stared.

For a full ten seconds, her brain simply refused to process the scene. The explosive gymnastics. The lightning-fast pushups. The swagger. The... the pickup line? It was too much, too fast. The sheer whiplash from the quiet, exhausted, humble man of yesterday to this supernova of pure, uncut Bruce was giving her vertigo.

And then a single, coherent, and utterly terrifying thought bubbled up from the depths of her subconscious.

*My BruceLeeroy is back.*

She froze. Wait... *My* BruceLeeroy? When did he become hers? When had her brain made that possessive, proprietary leap? A hot blush crept up her neck, and she shook her head, not once, but twice, as if to physically dislodge the dangerous thought.

"Don't... don't call me that," she stammered, finally finding her voice. She bent down and began hastily gathering the bandages she'd dropped, mostly to avoid looking at his infuriatingly charming smile. "And stop... doing that. You'll pull something."

She stood up, her arms full of linen rolls, her professional demeanor reasserting itself like a hastily rebuilt fortress wall.

"The Test of the Inner Gate," she said, her voice now all business. "I don't know much. It's an ancient trial, usually reserved for the most elite martial disciples who are seeking to become Inner Sect members. It's not a written test."

She looked at him, her expression turning serious. "They say it tests a person's Spirit, Will, and Power. It's supposed to be... incredibly difficult. People have been broken by it, Bruce. Mentally and physically. No one from the Healing Hand Sect has taken it in over a hundred years."

She clutched the bandages to her chest, her knuckles white. "Whatever you did yesterday... it got the First Elder's attention. He isn't testing you to see if you'll pass, Bruce. He's testing you to see what you're made of."

The gravity of Lin's warning, the cautionary tale of broken minds and a hundred years of precedent, flew right over Bruce's head like a gentle breeze. He latched onto the one phrase that resonated with his action-hero soul.

"What I'm made of?" he repeated, his voice dropping to a low, incredulous rumble. Then, it exploded into a full-throated, rhetorical roar. "What I'm MADE OF?!"

He pushed himself off the supply cabinet and began to pace, a caged tiger of pure, magnificent ego.

"I'll tell you what I'm made of, Lin!" he declared, pointing a finger at his own chest. "I'm made of midnight training sessions and the last-minute disarming of ticking time bombs! I'm made of pure, unadulterated awesome and the quiet, stoic nod of a job well done! I am the living embodiment of a rockin' 80s guitar solo and the triumphant final frame before the credits roll!"

He stopped pacing and struck a dramatic pose, one fist held high in the air. "I am forged from the fires of heroic sacrifice and tempered in the cool, refreshing waters of... of... justice!"

He held the pose, his face a mask of epic seriousness. Lin, who had been watching this verbal cyclone with a stern, professional expression, felt the corner of her mouth twitch. She fought it. She fought it with all her might, biting the inside of her lip.

She failed.

A small, suppressed snort escaped her, followed by an undeniable, blossoming smile. She tried to hide it behind her armful of bandages, but it was too late.

Bruce's heroic expression instantly melted away, replaced by a triumphant, "gotcha" grin. He pointed an accusatory finger at her.

"I saw that!" he declared, his voice full of glee. "You smiled! You think my ingredients are funny!"

And then he delivered it. The full, unadulterated, face-scrunching, eye-crinkling, magnificent wink.

Lin's smile broke free entirely, turning into a reluctant, exasperated, but genuine laugh. It was impossible. He was impossible. And her fortress of professionalism had just been completely and utterly demolished by a single, infuriating wink.

After her defenses had been so thoroughly breached, Lin's laughter subsided into a resigned sigh. The brief moment of levity passed, replaced by the heavy reality of the situation. She looked at him, her smile fading, her expression turning to one of genuine concern.

"Bruce," she said, her voice quiet and serious now. "The summons is for you alone. I can't go with you. You have to do this by yourself."

She took a step closer, instinctively reaching out as if to straighten his tunic before stopping herself. "Listen. The First Elder and Master Tenjin... they aren't like Jian. They aren't bullies you can trick. They're powerful, and they're dangerous. Please," her voice dropped to a near-whisper, "just be careful."

A warm, confident smile spread across Bruce's face. He gently took the armful of bandages from her and set them on a nearby table, freeing her hands.

"Lin," he said, his voice soft and reassuring. "Careful is my middle name."

He gave her a final, confident nod. Then, with his shoulders squared and a heroic spring in his step, he turned and began to saunter toward the infirmary exit with the swagger of a man walking to his own coronation.

He sauntered left.

He reached the wall, turned around with a flourish, and sauntered right.

He reached the other wall. He stopped. He turned back and looked at Lin, who was watching his bizarre, zigzagging exit with a look of utter bewilderment.

His confident, heroic expression was replaced by one of mild, sheepish confusion.

"Uhm..." he said, scratching the back of his head. "Where do I go again?"

Lin pointed toward the main exit of the infirmary. "The Chamber of the Inner Gate is on the highest level of the Hub, past the training yards. Just... follow the big ceremonial banners."
