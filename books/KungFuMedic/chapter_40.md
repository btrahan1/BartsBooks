# Chapter 40: The Inner Gate

Before Bruce could set off on another directionally-challenged saunter, a figure appeared in the doorway, blocking his path. It was a young acolyte, no older than sixteen, dressed in crisp, formal grey robes. He held a rolled-up scroll in his hands with a reverence usually reserved for holy relics. He looked nervous, but his voice, when he spoke, was clear and officious, a well-rehearsed proclamation.

"BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent!" the acolyte announced, his eyes fixed on a point just above Bruce's head. He unrolled the scroll with a snap. "By the authority of the First Elder, you have been summoned to present yourself for The Test of the Inner Gate! Please follow me!"

Bruce's face broke into a wide, relieved grin. He clapped his hands together once, a loud, decisive sound.

"About time!" he boomed, striding forward. He leaned in conspiratorially toward the startled acolyte, lowering his voice. "And hey," he whispered, "quick question. Do they serve lunch during this thing? Or is it a 'bring your own snacks' kind of deal? Because I am starving."

The young acolyte's jaw went slack. He stared at Bruce, his carefully practiced formality completely shattered. He had been trained to summon legendary warriors to a life-altering trial, not to answer questions about catering.

"I... uh... there is no... lunch," he stammered.

Bruce sighed dramatically, slinging a companionable arm around the flustered acolyte's shoulders. "No lunch? Big mistake. A warrior fights best on a full stomach. C'mon, kid, let's get this over with. The sooner we start, the sooner I can eat."

And with that, he guided the stunned summoner out of the infirmary, leaving Lin standing alone, a silent observer to the most absurd, and possibly the last, walk to destiny she would ever witness.

The Chamber of the Inner Gate was a place designed to inspire awe and humility. The ceiling soared a hundred feet into the air, lost in shadow. The walls were carved with the images of legendary masters and mythical beasts. A single, wide, circular platform dominated the center of the room, polished to a mirror shine.

At the far end of the chamber, seated on a simple, elevated stone throne, was the First Elder. He sat as still as the carved masters on the walls, his presence filling the vast space. Master Tenjin stood at his right hand, his arms crossed, his face a thundercloud of disapproval.

The young acolyte led Bruce to the edge of the central platform and, with a deep, terrified bow, scurried away, leaving Bruce alone in the immense, silent chamber.

Bruce did not seem intimidated. He did not seem awed. He looked around the cavernous room with the mild curiosity of a tourist. He then fixed his gaze on the figure on the throne. With a confident stride, he walked to the center of the platform, came to a stop, and gave a slight, almost casual nod.

"So," Bruce's voice boomed, completely undaunted by the solemnity of the room. "You must be the head honcho around here. The big cheese. The top dog." He puffed out his chest. "I am BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent."

He immediately launched into his well-rehearsed litany. "I am the Fist of the First Response! The Sultan of Splints! The Unflinching Annihilator of Ailments! They call me the..."

The First Elder raised a single, wrinkled hand. The gesture was small, almost imperceptible, but it carried an authority that cut through Bruce's monologue like a razor.

"That will do," the Elder's voice was a quiet rumble, yet it filled the entire chamber.

Bruce stopped, mid-title, looking genuinely disappointed, like an actor who'd been cut off in the middle of a great speech.

"Okay, then," he said, shrugging. "So. What's first on the agenda? Written exam? Physical challenge? Interpretive dance?"
