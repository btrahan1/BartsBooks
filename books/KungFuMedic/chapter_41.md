# Chapter 41: The Test of Will

The First Elder did not respond to Bruce's questions. Instead, he made a subtle gesture with his hand. From a slot in the center of the polished platform, a single, smooth pedestal rose silently. Atop it rested a crystalline sphere, about the size of a human head, that seemed to pulse with a faint, internal light. Connected to the sphere by a thin, silvery tube was a tall, narrow glass cylinder, marked with etched lines from bottom to top.

"First," the Elder's voice echoed in the chamber, "is the Test of Will." He gestured to the apparatus. "Simply place your hand on that sphere and hold it there for as long as you can."

He pointed to the glass cylinder. "Your Will will be measured as the liquid inside the tube rises. The color of the liquid indicates the strength of your Will; the height indicates its depth. When the pain becomes too much, simply remove your hand."

The implications were clear. This was a test of endurance. A trial of withstanding agony.

Bruce looked at the sphere, then at the tube, and a broad, confident grin spread across his face.

"No problemo, muchacho," he declared, rolling up the sleeve of his burlap tunic. He cracked his knuckles. "No pain, no gain! That's what I always say."

He strode over to the pedestal and, without a moment's hesitation, slapped his open palm firmly onto the surface of the crystal sphere.

"You know," he said cheerfully, looking back at the two silent figures on the throne, "I played a game just like this once at the county fair. It had a big hammer and a bell on a pole. The goal was to ring the bell. Couldn't quite get it to ding, but I got close! Drove the carny nuts."

He gave the sphere a confident pat with his other hand. "So, what's the high score on this thing?"

The moment Bruce's hand made full contact with the sphere, a jolt of energy shot up his arm. It wasn't a painful shock, not at first. It was a strange, vibrating hum, a tingling sensation that vibrated deep in his bones.

"Son of a..." he began, his cheerful expression faltering for a second. "...Wow. Okay. This kind of... tickles."

He watched as a thin, pale blue liquid began to seep into the bottom of the glass cylinder, slowly rising to the first mark.

"Hey, it's working!" he announced, a note of genuine surprise in his voice.

The tingling sensation intensified. It was no longer a tickle. It was a deep, insistent ache, like the burn of a muscle held under strain for too long. The hum in his bones became a low, grinding vibration. He gritted his teeth, a bead of sweat forming on his temple.

"Okay," he said, his voice a little strained now. "Maybe... maybe more than a tickle. Like when your foot falls asleep, but... it's my whole arm."

He looked at the cylinder. The liquid had risen a quarter of the way up. The color had deepened from a pale, sky blue to a solid, vibrant cerulean. It was the exact same color as his healing flashes.

A grin, strained but genuine, returned to his face.

"Oh, cool," he said through clenched teeth, his focus shifting from the growing discomfort to the visual display. "It's still going up. And it's light blue. That makes sense."

He glanced up at the First Elder. "Blue is my favorite color! Did you guys know that?"

The vibrant blue continued its slow, steady climb up the glass cylinder, darkening in shade with each new line it passed. The humming in Bruce's bones was a full-throated groan now, a deep, resonant vibration that seemed to be shaking him from the inside out. The ache in his arm was no longer a dull burn; it was a sharp, biting pain, like a thousand icy needles pricking him from his fingertips to his shoulder.

The liquid reached the halfway point on the cylinder. The color was now a deep, royal blue, almost sapphire. Its upward momentum, which had been steady, began to slow, crawling toward the next line with agonizing reluctance.

"Okay... now..." Bruce gritted out, his voice a low growl, his jaw muscles corded with tension. His entire body was trembling with the effort of keeping his hand pressed to the sphere. Sweat was now freely pouring down his face, his breathing coming in short, sharp hisses through his clenched teeth.

He squeezed his eyes shut, trying to find a place in his mind to hide from the pain. His thoughts, usually a chaotic whirlwind of movie quotes, became a desperate, scattered mess.

"We... get... snacks... after this... right?" he gasped, the question forced out by sheer, desperate hope.

The liquid inched past the halfway mark. The pain spiked again. It was no longer just in his arm. He could feel it spreading into his chest, a searing, white-hot agony that felt like it was trying to boil his blood.

His eyes snapped open, wide with a mixture of pain and genuine curiosity.

"Owww," he hissed, looking at the sphere as if it had personally offended him. "What's in this thing, acid?"

The royal blue column crawled. It fought for every millimeter, a visual representation of Bruce's struggle against a tide of pure, unadulterated agony. The sapphire deepened to a dark, midnight indigo, the color of a deep ocean trench. It passed the three-quarters mark on the cylinder, and then, with a final, shuddering effort, it stopped.

Bruce's body was rigid, a statue of pure, strained muscle. His knuckles were white, his teeth ground together so hard it was a wonder they didn't crack. A low, animalistic groan was the only sound he was making. He was no longer thinking about snacks or carnivals. He was in a place beyond thought, a raw, primal place of pure endurance.

For another sixty seconds, an eternity in the silent chamber, he held on. His hand remained pressed to the sphere not by conscious thought, but by some deep, stubborn, instinctual refusal to quit.

Then, his body gave out. His eyes, which had been squeezed shut, rolled back into his head. His body went limp, and he crumpled to the polished floor in a heap, his hand sliding off the sphere. He was unconscious before he hit the ground.

The moment his contact was broken, the deep indigo liquid in the cylinder vanished, leaving the apparatus as clear and empty as it was before.

Master Tenjin took a half-step forward, a look of grudging disbelief on his face. "He... he actually held on until he passed out," he said, his voice a low murmur. "He chose unconsciousness over willingly giving up. Hmm. Impressive."

The First Elder slowly rose from his throne. He glided to the edge of the platform and looked down at the unconscious healer, then at the now-empty cylinder.

"I would not have expected these readings," the Elder mused, his voice calm and analytical. "The strength of his Will is not exceptional; the indigo color is common among our more determined disciples. But the depth... to reach that high on a first attempt shows a reservoir of endurance that is rare indeed. He didn't get the 'high score,' as he put it, but it's in the top ten percent of all inductees we have ever tested."

He turned his gaze from Bruce to the two attendants who had scurried out from the shadows.

"When he wakes up," the First Elder commanded, "bring him to the dining hall. We will give him his 'snacks'... and explain the next part of the test."
