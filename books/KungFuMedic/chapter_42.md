# Chapter 42: The Test of Endurance

Bruce awoke to the smell of roasted meat and warm bread. He wasn't on the cold floor of the chamber; he was seated in a comfortable, high-backed chair in a private, well-appointed dining hall. A small table in front of him was laden with a platter of delicacies: glistening roasted fowl, fresh fruit, and a plate of golden, fried sticks of what looked like cheese.

Lin was there, standing nervously by the wall. The First Elder and Master Tenjin were seated across the table from him, watching him with unreadable expressions.

Bruce, never one to question a good meal, was already halfway through his first cheese stick. He took a large bite, chewed thoughtfully, and swallowed.

"Wow!" he announced to the room. "These cheese stick thingees are pretty good. Smells kind of funny, but I like 'em."

He gestured with the half-eaten stick at the First Elder. "Anyway, how'd I do, Boss-man? Did I pass?"

The First Elder did not answer immediately. He simply watched Bruce take another enthusiastic bite.

"You did not fail," the Elder finally said, his voice a low rumble.

"I'll take it!" Bruce said cheerfully. "So, what's next? Is it the physical part? Say it's pushups. I'm really good at pushups. Or maybe... competitive flexing?" He struck a quick bicep pose for emphasis.

"The second trial," Master Tenjin interjected, his voice a flat, hard slab of granite, "is the Test of Spirit."

"Spirit?" Bruce asked, his mouth full. He swallowed. "Okay. Sounds cool. What's it involve? Fighting a ghost? Arm-wrestling a demon? I'm game."

The First Elder raised a hand, silencing him. "The Test of Spirit is not a test of combat. It is a test of character. You will be presented with a scenario. A choice. Your response is the test." He leaned forward slightly, his ancient eyes locking onto Bruce's.

"We will see what you are truly made of, BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent. We will see if the Will you displayed is in service of a noble Spirit... or merely a stubborn one."

The First Elder's pronouncement, heavy with ancient gravity, hung in the air. The Test of Spirit. A test of character.

Bruce sat there, a half-eaten cheese stick in his hand, a look of deep, profound thought on his face. He nodded slowly, as if absorbing the weight and wisdom of the Elder's words. He looked from the Elder to Master Tenjin, his expression serious and contemplative.

Then, while the two masters were watching him, expecting a response, he leaned his chair back on two legs, turning his head slightly towards the wall where Lin was standing. He covered the side of his mouth with his free hand, creating a conspiratorial whisper-tunnel.

"Psst," he hissed, his voice a stage whisper that was clearly audible to everyone in the room. "Lin. Hey. Over here."

Lin, who had been trying her best to look like a piece of the architecture, froze, her eyes wide with panic.

"What is 'spirit'... exactly?" Bruce whispered, his brow furrowed in genuine, befuddled curiosity. "Like... a ghost? Or is it more like... team spirit? Like, 'Go Team Sect!' Rah rah rah?"

The First Elder's impassive mask cracked, a single, ancient eyebrow arching in sheer, unadulterated disbelief. Master Tenjin, who had been glaring daggers at Bruce, simply closed his eyes and let out a long, slow breath, as if purging his body of a great and terrible poison.

Lin just stared at Bruce, her face a perfect portrait of someone who was trying to decide whether to laugh, cry, or simply turn and run out of the room forever.

The First Elder, recovering from his moment of disbelief, decided to intervene before Lin was forced to try and explain a core tenet of their entire philosophical existence.

"Spirit," the Elder began, his voice surprisingly patient, as if explaining a concept to a very small, very loud child, "is a test of character. It is the core of who you are when all else is stripped away."

He gestured vaguely toward the far end of the hall. "You will walk the Path of Sorrows. It is a... constructed illusion. A waking dream. And in this dream, you will be given choices. The choices you make will be used to measure your Spirit."

He steepled his fingers, his gaze intense. "There are no 'right' answers. There is, in a cosmic sense, no such thing as a 'wrong' Spirit. However," he added, a glint of steel in his eyes, "there are certain qualities that we look for in this sect. Courage. Compassion. Sacrifice. Wisdom."

"Some challenges will be questions," the Elder continued. "Some will be more... physical. Some will be mental. The path will adapt to you. The only instruction is this: Just be... yourself."

The gravity of the instruction seemed to be lost on Bruce. His face, which had been a mask of confusion, lit up as if the Elder had just given him the easiest cheat code in the world.

"Be myself?" he boomed, slapping the table with his open palm, making the platters jump. "Well then! This will be a breeze!"

He sprang to his feet, tossing the rest of the cheese stick into his mouth. "There is only one BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent! Who else could I possibly be?"

He struck a heroic pose, one fist on his hip, chest puffed out, radiating an aura of absolute, unshakeable, and completely clueless self-confidence.

Master Tenjin just rubbed his temples, a gesture of a man who was beginning to understand the true nature of a migraine. The First Elder simply watched, a faint, unreadable smile touching the corners of his ancient lips. This, he thought, was going to be the most interesting Test of Spirit in a hundred years.

The First Elder gestured toward a simple, unadorned stone archway at the back of the dining hall. As Bruce approached, the space within the arch shimmered, the solid wall behind it dissolving into a swirling, grey mist.

"Your path begins now," the Elder's voice rumbled from behind him. "Be yourself."

"Right. Be myself. Piece of cake," Bruce muttered, giving a final, confident thumbs-up over his shoulder without looking back. He took a deep breath, puffed out his chest, and stepped through the portal.

The world vanished.
