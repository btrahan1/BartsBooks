# Chapter 43: The First Test

One moment he was in a warm, torch-lit hall. The next, he was... somewhere else. He stood on a simple, well-worn path of grey flagstones that stretched forward into a thick, featureless mist and backward into the same. There were no walls, no ceiling, no sun. Just the path beneath his feet and an endless, silent, grey void in all directions.

And it was cold.

Man, it was cold. The kind of biting, damp chill that sinks right into your bones. It was, Bruce thought with a shiver, a bit tit-nipply in here. He rubbed his bare arms, the thin burlap of his tunic offering no protection whatsoever.

Then, a voice spoke. It came from everywhere and nowhere at once, a deep, resonant, and utterly impersonal sound that seemed to vibrate in the air itself.

"Proceed down the path."

The voice paused, a heavy, silent beat.

"A word of warning. Do not stray from the path."

Bruce looked around, searching for the source of the voice, but there was nothing to see but the mist. He shrugged.

"Okay, okay, I get it," he said to the empty air. "Stay on the yellow brick road. Don't talk to strangers. Got it."

He rolled his neck, shook out his arms to get the blood flowing, and with a confident nod, took his first step into the unknown.

Bruce walked. The grey flagstone path unspooled before him, an endless, monotonous treadmill in a world of mist. The silence was absolute, broken only by the sound of his own footsteps. It was, he decided after about five minutes, super boring.

"Man, for a 'Path of Sorrows,' this is more like a 'Path of Extreme Lameness'," he muttered to himself. "I was expecting spikes, or fire, or at least some dramatic background music."

As if on cue, a new sound cut through the silence.

"Help me! Help me!"

The voice was thin, reedy, and filled with terror. It was the voice of a child. Bruce stopped dead in his tracks, his head snapping to the right.

There, about thirty feet off the path, the mist swirled and thinned, revealing a small clearing. In the center of the clearing stood a young girl, no older than Mei, her face pale with fear. And circling her, their movements low and predatory, their yellow eyes glowing in the grey light, were five massive, shadowy wolves.

The disembodied voice echoed in his memory. *Do not stray from the path.*

Bruce snorted, a sound of pure, contemptuous disbelief.

"Yeah, right," he scoffed.

A low, familiar, and heroic synth beat began to hum in his mind. He started humming along with it, his body unconsciously shifting into a low, ready stance.

"Dun, dun, dun... da da dun dun..."

He cracked his knuckles, a wide, predatory grin spreading across his face.

"HA!" he shouted, the sound exploding into the silence. He pointed a dramatic, heroic finger at the wolves.

"Have no fear, little citizen!" he roared, his voice a tidal wave of pure, magnificent confidence. "BruceLeeroy TheMagnificent is here!"

And, of course, without a moment's hesitation, he took a running leap off the safety of the path and plunged headlong into the misty unknown.

Bruce didn't just run toward the clearing; he launched himself. His leap carried him in a soaring arc, a perfect embodiment of a hero answering the call. He landed with a heavy, solid thud right in the middle of the circling pack, his body a living shield between the girl and the snarling wolves.

The wolves recoiled, startled by his sudden, explosive entrance. Bruce rose to his full height, a wild, joyful grin on his face. This was it. No tests. No questions. Just pure, simple, heroic action.

A groovy, instantly recognizable bassline kicked in inside his head.

"Everybody was..." he sang, his voice a booming, enthusiastic baritone, "...KUNG FU FIGHTING!"

"HA!"

He exploded into a whirlwind of motion. A spinning back kick, a flurry of knife-hand strikes, a jumping roundhouse. He was a tornado of classic, badly-dubbed martial arts moves. His form was impeccable. His speed was dazzling. His kiai shouts were fierce.

His accuracy, however, was a little less impressive.

For every three, or four, or maybe five flamboyant, incredibly cool-looking moves he threw, one would actually connect. A spinning kick that was meant for one wolf's head would sail harmlessly over its back, but the follow-through crescent kick would clip another wolf on the flank, sending it yelping. He'd unleash a furious, ten-punch combination, with nine of the punches hitting nothing but misty air, but the final, wild haymaker would catch a wolf square on the snout with a satisfying *thwack*.

He wasn't a master technician. He was a walking, talking, kicking barrage. He was winning not through precision, but through sheer, overwhelming, and incredibly inefficient enthusiasm.

Back in the Chamber of the Inner Gate, the central platform was no longer a polished mirror. It now showed a swirling, three-dimensional image of the misty clearing, a perfect, god's-eye view of the test in progress.

Master Tenjin, who had been watching with a critical, unforgiving eye, covered his face with his hand, his fingers pressing into his temples.

"And of course," he groaned, his voice a low rumble of pure exasperation, "the very first thing he does is leave the path. The one and only instruction he was given."

He watched Bruce's whirlwind of flailing limbs and occasional, accidental connections. "And his accuracy is atrocious. He fights like a drunken peacock. All flash, no substance."

The First Elder remained silent, his gaze fixed on the image, his expression unreadable.

Then, a new voice, soft but firm, spoke up from the side. It was Lin. She had been allowed to stay and observe, a rare privilege. She stepped forward, her hands clasped behind her back, and peered at the image on the platform.

"This is the Test of Spirit, is it not, Masters?" she asked, her voice respectful but unwavering. "I believe that qualifies as 'Courage,' yes? To ignore a warning for one's own safety in order to protect a child?"

Master Tenjin lowered his hand and shot her a glare that could have withered a lesser acolyte. "The boy is a fool. Courage without discipline is just recklessness."

"It is, indeed, courage," the Sect Leader responded, his voice calm, cutting off any further debate. He looked from the image to Lin, acknowledging her point. "However," he added, his gaze turning to stone, "following directions is also a character trait we admire."

Lin met his gaze, her own resolve hardening. "Even when children are in danger, First Elder?" she fired back, the question more of a challenge than an inquiry.

A heavy silence fell over the chamber. Master Tenjin looked at Lin as if she had just sprouted a second head. The First Elder's impassive mask did not change, but for a fraction of a second, a flicker of something---surprise? respect?---flashed in his ancient eyes.

With a final, satisfying punt that sent the last wolf yelping into the mist, the clearing was secured. Bruce stood amidst the swirling grey, breathing heavily, a triumphant grin on his face. The synth music in his head reached a crescendo and then faded out.

He turned to the little girl, who was looking up at him with wide, adoring eyes. "You okay, little one?"

She nodded, speechless.

"Good," Bruce said, scooping her up into his arms in a single, fluid motion. "Let's get you back to safety."

With a powerful leap, he launched himself from the misty clearing and landed with a solid thud back on the grey flagstones of the path.

The instant his feet touched the stone, the little girl in his arms dissolved into a cloud of grey mist and vanished.

"You stepped off the path," the disembodied voice boomed, the tone flat and accusatory.

Bruce, still breathing hard, straightened up and glared at the empty air around him.

"I did not!" he retorted, his voice ringing with the flawless logic of a pedantic child. "The instructions were 'do not stray from the path.' The path is made of these grey stones. 'Straying' implies wandering. I did not wander."

He began to pace on the narrow path, his hands gesturing to illustrate his airtight legal argument.

"I executed a controlled, targeted, tactical leap from this point on the path," he declared, pointing to his feet, "to that point over there." He pointed to the now-empty clearing. "It's solid ground there. Same ground as here, just without the convenient paving stones. Then, upon completion of my mission, I executed a second controlled, targeted, tactical leap back to the path. I transitioned. I did not 'stray.'"

He stopped, crossed his arms, and glared defiantly into the void. "Case closed."
