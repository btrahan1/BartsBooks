# Chapter 45: The Chamber

In the Chamber, the image of the now-empty path shimmered. Master Tenjin was rubbing his jaw, a strange, conflicted look on his face. He despised the healer's arrogance, but the answer... the answer was undeniably powerful. To reject the premise of the test itself was a move of profound, if frustrating, character.

Lin, however, was beaming. She couldn't help it.

"That's two for two on 'Compassion' and 'Sacrifice,' by my count," she said quietly, though her voice was filled with pride. "He refused to harm an innocent, and his first instinct was to sacrifice himself for the good of the group." She looked at the First Elder, a hint of a challenge in her eyes. "Those are qualities you look for, are they not?"

The First Elder didn't respond. He simply stroked his long, white beard, his gaze distant and thoughtful, his ancient mind processing the choices of this most unusual of healers.

As Bruce continued his walk, the single path before him split, forming a clear intersection. The mist swirled, and two new, horrifying scenes materialized.

To the left, a set of iron tracks appeared. On the tracks, a group of about thirty people were bound and helpless, completely unaware of their impending doom. In the distance, a massive, smoke-belching train was bearing down on them at an impossible speed.

To the right, a second set of tracks branched off. On this track, a smaller group of five people were similarly bound.

Right in front of Bruce, where the path met the tracks, a large, iron lever jutted out from the ground.

The logic of the trap settled into his mind with a sickening weight. He could do nothing, and the thirty would die. Or he could pull the lever, divert the train, and the five would die. It was a classic, horrible choice of sacrificing the few to save the many. He deduced the scenario with a surprising quickness, the gears in his head turning with a low, grinding groan of mental strain.

He looked at the lever. He looked at the thirty people. He looked at the five people. He looked at the oncoming train.

He did not touch the lever.

He did not hesitate.

With a primal roar, Bruce took a running start and leaped. It was a jump of impossible height and distance, a gravity-defying feat of pure, heroic desperation. He sailed through the air, over the tracks, and landed with a solid, metallic CLANG on the roof of the conductor's car, the train still speeding beneath him.

Without missing a beat, he flipped forward, tucked into a roll, and smashed through the front window of the conductor's cabin, landing in a shower of illusionary glass. The conductor, a shadowy, featureless figure, looked up in shock. Bruce ignored him, grabbed the massive brake lever on the console, and hauled it back with every ounce of his strength.

The train's wheels screamed, a deafening, metallic shriek that echoed in the misty void. Sparks flew from the tracks as the immense machine fought against its own momentum. The train shuddered, slowed, and with a final, groaning lurch, came to a dead stop, its front mere feet from the terrified group of thirty people.

Bruce stood up, dusted the imaginary glass off his tunic, and got right in the conductor's face.

"Watch where you're going!" he snarled.

He then turned, climbed back out through the shattered window onto the roof of the car, took two running steps, and leaped back to the path, landing perfectly at the intersection. The train, the tracks, and the people all dissolved back into the mist.

"Next!" he roared into the void, his heart still hammering with adrenaline.
