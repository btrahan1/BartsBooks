# Chapter 5

The brass bell on the door of Henderson's Hardware sang out. *Ding!*

Damn bell was too cheerful. It sounded like it was celebrating
something, and all I was doing was walking into a store.

"Told you he'd be back today!" Will's voice boomed from behind the
counter. The Greek chorus of John and a second man, who I now mentally
labeled as 'Earl,' were perched on their usual stools by the stove. They
both grunted in what I assumed was agreement.

It was the son, Robert, who spoke to me directly. He was organizing a
display of drill bits, his movements precise and economical. He looked
up, a faint, knowing look in his eyes. "Whatcha need, Mike?"

Inspiration struck. It was a calculated social maneuver, a probe to see
if a friendly gesture would be reciprocated or viewed with suspicion. It
was time to field-test gratitude.

I met Robert's gaze. "Hey. Thanks for your help yesterday. I slept last
night without a drippy faucet."

The effect was immediate. Robert gave a slight, surprised nod, a flicker
of a genuine smile touching his lips. "Glad it worked."

Will, however, reacted as if I'd just announced I was running for mayor.
"Hear that, boys? The man appreciates quality service! My boy knows his
stuff, I tell ya!"

John and Earl nodded sagely, as if this confirmed a long-held worldview.
I felt a flush of heat on my neck. The positive reinforcement was
overwhelming. I immediately broke contact and focused on the mission,
grabbing a shopping basket and consulting my new list.

*New lock for the front door (double cylinder deadbolt).* *Spackle and a
putty knife.* *Rake.* *Light bulbs.*

I moved through the aisles with purpose, collecting the items. The work
yesterday had only revealed more work to be done. The house was a
patient, and I was slowly diagnosing all its ailments. It was a good
feeling. Clear objectives. Tangible results.

I brought my haul to the counter. As Will started ringing things up with
his usual *cha-chunk* efficiency, I prepared my next move. This was the
primary objective of the visit: intelligence gathering.

"Hey," I started, trying to make my voice sound casual. "Two ladies
dropped by yesterday. Brought a dish." I paused, searching for the right
words, the kind of folksy exaggeration a normal person might use. "It
tasted so good I almost cried."

The line landed with the subtlety of a flash-bang grenade in a library.

Will stopped counting, looked up at me, and let out a bark of laughter
so loud it made the paint cans on a nearby shelf seem to rattle. "HA!
Almost cried, he says! John, you hear that? Eleanor's hotdish brought a
tear to the man's eye!"

John slapped his knee, a dry, wheezing cackle escaping him. "That'll do
it," he rasped. "Her secret ingredient is lard. Gets you right in the
ticker."

I had overplayed my hand. The attempt at relatable humor had turned me
into the main attraction. But it was working.

"Oh, that'd be Eleanor Vance and my cousin Brenda," Will said, wiping a
tear of mirth from his eye. "Eleanor's the unofficial queen of
Greenville. Knows everything about everyone. Brenda's a sweetheart. Sold
you that house, technically."

"Right," I said, seizing the opening. "I need to return the dish. I was
thinking, maybe they need something fixed? A loose railing? A squeaky
door? I'd like to repay them for the food."

I laid it out like a business proposal. A transaction. *Quid pro quo.*

Will finished ringing up my items and looked at me, his expression
softening from amusement to something more like pity. He leaned over the
counter, lowering his voice.

"Son," he said, and the way he said it made me feel about twelve years
old. "That's not how it works here. You don't 'repay' a
welcome-to-the-neighborhood casserole with manual labor."

This was new intelligence. The rules of engagement were completely
foreign. "What do I do, then?"

"You wash the Tupperware," Will said, ticking the points off on his
fingers. "You walk it over to Eleanor's house---the blue one with the
prize-winning petunias, you can't miss it. You thank her. And if you
really want to make a splash, you stop by the grocery store and pick up
a can of that fancy coffee she likes. The hazelnut kind."

He pointed towards the front window. "Brenda's easy. She works out of
the real estate office right across the street. Just drop the brownie
container off with her secretary if she's busy. And for God's sake,
don't offer to fix her desk."

It was a mission brief. Simple, clear, and utterly baffling. The
currency wasn't service or money. It was... coffee? And politeness?

"Okay," I said, feeling like I was being taught a new, ridiculously
complicated form of math. "Hazelnut coffee."

"That's the ticket," Will beamed, bagging my items. "You're learning,
Mike. You'll be a local in no time."

I paid, took the bag, and headed for the door. The bell dinged on my way
out. I was a trained killer, a ghost who could dismantle an entire
criminal organization from the shadows. And my next mission was to buy a
can of flavored coffee.

Of course. The work is his sanctuary, but the outside world, with its
strange customs and emotional landmines, is the real challenge.
