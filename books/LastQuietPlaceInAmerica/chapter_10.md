# Chapter 10

Brenda's expectant smile hung in the air over the table. The chili
cook-off. It sounded like my own personal version of hell: a crowded
public space, subjective judgment from strangers, and forced
socialization.

My mind raced through possible evasion tactics. I could claim a prior
engagement, but in a town this small, a lie like that would be
fact-checked by Eleanor before I'd even paid my bill. I could feign
illness, but that would just trigger a fresh wave of casseroles and
neighborly concern. Direct refusal was an option, but it would be noted,
filed away as anti-social behavior.

"Uhm..." I started, the sound weak even to my own ears. "I'm not much of
a cook."

It was the unvarnished truth. My culinary skills began and ended with
searing a piece of meat on a hot surface and boiling water without
starting a fire. For the last decade, food had been fuel, not art.

Brenda's smile didn't falter. "Oh, that's okay! It's more about
participation than winning. Mostly. Unless you're the mayor." She leaned
in a little closer, her voice dropping conspiratorially. "He uses canned
chili and just adds extra cumin. It's a town scandal, but no one's been
able to beat him."

My mind seized on the tactical data. A corrupt official. An established
but beatable champion. A populace hungry for a challenger. It was the
framework of a hundred jobs I'd taken. But the weapon wasn't a rifle; it
was a ladle.

Then, a lifeline. An idea sparked, a way to participate without taking
the lead. A support role.

"Are you entering?" I asked, my tone shifting from evasive to
inquisitive. "Maybe I could help? I don't know... stir?"

The word "stir" came out sounding ridiculous, like a surgeon offering to
hand out band-aids. But it was the only thing I could think of. I could
be an assistant. An apprentice. A grunt. It was a position I understood.

Brenda's eyes lit up. The maneuver had worked. She hadn't seen it as a
deflection, but as an offer.

"Me? Oh, heavens no," she laughed, taking a sip of her coffee. "I tried
one year. I set off the fire alarm in the town hall. They still talk
about it. My aunt Eleanor is one of the judges, so I'm on the organizing
committee now. Setting up tables, handing out the little plastic cups,
that sort of thing."

The lifeline evaporated. My offer of support had been made to a
non-combatant. I was back in the spotlight.

"But..." she said, tapping a thoughtful finger on her mug. "I know
someone who could use a partner." She scanned the diner, her gaze
landing on the far end of the counter where a man sat alone, staring
into a plate of untouched fries. He was younger than me, maybe late
twenties, with a perpetually worried look and the slumped shoulders of a
man who had been defeated by life one too many times. "That's Kyle. He
runs the animal shelter. He enters every year, bless his heart. He's got
this... vegan, gluten-free, five-alarm chili that tastes like hot grass.
He always comes in dead last."

Kyle. The ideal ally. Someone with a clear history of failure, low
expectations, and a desperate need for a new strategy. Partnering with
him wouldn't be a bid for the championship; it would be an act of
charity. Perfect camouflage.

"Maybe he could use some help," I said, my voice steady now. I had a new
mission objective. "Maybe we could make something... not vegan."

Brenda's smile turned into a full-blown grin. She saw a lonely newcomer
reaching out. She saw a community project, a feel-good story. I saw a
way to infiltrate the chili cook-off under the cover of a pity case.

"Mike, that is the nicest idea I have ever heard," she said, her
sincerity almost knocking me backward. "He'd be thrilled. C'mon."

Before I could object, she was sliding out of the booth and waving me to
follow. We walked the length of the diner, every eye in the place
following our little procession. Brenda was a beloved local figure, and
I was the mysterious new variable. This was the equivalent of a public
endorsement.

We stopped behind the lonely figure at the counter. "Kyle?" Brenda said
cheerfully.

Kyle looked up, startled. His eyes were wide and nervous. He looked like
a man who was permanently expecting bad news.

"Brenda, hi," he mumbled.

"Kyle, this is Mike Sullivan. He's new in town," she said, putting a
hand on my arm. "Mike, this is Kyle. He runs our wonderful animal
shelter."

I gave Kyle a nod. He gave me a weak, hesitant one in return.

"Mike here isn't much of a cook," Brenda continued, bulldozing ahead
with her plan. "And you, my friend, could use a new recipe. I was
thinking... maybe you two could team up for the chili cook-off this
Saturday?"

Kyle stared at me, then at Brenda, then back at me. His expression was a
mixture of confusion, suspicion, and a tiny, flickering spark of hope.
He looked at his plate of sad, cold French fries. He looked at me, a
complete stranger.

"Team up?" he asked, the words tasting foreign in his mouth.

"I can chop vegetables," I said, offering a concrete, non-threatening
skill. "And I'm a very good stirrer."

My life had become a complete and utter farce. Twenty-four hours ago, I
was mapping sightlines and planning exfiltration routes. Now I was
negotiating a culinary alliance with the world's saddest-looking vegan
to overthrow a corrupt small-town mayor.

"Okay," Kyle said, a slow, dawning realization on his face. "Yeah. Okay.
Maybe... maybe that could work."

I had done it. I had successfully integrated myself into the chili
cook-off. My cover was perfect. All I had to do now was learn how to
make chili.

This is gold. His one attempt to take a backseat has failed
spectacularly, and now he has to seek aid from the town's all-powerful
matriarch. The clash is inevitable and perfect.
