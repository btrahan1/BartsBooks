# Chapter 19

The showdown was chaos. A cheerful, well-organized, friendly chaos. For
two hours, the citizens of Greenville milled around the town hall lawn,
moving from table to table with small plastic cups and even smaller
plastic spoons. They tasted, they deliberated, they gossiped.

Our table was a runaway success. Buddy was the greatest marketing tool
ever conceived. People came for the dog and stayed for the chili. He sat
beside our table like a furry, four-legged king, accepting pats and
praise with a quiet dignity, his tail a constant, metronomic beat of
goodwill.

"Oh, this is the one with the cute dog!" "I heard this was Eleanor's
secret recipe!" "My goodness, this is better than the mayor's!"

Kyle was in his element, transformed from a nervous wreck into a
confident chili evangelist. He ladled out samples, chatted with the
tasters, and told everyone the story of our unlikely partnership. I
stayed in the background, the silent partner, the logistics man, keeping
the hot plate at the perfect temperature and observing the crowd.

Mayor Thompson, a portly man with a politician's smile that never quite
reached his eyes, strolled by our table. He tasted our chili, his smile
tightening for a fraction of a second before he declared it "a valiant
effort" and moved on. I saw it in his eyes. He knew he was in trouble.

Finally, Brenda Gable, looking official with a clipboard and a
megaphone, stepped up to the small portable stage.

"Alright, Greenville, settle down, settle down!" she announced, her
voice booming across the lawn. "Thank you all for coming out and thank
you to our wonderful contestants! Every chili was a winner today!"

A polite smattering of applause.

"But," Brenda continued, a dramatic glint in her eye, "we do have to
name a champion. Our judges have tallied the people's choice votes, and
we are down to the semifinals! The four finalists will now present a
fresh sample of their chili to our esteemed panel of judges. This will
be a blind taste test to determine the ultimate winner!"

A buzz of excitement went through the crowd. This was it. Kyle gripped
my arm, his knuckles white. Buddy let out a small, encouraging "woof."

Brenda raised the megaphone again. "Our first finalist... Mayor
Thompson, with his 'Four-Time Champ Chipotle Chili'!"

A loud, slightly obligatory cheer went up for the mayor. He beamed,
waving to the crowd.

"Our second finalist," Brenda called out, "is Betty Anne Miller, with
her 'White Lightnin' Chicken Chili'!"

The woman from the table next to us gasped and clapped her hands to her
chest. The crowd cheered.

"Our third finalist is 'Fireman Frank' Peterson, with his 'Five-Alarm
Inferno'!"

The man on our other side, a burly firefighter, pumped his fist in the
air to another round of applause. My heart was pounding. We had a real
shot.

"And our fourth and final semifinalist is..." Brenda paused for dramatic
effect, a huge grin spreading across her face. She looked right at our
table.

This was the moment. Our hard work, Eleanor's intel, Kyle's optimism, my
meticulous execution---it was all about to pay off.

Brenda raised the megaphone to her lips and announced our name.

"...And finally, 'Buddy's Chili'!"

The name hit me like a physical blow. *Buddy's Chili.* Not "Mike and
Kyle's Chili." Not "The Underdog's Chili." It wasn't our chili at all.
It was the dog's.

The crowd roared. It was the loudest cheer of the day. They weren't
cheering for us. They were cheering for the scruffy, one-eared mutt who
was now wagging his tail so hard his whole body shook. People were
chanting his name. "BUD-DY! BUD-DY!"

Kyle was ecstatic, jumping up and down and hugging me. "We did it! We
did it! They love us!"

I didn't move. I just stood there, the roar of the crowd washing over
me. All my planning, my control, my attempts to blend in by taking a
supporting role had led to this. I had been completely and utterly
erased from my own operation, replaced by my dog.

I hung my head, a slow, weary sigh escaping my lips. I could feel a
smile tugging at the corner of my mouth, a smile of pure, unadulterated
defeat. I was a ghost again, but not the kind I had intended to be. I
was just the guy who stirred the dog's chili.

This is it. The final showdown. The tension is surprisingly high for a
small-town cook-off. Let's bring it home.
