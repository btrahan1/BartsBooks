# Chapter 4

The Greenville Town Hall hadn't seen this many people since the
centennial celebration in '98. Every seat was filled, and a crowd of
latecomers stood three-deep along the back wall. The air was thick with
the smell of damp wool coats and nervous energy. It felt less like a
town council meeting and more like a war council.

I stood at the front of the room, next to a podium I had no intention of
using. Mayor Thompson sat behind me at the official council table,
looking less like a leader and more like a nervous observer. He had
tried to open the meeting, but after three sentences, all eyes had
shifted to me. I hadn't asked for command, but it had been given to me.

Buddy sat at my feet, a furry, four-legged sergeant-at-arms, his
presence a calming anchor in the anxious room.

"Alright," I began, my voice carrying easily in the hushed space. "Let's
be clear about the objective. The goal is not to stop people from
coming. We can't. The goal is to manage the influx. We need to control
the narrative."

I looked out at the faces. Farmers, shopkeepers, teachers, retirees.
These were not soldiers, but they were resolute.

"They're coming here to see 'The Last Quiet Place'," I said. "So that's
what we give them. We give them exactly what they expect: a quiet,
friendly, slightly boring town where nothing much happens. We don't give
them drama. We don't give them a story. We absorb them."

Eleanor Vance, seated in the front row, raised a perfectly manicured
hand. "And how, precisely, do we do that, Michael?"

"With a coordinated defense," I replied. "Phase one: Intelligence." I
pointed to Eleanor. "The Welcoming Committee is now our first line of
defense. You are the reconnaissance unit. When a new car, a tourist, a
reporter shows up, you are the first point of contact. You greet them,
you give them a muffin, and you find out who they are, why they're here,
and how long they're staying. Then you report that information back to a
central point."

"And who's the central point?" Will asked from the back.

All eyes, including mine, swiveled to Brenda Gable. She blinked,
surprised.

"Brenda's real estate office is the town's nerve center," I explained.
"It's central, it's always staffed, and she knows how to handle people.
All intelligence flows through Brenda."

Brenda sat up a little straighter, a look of grim determination on her
face. She nodded. "Okay. I can do that."

"Phase two: The Perimeter," I continued. I looked at Will. "Will, you
and the hardware store crew are the town's early warning system. You
know every car, every truck. You and your coffee clutch on that bench
are now our watchtowers. You spot an out-of-state plate, you make a call
to Brenda."

Will grinned, puffing out his chest. "We can handle that."

"Phase three: Information Control." I looked at Peggy. "Peggy, the diner
is the public square. You will hear everything. Your job is to be
friendly, serve the best meatloaf in the state, and be completely,
utterly boring. When they ask for stories, you tell them about the
weather. When they ask for local gossip, you tell them about the
upcoming bake sale. Under no circumstances do you mention the chili
cook-off, the previous mayor, or..." I paused, my gaze sweeping the
room. "...me."

A ripple of understanding went through the crowd. This was their part of
the mission. To protect the secret.

"We give them quiet," I said, my voice dropping. "We give them polite.
We give them friendly. We bore them back across the county line. Any
questions?"

Fireman Frank raised a hand. "What if they don't want to leave? What
about those developer types you mentioned?"

"That's Phase four," I said, my voice hardening slightly. "If they get
past the muffins, the watchtowers, and the boring stories... then they
deal with me."

The room was silent. They understood the implication. I was the final
line of defense. The quiet enforcer for the last quiet place.

"Alright," Mayor Thompson said, finally finding his voice and seizing
the moment. "You all heard the man! Let's get to it! Greenville is
officially on friendly alert!"

The meeting broke up, not in a chaotic rush, but with a quiet, shared
purpose. People filed out, talking in low, serious tones. They weren't
just neighbors anymore. They were a unit.

As I gathered my things, Brenda came up to me, a small, worried frown on
her face. "Mike, this is a good plan. But what about you? They're going
to be looking for the man with the famous dog. You're the one person in
this story they can't see."

"That's the point," I said. "The most interesting thing in this town
right now is a ghost. And ghosts are hard to find."

She didn't look convinced. "We've got your back, you know," she said
softly.

"I know," I replied. And the terrifying thing was, for the first time in
my life, I believed it. The town was mobilizing to protect its quiet.
And in doing so, they were building a wall around me.

My own role was that of a ghost. I changed my routine, walking Buddy in
the early dawn hours and late at night. During the day, we stayed in the
house or in the backyard. I was the missing piece of the puzzle, the one
"local character" the tourists couldn't find. They were looking for the
man with the famous dog, but all they found was a town full of polite,
friendly, and profoundly boring people.

Brenda's office became the nerve center as planned. A large map of the
town was pinned to her wall, and she used different colored thumbtacks
to track the movements of the tourists---the "friendlies."

I stopped in on the fifth day. The phone rang while I was there. Brenda
answered, listened for a moment, and made a note.

"That was Will," she said after hanging up. "A silver Mercedes with
California plates just pulled up to the Inn. One man, business suit.
Looks like he's here to stay."

"Did Eleanor's team make contact?" I asked.

"Not yet," Brenda said, her expression serious. "He walked right past
them. Didn't even take the muffin."

A chill went down my spine. The muffins were our first line of defense.
This was different. This wasn't a tourist. This was the next wave. This
was the developer. The plan had worked, but its success had just
attracted a much bigger shark.

Excellent. The shark has entered the placid waters. Time to see how the
town's defenses hold up against a predator who isn't interested in
muffins.
