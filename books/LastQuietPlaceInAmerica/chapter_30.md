# Chapter 30: The next morning, the silver Mercedes was packed and idling in front of the Inn before the sun had fully risen. Terrence Carpenter emerged, his face pale and drawn. He didn't speak to the innkeeper; he just dropped the key on the front desk and walked out, his assistant scurrying to keep up.

As the slick car pulled out onto Main Street, an eerily familiar scene played itself out.

Word had spread with the town's usual impossible speed. It wasn't planned. It just... happened. People began to emerge from the diner, the hardware store, the post office. They lined the sidewalks, a silent, watching crowd.

Carpenter saw them and flinched, expecting jeers or insults. But this was Greenville.

They just smiled.

The same friendly, placid, unreadable smiles they had given him in the town hall. *It was a wave of relentless, silent, cheerful victory. It was, he would later reflect, the most humiliating defeat of his entire career.*

He hit the gas and didn't look back.

The moment the Mercedes was out of sight, the silence on the street broke. But the crowd didn't turn to me. I was standing near the back, by the diner, having watched the whole thing unfold. I had already started to melt back into the shadows, my part in the play complete.

Instead, all eyes turned to a single, blushing figure.

Kyle was standing nervously outside the animal shelter, trying to pretend he was just sweeping the front walk. But Eleanor Vance walked right up to him, a beatific smile on her face.

"Kyle, dear," she said, her voice full of pride. "We heard you had a little 'chat' with our guest last night."

Kyle blushed a furious, blotchy red. "I... uh... I don't know what you're..."

He was cut off as Will from the hardware store came up and slapped him heartily on the back. "Don't be modest, son! Peggy heard the whole thing through the wall! Said you were as cool as a cucumber! Talked him right out of town!"

"A friend of Buddy's!" John cackled from the sidewalk. "That was the best part!"

The town then, once again, circled their hero. They swarmed Kyle, a flurry of back-pats, handshakes, and praise.

"You stood up for us, Kyle!"
"Never knew you had it in you!"
"Way to go!"

Brenda pushed through the crowd and gave him a huge, spontaneous hug. "You were so brave!"

Kyle looked like he was going to pass out from a combination of pride, embarrassment, and sheer terror. He looked over the heads of the crowd, his eyes desperately searching for me.

I gave him a slow, subtle wink. *You earned it.*

*He was the perfect hero for Greenville. He was one of them. Kind, unassuming, and secretly made of steel. They would never suspect he was acting on my instructions. They saw a local boy who had found his courage and stood up to a bully. It was a story they could understand, a victory they could own.*

The applause started, a spontaneous, joyful eruption. They were clapping for Kyle. For their friend. For the man who had, in their eyes, single-handedly saved their town.

I watched for another moment, a genuine, private smile on my face. Then, I turned and slipped back into the diner. Peggy was at my booth with a fresh pot of coffee.

"He did good, didn't he?" she said, pouring me a cup. Her eyes were shining with pride for the young man being celebrated outside.

"He's a natural," I said.

Buddy, who had been sleeping under the table, let out a soft "woof" of agreement.

*The town had its hero. My anonymity was secure. And I could finally, maybe, just for a little while, go back to having a quiet life. At least until the next fire needed putting out.*
