# Chapter 31: The Ghost's Shield

***

### Epilogue

Two weeks later, the quiet had returned. The tourists had dwindled to a trickle, the national spotlight had moved on, and Greenville had gone back to being Greenville. The only lasting change was Kyle, who now walked with a newfound confidence, the town’s celebrated, unassuming hero.

I was down at Farmer McGregor’s place, helping him mend a section of fence that had been knocked down in a windstorm. It was good, honest work. *My hands were calloused, my back ached, and my mind was blissfully, beautifully empty. Buddy was chasing field mice in the tall grass. For the first time, I felt like the man I was pretending to be.*

That's when the black SUV pulled up in front of the diner.

It was a professional vehicle. Tinted windows, no markings, and it moved with a silent, predatory grace. Three men stepped out. They were hard-looking, dressed in plain, functional clothes that couldn't hide the disciplined set of their shoulders. They moved like a unit.

*These weren't tourists.*

The chattering in the diner stopped cold when they walked in. The cheerful bell on the door sounded like an alarm.

One of them stood by the door, his hands clasped in front of him, his eyes scanning the room. The second man did a quick, professional sweep of the short hallway leading to the restrooms. The third, clearly the leader, took a seat in a booth near the front.

Brenda, who was helping Peggy with the lunch rush, didn't miss a beat. She walked over to the leader’s table, pad and pen in hand, her expression one of polite, professional calm. "How can I help you gentlemen?"

The seated man didn't look at the menu. He pushed a photograph across the table toward Brenda. It was a grainy surveillance photo, but the face was unmistakable. It was me. Michael Corrigan.

"We're looking for this man," the leader said. His voice was flat, devoid of emotion. His eyes were cold. "He goes by the name of 'Mike'."

Brenda glanced at the photo, her expression barely flickering. "Oh, THAT guy!" she said, her voice full of a sudden, weary recognition. "Yeah, I've seen him. Real character, that one. Cold eyes, kind of like yours." She tapped her pen on her pad. "Didn't talk much." She turned and called out across the diner. "Hey, Kyle, come take a look at this. It's that guy who stole Buddy!"

Kyle, who had been having lunch at the counter, stood and walked over. He looked at the photo, his face scrunching up in distaste. "Oh, yeah. Mike," he said, and then lowered his voice conspiratorially. "Between you and me, he was kind of an ass. Just kept to himself, didn't talk to anyone." He looked at Brenda. "He skipped town with our mascot, how long ago was that?" Then he called out, "Hey, Peggy, how long ago was it that 'Mike' guy got on the bus?"

Peggy walked over, frowning as she looked at the photo. "Hmm... let me think. Well, I know he skipped out on his last rent payment and left on the morning bus without so much as a 'thank you'." She tapped a finger to her chin. "Guess that must've been three or four weeks ago? Hey, Chef," she yelled into the kitchen pass-through. "You remember this guy, right?"

The chef poked his head out, wiping his hands on a towel. He squinted at the photo. "Yeah. Bad attitude, bad tipper. Good riddance."

The leader looked from one face to the next. He saw a consistent, unified story, delivered with the casual conviction of truth. He stood, dropping a five-dollar bill on the table. "Thank you for your time." He paused. "Which direction was that bus headed?"

"Only one way to go from here, mister," Brenda said, pointing a thumb down the road leading out of town. "South."

The three men left as quietly as they had come. The black SUV pulled away from the curb and headed south. The diner was silent for a full minute, and then, as if a switch had been flipped, the conversations started back up, the forks scraped against the plates, and the bell on the door dinged as a new customer came in. Nothing had happened.

Later that evening, I finished up at the farm. Buddy, tired and happy, hopped into the passenger seat of my truck. We drove back into town and I took my usual seat at the diner. The place was warm and bright, a refuge from the growing dark outside.

Brenda came over with a fresh pot of coffee. "Long day, Mike?"

"You could say that," I replied, a good ache settling into my bones. "Coffee, please, Brenda."

"Sure thing, Mike," she said, filling my cup with a steady hand.

I took a sip. It was perfect. I looked around at the familiar faces, the comfortable quiet.

"Anything interesting happen today while I was down at the farm?"

Brenda and Peggy, who was cleaning the counter nearby, just shook their heads.

"Not a thing, Mike," Brenda said, a small, secret smile playing on her lips. "Just another day in The Last Quiet Place in America."
