# Chapter One: The Art of the Morning Chore

The universe breathed. Leon breathed with it.

At 4:00 AM, the world was a quiet place, defined by the chill in the
floorboards and the deep, rhythmic silence of the farmhouse. Leon sat
cross-legged on his straw mattress, his eyes closed, his hands resting
loosely on his knees.

Most Cultivators in the great sects of the East sat upon jade cushions,
inhaling the refined spiritual vapors of mountain peaks to sharpen their
killing intent. Leon sat on a quilt his Grandmother had patched three
times, inhaling the smell of old wood and drying herbs to wake up his
left foot, which had fallen asleep.

Inhale.

He felt the energy---Ki, Essence, Mana, the labels didn't matter much to
him---drift into his nose. He visualized it not as a raging fire or a
striking viper, but as a slow, warm brook. He guided the energy down his
throat, past the solar plexus, and into his core. His core was a dense,
heavy thing. Years ago, it had felt like a pebble. Now, after a decade
of morning rituals, it felt like a solid iron cannonball sitting in his
gut.

Exhale.

He pushed the warmth down his legs, forcing the blood to flow, expanding
the meridians that ran along his shins. The pins and needles in his left
foot vanished, replaced by a sturdy, grounded warmth.

Leon opened his eyes. They were brown, clear, and entirely average. He
stretched, his joints popping with a sound like snapping dry branches.

"System," he whispered, mostly out of habit.

A translucent blue screen flickered into existence before his eyes. It
was dim, as if the System itself was tired of showing him the same thing
every morning.

**\[Name: Leon\]** **\[Class: Farmer (Provisional)\]** **\[Level: 1\]**
**\[Experience: CAPPED\]** **\[Strength: 5 (limit reached)\]**
**\[Agility: 5 (limit reached)\]** **\[Intellect: 4 (limit reached)\]**
**\[Wisdom: 8 (limit reached)\]** **\[Mana: 10/10\]**

Leon sighed, swiping the box away with a calloused hand. It was the same
story as yesterday, and the year before that. The "Limit Reached" tags
were mocking him. He knew he was stronger than a five---Grandpa said a
Strength of five was barely enough to lift a sack of flour without
grunting---but the System was a stickler for rules. Without leveling up,
the numbers wouldn't move. And to level up, something had to die.

"Not today," Leon muttered, swinging his legs out of bed. "Got beans to
plant."

He dressed quickly, pulling on a roughspun tunic and trousers that had
been mended so many times they were more patch than original fabric. He
stepped into his boots, stomping the heels down, and headed out the
door.

The morning air was crisp, smelling of dew and wet grass. The sun hadn't
crested the horizon yet, leaving the farm in a wash of grey and deep
blue.

First stop: The coop.

"Morning, ladies," Leon said softly as he unlatched the wooden door.

Inside, twelve chickens blinked at him. General Cluck, the rooster who
took his job far too seriously, puffed out his chest and eyed Leon with
suspicion. The bird was a Level 2 Fowl Beast, technically a higher level
than Leon.

"Easy, General," Leon said, moving with a fluid, gliding step that
didn't disturb the straw. "Just checking for eggs."

General Cluck crowed, a sound like a rusty trumpet, and pecked at Leon's
boot. A normal Level 1 would have taken a point of damage. Leon didn't
even feel it through the leather. He moved his hand---not fast, just
efficiently---and scooped three brown eggs from under a brooding hen
before she realized she'd been robbed.

"Thank you, Matilda. Good work, Henrietta."

He cycled his Ki again, moving it to his fingertips. He didn't use a
skill---he didn't have any "Egg Gathering" skills unlocked---but he used
the warmth to check the shells. One was slightly thin. Henrietta needed
more calcium. He'd have to grind up some oyster shells into the feed
later.

After the chickens came the heavy lifting.

The storm three nights ago had been a nasty one. A massive oak branch,
thick as a man's torso, had snapped off the old tree by the creek and
landed squarely across the fence line. The sheep were currently staring
at the gap in the fence, contemplating a prison break.

Leon approached the branch. It was heavy, waterlogged wood, easily
weighing four hundred pounds.

A party of adventurers had passed by yesterday. The mage, a flashy man
in red silk robes, had offered to blast it with a Fireball for two
silver pieces. Leon had politely declined. Burning good wood was
wasteful, and besides, the sheep didn't like fire.

Leon planted his feet. He widened his stance, sinking his weight into
the mud.

Rooting Stance.

It wasn't a System skill. It was just something his Grandpa had yelled
at him while he was learning to chop wood. *"Be the tree, Leon! Don't
let the axe swing you!"*

Leon placed his hands under the rough bark. He didn't just pull with his
back; that was how you slipped a disc. He cycled his breath, visualizing
the earth beneath his boots. He drew stability up through his legs,
locking his knees, hips, and spine into a single, unbreakable structure.

"Up," he whispered.

The System said his Strength was 5. A Strength of 5 should have resulted
in Leon straining, turning purple, and failing.

Instead, the log groaned. Leon's muscles didn't bulge grotesquely; they
just hardened like cured leather. The Ki in his meridians flared,
reinforcing his bones, acting as a hydraulic system beneath his skin.

Slowly, smoothly, the massive branch lifted. Leon gritted his teeth, his
breath hissing out in a controlled stream. He walked it backward, his
boots sinking two inches into the soil with every step, until he was
clear of the fence. He dropped it with a ground-shaking *thud* that
startled the sheep into a bleating panic.

Leon wiped a bead of sweat from his forehead. "Good firewood for
winter," he noted, patting the bark.

"Talking to wood again, boy?"

Leon turned to see his Grandfather standing on the porch, leaning on a
cane. The old man looked like a dried apple doll left out in the
sun---wrinkled, tough, and enduring. A 'retired' Level 15 Militia
Sergeant, Grandpa had a bad knee and a sharp tongue.

"Just sizing it up, Grandpa," Leon said, smiling.

"Breakfast is on," Grandpa grunted, turning back inside. "And don't
think I didn't see you babying that Rooster. If that bird pecks me one
more time, he's going in the pot."

"The General is just protective," Leon defended, jogging up the steps.

Breakfast was porridge, but in the Leon household, porridge was a
serious affair. The oats were Golden-Husk Oats, a variety Leon had spent
three years cross-breeding for maximum heartiness. The milk was from
Bessie, who Leon brushed for twenty minutes every evening to ensure her
stress levels remained low. The honey was from the hives near the clover
patch Leon had hand-weeded.

As he ate, a warm feeling spread through his chest.

**\[System Notification: You have consumed** '**Hearty Breakfast**'**.
Stamina regeneration +10% for 4 hours.\]**

Leon ignored the box. He just liked the taste of the honey.

"Town day," Grandma said, pouring him a mug of herbal tea. She was a
round, soft woman who smelled perpetually of yeast and lavender. "Need
you to take the cart in. We're low on salt, and I need more yarn for the
winter blankets."

"Yes, Ma'am," Leon said.

"And take a bath," Grandpa added, eyeing Leon's muddy boots. "You smell
like wet oak and chicken coop. You'll scare the city folk."

The road to Oakhaven was a well-traveled dirt track that wound through
the rolling green hills of the valley. It was a beautiful day, and Leon
whistled as he walked beside the mule, pulling the cart. He didn't ride
in the cart---Old Sal, the mule, had a touch of arthritis in her left
hock, so Leon usually walked and pulled alongside her to ease the load.

About a mile from town, the quiet was broken by the sound of laughter.

A group of four adventurers was walking ahead of him. They were
unmistakable: brightly colored armor, weapons that glowed with faint
magical auras, and an air of unearned confidence.

Leon recognized the gear. Steel plate, likely Level 10 or higher. A
staff topped with a mana crystal. These were professionals.

As Leon approached, pulling the cart with a strap across his chest, the
group stopped and turned.

"Ho there, traveler!" the leader called out. He was a warrior with a
jawline so sharp it could cut glass and blonde hair that defied gravity.
"Fine day for hauling dung!"

Leon smiled politely. "It's actually turnips and wool, sir. But it is a
fine day."

The warrior elbowed his companion, a rogue in tight black leather. "Get
a load of this guy. Hey, check him."

The mage in the back narrowed his eyes. "Using \[Inspect\]... wait.
What?" The mage started snickering. "You're kidding."

"What is it?" the warrior asked.

"Level One," the mage wheezed, slapping his knee. "He's Level One. And
look at the class. 'Farmer.' Not even 'Warrior' or 'Laborer.' Just
Farmer."

The group erupted into laughter.

"Hey, Buddy!" the warrior shouted, grinning. "Careful with those
turnips! If one falls on your toe, it might deplete your HP!"

Leon blinked. He looked down at his heavy leather boots, then back at
the turnips in the cart. He did a quick mental calculation. A turnip
weighed perhaps two pounds. Falling from the height of the cart...

"I don't think so," Leon replied earnestly. "These boots have a
reinforced toe cap. I made them myself. The turnip would just bounce
off."

The rogue cackled. "Oh my god, he's serious. He thinks we're worried
about his toes."

"L.O.L.," the mage said, wiping a tear from his eye. "Look. Only.
Level-one."

Leon tilted his head. *L.O.L.* He had heard that term before. It was
what the scribes wrote in the town newsletters when something was funny.
He smiled broadly. It was nice that these high-level heroes were in such
a good mood. Being an adventurer must be stressful work, fighting
monsters and all. It was good they could find joy in a simple turnip
cart.

"I'm glad you're enjoying the morning," Leon said, nodding to them.
"Safe travels."

He leaned forward, engaging his core. The cart was heavy, loaded with
three hundred pounds of produce and wool. He pushed his Ki into his
legs, finding the rhythm. *Step. Pull. Step. Pull.*

The warrior stopped laughing as Leon passed. He watched the wheels of
the cart. They were deep in the rutted road, churning through thick,
drying mud.

"Wait," the warrior muttered. "Look at the mule."

The mage looked. "What about it?"

" The mule isn't pulling," the warrior said, frowning. "The rope is
slack."

The group fell silent, watching the young man in the patched tunic walk
away. The strap across his chest was taut, humming with tension. Old Sal
the mule was just trotting along happily, munching on a piece of clover
she'd snatched, while Leon hauled the entire cart, and the mule attached
to it, up the incline of the hill.

"Must be a gravity enchantment on the cart," the mage concluded, shaking
his head. "Some rich kid playing peasant."

"Yeah," the rogue scoffed, spitting on the ground. "Level One my ass.
Probably a smurf."

Leon didn't hear them. He was too busy listening to the axle of the
cart. The right wheel was squeaking. A C-sharp squeak.

*Needs grease,* Leon thought. *I'll pick up some lard at the butcher's.*

He crested the hill and looked down at Oakhaven. The town was bustling,
flags snapping in the wind. It was a place of commerce, quests, and
noise. Leon adjusted his grip on the cart strap, took a deep breath of
the dusty road air, and prepared himself.

He hoped he wouldn't run into any girls. They always asked such
confusing questions.
