# Chapter Two: Hazard Removal

Oakhaven Market was a sensory assault, a riot of smells ranging from
roasting pork and freshly baked bread to the less pleasant tang of
unwashed bodies and donkey manure. It was loud, chaotic, and
vibrant---everything the farm was not.

Leon navigated the crowd with the cart, his movements precise. To a
casual observer, he was just a big guy pulling a cart. To someone paying
attention, his pathfinding was miraculous. He didn't jostle a single
shoulder; he anticipated the ebb and flow of the crowd like he
anticipated the flow of water in an irrigation ditch.

He parked Old Sal in the designated beast-wait area behind Mrs. Gable's
General Store.

"Now, Sal," Leon said, untying the harness. He pulled a fresh, crisp
carrot from his pocket---a Level 2 *Sun-Kissed Carrot* he'd grown
specifically for bribe purposes. "Don't eat the hitching post. It's
treated pine. It'll give you gas."

Sal crunched the carrot loudly, looking smug.

Leon hoisted two sacks of wool, one on each shoulder. They weighed
eighty pounds apiece. He carried them as if they were filled with
feathers, walking around to the front of the shop.

"Mrs. Gable?" Leon called out as he ducked his head to enter the store.

The bell above the door jingled. Mrs. Gable, a woman who had seen sixty
winters and possessed a smile that could melt butter, looked up from the
counter. Her eyes crinkled.

"Leon! I was hoping you'd come in today. Is that the winter shear?"

"Yes, ma'am," Leon said, setting the bags down gently. "Grandma washed
and carded it. It's the Merino-Cross. Very soft."

Mrs. Gable opened a sack and ran her hand through the white fluff. "Oh,
this is divine. Better than the silk the caravans bring from the South.
And how are you, dear? Still single?"

Leon blinked. "I am... solitary, yes. But I have the chickens."

Mrs. Gable sighed, a long-suffering sound. "Leon, my niece, Clara? The
one with the red hair? She asked about you. She said she saw you lifting
the church pew last week to sweep under it. She was quite impressed."

Leon frowned, puzzled. "The dust accumulation under those pews is a fire
hazard, Mrs. Gable. Dry dust acts like tinder. Clara should be careful
if she was impressed by the dust; she might have allergies."

Mrs. Gable stared at him for a long moment, then patted his hand.
"You're a good boy, Leon. I'll give you twelve silver for the wool.
That's two over market price."

"Market price is ten," Leon corrected automatically. "I can't take
twelve. That would mess up your margins."

"Take the twelve, you stubborn mule, or I won't buy your turnips."

Leon accepted the coins reluctantly. He didn't understand why people in
town were so bad at math.

After unloading the turnips (which caused a minor bidding war between
two tavern owners who knew the quality of Leon's root vegetables), Leon
wandered toward the Alchemist's Quarter. He needed liniment for Old
Sal's joints and some specialized salts for curing meat.

The Alchemist's Quarter was usually quieter, smelling of sulfur and
peppermint. But today, there was a commotion.

A crowd had gathered around a small, ramshackle stall run by
Mr. Abernathy. Abernathy was ancient, a retired hedge-wizard who sold
basic healing salves and headache powders. He was shaking, his hands
raised in a defensive posture.

Standing over him was a massive man clad in shimmering blue plate armor.
A Greatsword was strapped to his back---a *Frost-Brand*, by the look of
the mist curling off the hilt. The man's helmet was off, revealing a
scarred face and eyes that held the glazed, aggressive look of someone
who had drunk too many strength potions.

"I said," the armored man boomed, his voice cracking with rage, "this
potion is defective! I drank it, and my stamina didn't refill
instantly!"

"It... it's a minor stamina tonic, sir," Mr. Abernathy stammered. "It
restores fatigue over ten minutes. It says so on the label."

"I don't read labels!" the warrior roared. He swept his arm out,
knocking a row of glass vials off the counter.

Crash.

The sound of shattering glass cut through the street chatter. The crowd
flinched, but nobody stepped forward. The warrior was clearly
high-level---likely Level 20 or higher. To interfere was to invite a
hospital visit.

"You scammed me!" The warrior grabbed the front of Mr. Abernathy's
robes, lifting the frail old man off the ground. "Refund me. Double.
Now."

Leon stopped.

He looked at the broken glass on the cobblestones. He looked at the
terror in Mr. Abernathy's eyes.

Leon didn't feel anger. Anger was a hot, useless emotion that made your
hands shake. What he felt was the same sensation he got when he saw a
fox trying to get into the henhouse. It was a problem. A disruption of
order. A hazard.

Leon walked through the crowd. "Excuse me. Pardon me. Watch your step,
there's glass."

He arrived at the stall just as the warrior pulled back a gauntleted
fist.

"Sir," Leon said. His voice wasn't loud, but it projected from his
diaphragm, deep and resonant.

The warrior froze, turning his head. He looked down at Leon. He took in
the patched tunic, the smell of turnips, and the lack of visible
weapons.

"Get lost, peasant," the warrior sneered. "Unless you want to be scraped
off the pavement."

"You dropped these," Leon said, pointing to the shattered vials. "And
you are holding Mr. Abernathy in an unsafe manner. His bones are
brittle. If you drop him, he could fracture a hip."

The warrior laughed, a harsh, barking sound. "System! \[Inspect\]!"

The warrior's eyes glazed over for a split second as he read the prompt.
Then, his grin widened into something predatory.

"Level One," the warrior announced to the crowd. "We have a hero here,
folks! A Level One Farmer wants to teach me about safety." He dropped
Mr. Abernathy, who scrambled back behind his counter.

The warrior turned fully to Leon, cracking his knuckles. "You know, I
need to keep my combat multiplier up. Beating you into a pulp might just
give me a .01% boost."

"Violence isn't necessary," Leon said calmly. He shifted his stance. His
feet slid apart slightly, sinking into the rhythm of the earth. *Rooting
Stance.* "You just need to pay for the damage and apologize."

"Die," the warrior said simply.

He threw a punch.

It was a sloppy punch, fueled by arrogance and stats rather than skill.
But it was fast. A Strength of 40 backed that fist. It would have taken
the head off a normal man.

Leon didn't block it. Blocking a force that strong would bruise his
forearms. Instead, he treated the warrior's arm like a bolt of lightning
hitting a lightning rod---he grounded it.

Leon stepped *inside* the swing. His left hand came up, not to stop the
fist, but to guide it. He slapped the warrior's wrist, redirecting the
kinetic energy over his shoulder.

At the same time, Leon's right hand shot out. He didn't make a fist. He
used the \[Claw Grip\] he used for catching panicked sheep by the
scruff.

His fingers clamped onto the warrior's breastplate. Specifically, the
leather straps holding the pauldron to the cuirass.

"You're top-heavy," Leon noted.

Leon twisted his hips. It was the same motion he used to toss a bale of
hay onto the loft. *Dip. Twist. Extend.*

The warrior's momentum, combined with Leon's sudden redirection,
resulted in physics taking swift revenge. The warrior's feet left the
ground. He spun in the air, a blur of blue steel, and slammed into the
cobblestones with a bone-rattling *CLANG*.

The warrior wheezed, the wind knocked out of him. He scrambled to get
up, his face red with humiliation. "You... lucky... trash!"

He reached for the Frost-Brand on his back.

Leon's eyes narrowed. Drawing a weapon in a crowded market. That was a
Class A Safety Violation.

As the warrior drew the sword, Leon stepped forward. He didn't strike
the man. He struck the *sword*.

Leon's hand, calloused from years of gripping hoe handles and axe hafts,
chopped down on the flat of the blade just as it cleared the scabbard.

He channeled his Ki. *Polished Core. Iron Skin.*

PING.

The sound was like a church bell ringing underwater. The vibration
traveled down the sword and into the warrior's hands. The warrior
screamed and dropped the weapon, clutching his numbed, tingling fingers.

Before the man could recover, Leon was there. He moved behind the
warrior, grabbed his right arm, and folded it behind the man's back. It
was a \[Restraining Hold\], the same one Leon used when he had to trim
the hooves of a stubborn calf.

"Let go!" the warrior shrieked, thrashing.

"Stop moving," Leon said gently. "You'll dislocate your shoulder. I have
you in a leverage lock. The more you struggle, the more it hurts. That's
just anatomy."

Leon applied a fraction more pressure. The warrior yelped and went limp,
forced onto his knees.

The market was dead silent. The crowd stared. A Level One Farmer was
holding a Level 20 Warrior in a hammerlock, lecturing him like a naughty
schoolboy.

"Now," Leon said, looking at the warrior's ear. "Mr. Abernathy is a
small business owner. His margins are thin. You broke three vials of
Minor Stamina Tonic and one vial of Headache Powder. That comes to...
four silver and ten copper. Plus the labor for cleanup."

"I'll kill you!" the warrior sobbed.

"That's a threat," Leon sighed. "Another violation." He squeezed the
wrist slightly. "Pay the man."

"Okay! Okay! In my pouch! Take it!"

Leon reached into the warrior's belt pouch with his free hand, counted
out exactly five silver pieces, and placed them on the counter.

"Keep the change, Mr. Abernathy," Leon said politely.

He released the warrior and stepped back. "You should check your armor
straps," Leon added helpfully. "The leather is dry-rotting near the
buckles. If that snaps in a dungeon, you'll lose your pauldron."

The warrior scrambled to his feet, cradling his arm. He looked at Leon
with pure, unadulterated horror. He looked at the sword lying on the
ground, then back at the farmer. He didn't say a word. He grabbed his
sword and ran, clanking loudly as he vanished into the crowd.

Leon looked down at his own hand. There was a smudge of grease on his
palm from the warrior's armor. He frowned.

"I need to wash up," Leon muttered to himself. "Grandma hates grease
stains."

He looked up to see Mr. Abernathy and half the market staring at him
with their mouths open.

"Are you alright, Mr. Abernathy?" Leon asked. "Did any glass get in your
sandals?"

Mr. Abernathy swallowed hard. "I... I'm fine, Leon. Thank you."

"Good," Leon nodded. "I'll take three jars of the muscle liniment,
please. Old Sal has had a long morning."

Somewhere in the back of the crowd, a young woman with a notebook---a
scribe for the Adventurer's Guild---was furiously writing down what she
had just seen. She paused, tapped her quill against her chin, and wrote
a title for the entry.

The L.O.L. Incident.

You are absolutely right. That was a "shonen anime" escalation error on
my part.

If he beats a Level 20 with ease in Chapter 2, we have nowhere to go.
The tension evaporates because he's already a demigod.

Scaling it down to **Level 6** (an "Initiate" or "Bronze Rank"
adventurer) is much better because: 1. **The Math makes sense:** A Level
6 Warrior might have a Strength of 15 or 20. Leon has a "System
Strength" of 5, but his "Cultivated Strength" (Ki-reinforced) might be
effectively 25. It's a close fight on paper, but Leon wins because of
*skill* and *leverage*, not just because he's a secret god. 2. **The
Reaction:** Losing to a Level 1 is embarrassing for a Level 6 (a bully).
Losing to a Level 1 for a Level 20 (a master) would break the world's
logic and probably summon the King's Guard to investigate. 3. **The
Stakes:** It leaves room for the Level 20s to actually be scary later
on.

### []{#anchor}Thoughts on "Punching Up" (The Dire Circumstances Mechanic)

Since you mentioned he can "punch up" when things get dangerous, but
isn't a pacifist, here is a proposal for how that magic system works:

**The** "**Over-Draft**" **Mechanic:** Since his XP is capped, maybe he
stores excess energy in his **Core** (like a battery). \* **Normal
Mode:** He uses ambient Ki. He is strong, but limited. \* **Dire Mode:**
He "burns" his foundation. He taps into that dense reserve of energy
he's been saving for leveling. \* **The Cost:** It hurts. It burns his
meridians (like red-lining an engine). After he does it, he is "sick"
for a few days (The Grandma Factor: "Leon, you look pale, no chores for
you!"). This prevents him from spamming it and keeps the tension high.
