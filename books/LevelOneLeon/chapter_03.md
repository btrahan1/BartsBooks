# Chapter Three: The Rhythm of Steel

The Oakhaven Smithy was usually the loudest place in town, a cacophony
of ringing anvils and roaring bellows. But when Leon arrived, dragging
his cart with the squeaky wheel, the shop was strangely quiet.

Master Gondar, the town's blacksmith, was sitting on a stool outside the
open bay doors, nursing a mug of something that smelled suspiciously
like medicinal hops. Gondar was a dwarf, though he insisted he was just
"vertically efficient." He had arms like tree trunks and a beard that he
tucked into his belt to keep it from catching fire.

"Morning, Master Gondar," Leon said, stopping the cart.

Gondar groaned, shielding his eyes from the sun. "Not so loud, lad. The
anvil gnomes are hammering inside my skull today."

Leon winced sympathetically. "Ale at the Broken Shield last night?"

"Dwarven Stout," Gondar corrected, rubbing his temples. "Imported. Hits
like a warhammer. What brings you by? Need more nails?"

"The coulter on the plow cracked," Leon explained. He reached into the
cart and pulled out the heavy iron blade. It had a jagged fissure
running down the center, a victim of a particularly stubborn granite
rock in the north field. "I need it forge-welded and tempered. The
planting season starts in two days."

Gondar squinted at the metal. "I can't do it, Leon. My hands are shaking
like a leaf in a gale. If I try to use \[Smithing Strike\] today, I'll
likely miss and hit my own thumb."

Leon looked at the cold forge, then at the broken plowshare. He couldn't
wait. If the beans didn't go in by Tuesday, the harvest would be late.

"Do you mind if I use the anvil?" Leon asked. "I'll pay for the coal."

Gondar waved a hand dismissively. "Be my guest. Just don't organize my
hammers again. I can never find the ball-peen when you put it in the
'logical' spot."

Leon smiled gratefully and stepped into the gloom of the smithy.

It took him ten minutes to get the fire right. He shoveled the coal,
arranging it in a pyramid to concentrate the heat. He worked the
bellows, pumping the handle with a steady, rhythmic motion. *Whoosh.
Whoosh.* The coals glowed from dull red to a vibrant, angry orange.

He was just rolling up his sleeves to the elbow when a shadow fell
across the doorway.

"Excuse me. Is the smith available?"

The voice was cool, melodic, and possessed a clarity that cut through
the ambient noise of the street.

Leon turned. And froze.

Standing in the doorway was a woman. She wasn't from Oakhaven. The local
girls wore woolen dresses or aprons stained with flour. This woman wore
leather armor that looked like it had been grown rather than
stitched---a seamless blend of forest green and deep brown hues. A
longbow made of white ash was slung over her shoulder, humming with a
faint, magical resonance.

But it was her face that made Leon's brain short-circuit. She had high
cheekbones, eyes the color of moss in sunlight, and hair that was a
cascade of autumn copper. She was, objectively, the most beautiful thing
Leon had ever seen, surpassing even the prize-winning pumpkin from the
County Fair three years ago.

Leon opened his mouth. "Uhh," he said.

The woman raised an eyebrow. She stepped inside, her boots making no
sound on the grit-covered floor. "I need a repair on a bracer. The clasp
snapped during a skirmish."

She held out her arm. The leather bracer was exquisite work, likely
Level 15 or higher gear, but the silver buckle was twisted.

"Master Gondar is... indisposed," Leon managed to say, gesturing vaguely
toward the groaning dwarf outside. He felt heat rising in his neck. He
wiped his hands on his apron, suddenly conscious of the dirt under his
fingernails and the patch on his left knee.

The woman looked at Gondar, then back at Leon. Her gaze was sharp,
analyzing him. "Are you his apprentice?"

"No," Leon said quickly. "I'm... I'm a farmer. Leon."

"A farmer," she repeated, her tone neutral. "I see. Well, perhaps I
should wait for him to recover."

She leaned against a workbench, crossing her arms. She clearly intended
to wait.

Leon swallowed hard. *Don't look at her,* he told himself. *She's way
out of your league. She's probably a Countess or a high-level Ranger.
Just fix the plow.*

He turned back to the fire. He took a deep breath, closing his eyes for
a second to center himself.

*The fire is the heart,* he recited silently. *The iron is the blood.*

He picked up the tongs and thrust the broken plowshare into the coals.

As the heat took hold, Leon's nervousness evaporated. The presence of
the beautiful woman faded into the background. There was only the heat,
the metal, and the rhythm.

He waited until the iron was a bright, cherry red---the color of a
setting sun. Not white (too hot, burns the carbon), not orange (too
cold, cracks the grain).

He pulled the metal out and placed it on the anvil.

He picked up the four-pound cross-peen hammer. To a normal man, it was a
heavy tool. In Leon's hand, it felt weightless.

Clang.

He struck. It wasn't a wild bash. It was a precise, controlled impact
that sent a shockwave through the metal, forcing the molecules to align.

Clang. Clang. Clang.

He fell into the trance. He wasn't using a System Skill. When a
Blacksmith used \[Repair\], the System just consumed Mana and magically
fused the metal back together. It was fast, but it left the metal
"dumb." It fixed the break, but it didn't improve the steel.

Leon was *folding* the metal. He hammered the crack shut, then folded
the edge over, hammering it flat again. He was cycling his Ki with every
swing.

*Inhale (lift hammer).* *Exhale (strike).*

The Ki flowed from his core, down his shoulder, through the hickory
handle, and into the red-hot iron. He was pushing the impurities out.
Tiny sparks of slag flew like fireflies, fizzling out on his leather
apron.

He worked the bellows again, heating the metal, then returned to the
anvil. He was sweating now, his tunic clinging to his back, the muscles
of his forearms corded like steel cables.

He wasn't just fixing a plow. He was convincing the metal that it wanted
to be whole again. He was treating the iron with the same patience he
used to coax a seedling out of hard soil.

Lyra watched from the corner, her boredom rapidly turning into
confusion, and then fascination.

She was a Level 18 Ranger, a pathfinder for the Silver-Leaf Guild. She
knew artisans. She had seen Grandmaster Smiths in the capital forge
enchanted blades.

But she had never seen this.

Normally, smithing was a flash of blue light. The crafter would tap the
item, a progress bar would fill, and the item would be done.

This farmer... he was doing it the hard way. The *ancient* way.

But it was his movement that caught her eye. As a Ranger, she valued
economy of motion. Leon didn't waste a millimeter. His hammer strikes
were perfectly consistent. He moved around the anvil with a footwork
that reminded her less of a laborer and more of a monk practicing a
kata.

She engaged her \[Mana Sight\].

The world turned grey, lit by currents of energy.

Usually, low-level civilians had a dim, flickering aura. Leon's core
was... dense. It was a solid, throbbing sphere of white light in his
chest. And as he struck the metal, she saw wisps of that light leaking
into the iron.

*He's infusing it,* she realized, her eyes widening. *He's not using
Mana. He's using pure Life Essence.*

He wasn't enchanting the plowshare. He was *reinforcing* it at a
molecular level. That piece of scrap iron was becoming harder than
castle-forged steel right in front of her eyes.

Leon plunged the metal into the oil trough.

HISS.

A massive cloud of white steam erupted, smelling of burnt oil and hot
iron.

Leon held it there, counting the seconds by the beat of his heart,
waiting for the temper to set. He pulled it out, dripping, and wiped it
with a rag.

The plowshare was dark grey, with a subtle, rippled pattern in the
steel---grain lines that looked like flowing water. It wasn't magical,
but it looked... unbreakable.

Leon exhaled a long breath, wiping the sweat from his forehead with his
forearm. He turned, blinking, as if surprised to find he wasn't alone.

He saw Lyra staring at him.

The panic returned instantly. "Oh. Um. Sorry about the smoke," Leon
stammered, holding the plowshare awkwardly. "I... I get carried away."

Lyra pushed off the workbench and walked toward him. She moved into his
personal space, looking closely at the plowshare.

"May I?" she asked.

"Be careful, it's still warm," Leon warned.

She touched the metal. It hummed slightly under her fingertips,
resonating with the Ki he had poured into it.

"You folded the steel," she said, looking up at him. Her eyes were very
green, and very intense. "That creates a laminar structure. It increases
durability by 300% compared to a standard cast repair."

Leon scratched the back of his neck. "Does it? Grandpa just said it
makes the blade slide through the clay better. Sticky clay is a real
nuisance in the north field."

Lyra stared at him. "You did this to cut clay better?"

"Well, yes. And rocks," Leon added earnestly. "If you hit a rock with a
brittle blade, it snaps. This way, the blade just rings like a bell, and
the rock moves."

Lyra looked at the simple farm tool as if it were a legendary artifact.
"You have incredible control. Your hammer strikes... I've never seen a
rhythm like that. It was like you were breathing life into the metal."

Leon blushed so hard his ears turned red. "I was just trying to keep the
heat even. The bellows are a bit sticky on the intake."

She was complimenting his *technique*, calling it 'breathing life.' He
assumed she was being polite about his sweating.

"I have a broken buckle," Lyra said softly, holding out her arm again.
"Would you fix it? I'll pay you."

Leon looked at the delicate silver clasp on her bracer. It was fine
jewelry work, not heavy iron.

"I... I'm not a silversmith, Miss," Leon said apologetically. "I don't
have the small tools. I'd probably crush it with these hammers." He held
up his heavy cross-peen hammer to demonstrate. "This is for beating
iron, not whispering to silver."

"I trust you," Lyra said.

"I really shouldn't," Leon insisted. He glanced at the sun outside. "And
I have to get back. The cows need milking by five, or they get cranky.
Bessie holds a grudge."

He grabbed his plowshare and started backing away. "Master Gondar should
be sober in an hour. He's very good with silver. Just... don't shout."

Leon grabbed the handle of his cart, gave her a quick, shy nod, and
practically fled the shop, dragging the cart behind him.

Lyra stood in the quiet smithy, the smell of ozone and honest sweat
lingering in the air. She looked at the door where the "Level One
Farmer" had disappeared.

"Bessie," she murmured to herself, testing the name. "Who is Bessie?"

She frowned, feeling a strange prickle of irritation. Was this Bessie
the reason he rushed off?

Outside, Gondar let out a loud belch.

Lyra looked down at the dwarf. "Master Gondar. That boy. Who taught
him?"

Gondar cracked one eye open. "Leon? Nobody taught him. That's the
problem. He learns the wrong way to do everything, then practices it
until it works better than the right way."

Lyra looked back at the empty road. A smile, small and intrigued, tugged
at the corner of her mouth.

"Interesting," she whispered.

Lyra reached into a pouch on her belt---one made of silk rather than
leather, where she kept her alchemy supplies. She produced a small,
corked vial filled with a shimmering, effervescent yellow liquid.

"Alchemist's Sober-Up," she said, holding it out with a dazzling,
persuasive smile. "It contains ground ginger, electrolytes, and a mild
trace of Mana to flush toxins. It works instantly."

Gondar eyed the vial like a drowning man eyeing a life raft. He snatched
it, uncorked it with his teeth, and downed it in one gulp.

He shuddered, his beard vibrating. Then, he let out a long, satisfied
sigh. The redness in his eyes faded, and his hands stopped trembling.

"Bless you, lass," Gondar grunted, wiping his mouth. "That hits the spot
better than a cleric's slap. Alright, let's see that buckle."

He took Lyra's arm. His movements were professional now, his thick
fingers manipulating the delicate silver with surprising grace. He
pulled a small jewelry hammer from his belt---a stark contrast to the
monster Leon had been wielding.

"So," Lyra said casually, leaning her hip against the anvil. "Leon."

Gondar chuckled, tapping a tiny pin back into place. "I saw you watching
him. You and every other girl who needs a horseshoe fixed. Don't waste
your time, Ranger. The boy is dense as a bag of wet cement."

"I'm just curious," Lyra lied smoothly. "He has... unusual mana density
for a civilian. And his technique is archaic. Where did he learn to
cycle Ki like that?"

"Grandpa," Gondar muttered. "Old Man Silas. Retired Sergeant. Drill
instructor type. He taught the boy that if you're going to breathe, you
might as well do it efficiently. Leon took it to heart. He does
everything with 'intent.' Sweeping the floor, churning butter, hammering
iron. It's all training to him, even if he doesn't call it that."

Lyra watched Gondar work. "And he's really Level One?"

"Level One Leon," Gondar confirmed. "L.O.L. The town joke. He won't kill
anything. Not a goblin, not a wolf, not even a glitched slime. He says
all life has a purpose." Gondar snorted. "Though, if you ask me, the
purpose of a goblin is to die on the end of a spear. But Leon... he just
relocates them. Caught a wolf last winter that was eating his sheep.
Wrestled it down, tied its muzzle, and walked it twenty miles into the
deep woods to release it."

Lyra raised an eyebrow. "He wrestled a wolf? At Level One?"

"Like I said," Gondar tapped the buckle one last time. "He does things
the hard way. Done."

He released her arm. The buckle was perfect, snapping shut with a
satisfying click.

Lyra flexed her wrist. "Excellent work, Master Gondar." She placed three
silver coins on the anvil. "One last question. This... Bessie. The one
he rushed off for?"

Gondar looked at her, and a grin split his beard. He started to
laugh---a deep, belly-shaking sound.

"Bessie?" Gondar wheezed. "Oh, you don't have to worry about her, lass.
Unless you're afraid of udder rot."

Lyra blinked. "Udder rot?"

"Bessie is a cow," Gondar explained, wiping a tear from his eye. "A
Holstein, I believe. Leon loves that cow more than he loves breathing.
If she's late for milking, she bellows loud enough to wake the dead, and
Leon can't stand to see her upset."

Lyra stood there for a moment, processing this. The "other woman" she
had felt a micro-second of jealousy toward was a cow.

A genuine smile broke across her face---not the practiced, charming
smile of a Guild negotiator, but a real, amused grin.

"A cow," she repeated. "Of course."

"He's a simple lad," Gondar said, pocketing the coins. "But he's got a
foundation like bedrock. If he ever decided to actually *play the
game*---to kill, to level up---he'd be terrifying. But he won't. So he's
just... Leon."

Lyra adjusted her bracer. "The Silver-Leaf Guild is looking for scouts
to map the northern sector. We've had reports of... odd energy spikes
near the foothills. Undead signatures."

Gondar stopped smiling. "Undead? That's close to the farmlands."

"Very close," Lyra said, her voice dropping to a professional tone. "If
a Dungeon is spawning, it could be trouble. I was going to scout it
solo, but..." She looked at the door where Leon had exited. "I might
need a local guide. Someone who knows the woods. Someone who can wrestle
a wolf."

"Leon knows the woods," Gondar admitted. "But he won't fight for you."

"I don't need him to fight," Lyra said, turning to leave. She adjusted
her bow, her green eyes glinting with a new plan. "I just need him to
help me... clear some weeds."

"Don't break him, Ranger," Gondar warned, picking up his hammer. "He's
too nice for the likes of you adventurers."

"I have no intention of breaking him, Master Gondar," Lyra called back
over her shoulder. "I just want to see what happens when that bedrock
finally shifts."

She stepped out into the bright afternoon sun. The town was noisy, but
she ignored it. She looked north, toward the rolling hills where a
certain farmhouse---and a cow named Bessie---resided.

"Level One," she whispered to herself. "We'll see about that."
