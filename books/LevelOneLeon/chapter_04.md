# Chapter Four: The Stealth Tea Party

The sun was beginning to dip toward the western hills, painting the sky
in strokes of bruised purple and burnt orange. It was the "Golden Hour,"
a time when photographers and poets rejoiced.

For Lyra, it was "High Visibility Hour," which was annoying.

She was currently prone in a patch of tall fescue grass about three
hundred yards from the farmhouse. Her Guild cloak, enchanted with a
\[Chameleon Weave\], rippled to match the swaying green stalks. She had
activated \[Skill: Shadow Breath\], lowering her heart rate to ten beats
per minute and masking her body heat.

To the naked eye, she was invisible. To a magical sensor, she was a
void.

She brought her spyglass to her eye.

"Target acquired," she whispered to herself.

Leon was in the vegetable garden. He was hoeing.

Lyra frowned. *He's been hoeing for three hours.*

She watched him. The motion was hypnotic. *Lift. Drop. Drag.* There was
no wasted energy. Every time the hoe struck the earth, she saw a faint,
unintentional pulse of Ki travel from his feet, through his hips, and
into the ground.

*He's tilling the soil with his internal energy,* Lyra realized,
stunned. *He's aerating the earth a foot deep without even digging.*

She shifted her weight slightly. A dry twig snapped under her elbow. The
sound was microscopic---a tiny *click* that shouldn't have been audible
over the wind and the rustling leaves.

Three hundred yards away, Leon stopped hoeing.

He didn't look around wildly. He didn't cast a detection spell. He
simply paused, tilted his head slightly to the left---exactly where she
was hiding---and frowned. Then, he shrugged and went back to work.

"Coincidence," Lyra muttered. "He couldn't have heard that."

She waited another twenty minutes. The shadows lengthened. She prepared
to move closer, planning to circle around to the barn to inspect the
livestock (and verify the existence of this 'Bessie').

Leon suddenly stopped again. He stuck the hoe into the ground, wiped his
hands on his trousers, and turned. He walked purposefully toward the
farmhouse, disappearing inside.

*Opportunity,* Lyra thought. *He's gone for water. I can move up.*

She crawled forward, moving like a viper. She reached the edge of the
garden fence, pressing herself against the wooden posts. She held her
breath, listening.

The back door of the house creaked open.

Lyra tensed, hand drifting to the dagger at her belt.

"You can come out now," Leon's voice called out. It wasn't a shout; it
was a conversational tone, carried on the wind. "The grass is full of
chiggers this time of year. You'll be itching for a week."

Lyra froze. *He's bluffing.*

"I made biscuits," the voice added. "And there's fresh mint tea. I put
honey in it."

Lyra hesitated. She looked down at her invisible cloak. She checked her
\[Stealth\] status icon. It was still active.

She stood up slowly, deactivating the cloak. The shimmering camouflage
faded, revealing her copper hair and green leather armor.

Leon was standing on the porch. He wasn't holding a weapon. He was
holding a wooden tray with a steaming teapot, two earthenware mugs, and
a plate of biscuits that smelled like heaven and butter.

"How?" Lyra asked, walking out of the tall grass. She didn't sound like
a cool Ranger; she sounded like an offended professional. "I have a
Agility of 45. My stealth skill is Expert Rank. I masked my thermal
signature."

Leon set the tray down on a small patio table. "The blue jays," he said
simply.

"The... birds?"

"Blue jays are nosy," Leon explained, pouring the tea. "They scream at
everything. Cats, foxes, me. But for the last hour, the ones in the
south pasture have been dead silent. They only do that when they're
watching a predator they can't figure out. Plus, the wind shifted. You
smell like ozone and leather polish."

Lyra stared at him. "You tracked me by the silence of birds and the
smell of my boots?"

"And the twig," Leon added, pushing a mug toward the empty chair.
"Sugar?"

Lyra walked up the steps and sat down, defeated. "Honey is fine."

She took the tea. It was perfect. Hot, soothing, and rich with
mana-infused mint. She looked at the biscuits. They were golden brown.

"You're weird, Leon," she said bluntly.

"Grandpa says I'm 'particular'," Leon corrected. He sat down, looking
uncomfortable in the presence of a guest, especially one wearing armor.
"So. Are you here to fix another buckle? Or is this about the 'weeds'
you mentioned to Master Gondar?"

Lyra set the cup down. "The weeds. The Guild has confirmed it. A
necrotic node has opened in the Whispering Woods, about four miles north
of here. It's an Undead Dungeon."

Leon winced. "That explains the sour taste in the water table. The creek
has been running sluggish."

"It's going to get worse," Lyra said. "Skeletons, Zombies, maybe a Ghoul
if we wait too long. I'm going in to clear it. I need a guide to get me
to the entrance without alerting the whole forest. Gondar says you know
the woods."

"I do," Leon nodded. "I forage for mushrooms there."

"I can pay you," Lyra said. "Ten gold pieces."

Leon's eyes widened. "Ten gold? That's enough to replace the roof on the
barn."

"Then you'll do it?"

Leon looked conflicted. "I don't... I don't like fighting, Miss Lyra.
Hitting things feels... rude. And loud."

"You won't have to fight," Lyra promised. "I'm a Ranger. I deal damage
from a distance. You just lead me there, carry the supplies, and maybe
watch my back. If a monster gets close, you just... push it away. Like
you did with that warrior."

Leon thought about it. He thought about the sour water in the creek. If
the water went bad, the grass would die. If the grass died, Bessie would
be sad.

"Okay," Leon said. "But we have to be back by sunset. I can't leave the
chickens out at night."

"Deal," Lyra said.

She watched him drink his tea. A question had been burning in her mind
since the smithy.

"Leon," she asked softly. "Can I see your Status? Just the first page."

It was a rude question in adventurer culture, but Leon didn't seem to
mind. He shrugged. "Sure. It's pretty boring though. The lights keep
blinking."

"Blinking?"

Leon waved his hand. "System. Open."

The blue screen appeared. Leon tapped a permission setting, allowing
Lyra to view it.

Lyra leaned in.

**\[Name: Leon\]** **\[Level: 1\]** **\[Experience: 99,999 / 100
(OVERFLOW ERROR)\]**

Lyra choked on her biscuit. "Leon. What is that?"

"The error?" Leon pointed at the flashing red text. "Yeah, it's been
doing that for years. Every time I harvest a bumper crop or finish a big
project, that number goes up, but the Level stays at 1."

"You have... ninety-nine thousand experience," Lyra whispered. "That's
enough to reach Level 20 instantly. Why haven't you clicked it?"

"I tried once," Leon said, rubbing the back of his neck. "But when I try
to Level Up, it gives me a warning."

He tapped the \[Level Up\] button.

A jagged red box popped up, vibrating with aggressive text.

**\[SYSTEM ALERT\]** **\[Error: Soul Density Exceeds Vertical
Parameters.\]** **\[Subject Foundation is too heavy for Ascended
Mana.\]** **\[Cannot Level Up.\]**

**\[Alternative Solution Available:\]** **\[Convert Overflow Experience
to Foundation Density?\]** **\[YES / NO\]**

"See?" Leon said. "It wants to 'Convert to Density.' I don't really know
what that means. 'Level Up' sounds like floating away. I don't want to
float away. I like the ground. So I just leave it."

Lyra stared at the screen. Her mind was racing. The System was saying
his soul was too *heavy* to level up? That defied all magical theory.
Leveling up was supposed to expand the soul, not lift it. Unless...
unless he had cultivated his base so thoroughly that the System
literally couldn't stretch him out to the next level without breaking
him.

"Leon," Lyra said slowly. "If you press 'Yes'... I don't think you'll
float away. I think it just... packs the soil tighter."

Leon looked at her. "Like tamping a fence post?"

"Exactly. Like tamping a post so it never moves, no matter how hard the
wind blows."

Leon looked at the blinking red button. He looked at the 99,999 XP he
had earned from years of farming, crafting, and 'relocating' wolves.

"Well," Leon said. "I do like a sturdy fence."

He reached out and pressed **\[YES\]**.

The world didn't explode. There was no pillar of light. No choir of
angels.

Instead, a deep, resonant *THRUM* vibrated through the patio. The tea in
the mugs rippled. The house creaked. The air pressure dropped for a
split second, popping Lyra's ears.

The numbers on the screen drained away.

**\[Experience Converted.\]** **\[Foundation Density: +10%\]**
**\[Level: 1\]**

Leon blinked. He looked at his hands. "I don't feel different."

Lyra looked at him with \[Mana Sight\].

Before, he had been a bright light. Now? He looked like a black hole.
The mana wasn't radiating off him; it was orbiting him. He felt...
heavy. Like a mountain compressed into the shape of a man.

"System?" Leon asked. "Am I still Level One?"

\[Level: 1\]

"Good," Leon smiled, relieved. "I was worried I'd have to buy new
clothes."

He stood up, picking up the tray. "I'll go pack some rope and a shovel.
We can head out in the morning."

Lyra sat on the porch, staring at the empty space where the "Level One"
farmer had been. She took a sip of her tea. Her hand was shaking, just a
little.

"I am going to guide a monster into a dungeon," she whispered to the
sunset. "And he's bringing a shovel."
