# Chapter Five: The Stick of Walnut

The morning sun was struggling to pierce the mist that clung to the
valley floor. It was a "soup day," as Grandma would call it---damp,
grey, and clinging to the bones.

Leon stood in the mudroom of the farmhouse, staring at the corner behind
the umbrella stand.

There, leaning against the wall, was *The Staff*.

It wasn't a magical artifact. It didn't glow. It was a six-foot length
of Black Walnut, perfectly straight, about as thick as Leon's wrist.
Grandpa had cut it twenty years ago, cured it in the smokehouse for
three years, and then sanded it until it felt like glass.

Grandpa called it a *Bo*. Leon called it "The Stick."

For ten years, Grandpa had made Leon practice with it every evening
before supper. *"Sweep the sky, Leon! Block the rain! Thrust like you're
poking a sleeping bear!"*

Leon had done it, mostly to humor the old man. He had twirled it, thrust
it, and swung it until the wood felt like an extension of his own arm.
But as a farm tool? It was useless. You couldn't attach a hoe head to it
(the wood was too hard to drill). It was too smooth to use as a fence
post. It was just... a stick.

Leon frowned at it.

"I shouldn't need it," he muttered. "I have the shovel."

But then he thought about what Lyra had said. *Undead.*

Undead things were rotting. They were teeming with bacteria, mold, and
grave-soil parasites. If he touched a zombie with his bare hands to move
it out of the way, he'd have to scrub with lye soap for an hour to get
the grime off. And if one of them tried to bite him? He wasn't worried
about the damage---his skin was tough enough to break a mosquito's
beak---but the *germs*.

And Lyra... she was wearing leather. It looked sturdy, but it had gaps
at the joints.

Leon hesitated. He reached out and wrapped his hand around the dark
wood.

It was heavy. Denser than it should be. Over the years, Leon had
unconsciously poured his excess Ki into the wood during his practice
sessions, just like he did with his plow. The Black Walnut had absorbed
the essence, becoming hard as iron and heavy as lead.

"Better safe than sanitary," Leon decided.

He grabbed the staff, slung his canvas pack over his shoulder (filled
with rope, dried apples, and a small jar of antiseptic salve), and
stepped out onto the porch.

Lyra was waiting by the gate. She looked tense, her hand resting on the
pommel of a short sword at her hip, her bow already strung. She scanned
the tree line with professional paranoia.

She turned as Leon approached, her eyes dropping immediately to the
staff.

"You brought a weapon," she said, sounding relieved. "Is that an
enchanted quarterstaff? I don't recognize the wood."

"It's walnut," Leon said, tapping it against his boot. *Thud.* The sound
was deep, like a stone hitting the earth. "Grandpa made me practice with
it. I figured... if the undead are sticky, I can use this to push them
back without getting goop on my shirt."

Lyra stared at him. "Right. To avoid... goop."

"Ready?" Leon asked.

"Lead the way, Pathfinder."

The Whispering Woods lived up to their name. As soon as they crossed the
tree line, the ambient noise of the farm---the cows, the wind, the
distant creek---vanished. It was replaced by an oppressive silence,
broken only by the creaking of dead branches.

The air smelled wrong. It smelled like stagnant water and copper.

Leon frowned, stopping to inspect a bush.

"Leaf blight," he noted, touching a blackened, curled leaf. "The
necrotic energy is choking the vascular system of the plants. This is
going to take months to compost back to health."

"Leon, eyes up," Lyra whispered. She was moving in a crouch, her steps
silent. "We're in the aggro range."

"The what?"

"The aggression range. Monsters will---"

CRACK.

The sound of snapping wood echoed through the clearing.

Lyra spun around, drawing an arrow in a blur of motion. "Contact! Three
o'clock!"

From the dense underbrush, a figure stumbled out. It was a Skeleton,
bleached white and wearing the rusted remnants of chainmail. It clutched
a jagged, notched sword in one bony hand.

It saw them. It opened its jaw in a silent scream and charged.

"Skeleton Warrior. Level 4," Lyra announced, sighting down the shaft of
her arrow. "I'll aim for the skull, that's the crit spot---"

"Wait," Leon said, stepping in front of her.

"Leon! Move! It has a sword!"

Leon ignored her. He watched the skeleton running toward them. It was
clumsy. Its gait was uneven---the left femur was slightly shorter than
the right.

"It's just bones," Leon said calmly. "Don't waste an arrow. Arrows are
expensive."

The skeleton lunged, swinging the rusty sword in a wild, overhead arc.

Leon didn't dodge. He didn't cower. He simply... planted his feet.

He held the staff loosely in his right hand. As the sword came down,
Leon flicked his wrist. It was a motion he used to knock unripe apples
from the high branches without bruising them.

Click.

The tip of the staff tapped the skeleton's sword arm.

It wasn't a hard hit. It looked like a gentle reprimand.

But the **Foundation Density** behind that tap was equivalent to a
falling anvil.

The skeleton's radius and ulna didn't just break; they vaporized. The
rusty sword went flying into the bushes.

The skeleton paused, looking at its missing arm stump. The red pinpricks
of light in its eye sockets seemed to flicker in confusion.

Leon didn't stop. He stepped forward, sliding his hand up the staff, and
used the bottom end to perform a sweep. It was the same motion he used
to clear dead leaves off the porch.

Swish.

The staff connected with the skeleton's ankles.

The skeleton flipped horizontally in the air, spinning like a tossed
coin, and slammed into the dirt face-first.

Leon walked over to it. The skeleton tried to push itself up with its
remaining arm.

"Stay down," Leon said firmly.

He placed the tip of the staff on the center of the skeleton's
spine---right between the shoulder blades---and applied a little
pressure.

Crunch.

The spine shattered. The necrotic magic holding the bones together
disrupted instantly. The skeleton collapsed into a pile of disjointed
calcium. The red lights in its eyes faded.

**\[System Notification: You have defeated Level 4 Skeleton Warrior.\]**
**\[XP: 40\]** **\[Experience Converted to Density.\]**

Leon sighed, looking at the pile of bones. "Poor thing. Probably a
soldier once. Leaving bones out like this is disrespectful. We should
bury it later."

He turned back to Lyra.

Lyra's bow was lowered. Her mouth was slightly open. She looked from
Leon to the pile of dust, then back to Leon.

"You..." she started, then stopped. She shook her head. "You just...
tapped it."

"It was off-balance," Leon explained, wiping the tip of the staff on a
patch of grass to clean it. "No leverage. Like a scarecrow in a
windstorm."

"Leon," Lyra said, her voice strained. "That was a Level 4 mob. It has
damage resistance against blunt weapons. You turned its arm into
powder."

"Dry rot," Leon said confidently. "The bones were old. Very brittle. You
have to be careful with calcium deficiency."

Lyra pinched the bridge of her nose. "Calcium deficiency. Of course."

"We should keep moving," Leon said, pointing with the staff deeper into
the woods. "The smell is getting stronger up ahead. Smells like... old
cabbage and dark magic."

He started walking, using the staff as a walking stick again.

Lyra holstered her arrow. She watched his back---the broad shoulders,
the patched tunic, the terrifyingly heavy stick of walnut.

"I'm being escorted by a raid boss," she whispered to herself. "I'm
being escorted by a raid boss who thinks he's a gardener."

She hurried to catch up. "Leon! Wait up! And... nice stick."

Leon smiled over his shoulder. "Thanks. Grandpa made it."
