# Chapter Six: The Weight of the Bear

The forest had stopped whispering and started groaning.

The trees here were grey and twisted, their roots bulging from the earth
like varicose veins. The mist was thick enough to taste---a metallic,
copper tang that coated the back of the throat.

"Formation," Lyra snapped.

She slid into a crouch behind a rotted stump, her bow drawn. Leon stood
five paces to her left, guarding her flank. He held the walnut staff
across his body, his eyes scanning the swirling grey fog.

"Here they come," Lyra whispered.

It wasn't a patrol this time. It was a swarm.

From the gloom, they shambled. Zombies. Not fresh ones, but desiccated
husks that had likely clawed their way out of shallow graves. There were
at least twenty of them, moaning a discordant chorus of hunger.

"Standard suppression," Lyra ordered. "I'll thin the herd. You keep them
off me."

"Understood," Leon said. He tightened his grip on the staff. "No biting
allowed."

Lyra let loose.

Thwip. Thwip. Thwip.

She was a whirlwind of efficiency. Her hands moved so fast they were a
blur. Every arrow found a mark---an eye socket, a throat, a knee. She
was using \[Rapid Fire\], a skill that allowed her to loose three arrows
in the time it took to draw a breath.

The zombies fell in waves, but more replaced them. They were mindless,
driven by the dungeon's necrotic pulse.

"Back!" Leon grunted.

Three zombies lunged at him. Leon didn't strike to kill; he struck to
control. He thrust the staff forward, catching the center zombie in the
chest, pushing it backward into its fellows. They tangled together, a
heap of rotting limbs.

Sweep. Tap. Shove.

Leon was a wall. He treated the undead mob like a herd of stubborn
cattle trying to break through a fence. He didn't use flashy spins. He
used geometry. He kept the staff moving, creating a perimeter that
nothing could cross.

"Reloading!" Lyra shouted, reaching for a fresh quiver.

"Take your time!" Leon called back, sweating slightly. "They're
persistent!"

He swung the staff low, tripping a ghoul that tried to flank them. He
was holding his own, but he could feel the strain. His muscles burned.
His *cultivated* strength was high, but his System Stats were still
Level 1. His stamina bar was blinking yellow. He was tired.

And then, the ground shook.

BOOM. BOOM.

Heavy footsteps echoed from the right---Lyra's blind spot.

Leon turned. Through the mist, a massive shape emerged.

It was a bear. Or, it had been. It stood seven feet tall at the
shoulder. Its fur was matted with black ichor, and half its face was
missing, revealing the skull beneath. But the worst part was the green
fire burning in its remaining eye.

\[Plague Bear - Level 12 (Elite)\]

"Lyra!" Leon shouted. "Big one! Right flank!"

"I'm pinned!" Lyra yelled back. She was firing point-blank into a
cluster of skeletons. She couldn't turn.

The bear roared---a wet, gurgling sound---and charged.

It wasn't clumsy like the skeletons. It was fast. It moved with the
terrifying speed of a apex predator.

Leon stepped out of the defensive line. He had to intercept it before it
reached Lyra.

"Hey!" Leon shouted, banging his staff on a rock. "Over here!"

The bear swiveled its massive head. It saw Leon. It didn't care that he
was Level 1. It just saw meat.

It swiped.

Leon brought the staff up to block. *Iron Gate Stance.*

CRACK.

The impact was like being hit by a falling tree.

The bear's paw slammed into the walnut staff. The wood held---Grandpa's
curing process was miraculous---but Leon was thrown backward. His boots
skidded through the mud, digging two deep trenches.

He slammed his back against a tree, the air leaving his lungs in a
painful *whoosh*.

"Oof."

Before he could recover, the bear was on him. It reared up, towering
over him, and brought both claws down.

Leon rolled.

Ssssshhhht.

The claws missed his chest but caught his left shoulder. The fabric of
his tunic shredded instantly. Three jagged lines of red appeared on his
skin, burning like fire.

\[Warning: Health Critical. Bleeding Effect Applied.\]

Leon gritted his teeth. The pain was sharp, real, and grounding.

He scrambled to his feet, breathing hard. His left arm hung heavy, blood
dripping onto the dead leaves.

The bear turned, ready to finish him.

Leon looked at the bear. He didn't see a monster. He saw an animal that
was trapped. It was suffering. The necrotic magic was forcing its
muscles to move long after they should have rested.

"You're tired," Leon whispered, his heart hammering against his ribs. "I
know. It hurts."

The bear lunged again.

Leon knew he couldn't stop it with his Level 1 strength. The System
wouldn't let him. His muscles were capped at 5.

*Then I won't use muscle,* Leon thought.

He closed his eyes for a split second. He visualized the "Lake" in his
core. The density he had built for ten years. The "Overflow" he had just
packed down.

He grabbed the valve on that energy and *twisted*.

BURN.

It felt like swallowing a coal. Heat exploded in his gut, rushing
through his meridians. It was too much power for his un-leveled
channels. He felt his veins bulging, the microscopic vessels tearing
under the pressure.

\[System Alert: Ki Overload. Durability failing.\]

Leon ignored the warning. He opened his eyes. They weren't brown
anymore. For a second, they were white-hot.

The bear swung.

Leon didn't block. He stepped *into* the guard.

"Rest," Leon grunted.

He drove the end of the staff into the ground, pivoting around it, and
delivered a palm strike to the bear's chest.

It wasn't a punch. It was a release of pressure.

BOOM.

The sound was like a cannon shot.

The Ki blasted out of Leon's hand, bypassing the bear's rotting hide and
detonating inside its ribcage. The force was so dense it created a
vacuum.

The massive, thousand-pound beast was lifted off its feet. It flew
backward, crashing into a thick oak tree with enough force to shake the
remaining leaves from the branches.

The bear slid down the trunk. The green fire in its eye flickered and
went out. It didn't move again.

Leon stood there, his arm extended, steam rising from his skin.

He exhaled, and the steam vanished. He stumbled, catching himself on the
staff. His vision swam.

"Leon!"

Lyra was there. The zombies were dead, littered around the clearing like
ragdolls. She slid to a halt next to him, her eyes wide with panic.

"I'm okay," Leon wheezed. He clutched his bleeding shoulder. "Just... a
scratch. Tetanus shot needed."

Lyra looked at the dead bear. She looked at the crater in the bear's
chest. Then she looked at the blood soaking Leon's sleeve.

"You tanked an Elite," she said, her voice trembling slightly. "And you
took aggro off me."

She quickly pulled a potion from her belt---a high-grade red liquid.
"Drink this. Now."

Leon shook his head weakly. "That's a Greater Healing Potion. Those cost
five gold. Just put some moss on it."

"Drink the damn potion, Leon!" Lyra uncorked it and forced it into his
hand.

Leon drank it. It tasted like cherries and static electricity. The
bleeding stopped instantly. The jagged cuts on his shoulder knit
together, leaving behind faint pink scars.

He slumped against the tree, sliding down until he was sitting in the
dirt.

"Sorry," Leon muttered. "I let it get too close. I got sloppy."

Lyra stared at him. She looked at the bear again---a Level 12 Elite that
usually required a full party to take down. Leon had soloed it with a
stick and a palm strike, and he was apologizing for getting scratched.

"You're an idiot," Lyra said, but there was no bite in her voice. She
knelt beside him, checking his bandages. "A brave, incredibly strong,
complete idiot."

"Is the bear... at peace?" Leon asked quietly.

Lyra looked at the corpse. The menacing aura was gone. It just looked
like a pile of fur and bones now.

"Yes," Lyra said softly. "You freed it."

Leon nodded, his eyes drooping. "Good. That's good."

"We need to move," Lyra said, offering him a hand. "The Dungeon entrance
is close. And if that was the guard dog... I don't want to meet the
owner."

Leon took her hand. His grip was firm, despite his exhaustion. He pulled
himself up, using the staff for support.

"Right," Leon said. "Let's finish the weeding."

Leon knelt in the mud. The adrenaline from the \[Ki Overload\] was
fading, leaving behind a deep, aching throb in his meridians, like he
had swallowed hot gravel. But he ignored it.

He placed his hand on the bear's remaining patch of uncorrupted fur near
the shoulder. The beast was still warm, though the unnatural heat of the
necrosis was rapidly cooling into the chill of true death.

"We should harvest it when we get back," Leon said softly, his voice
thick with regret. "It would be a shame to let such a magnificent
creature go to waste."

Lyra, who was busy retrieving her arrows from the skulls of the nearby
zombies, froze. She looked over at him.

"Harvest it?" she repeated, blinking. "Leon, it's a *Plague* Bear. It's
literally made of rot and hate. You can't eat that meat. It would kill
you before it hit your stomach."

"Not for eating," Leon corrected gently. He bowed his head, closing his
eyes. "Great Earth, accept this vessel. The struggle is over. Let the
strength of this brother return to the roots to feed the oaks. Ash to
ash, dust to loam."

He stayed there for a moment, breathing in silence, before looking back
up at Lyra with practical eyes.

"The meat is spoiled, yes," he agreed, standing up with a groan and
leaning heavily on his staff. "But the bones? Once boiled in lime and
crushed, that's high-grade phosphorus. The pumpkin patch would love it.
And the hide... if I scrub it with a strong lye solution and tan it with
oak bark, it would make a very durable rug for the mudroom. It's
waterproof."

Lyra stared at the gruesome, half-skeletal carcass. "You want to put
that... in your mudroom."

"It deserves to be useful," Leon said simply. "Leaving it here to be
eaten by maggots seems... dismissive. It fought hard. It deserves to
keep working."

Lyra opened her mouth to argue---to explain that adventurer culture
dictated burning such abominations to ash---but she stopped. She looked
at Leon's face. He wasn't being greedy. He was grieving. In his world,
usage was a form of honor. To be useful was to be good.

"Fine," Lyra sighed, shaking her head. "If we survive the dungeon, you
can come back with your cart and... recycle the monster. But I am not
helping you skin it."

"Fair enough," Leon agreed. He pulled a piece of red chalk from his
pocket (usually used for marking timber) and drew a small 'X' on the
tree above the bear. "I'll find it later."

"You're bleeding again," Lyra noted, pointing to his shoulder.

The movement of bowing had reopened the wound slightly. A fresh trickle
of blood stained the bandage.

"It's fine," Leon lied. It wasn't fine. His arm felt like it was filled
with lead, and his core felt empty, like a well run dry. That single
palm strike had burned through a week's worth of cultivation.

"We're close," Lyra said, her voice dropping to a whisper. She pointed
through the trees.

Ahead of them, the mist swirled in a tight, unnatural vortex. The trees
peeled away to reveal a rocky outcropping that jutted from the earth
like a jagged tooth. At the base of the rock was a cave mouth.

It wasn't a natural cave. The darkness inside was absolute, swallowing
the light. A low, rhythmic thumping sound emanated from within---like
the heartbeat of a dying giant.

"The Node," Lyra confirmed. She checked her weapons. "That's where the
corruption is pumping from. We have to go in, find the Core, and shatter
it."

She looked at Leon. "You don't have to go inside. You've done enough.
You killed an Elite. You can guard the entrance."

Leon looked at the cave. He felt the sickness radiating from it. It felt
like the land had a fever.

"If I stay here," Leon said slowly, "and something happens to you
inside... who brings you back?"

Lyra hesitated.

"Besides," Leon added, gripping his walnut staff with his good hand.
"I've never seen a Dungeon Core. Is it like a weed root?"

"Sort of," Lyra admitted. "A big, magical, crystal weed root."

"Then it needs to be pulled," Leon said. "I'm coming."

Lyra looked at him---bruised, bleeding, Level One, and holding a stick.
She smiled, a sharp, fierce expression.

"Alright, Farmer. Stay behind me. And try not to overload your soul
again. I'm out of potions."

"No more explosions," Leon promised. "I'll be gentle."

Together, they stepped into the dark.
