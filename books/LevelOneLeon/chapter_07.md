# Chapter Seven: The Renewable Crop

The inside of the dungeon didn't look like a cave. It looked like the
inside of a ribcage.

The walls were lined with curved, white stone that resembled bone, and
the air was cold enough to see their breath. Glowing lichen clung to the
ceiling, casting a sickly pale-green light over the corridor.

Leon walked slightly behind Lyra, his staff tapping rhythmically against
the floor. He was moving slower than usual. The \[Ki Overload\] had left
him feeling hollowed out, like a gourd left in the sun too long.

"Watch your step," Lyra whispered. "Bone Caltrops on the floor."

Leon looked down. Sharp, jagged shards of bone were embedded in the
dirt.

"Messy," Leon muttered. He used the butt of his staff to crush a path
through them, grinding the sharp points into dust so they wouldn't
puncture his boots.

A skeleton shambled around the corner---a basic Level 2 grunt.

Before Lyra could draw, Leon stepped forward. He didn't use force this
time. He just hooked the skeleton's ankle with his staff and pulled. The
skeleton fell, and Leon stepped on its skull with a heavy boot.
*Crunch.*

**\[XP: 20\]** **\[Converted to Density.\]**

Lyra watched him. She watched the blue light of the XP notification
reflect in his eyes, and then vanish into the black hole of his core.

She slowed her pace, falling back to walk beside him.

"Leon," she said quietly. "I've been thinking."

"About the bear?" Leon asked, scanning the shadows.

"About you. And this place." She gestured to the glowing, necrotic
walls. "You said you wanted to pull the root. To destroy the Core."

"It's a weed," Leon said, shifting his grip on his staff. "It poisons
the water. You pull weeds."

"True," Lyra agreed. "But... some weeds are useful. Dandelions make tea.
Nettles can be made into soup or cloth."

Leon frowned, looking at her. "You want to make soup out of skeletons?"

"No," Lyra said quickly. "I want you to think about this dungeon
differently. Leon, look at what you just did. You destroyed that
skeleton. Did you feel bad?"

Leon paused. "No. It wasn't alive. It was just magic trapped in old
bones. Releasing it felt... correct. Like popping a blister."

"Exactly," Lyra said, her voice growing intense. "It's guilt-free. And
when you did it, you got that... 'Density' stuff. Experience."

She stopped walking, turning to face him.

"Leon, you've been Level One for ten years because you refuse to kill
living things. That's honorable. But it limits you. This place? It
spawns things that *aren't* alive. They are literally just magic and
dust."

Leon looked at the crushed skeleton on the floor.

"If we destroy the Core," Lyra explained, "the dungeon collapses. The
skeletons stop coming. The resource is gone."

"But if we leave it," Leon countered, "the corruption spreads. Bessie
gets sick."

"Not if we *manage* it," Lyra pressed. "We don't destroy the Core. We
prune it. We build a perimeter. We put up a Containment Ward at the
entrance---I have a scroll for one in my pack. It keeps the corruption
inside the cave, but keeps the Core active."

She pointed deeper into the tunnel.

"Think of it as a crop, Leon. A crop that plants itself every morning.
You come here, you 'harvest' the skeletons. You get the bone meal for
your farm. You get the 'Density' for your foundation. And you keep the
region safe by keeping the population down."

Leon's eyes widened slightly. He looked at the dungeon walls. He didn't
see a terrifying monster lair anymore.

"A renewable resource," Leon murmured. "Like coppicing a willow tree for
firewood."

"Exactly," Lyra smiled. "You don't kill the tree. You just cut the
branches. And in return, you get stronger. Much stronger."

She looked at him, her expression serious. "I've seen the Guild reports,
Leon. The Kingdom is getting unstable. There are rumors of war in the
South. Oakhaven is a quiet town, but it won't stay that way forever. If
trouble comes... a Level One Farmer might not be enough. But a Farmer
with the density of a mountain?"

Leon looked down at his hands. He thought about the arrogant warrior in
the market. He thought about the bear that had almost killed Lyra. He
thought about his Grandparents, who were getting too old to run if
soldiers came.

"I need to be sturdier," Leon admitted softly.

"Then let's not kill the dungeon," Lyra said. "Let's turn it into your
private field."

Leon nodded slowly. "Okay. We prune it. But we have to make sure the
Containment Ward is tight. I won't have stray zombies walking into the
cabbage patch."

"Deal," Lyra said.

They reached the end of the corridor. The tunnel opened up into a large,
circular cavern.

In the center of the room, hovering three feet off the ground, was the
Core. It was a jagged crystal the size of a pumpkin, pulsing with a
deep, violet light.

But standing between them and the Core was the Guardian.

It wasn't a skeleton. It was a Construct.

It stood eight feet tall, made of disparate bones fused together with
dark sludge. It had four arms, each holding a different rusted
weapon---a spear, an axe, and two swords. Its head was a horse skull,
giving it a nightmarish, elongated profile.

\[Bone Construct Guardian - Level 15\]

"Okay," Lyra whispered, drawing her bow. "New plan. We don't smash the
crystal. We just dismantle the scarecrow."

Leon tightened his grip on the walnut staff. He felt the ache in his
shoulder, but he pushed it down.

"Dismantle," Leon repeated. "I can do that."

"I'll take the arms," Lyra said, nocking an arrow. "You take the legs.
If it falls, it's done."

"Right," Leon said. He took a deep breath, cycling the last dregs of his
Ki. "Harvest time."

The Bone Construct saw them. The horse skull shrieked---a sound of
grinding bone---and it charged.

Leon didn't wait. He moved forward, not with the explosive speed of the
bear fight, but with the steady, plodding rhythm of a man who had a
field to clear before supper.

He wasn't fighting a monster. He was just breaking up clods of hard
earth.

Thump.

The staff hit the floor. Leon set his feet.

"Let's get to work," he said.
