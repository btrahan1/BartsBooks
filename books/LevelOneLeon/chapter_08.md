# Chapter Eight: The Pulse of the Earth

The Bone Construct was a whirlwind of rusted steel and ancient hate.

It spun its torso, swinging a notched greataxe and a spear in a lethal
horizontal arc. The reach was massive---ten feet of killing zone.

"Duck!" Lyra screamed, sliding underneath the swing on her knees, firing
an arrow upward into the Construct's ribcage.

Leon didn't duck. He didn't have the agility to slide like that.
Instead, he planted the walnut staff into a crack in the stone floor and
leaned his weight against it, creating a rigid angle.

Clang.

The haft of the Construct's spear slammed into the staff. The impact
vibrated through Leon's teeth, rattling his molars.

"Heavy," Leon grunted.

He pushed back. Usually, this was where the System stopped him. A
Strength of 5 was a hard wall. No matter how much he strained, the
System simply didn't allow his muscles to generate more force than the
parameters allowed. It was like hitting an invisible ceiling.

But as he looked past the flailing limbs of the Construct, his eyes
locked onto the Dungeon Core.

It was pulsing. *Thump-thump. Thump-thump.*

The violet light waxed and waned in perfect sync with the Construct's
movements. When the Core flared, the Construct swung faster. When the
Core dimmed, the Construct slowed.

*It's feeding it,* Leon realized.

He watched the light ripple through the crystal. It looked frantic. It
looked... scared.

*It's not a machine,* Leon thought, his boots sliding backward inch by
inch on the slick stone. *It's a heart. It's trying to protect itself.
Just like a badger cornered in a burrow.*

A wave of relief washed over him. Lyra was right. Destroying the Core
would have been wrong. It would have been killing a living thing---a
strange, magical, subterranean thing, but a life nonetheless.

"We're not going to kill you," Leon grunted at the Core, sweat stinging
his eyes. "We're just going to... trim your fingernails."

The Construct roared---a sound of grinding limestone---and brought its
third arm down. A rusted longsword aimed for Leon's head.

Leon had to move. He had to push the spear away to make room to dodge.

He dug deep. He didn't use the Ki Overload---he didn't have enough juice
left for an explosion. He just used good, honest effort. He pushed
against the "Ceiling" of his stats.

Move. Just move.

He felt something in his status window *creak*. It wasn't a sound; it
was a sensation in the back of his mind, like a rusty hinge being forced
open.

**\[System Alert: Foundation Density Exceeds Parameter Limits.\]**
**\[Stress Detected on Attribute Cap.\]** **\[Adjusting...\]**

Leon shoved.

The Construct's spear didn't just stop; it bounced back. Leon generated
force that shouldn't have been possible for a Level 1.

He saw the notification flicker in the corner of his eye.

\[Strength: 5 \> 6 (Override)\]

He didn't have time to process it. He used the opening. He pivoted the
staff, swinging the heavy walnut end toward the Construct's knee joint.

"Timber!" Leon shouted.

CRACK.

The sound was sickeningly loud. The Construct's right leg, made of
brittle femur bones, shattered under the impact of the density-laden
wood.

The Construct listed to the side, losing its balance.

"Lyra! The shoulder!" Leon called out.

Lyra didn't need telling twice. She had already drawn a \[Piercing
Arrow\]. The tip glowed with green mana.

She released. The arrow whistled through the air and slammed into the
joint where the horse-skull head met the torso.

The magic detonated. The connective sludge holding the Construct
together evaporated.

The massive pile of bones collapsed. The weapons clattered to the
floor---axe, spear, swords---creating a deafening cacophony of steel on
stone.

Silence fell over the cavern, save for the rhythmic *thump-thump* of the
Core.

Leon stood there, chest heaving. He leaned on his staff, staring at the
pile of debris.

"Is it... is it down?" Lyra asked, stepping cautiously toward the
remains, her bow still drawn.

"It's disassembled," Leon corrected. He nudged a loose rib bone with his
toe. "It won't be getting up until the Core has enough energy to knit it
back together. Probably twenty-four hours."

He turned to the Core. The violet light had dimmed, pulsing slowly now,
like an animal playing dead.

Leon walked up to it. He raised a hand.

"Leon, careful," Lyra warned. "Don't touch it."

Leon didn't touch it. He just held his hand near the warmth of the
crystal. "It's alive, Lyra. I can feel it. It was scared."

Lyra holstered her bow. She looked at the glowing rock. "It's a
semi-sentient magical node. I suppose 'alive' is a matter of
perspective."

"It's alive enough," Leon said firmly. "We did the right thing. We won't
kill it."

He stepped back, looking at the floating interface that was still
hovering in his peripheral vision.

**\[Name: Leon\]** **\[Level: 1\]** **\[Strength: 6\]** **\[Agility:
5\]** **\[Stamina: 5\]**

Leon blinked. The 'Limit Reached' tag next to his Strength was gone. The
number sat there, unadorned. It wasn't a massive jump---he wasn't
suddenly Level 50. But the wall had moved.

"Lyra," Leon asked quietly. "Can you lose a level cap without leveling
up?"

Lyra was busy pulling a roll of parchment from her bag---the Ward
Scroll. "What? No. That's impossible. Stats are hard-coded to your
level. Why?"

"Nothing," Leon said quickly, swiping the window away. "Just... feeling
a bit stronger. Must be the exercise."

*Maybe the bucket is finally stretching,* he thought with a private
smile.

Lyra unfurled the scroll. "Okay. This is a Barrier Ward. I'm going to
anchor it to the archway. It will keep the skeletons inside, but it
won't stop you from entering. You'll be the Keyholder."

She began to chant, tracing glowing runes in the air.

Leon watched her, then looked back at the "dead" Construct. He looked at
the rusted axe on the floor.

"Hey, Lyra?"

"Busy chanting," she muttered.

"That axe head," Leon pointed. "It's badly rusted, but the steel looks
like high-carbon stock. And that spear shaft... looks like seasoned
ash."

Lyra paused her chanting for a second to glare at him. "Leon. We are
securing a darkly magical threat to the kingdom."

"I know," Leon said, stooping down to pick up the rusty axe. It was
heavy, but it felt lighter than it had a minute ago. "But scrap metal is
expensive. And if I'm going to be managing this 'field,' I should
probably clean up the litter."

He shoved the axe head into his canvas bag.

"Waste not, want not," Leon whispered to the Dungeon Core.

The Core pulsed once---a soft, warm light. Leon took it as a thank you.
