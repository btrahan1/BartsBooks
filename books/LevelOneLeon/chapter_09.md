# Chapter Nine: The Morning Commute

The rooster crowed at 5:00 AM, sharp.

By 5:30, Leon had milked Bessie, fed the chickens, and greased the axles
of the handcart.

By 6:00, Lyra stumbled out onto the porch, rubbing sleep from her eyes.
She wasn't wearing her armor yet---just a linen tunic and trousers. She
looked less like a deadly Ranger and more like someone who deeply
regretted waking up before the sun.

"It's bright," she complained, shielding her eyes.

"It's morning," Leon said cheerfully. He was loading empty burlap sacks
and a pry bar into the cart. "Porridge is on the stove. Eat up. We have
a harvest to get to."

Lyra stared at the cart. "Leon. You're bringing the handcart."

"We left a lot of scrap metal yesterday," Leon explained. "And if the
Construct has respawned, that's a lot of bone meal. I can't carry it all
in my pockets."

Lyra sighed, accepting the mug of tea he offered. "I am the Silver-Leaf
Guild's most promising scout. I have mapped the peaks of the Dragon's
Spine. And today, I am going on a garbage run with a farmer."

"Recycling run," Leon corrected.

The containment ward Lyra had set up was doing its job perfectly.

When they arrived at the rocky outcropping in the Whispering Woods, the
mist had receded significantly. The trees near the entrance looked less
grey, and the oppressive smell of copper had faded to a dull background
note.

A translucent golden shimmer covered the cave mouth.

"Barrier is holding," Lyra noted, checking the runes. "Nothing came out
last night."

"Good," Leon said. He parked the cart outside the barrier. "Stay here,
Sal. Don't eat the necrotic moss."

Sal the mule flicked an ear, uninterested.

Leon grabbed his staff and a stack of sacks. He stepped through the
golden shimmer. It tingled, recognizing him as the Keyholder, and let
him pass.

Inside, the dungeon felt... different.

Yesterday, the air had been heavy with malice. Today, it felt expectant.
The violet light of the lichen seemed brighter, pulsing with a steady
rhythm.

"Do you feel that?" Lyra asked, nocking an arrow. "The ambient mana
density has increased. It feels... thicker."

"It had a good meal," Leon said, stepping over a patch of bone caltrops.
"We fed it a lot of energy yesterday when we broke those skeletons."

"That's not how Dungeons work, Leon. We *destroyed* its minions. It
should be weakened."

"Grandpa says if you prune an apple tree, it grows back stronger," Leon
replied. "You take the dead wood, and the roots go deeper."

As if to prove his point, three skeletons stepped out from the shadows.

They looked sturdier than yesterday's batch. Their bones were whiter,
less brittle. The rust on their swords was less flaky.

\[Skeleton Warrior - Level 5\]

"Level 5," Lyra warned. "They leveled up."

"Good," Leon said. "Better quality calcium."

The center skeleton drew a shortbow. It raised the weapon, the string
creaking taut.

"Shield!" Lyra shouted, realizing Leon had no cover.

The skeleton released. The arrow flew.

At a distance of twenty feet, a shortbow arrow travels at roughly 150
feet per second. For a normal human, it's a blur. For a Level 1 Farmer
with a capped Agility of 5, it is a mathematical certainty of being hit.

Leon didn't think about math. He thought about the horsefly that had
tried to bite his neck last summer.

He didn't panic. He just... tilted.

He shifted his weight to his left foot, dropping his shoulder and
twisting his torso in a fluid, minimalist motion.

Zip.

The arrow passed through the space where his collarbone had been a
fraction of a second ago. It nicked the fabric of his tunic, but didn't
touch skin.

Time seemed to stutter.

**\[System Alert: Evasion Calculation Failed.\]** **\[Subject Speed
Exceeds Agility Parameter.\]** **\[Refining Vessel Limits...\]**

\[Agility: 5 \> 6 (Override)\]

Leon straightened up, brought his staff around, and knocked the bow out
of the skeleton's hands with a *clack*.

"That was rude," Leon scolded the skeleton.

He followed up with a thrust to the sternum, shattering the spine. The
skeleton collapsed.

Lyra lowered her bow. She hadn't even fired.

"You dodged," she said flatly. "You side-stepped a point-blank arrow."

"It telegraphed the shot," Leon shrugged, moving to the next skeleton.
"It dipped its shoulder before releasing. Just like General Cluck does
before he pecks."

He dispatched the remaining two skeletons with efficient, practiced
sweeps of the staff. *Crack. Crunch. Dust.*

As the skeletons dissolved into piles of loose bone, the Dungeon Core
deep in the tunnel pulsed. A wave of warm air washed over them.

"See?" Leon said, kneeling to scoop the bones into a sack. "It likes it.
We clear the clutter, it gets the energy back, and it grows new ones.
It's a cycle."

Lyra looked at the walls. The bone-white stone seemed to be vibrating
contentedly.

"Symbiosis," she whispered. "You aren't conquering the dungeon.
You're... cultivating it."

"I'm farming it," Leon corrected, tying off the sack. "Now, let's go see
if the Construct is back. I need that scrap metal."

Lyra shook her head, a smile tugging at her lips. She looked at his
status again.

\[Agility: 6\]

"You're impossible, Leon," she muttered.

"I'm just efficient," he replied, hoisting the sack over his shoulder.
"Come on. Daylight's burning."
