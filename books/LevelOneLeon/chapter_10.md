# Chapter Ten: The Alchemist of the Compost Heap

The Plague Bear was heavy.

Even with Leon's "override" strength and the mechanical advantage of the
handcart, dragging the carcass back to the farm had been a grueling
affair. Lyra had mostly supervised, keeping a lookout for scavengers,
while Leon treated the gruesome task like he was hauling a prize hog to
the market.

Now, a day later, Lyra sat on the porch railing, her boots dangling, an
apple in her hand.

She was watching Leon work.

It was... exhausting just to look at him.

Most adventurers rested after a dungeon run. They drank ale, slept until
noon, and boasted about their kills. Leon had woken up at 4:00 AM,
milked the cows, and was currently standing in the backyard, elbow-deep
in a massive vat of foul-smelling liquid.

He was tanning the bear hide.

"You really are going to make a rug," Lyra mumbled, taking a bite of the
crisp apple.

"It's good leather," Leon called out, not looking up. He was stirring
the vat with a long wooden paddle. "The necrotic energy actually cured
the collagen fibers. Once I neutralize the acid with wood ash, this hide
will be waterproof and fire-resistant."

Lyra shook her head. A Level 12 Elite Beast drop. It could have been
made into a *Breastplate of the Decay Walker* for a high-level Rogue.
Leon was making a doormat.

She watched him move. There was that rhythm again. *Stir. Pause. Add
ash. Stir.*

She had expected him to hit a wall. She kept waiting for the "Level One"
reality to crash down on him. But instead, she was watching a man
systematically dismantle the laws of the System, one chore at a time.

First Strength. Then Agility.

She checked her own status. Her mana was fully regenerated. She should
probably head back to the Guild, file the report, and collect the
bounty. But she didn't want to leave. She felt like she was standing on
the edge of a cliff, looking down at something vast and unexplored.

Leon stopped stirring. He wiped his hands on a rag and walked over to a
different pile---the bone sacks from the dungeon.

He carried them over to the compost heap.

To the uninitiated, Leon's compost heap looked like a pile of rotting
garbage. To Lyra, who had \[Nature Sense\], it looked like a complex
alchemical reactor. Layers of brown (carbon), green (nitrogen), and
magical residue were stacked with geometric precision.

Leon opened a sack of skeleton dust.

"What are you doing now?" Lyra called out.

"Balancing the pH," Leon replied. "Bone meal is alkaline. The manure is
acidic. If I mix them right, the heat from the decomposition will purify
the necrotic residue left in the bones."

He began to sprinkle the white dust over the dark soil.

Lyra leaned forward.

This wasn't just gardening. This was high-level purification. Usually, a
Cleric would cast \[Sanctify\] to cleanse undead remains. Leon was
using... dirt.

He worked with his eyes closed. He grabbed a handful of dried chamomile
flowers (Solar energy) and tossed them in. Then a scoop of lime. Then
water.

He wasn't measuring. He wasn't using a scale.

He paused, his hand hovering over the pile. He tilted his head,
listening to something Lyra couldn't hear.

*The ratio is off,* she saw him mouth.

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a handful of the Plague Bear's
fur---black, oily, and reeking of death.

"Leon, no!" Lyra started to shout. "That will poison the soil!"

Leon didn't listen. He sprinkled the cursed fur into the mix.

Lyra braced herself for the backlash. Mixing necrotic fur with
life-aligned herbs should have caused a reaction---a small explosion of
rot gas, at the very least.

But Leon didn't stop. He plunged his bare hands into the pile and
*turned* it.

He cycled his Ki.

Lyra felt it. It wasn't the heavy, physical thrum of his Strength, or
the sharp snap of his Agility.

This was different. It was a wave of... *understanding*.

The air around the compost heap shimmered. The heat rising from the pile
didn't smell like rot anymore. It smelled like deep, rich earth. It
smelled like rain on hot asphalt. It smelled like *life*.

Leon had intuitively understood the connection between the death of the
bear and the life of the soil. He hadn't just neutralized the poison; he
had converted it. He had realized that rot is just life waiting to
happen again.

Snap.

Lyra felt the vibration in her own teeth. It was a soundless bell
ringing in the astral plane.

She saw Leon stiffen. He pulled his hands out of the dirt, looking at
them with a puzzled expression. The air around his head seemed to clear,
as if a layer of fog had evaporated.

Lyra hopped off the railing. She didn't walk; she marched.

She crossed the yard in ten strides, grabbing Leon by the arm.

"Stop," she commanded.

Leon blinked, looking down at her. "Lyra? Is something wrong? Did the
smell get to you?"

"Open it," she demanded. "Right now."

"The compost?"

"The System, Leon! Open it!"

Leon wiped his dirty hands on his trousers, looking confused. "Okay,
okay. System. Open."

The blue screen flickered into existence between them.

**\[Name: Leon\]** **\[Level: 1\]** **\[Strength: 6\]** **\[Agility:
6\]** **\[Stamina: 5\]** **\[Intellect: 4\]** **\[Wisdom: 8 \> 9
(Override)\]**

Lyra stared at the number. The **9** sat there, innocent and unassuming.

"You did it again," she whispered. She looked up at him, her green eyes
wide with a mix of horror and awe. "Wisdom. You just broke the cap on
Wisdom."

Leon looked at the stat. "Oh. That explains why I suddenly remembered
where I left my other pair of gloves."

"That is *not* what Wisdom is!" Lyra threw her hands up. "Wisdom is the
connection to the metaphysical! It's the ability to perceive the weave
of magic and reality! You just... you just synthesized High-Grade Holy
Fertilizer by instinct!"

"Is that what this is?" Leon looked at the steaming pile of dirt. "I
just thought it felt balanced. The bear wanted to be useful. The
chamomile wanted to help. I just introduced them."

Lyra paced in a small circle. "You introduced them. He introduced the
necrotic bear fur to the chamomile. Of course."

She stopped pacing and poked him in the chest.

"Leon. Do you realize what you are doing? You are forcefully evolving
your soul container without the System's permission. You are... you are
hacking the universe with chores!"

Leon looked hurt. "I'm not hacking anything. I'm just working. The
System is the one that keeps trying to stop me."

He looked back at the screen.

"Although," he mused. "Nine Wisdom... I bet I can tell if it's going to
rain three days out now. That will be handy for the hay harvest."

Lyra stared at him. She looked at the square jaw, the dirt-smudged nose,
the complete lack of arrogance.

He genuinely didn't care about the power. He cared about the hay.

"You're a monster," she said affectionately, leaning back against the
fence. "A terrifying, gardening monster."

"Does that mean you don't want any of the fertilizer for your Guild
plants?" Leon asked.

Lyra paused. Silver-Leaf Guild had a garden of rare medicinal herbs that
were notoriously difficult to grow.

"I didn't say that," she said quickly. "Pack me a sack. I'll pay you in
mana potions."

"Deal," Leon smiled. "Now, if you'll excuse me, I need to go wash up. I
have a date."

Lyra froze. Her heart did a very traitorous little flip in her chest.

"A date?" she asked, her voice slightly higher than usual. "With...
who?"

Leon pointed to the barn.

"Bessie," he said. "She gets cranky if I'm late with the milking. And
with this new Wisdom, I bet I can figure out exactly where she wants to
be scratched behind the ears."

He picked up his bucket and walked toward the barn, whistling a tune.

Lyra stood alone in the yard, staring at the steaming pile of miracle
compost.

"A cow," she reminded herself. "I am jealous of a cow."

She sighed, grabbed a shovel, and started filling a sack with the
fertilizer.

"At least the cow doesn't have a Wisdom of nine," she muttered. "Yet."
