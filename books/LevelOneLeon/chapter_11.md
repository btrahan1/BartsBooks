# Chapter Eleven: The Mathematics of the Soul

The sun had set, leaving the sky a bruised purple that matched the color
of the bruised plums Leon had harvested earlier. Fireflies were
beginning to blink in the tall grass, mimicking the distant pulse of the
stars.

It was a perfect, romantic evening.

Lyra was ruining it with math.

"Sit," she commanded, slamming a piece of slate onto the small wooden
table on the porch. She held a stick of white chalk like a dagger.

Leon sat. He placed his hands on his knees, looking like a student in
trouble with the headmaster.

"I've been running the numbers," Lyra began, her voice tight. She drew a
vertical axis and a horizontal axis on the slate. "And they don't add
up. Leon, pay attention."

"I am," Leon said.

He was looking at her face. The lantern light on the table cast a warm,
golden glow across her features. He noticed that her eyelashes were
lighter at the tips than at the roots. He noticed the way a single
strand of copper hair had escaped her braid and was curling near her
ear. She was very pretty. Like a sunflower that had decided to become a
weapon.

"This," Lyra tapped the slate, drawing a steep curve, "is the Standard
Progression Curve. This is how the universe is *supposed* to work."

She drew a stick figure at the bottom.

"This is a Level One Human. Base stats are capped at 5. Strength,
Agility, Intellect. 5 is the hard limit. A strength of 5 means you can
lift about 150 pounds comfortably. You can run a mile in eight minutes.
You are... average."

She drew a line up the graph to the number 10.

"This is a Level 10 Warrior. They have gained 9 levels. Each level gives
them +2 to primary stats. So, a Level 10 Warrior has a Strength of
roughly 23 to 25. They can lift a cart. They can punch through a wooden
door."

She drew another line, higher up.

"Level 10 Mage. Intellect of 25. They can memorize three complex spell
circles and cast \[Fireball\] four times before mana exhaustion."

She turned to Leon, tapping the chalk against the slate. *Click. Click.
Click.*

"Do you understand so far?"

Leon blinked. He had been distracted by the way the light caught the
freckles on her nose. "Level 10. Lifts carts. Throws fireballs. Got it."

"Good," Lyra said. She wiped the slate with her sleeve, creating a cloud
of white dust. "Now, here is the problem."

She drew a single dot at the very bottom of the graph.

"This is you. Level One."

Then she drew a line that shot straight up, broke through the top of the
graph, went off the slate, and metaphorically punched a hole in the
porch roof.

"You have a Strength of 6. Officially. But you one-shot a Level 12 Elite
Bear that had a constitution rating of at least 40. By the laws of
physics, your hand should have shattered on impact. Instead, you
shattered the bear."

She leaned over the table, her green eyes searching his.

"It's not just the numbers, Leon. It's the *quality* of the numbers. A
normal 'Strength Point' is like... a cup of water. Your 'Strength
Points' are like cups of mercury. They weigh more."

Leon nodded slowly. "Mercury is dangerous. You shouldn't touch it."

Lyra groaned, running a hand through her hair. Leon watched the motion.
It was graceful. He wondered if she used herbal oils; her hair looked
very soft.

"I'm trying to quantify you, Leon! I'm trying to figure out your
Effective Level. If we go by raw output... you're probably hitting like
a Level 15 Striker. If we go by durability? You tanked a claw swipe that
shreds plate armor. You're maybe a Level 20 Guardian."

She drew a circle around the 'Wisdom' stat she had written down earlier.

"And this? Wisdom 9? Do you know what a Level 10 Cleric's Wisdom is?
Maybe 18. You are half as wise as a trained priest, but you're Level
One. That shouldn't be possible. The 'Container'---your soul---shouldn't
be able to hold that much density without expanding the vessel."

She paused, breathing hard. She looked genuinely distressed.

"I'm worried, Leon. The System caps exist for a reason. If you
over-pressurize a vessel, it bursts. I don't want you to explode."

Leon finally tore his eyes away from the concerned crease in her
forehead. He looked at the slate. He looked at the frantic chalk marks.

He thought about the tomatoes in the garden.

"Lyra," he said softly.

"What?"

"You know the Heirloom Tomatoes? The big red ones?"

Lyra stared at him. "We are talking about your soul exploding, and you
bring up vegetables."

"Bear with me," Leon said. "Sometimes, if it rains too much after a dry
spell, the tomato grows too fast inside. The skin can't keep up. It
splits. It's messy."

"Exactly!" Lyra pointed the chalk at him. "That's what I'm saying!"

"But," Leon continued, leaning forward. "That only happens if the skin
is thin. If you breed the tomato to have a thick skin... if you grow it
slow, season after season, hardening it against the wind and the sun...
it doesn't split. It just gets heavy. Dense. Flavorful."

He reached out and gently took the chalk from her hand. His fingers
brushed hers. Her hand was warm, callous-rough but delicate compared to
his. He saw a faint blush rise on her cheeks.

"I've been Level One for ten years," Leon said. "My skin is very thick,
Lyra. I'm not going to explode. I'm just... ripening."

Lyra looked at him. She looked at his hand covering hers. She looked at
his earnest, brown eyes.

The academic panic drained out of her, replaced by a different kind of
tension. The evening air suddenly felt very still.

"Ripening," she repeated, her voice quiet. "You compare yourself to a
tomato."

"A very sturdy tomato," Leon corrected.

Lyra let out a short, breathless laugh. She pulled her hand back,
tucking a loose strand of hair behind her ear.

"You are impossible to argue with," she said. "You defeat logic with
agriculture."

"Logic is just fancy common sense," Leon said. "And common sense says
you're tired. You've been thinking about numbers all day."

He stood up, the slate forgotten.

"I made stew," he said. "Root vegetable and beef. It's been simmering
since noon. It has a high... what did you call it? Nutritional density?"

Lyra looked at the slate, then at Leon. She realized she wasn't going to
solve the riddle of Leon tonight. And looking at his broad shoulders as
he turned toward the door, she realized she didn't really want to solve
it. She just wanted to see what he did next.

"Stew sounds good," she said, standing up.

"Great," Leon smiled. He held the door open for her. "After dinner, I
can show you the stars. With my new Wisdom, I think I figured out a new
constellation. It looks like a plow."

Lyra walked past him, smelling the scent of woodsmoke and earth that
clung to his tunic.

"Of course it does," she whispered, hiding a smile. "Of course it looks
like a plow."
