# Chapter Twelve: The Harvest Festival Strategy

The beef and root vegetable stew was, objectively, a masterpiece.

It was thick, hearty, and possessed a depth of flavor that usually
required high-level Alchemist spices. Leon had achieved it with
slow-roasted onions and patience.

They sat on the porch steps, empty bowls beside them. The night air was
cooling, but the warmth from the food---and the residual heat of Leon's
presence---kept the chill at bay.

Leon was fidgeting.

He was usually as still as a stone statue, but tonight he was picking at
a loose thread on his trousers. He cycled a breath, exhaled, and then
cleared his throat.

"Lyra?"

"Mmm?" Lyra was leaning back on her elbows, staring up at the vast
expanse of the Milky Way. She felt heavy, but in a good way. The stress
of the Guild, the looming threat of the undead, the politics of the
Kingdom---it all felt very far away from this porch.

"You're... worldly," Leon started, his voice cracking slightly on the
second word. He winced, cleared his throat again, and dropped his voice
an octave. "You've traveled. You've met people."

Lyra turned her head to look at him. "I have. Usually, I shoot them, but
I've met them."

Leon looked down at his boots. "Right. But... you know about social
things. Interactions."

Lyra sat up slowly. The playful mood evaporated, replaced by a soft
curiosity. She saw the way Leon's ears were turning a bright, tell-tale
crimson.

"Leon," she said gently. "What is this about?"

Leon rubbed the back of his neck. "Girls."

Lyra's heart did a strange little stutter. *Oh.*

"What about them?" she asked, keeping her voice neutral.

"They are... significantly harder to understand than livestock," Leon
admitted. "With a cow, you know where you stand. If the ears are back,
she's mad. If the tail is swishing, she's happy. But girls..." He
gestured helplessly at the air. "I never know what the signals mean. I
miss the cues. Everyone laughs."

He turned to her, his brown eyes wide and painfully earnest.

"You're a girl, Lyra. And you're... well, you're you. You're
incredible."

Lyra felt the blush rising on her own cheeks.

"So," Leon continued, rushing the words now. "If... theoretically... a
farmer wanted to tell a high-level adventurer that he... appreciated her
company... and maybe wanted to buy her a ribbon at the fair... how would
he do that? Without looking like a L.O.L.?"

Lyra stared at him.

It was the sweetest, clumsiest confession she had ever heard. He was
asking for advice on how to court her, while asking her. It was so
transparent it was invisible.

For a moment, she wanted to say, *Just buy the ribbon, you idiot.*

She wanted to lean in and close the distance. She thought about his
strength, his kindness, the way he respected the earth and the dead. He
was stable. He was safe.

And that was the problem.

She was a Ranger. Next week, the Guild would send her to the Northern
Wastes to hunt Ice Drakes. Or to the Capital to quell a riot. Her life
was blood, mud, and sleeping in trees.

Leon belonged here. He belonged with his soil, his stars, and his
Bessie. If she started this---if she let him "buy the ribbon"---she
would eventually break his heart, or worse, get him killed trying to
follow her.

He wasn't ready for her world. And she couldn't clip her wings to stay
in his.

The silence stretched too long. Leon started to panic.

"I mean," he stammered, pulling back. "Hypothetically. Just... in
general."

Lyra forced a smile onto her face. It felt brittle, but she held it.

"Leon," she said, her voice bright and professional. "You're asking the
right person. I have seen many adventurers crash and burn in the romance
department. You need a strategy."

Leon blinked. "A strategy?"

"Yes," Lyra said, shifting into 'Mentor Mode' to hide the ache in her
chest. "The Oakhaven Harvest Festival is in three days, right?"

"Yes," Leon said, his shoulders slumping slightly. "Saturday."

"Perfect," Lyra said, slapping him on the knee. "That is your
battleground. We are going to get you a date."

Leon looked confused. "But... I thought..."

"You need practice, Leon," Lyra interrupted gently. "You said it
yourself. You miss the cues. You need to level up your \[Social\] skills
before you try to tackle a... high-level boss."

She saw the light in his eyes dim. He understood. She was telling him
*No*, but she was doing it by telling him *Not yet*.

"Oh," Leon said quietly. "Right. I need... experience."

"Exactly," Lyra said, forcing enthusiasm into her voice. "I will be your
wingman. I'll be your coach. We'll go to the festival, and I will teach
you how to read the signals. I'll point out who is interested. I'll tell
you when to buy the ribbon and when to walk away."

"You'll help me find... someone else?" Leon asked.

"I'll help you learn the game," Lyra corrected. "There are plenty of
nice girls in Oakhaven, Leon. Clara the baker's niece? The one who likes
dust? She's sweet. Or the Miller's daughter."

Leon looked at the stars again. The Plow constellation didn't look quite
as bright as it had a moment ago.

He knew he had been rejected. He didn't have high Intelligence, but his
Wisdom was a 9. He could feel the sorrow radiating off Lyra. She wasn't
rejecting him because she didn't like him. She was rejecting him because
she thought she was doing him a favor.

He decided to trust her. She was the Ranger. She knew the path better
than he did.

"Okay," Leon said, forcing a small smile. "A strategy. I guess I could
use the help. Last year, I tried to compliment a girl's sturdy ankles.
She threw a drink on me."

Lyra laughed, and this time, it was genuine. "Oh, Leon. Never mention
the ankles. We have a lot of work to do."

"Three days," Leon nodded, standing up and dusting off his trousers. "I
should... I should go check the coop. General Cluck gets anxious if I
don't say goodnight."

"Goodnight, Leon," Lyra said softly.

He walked into the darkness toward the barn. Lyra watched him go, the
smile fading from her lips. She wrapped her arms around herself,
suddenly feeling the cold.

"You're doing the right thing," she whispered to herself. "He needs a
wife, not a widow-maker."

She looked up at the stars.

"But damn," she muttered. "I really wanted that ribbon."
