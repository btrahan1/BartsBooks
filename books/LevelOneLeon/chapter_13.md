# Chapter Thirteen: The Waltz of the Heavy Foot

The barn smelled of dry hay and sweet feed. Sunlight streamed through
the gaps in the loft, illuminating dust motes that swirled in the air.

It was a beautiful, rustic setting.

Leon was sweating bullets.

"I don't think I can do this," he said, staring at his boots as if they
had betrayed him. "My feet are size fourteen. They aren't made for...
gliding. They are made for traction."

Lyra stood in the center of the swept floor. She had cleared away the
straw and moved the thresher to the corner to create a makeshift dance
floor.

"Nonsense," she said, tapping her foot impatiently. "Dancing is just
combat without the hitting. It's footwork, Leon. You have excellent
footwork when you're fighting skeletons. Why is this different?"

"Because the skeletons don't have toes I can crush," Leon pointed out.
"And skeletons don't smell like lavender."

Lyra ignored the compliment, though she smoothed her tunic
self-consciously. "We are starting with a simple box step. It's a
square, Leon. You like geometry. Left foot forward, right foot side,
left foot close. Right foot back, left foot side, right foot close."

She held up her hands. "Frame up."

Leon approached her like he was approaching a bomb that was ticking. He
gently took her right hand in his left. He placed his right hand on her
shoulder blade. His touch was incredibly light, terrified of exerting
even a fraction of his new Strength.

"Okay," Lyra nodded. "On my count. And... one, two, three."

Leon moved.

THUD. Scuff. CLOMP.

"Ouch," Lyra winced.

"Sorry!" Leon jumped back. "I told you! I have the turning radius of a
wagon!"

"You're marching," Lyra corrected. "You're attacking the floor. Don't
attack the floor, Leon. The floor is your friend. You need to... float."

"Float," Leon repeated skeptically. "Lyra, I weigh two hundred and forty
pounds. I don't float. I sink."

Lyra sighed. She needed a translation. She looked around the barn. Her
eyes landed on the drying rack where Leon hung the linens.

"Leon," she said. "When you hang the sheets on the line... how do they
move in the wind?"

"They ripple," Leon said instantly. "They catch the air and flow with it
so they don't tear."

"Be the sheet," Lyra said seriously. "I am the wind. If I push, you
ripple backward. If I pull, you flow forward. Don't resist the gravity.
Ride it."

Leon closed his eyes. *Be the sheet. Ride the wind.*

It sounded like high-level monk philosophy.

He took her hand again.

"One, two, three," Lyra hummed a simple folk tune.

Leon didn't look at his feet. He looked at Lyra's chin. He felt the
slight pressure of her hand on his shoulder.

She moves back.

Leon pushed off his back foot. But instead of stomping, he pushed Ki
into his calf muscle. Not to harden it, but to *spring* it.

He visualized his weight not as a heavy stone, but as water in a bucket.
When you move a bucket of water, you don't jerk it; you slide it,
keeping the surface level.

Slide. Slide. Close.

"Better," Lyra whispered, her eyes watching his feet. "Again."

They moved across the barn floor.

At first, it was mechanical. But then, Leon started to anticipate. With
his **Wisdom of 9**, he began to see the pattern before it happened. He
noticed the shift in Lyra's hip before she took a step. He saw the
tension in her shoulder before she turned.

*She's going to spin,* Leon realized.

A normal Level 1 would have stumbled. A normal Agility of 6 would have
been too slow to react to Lyra's Level 18 Ranger speed.

But Leon didn't want to step on her toes. The fear of hurting her was a
powerful motivator.

He poured Ki into his hips. He loosened the joints, lubricating them
with essence.

Lyra spun. It was a fast, tight turn.

Leon didn't just follow; he orbited. He pivoted on a single heel, his
massive body moving with the grace of a figure skater. He swung around
her, his coat tails flaring out, matching her velocity perfectly.

Whoosh.

They stopped, perfectly in time with the imaginary music. Leon was
holding her in a dip, his arm supporting her back with rock-solid
stability.

Lyra blinked, looking up at him. She was breathless. "That was... that
was an Advanced Pivot."

Leon was staring at something over her shoulder. His eyes were
unfocused.

"Leon?"

"My knees felt funny," Leon muttered. "Like they turned into oil."

A blue screen popped up right between their faces.

**\[System Alert: Kinetic Friction Negated.\]** **\[Subject Mobility
Exceeds Structural Parameters.\]** **\[Lubricating Joints...\]**

\[Agility: 6 \> 7 (Override)\]

"Seven," Lyra read the number, still hanging in his arms. She started to
laugh. It was a hysterical, disbelief-filled sound. "Of course. You
learned to dance, so naturally, you broke the laws of physics again."

Leon pulled her upright, setting her gently on her feet. He looked at
the notification.

"Seven Agility," he mused. "That will be useful for chasing the piglets
when they escape."

Lyra punched him lightly on the arm. "You just performed a flawless
Viennese Waltz pivot, and you're thinking about pigs?"

"Pigs are fast, Lyra," Leon said defensively. "They have a low center of
gravity."

"Well," Lyra wiped her forehead, hiding the flush in her cheeks. "You're
ready. Just... try not to dip Clara the Baker's niece that low. She
might faint."

"Right," Leon nodded seriously. "Shallow dips only. Safety first."

From the corner of the barn, Bessie the cow let out a low, mournful moo.

"I know, Bessie," Leon called out. "My form was a little stiff on the
box step. I'll work on it."

Lyra grabbed her water skin, taking a long drink. She watched Leon
practicing his steps alone, muttering "one-two-three" to the chickens.

She realized with a sinking feeling that he wasn't just going to survive
the ball. With Agility 7, Strength 6, and Wisdom 9... he was going to be
the most dangerous dancer on the floor.

"Gods help us all," she whispered.
