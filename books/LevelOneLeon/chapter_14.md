# Chapter Fourteen: The Logic of Small Talk

The kitchen table had become a battlefield.

There were no weapons, no undead, and no maps. There was only a plate of
biscuits and Lyra, who was looking at Leon with the intensity of a drill
sergeant.

"Again," she said. "And this time, do not mention her internal organs."

Leon put his head in his hands. "It was a compliment! Clear eyes usually
mean a healthy liver. Grandmothers love hearing that their grandchildren
are healthy."

"You aren't courting a grandmother, Leon," Lyra sighed, rubbing her
temples. "You are courting a young woman. Young women do not want to be
reminded of their livers. They want to be reminded that they are
charming, or funny, or pretty."

"But pretty is subjective," Leon argued. "Liver function is objective
fact."

"Leon."

"Okay. Okay. No organs."

Leon straightened up. He cycled a breath. He looked at Lyra, who was
currently roleplaying as "Marie, the Miller's Daughter."

"Hello, Marie," Leon said stiffly. "Your... dress... has high structural
integrity. The stitching is very consistent."

Lyra stared at him. "I'm going to scream."

"It's a fact!" Leon threw his hands up. "It's a good dress! It won't
tear if she gets snagged on a bramble!"

"It's boring, Leon! It's dry! It's like talking to a textbook!"

Lyra stood up and paced the kitchen. "You have high Wisdom. You can
sense moods. Why can't you talk?"

"Because words are messy," Leon admitted, feeling defeated. "With
animals, it's feelings. Intent. With people, they say one thing but mean
another. They use metaphors. They use sarcasm. It's... it's too much
data. I can't process it fast enough."

Lyra stopped pacing. She looked at him.

"Data," she repeated.

"Yes. Data. Tone, facial expression, body language, context, past
history. It's a thousand variables hitting me at once. My brain just...
freezes."

Lyra sat back down. She looked at him with a new expression. She wasn't
looking at him like a social case anymore; she was looking at him like a
puzzle.

"You're trying to feel your way through it," she realized. "But you
don't have the experience for intuition yet. So stop trying to *feel*
it. *Calculate* it."

Leon blinked. "Calculate it?"

"Treat a conversation like a crop rotation," Lyra said. "Input and
output. If you plant beans (a question), what does the soil need next?"

"Nitrogen," Leon said instantly. "Or a rest period."

"Right. Conversation is the same. If you ask a question (Input), she
gives an Answer (Output). You have to analyze the Output to find the
'hook'---the Nitrogen---for the next Question."

She leaned forward. "Let's try again. I'm Marie. I just told you: '*I
love the autumn festival, the apple pies are my favorite.*' Analyze the
data. What are the keywords?"

Leon frowned, concentrating. "Autumn. Festival. Apple pies. Favorite."

"Good. Now, construct a response based on 'Apple Pies'. Don't tell me a
fact about apples. Ask me a question about the *process*."

Leon thought. *Process.*

"Did you... bake the pies yourself?" Leon tried.

"Yes!" Lyra smiled. "I did. I used my grandmother's recipe."

"New data," Leon murmured. "Grandmother. Recipe. Okay. Hook: Recipe."

He looked at Lyra. He didn't panic this time. He visualized the
conversation as a flow chart in his mind. *If A, then B.*

"Does the recipe use... cinnamon or nutmeg?" Leon asked.

Lyra's smile widened. "Both! But mostly cinnamon. I love the smell of
cinnamon."

Leon felt a strange clicking sensation in his brain. It was like a gear
that had been stuck for twenty years finally finding some grease.

*She likes the smell.* Smell is a sensory detail. Sensory details lead
to emotional connections.

"Cinnamon smells like... warmth," Leon ventured, calculating the
association. "It must make your kitchen feel very cozy in the winter."

Lyra blinked. Her roleplay character slipped for a second. That was
actually... a very charming thing to say.

"Yes," Lyra said softly. "It does."

Leon stared at the air above her head. He wasn't looking at her; he was
looking at the equation.

Statement + Emotional Association = Connection.

Connection + Follow-up Question = Interest.

It wasn't magic. It wasn't a mystery. It was an algorithm. It was just a
different kind of farming. You plant the seed of a topic, water it with
attention, and harvest the rapport.

"And," Leon continued, his eyes narrowing as he processed the next step.
"If you like baking, you probably get flour from your father's mill. Is
it true that the winter wheat grinds finer than the spring wheat?"

Lyra laughed. "Okay, that's a bit technical, but for a Miller's
daughter, it works. You're asking about her expertise."

Leon sat back. His brain felt hot. Not the burning sensation of Ki
Overload, but a sharp, electrical tingling in his frontal lobe. He could
*see* the pathways of the conversation branching out like roots in the
soil.

**\[System Alert: Neural Pathway Expansion.\]** **\[Social Calculation
Matrix Established.\]** **\[Processing Speed Increased.\]**

\[Intellect: 4 \> 5 (Max Capacity Reached)\]

Leon rubbed his forehead. "I... I think I get it. It's not about being
clever. It's about listening to the keywords and building a bridge to
the next sentence."

"Yes," Lyra beamed. "That's it! That's Intelligence, Leon. It's not just
memorizing spells; it's connecting dots."

Leon looked at his status screen. Intellect 5. He was officially
average. For the first time in his life, he wasn't "slow." He was
just... deliberate.

"So," Leon said, testing his new processing power on Lyra. "You are
teaching me. Teachers usually enjoy seeing their students succeed. Are
you excited to see if your strategy works at the festival?"

Lyra paused. He had turned the algorithm on her. And he was right.

"I am," she admitted. "But I'm also... nervous."

"Why?" Leon asked. *Keyword: Nervous.*

"Because," Lyra said, standing up and brushing crumbs from her lap. "If
I do too good a job, you might actually find someone. And then who will
make me stew?"

Leon processed the data.

*She is joking.* (Context: Smile). *She is sad.* (Wisdom:
Micro-expression in eyes). *She likes the stew.* (Fact).

"I can still make stew," Leon said simply. "Even if I find a girl.
Friendship has a high durability rating. It doesn't break just because
the context changes."

Lyra looked at him. He had missed the romantic subtext (his Intellect
was only 5, after all), but he had hit the emotional core perfectly.

"You're getting smarter, Farmer," she whispered. "And that is
dangerous."

"It's not dangerous," Leon said, standing up. "It's just... logical."

He walked to the door.

"I'm going to check the fence line. I want to practice this... 'talking'
on the sheep. They are good listeners."

"Don't compliment their livers," Lyra called after him.

"No promises," Leon replied.

Lyra watched him go. She touched her chest.

"Intellect five," she mused. "He figured out the formula. God help us
when he figures out the variables."
