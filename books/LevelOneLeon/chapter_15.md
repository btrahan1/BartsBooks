# Chapter Fifteen: The unintended Consequences of Competence

The Oakhaven Harvest Festival was a riot of orange bunting, fiddle
music, and the smell of roasting pork. It was the night when the town
forgot about the looming war rumors and the price of grain, and focused
entirely on cider and gossip.

Lyra stood in the shadow of the cider tent, nursing a mug of mulled
wine. She was wearing her "civilian" clothes---a deep green dress that
brought out her eyes---but she felt as exposed as if she were naked.

She was watching her creation.

"He's going to crash," she whispered to herself. "He's going to mention
a spleen. I just know it."

Leon was standing near the pie stall. He had shaved. He was wearing a
clean white shirt (ironed, likely using heat-transfer techniques he
learned from smithing) and a dark vest. He looked... broad. Stable. Like
a frantic world could anchor itself to him and be safe.

A group of three girls---including Marie the Miller's daughter and Clara
the Baker's niece---were hovering near him. They were giggling, waiting
for the "L.O.L." to say something stupid so they could dismiss him.

Lyra saw Leon's eyes narrow slightly.

*He's processing,* she realized. *He's running the algorithm.*

Leon turned to Marie. He didn't look at the ground. He looked her in the
eye.

"Marie," Leon said. His voice carried over the music---deep and calm. "I
saw your father's delivery wagons this morning. The axles were riding
low. A heavy harvest this year?"

Marie blinked, surprised he wasn't stammering. "Oh! Yes. Best wheat
yield in five years."

Keyword: Yield. Emotional association: Pride/Relief.

"That must be a relief," Leon said, nodding solemnly. "Grinding that
much grain is hard work, but it means the town won't go hungry this
winter. You and your family carry a heavy burden for us all."

Marie's jaw dropped slightly. It wasn't a pickup line. It was
validation. He had just called her family the saviors of the town.

"We... we do our best," Marie stammered, blushing pink.

"It is appreciated," Leon said. He turned to Clara.

Target: Clara. Context: Baker. Item: Hands.

"Clara," Leon said. "I see you have a burn mark on your wrist."

Lyra flinched in the shadows. *Abort! Abort! Don't mention the injury!*

"Oven rack?" Leon asked gently.

Clara hid her hand behind her back, looking embarrassed. "Yes. It was
clumsy of me."

"It's not clumsy," Leon corrected softly. "It's a badge of office. It
shows you were working while the rest of us were sleeping, just so we
could have fresh bread." He smiled---a genuine, warm expression that
crinkled the corners of his eyes. "Thank you for the bread, Clara."

Clara looked like she might faint. She didn't look away. She stepped
*closer*.

"He's doing it," Lyra whispered, her grip on the mug tightening. "He's
farming them. He's planting compliments and harvesting adoration."

Then, the fiddles struck up a fast-paced reel.

"Would... would you like to dance, Leon?" Clara asked, bold with the
courage of being validated.

"I would be honored," Leon said. "But please, mind your step. I have
large feet."

Lyra held her breath. *Agility 7. Don't fail him now.*

Leon led Clara to the wooden dance floor. Usually, when Leon danced, it
looked like a bear fighting a beehive.

But tonight...

He took Clara's hand. He cycled his Ki into his joints. The
**\[Lubricated Joints\]** perk activated.

He didn't stomp. He *glided*.

Leon moved with a terrifying fluidity. He spun Clara out, his movements
precise and calculated. He wasn't just dancing to the music; his
**Wisdom 9** was anticipating the beat. He knew where the other couples
were going to be before *they* knew.

He navigated the crowded floor like water flowing around rocks. He
dipped Clara---safely shallow, as instructed---and brought her back up
with a strength that made her squeak with delight.

The whispers started.

"Is that Leon?" "Is he... floating?" "Did he buy a Potion of Grace?"
"No, look at his shoulders. That's just him."

The song ended. Clara looked starstruck. But Leon didn't stop. The
algorithm demanded efficiency.

He bowed to Clara, thanked her, and immediately offered his hand to
Marie.

He danced with Marie. Then with the Blacksmith's daughter. Then with the
Mayor's secretary.

He was a machine. A charming, waltzing, compliment-dispensing machine.
He treated every girl like a prize-winning pumpkin---with gentleness,
respect, and total attention.

Lyra watched it all.

She should have been thrilled. Her student had graduated. He wasn't the
lonely farmer anymore. He was the most eligible bachelor in Oakhaven.

So why did her chest feel like she had swallowed a handful of gravel?

She watched him spin Marie, laughing at something she said. He looked
happy. He looked... normal.

"He doesn't need a Ranger anymore," Lyra realized, the thought cold and
sharp. "He doesn't need someone to fight his battles or explain the
world to him. He fits in now."

She set her untouched wine on a barrel.

The music slowed to a romantic ballad. The couples moved closer.

Leon was standing alone for a moment, scanning the crowd. He was looking
for someone.

Lyra shrank back into the shadows of the tent. She didn't want him to
see her. She didn't want him to ask her to dance out of pity, or
gratitude, or obligation. She was the Coach. Coaches don't play in the
game.

She turned and slipped out the back of the cider tent, heading away from
the lights and the music, toward the dark quiet of the inn.

"Mission accomplished," she whispered to the night air. Her voice
trembled, just a little.

Back on the dance floor, Leon ignored the three girls vying for his
attention. He stood on his tiptoes---an effortless motion with Agility
7---and scanned the edges of the crowd.

"Lyra?" he murmured.

He saw the cider tent. He saw the abandoned mug of wine. He saw the
ripple of the tent flap where someone had just left.

He frowned.

"Data mismatch," he calculated. *She said she wanted to watch. She
left.*

*Hypothesis: She is tired.* *Alternative Hypothesis: She is...*

His Wisdom 9 pinged. It was a sharp sensation, like a plucked string.

She is sad.

Leon looked at the girls waiting for him. They were nice. They smelled
like lavender and flour. They were safe.

He looked at the dark exit of the tent.

"Excuse me, ladies," Leon said, bowing politely. "I have to go. I forgot
something important."

"But the night is young!" Clara protested. "Where are you going?"

"To find the Coach," Leon said.

He stepped off the dance floor, moving not with the grace of a dancer,
but with the unstoppable momentum of a farmer who realized he had left
the gate open.
