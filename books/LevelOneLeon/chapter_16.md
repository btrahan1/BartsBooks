# Chapter Sixteen: The Seed of Adventure

The music of the festival faded behind him, reduced to a hum of distant
fiddles and laughter.

Leon moved through the dark streets of Oakhaven. He didn't run---running
was for emergencies---but he walked with a stride length that ate up the
distance. His **Agility 7** allowed him to step silently over
cobblestones; his **Wisdom 9** told him exactly which shadow Lyra had
slipped into.

He found her sitting on the stone wall of the old bridge that spanned
the creek, looking down at the black water.

"You're missing the reel," Lyra said without turning around. "Clara was
looking for you."

Leon stopped a few feet away. He leaned against the stone railing,
mirroring her posture.

"I didn't want to dance the reel," Leon said. "The tempo is too fast.
It's inefficient."

"It's fun, Leon," Lyra sighed, finally looking at him. Her face was pale
in the moonlight, her expression guarded. "You were the star of the
night. You cracked the code. You fit in."

"I did," Leon agreed. "The algorithm works. Input, output, emotional
hook. It's logical."

"So why are you here?" Lyra gestured back toward the town lights.
"That's your world, Leon. Safe. Warm. Full of nice girls who don't carry
daggers or hunt undead bears. You won the game."

Leon looked at the water. He thought about the farm. He thought about
the peace of the morning milking. It was a good life. It was the life he
had always wanted.

But then he thought about the Dungeon. He thought about the feeling of
the walnut staff vibrating in his hand when he shattered the skeleton.
He thought about the satisfaction of turning a curse into compost.

And mostly, he thought about the woman sitting next to him, who smelled
like pine needles and dangerous magic.

"Grandpa says you can't plant winter wheat in the spring," Leon said
slowly. "It doesn't take root. The season has to be right."

"Leon, no metaphors. Please."

"I'm saying," Leon turned to face her fully, "that I fit in back there,
yes. But it felt... small. Like wearing a boot that's one size too
tight. I could walk in it, but eventually, it would blister."

He took a step closer.

"You showed me something else, Lyra. You showed me that my skills---my
farming, my work---can do more than just grow turnips. I can heal the
land. I can protect people."

Lyra looked down. "You're Level One, Leon."

"I know," Leon said. "And I have a long way to go. I can't follow you
yet. Not to the Dragon's Spine. Not to the wars in the South. I'm just a
farmer with a heavy stick."

He paused, taking a deep breath. He wasn't calculating an algorithm now.
He wasn't looking for keywords. He was just projecting his truth.

"But I don't want to stay behind the fence anymore. I want to see the
capital. I want to visit the Great Markets in Ironhold. I want to see if
my fertilizer can save the Royal Gardens."

He looked her dead in the eye, his gaze steady and heavy.

"I am going to get stronger, Lyra. My own way. And when I'm ready... I'm
going to find you. And I'm going to buy you that ribbon."

Lyra stared at him.

He wasn't the shy boy stammering about livers. He wasn't the awkward
dancer crushing toes. He stood there with a quiet, undeniable gravity.
He didn't demand her attention; he commanded it, simply by being
entirely, apologetically himself.

She felt a shiver run down her spine. It wasn't magic. It was presence.

**\[System Alert: Social Influence Critical Success.\]** **\[Subject
projects Leadership aura.\]** **\[Charisma: 5 \> 6 (Override)\]**

Lyra let out a breath she didn't know she was holding. A smile broke
through her defenses---not polite, not cynical, but radiant.

"Ironhold is a long journey, Farmer," she said softly. "The roads are
dangerous."

"I have a very sturdy shovel," Leon smiled back.

Lyra hopped off the wall. She adjusted her cloak, the Ranger mask
slipping back into place, though her eyes remained soft.

"I have to go," she said. "The Guild summons came this morning. I'm
heading North at dawn."

"I know," Leon said. "I packed you a travel bag. Dried fruit, jerky, and
a jar of the 'special' ointment for your joints. It's in your
saddlebag."

Lyra shook her head, laughing quietly. "Of course you did."

She stepped forward. She hesitated for a split second, then reached up
and placed a hand on his cheek. Her thumb brushed his jawline.

"Don't level up too fast, Leon," she whispered. "Leave some XP for the
rest of us."

"I'll try," Leon promised. "But the harvest waits for no one."

She dropped her hand, turned, and walked into the darkness of the road
leading out of town. She didn't look back. She didn't need to. She knew
he was watching.

Leon stood on the bridge for a long time.

He pulled up his status screen.

**\[Name: Leon\]** **\[Level: 1\]** **\[Strength: 6\]** **\[Agility:
7\]** **\[Stamina: 5\]** **\[Intellect: 5\]** **\[Wisdom: 9\]**
**\[Charisma: 6\]**

He looked at the numbers. They were small. They were humble. But they
were his.

"Level One," Leon said to the moon.

He turned back toward the farm. He had cows to milk in five hours. He
had a dungeon to prune. And now, he had a map to study.

"For now," he amended.

Leon started walking home, his step light, his mind already calculating
the logistics of a road trip to Ironhold.

Step one: Upgrade the cart.
