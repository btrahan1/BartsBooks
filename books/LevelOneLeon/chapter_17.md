# Chapter Seventeen: The Celestial Building Inspector

The morning after Lyra left, the farm felt quiet. Not the peaceful quiet
of dawn, but the empty quiet of a house where a guest has just packed up
and gone.

Leon was mending the west fence. A wild boar---a normal one, thank
goodness---had rooted around the post during the night, loosening the
foundation.

Thud.

Leon drove the tamper down.

Thud.

Usually, he enjoyed this. He liked the smell of the damp earth and the
satisfaction of a sturdy post. But today, his rhythm was off. He kept
looking down the road, toward the bridge, as if expecting to see a flash
of copper hair or the glint of a green cloak.

"She has work to do," Leon muttered to himself. "And so do I."

He lifted the tamper for another strike.

Suddenly, the tamper stopped in mid-air.

It didn't hit an invisible wall. It just... stopped existing in motion.
The world turned a shade of desaturated grey. The birdsong cut out like
a candle snuffed by wet fingers. The wind died.

Leon blinked. He tried to pull the tamper down. It was frozen in
space-time.

"Oh," Leon said calmly. "A server lag."

"It is not lag," a voice clipped. "It is a Administrative Pause."

The air in front of Leon shimmered, pixels coalescing into a form. A
woman appeared. She was tall, wearing robes that seemed to be woven from
starlight and spreadsheets. She wore rimless glasses and held a glowing
clipboard. Her hair was pulled back in a bun so tight it looked painful.

She didn't look like a Goddess. She looked like a Librarian who had just
found someone eating soup in the reference section.

**\[Entity: System Adjudicator 74-Beta\]** **\[Level: ???\]**

"Mr. Leon," the woman said, looking at her clipboard. "We need to talk."

Leon set the tamper down (now that time had resumed slightly). He wiped
his hands on his trousers and bowed politely.

"Good morning, Ma'am. Are you from the Tax Office?"

The woman sighed, the sound echoing strangely, like static. "No. I am
from the System Backend. I am Adjudicator Beta. I handle anomalies,
glitches, and data corruption."

She looked him up and down with a critical eye.

"And you, sir, are a walking syntax error."

Leon frowned. "I don't think so. I eat plenty of fiber."

"Not that kind of regularity," the Adjudicator snapped. She tapped the
air, and a holographic projection of Leon's soul appeared between them.
It looked like a dense, swirling ball of neutron star matter.

"Look at this," she pointed. "This is your container. You are Level One.
The parameter for a Level One soul is Volume Class A. You are currently
operating at Volume Class... Z. You are vibrating at a frequency that is
giving the local server a headache."

"I have been feeling a bit stronger lately," Leon admitted.

"Stronger?" The Adjudicator laughed dryly. "Mr. Leon, if you were to
accidentally sneeze while cycling your Ki, you might disconnect the
local lay-line. You are hoarding Experience Points like a dragon hoards
gold, but you refuse to spend them on Vertical Ascension."

"Leveling up feels... flighty," Leon explained earnestly. "I prefer
density. It's better for farm work. Less back pain."

"It creates gravitational drag on the interface!" she argued. "Every
time you interact with a low-level object, the System has to run a
million calculations to make sure you don't atomize it by accident. It
is inefficient."

She took a breath, composing herself.

"However," she continued, smoothing her robes. "The System cannot force
you to level up. Free Will protocols and all that. But we cannot have a
Level One walking around with the mass of a Level 40 Paladin. It
confuses the monsters. It confuses the economy. It confuses the loot
tables."

Leon nodded understandingly. "Like putting a draft horse in a pony show.
It upsets the ponies."

"Precisely."

"So," the Adjudicator tapped her clipboard. "I am here to offer a
compromise. A Patch, if you will."

Leon crossed his arms. "I won't level up. Not until I'm ready."

"We know," she said wearily. "You are stubborn. Your Wisdom score is
affecting your decision matrix, making you immune to our standard
coercion pop-ups. So, we propose a **Masking Protocol**."

"A mask?"

"A Display Layer," she corrected. "To the world---to Guild scanners, to
party members, to inspecting monsters---you will not appear as Level
One. You will appear as a level that represents a... safe percentage of
your capabilities."

Leon thought about it. "So, I would lie?"

"You would *buffer* the truth," she corrected. "Think of it as...
wearing a coat over your armor. It makes people comfortable. It stops
the local bandits from attacking you thinking you are helpless, which
saves you from having to hurt them, which saves the System from having
to calculate their splattered remains."

Leon's ears perked up. "It would stop people from attacking me?"

"Yes. A Level One is a target. A Level Five is a nuisance. A Level Ten
is a threat."

"I don't want to be a threat," Leon said. "But... I do get tired of
people trying to mug me when I take the cart to town. It wastes
daylight."

"Exactly," the Adjudicator smiled thinly. "So, here is the proposal. We
implement a **Dynamic Level Display**. It will show a public level
equivalent to roughly 20% of your actual 'Density Value.' As you grow
denser, the display level will slowly rise. It will appear natural. No
sudden jumps."

She tapped her clipboard again. "According to my calculations, based on
your current overflow... we should start your Public Display at **Level
3**."

Leon looked at the number. Level 3.

It wasn't much. To an adventurer like Lyra (Level 18), it was still
adorable. But to a commoner? To the town bully? Level 3 was a
respectable, "I know how to use a pitchfork" level.

"Level 3," Leon mused. "That seems honest enough. It reflects my ability
to lift heavy rocks."

"It reflects your ability to not die immediately," the Adjudicator
corrected. "Do we have an accord?"

"Will it hurt?" Leon asked.

"No. It's just a UI update."

"Okay," Leon nodded. "I accept the patch. But I want to keep my 'L.O.L.'
status in the internal log. It keeps me humble."

"Your internal log will remain unchanged," the Adjudicator promised.
"You will always be Level One in your heart, Mr. Leon."

She tapped the air. A ripple of blue light washed over Leon. He felt a
slight tingling in his scalp, like static electricity.

**\[System Update: Patch 1.01 Applied.\]** **\[Public Display Protocol:
Active.\]** **\[Current Public Level: 3\]**

The grey world began to fade. Color bled back into the grass. The birds
started singing again.

"One last thing," the Adjudicator said, her form becoming transparent.
"You are planning to travel to Ironhold?"

Leon blinked. "How did you know?"

"We process Quest Logs," she said. "A word of advice. The System logic
in big cities is more rigid than out here in the boonies. Try not to
break any gates. The paperwork is atrocious."

And with a sound like a closing book, she vanished.

Leon stood alone by the fence. He looked down at his hands. They looked
exactly the same. He grabbed the tamper.

Thud.

It hit the earth with the same satisfying solidity.

"System," Leon whispered.

**\[Name: Leon\]** **\[Level: 1 (Public: 3)\]** **\[Strength: 6\]**
**\[Agility: 7\]** **\[Stamina: 5\]** **\[Intellect: 5\]** **\[Wisdom:
9\]** **\[Charisma: 6\]**

He smiled. Level 3. He wasn't the "Loser of Lands" anymore. He was
just... a very low-level novice.

"Well," Leon said to the fence post. "I guess I've been promoted."

He looked down the road one last time. The listlessness was gone,
replaced by a sense of order. The System had acknowledged him. He had a
plan. He had a level (sort of).

"Bessie," he called out toward the barn. "Pack your bags. We're going to
need a bigger cart."

From the barn, a cow mooed in agreement. It sounded like she was ready
for a vacation.

in hard labor, and how his "stats" are now integrating seamlessly into
his daily life, turning backbreaking chores into a satisfying workout.
