# Chapter Eighteen: The Joy of Heavy Lifting

For the first time in a week, Leon woke up without a sense of looming
dread or social anxiety.

The sun was shining. The air smelled of pine and possibility. The
Adjudicator had stopped time to yell at him, but she hadn't stopped him
from working. In fact, by giving him a "Public Level 3," she had
essentially given him a building permit to expand his foundation.

"So much to do," Leon whispered, throwing off the quilt. He felt
vibrant. His meridians were humming like a well-tuned engine.

He grabbed his list---written on a scrap of parchment with
charcoal---and checked the items.

1.  Weed the Cave.
2.  Plow the North Field (Clay is heavy this year).
3.  Reinforce the Travel Cart.
4.  Harvest Grave Moss for Lyra.

It was a beautiful list. It promised sweat, dirt, and tangible results.

Task 1: The Weeding

Leon arrived at the Dungeon entrance at 6:00 AM. The golden barrier
shimmered, recognizing his bio-signature and letting him pass with a
soft *thrum*.

Inside, the dungeon was thriving.

Since he had started "managing" the ecosystem, the air inside wasn't
stagnant anymore. It was crisp, cold, and smelled faintly of ozone. The
bioluminescent lichen on the ceiling had bloomed into a vibrant neon
green, lighting the corridors better than torches.

"Morning," Leon called out to the darkness.

The Core, deep in the earth, pulsed a warm violet greeting.

A group of five skeletons clattered around the corner. They were Level 6
now---sturdier, with thicker bones and weapons that looked less like
scrap metal and more like actual iron.

They saw Leon. They shrieked. They charged.

Leon smiled. He didn't see enemies. He saw overgrown brambles blocking a
path.

He spun the walnut staff. *Agility 7.*

The staff moved so fast it created a low hum in the air. Leon didn't
stop moving. He danced through the skeletons, his feet sliding over the
stone floor with frictionless grace.

Tap. Shatter. Sweep. Crush.

It was a rhythm. *Left foot pivot (dodge sword).* *Right hand thrust
(break ribcage).* *Spin (disarm).* *Overhead tap (dust).*

In ten seconds, the hallway was clear. Piles of pristine white bone dust
littered the floor.

**\[XP: 120\]** **\[Converted to Density.\]**

Leon took a deep breath, inhaling the mana-rich air. He felt his core
absorb the energy, packing it down tight. It felt good. It felt like
eating a hearty stew after a long fast.

He crouched down near the wall. Growing in the cracks of the bone-stone
was a patch of dark, velvet-textured moss.

"Grave Moss," Leon identified. "High affinity for alchemy. Lyra said the
Guild pays silver for this."

He pulled a small trowel from his belt and carefully harvested the moss,
leaving the roots intact so it would grow back. He placed it in a
wax-paper lined pouch.

"Grow strong," he whispered to the remaining spores. "I'll be back in a
few days."

Task 2: The North Field

By 9:00 AM, Leon was standing at the edge of the North Field.

It was the toughest plot of land on the farm. The soil here was dense,
red clay, packed tight by years of rain. It was notorious for breaking
plows and breaking spirits.

Grandpa was sitting on the fence, watching.

"You sure about this, boy?" Grandpa chewed on a piece of straw. "Old Sal
is looking at that mud like she'd rather die than pull through it."

Leon looked at Sal the mule. She was indeed giving the field a very
skeptical side-eye, flicking her tail in irritation.

"Sal deserves a break," Leon said. "She hauled the rock for the wall
yesterday."

Leon walked up to the heavy iron plow---the one he had reforged with
Lyra watching. He unhooked the leather harness meant for the mule.

Then, he picked up the yoke.

It was a heavy wooden beam, meant to rest across the shoulders of an ox.
Leon slid his head under it, settling the wood across his own broad
shoulders. He gripped the handles of the plow behind him.

"Leon," Grandpa warned. "That's a two-ox plow. The drag coefficient on
that clay is---"

"It needs to be done, Grandpa," Leon said. "And I need to test
something."

He dug his boots into the soil. He cycled his breath.

*Core engaged.* *Strength: 6.*

"Hup!"

Leon pushed with his legs. His thighs, corded with muscle and reinforced
by Ki, didn't strain. They just... drove.

The plow lurched forward. The iron blade bit into the hard clay.

RIIIIIP.

The sound of the earth tearing was satisfyingly loud. Leon walked. He
didn't run; you can't plow running. He walked with a steady, unstoppable
cadence. The plowshare, sharpened and laminar-folded by Leon's own hand,
sliced through the clay like a hot knife through butter.

Grandpa's jaw dropped. The straw fell out of his mouth.

He watched his grandson dragging a three-hundred-pound plow through
drag-heavy clay, cutting a furrow straight as an arrow, without even
breathing hard.

"System," Grandpa whispered. "Inspect."

**\[Name: Leon\]** **\[Level: 3\]**

Grandpa frowned. "Level 3 my aching back. That boy has the torque of a
siege engine."

Leon reached the end of the row. He needed to turn. Usually, this
required lifting the plow out of the earth, turning the animal, and
resetting.

Leon just planted his feet, twisted his hips (*Agility 7*), and swung
the entire plow around in a tight arc, keeping the blade hovering an
inch off the ground. He dropped it into the next row and started back.

He was sweating, but it wasn't the exhausting sweat of failure. It was
the cleansing sweat of capacity. He could feel his Stamina bar draining,
but deeper down, he could feel the "vessel" stretching.

*Just a few more rows,* Leon thought. *The winter wheat will love this
aeration.*

Task 3: The Travel Cart

By 2:00 PM, the field was done. Leon had eaten a lunch of four
hard-boiled eggs, a loaf of bread, and a pitcher of milk.

Now, he was in the barn, looking at the travel cart.

It was a sturdy vehicle, but it was designed for hauling hay to town,
not for a cross-country journey to Ironhold. The axle was oak, the
wheels were iron-rimmed, and the suspension was... non-existent.

"If I hit a pothole in the mountains, the axle snaps," Leon diagnosed.
"And Bessie will get jostled. Bessie hates potholes."

He looked at the pile of "scrap" he had salvaged from the dungeon. The
Bone Construct's weapons.

He picked up the spear shaft. It was made of some unknown,
dungeon-hardened ash wood. It was light, but when he tried to bend it,
it sprang back with incredible force.

"Leaf springs," Leon realized.

He set to work.

He wasn't using the System \[Craft\] skill. He was using physics and Ki.

He heated the rusted metal axe-heads in the forge, beating them flat to
create brackets. He cut the dungeon-wood spear shaft into strips. He
layered them under the cart's main carriage, creating a rudimentary
suspension system.

He worked for hours. The rhythm of the hammer, the smell of sawdust, the
puzzle of fitting the pieces together---it was meditation.

As he tightened the final bolt (made from a goblin's mace handle), he
felt a wave of exhaustion hit him. Not just tired muscles, but a deep,
cellular fatigue. He had been pushing his body non-stop for twelve
hours.

He leaned against the cart, wiping grease from his forehead.

"That should hold," he muttered. "Smooth ride for the cow. Smooth ride
for the loot."

A soft chime echoed in his mind.

It wasn't the harsh buzz of an error. It was a polite, gold-rimmed
notification.

**\[System Notification: Prolonged Physical Exertion Detected.\]**
**\[Workload exceeds current Sustainability Threshold.\]**
**\[Foundation Adaptation Complete.\]**

\[Stamina: 5 \> 6\]

Leon smiled tiredly.

"Six," he whispered. "Finally."

He patted the cart. He looked out the barn door. The sun was setting
over the freshly plowed field. The furrows looked perfect, drinking in
the twilight.

He had leveled up his Strength, his Agility, his Wisdom, his Charisma,
and now his Stamina.

"One more thing," Leon said, pushing himself off the cart. "I need to
pack the seeds."

He walked toward the house, his steps heavy but steady. He wasn't just a
farmer anymore. He was a Level 3 Adventurer (on paper), with a Level 1
Soul (in theory), and the workload of a small army.

And tomorrow, he would begin the packing. Ironhold awaited. And
somewhere north of there, a Ranger was probably eating dried fruit and
thinking about ribbons.

"Wait for me, Lyra," Leon whispered. "I'm bringing the good fertilizer."
