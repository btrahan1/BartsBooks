# Chapter Nineteen: The Calibration of Mediocrity

The kitchen table was covered in crumbs and charcoal dust.

Leon sat staring at a blank piece of slate, his brow furrowed in deep
concentration. He was trying to access a corrupted file in his memory
banks.

*File Name: Lyra's Lecture on Stat Scaling.* *Date: Three nights ago.*
*Status: Compromised by Romance.*

"Think, Leon," he muttered, tapping the chalk against his chin. "She
drew a graph. There was a curve."

He closed his eyes, engaging his **Intellect 5** and **Wisdom 9**.

The memory surfaced. He saw the porch. He saw the slate. He saw Lyra
pointing at the chalk lines.

*"A Level 10 Warrior has a Strength of roughly 25,"* her voice echoed in
his mind.

Leon wrote **25** on the slate.

"A Level 1 Human has a cap of 5."

He wrote **5**.

Then the memory fuzzed out. In his mind's eye, the image shifted from
the numbers on the slate to the way the lantern light had caught the
curve of Lyra's neck. He remembered thinking that her pulse point looked
very strong and healthy. He remembered the smell of pine needles and
rain.

"Useless," Leon groaned, opening his eyes. "I was looking at the wrong
data set."

He took a deep breath. *Algorithm mode.*

*Fact A:* Level 1 = 5 Stats. *Fact B:* Level 10 = 25 Stats.
*Calculation:* That is a gain of 20 points over 9 levels. *Average:*
2.22 points per level.

Leon scratched out the math.

*Target:* Level 3 (His new "Public" Level). *Equation:* 5 + (2.22 x 2
levels) = \~9.4.

Leon looked at the number. **9.**

He pulled up his own System status.

\[Strength: 6\]

Leon frowned. "This is a problem."

According to the math, a standard Level 3 adventurer should have a
Strength of 9. Leon only had a Strength of 6.

On paper, he was weak for his level.

But in reality, his "6" was made of neutron star matter, while a normal
"9" was made of styrofoam.

"If I go to the tournament," Leon reasoned, talking to the empty
kitchen, "and I hit someone with my full 'Six,' I will launch them into
the grandstands. That is not Level 3 behavior. That is Level 20
behavior."

"But," he continued, pacing the floor, "if I try to hold back too much,
I'll look like a Level 1, and the judges might disqualify me for being a
safety hazard."

He needed to find the "Sweet Spot." He needed to simulate the output of
a standard Strength 9.

"I need a test subject," Leon decided.

The barn was quiet, save for the rhythmic chewing of Bessie the cow.

Leon stood before a stack of hay bales. He had wedged a thick oak
log---leftover from the fence repair---vertically between two bales. It
was roughly the thickness of a man's torso.

"Grandpa!" Leon called out.

Grandpa Silas hobbled out of the tack room, leaning on his cane. "What
is it, boy? Cows loose again?"

"I need you to Inspect me," Leon said. "I need to calibrate my swing."

Grandpa squinted at him. "Calibrate? You sound like a gnome tinkerer.
What are you doing?"

"I'm Level 3 now," Leon explained. "Publicly. I need to make sure I
fight like a Level 3. Not a Level 1, and not... whatever I actually am."

Grandpa chuckled, scratching his beard. "Acting, eh? Alright. I remember
what a Level 3 recruit looks like. Green. Sloppy. Over-eager."

"Okay," Leon said. He picked up his walnut staff. "I'm going to hit this
log. You tell me if it looks right."

Leon took his stance. He cycled his Ki. He felt the immense, coiled
power in his core.

*Throttle down,* he told himself. *Reduce output to 15%.*

He swung.

WHAM.

The log didn't break, but the impact sent a shockwave through the hay
bales, blowing straw dust into the air. The log rattled violently, and
the entire barn shook.

Grandpa shook his head. "Too clean. Too hard. That looked like a Level 8
Striker using \[Power Bash\]. If you hit a novice with that, you'll cave
in their breastplate."

"Too hard," Leon muttered. "Okay. Reducing efficiency."

He loosened his grip. He deliberately broke his posture, slouching his
shoulders. He swung again.

Bonk.

The staff bounced off the log harmlessly.

"Pathetic," Grandpa judged. "That was a Level 1 toddler chasing a
butterfly. You'd get laughed out of the ring."

Leon sighed, wiping sweat from his brow. "This is harder than actual
work. Being mediocre requires so much... calculation."

"Stop thinking about numbers," Grandpa commanded. He pointed his cane at
Leon. "Think about the *tool*, Leon. When you're driving a nail, do you
smash it with all your might?"

"No," Leon said. "You bend the nail."

"Exactly. You use just enough force to seat the head. Imagine your
opponent is a nail you don't want to bend."

Leon looked at the log. *Don't bend the nail.*

He visualized the force. He didn't want to blast through the log. He
wanted to *knock* on it. A firm, authoritative knock.

He widened his stance. He cycled his Ki, but instead of packing it into
the staff, he kept it circulating in his body, using it only for
stability.

He swung. It wasn't a kill-shot. It was a "Get Back" swat.

THWACK.

The sound was solid---a sharp report of wood on wood. The log jumped
back a few inches, absorbing the blow without exploding. It was
forceful, but controlled.

Grandpa nodded slowly. "Better. That looked like a sturdy Level 3. Good
form, decent power, but no magical shockwave."

"Agility?" Leon asked.

"Let's see you dodge." Grandpa suddenly lashed out with his cane, aiming
for Leon's shin.

Leon's **Agility 7** screamed at him to backflip. His **Wisdom 9**
calculated the trajectory instantly.

*Don't backflip,* Leon ordered his legs. *Just... stumble efficiently.*

Leon jerked his leg back. He managed to avoid the cane, but he let his
boot scuff the dirt, kicking up a little cloud of dust. He flailed his
arms slightly for balance.

"Perfect!" Grandpa crowed. "You looked like a drunk duck! Very authentic
Level 3."

Leon straightened up, brushing dust off his trousers. "I looked clumsy."

"You looked *human*," Grandpa corrected. "That's the disguise, Leon.
Perfection is suspicious. Flaws are blending in."

Leon considered this. He hated flaws. A flaw in a fence let the wolves
in. A flaw in a plow line wasted water.

But Lyra had said he needed to fit in. And fitting in meant having
flaws.

"Okay," Leon nodded. "I will practice my wobbling. I will ensure my
strikes have a 5% margin of error."

"Don't overthink the error, boy," Grandpa sighed, turning back to the
tack room. "Just try not to be perfect for five minutes. It'll be good
for your character."

Leon stood before the log. He raised his staff.

He practiced hitting it. He practiced stumbling. He practiced looking
winded when he wasn't tired.

It was the hardest workout he had ever done.

By sunset, Leon was mentally exhausted. He sat on a hay bale, looking at
his status screen.

\[Level: 1 (Public: 3)\]

"I am ready," Leon whispered. "I will be the most convincing, average,
slightly clumsy Level 3 warrior they have ever seen."

He stood up and gave the log one last, gentle pat.

"And if that doesn't work," he muttered, "I'll just grapple them until
they fall asleep. Everyone understands a hug."
