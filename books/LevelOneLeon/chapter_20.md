# Chapter Twenty: The Paper Tiger

The morning of the Oakhaven Amateur Tournament dawned crisp and clear,
the kind of day that usually signaled excellent drying weather for hay.

Leon, however, was not thinking about hay. He was staring at the blue
rectangle floating above his porridge.

**\[System Notification: Density Threshold Updated.\]** **\[Public
Display Protocol: Auto-Adjusted.\]**

**\[Name: Leon\]** **\[Public Level: 4\]** **\[Strength: 10\]**
**\[Agility: 9\]** **\[Constitution: 10\]** **\[Intellect: 8\]**
**\[Wisdom: 9\]** **\[Charisma: 7\]**

Leon lowered his spoon.

"Four," he whispered. "I'm Level Four."

He felt a strange mix of pride and impostor syndrome. He hadn't
*actually* leveled up. He hadn't spent a single point of his hoarded
99,999 XP. This was just the Adjudicator's algorithm doing the math on
his soul density and deciding that "Level 3" was no longer a big enough
box to hold him.

He looked at the stats.

*Strength 10.* Double digits. That was the benchmark for a serious
laborer. *Constitution 10.* That meant he could theoretically run a
marathon without collapsing. (Or plow the North Field without a nap).
*Intellect 8.* This one surprised him the most. Lyra's "Social
Algorithms" must have counted as high-intensity brain training. He
felt... sharper. He realized he was currently thinking about the spoon,
the porridge, and the structural integrity of the table simultaneously.
*Multitasking.*

"Grandpa!" Leon called out. "The System promoted me again!"

Grandpa Silas walked into the kitchen, dressed in his 'Town Clothes'---a
clean tunic and a vest that smelled of mothballs. He peered at the
screen.

"Level 4," Grandpa whistled. "And look at those stats. Ten Constitution?
You're built like a brick outhouse, boy."

"It's just the mask, Grandpa," Leon reminded him, finishing his
porridge. "It's what the judges will see when they Inspect me. It means
they won't put me in the toddler bracket."

"It means they'll put you against people who know which end of the sword
goes where," Grandpa corrected. "Are you ready?"

Leon stood up. He cycled his Ki. He felt the familiar, dense weight of
his core. It felt like a coiled spring made of titanium.

"I have practiced my stumbling," Leon said confidently. "I have
calibrated my 'medium' hit. And my boots are polished."

"Good enough," Grandpa nodded. "Let's load the cart. And bring the
salve. Tournaments always end with bruises."

The journey to town was smooth. The new suspension system on the cart
worked perfectly; the leaf springs absorbed the ruts in the road, and
Bessie (who wasn't coming, but Leon monitored the vibration levels for
her sake anyway) would have been pleased.

Oakhaven was transformed.

Usually a quiet farming hub, today it was a sea of tents, banners, and
noise. The Tournament Grounds had been set up in the large common
pasture outside the walls. There were flags from neighboring villages,
the smell of roasting sausage, and the metallic clang of weapons being
tested.

Leon pulled the cart into the "Competitor Parking" area (which was just
a field with a rope around it). He unhitched Old Sal and gave her a
bucket of oats.

"Stay," Leon told the mule. "If a bard tries to pet you, you have
permission to bite. Bards are untrustworthy."

He grabbed his walnut staff and walked toward the registration tent.

The line was long. There were young men in shiny, ill-fitting armor
practicing their war cries. There were grim-faced dwarves sharpening
axes. There was a mage in robes that were slightly too short, muttering
incantations to a hamster.

Leon waited patiently. He used the time to analyze the fence
construction around the arena. *Post depth looks shallow,* he noted. *If
a Minotaur hits that rail, the spectators in the front row are going to
have a bad day.*

"Next!"

Leon stepped up to the table. The registrar was a bored-looking Guild
official with ink stains on his fingers.

"Name?"

"Leon."

"Class?"

"Farmer."

The official paused. He looked up, pen hovering. "Farmer? This is a
combat tournament, son. Not a vegetable competition. The giant pumpkin
weigh-in is next month."

"I am aware," Leon said politely. "I am entering the Amateur Melee
bracket."

The official sighed, clearly used to deluded peasants. "Alright. Let me
verify your eligibility. Use the crystal."

He pointed to a large, clear quartz on the table.

Leon placed his hand on it.

He expected it to glow white. Maybe a soft blue.

Instead, the crystal hummed. It vibrated. A deep, resonant *THRUM* shook
the table, causing the official's inkwell to rattle. The light that
filled the stone wasn't a flash; it was a solid, heavy gold.

**\[Scan Complete\]** **\[Name: Leon\]** **\[Class: Farmer\]**
**\[Level: 4\]** **\[Rating: Solid\]**

The official's eyebrows shot up. He looked at the crystal, then at Leon,
then back at the crystal.

"Level 4?" the official muttered. "With a 'Solid' rating? Usually,
Farmers are... squishy."

He looked at Leon's stats on his own private display.

"Strength 10... Constitution 10..." The official whistled low. "You've
been eating your vitamins, haven't you?"

"Kale," Leon said earnestly. "And lots of root vegetables. They ground
the humors."

The official stamped a piece of parchment. "Right. Kale. Okay, Leon.
You're in. You're seeded in Group C. That's the 'Light Heavyweight'
novice division. First match is in an hour against..." He squinted at
the roster. "...'Gorlag the Destroyer'."

"Gorlag," Leon repeated. "Is he... an Orc?"

"No. He's a baker from the next village over. But he has a very large
rolling pin."

"Ah," Leon nodded. "Bakers have strong forearms. Kneading dough builds
grip strength."

"Sure," the official waved him off. "Next!"

Leon took his entry badge---a wooden token with the number **42**
painted on it---and walked toward the waiting area.

He felt eyes on him.

"Hey," a voice hissed. "Is that him? The L.O.L.?"

"Yeah. That's Level One Leon."

"I heard he tanked a bear last week."

"Bullshit. Look at him. He's holding a stick. He probably poked the bear
while it was sleeping."

"My cousin Marie danced with him. She said he floats."

"He floats? What is he, a ghost?"

Leon ignored the whispers. He engaged his **Intellect 8** to filter out
the background noise. He focused on his breathing.

He found a quiet corner near the weapon racks and sat down on a bench.
He placed the walnut staff across his knees.

He closed his eyes.

*Level 4,* he thought. *Stats are respectable. I am ready.*

He visualized the match. Gorlag the Destroyer. A baker. Strong grip.
Likely uses wide, sweeping swings (rolling pin technique).

*Strategy:* Step inside the arc. Control the weapon. Gently escort him
out of bounds.

"Excuse me?"

Leon opened his eyes.

Standing in front of him was a young woman. She was wearing leather
armor that looked brand new, and she was holding a clipboard. She looked
nervous.

"Are... are you Leon?"

"I am."

"I'm a scribe for the *Oakhaven Weekly*," she squeaked. "We're doing a
piece on 'Dark Horses' of the tournament. Is it true you leveled up
three times in one week just by gardening?"

Leon blinked. "Gardening is very intense. The weeds are aggressive this
year."

The scribe scribbled furiously. "Aggressive weeds. Got it. And what is
your strategy for the tournament?"

Leon stood up. He looked at the arena, where the crowd was starting to
cheer.

"My strategy," Leon said, tightening his grip on the staff, "is to
ensure that no one gets hurt. Especially me."

He smiled. It was a Charisma 7 smile---reassuring, confident, and
strangely terrifying.

"And to win the fifty silver prize. My grandmother needs new
spectacles."
