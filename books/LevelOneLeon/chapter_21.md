# Chapter Twenty-One: The Green-Eyed Observer

The waiting area for Group C was a pen of nervous energy. Men paced,
stretched, and vomited into buckets. The air smelled of cheap leather
oil and anxiety.

Leon sat on his bench, perfectly still. He was breathing in a four-count
box pattern---*Inhale, Hold, Exhale, Hold*---which regulated his heart
rate to a resting forty beats per minute.

He wasn't nervous. He was observant.

And he was being watched.

Perched on top of the weapon rack, about six feet away, was a cat.

It was small, sleek, and black as a moonless midnight. It sat with its
tail wrapped neatly around its paws, staring at Leon with unblinking
intensity.

Its eyes were green. Not the yellow-green of a normal barn cat, but a
deep, vivid moss-green.

Leon tilted his head. The cat tilted its head.

"Hello," Leon whispered.

The cat didn't meow. It just narrowed its eyes slightly, as if assessing
his structural integrity.

Wisdom Check (9).

Leon felt a strange prickle at the base of his skull. This wasn't a
stray looking for scraps. Strays had a desperate, skittish energy. This
cat had the posture of a supervisor. It sat with the arrogant confidence
of someone who knew exactly where the exits were.

"You have excellent grooming," Leon complimented the cat. "Very sleek
coat. High protein diet?"

The cat blinked slowly. It seemed to accept the compliment.

"Leon?"

Leon turned. Standing near the entrance of the waiting pen was Clara,
the Baker's niece. She was holding a wicker basket covered with a
checkered cloth, looking out of place amidst the sweaty warriors.

Leon's **Intellect 8** spun up the Social Algorithm instantly.

*Target: Clara.* *Previous Interaction: The Waltz (Success).* *Current
Status: Flushed cheeks, nervous gripping of basket handle.* *Objective:
Engage and validate.*

Leon stood up. He didn't just stand; he unfolded. His movements were
smooth, shedding the "L.O.L." awkwardness like an old skin.

"Clara," Leon said, stepping over a discarded shield to reach her. "Good
morning."

"Hi, Leon," Clara breathed, her eyes darting to the other fighters
before settling on him. "I... I brought you something. For energy.
Before your match."

She lifted the cloth. Underneath sat three golden-brown buns, glazed
with honey and dusted with crushed nuts.

"Honey-Nut Brioche," Leon identified. "A complex dough. Requires a
double rise and precise oven temperature."

He looked at her.

*Don't just state facts,* his internal voice chided. *Find the hook.*

"They look perfect," Leon said warmly. "You must have been up before
dawn to get this glaze to set. That shows incredible dedication to your
craft."

Clara beamed. "I was! The humidity is terrible today, I was so worried
they would fall flat."

"They have excellent loft," Leon assured her, picking one up gently.
"Thank you, Clara. This is... thoughtful."

He took a bite. It was delicious.

"It's good?" she asked, twisting her apron.

"It's efficient," Leon said, then caught himself. "I mean... it is
comforting. It tastes like a warm kitchen on a cold morning."

Clara let out a small giggle. "You have a way with words, Leon. Everyone
says you're so quiet, but... you notice things."

"I try to," Leon said.

He felt a sudden, sharp pain in his ankle.

He looked down. The black cat had hopped off the weapon rack and swatted
his boot. It wasn't a playful tap; it was a judgemental smack. The cat
stared at him, then looked at Clara, then narrowed its eyes at Leon
again.

"Oh! A kitty!" Clara cooed, reaching down. "Here, puss-puss."

The cat looked at Clara's hand with profound disdain. It didn't hiss,
but it radiated an aura of '*Do not touch me, civilian.*'

Leon chuckled. "Careful, Clara. That one has a high dignity rating. I
don't think it likes baby talk."

"Is it yours?" Clara asked, retracting her hand.

"No," Leon said, looking at the green eyes. "It's just... observing. I
think it's scouting the competition."

The cat let out a low, chirping sound, turned its back on Clara, and
walked a tight circle around Leon's legs, rubbing its side against his
shin. It was marking him. Claiming him.

Clara looked a little jealous. "It likes you."

"Animals usually do," Leon said. "They know I'm warm."

"I..." Clara started, stepping closer. "I know that too. From the
dance."

Leon's **Charisma 7** flared. He realized she was flirting. The old Leon
would have panicked and talked about livestock diseases. The new
Leon---the Level 4 Leon---just smiled.

"Save a dance for me at the victory celebration?" Leon asked.

Clara's face went a shade of red that matched the strawberries in her
uncle's tarts. "I... yes! Yes, I will! Good luck, Leon! Beat Gorlag!"

She turned and hurried away, nearly tripping over a discarded spear,
looking giddy.

Leon watched her go, finishing the bun.

"Algorithm successful," he muttered. " Rapport established. Morale
boosted."

He looked down at the cat.

"What do you think?" Leon asked. "Too forward?"

The cat stared at him. Then, very deliberately, it sat down and began to
clean its paw. It paused, looked Leon in the eye, and gave a single,
sharp nod.

Approval?

"Group C! Match One!" the announcer's voice boomed from the arena. "Leon
the Farmer versus Gorlag the Destroyer!"

Leon wiped the crumbs from his mouth. He grabbed his walnut staff.

"Showtime," Leon said to the cat. "Guard my seat?"

The cat curled up on the bench Leon had vacated, wrapped its tail around
its nose, and closed its eyes. It was the picture of a creature that had
seen everything and was unimpressed by all of it.

Leon smiled. Those green eyes... they reminded him of the forest.

He walked toward the arena gate, the cheers of the crowd washing over
him. He felt light. He felt ready. And for some reason, he felt like he
shouldn't disappoint the cat.
