# Chapter Twenty-Two: The Safety Latch

The arena floor was packed dirt, smelling of sawdust and sweat. The sun
beat down on the back of Leon's neck as he stepped through the gate.

The crowd was loud. Half of them were chanting "L.O.L.", treating his
presence like a comedy act. The other half---mostly the girls from the
festival and a few farmers who knew the quality of his turnips---were
cheering with surprising ferocity.

Leon raised a hand to wave at Clara, who was jumping up and down in the
third row.

Then, he crossed the white chalk line of the ring.

**\[System Notification: Sanctioned Competition Zone Detected.\]**
**\[Safety Protocol:** '**Muffler**' **Engaged.\]** **\[Enforcing
Display Stats...\]** **\[Warning: Output limited to Public
Parameters.\]**

Leon stumbled.

It felt like someone had suddenly thrown a wet wool blanket over his
soul.

The infinite, deep lake of his Core didn't vanish, but a massive dam
slammed down in front of it. The "Neutron Star" density he had grown
accustomed to was walled off.

He squeezed his walnut staff.

It felt... heavy.

For the last week, the staff had felt like a twig. Now, it felt like six
pounds of solid wood. He flexed his biceps. They didn't feel like
hydraulic pistons anymore. They felt like muscle. Good, honest muscle,
but just muscle.

*Strength 10,* Leon realized. *I am actually Strength 10 right now.*

He tested his footing. He felt lighter, less grounded. If he tried to
Root against a bear like this, he would be ragdolled into the next
county.

"Uh oh," Leon muttered.

"Ready to be flattened, Farmer?"

Gorlag the Destroyer stood across the ring. He was a mountain of a man,
easily six-foot-five, wearing a flour-stained apron over leather armor.
His weapon was, indeed, a reinforced iron-shod rolling pin the size of a
fence post.

\[Gorlag - Baker - Level 7\]

Leon's **Wisdom 9** (thankfully still active, as it was within the cap)
analyzed the opponent.

*Gorlag Strength Estimate: 14.* *Leon Current Strength: 10.*

"This seems fair," Leon admitted to himself. "Safety first. If I hit him
with my real strength, he would turn into puff pastry."

But fair didn't mean easy. For the first time in years, Leon was the
underdog in terms of raw power.

"FIGHT!" the referee shouted.

Gorlag didn't waste time. He roared---a sound like a bear waking
up---and charged. He swung the massive rolling pin in a wide, horizontal
arc aimed at Leon's ribs.

Leon's instinct was to simply stand there and stop the weapon with one
hand.

Wait. Muffler.

If he tried to tank a Level 7 swing with Level 4 stats, his ribs would
crack.

Leon moved.

He didn't float. The 'Oil' sensation in his joints was gone. He had to
use actual effort. He ducked under the swing, feeling the wind of the
weapon ruffle his hair.

He popped up inside Gorlag's guard and thrust his staff toward the
baker's stomach.

Thump.

It was a solid hit. But instead of Gorlag doubling over or flying
backward, the big man just grunted. He absorbed the Level 4 impact with
his layer of 'Baker's padding.'

Gorlag grinned. "Tickles!"

He brought the rolling pin down in a vertical smash.

Leon raised his staff to block, gripping it with both hands.

CLANG.

The impact jarred Leon to his bones. His boots slid backward in the
dirt, leaving six-inch furrows. His arms shook.

*He's stronger than me,* Leon thought, a thrill of genuine excitement
shooting through him. *I have to actually try.*

The crowd gasped. They saw Leon get pushed back. They saw him struggle.

"Get him, Gorlag! Mash him!"

Gorlag wound up for another swing. "I'm gonna knead you like dough,
boy!"

Leon took a breath. He couldn't win a contest of force. The System
wouldn't let him.

But the System can't nerf geometry.

Grandpa's voice echoed in his head: *"Don't break the nail. Bend it."*

Gorlag swung again---a haymaker from the right.

Leon didn't block. He stepped *into* the swing, just like he practiced.
But instead of stopping the arm, he hooked his staff under Gorlag's
armpit.

Leverage point acquired.

Leon used his **Agility 9**. He spun.

He clamped the staff down, trapping Gorlag's arm against the wood. He
placed his hip against Gorlag's hip.

"Center of gravity," Leon whispered.

He pulled down on the front of the staff and pushed up with his hips.

It was the same motion he used to uproot a stubborn stump. You don't
pull it straight up; you rock it until the roots give way.

Gorlag's eyes went wide. His feet left the ground.

"Whoa!"

Leon didn't have the strength to throw him across the ring. But he had
enough strength to tip him over.

He guided the massive baker over his hip. Gorlag rotated in the air, his
limbs flailing.

THUD.

Gorlag hit the dirt flat on his back. The wind left him in a wheezing
*whoosh*.

Leon didn't stop. He knew Gorlag had higher Constitution. He would get
up.

Leon spun the staff and gently placed the tip of it against Gorlag's
throat. Not hard enough to choke, but firm enough to pin.

Then, he sat down.

Literally. Leon sat on Gorlag's chest.

"Get off!" Gorlag wheezed, thrashing.

"I can't," Leon said calmly, pressing the staff down to pin the baker's
weapon arm. "You are very strong, Gorlag. If I let you up, you'll hit me
with that rolling pin. That would hurt."

"I... I can't breathe!"

"You're talking, so your airway is patent," Leon diagnosed. "I'm just
compressing your diaphragm. It's like a weighted blanket. Very calming."

Gorlag struggled for ten seconds. He tried to bench-press Leon, but Leon
shifted his weight, using his legs to hook Gorlag's legs. It was the
'Grappling Vine' technique---or as Leon called it, 'Holding the Pig for
the Vet.'

Finally, Gorlag's face turned a shade of purple that matched a ripe
turnip. He tapped the ground three times.

"Yield! I yield!"

"Winner! Leon the Farmer!" the referee shouted, looking mildly
surprised.

The muffler disengaged instantly.

WHOOSH.

The power flooded back into Leon's core. The neutron star returned. He
felt weightless again.

He stood up, offering a hand to Gorlag.

"Good match," Leon said sincerely. "You have excellent rotational
torque. Your obliques must be very developed."

Gorlag took the hand, letting Leon hoist him up effortlessly. The baker
rubbed his chest, looking at Leon with new respect.

"You're heavy for a twig," Gorlag grunted. "What kind of farming do you
do?"

"mostly rocks," Leon smiled.

As he walked out of the ring, the crowd cheered. It wasn't the mocking
laughter of "L.O.L." anymore. It was the appreciative roar for a
technical victory.

Leon looked up at the stands. He saw Clara cheering.

Then he looked at the weapon rack near the entrance.

The black cat was still there. It sat tall, its green eyes glowing. It
gave him a slow blink.

*Acceptable,* the cat seemed to say. *But your footwork was sloppy in
the second measure.*

Leon wiped the sweat from his brow.

"I missed my stats," he muttered to himself. "But I think I like the
challenge."
