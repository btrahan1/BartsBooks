# Chapter Twenty-Five: The Lesson in Restraint

The final match of Group C was drawing a crowd. The energy in the arena
had shifted. It wasn't the festive, cheering atmosphere of the earlier
rounds. It was tighter, sharper. Like the air before a thunderstorm.

Clara stood by the railing of the holding pen, her knuckles white as she
gripped the wood.

"That's Gorlach," she whispered, her voice trembling. "Not Gorlag the
Baker. *Gorlach*."

Leon looked across the arena. His opponent was pacing like a caged
tiger. He was a slab of a man, shirtless to show off a roadmap of scars
and muscles that seemed to be fighting each other for space under his
skin. He wasn't carrying a practice weapon. He was wearing spiked brass
knuckles.

"Gorlag. Gorlach," Leon mused, checking his staff for splinters. "The
nomenclature is confusing. Is he a baker too?"

"He's a debt collector," Clara said grimly. "For the shady guilds in the
lower district. In the last round, his opponent tapped out, and Gorlach
broke his arm anyway. Just to hear it snap."

Leon stopped checking his staff.

His hands went still. The playful light in his brown eyes vanished,
replaced by the flat, hard look of a man who spots a wolf stalking the
lamb pen.

"He ignored the yield?" Leon asked softly.

"Yes. The judges didn't disqualify him because he claimed he 'couldn't
stop his momentum.' But everyone saw him smile."

Leon looked at Gorlach. The big man was sneering at the crowd, flexing
his biceps and spitting on the ground.

"Grandma needs new spectacles," Leon said, his voice dropping an octave.
"And someone needs a lesson in restraint."

He stood up. He didn't bounce on his toes. He didn't cycle his Ki for
agility. He planted his heels.

"Be careful, Leon!" Clara called out as he walked to the gate. "He
fights dirty!"

"Dirt washes off," Leon said calmly. "Broken bones do not."

"Final Match!" The announcer yelled, sounding a little apprehensive.
"Leon the Farmer versus Gorlach the Bone-Breaker!"

The crowd booed Gorlach. Gorlach soaked it up, grinning. He turned his
eyes to Leon.

"A farmer?" Gorlach laughed, cracking his knuckles. The sound was like
pistol shots. "I'm gonna use that stick to pick my teeth, boy. After I
snap your legs."

Leon didn't smile. He didn't bow.

"You hurt your last opponent after he yielded," Leon stated. It wasn't a
question. It was a charge.

"He was weak," Gorlach shrugged. "Weak things break. That's nature."

"No," Leon corrected, tightening his grip on the walnut staff. "Nature
kills for food or defense. Only monsters break things for fun. And on my
farm, we don't tolerate monsters."

"FIGHT!"

Gorlach didn't charge. He reached into his belt and threw a handful of
sand straight at Leon's face.

The crowd gasped.

Leon's **Wisdom 9** had predicted it. *Dirty fighter. Projectile
imminent.*

Leon didn't flinch. He just closed his eyes.

He didn't need to see Gorlach. He could hear the heavy, aggressive stomp
of his boots. He could smell the stale ale and cruelty coming off him.

Gorlach lunged, thinking Leon was blinded. He aimed a spiked fist at
Leon's throat.

Leon moved.

It wasn't the floating waltz of the Agility upgrade. It was the
implacable force of the Strength upgrade.

Leon stepped inside the punch. He dropped his staff, letting it fall to
the dirt. He didn't want a weapon. He wanted hands-on control.

He caught Gorlach's wrist.

GRIP.

Leon's **Strength 10** (Muffler Engaged) met Gorlach's Strength 12.

Gorlach was stronger on paper. But Leon wasn't using strength to push.
He was using strength to *hold*. It was the Blacksmith's Vice grip.

Gorlach's eyes went wide. He tried to yank his hand back. He couldn't.
It was like his arm was embedded in stone.

"Let go!" Gorlach roared, swinging his other fist.

Leon caught that one too.

Now they were locked, chest to chest. Gorlach snarled, trying to
headbutt Leon.

Leon tilted his head, letting Gorlach's forehead graze his ear, then
slammed his own forehead into Gorlach's nose.

Crunch.

It was a controlled headbutt. Enough to water the eyes, not enough to
shatter the skull.

Gorlach stumbled back, blood streaming from his nose. "You...!"

"Restraint," Leon said calmly. "Rule number one: Control your animal."

Gorlach roared and tackled Leon.

They hit the dirt. Gorlach was on top, raining blows down.

The crowd screamed.

But Leon wasn't taking damage. He had his guard up, his forearms
absorbing the hits. With **Constitution 10**, his bones were dense as
ironwood. Gorlach wasn't hurting Leon; he was bruising his own knuckles
on Leon's arms.

"Are you done?" Leon asked from beneath the barrage.

"Die!" Gorlach screamed.

"Okay," Leon said. "Lesson time."

Leon bucked his hips. *The Plow Twist.*

He threw Gorlach off him. As the big man rolled, Leon scrambled up and
pounced.

He didn't punch. He moved behind Gorlach. He slipped his arms under
Gorlach's armpits and locked his hands behind the man's neck.

The Full Nelson.

"This is a containment hold," Leon explained into Gorlach's ear.

He applied pressure.

Gorlach thrashed. He kicked. He screamed. But Leon was a backpack of
solid muscle.

"You like breaking arms?" Leon whispered. "It takes seven pounds of
pressure to dislocate a shoulder in this position. I am currently
applying four."

He squeezed.

Gorlach howled in pain.

"Yield," Leon commanded.

"Never!"

Leon increased the pressure. "Five pounds. Your rotator cuff is
straining. If I go to six, the tendon tears. If I go to seven, you can't
feed yourself for three months."

The cold, clinical description terrified Gorlach more than any threat of
death. Leon wasn't angry. He was doing math with Gorlach's anatomy.

"Six pounds," Leon said.

Gorlach felt his shoulder joint pop. "AGHH! OKAY! YIELD! I YIELD!"

Leon didn't let go immediately.

"The last man yielded too," Leon said, his voice hard. "And you broke
him. Why should I trust you?"

"I swear! My arm! Please!" Gorlach was sobbing now.

Leon held him for three more seconds---an eternity in a fight---letting
the fear sink deep into Gorlach's bones.

"If you ever hurt someone after a yield again," Leon whispered, "I will
find you. And I won't stop at six pounds."

He released the hold.

Gorlach collapsed into the dirt, cradling his shoulder, weeping. He
wasn't broken, but his ego was pulverized.

"Winner! Leon the Farmer!"

The crowd was silent for a heartbeat, stunned by the brutality of the
efficiency. Then, they erupted. It was a roar of approval. They had seen
justice done.

Leon stood up. He picked up his staff and dusted it off.

He looked down at Gorlach.

"Ice that shoulder," Leon advised calmly. "And drink plenty of water. It
helps with the shock."

He turned and walked away.

He didn't wave to the crowd this time. He just walked to the gate, his
face impassive.

Clara was waiting for him. She looked a little scared.

"Leon?" she asked.

Leon looked at her. The hard look in his eyes melted away, replaced by
his usual warmth.

"He was a very rude man," Leon said, rubbing his own forehead where he
had headbutted Gorlach. "I think I have a headache. Do we have any more
of those brioche buns?"

Clara let out a breath and hugged him. It was a fierce, impulsive hug.

"You got the spectacles," she whispered into his chest.

"Yes," Leon smiled, hugging her back gently with one arm. "Now Grandpa
can read the fine print on the seed packets. It's a good day."

From the weapon rack, the black cat watched them. It gave a slow,
satisfied blink, then stood up and stretched.

*Lesson delivered,* the cat seemed to think. *Now, about that fifty
silver... I believe salmon is expensive.*
