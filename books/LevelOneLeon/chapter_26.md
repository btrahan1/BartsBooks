# Chapter Twenty-Six: The Caretaker and the Goodbye

The black cat was sitting on the newly reinforced suspension of the
travel cart, testing the bounce.

"It is rated for mountain roads," Leon informed the animal. He was
polishing the brass fittings on the harness. "I used the ash wood from
the dungeon. It has a high elasticity coefficient."

The cat looked at him, then at the cart, and gave a short, affirmative
*chirp*.

"I need a name for you," Leon mused. "I can't just call you 'Cat.'
That's a category, not a designation."

He thought about it. The cat was black. It was observant. It judged
things silently and demanded high standards.

"Inspector," Leon decided. "I'll call you Inspector."

The cat froze. It turned its green eyes toward him. It seemed to roll
the word around in its mind. *Inspector.* It implied authority. It
implied a job.

The cat blinked slowly. *Acceptable.*

"Good," Leon nodded. "Inspector it is. Now, please get off the axle. I
need to grease the bearing."

"Talking to the livestock again?"

Grandpa Silas walked into the barn. He looked different today. He wasn't
leaning as heavily on his cane. There was a sharpness in his eyes that
Leon hadn't seen in years---the look of the Militia Sergeant who used to
drill recruits until they puked.

"He's not livestock," Leon corrected. "He's management. Grandpa, we need
to discuss the... field."

"The glowy cave in the woods?" Grandpa spat into the straw. "Lyra told
me about it before she left. Said you turned a death-pit into a compost
machine."

"It's a renewable resource," Leon said. "But it needs weeding. If I
leave for Ironhold, the skeletons will overpopulate. They might break
the Ward."

Grandpa walked over to the workbench. He picked up the spare walnut
staff---a slightly lighter one Leon had made years ago. He gave it a few
experimental swings. *Whoosh. Whoosh.* The air whistled.

"My knees are bad, Leon," Grandpa said. "But my arms still work. And
I've been bored. Watching corn grow is fine, but sometimes a man misses
the crunch of impact."

Leon looked at his grandfather. He saw the Level 15 stats hidden under
the wrinkled skin.

"They are Level 6 now," Leon warned. "They use formation tactics."

"I invented formation tactics," Grandpa scoffed. "Don't you worry about
the farm, boy. I'll keep the weeds down. It'll give me good exercise.
Besides, the bone dust makes the pumpkins huge. I want to win the County
Fair this year."

Leon felt a massive weight lift off his chest. "Okay. I'll transfer the
Keyholder permission to you tonight."

"Good," Grandpa nodded. "Now, go wash up. You smell like axle grease,
and Clara is due for dinner in ten minutes. Don't make the girl wait."

Dinner was a quiet affair, though the food was loud in its excellence.

Leon had prepared a pot roast using the prize-winning carrots and
potatoes from the North Field. The meat fell apart at the touch of a
fork. The gravy was rich and dark.

Clara ate with enthusiasm, but her eyes were sad.

She wore her best blue dress---the one she wore to church. She had
braided a blue ribbon into her hair. Leon noticed it immediately.
*Variable: Blue Ribbon. Context: Effort.*

"The roast is wonderful, Leon," Clara said softly.

"It's the clay soil," Leon explained, pouring her more cider. "It adds a
mineral depth to the potatoes."

Grandpa Silas wiped his mouth, stood up, and groaned theatrically.
"Well, my old bones are crying out for the mattress. You two young folks
finish the cider. Don't stay up too late talking about... yield rates."

He winked at Leon---a terrifying gesture involving his entire face---and
hobbled off to his room.

The silence in the kitchen stretched. The Inspector, who had been
sleeping under the stove, walked out and jumped into Leon's lap, curling
up possessively.

Clara looked at the cat, then at Leon.

"You're leaving," she said.

It wasn't a question.

"I am," Leon admitted. "Tomorrow morning. First light."

Clara traced the rim of her cider mug. "To Ironhold."

"To start," Leon said. "I need to see the world, Clara. I need to see if
my... if my density holds up out there. Oakhaven is safe. But I feel
like a tree that's outgrown its pot. If I stay, my roots will start to
strangle me."

Clara nodded. She looked out the window at the darkened bakery down the
road.

"I belong here," she said quietly. "I love the smell of the yeast in the
morning. I love knowing everyone who walks through the door. I love that
my uncle needs me."

"You are vital to this town," Leon said earnestly. "The algorithm of
Oakhaven relies on you. Without your bread, morale would drop by at
least 15%."

Clara laughed, a watery, choked sound. She reached across the table and
took his hand. Her palm was warm and dusted with flour.

"You're special, Leon. We all made fun of you for being Level One. But
you were just... waiting." She squeezed his hand. "Go. Go be a hero. Or
a farmer. Or whatever you are."

She reached into her pocket and pulled out a small cloth bundle.

"I made this. It's not magical. It doesn't have stats. But..."

Leon unwrapped it. It was a handkerchief, embroidered with infinite
care. It depicted a stalk of golden wheat crossed with a walnut staff.

"It's... accurate," Leon whispered. He felt a lump in his throat. "The
stitching is very high quality."

"Use it to wipe the sweat off," Clara said, standing up. "And when you
come back... bring me a recipe from Ironhold. Something exotic. Like...
cinnamon buns."

Leon stood up. He carefully folded the handkerchief and placed it in his
breast pocket, right over his heart.

"I will," Leon promised. "I will bring you the best cinnamon in the
world."

He walked her to the door. The night air was cool.

Clara paused on the porch. She looked up at him. The height difference
was significant---Leon loomed over her, broad and sturdy.

She went up on her tiptoes. Leon froze, his **Intellect 8** frantically
trying to calculate the trajectory.

She kissed him on the cheek. Soft, quick, and smelling of vanilla.

"Goodbye, L.O.L.," she whispered.

She turned and ran down the path, her blue ribbon fluttering in the
moonlight.

Leon stood on the porch, touching his cheek. The spot tingled.

The Inspector sat on the railing, watching the girl go.

"That was efficient," Leon said to the cat, his voice thick. "A clean
break. Minimal structural damage."

The cat meowed softly.

"I know," Leon admitted, looking at the empty road. "It still hurts a
little."

He turned back to the house. The cart was packed. The dungeon was
managed. The goodbyes were said.

"Get some sleep, Inspector," Leon said, extinguishing the porch lantern.
"Tomorrow, we hit the road. I hear the bandits in the foothills are very
rude."
