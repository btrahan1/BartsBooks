# Chapter Twenty-Eight: The Bandit Protocol

The first night on the King's Road was quiet. Unnervingly so.

Leon had pulled the cart off the main road into a small clearing
sheltered by an overhang of grey rock. He had unhitched Bessie, groomed
her down, and set up a small campfire.

He was currently cooking beans.

"It's not the same without the clay pot," Leon murmured, stirring the
tin mess kit. "Heat distribution is uneven. The beans on the bottom are
scorching."

The Inspector, perched on a rock, suddenly flattened his ears. His tail
twitched.

Leon didn't need the warning. His **Wisdom 9** picked up the sound of
snapping twigs fifty yards out. His **Grandmaster Grappling** instincts
told him exactly how many footsteps there were.

Three targets. Uneven gait. Poor stealth discipline.

"Company," Leon whispered to the cat. "Stay with the cow."

He stood up, dusting off his trousers. He didn't pick up his staff
immediately. He wanted to appear non-threatening. He picked up his ladle
instead.

Three men emerged from the brush. They were classic roadside bandits:
dirty leather armor, mismatched weapons, and teeth that hadn't seen a
brush in a decade.

"Ho there, traveler!" the leader grinned. He was holding a rusted
falchion. "Smells like good beans."

"They are scorched," Leon said honestly. "The tin is too thin."

"We'll take 'em anyway," the leader sneered, stepping closer. "And the
cart. And the cow. Beef fetches a high price in the capital."

Leon sighed. It was a deep, weary sound.

"Bessie is a dairy cow," Leon explained patiently. "She is five years
old. Her meat would be tough and stringy. She is valuable for milk and
companionship, not steaks."

"Did I ask for a lecture?" The leader spat. "Get him, boys!"

The two lackeys lunged. One had a club; the other had a dagger.

Leon looked at them.

In his mind, the **Combat Algorithm** flared to life. But it wasn't just
math anymore. It was **Skill Memory**.

He saw the Club Bandit swing. Leon didn't just see a club; he saw the
trajectory, the flaw in the grip, the weak point in the wood, and the
exact pressure point on the man's wrist to induce paralysis.

*Blunt Weapon Mastery (Level 42) says: Step left. Strike wrist.*
*Grappling (Level 48) says: Use his momentum to throw him into Dagger
Man.*

Leon moved.

He didn't use the ladle. He stepped forward, caught the Club Bandit's
wrist with his left hand, and twisted.

*CRACK.* (Not a bone, just the sound of the club hitting the ground).

"Yowch!" the bandit screamed.

Leon didn't stop. He pivoted, dragging the Club Bandit across his body
like a shield.

The Dagger Bandit tried to stop his thrust, but he was too slow. He
slammed into his friend.

Leon released the wrist and grabbed both men by the backs of their
collars.

Skill: Agrarian Arts (Sack Hauling).

He knocked their heads together.

THUNK.

It was a precise, controlled collision. Just enough force to scramble
the equilibrium. The two men went limp, sliding to the ground in a heap.

The Leader stared at Leon. He looked at his two unconscious goons. He
looked at the ladle in Leon's hand.

"You... you're a mage!" the Leader stammered. "Mind magic!"

"Physics," Leon corrected. "And momentum."

The Leader roared and charged, swinging the falchion.

Leon side-stepped. He brought the ladle down on the flat of the blade.

PING.

The vibration traveled up the sword. The Leader dropped it, clutching
his hand.

Leon swept the man's legs (a gentle tap of the boot) and caught him
before he hit the ground, lowering him gently. Then he applied a
\[Restraining Hold\].

"Please stay down," Leon said politely. "The ground is cold, but you
seem overheated."

The bandit leader looked up at Leon with terrifying clarity. He saw a
man who wasn't breathing hard, wasn't angry, and had just dismantled
three armed men in four seconds while holding a soup spoon.

"I yield!" the bandit squeaked. "Take the beans! Just don't kill me!"

"Go," Leon said, releasing him. "Take your friends. If I see you again,
I will have to be less polite."

The bandit scrambled up, woke his groggy friends with kicks, and they
fled into the night, screaming about a "Demon Chef."

Leon watched them go. He looked at the ladle. It was bent.

"Poor quality tin," he muttered.

DING.

The sound was sharp and immediate.

**\[System Alert: Anomaly Detected.\]** **\[Combat Efficiency Exceeds
Public Profile.\]** **\[Subject:** '**Level 4 Farmer**' **displayed
Grandmaster-tier technique.\]**

The blue screen flickered, and the familiar form of Adjudicator Beta
appeared (miniaturized this time, hovering over the campfire). She
looked annoyed.

"Mr. Leon," she sighed, adjusting her spectral glasses. "What did we
discuss about being inconspicuous?"

"They wanted to eat Bessie," Leon defended. "I used minimal force."

"You used a Level 48 Grappling technique to knock two heads together
with the precision of a Newton's Cradle," she snapped. "If a high-level
Paladin had seen that, they would have flagged you for investigation."

"So what do I do?" Leon asked. "Forget how to fight?"

"No. We filter it."

She tapped her clipboard.

"We are applying a **Skill Mask** to your Public Sheet. You keep the
skills, but to the outside world, they look... adorable."

\[System Update: Skill Mask Applied.\]

Leon watched his status screen shift.

- **Blunt Weapon Mastery (Lvl 42)** changed to -\> **\[Stick Waving
  (Novice)\]**
- **Unarmed Combat (Lvl 48)** changed to -\> **\[Tavern Brawling
  (Intermediate)\]**
- **Agrarian Arts (Lvl 99)** changed to -\> **\[Gardening (Hobbyist)\]**
- **Blacksmithing (Lvl 50)** changed to -\> **\[Metal Tinkering\]**

"Stick Waving?" Leon frowned. "That sounds undignified."

"It sounds harmless," the Adjudicator corrected. "And because you
successfully defended your cargo against hostile mobs, the System is
forced to award you Experience."

She tapped the screen again.

**\[Level Up!\]** **\[Public Level: 4 \> 5\]**

**\[Stats Adjusted for Level 5:\]** \* **Strength: 10 \> 12** \*
**Agility: 9 \> 11**

"There," she said. "Level 5. You are now officially an 'Apprentice
Adventurer.' You have permission to hit things slightly harder without
breaking the narrative."

She faded away. "Try to use the staff next time, Leon. The ladle is not
a sanctioned weapon."

Leon stood alone in the clearing. He looked at his new stats.

Level 5.

He looked at his skills.

Gardening (Hobbyist).

He chuckled. It was ridiculous. It was bureaucratic. But it was safe.

"Come on, Inspector," Leon said, sitting back down by the fire. "The
beans are done. And apparently, I'm just a hobbyist."

The cat looked at the bent ladle, then at the fleeing bandits' tracks,
and purred. *A hobbyist who breaks swords with spoons.*

Leon ate his burnt beans under the stars, feeling strangely content. He
was a fraud, a glitch, and a lie. But his cow was safe, and that was all
that mattered.
