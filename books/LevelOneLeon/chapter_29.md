# Chapter Twenty-Nine: The Professional Amateur

The road to Silt-River was winding and smelled of wet pine.

Leon sat on the bench of the cart, reins loosely in one hand, a piece of
charcoal in the other. He was writing on a fresh piece of slate.

"Inspector," Leon said, tapping the slate. "I have developed a theory."

The black cat, currently sunning itself on Bessie's rump, opened one
eye.

"Grandmaster status is suspicious," Leon explained. "If I walk into a
town and forge a masterwork sword, people ask questions. They ask for
licenses. They ask why a Level 5 Farmer is better than the Guild
Master."

The cat yawned. *Obviously.*

"However," Leon continued, circling a word on his slate. "If I am an
*Apprentice*, mistakes are expected. And excellence is just considered
'promising potential.' It is the perfect camouflage."

He showed the slate to the cat.

**THE LIST:** 1. **Find a Smithy:** *Apply as Apprentice. (Cover for
crafting).* 2. **Find a Tanner:** *Apply as Apprentice. (Process the
Bear Rug).* 3. **Local Gardening:** *Check for monster infestations.
Prune aggressively.* 4. **Culinary Research:** *Find cinnamon buns for
Clara.*

"I will be Leon the Apprentice," he declared. "I will be the best
student they have ever had. I will sweep floors with Level 99
efficiency."

Silt-River was larger than Oakhaven. It was a rugged, industrial town
built on the banks of a fast-flowing grey river. Giant water wheels
churned the water, powering sawmills and grain crushers. The air was
thick with the scent of sawdust and coal smoke.

The gate guards barely glanced at him.

"Name?" "Leon." "Class?" "Adventurer. Apprentice." "Level?" "Five."

The guard waved him through. "Keep the cow out of the main plaza. We
don't want dung on the cobblestones."

"Bessie is very polite," Leon assured him, but he guided the cart toward
the trade district anyway.

He parked the cart at a livery stable, paying extra for a deluxe stall
for Bessie.

"Fresh straw," Leon instructed the stable boy. "And she likes music. If
you can hum while you brush her, she produces sweeter milk."

The stable boy looked at the massive, battle-hardened farmer. "Uh...
sure. I know a few sea shanties."

Leon grabbed his pack and his staff. "Come along, Inspector. Time to
find a master."

The sign above the door read "**The Anvil & Oar.**"

It was a chaotic workshop. The heat was intense, and the noise was
deafening. Half-finished anchors, chains, and logging axes lay in piles.

A massive man with a beard singed on one side was screaming at a
furnace.

"Blast it! The carbon content is too low! It's cracking!"

Leon stepped inside. He took a deep breath. He smelled sulfur, iron,
and... frustration.

He walked up to the smith.

"Excuse me," Leon shouted over the roar of the bellows.

The smith spun around, wielding a pair of tongs like a weapon. "We're
closed! Backlogged! Unless you're here to deliver the coal shipment, get
out!"

"I am not the coal delivery," Leon said calmly. "I am Leon. I am looking
for an apprenticeship."

The smith blinked. He looked at Leon's clean tunic (Leon had used a
repulsion cantrip---or rather, a 'Soil Repelling' technique---to keep it
clean).

"An apprentice?" The smith scoffed. "Boy, I've fired three apprentices
this week. They whine about the heat. They drop the tongs. They can't
swing a hammer for more than ten minutes without crying."

"I have excellent stamina," Leon said. "And I follow instructions."

"Oh yeah?" The smith pointed to a pile of scrap metal in the corner---a
twisted mess of old chains and broken saw blades. "You want to learn?
Sort that scrap. High carbon steel in one bin, wrought iron in the
other, pig iron in the third. If you get one wrong, you ruin the batch."

It was a test designed to make a novice quit. Telling high carbon steel
from wrought iron by sight alone was difficult for even experienced
eyes. Usually, you had to spark-test it.

"Understood," Leon said.

He walked over to the pile.

He didn't use a spark test. He didn't use a file.

He picked up a rusty chain link. *Texture: Grainy.* *Weight: Dense.* *Ki
Resonance: Dull.*

"Wrought iron," Leon stated, tossing it into the left bin.

He picked up a saw blade. *Resonance: Sharp. Singing.* "High carbon,"
Leon said, tossing it right.

He moved like a machine. *Clang. Left. Clang. Right. Clang. Middle.*

The Inspector sat on a workbench, watching his human work.

The smith, whose name was Borrin, turned back to his furnace, muttering.
He expected the kid to give up in ten minutes.

Five minutes later, the noise stopped.

"Done," Leon announced.

Borrin turned around. "Don't lie to me, boy, I---"

He stopped. The massive pile of scrap was gone. The three bins were
filled to the brim. And not only were they sorted, but Leon had also
stacked the metal plates by size and coiled the chains neatly.

Borrin walked over. He picked up a piece from the 'High Carbon' bin. He
inspected it. He picked up another.

"How?" Borrin whispered. "You didn't even use a grinding wheel to check
the sparks."

"The metal speaks," Leon said simply. "You just have to listen to the
grain."

Borrin looked at Leon. He looked at the empty floor, which Leon had also
taken the liberty of sweeping while he was walking back and forth.

"You're hired," Borrin grunted. "Two coppers a day. You sleep in the
loft. Don't touch my hammer."

"Deal," Leon smiled. "I am eager to learn, Master Borrin."

"Right," Borrin rubbed his face. "Well. Since you're so fast... the
bellows are stuck. The airflow is uneven."

Leon walked over to the massive leather bellows. He inspected the hinge.

"The leather is dry," Leon diagnosed. "It's cracking, causing air leaks.
And the fulcrum pin is rusted."

He pulled a small jar of his homemade 'Livestock Salve' from his pocket.
He rubbed it into the leather. He applied a drop of oil to the pin.

He gave the handle a gentle pump.

WHOOSH.

The furnace roared to life, the flame turning a brilliant, hot white.

"Optimization complete," Leon noted.

Borrin stared at the fire. He had been fighting that fire for six
months.

"Who are you?" Borrin asked, eyeing Leon suspiciously.

"I'm Leon," Leon said, his face the picture of innocent eagerness. "I'm
Level 5. I used to be a farmer."

"A farmer," Borrin repeated.

"Yes. Bellows are a lot like milking a cow," Leon explained. "It's all
about the rhythm."

Borrin decided not to ask any more questions. Good help was hard to
find.

"Alright, Farmer," Borrin said. "Grab a hammer. Let's see if you can
make a nail without smashing your thumb."

Leon grabbed a hammer. He suppressed his Level 50 Grandmaster muscle
memory. He dialed his coordination down to 'Level 5 Apprentice.'

He hit the nail. He let it be *slightly* crooked.

"Oops," Leon said. "I need to work on my angle."

"Keep your elbow tucked!" Borrin barked, falling into teacher-mode.
"Straighten your back!"

"Yes, Master!" Leon chirped.

*Item 1: Check,* Leon thought.

"By the way, Master Borrin," Leon asked between hammer strikes. "I
noticed the local flora is... agitated. Are there any pests in the area?
I enjoy gardening in my off hours."

Borrin snorted. "Gardening? Unless you plant rocks, good luck. But if
you want pests... the Silt-River Mud Crabs have been swarming the lower
docks. Size of dinner tables. They've been snapping the water wheel
paddles."

Leon's eyes lit up.

*Mud Crabs.* *Shells = Lime/Calcium for fertilizer.* *Meat = High
protein rations.*

"Terrible," Leon said solemnly. "Someone should really prune them."

The Inspector purred.

It was going to be a productive week.
