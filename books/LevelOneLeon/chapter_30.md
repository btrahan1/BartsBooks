# Chapter Thirty: The Midnight Maintenance Crew

The church bell of Silt-River chimed three times.

In the loft above the *Anvil & Oar*, the air was thick with the sound of
snoring. Master Borrin snored like a clogged bellows. The other
apprentice, a lanky boy named Jace, whistled when he exhaled.

Leon lay on his straw pallet, eyes wide open.

He had gone to sleep at midnight. It was now 3:00 AM.

His body felt... done.

Usually, a day of sorting scrap metal and hauling coal would leave a man
wrecked until dawn. But Leon's **Constitution** was now **12** (Public
Level 5 adjusted, though his internal Foundation was likely far higher).
His metabolic recovery rate was absurd. His muscles had repaired
themselves, his energy stores had refilled, and his brain was bored.

"This is inefficient," Leon whispered to the rafters. "Lying horizontal
when fully charged is a waste of daylight. Even if there is no
daylight."

He sat up. The straw didn't even creak; his **Agility 11** allowed him
to move like smoke.

A pair of green eyes glowed in the darkness. The Inspector was sitting
on a crate, watching him.

*You're awake,* the cat's posture said. *Finally. I'm hungry.*

"I can't cook here, Inspector," Leon whispered, pulling on his boots.
"Master Borrin counts the coal. But... Master Borrin also mentioned the
water wheel was losing torque."

Leon grabbed his walnut staff and his canvas sack. He crept to the
window, unlatched it silently, and slipped out onto the roof.

The night air was cool and smelled of river mud. Silt-River was asleep,
the industrial clamor replaced by the rushing sound of the water.

"Let's go inspect the infrastructure," Leon said.

The docks were shrouded in mist. The massive water wheels---great wooden
structures three stories tall---churned sluggishly in the current.

Leon approached the main wheel that powered the smithy's district. He
frowned.

*Rhythm analysis: Irregular.* *Sound: Grinding.* *Efficiency: 65%.*

"It's off-balance," Leon diagnosed. "Something is dragging on the
paddles."

He walked to the edge of the pier and peered down into the murky water.

There, clinging to the submerged wood, were the Mud Crabs.

They were massive---the size of wagon wheels---with shells that looked
like wet boulders. They were snapping at the wood, tearing off chunks of
the treated oak to get at the algae growing in the grain.

**\[River Mud Crab - Level 8\]** **\[Behavior: Aggressive Scavenger\]**

"Vandalism," Leon declared softly.

He looked around. The docks were deserted. No guards. No witnesses.

"Inspector, secure the perimeter. I'm going down."

The cat hopped onto a piling to keep watch.

Leon didn't jump. He stepped off the pier and slid down a support pylon,
gripping the slick wood with his **Grandmaster Grappling** friction
control. He landed softly on a maintenance walkway just inches above the
rushing water.

A Mud Crab saw him. It clicked its claws menacingly and scuttled
forward, foam dripping from its mandibles.

Leon sighed. "You are damaging public property."

The crab lunged.

Leon didn't use his staff this time. He wanted to test his **\[Tavern
Brawling (Intermediate)\]**---or rather, his masked Unarmed Combat.

He stepped inside the claw snap. He grabbed the crab by the base of its
eye-stalks and the rear of its shell.

Leverage point.

"You need to be relocated," Leon grunted.

He lifted the two-hundred-pound crustacean effortlessly. He spun. He
tossed it.

The crab flew through the air, flailing, and landed with a splash in the
deep current, far away from the wheel.

"One," Leon counted.

Two more crabs emerged from the water, clacking their claws.

"Shells," Leon noted, looking at their backs. "High calcium carbonate
content. Good for neutralizing the acidity in the tomato patch."

He changed tactics.

He swung his staff. He didn't smash the shells (that would ruin the
harvest). He aimed for the nerve cluster at the base of the neck.

Tap. Tap.

The crabs went limp instantly. Leon hooked them with the staff and
dragged them onto the walkway. He pulled out his skinning knife.

He worked fast. It wasn't combat; it was processing.

*Harvest meat (Keep for stew).* *Clean shell.* *Stack.*

In twenty minutes, the walkway was clear of live crabs. A neat stack of
cleaned shells sat by the wall.

But the wheel was still groaning.

Leon looked at the axle. One of the iron brackets had been bent by the
crabs' weight. It was grinding against the stone housing.

"Metal Tinkering," Leon muttered.

He didn't have a forge. He didn't have a hammer.

He put his hands on the thick iron bracket. It was cold and wet.

He cycled his Ki. **Blacksmithing Level 50** flared in his veins. He
didn't need heat to bend metal; heat just made the molecules excited.
Leon could excite them with pure kinetic resonance.

He *vibrated* his hands. A low hum filled the air under the pier.

"Bend," Leon commanded.

He pushed. The cold iron groaned, protesting, but under Leon's grip, it
moved. He realigned the bracket, smoothing out the warp until it sat
flush against the stone.

The grinding noise stopped.

The wheel's rotation smoothed out. It picked up speed. *Whoosh-splash.
Whoosh-splash.*

"Efficiency: 98%," Leon nodded, satisfied.

He wiped the grease from his hands onto a rag. He packed the crab meat
and the smaller shell fragments into his sack.

"A productive nap," Leon said to the Inspector.

The next morning, the *Anvil & Oar* was buzzing with confusion.

Master Borrin was staring at the main drive shaft that ran through the
ceiling. It was spinning faster and smoother than it had in years. The
trip-hammers were pounding with a vigorous, rapid rhythm.

"I don't understand," Borrin muttered, scratching his singed beard. "The
water flow hasn't changed. Why is the torque up by thirty percent?"

Leon walked in from the back room, carrying a broom. He looked fresh,
rested, and annoyingly cheerful.

"Good morning, Master Borrin," Leon chirped. "I made breakfast. Crab
bisque."

"Bisque?" Borrin blinked. "Where did you get crab? Those river bastards
tear nets to shreds."

"I found some... lying around the docks," Leon said innocently. "I think
they had an accident."

He set a steaming bowl on the workbench. It smelled rich, creamy, and
was garnished with wild chives Leon had found growing between the
cobblestones.

"Also," Leon added, sweeping a pile of dust. "I think the river spirits
fixed the wheel. I heard a hum last night. Must have been a blessing."

Borrin looked at the wheel. He looked at the bisque. He looked at his
new apprentice, who was currently sweeping with a rhythm that was almost
hypnotic.

"River spirits," Borrin grunted, taking a spoonful of soup. He tasted
it. His eyes widened. It was the best thing he had eaten in a decade.
**\[Comfort Food\]** buff applied: *Mental stress reduced.*

"Right. Spirits," Borrin agreed, deciding not to question the universe
when it was giving him free horsepower and gourmet soup. "Well, don't
just stand there, boy. Since the hammers are fast today, we're doubling
the quota on horseshoe nails!"

"Yes, Master!" Leon beamed.

He liked the fast hammers. It meant he could work out his frustrations
about not having a farm to plow.

And tonight? Tonight he was going to check the saw-mill. He had heard a
rumor about termites, and Leon had very strong opinions about
wood-boring insects.
