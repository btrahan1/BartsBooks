# Chapter Thirty-One: The Unmovable Polite Object

Leon sat on a crate outside the *Anvil & Oar*, consulting his slate.

**Social Quest Log:** 1. *Identify Town Morale Leaks.* 2. *Apply Verbal
Fertilizer (Compliments).* 3. *Prune Negativity.*

"It is a simple equation, Inspector," Leon explained to the cat, who was
busy grooming a speck of coal dust from its paw. "If I increase the
ambient happiness of the sector, the economy flows better. People buy
more horseshoes. Master Borrin yells less."

The cat stopped grooming and gave him a look. *You just want people to
stop staring at you like you're a weirdo.*

"That too," Leon admitted.

He stood up, adjusted his tunic, and marched into the bustling street.
It was lunch hour, and Silt-River was teeming with workers.

Leon spotted his first target: Elara (no relation to the Ranger), a
barmaid at the *Salty Eel* tavern who was currently balancing five heavy
tankards on one tray.

Leon engaged the **Social Algorithm**.

*Observation: High dexterity. Significant wrist stability.* *Emotional
Hook: Validation of effort.*

Leon walked up to her. "Excuse me, Miss."

Elara flinched, expecting a complaint. "Yeah? What is it? Ale is two
coppers."

"I just wanted to observe," Leon said seriously, "that your center of
gravity is impeccable. Carrying that much fluid mass without spilling a
drop requires elite-level proprioception. You must have tendons of
steel."

Elara blinked. She looked at her tray. She looked at Leon's earnest,
square-jawed face.

"I... well, thanks," she stammered, a flush rising on her neck. "My
wrist does ache sometimes."

"You should soak it in willow-bark tea," Leon advised kindly. "It
reduces inflammation. Keep up the good work. You are the structural
pillar of this establishment."

He nodded and walked away.

Elara stood there, grinning like an idiot. "Tendons of steel," she
whispered, standing a little taller.

Target Two: A scrawny boy of about twelve, struggling to drag a sack of
grain toward the bakery.

Leon approached. The boy looked terrified.

"Do not lift with your back," Leon instructed gently.

The boy froze. "I... it's heavy, sir."

"It is heavy because you are fighting the physics," Leon said. He knelt
down. "Widen your stance. Drop your hips. Engage the glutes. Like this."

He demonstrated the posture. The boy mimicked him.

"Now, drive through the heels," Leon commanded.

The boy pushed. The sack lifted. It felt lighter.

"I did it!" the boy gasped.

"Excellent form," Leon beamed. "You generated significant torque. You
are going to be a very strong man one day."

The boy's chest puffed out. He looked at Leon with hero-worship in his
eyes. "Thanks, Mister Leon!"

Leon checked his mental list. *Morale Fertilizer applied.*

He spent the next hour walking the market. He complimented a weaver on
her thread tension. He told a fishmonger that his ice-packing technique
was scientifically sound. He helped an old woman tighten the loose wheel
on her cart (using his bare hands as a wrench).

Everywhere he went, he left a trail of confused but smiling people.

But not everyone was smiling.

Leaning against the wall of the General Store were three young men. They
wore leather vests with iron studs---too many studs to be practical,
just enough to be edgy. They were the "Ironhead Gang," a group of local
toughs who made their money by "protecting" vendors from accidents that
they themselves caused.

Their leader, a jagged-toothed youth named Brance, watched Leon help the
old woman.

"Look at him," Brance spat. "The new guy. The 'Apprentice.' Walking
around like he owns the place."

"He's making us look bad, Brance," one of his lackeys muttered. "He
fixed Old Maude's cart for free. We were gonna charge her five silvers
to 'escort' it home."

Brance cracked his knuckles. "He's disrupting the market. Time for a
little correction."

Leon was just inspecting a barrel of cinnamon (finding it acceptable,
though slightly dry) when a shadow fell over him.

He turned. Brance and his two goons were blocking his path.

"Hey, Farm Boy," Brance sneered.

Leon looked at them. His **Wisdom 9** pinged immediately.

*Threat Assessment: Low.* *Posturing: High.* *Social Intent:
Intimidation.*

"Hello," Leon said pleasantly. "I like your vests. The stud spacing is
very aggressive. It must offer decent abrasion resistance."

Brance blinked, thrown off rhythm. "Shut up about the vest. You're
causing trouble, apprentice."

"I am?" Leon frowned. "I haven't broken anything today. Except a crab
shell, but that was sanitation."

"You're stepping on toes," Brance growled, stepping into Leon's personal
space. He poked Leon in the chest. "This is Ironhead territory. If
people need help, they pay us. You doing favors for free... that's bad
for business."

Leon looked at the finger poking his chest. It felt like a twig poking a
mountain.

"You charge people to help them?" Leon asked. "That seems inefficient.
Cooperation yields higher community dividends."

"I said shut up!" Brance shoved Leon.

Or rather, he tried to.

Brance put his weight into the shove. He expected Leon to stumble back.

But Leon's **Constitution 12** (Public) and **Foundation Density**
(Actual) meant that Leon had the mass of a granite statue.

Brance's hands hit Leon's chest. Leon didn't move a millimeter. Brance,
however, bounced off his own shove, stumbling backward.

"What the..." Brance stared at Leon. "What are you made of? Lead?"

"Bone and corn-fed muscle," Leon replied. "And you should check your
footing. You have no traction."

"Get him!" Brance screamed, embarrassed.

The two lackeys lunged. One swung a fist; the other tried to tackle
Leon.

Leon sighed. *Social Interaction Failed. Initiating Safety Protocol.*

He didn't want to hurt them. That would be rude.

He side-stepped the punch (**Agility 11**), caught the attacker's arm,
and guided it gently into a "Chicken Wing" hold.

"Your elbow is hyperextending," Leon noted helpfully. "Stop moving or it
will pop."

The tackler hit Leon's waist. It was like tackling a tree trunk. The boy
just slid down Leon's leg, dazed.

Brance pulled a knife. A clumsy, rusty shiv.

"I'm gonna cut you!"

The crowd in the market gasped.

Leon looked at the knife.

"That is a safety hazard," Leon said firmly.

He released the Chicken Wing boy (who slumped to the ground) and stepped
toward Brance.

Brance stabbed.

Leon's hand moved in a blur---**\[Stick Waving (Novice)\]** translated
into a palm deflect. He slapped the flat of the blade, knocking it wide.

Then, Leon stepped in and hugged him.

It was the **Bear Hug of Peace**.

Leon wrapped his arms around Brance, trapping the boy's arms to his
sides. He squeezed. Just a little. Just enough to compress the air out
of the lungs and lift Brance two inches off the ground.

"Let... go..." Brance wheezed, his feet kicking uselessly.

"I cannot," Leon whispered into his ear. "You have a weapon. Until you
drop it, you are a danger to yourself and others. This is a mandatory
hug."

Clatter.

The knife hit the cobblestones.

"I dropped it! I dropped it!"

Leon held him for another five seconds, just to let the calmness settle
in.

"Violence is a cry for attention," Leon said to the gathered crowd,
still holding the thug. "Brance is just frustrated. Perhaps he needs a
hobby. Have you tried knitting? It is very soothing."

He set Brance down.

Brance looked at Leon with sheer terror. He looked at the crowd, who
were snickering.

"You're a freak!" Brance yelled, his voice cracking. He scrambled back,
tripping over his own lackey. "This isn't over!"

The Ironhead Gang scrambled away, retreating into the alleyways.

Leon picked up the knife. He tested the edge with his thumb. "Dull. And
rusty. Tetanus risk."

He walked over to a nearby trash barrel and dropped it in.

"Are you okay, Mister Leon?"

It was the scrawny boy from earlier. He was looking at Leon with eyes
the size of dinner plates.

"I am fine," Leon smiled, adjusting his tunic. "Just a minor
disagreement about community service."

**\[System Notification: Social Standing Updated.\]** **\[Reputation
with Silt-River Commoners: Friendly.\]** **\[Reputation with Criminal
Element: Hostile.\]** **\[Charisma: 7 \> 8\]**

Leon checked his slate.

"Item 3: Prune Negativity," he muttered. "Check."

He turned to the boy. "Now, do you know where I can find the best
cinnamon in town? I have a delivery to plan."

From the roof of the General Store, the Inspector watched the scene. The
cat licked its paw, satisfied.

*The human is finally learning,* the cat thought. *A hug is just a
stranglehold with better PR.*
