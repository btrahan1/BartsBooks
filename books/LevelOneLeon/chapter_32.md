# Chapter Thirty-Two: The Aggression Redirection Program

It was dusk when Leon found the Ironhead Gang again.

They were huddled behind the old mill, kicking empty crates into the
river. The mood was sour. Their leader, Brance, was throwing rocks at a
floating log, muttering curses about "hugging farmers."

Leon approached silently. His **Agility 11** meant his boots made no
sound on the gravel. He was carrying a large burlap sack over one
shoulder.

"That log did nothing to you," Leon observed.

The gang jumped. Brance spun around, hand going for a backup knife he
had concealed in his boot.

"You!" Brance snarled, backing up until he hit the brick wall of the
mill. "You come to gloat? Or are you gonna hug us to death?"

"I am here to discuss efficiency," Leon said, dropping the heavy burlap
sack. It hit the ground with a dull, clattering thud.

Leon opened the sack. Inside were the cleaned, iridescent shells of
three massive Mud Crabs.

"Do you know what the Alchemist Guild pays for intact Mud Crab
carapace?" Leon asked.

Brance stared at the shells. "Uh... no?"

"Four silver pieces per pound," Leon said. "Because it is hard to get.
The crabs are aggressive. They have high armor ratings. Most adventurers
smash the shell to kill them, which ruins the value."

Leon looked at Brance. He looked at the two lackeys, Jory and Pike.

"I observed your combat style earlier," Leon said calmly. "You prefer
swarming tactics. You flank. You use short blades. And you have..." He
gestured to their studded vests. "...very thick leather armor."

"Yeah?" Brance bristled. "So what?"

"So, you are perfectly spec'd for Crab Hunting," Leon concluded. "You
are wasting your potential mugging old ladies for coppers when you could
be making gold in the mud flats."

The boys looked at each other. The anger in their eyes was replaced by
confusion, and then a flicker of greed.

"Gold?" Pike asked.

"The crabs come out at low tide," Leon said. "In twenty minutes. They
are dangerous. They swarm. They fight dirty."

Leon stepped closer, his voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper.

"They are exactly like you. But they taste better."

Brance hesitated. "You want us to... work?"

"I want you to stop being inefficient," Leon corrected. "You have
aggression. You have weapons. You have a gang. Why not be a *mercenary
company* instead of a nuisance? Mercenaries get respect. Nuisances get
hugged."

Brance looked at his knife. He looked at the river.

"Show us," Brance said.

The mud flats were a treacherous expanse of grey slime and jagged rocks.
As the tide went out, the Mud Crabs emerged---clicking, hissing
nightmares the size of coffee tables.

"Formation," Leon instructed.

He stood on a dry rock, holding his staff like a conductor's baton. The
Ironhead Gang stood in the muck, looking disgusted.

"Target at three o'clock," Leon pointed. "Jory, you take the front.
Distract it. Use your club to block the claws. Don't hit the shell."

Jory stepped forward, waving his club. The crab hissed and snapped.

"Brance, Pike. Flank," Leon commanded. "Go for the leg joints. Use the
pry-bar motion I showed you. Don't stab; *lever*."

Brance moved. To his surprise, he was fast. Years of running from city
guards had given him decent agility. He slipped behind the crab while it
was focused on Jory.

He jammed his knife into the soft tissue of the joint and twisted.

CRACK.

The crab's leg gave way. The beast listed to the side.

"Now!" Leon shouted. "Flip it!"

The three boys worked together. They grabbed the shell rim and heaved.
The massive crab flipped onto its back, its soft underbelly exposed.

"Finish it," Leon said. "Clean kill. Blade to the nerve cluster."

Brance drove his dagger home. The crab went still.

They stood there, panting, covered in mud.

"We... we got it," Pike breathed. "That thing could have taken my hand
off."

"But it didn't," Leon said, stepping down from his rock. "Because you
worked as a unit. You protected each other."

He walked over to the carcass.

"Intact shell," Leon appraised. "High quality meat. That's about fifteen
silver pieces right there. Split three ways, that's five silver each for
two minutes of work."

Brance looked at his muddy hands. Then he looked at Leon. There was no
mockery in the farmer's face. Only professional approval.

"Five silver," Brance whispered. That was more than they made in a week
of shakedowns.

"There are six more crabs down by the pier," Leon noted, pointing with
his staff. "Are you tired? Or are you hungry?"

Brance grinned. It wasn't the jagged, cruel grin of a bully. It was the
fierce grin of a hunter.

"Let's get 'em, boys," Brance shouted.

Two hours later, the Ironhead Gang marched into the Town Square.

They were filthy. They smelled like river bottom. But they were dragging
a makeshift sled piled high with crab shells and meat.

The townspeople stopped and stared. They expected Brance to yell insults
or demand a toll.

Instead, Brance walked up to the Alchemist's shop and kicked the door
open.

"Delivery!" Brance shouted. "Fresh carapace! Top grade! And don't try to
lowball us, we know the market rates!"

Leon stood in the shadows of the alley, watching.

The Alchemist came out, looking terrified, then confused, then delighted
as he saw the haul. He started counting out silver coins.

The boys cheered, high-fiving each other.

"They have poor hygiene," the Inspector noted from Leon's shoulder.

"They're working on it," Leon whispered. "But look at their posture.
Shoulders back. Heads up."

Brance spotted Leon in the alley. The boy froze. He looked at the bag of
coins in his hand.

He walked over to Leon. He didn't sneer. He didn't bow. He just nodded,
man to man.

"We're gonna buy new boots," Brance said gruffly. "Better traction. For
the mud."

"A wise investment," Leon agreed. "Good boots are the foundation of a
good worker."

"We... uh..." Brance scratched his nose, leaving a streak of mud. "We're
going out again tomorrow night. If you... if you spot any big nests..."

"I will mark them on the map," Leon promised.

"Right. Cool." Brance turned to leave, then stopped. "Hey, Farmer.
Thanks. For not... you know. Breaking our arms."

"I prefer to fix things," Leon said.

Brance laughed. "Yeah. We noticed."

He ran back to his gang. "Alright, Ironheads! First round of meat pies
is on me! Then we sharpen the blades! We got work to do!"

Leon watched them go.
