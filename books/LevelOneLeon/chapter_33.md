# Chapter Thirty-Three: The Reparations Protocol

The morning fog still clung to the riverbanks when Brance found Leon
behind the *Anvil & Oar*. Leon was organizing the scrap pile again,
mostly because he found the sound of sorting metal meditative.

Brance looked different. He was wearing new boots---sturdy, leather ones
with good tread---and his face was clean. But he looked miserable.

"It didn't work," Brance grumbled, kicking a piece of slag.

"The boots?" Leon asked, pausing his work. "Did you not break them in?
Blisters can be debilitating."

"No, the boots are fine. It's the town." Brance crossed his arms. "We
walked through the market yesterday. We didn't push anyone. We didn't
steal anything. We even tipped the baker. But everyone still looked at
us like we were going to rob them. Old Maude crossed the street just to
avoid walking near Pike."

Leon set down a rusted hinge. "Of course she did. You scared her."

"But we changed!" Brance argued. "We're mercenaries now! We're
legitimate business owners!"

"You changed yesterday," Leon corrected gently. "Maude has three years
of data saying you are dangerous. One day of good behavior is a
statistical anomaly. It does not alter the trend line."

Brance slumped against the wall. "So what? We just gotta wait three
years?"

"No. You accelerate the data collection," Leon said. He picked up his
slate. "You have coin now. But you have a deficit in 'Goodwill.' You
need to balance the ledger."

"How?"

"Restitution," Leon said. "You took from the community. Now, you invest
back into it. It builds character, which increases efficiency. A man who
can look his neighbor in the eye walks straighter than a man who has to
look at his feet."

Leon handed Brance the slate.

**MISSION: OPERATION ROOF RAISER** \* *Target: Old Maude's Cottage.* \*
*Objective: Repair the thatch. Fix the gate. Apologize.* \* *Cost:
Labor + Materials.*

Brance stared at the slate. "You want us to fix Maude's roof? She hates
us."

"She fears you," Leon corrected. "Fix the fear, fix the reputation."

An hour later, the Ironhead Salvage Crew stood in front of Old Maude's
cottage. It was a ramshackle thing near the edge of town, with a roof
that sagged like a wet blanket.

Maude was peeking through her curtains, clutching a broom like a spear.

"We're not here to rob you, Maude!" Brance shouted, sounding angry
because he was nervous.

"Try a lower volume," Leon whispered from the bushes where he was
supervising. "Project benevolence."

Brance cleared his throat. "Uh... Mrs. Maude? We... we noticed your roof
leaks. And Pike broke your gate last month. We... we want to fix it. For
free."

The curtains didn't move.

"We brought lumber!" Jory shouted, holding up a plank.

Slowly, the door creaked open. Maude peered out, suspicious. "What's the
catch? You want my cat?"

"We have plenty of cats," Brance said (The Inspector was currently
supervising from a tree branch). "No catch. Just... making things right.
Sir."

Maude blinked. "Did you just call me Sir?"

"I panicked," Brance hissed to Pike.

It took all day. Leon didn't do the work for them, but he directed it.
He taught them how to weave new thatch. He showed them how to re-hang
the gate so it swung silently. He taught them that sweating for someone
else felt different than sweating for yourself.

By sunset, the roof was tight. The gate was solid.

Maude came out with a tray. It wasn't money. It was three slices of
rhubarb pie.

"It's tart," Maude warned. "But the crust is lard-based."

Brance took a slice. He took a bite. He looked at Maude, then at the
repaired roof, then at his friends.

"Thanks, Maude," Brance mumbled, his mouth full.

"Don't talk with your mouth full, ya hoodlum," Maude scolded, but she
was smiling.

Leon watched from the shadows.

**\[System Notification: Leadership Milestone.\]** **\[Subject has
successfully rehabilitated hostile units.\]** **\[Social Impact:
Positive.\]**

He smiled. The boys stood taller now. Their shoulders were back. They
weren't skulking. They were citizens.

"Good work," Leon whispered. "Now, it is my turn."

The *Salty Eel* was crowded, warm, and smelled of roasted garlic and
ale.

Elara the barmaid was weaving through the crowd, balancing her tray with
the grace of a acrobat. She looked tired. There was a sheen of sweat on
her forehead, and her smile was starting to fray around the edges.

Leon walked in. He wasn't wearing his work apron. He had bathed, shaved,
and put on his 'Sunday' tunic.

He found a small, empty table in the corner and sat down.

When Elara finally made it to his table, she let out a long breath.
"Hey, 'Tendons of Steel.' What can I get you? The usual stew?"

"No," Leon said. "I am not here for stew. I am here for you."

Elara froze. The tray lowered slightly. "Excuse me?"

Leon's **Charisma 9** didn't make him smooth. It just made him
incredibly, disarmingly honest.

"I have been observing," Leon said. "You work very hard. You are kind to
the drunks even when they are rude. You smile when your feet hurt. You
are... structurally sound."

Elara blushed, wiping her hands on her apron. "Leon, I'm working."

"I know," Leon said. "I don't want to interrupt your shift. But when
your shift ends... I would like to walk you home. And perhaps share a
meal that you do not have to serve."

Elara looked at him. She looked for the mockery, the pick-up line, the
angle.

She saw a man with hands scarred from hard work, eyes that were calm and
steady, and a cat sitting on the chair next to him looking bored.

"A meal?" Elara asked quietly. "Like... a date?"

"A companionable interaction," Leon corrected. "I am... I am traveling,
Elara. My road goes to Ironhold eventually. I cannot offer you a
farmhouse or a future right now. My heart is... complicated." (He
thought of a green cloak).

"But," he continued, meeting her eyes. "I enjoy your company. You make
the room brighter. I would like to be the reason you smile tonight,
instead of the customers."

Elara stared at him. It was the most un-romantic, practical, and yet
sweetest proposition she had ever heard. He wasn't promising the moon.
He was promising a nice Tuesday evening.

"I get off in twenty minutes," Elara said, a real smile breaking through
the fatigue. "And I like roasted duck."

"I know a place," Leon nodded. "I inspected their kitchen hygiene
yesterday. It is acceptable."

The date was... nice.

That was the only word for it. It wasn't the fiery, confusing tension he
felt with Lyra. It was a warm, comfortable blanket.

They walked by the river. They ate roasted duck. Elara talked about her
annoying brother and her dream of owning her own tavern. Leon
listened---really listened---using his **Intellect 8** to ask follow-up
questions about inventory management and ale fermentation.

Elara loved it. Most men tuned out when she talked about keg deposits.
Leon was fascinated.

At the end of the night, they stood outside her boarding house.

"This was lovely, Leon," Elara said. She reached out and squeezed his
hand. Her grip was strong---barmaid strong.

"It was efficient," Leon smiled gently. "And pleasant. Your laugh has a
very pleasing frequency."

"You're weird," she chuckled. "But a good weird."

She leaned in and kissed him on the cheek. It lingered a little longer
than Clara's had.

"If you're still in town on Friday," she whispered, "come by the tavern.
I'll save you the good ale."

"I put it on the schedule," Leon promised.

He watched her go inside. He felt warm. He felt... human. Not a L.O.L.,
not a glitch. Just a man who had a nice dinner with a nice woman.

He turned to walk back to the smithy. The Inspector padded alongside
him.

*Not bad,* the cat purred. *You didn't mention her liver once.*

"I focused on her ambitions," Leon said. "Ambitions are the root system
of the soul."

As he walked, a chime echoed in the quiet street.

**\[System Notification: Social & Leadership Milestone Achieved.\]**
**\[Subject has mentored subordinates (Ironheads) and successfully
navigated courtship protocols.\]** **\[Social Complexity Threshold
Met.\]**

**\[Level Up!\]** **\[Public Level: 5 \> 6\]**

**\[Stats Adjusted for Level 6:\]** \* **Charisma: 9 \> 10** \*
**Intellect: 8 \> 9**

Leon stopped. He looked at the screen.

Level 6.

He felt the change. His thoughts seemed to accelerate, organizing
themselves into neat rows. His understanding of the people around
him---their wants, their fears, their 'data'---felt sharper.

"Ten Charisma," Leon whispered. "I suppose that means I am now
officially 'Charming'."

The Inspector stopped and looked at him. *Don't let it go to your head.*

"I won't," Leon said, continuing his walk. "But it might help get a
discount on the cinnamon."
