# Chapter Thirty-Four: The Cultivation of Warriors

A bright yellow flyer was nailed to the post outside the *Anvil & Oar*.

**THE SILT-RIVER SKIRMISH** *3v3 Team Melee Tournament* *Prize: 30 Gold
Pieces & The Gilded Cup* *Open to all Adventurers and Mercenaries!*

Leon stood before the poster, a basket of laundry in his hands. The
Inspector sat on his shoulder, reading along.

"Thirty gold," Leon mused. "That would buy a very high-quality seed
drill. And perhaps a new dress for Clara."

The cat stiffened. *Don't do it. You're supposed to be an Apprentice.*

"I know," Leon sighed. "If a 'Level 6 Metal Tinkerer' enters the ring
and starts throwing people around like hay bales, the Adjudicator will
have a stroke. My cover requires mediocrity."

He started to walk away, disappointed. Then he stopped.

Down the street, the Ironhead Salvage Crew (formerly the Ironhead Gang)
was arguing with a merchant.

"We ain't thugs!" Brance was shouting, though he was trying to keep his
hands visible. "We're security consultants!"

"You're street rats!" the merchant sneered. "I don't hire trash."

Leon watched Brance's face fall. The boy clenched his fist, looking like
he wanted to throw a rock. But he didn't. He swallowed his pride and
walked away, kicking the dirt.

Leon's **Intellect 9** connected the dots. *Problem A: The boys have no
respect.* *Problem B: The boys have raw aggression but no discipline.*
*Solution C: The Tournament.*

"Inspector," Leon smiled. "I cannot fight. But I can farm. And those
boys look like excellent soil."

Leon found them by the river, skipping stones. The mood was grim.

"It's no use," Pike grumbled. "We got the boots. We got the crabs. But
everyone still thinks we're just gonna mug 'em."

"Gentlemen," Leon announced, stepping onto the muddy bank.

The boys jumped to attention. It was instinct now. When the Farmer
showed up, you stood straight.

"I saw the flyer," Leon said. "The Skirmish."

Brance snorted. "Yeah? We saw it too. But that's for pros. Guild types.
'The Crimson Blades' are entering. They're Level 10s. They'd eat us
alive."

"They would beat you in a brawl," Leon agreed. "Because in a brawl, you
fight with anger. Anger is messy. It wastes energy."

Leon planted his staff in the mud.

"But in a structured bout? If you fight with *geometry*? You can win."

"Geometry?" Jory scratched his head. "Is that a type of sword?"

"It is the science of angles," Leon said. "I am offering to sponsor your
team. I will pay the entry fee. I will provide the equipment
maintenance. And I will train you."

Brance looked at Leon. "You want us to fight the Crimson Blades?"

"I want you to beat them," Leon corrected. "But there is a condition.
You fight my way. No sand in the eyes. No biting. No low blows."

"But that's how we win!" Pike protested. "We fight dirty!"

"Fighting dirty is for desperate people," Leon said, his voice dropping
to that calm, heavy tone that made the air feel thick. "Honorable combat
is for professionals. If you want respect, you have to earn it. You
don't get respect for kicking a man in the groin. You get respect for
putting him on his back while looking him in the eye."

Brance looked at his new boots. He looked at the town that despised him.

"Honor," Brance tested the word. It felt heavy. But good.

"Okay, Coach," Brance nodded. "We're in. Teach us the geometry."

The Training Ground (An abandoned barn outside town)

Leon stood before his three students.

"Jory," Leon pointed to the largest boy. "You are big. But you swing
your club like you are swatting a fly. You are off-balance."

Leon handed Jory a heavy wooden tamper.

"This is not a weapon. It is a tool for setting fence posts. Your job is
to be the Post. You do not move. You do not chase. You root."

"I root," Jory repeated, confused.

"Widen your stance. Knees bent. When they hit you, absorb it into the
earth. When you hit them, drive from the heel. You are the Anvil."

Leon turned to Pike, the scrawny one with the daggers.

"Pike. You run too much. You circle like a nervous dog."

Leon handed him a pair of hedge shears.

"Pruning," Leon said. "When you prune a rosebush, do you hack at it?
No. You find the weak branch. The joint. You snap it cleanly."

"So... stab the joints?" Pike asked.

"Disarm the joints," Leon corrected. "Target the wrist. Target the back
of the knee. Small cuts. Accumulation of damage. You are the Shears."

Finally, he looked at Brance.

"Brance. You are the Shepherd. Your job is not to kill the wolves. It is
to move them where you want them."

Leon drew a diagram in the dirt.

"Triangulation. Jory is the Anvil. He stops them. Pike is the Shears. He
harasses them. You drive them into Jory. You don't fight three 1-on-1
battles. You fight one 3-on-1 battle, three times."

Tournament Day

The arena was packed. The "Crimson Blades"---a trio of flashy,
red-armored adventurers---were waving to the crowd. They looked
polished, arrogant, and very Level 10.

Across the ring stood the "Ironhead Salvage Crew."

They weren't wearing flashy armor. They were wearing cleaned leather
vests (studs polished) and sturdy work boots. They looked terrified.

Leon stood in their corner, holding a water bucket and a towel.

"Breathe," Leon instructed calmly. "4-count box breath. Regulate the
heart rate."

"They look big, Coach," Jory whispered, his knuckles white on his club.

"They are top-heavy," Leon assessed. "They skip leg day. Jory, they will
underestimate you. They will crash into you like water on a rock. Be the
rock."

"FIGHT!"

The Crimson Blades charged. They didn't use tactics. They just assumed
they would steamroll the street rats.

The leader, a swordsman, lunged at Jory.

Jory didn't flinch. He dropped his hips. *The Fence Post Stance.*

CLANG.

The sword hit Jory's club. Jory didn't budge. The shockwave traveled up
the swordsman's arm.

"What?" the swordsman blinked.

"Now, Pike!" Brance shouted.

Pike didn't stab. He slid in low. *Pruning.* He hooked the swordsman's
ankle with the hilt of his dagger and pulled.

The swordsman stumbled.

Brance stepped in. *Shepherding.* He shoved the swordsman
sideways---straight into the path of the Crimson Mage who was trying to
cast a fireball.

Crash.

The two adventurers tangled. The fireball fizzled out.

"Formation!" Leon called out from the corner. "The Plow!"

The crowd watched in stunned silence as the three street rats moved in
perfect sync. They formed a wedge. Jory in front, the others on the
flanks. They drove forward.

They didn't fight dirty. No sand. No groin kicks.

Jory blocked. Pike disarmed. Brance shoved.

They dismantled the Level 10 team piece by piece. It wasn't flashy
magic. It was brutal, industrial efficiency.

Finally, the Crimson Leader was the last one standing. He was panting,
disarmed, surrounded by three boys who looked calm, focused, and utterly
professional.

"Yield," Brance said. He held his sword steady, tip pointed at the
ground. Not threatening. Commanding.

The adventurer looked at the boys. He looked at the silent crowd. He
dropped his head.

"I yield."

"Winner! The Ironhead Salvage Crew!"

The crowd didn't just cheer. They applauded. It was a respectful,
thunderous sound.

Brance looked at the crowd. He looked at the merchant who had called
them trash. The merchant was clapping.

Brance looked at Leon.

Leon was smiling. A proud, Charisma 10 smile. He nodded once.

Good harvest.

The boys ran to the corner.

"We did it!" Pike screamed. "Did you see that pivot? I pruned him!"

"You did," Leon agreed, handing them water. "Your joint targeting was
excellent."

"We didn't cheat," Brance said, breathless, eyes shining. "We didn't
cheat and we won."

"Honor is a force multiplier," Leon said, wiping a smudge of dirt from
Brance's cheek with the towel. "People cheer for heroes, Brance. They
only fear thugs. It is better to be cheered."

"Here," Jory said, shoving the pouch of gold into Leon's chest. "Take
it. The entry fee. And... interest."

"Keep it," Leon pushed it back. "Invest it. Buy better tools. Expand the
business."

He looked at the boys, who were now standing taller than the Level 10s
they had just beaten.

"But," Leon added. "You can buy me a cinnamon bun. I believe I have
earned a commission."

The Inspector, sitting on the fence post of the arena, watched the
celebration. The cat licked its paw.

*The human is building an army,* the cat mused. *An army of polite,
efficient, well-booted laborers. The world is not ready.*
