# Chapter Thirty-Five: The Paladin's Audit

Silt-River was changing. It wasn't an overnight miracle---the mud still
smelled like sulfur and the ale was still watery---but the *flow* was
different.

It was in the details.

The graffiti on the mill wall had been scrubbed off. The potholes in the
main trade road had been filled with gravel. And the Ironhead Salvage
Crew, once the terror of the market, was currently engaged in a heated
debate about structural engineering.

"You can't use pine for the handrail, Pike!" Brance was shouting,
pointing at the bridge that spanned the river. "Pine rots in the damp.
We need treated oak. Quality materials ensure longevity!"

"But oak is expensive!" Pike argued, holding a hammer.

"So we work an extra shift at the mud flats!" Brance countered. "Do you
want Old Maude to lean on this and fall in? Liability, Pike! Think about
the liability!"

Leon sat on a bench nearby, eating a pear. He watched the argument with
deep satisfaction.

"They are arguing about safety standards," Leon whispered to the
Inspector. "It is beautiful."

The cat purred, vibrating against Leon's thigh. *They are terrified of
disappointing you. It is effective.*

"It is not fear," Leon corrected. "It is professional pride."

Just then, the rhythm of the street changed. The chatter in the market
died down. The clatter of carts stopped.

A cavalcade was entering the northern gate.

These weren't merchant wagons. They were warhorses, clad in barding of
polished silver and blue steel. The riders wore heavy plate armor and
white cloaks embroidered with a golden shield.

**\[Faction: The Order of the Argent Shield\]** **\[Unit: Forward
Command\]**

The townsfolk scrambled to the sides of the road, bowing low. The Order
was the Kingdom's elite enforcement arm---Paladins, Clerics, and
Inquisitors. They usually only showed up when a town was being razed or
purged.

The lead rider, a massive man with a greying beard and a scar running
through his left eyebrow, signaled for a halt. He scanned the street
with eyes that looked like chipped flint.

\[High Paladin Kaelen - Level 45\]

Kaelen dismounted. His armor clanked heavily, a sound of absolute
authority. He walked toward the bridge, his gauntleted hand resting on
the pommel of a massive longsword.

He stopped in front of the Ironhead boys.

Pike dropped his hammer. Jory looked like he wanted to run. But
Brance---wearing his clean boots and a vest with the studs polished to a
mirror shine---stepped forward.

Brance didn't cower. He stood at ease, shoulders back, just like Leon
taught him.

"Good afternoon, my Lord," Brance said. His voice shook slightly, but he
held his ground. "The bridge is currently under maintenance. Please
watch your step on the left side."

Kaelen peered at the boy. He looked at the tools. He looked at the fresh
lumber.

"I was told," Kaelen rumbled, his voice like grinding stones, "that
Silt-River was a hive of scum and villainy. I was told the bridges were
unsafe and the gangs ran the streets."

"We cleaned it up, sir," Brance said. "Inefficiency is bad for trade."

Kaelen raised an eyebrow. "Inefficiency?"

"Yes, sir. Chaos disrupts the supply chain. We are the Ironhead Salvage
Crew. We handle... infrastructure optimization."

The Paladin stared at the former street rat. He cast a silent \[Detect
Evil\] spell.

His eyes flashed gold for a second.

He expected to see the dark red stain of criminal intent. Instead, he
saw a faint, grey-white aura of *Neutral Good*---the color of someone
who is trying very hard to be useful, mostly because they are afraid of
a lecture.

"Who is your captain?" Kaelen asked sharply.

Brance hesitated. He glanced toward the bench where Leon was sitting.

Kaelen followed the gaze. He saw a man in a simple tunic, eating a pear,
with a black cat on his lap.

Kaelen walked over. The crowd held its breath.

Leon didn't stand up immediately. He finished chewing, swallowed, and
wiped his mouth with Clara's embroidered handkerchief. Then he stood.

He bowed. Not a peasant's scrape, but a respectful nod of equals.

"Sir Knight," Leon said.

Kaelen towered over Leon. He was a veteran of a dozen wars. He radiated
an aura of **\[Holy Dread\]** that usually made civilians weep.

Leon just looked at him calmly.

"You possess a strange aura, citizen," Kaelen noted, narrowing his eyes.
"It is... dense. Like bedrock."

"I eat a lot of root vegetables," Leon explained.

Kaelen looked back at the boys on the bridge. "You did this? You turned
a pack of jackals into... bridge builders?"

"They were bored," Leon said. "They had excess energy and no direction.
I simply provided a trellis for them to grow on."

"A trellis," Kaelen repeated flatly.

"Yes. Tomatoes grow wild if you let them sprawl. Stake them up, and they
produce fruit."

Kaelen looked at Leon for a long, silent minute. He had seen Generals
struggle to discipline raw recruits. He had seen drill sergeants break
men to build soldiers. But this... this was something else. This was
*cultivation*.

"We are riding South," Kaelen said abruptly. "The front lines at the
Cinder Pass are holding, but morale is breaking. The supply lines are
chaotic. The recruits are terrified."

He stepped closer, his steel greaves clanking.

"The Kingdom needs men who can build order out of chaos. Not just
fighters. Builders."

He reached into a pouch at his belt and pulled out a heavy iron coin. It
bore the stamp of the Argent Shield.

"If you ever tire of gardening, Farmer," Kaelen said, pressing the coin
into Leon's hand, "come to the Southern Front. Show this to Commander
Vance. Tell him Kaelen sent you."

Leon looked at the coin. It was heavy. It felt like responsibility.

"I am headed to Ironhold," Leon said. "To see the markets."

"Ironhold is on the way to the war," Kaelen said grimly. "Keep your eyes
open, citizen. The rot spreading from the South isn't just undead. It's
despair. And despair kills faster than steel."

Kaelen turned on his heel. He mounted his horse in one fluid motion.

"Move out!" he roared.

The cavalcade thundered across the bridge. The Ironhead boys watched
them go, eyes wide.

Brance ran over to Leon. "Coach! That was the High Paladin! He gave you
a coin! What did he say?"

Leon looked at the iron coin in his palm. He felt the weight of the
Paladin's words. *Despair kills faster than steel.*

"He said," Leon pocketed the coin, "that you boys did a good job on the
bridge."

Brance beamed. He turned to his crew. "You heard him! Back to work!
Pike, get the oak! We're making this bridge fit for a King!"

Leon sat back down on the bench. The Inspector looked at his pocket.

*The War comes to find you,* the cat noted.

"I know," Leon whispered. "But not today. Today, we finish the
apprenticeship."

He looked at the slate in his lap.

**THE LIST:** 1. ~~Find a Smithy~~ (Complete) 2. ~~Find a Tanner~~
(Complete) 3. ~~Local Gardening~~ (Complete) 4. *Culinary Research*
(Pending)

"Come on, Inspector," Leon stood up. "I smell cinnamon. Let's finish the
list so we can hit the road. I have a feeling the traffic is going to
get heavy."
