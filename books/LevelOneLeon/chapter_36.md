# Chapter Thirty-Six: The Narrative Consistency Patch

Leon stood in the stall of the livery stable, brushing Bessie. The cow
was glowing with health, her coat shiny and her mood excellent (thanks
to the stable boy's humming).

But Leon was frowning.

He held the heavy iron coin the High Paladin had given him. He looked at
the slate where he tracked his tasks. He looked at the Ironhead boys,
who were currently outside arguing over who got to carry the lumber for
the new orphanage roof.

"Inspector," Leon whispered. "I have a problem."

The cat, currently batting at a piece of straw, paused. *We have plenty
of food. The cart is fixed. What problem?*

"The narrative," Leon said seriously. "Grandpa always said, 'Don't try
to pull a plow with a cat.' It looks wrong. It makes people ask
questions."

He paced the small stall.

"I am Public Level 6. I am supposed to be a 'Talented Apprentice.' But
yesterday, a Level 45 High Paladin treated me like a peer. The Ironheads
follow my orders like I am a General. If I go to Ironhold and start
'fixing' things there, people will start inspecting me. And when they
see 'Level 6,' they won't think I'm talented."

He stopped pacing.

"They'll think I'm a spy. Or a demon. Or a god in disguise. All of those
options involve paperwork and dungeons. I just want to visit the
market."

Leon looked up at the rafters. He cycled his breath, centering his mind
on the concept of *Administrative Order*.

"System," Leon stated clearly. "I require a consultation regarding
plausible deniability."

The world went grey. The chewing sound of Bessie stopped mid-crunch.

A shimmer of static appeared next to the hay bale, and Adjudicator Beta
materialized. She looked worse than last time. Her spectral hair bun was
slightly askew, and she was holding a mug of what looked like binary
code coffee.

"Mr. Leon," she said, her voice sounding tinny and exhausted. "I was in
the middle of patching a physics exploit in the Fire Realm. A wizard
figured out how to cast infinite ham sandwiches. What is it?"

"I need a promotion," Leon said.

Beta lowered her mug. "Excuse me?"

"The mask is slipping," Leon explained. "I am too efficient. My Charisma
is overriding the local hierarchy. I accidentally radicalized a street
gang into a construction company, and a Paladin gave me a command coin.
My 'Level 6 Apprentice' tag is straining suspension of disbelief."

Beta rubbed her temples. "I saw the report. You successfully intimidated
a debt collector by doing algebra on his rotator cuff. That was...
creative."

"It was necessary," Leon said. "But now I am heading to Ironhold. Big
city. Guild scanners. If I walk in there acting like *this*"---he
gestured to his broad, capability-radiating self---"while wearing a
'Level 6' badge, I will attract attention. I need to level up.
Publicly."

Beta tapped her clipboard. A holographic chart appeared. It showed a red
line (Leon's Influence) skyrocketing past a blue line (Leon's Public
Level).

"You are correct," Beta admitted. "You are trending dangerously close to
'Messiah Figure' status in this sector. If you keep this up, we'll have
to spawn a prophecy, and nobody has the budget for that."

She scrolled through a list.

"Okay. We need to get you out of the 'Apprentice' bracket. You need a
Class Evolution."

"I like Farmer," Leon said.

"Farmer doesn't explain why you can tank a bear," Beta countered. "We
need a class that explains broad competence, high physical stats, and
weirdly high charisma, without implying you are a combat specialist."

She swiped through a few options.

- *Monk?* (Too religious).
- *Brawler?* (Too aggressive).
- *Merchant?* (Not enough muscle).

"Here," Beta pointed. "**Journeyman Wanderer**."

"Journeyman?"

"It's a catch-all Civilian/Adventurer hybrid," Beta explained. "The lore
implies you travel the world learning trades. It justifies high Strength
(hauling gear), high Constitution (walking), high Wisdom (experience),
and high Charisma (meeting people). It's the perfect cover for a
jack-of-all-trades who is weirdly good at everything."

Leon considered it. "Journeyman. It sounds professional. It implies I am
still learning, but I am reliable."

"Exactly. It raises your Public Level Cap to 15 immediately, and unlocks
the 'Generalist' skill tree for display."

"Do it," Leon nodded. "Level me up."

"I can't jump you to 15 instantly," Beta warned. "That's too suspicious.
We'll bump you to **Level 9** for now. That's the threshold of
'Competent Veteran.' It will make the Paladin's interest in you seem
plausible---he just spotted a 'Diamond in the Rough'."

She tapped the air.

**\[System Update: Narrative Patch 1.02 Applied.\]** **\[Class
Evolution: Apprentice -\> Journeyman Wanderer.\]** **\[Public Level: 6
\> 9\]**

\[Stats Recalibrated for Narrative Consistency:\]

- **Strength: 12 \> 14** (You haul a lot of luggage).
- **Agility: 11 \> 12** (Roads are treacherous).
- **Constitution: 12 \> 14** (You sleep on rocks).
- **Intellect: 9 \> 10** (You manage logistics).
- **Wisdom: 9 \> 11** (You've seen some things).
- **Charisma: 10 \> 12** (Travelers have stories).

Leon felt the shift. The "Muffler" that restricted his output loosened
significantly. He felt... roomy.

"Level 9," Leon breathed. "Strength 14. I can finally lift the cart axle
without bracing my back first."

"Don't go crazy," Beta warned, fading away. "Level 9 is respectable, not
invincible. If you suplex a dragon, I *will* delete your save file."

"Understood," Leon promised. "I will only suplex moderate-sized
threats."

"Goodbye, Leon. Please try to be boring for at least three days."

The world snapped back to color. Bessie finished her crunch.

Leon stood in the stall, feeling the new numbers settle over him like a
well-fitted coat.

**\[Name: Leon\]** **\[Class: Journeyman Wanderer\]** **\[Public Level:
9\]**

He picked up his slate and wiped away the "Apprentice" task list.

"Inspector," Leon said, a new confidence in his voice. "We are no longer
students. We are Journeymen."

The cat looked at him. *Does that mean we get better rations?*

"It means," Leon said, hitching Bessie to the newly reinforced cart,
"that when we get to Ironhold, people might actually listen when I tell
them their city walls need repointing."

He climbed onto the cart seat. He looked back at Silt-River one last
time. The water wheel was spinning smoothly. The Ironhead boys were
fixing a roof. The town was humming.

"Work here is done," Leon nodded. "Hup, Bessie. To the horizon."

The cart rolled out of the stable, the wheels singing on the
cobblestones. Leon the Farmer---now Leon the Journeyman---was officially
on the road. And he was Level 9.

God help the bandits.
