# Chapter Thirty-Seven: The Toll Booth Consultant

The departure from Silt-River was not a quiet affair.

Master Borrin had gifted Leon a bag of high-grade coal ("For the
road!"). Elara had given him a basket of travel rations that smelled of
rosemary and affection. Even Old Maude waved from her newly thatched
porch.

But the real send-off came at the gate.

Brance, Jory, and Pike stood in formation. They wore matching vests.
They stood at attention.

"Report," Leon said, stopping the cart.

"North Bridge secure," Brance barked, chest puffed out. "Market patrol
established. Mud Crab population stabilized at sustainable harvest
levels."

Leon nodded slowly. He looked at the boys. They weren't street rats
anymore. They were the foundation of the town's order.

"Remember," Leon said, leaning on his staff. "A town is just a large
garden. If you let the weeds grow, they choke the good crops. But if you
prune too hard, you kill the bloom. Balance."

"Balance," Brance repeated. He reached out and shook Leon's hand. "Safe
travels, Boss. If you ever need a bridge built... or a leg broken...
call us."

"I will call you for the bridge," Leon smiled. "Leave the legs alone
unless absolutely necessary."

Leon climbed onto the cart. "Hup, Bessie."

The cart rolled out. The Ironhead Salvage Crew saluted.

Two hours later, the industrial smoke of Silt-River was a grey smudge
behind them. The road ahead cut through a dense forest of pines, the air
turning sharp and cold as the elevation rose toward the Ironhold pass.

DING.

The sound rippled through Leon's mind, clear as a temple bell.

**\[System Notification: Regional Quest Complete.\]** **\[Quest:**
"**The Apprentice's Touch**"** - Silt-River Stabilized.\]**
**\[Experience Threshold Met.\]**

**\[Level Up!\]** **\[Public Level: 9 \> 10\]**

**\[Tier 1 Threshold Unlocked.\]** **\[Aura Projection: Active.\]**

Leon took a breath. He felt the "Muffler" expand again.

Level 10.

In the world of adventurers, Level 10 was the dividing line. Below 10,
you were a novice. Above 10, you were a Professional. A Level 10 Warrior
could serve as a Sergeant in the King's Army. A Level 10 Mage could open
their own shop.

The air around Leon shimmered.

It wasn't magic. It was presence. The sheer density of his soul---that
neutron star he carried in his chest---was leaking out just a fraction
more. To a normal observer, Leon didn't just look like a traveler
anymore. He looked... heavy. He looked like a storm cloud that hadn't
decided whether to rain yet.

"Ten," Leon whispered. "I feel... authoritative."

The Inspector, dozing on a grain sack, opened one eye. *You feel loud.
Turn down the intensity. You're scaring the birds.*

Leon tried to dial it back, but before he could, the cart rounded a
sharp bend.

The road was blocked.

A fallen tree---too neatly cut to be natural---lay across the path.
Standing behind it were five men.

These weren't the starving, desperate bandits Leon had met on his first
night. These men wore matching studded leather. They had crossbows. They
had discipline.

**\[Highwayman - Level 8\]** **\[Highwayman Sergeant - Level 11\]**

The Sergeant stepped forward. He held a repeating crossbow leveled at
Leon's chest.

"Halt," the Sergeant said. His voice was bored, professional. "Toll
road. Assessment fee is ten silvers for the cart, five for the beast,
and whatever is in that sack."

Leon stopped Bessie. He engaged the parking brake.

He looked at the fallen tree. *Red Pine. Good timber. Wasteful to leave
it rotting.*

He looked at the bandits.

Usually, Leon would calculate a social hook. Or he would act harmless.

But he was Level 10 now. He was a Journeyman. And he was tired of people
putting obstacles on public infrastructure.

Leon stepped down from the cart.

He didn't grab his staff. He just walked toward the log.

The bandits stiffened. They sensed it. That "Air of Danger." It wasn't
aggression; it was the terrifying calmness of a man who knows exactly
how much force is required to remove an object.

"One more step and I loose!" the Sergeant warned, his finger tightening
on the trigger.

Leon stopped ten feet away. He looked the Sergeant in the eye.

"You have a good formation," Leon noted. "Two crossbows on the flanks,
three melee in the center. It is a standard pincer trap."

The Sergeant blinked. "Uh... yeah. Thanks?"

"But your business model is flawed," Leon continued. His voice wasn't
loud, but it carried a resonance that made the bandits' horses shift
nervously. "You are targeting inbound traffic to Ironhold. Most inbound
traffic carries raw materials. Low value, high weight. Hard to fence."

Leon gestured to his cart. "I have a cow and scrap metal. If you rob me,
you have to feed the cow. Do you have hay? Do you know how to milk? If
you don't milk her twice a day, she gets mastitis. Then your prize is
worthless."

The bandits looked at Bessie. Bessie looked back, chewing ominously.

"We... we'll eat the cow!" one bandit shouted.

"Inefficient," Leon said coldly. "One meal versus years of milk
production. Short-term thinking is why you are living in the woods."

The Sergeant lowered his crossbow slightly. "Who are you? A merchant?"

"I am a Consultant," Leon said. "And I am Level 10."

He let the aura flare. Just for a second.

The air pressure dropped. The bandits felt a phantom weight on their
shoulders, like gravity had just doubled. The Sergeant's horse whinnied
in panic and backed up.

"Level 10..." the Sergeant muttered. He inspected Leon.

\[Leon - Journeyman Wanderer - Level 10\]

It checked out. But the rating didn't say "Average." It said
**\[Solid\]**.

"We outnumber you five to one," the Sergeant gritted out, trying to
regain control. "Level 10 or not, you bleed."

"I do," Leon admitted. "But so do you. And I have a question."

Leon walked up to the log. He placed one hand on it.

"You cut this tree to block the road. It took you... what? An hour with
a two-man saw?"

"About that," the bandit muttered.

"And how much do you make in tolls per week?"

"Maybe... fifty silvers? On a good week?"

Leon shook his head. "Terrible. You have five able-bodied men. You have
tools. You have localized terrain knowledge."

Leon gripped the log.

He didn't use a lever. He didn't use a rope. He cycled his Ki into his
legs---**Strength 14 (Public)**, **Strength ??? (Actual)**---and
*lifted*.

The massive pine trunk, weighing nearly a ton, rose into the air. Leon
held it balanced on one shoulder like it was a sack of grain.

The bandits dropped their weapons. The Sergeant's crossbow clattered to
the rocks.

"This is Red Pine," Leon said calmly, holding the tree. "Ironhold pays
three gold pieces per log for timber of this quality. You are sitting on
a gold mine, and you are using the inventory to annoy travelers."

He tossed the log to the side of the road.

CRASH.

The ground shook.

Leon dusted off his shoulder.

"You are not bandits," Leon declared. "You are a Logging Company with
poor management."

The Sergeant stared at the log. He stared at Leon. He did the math.
*Three gold?* That was three hundred silvers. For one tree.

"We... we don't have a wagon to haul them," the Sergeant stammered, his
bravado shattered by the display of casual, monstrous strength.

"Then you invest," Leon said. "You take your toll money. You buy a
wagon. You clear the timber along the road. You make the road *safer*
and *wider*, which encourages more merchants, which increases the
economy."

Leon walked back to his cart.

"You have a choice," Leon called back. "You can try to fight me. I will
disarm you, bind you, and leave you for the King's Patrol. Or... you can
help me clear the rest of this debris, and I will show you how to
identify the trees worth the most money."

The bandits looked at each other. They looked at the massive log Leon
had tossed like a twig.

They looked at their measly pile of silver coins.

The Sergeant holstered his crossbow. He walked over to the log and
touched it.

"Three gold?" he asked.

"Market rate," Leon confirmed. "I checked the ledger in Silt-River."

The Sergeant looked at his men. "Boys... put the swords away. Get the
axes."

Growth Analysis:

- **The Bandits:** They learned that their skills (ambush, terrain
  knowledge, labor) were undervalued in the criminal market. They
  shifted from parasites to producers. They grew from desperate
  highwaymen into the "Red Pine Timber Co."
- **Leon:** He realized that his power isn't just for fighting or
  farming. It's for *vision*. At Level 10, he stops seeing obstacles and
  starts seeing assets. He grew from a man who fixes problems to a man
  who sees opportunities.
- **The Inspector:** The cat watched the bandits chopping wood with
  frantic energy.

*Humans are so easily manipulated by shiny metal disks,* the cat
thought. *But at least the road is clear.*

Leon climbed back onto the cart.

"That went well," Leon noted. "No violence. Just economics."

He snapped the reins. "Hup, Bessie. We have a mountain to climb."
