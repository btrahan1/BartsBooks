# Chapter Thirty-Eight: The Waypoint Reunion

The Ironhold Pass was less a road and more a scar cut into the side of
the mountain. The wind here was sharp, whistling through the crags with
a lonely, hollow sound.

Leon had pulled the cart into a small, wind-blocked alcove for the
midday meal. A fire was crackling (smokeless, thanks to his **\[Metal
Tinkering\]** on the stove grate), and a pot of soup was bubbling.

"Inspector," Leon said, stirring the pot. "Ears up."

The cat was already alert, staring at a cluster of boulders fifty yards
down the slope.

Leon didn't reach for his staff. He reached for a second bowl.

"You have a tailwind," Leon called out to the empty rocks. "It carries
the scent of pine soap and exhaustion. You can come out."

For a moment, nothing happened. Then, the air shimmered.

Lyra stepped out from the shadow of the boulder.

She looked tired. Her green leathers were stained with road dust, and
there was a new scratch on her left bracer. But her eyes were bright,
and as she looked at Leon, a smile broke through the fatigue like the
sun piercing the mountain mist.

"You heard me?" Lyra asked, walking up the slope. "My stealth is Level
25."

"The Inspector heard you," Leon corrected, nodding to the cat. "I just
smelled the soap. It's a very distinct blend."

Lyra reached the fire. She looked at the cart, then at the suspension
system, then at Bessie (who was wearing a custom-knitted wool scarf).
Finally, she looked at Leon.

He stood taller. His shoulders seemed broader, filling out the travel
tunic. He didn't have the uncertain posture of the farm boy she had left
on the bridge. He stood with the quiet, grounded gravity of a mountain.

"Hello, Farmer," she said softly.

"Hello, Ranger," Leon smiled. It was a **Charisma 10** smile---warm,
welcoming, and safe. "Soup? It has dumplings."

Lyra sat down heavily on a log. "Gods, yes. I haven't had hot food in
three days. I've been scouting the goblin tunnels ahead of the convoy."

Leon handed her the bowl. She ate with the desperate efficiency of an
adventurer, but she kept glancing at him between spoonfuls.

"You look... different," she admitted, wiping her mouth. "Heavier."

"I have been working out," Leon said. "And the System finally processed
my paperwork."

"Show me," Lyra demanded gently.

Leon didn't hesitate. "System. Display Public Profile. Authorization:
Lyra."

The blue screen hovered between them.

**\[Name: Leon\]** **\[Class: Journeyman Wanderer\]** **\[Level: 10\]**
**\[Strength: 14\]** **\[Agility: 12\]** **\[Constitution: 14\]**
**\[Intellect: 10\]** **\[Wisdom: 11\]** **\[Charisma: 12\]**

Lyra choked on a dumpling.

She coughed, thumping her chest. Leon handed her a waterskin.

"Ten?" she wheezed. "You're Level Ten? Leon, I've been gone for *two
weeks*."

"I had a busy week in Silt-River," Leon explained apologetically. "There
were structural issues with the economy. And pests. And I accidentally
mentored a street gang."

Lyra stared at the stats.

"Fourteen Strength," she whispered. "Leon, that's... that's higher than
most Knights at Level 20. And Charisma 12? You're statistically more
charming than a Bard."

"I just listen to people," Leon said. "And I fix things. Apparently, the
System likes it when you fix things."

Lyra looked up at him. The "L.O.L." she had met in the smithy---the boy
who blushed at ankles---was gone. In his place was a man who had
speed-run the early game through sheer, stubborn competence.

She felt a strange flutter in her chest. It wasn't just attraction
anymore. It was respect. And maybe a little bit of intimidation. He was
catching up to her fast.

"You're a Journeyman now," she noted, reading the class title. "That
means you're not tied to the land anymore."

"I carry the land with me," Leon said, patting a sack of his special
fertilizer. "But yes. I am a traveler. Like you."

Lyra stood up. She walked over to him. She didn't reach up to touch his
face this time; she put a hand on his shoulder, squeezing the hard
muscle there.

"My, how you've grown," she murmured. "I used to worry about you out
here alone. Now... I think I should be worried for the monsters."

"The monsters are fine," Leon assured her. "As long as they don't block
the road. I started a logging company with the last group."

Lyra laughed. It was a bright, clear sound that echoed off the rocks.
"Of course you did."

She adjusted her cloak. The moment was over. The mission pulled at her.

"I can't stay, Leon. The goblin tunnels are unstable. I have to report
to the Forward Command at Ironhold before nightfall."

"I know," Leon said. He reached into his pack and pulled out a small
jar. "Take this. It's a salve made from Grave Moss and chamomile. It
hides your scent from tracking beasts. I made it for the Inspector, but
it should work for a Ranger."

Lyra took the jar. She looked at him, her eyes soft.

"Ironhold," she said. "The Gilded Wyvern Inn. Meet me there?"

"I'll be the one parking a cow out front," Leon promised.

Lyra grinned. She stepped back, her silhouette blurring as her stealth
skills re-engaged.

"Safe travels, Level Ten," she whispered.

And then she was gone, leaving only the scent of pine soap and a
half-empty bowl of soup.

Leon stared at the spot where she had vanished.

The Inspector hopped onto his shoulder. *She likes the stats.*

"She likes the soup," Leon corrected, picking up the bowl.

He cycled his Ki. He felt good. He felt seen.

"Pack it up, Inspector," Leon commanded. "We have a date at the Gilded
Wyvern. And I am not going to be late."
