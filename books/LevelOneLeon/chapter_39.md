# Chapter Thirty-Nine: The Exclusive Harvest

The *Gilded Wyvern* was exactly the kind of establishment Leon expected
in a city like Ironhold. It was loud, the timber beams were reinforced
with iron bands, and the menu prices were inflated by at least 15% due
to the "Adventurer Tax."

Leon didn't mind. He had sold a significant amount of Mud Crab carapace
in Silt-River.

He sat at a corner booth, the Inspector curled up on the velvet cushion
beside him. Across the table, Lyra looked radiant. She had traded her
road-stained leathers for a soft tunic of deep forest green, and her
hair was loose, catching the light of the hearth fire.

"So," Lyra said, leaning over her plate of roasted venison. "Let me get
this straight. You turned a bandit ambush into a logging corporation?"

"Red Pine Timber Co.," Leon confirmed, cutting his steak with precise,
geometric strokes. "They just needed capital investment for a wagon.
Their cutting technique was actually quite good. The Sergeant had
excellent lats."

Lyra shook her head, a smile playing on her lips. "And the bridge in
Silt-River? The High Paladin said it was 'structurally inspiring.'"

"It was," Leon nodded. "Brance has a gift for load-bearing trusses. I
just gave him the lumber."

They talked for an hour. Lyra spoke of the goblin tunnels---tight,
claustrophobic fights in the dark, disarming traps, and marking
pathfinding runes for the army. Leon listened intently, his **Intellect
10** absorbing every detail of her trade, his **Wisdom 11** hearing the
exhaustion beneath her excitement.

She was living on the edge. He was building foundations. They were
opposites, yet they sat at the same table, and the space between them
felt smaller than it ever had.

As the plates were cleared and the ale mugs refilled, the noise of the
tavern seemed to fade into the background.

Leon went quiet. He stopped analyzing the structural integrity of the
table. He looked at Lyra.

His **Charisma 12** told him this was the moment. The lighting was
right. The rapport was high. The "Social Algorithm" he had built with
Elara and the others was flashing green lights.

But Leon pushed the algorithm aside. Algorithms were for variables. This
was a constant.

"Lyra," he started.

She looked up, sensing the shift in his tone. "What is it, Leon? Is the
ale flat?"

"The ale is adequate," Leon said. He placed his hands on the table,
clasping them together. "But I need to make a confession."

Lyra stiffened slightly. "A confession?"

"You told me to practice," Leon said slowly. "Back in Oakhaven. You told
me to level up my social skills. To learn the game. To find... other
girls."

Lyra looked down at her mug. "I did. You needed the experience, Leon.
You were... raw."

"I did," Leon admitted. "And I followed your advice. In Silt-River, I
practiced. I took a barmaid named Elara to dinner. We discussed her
ambitions. I complimented her stability. It was... pleasant."

Lyra's jaw tightened. She forced a smile. "That's... that's good, Leon.
I'm glad you found someone."

"I didn't," Leon corrected.

He looked her in the eye.

"It felt dishonest, Lyra. It felt like I was inspecting a field I had no
intention of planting. Elara was kind, and she deserved genuine
interest, not a training exercise."

He took a breath.

"I have learned how to talk to people. I have learned how to read cues.
My Charisma is higher now. I know I could go out into this city and find
'practice.' But I don't want to."

"Why not?" Lyra whispered.

"Because it is inefficient," Leon said, his voice dropping to that deep,
bedrock rumble. "And it is rude to my own heart."

He reached across the table. He didn't use a smooth move. He just laid
his hand palm up, waiting.

"I don't want to practice anymore, Lyra. I know who I want. I knew it
when you showed up in the smithy and critiqued my hammer technique."

Lyra stared at his hand. Her heart was hammering against her ribs like a
trapped bird.

"Leon," she said, her voice trembling. "I'm a Ranger. I'm leaving for
the Front in two days. You're a Wanderer. We... we can't have a white
picket fence right now. It's not realistic."

"I know," Leon said. "I'm not asking for a fence. Not yet."

He looked at her with absolute clarity.

"I am asking for... exclusivity. In agriculture, we call it 'optioning
the land.' It means no one else gets to bid on it while the farmer gets
the funds together."

Lyra let out a choked laugh. "You're optioning me?"

"I want to be your boyfriend," Leon said, the words heavy and clumsy and
perfect. "Even if it is in name only for now. Even if we are miles
apart. I want to know that when I look at the moon, I am not looking for
anyone else. And I want to know... that you aren't looking for another
Farmer."

He waited.

"I don't want to be 'on the market'," Leon finished softly. "I want to
be yours."

Lyra looked at him. She looked at the man who had terrified a Paladin,
tamed a street gang, and composted a plague bear, all because he wanted
to make the world a little tidier.

She reached out and placed her hand in his. Her fingers interlaced with
his.

"You idiot," she whispered, her eyes shining with unshed tears. "I
haven't looked for another Farmer since the day you made me tea in the
tall grass."

Leon squeezed her hand. "So... that is a yes? To the optioning?"

"Yes," Lyra squeezed back. "I will be your girlfriend, Leon. In name,
and... well, in everything else I can be, from a distance."

Leon let out a breath he felt like he'd been holding since Oakhaven.

**\[System Notification: Relationship Status Updated.\]** **\[Partner:
Lyra (Ranger).\]** **\[Status: Exclusive.\]** **\[Buff Applied:**
'**Long Distance Devotion**'** - Mental defense +20% when separated.\]**

Leon smiled. It was the brightest thing in the room.

"Good," he said. "That is settled. Now, I have something for you."

He reached into his pocket. He didn't pull out a ring---that was too
much. He didn't pull out a weapon.

He pulled out a small, folded piece of silk.

It was a ribbon. Deep, forest green, embroidered with a tiny, silver
plow.

"I bought it in the market this morning," Leon said shyly. "I thought...
for your braid. It matches your armor."

Lyra took the ribbon. She ran her thumb over the silver plow. It was
high-quality silk. He must have spent a fortune.

"It's perfect," she whispered.

She turned her head. "Tie it for me?"

Leon stood up. He walked around the booth. His large hands, capable of
bending iron brackets and crushing rocks, were infinitely gentle as he
gathered her copper hair.

He tied the ribbon at the end of her braid.

"Structural integrity secure," Leon murmured close to her ear.

Lyra leaned back against him for just a second, closing her eyes. She
felt his warmth, his solidity.

"I have to go to the Front," she said quietly.

"I know," Leon said, his hands lingering on her shoulders. "I have work
to do in Ironhold first. But I will head South eventually. The Paladin
gave me a coin."

"Then I'll see you in the war, Boyfriend," she said, turning to look up
at him.

"You will," Leon promised. "I'll be the one fixing the catapults."

On the seat, the Inspector watched them. The cat finished a piece of
dropped venison, licked its chops, and curled its tail over its nose.

*Finally,* the cat thought. *The mating dance was inefficient, but the
result is acceptable.*
