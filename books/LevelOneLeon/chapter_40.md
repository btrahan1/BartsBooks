# Chapter Forty: The Industrial Sleepwalker

The morning sun hit the iron-shod gates of Ironhold, casting long, sharp
shadows across the cobblestones.

Leon stood at the southern gate, watching the dust settle. Lyra's
company had ridden out an hour ago. She hadn't looked back---Rangers
don't look back---but the green ribbon in her braid had fluttered like a
banner.

Leon touched his chest. The **\[Long Distance Devotion\]** buff felt
like a warm stone sitting in his heart. It didn't make him sad; it made
him focused. The "Social Quest" was complete. The "Relationship Slot"
was filled.

His brain was now 100% free for logistics.

"Inspector," Leon said, turning to the cart where the cat was cleaning
its ears. "We are currently operating at a surplus of time and energy.
Courting took up approximately four hours a day of mental processing. I
need to reallocate those resources."

The cat blinked. *You need a hobby.*

"I need a job," Leon corrected. "Ironhold is a city of metal. Metal
breaks. Therefore, someone needs to fix it."

He grabbed the handle of the cart.

"Let's go find the backlog."

The Industrial District of Ironhold was a sensory assault. Smoke hung
low in the streets, tasting of sulfur and coal dust. The rhythmic
*thud-thud-thud* of trip-hammers was the heartbeat of the city.

Leon breathed it in deeply. "Smells like productivity."

He walked past the High Guilds---fancy shops with glass windows where
Master Smiths crafted enchanted blades for nobles. Leon ignored them.
Those shops had waiting lists and paperwork.

He went deeper, toward the military supply sector.

Here, the shops were open barns, roaring with heat. Carts piled high
with dented armor, snapped spears, and dull swords clogged the streets.
The War in the South was hungry, and it chewed up gear faster than the
smiths could chew their lunch.

Leon stopped in front of a massive, chaotic workshop called "**The Iron
Stomach.**"

It was aptly named. It seemed to be vomiting broken steel onto the
street.

A dwarf with soot-stained skin and arms the size of beer kegs was
standing on a crate, screaming at a group of exhausted-looking
apprentices.

"I don't care if your arm hurts!" the dwarf roared. "The 4th Legion
needs these breastplates by sunset! Hammer until you bleed, then hammer
the blood into the steel! It tempers it!"

Leon parked his cart (and Bessie) in a safe alleyway nearby.

"Stay here, Inspector. Guard the cow."

Leon walked up to the dwarf.

"Excuse me," Leon said, his voice cutting through the din.

The dwarf spun around. "What? Unless you're selling stamina potions or
time travel, get lost! We're swamped!"

"I am looking for work," Leon said calmly. "I am a Journeyman Wanderer.
Level 10. My specialty is... Metal Tinkering."

The dwarf eyed him. He saw the broad shoulders, the calm eyes, and the
lack of burn scars.

"Tinkering?" The dwarf spat. "Boy, this is a repair depot, not a jewelry
shop. We pound dents out of plate mail. It's grunt work. It's hot, it's
heavy, and it pays dirt."

"I like dirt," Leon said. "And I have Constitution 14. I don't get
tired."

The dwarf paused. Constitution 14 was... high. Higher than his own.

"You don't get tired?" the dwarf asked skeptically.

"Not easily," Leon clarified. "I also require very little sleep. I find
sleep inefficient."

The dwarf narrowed his eyes. "I'm Foreman Grolm. If you want a trial,
grab a hammer. That pile." He pointed to a mountain of battered tower
shields. "They need to be flattened, re-strapped, and polished. You get
one silver for every ten shields."

"Accepted," Leon said.

Leon walked over to the pile.

Most smiths hated repair work. It was tedious. You weren't creating; you
were fixing someone else's mistake.

Leon *loved* repair work.

He picked up a shield. It had a massive concave dent where a troll
(probably) had punched it.

Leon didn't put it in the forge. He didn't heat it up.

He laid it on the anvil. He engaged his **\[Metal Tinkering\]** (which
was actually **Blacksmithing Level 50** wearing a fake mustache).

He picked up a four-pound hammer.

Tap.

He didn't smash the metal. He spoke to it. He found the stress point in
the steel lattice.

Tap. Tap.

The dent popped out. The metal didn't just bend back; the molecular
structure realigned. Leon used his thumb to rub out a scratch, infusing
a tiny amount of Ki to polish the surface.

"Next," Leon whispered.

He fell into the rhythm.

Grab. Inspect. Tap. Polish. Stack.

He didn't stop to wipe his brow. He didn't stop to drink water (his
camel-like Constitution held hydration efficiently).

The apprentices around him took breaks. They complained. They went to
lunch.

Leon kept working.

By 2:00 PM, the pile of shields was gone.

Foreman Grolm walked by, checking on the progress. He stopped dead.

"Where are the shields?" Grolm barked. "Did you hide them?"

"They are stacked," Leon pointed to a neat tower of gleaming steel
against the wall. "I also reinforced the handle rivets. They were loose.
Loose rivets cause vibration injury to the user."

Grolm walked over. He picked up a shield. It looked brand new. Better
than new. It felt... balanced.

"You did fifty shields in four hours?" Grolm asked, looking at Leon.

Leon was standing there, barely glistening. "The geometry was simple.
Mostly impact trauma. Easy to reverse."

"Right," Grolm muttered. "Geometry." He looked at the next
pile---thousands of dull spearheads. "Can you sharpen?"

"I can hone," Leon corrected.

The sun went down. The apprentices went home, groaning and nursing their
blisters.

Foreman Grolm was locking up the office. He looked out onto the workshop
floor.

Leon was still there.

He had moved on to the spears. The grinding wheel was singing a high,
constant note. Sparks flew in a continuous stream.

"Hey! Wanderer!" Grolm shouted. "Shift's over! Go home!"

Leon looked up, surprised. "Is the shop closing?"

"People need to sleep, kid!"

"Oh," Leon said. "I don't really have a place to stay yet. Hotels are
expensive. Is it okay if I work the night shift? I can just sleep on the
coal pile for an hour if I need to."

Grolm stared at him. "You want to... keep working? For free?"

"No, I want the silver per unit," Leon said practically. "I am saving up
for a house. But I work faster at night. It's cooler."

Grolm shook his head. "You're a maniac. A highly profitable maniac." He
threw a ring of keys to Leon. "Lock the gate if you leave. Don't steal
the anvil."

"I have my own anvil," Leon said earnestly.

Grolm just walked away, muttering about "crazy humans."

Left alone in the massive workshop, Leon smiled.

The silence of the night settled over Ironhold. Just the distant sound
of the city guards and the wind.

Leon brought the Inspector inside. The cat hopped onto a warm spot near
the cooling forge.

"This is nice, Inspector," Leon said, picking up a spear. "Just us and
the work."

He began to grind.

Zzzzzzt.

As he worked, he let his mind wander. He thought about Lyra. He wondered
if she was cold on the road.

"I should make her something," Leon mused. "Armor. But not leather.
Something lighter."

He looked at the scrap pile. There were bits of Mythril alloy mixed in
with the steel---too small to use for a sword, but...

"Chainmail," Leon whispered. "But woven like knitting. Fine mesh."

He finished the spear and tossed it onto the 'Done' pile.

**\[System Notification: Constitution Milestone.\]** **\[Subject has
performed manual labor for 16 consecutive hours.\]** **\[Stamina
Regeneration increased.\]**

Leon didn't look at the notification. He picked up another spear.

"I bet I can clear this whole room by dawn," Leon told the cat.

The Inspector tucked his nose under his tail. *Do it quietly. Some of us
are sleeping.*

"Right," Leon whispered. "Stealth grinding."

He cycled his Ki, muffling the sound of the wheel, and continued his
work. He was Leon the Journeyman, and he had a lot of emptiness to fill
with productivity.

simply for being an unstoppable engine of labor.
