# Chapter Forty-One: The Night Shift Singularity

The pile of dull spears was roughly six feet high at midnight.

By 2:00 AM, it was three feet high.

By 4:00 AM, it was gone.

Leon stood at the grinding wheel, surrounded by a silence that felt
heavy. The only sound was the low hum of the wheel spinning down and the
rhythmic breathing of the Inspector, who was deep in REM sleep on a
stack of leather aprons.

Leon looked at the empty floor. He looked at his hands. They were
stained grey with steel dust, but they didn't shake. In fact, they felt
restless.

"I have run out of inventory," Leon whispered.

He looked around the massive workshop. He had fixed the shields. He had
sharpened the spears. He had even re-riveted the buckets by the water
trough because they were leaking (wasteful).

He checked his internal clock. *Sunrise in two hours.*

"Two hours is too long to idle," Leon decided. "Idleness breeds rust."

He grabbed a broom.

He swept the entire 5,000-square-foot workshop. He organized the scrap
piles by alloy type. He oiled the hinges of the main doors. He scrubbed
the soot off the windows so the morning light would hit the anvils
correctly (better visibility = fewer thumb injuries).

As he was sweeping under the main workbench, his broom hit something
heavy.

He knelt down. It was a bucket of "slag"---waste metal skimmed off the
top of the crucible. But Leon's **\[Metal Tinkering\]** (Blacksmithing
50) tingled.

He reached into the bucket and pulled out a twisted, shimmering lump.

"Mythril filaments," Leon murmured. "Mixed with slag. Master Grolm must
have thought it was too impure to save."

Leon frowned. Throwing away Mythril was like throwing away diamonds
because they were muddy.

"Inefficient."

He walked over to the small jeweller's anvil in the corner. He didn't
light the forge; he didn't want to wake the neighbors.

He held the lump of Mythril in his hands. He closed his eyes.

He engaged his **Constitution 14**. He became a furnace. He pumped Ki
into his palms until the air shimmered with heat.

He squeezed.

He didn't hammer the metal; he *kneaded* it. He pulled the impurities
out like pulling rocks from garden soil. He separated the slag from the
silver-blue metal.

Then, he began to pull.

He drew the metal out into a wire. Thin, strong, and flexible.

"It's like spinning wool," Leon noted.

He sat on a stool. He took two small metal files and used them as
knitting needles.

Click. Click. Loop. Pull.

He wasn't making a sword. He was knitting a mesh. A fabric made of metal
that flowed like silk but stopped arrows like plate.

He worked for ninety minutes. The rhythm was soothing. It reminded him
of watching Grandma knit socks by the fire.

Click. Click. Loop.

When the sun finally crested the horizon, Leon had a square of
shimmering chainmail mesh the size of a handkerchief. It was weightless.
It was unbreakable.

He folded it carefully and put it in his pocket. "Prototype secure."

The front door unlocked with a heavy *clank*.

Foreman Grolm walked in, rubbing sleep from his eyes, holding a mug of
strong tea.

"Alright, Wanderer," Grolm grumbled, not looking up. "Hope you didn't
burn the place do---"

Grolm stopped.

He dropped his mug. It shattered, splashing hot tea on his boots. He
didn't notice.

The workshop was spotless. The windows gleamed. The tools were hung in
size order.

And in the center of the room stood a pyramid of sharpened spears so
perfectly stacked it looked like an art installation. Next to it was a
wall of gleaming, dent-free shields.

Leon was standing by the broom, looking apologetic.

"Good morning, Foreman," Leon said. "I'm sorry about the floor. I didn't
use the proper wax, so the shine is a bit matte. I'll fix it tomorrow."

Grolm walked over to the spears. He touched the tip of one. It was
razor-sharp. He looked at the shields. The straps were oiled.

"You..." Grolm whispered. "You did the week's quota. In one night."

"The night was cool," Leon explained. "Less sweating. Higher
efficiency."

"You fixed the buckets," Grolm noted, pointing to the trough.

"They were dripping," Leon said. "Drips create mud. Mud is a slip
hazard."

Grolm turned to look at Leon. He looked at the massive farmer who was
standing there, holding a broom, looking like he was waiting for a
scolding.

"You're a monster," Grolm breathed. "You're a terrifying,
profit-generating golem."

DING.

The sound was loud. It resonated in Leon's chest like a gong.

**\[System Notification: Industrial Milestone Achieved.\]**
**\[Achievement: The One-Man Factory.\]** **\[Subject produced 500 units
of war material in a single session with 0% defect rate.\]**

\[XP Threshold Exceeded.\]

**\[Level Up!\]** **\[Public Level: 10 \> 11\]**

**\[Stats Adjusted for Level 11:\]** \* **Strength: 14 \> 15**
(Threshold: Can now lift heavy livestock one-handed). \* **Constitution:
14 \> 16** (Threshold: Sleep is now optional; coffee is purely
recreational). \* **Dexterity:** (Hidden Update: Fine Motor Control
increased for 'Knitting').

Leon blinked. Level 11.

He felt the density in his core shift. The "Muffler" clicked open
another notch. He felt... roomy. His muscles felt loose and capable.

"Level 11," Leon noted. "Strength 15. That is a nice, round number."

He looked at Grolm.

"Foreman," Leon said politely. "I have finished the spears. Is there
anything else? Perhaps some catapults that need recalibrating? I feel
like I am just getting warmed up."

Grolm stared at him. He saw the gold light fading from Leon's eyes.

"Catapults," Grolm squeaked. He cleared his throat. "Yes. The Western
Depot has a trebuchet with a jammed counterweight."

"Excellent," Leon beamed, grabbing his staff and waking the Inspector
with a gentle nudge. "A trebuchet. That is just a very large lever. I
love levers."

He marched toward the door, energized and ready.

"Thank you, Foreman!" Leon called back. "Grinding is very efficient!"

Grolm stood alone in the sparkling clean workshop. He looked at the
shattered mug on the floor.

"I need to pay him more," Grolm whispered. "Before the King finds him
and makes him the Minister of Industry."
