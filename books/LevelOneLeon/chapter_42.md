# Chapter Forty-Two: The Real Estate of Ruin

It was payday at the *Iron Stomach*.

Foreman Grolm handed Leon a heavy leather pouch. The dwarf looked tired,
mostly because he had spent the week trying to find enough work to keep
Leon occupied, which was like trying to find enough coal to feed a
volcano.

"Seventy silver," Grolm grunted. "Includes the overtime bonus and the
'Please Don't Leave Me' bonus."

Leon weighed the pouch. It joined the pile of coin sacks sitting on the
floor of the stable stall he was currently renting.

"Inspector," Leon said, looking at the stash. "We have a surplus."

The black cat, currently resting on a velvet pillow Leon had bought
(using the proceeds from the Mud Crab sale), opened one green eye.

*Buy better salmon,* the cat suggested.

"We have enough salmon," Leon countered. "We have enough for a down
payment."

He picked up his slate and wiped away the **\[Industrial Grinding\]**
schedule. He wrote a new header.

**PROJECT: HOME BASE** \* *Requirement 1: Pasture for Bessie.* \*
*Requirement 2: Workshop space.* \* *Requirement 3: Privacy (to avoid
System Scans).*

"Living in a stable is inefficient," Leon explained. "Bessie deserves a
barn, not a stall. And I need a place to plant the seeds I brought from
Oakhaven. They are germinating in my pocket."

He stood up, grabbing his staff. "Come along, Inspector. We are going
house hunting."

The Office of Vane & Gilder - Land Brokers

Mr. Vane was a man who wore too much cologne and smiled with too many
teeth. He looked at Leon's dusty tunic and the cat on his shoulder with
mild disdain, until Leon dropped a bag of gold on the desk.

The thud was heavy. Vane's smile instantly became genuine.

"Mr. Leon!" Vane beamed. "A pleasure! A Journeyman of your stature...
surely you are looking for a townhouse in the Gilded District? Close to
the taverns? Stone facade?"

"No," Leon said. "I need dirt."

"Dirt?"

"Soil," Leon clarified. "Arable land. A barn. Outbuildings. Preferably
outside the city walls where the zoning laws regarding 'magical
composting' are lax."

Vane frowned, tapping his quill. "Outside the walls? That's dangerous
territory, sir. Bandits. Wolves. We don't have many listings there. Most
farms were abandoned when the War in the South started."

"Abandoned means cheap," Leon noted. "Show me the worst one you have."

Vane blinked. "The... worst?"

"The one nobody wants," Leon said. "The one with the 'structural issues'
and the 'bad reputation.'"

Vane hesitated, then pulled a dusty scroll from the bottom of a pile. He
blew the dust off theatrically.

"Well," Vane said, lowering his voice. "There is... Blackwood Farm."

He unrolled the map. It showed a plot of land about three miles south of
the city, nestled against the edge of the Whispering Woods.

"Ten acres," Vane recited. "Farmhouse, large barn, silo. But it's been
vacant for twenty years. The locals say the soil is cursed. Nothing
grows but Razor-Vines. And there are rumors of a... presence."

"A presence?" Leon asked.

"A ghost. A poltergeist. Something that throws rocks at people." Vane
shuddered. "Price is fifty gold. Firm."

Fifty gold was a steal for ten acres. It was practically theft.

"I'll take it," Leon said.

"Sir, you should look at it first," Vane warned. "The roof is caved in.
The vines are carnivorous."

"I like plants with spirit," Leon said. "Let's go."

Blackwood Farm

It was a disaster.

Even Leon had to admit, standing at the rusted iron gate, that
"fixer-upper" was a generous term.

The farmhouse was a skeletal wreck of grey wood, covered in thick, black
vines with thorns the size of daggers. The barn leaned dangerously to
the left. The fields were a tangled nightmare of purple brambles that
seemed to writhe even though there was no wind.

The air smelled of ozone and rot.

"See?" Vane said, standing well back from the gate. "Uninhabitable. The
soil is necrotic. You can't farm this."

Leon walked up to the gate. A vine lashed out, trying to snag his arm.

Leon didn't flinch. He caught the vine mid-air.

He didn't rip it. He pulsed his **Wisdom 11** and **Agrarian Arts**.

Analysis: Darkroot Ivy. Invasive. Aggressive. High mana content.

"It's not necrotic," Leon observed. "It's hungry. The soil is too rich
in wild mana, and these vines are over-feeding."

He squeezed the vine gently. "Calm down. I'm the new management."

The vine went limp.

Leon released it and looked at the house.

"The foundation is stone," Leon noted, engaging his **\[Metal
Tinkering/Structure Analysis\]**. "Granite. Solid. The wood is rotted,
yes, but the bones are good."

THWACK.

A rock the size of a fist flew out of the barn door, sailing straight
for Leon's head.

Leon tilted his head to the left. **Agility 12.**

The rock whizzed past his ear and hit the broker, Mr. Vane, in the
shoulder.

"Ouch!" Vane yelped. "See! The ghost!"

Leon looked at the barn. He saw a small, shadowy figure dart behind a
hay bale.

"That wasn't a ghost," Leon said calmly. "Ghosts don't have mass. That
was a projectile with a physical trajectory."

He turned to Vane.

"I will buy it," Leon said. "But since there is an active infestation of
'rock-throwing entities,' I am deducting ten gold for the extermination
fee."

Vane rubbed his shoulder. "Fine! Forty gold! Just sign the papers and
get me out of here!"

Ten minutes later, Leon held the deed. Vane had fled back to the city in
his carriage.

Leon stood alone in the overgrown courtyard of Blackwood Farm. The wind
howled through the broken windows. The Razor-Vines hissed.

"Well, Inspector," Leon said, looking around. "It needs a little
polish."

The cat jumped down from the cart and sniffed a thorny vine. *It smells
like a challenge.*

"It smells like potential," Leon corrected.

He walked over to the barn---the leaning, "haunted" barn. He placed his
hand on the siding.

"Hello!" Leon called out. "I know you're in there. I have a deed. I own
this building now."

Silence. Then, a small, squeaky voice echoed from the rafters.

"Go away! My house!"

Leon smiled. It wasn't a demon. It wasn't a wraith.

"Kobold," Leon whispered to the cat. "Probably a squatter."

"I have food!" Leon announced. "I have fresh bread and... cheddar
cheese."

A small, scaly head poked out from the hayloft. It was a Kobold, dusty
and wearing a hat made of a discarded bucket.

"Cheese?" the Kobold asked suspiciously.

"Sharp cheddar," Leon promised. "If you stop throwing rocks and help me
fix the roof, I will share."

The Kobold eyed Leon. Then it eyed the massive walnut staff on Leon's
back. Then it looked at the cheese in Leon's hand.

"Deal," the Kobold squeaked. "But I get the top bunk."

Leon chuckled.

**\[System Notification: Property Acquired.\]** **\[Blackwood Farm added
to Assets.\]** **\[New Quest: Restoration.\]** **\[Current Inhabitants:
1 Kobold (Neutral), 10 Acres of Aggressive Flora.\]**

Leon rolled up his sleeves.

"Level 11," Leon said, grabbing his shovel from the cart. "Strength 15."

He looked at the field of carnivorous vines.

"Let's do some weeding."
