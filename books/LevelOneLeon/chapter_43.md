# Chapter Forty-Three: The Green Negotiation

The sun rose over Blackwood Farm, revealing the full extent of the
disaster.

The ten acres were a sea of purple, writhing thorns. The "Razor-Ivy" had
choked out everything---the grass, the old orchard, even the path to the
outhouse.

Leon stood on the porch (which was missing three planks), holding a pair
of heavy iron shears he had forged the night before.

"Okay," Leon said to the air. "Staff meeting."

The Inspector sat on the railing, tail twitching. From the hayloft of
the leaning barn, a small, scaly head popped out.

"I'm listening," the Kobold squeaked. "But if the vines eat you, I get
your boots."

"You have a name?" Leon asked.

"Kip," the Kobold said. "I am the caretaker. I take care of not dying."

"Nice to meet you, Kip. I'm Leon. And we aren't dying today."

Leon stepped off the porch.

A vine, thick as a python and covered in inch-long thorns, lashed out.
It aimed for Leon's ankle.

Leon didn't dodge. He planted his foot. **Strength 15.**

The vine hit his boot and stopped dead, vibrating from the impact.

Leon knelt down. He didn't hack at it. He placed his hand on the
thickest part of the vine, right near the root ball.

He closed his eyes. **Agrarian Arts (Level 99)** engaged.

He didn't send a command of death. He sent a command of *Purpose*.

*You are hungry,* Leon projected his intent into the plant. *You are
aggressive because the soil is uncontrolled. But you are wasting energy
fighting the air.*

He pushed a pulse of Ki into the vine. It was soothing, like cool water.

*Retreat,* Leon commanded. *The center is for the crops. The perimeter
is for you. Guard the fence. Eat the wolves. Leave the tomatoes alone.*

The vine shuddered. The purple color deepened to a healthy green.
Slowly, reluctantly, it uncoiled from Leon's leg.

"Good," Leon whispered.

He stood up and began to walk.

He walked the entire perimeter of the property. Every time a vine
struck, he caught it, infused it with Ki, and redirected it toward the
stone wall that marked the property line.

"Move," Leon ordered, pointing to the wall. "That is your station."

Kip watched from the barn, jaw hanging open. He had lived here for five
years. He had seen adventurers burn the vines, freeze them, and chop
them. The vines always grew back angrier.

This human was... *herding* them.

By noon, the transformation was terrifying.

The center of the property was bare dirt---black, rich, and humming with
mana.

The *perimeter*, however, was a fortress. The Razor-Ivy had woven itself
into the old stone wall, creating a living, thorny barricade ten feet
high. It hissed at a passing squirrel.

Leon wiped the sweat from his brow.

"Security system installed," Leon noted.

He looked at the soil. It was "Blackwood Loam." High acidity, high mana
retention.

"Kip!" Leon called out.

The Kobold scrambled down. "You... you're a Druid?"

"I'm a Farmer," Leon corrected. "Grab a shovel. We need to turn this
soil before the rain comes. If we plant winter wheat now, the mana in
the ground will make it grow in half the time."

Kip looked at the shovel Leon offered. He looked at the terrifying wall
of living thorns that was now protecting them instead of hunting them.

"Okay, Boss," Kip said, grabbing the shovel. "But I still want the
cheese."

"Cheese is for workers," Leon agreed. "Dig."
