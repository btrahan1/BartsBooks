# Chapter Forty-Four: The load-Bearing Logic

The next day, the problem was vertical.

The barn was leaning at a 15-degree angle. The main ridge beam had
cracked, and the support posts were rotting.

"Gravity is winning," Leon diagnosed, inspecting the structure. "We need
timber. And I need to understand wood. Metal bends; wood snaps. I need
to update my database."

He left Kip in charge of rock-clearing ("Don't throw them at neighbors")
and headed into Ironhold.

The Timber District

The Ironhold Lumber Yard was a forest of stacked logs and sawdust. The
air smelled of cedar and pine resin.

Leon walked into the main workshop. An old man with sawdust in his
eyebrows was arguing with two apprentices who were trying to lift a
massive oak beam with a pulley system.

"It's stuck!" one apprentice yelled. "The rope is fraying!"

"Hold it steady!" the old man roared. "If that beam drops, it cracks!"

The rope snapped.

The beam---two tons of seasoned oak---began to freefall.

Leon moved. **Agility 12.**

He didn't try to catch it (that would bruise the wood). He slid
underneath it.

He assumed the **\[Atlas Stance\]**.

He caught the beam on his shoulders. **Strength 15.**

OOF.

His boots sank an inch into the packed dirt floor. The weight was
immense. But Leon's core held. His spine locked into a column of
unyielding stability.

"Secure the line," Leon said calmly, his voice strained but steady.

The apprentices stared. The old man stared.

"Move, you idiots!" the master carpenter yelled. "He's holding a house!"

They scrambled, re-rigging the chains. Once the tension was back on the
line, Leon stepped out from under the beam, dusting sawdust off his
shoulder.

The old man walked up to him. He was short, knotty, and looked like he
was carved from driftwood.

"I'm Master Eamon," the man grunted. "You just saved me fifty gold
pieces. That's Red Oak. Who are you? A golem?"

"I'm Leon. I'm a Journeyman. I bought the Blackwood place."

Eamon spat. "Blackwood? The rot-pile? You'll need more than strong
shoulders to fix that. The wood is cursed."

"It's not cursed, it's neglected," Leon said. "I need beams. But I also
need to know how to join them. My background is in smithing. I
understand rivets, but I don't understand dovetails."

Eamon looked at Leon. He saw the sawdust on his tunic. He saw the
respect in his eyes.

"You saved my wood," Eamon said. "I'll teach you the joints. But you're
doing the lifting. My back is shot."

For the next three days, Leon was a fixture at the lumber yard.

He learned that wood was alive. Metal you could force; wood you had to
persuade.

- *Dovetails:* Interlocking fingers of strength.
- *Mortise and Tenon:* The peg in the hole. Simple, eternal.
- *Scarph Joints:* Lengthening the reach without losing integrity.

On the fourth day, Leon returned to the farm with a cart full of cured
oak beams (which he pulled himself, giving Bessie a break).

He stood inside the leaning barn.

"Kip," Leon said. "Hand me the sledgehammer."

Leon didn't use a crane. He used his shoulder.

He wedged a temporary support post under the sagging roof. He braced his
back against the main beam.

"Heave," Leon grunted.

He pushed the barn upright. The wood groaned. Dust rained down.

With one hand holding the barn straight, he used his other hand to slide
the new support beam into place.

*Thud.* The tenon slid into the mortise.

He grabbed the wooden peg and drove it home with a single blow of his
palm.

The groaning stopped. The barn stood straight.

"Structural integrity restored," Leon announced, dusting off his hands.

Kip, who had been hiding under a bucket, peeked out. "It didn't fall?"

"Triangles," Leon explained, pointing to the trusses he had built. "The
strongest shape in nature. Now, let's fix the roof. I don't want Bessie
getting wet when it rains."

That night, Leon sat on the newly repaired roof of the farmhouse,
hammering shingles under the moonlight.

He looked out over his land. The vines were guarding the wall. The soil
was turned. The barn was straight.

"It's coming together, Inspector," Leon said.

The cat, perched on the chimney, purred. *Now we need furniture.
Sleeping on the floor is undignified.*

"Night shift at the Smithy starts in an hour," Leon checked the moon.
"I'll pick up some nails. And maybe... I'll look for a rug."

He climbed down. He had a farm to build, and the night was young.
