# Chapter Forty-Five: The Rust on the Crown

The dawn shift at *The Iron Stomach* usually began with a groan.
Apprentices dragged themselves in, hungover or exhausted, dreading the
heat of the forge.

Today, however, they walked in to the smell of fresh coffee and the
sight of a completely empty "Urgent Repair" rack.

Foreman Grolm stood in his office doorway, holding a steaming mug. He
looked out at the workshop floor. Leon was there, methodically wiping
down the main anvil with an oily rag. The floor was swept. The tools
were hung. The pile of mangled breastplates from the 4th Legion---which
had arrived late last night looking like they'd been chewed by
dragons---was now a neat stack of gleaming, dent-free armor.

"You didn't go home," Grolm stated. It wasn't a question.

"I lost track of time," Leon admitted, folding the rag. "I was
experimenting with a new annealing process for the rivets. It reduces
vibrational stress by 15%."

Grolm sighed, walking down the metal stairs. He kicked a stool over to
the anvil. "Sit down, Wanderer. You're making the day shift look bad."

Leon sat. The Inspector, who had been sleeping on a shelf of leather
straps, hopped down to Leon's lap.

Grolm took a long pull of his coffee (which Leon had brewed; it was
excellent). The dwarf looked older in the morning light. The soot in the
wrinkles of his face seemed permanent.

"You've been here what... two weeks?" Grolm asked.

"Twelve days," Leon corrected. "And four nights."

"You churn out gear faster than a factory," Grolm said. "But have you
looked at what you're fixing, Leon? Really looked at it?"

Leon paused. He rested his hand on the stack of repaired armor.

"I have," Leon said quietly. "The damage patterns are... inconsistent.
It's not just sword cuts or arrow punctures anymore. Last night, I fixed
three shields that looked like they had been dissolved by acid. And one
breastplate that was crushed from the inside out."

Grolm nodded grimly. "That's the report from Cinder Pass."

"The War in the South," Leon said. "High Paladin Kaelen mentioned it. He
said the morale was breaking."

"It's worse than that," Grolm grunted. He lowered his voice, leaning in.
"Look, Leon. You're a Journeyman. You travel. You see things. You should
know what the Guilds are whispering."

"I am listening," Leon said. His **Intellect 10** spun up, ready to
categorize the data.

"The King hasn't been seen in six months," Grolm revealed. "The Royal
Decree said he's 'indisposed with a spiritual malady.' Meanwhile, the
Dukes are hoarding grain. They're pulling their personal levies back to
protect their own estates, leaving the Royal Army---and the
Paladins---to hold the line alone."

"That is a supply chain failure," Leon noted, frowning. "If the central
leadership disconnects, the distribution network collapses."

"It's a kingdom collapse," Grolm corrected. "And the enemy... it ain't
just rebels or goblins, Leon. It's the Hollow Legion. Constructs.
Undead. Things that don't eat, don't sleep, and don't stop. They spread
that 'Rot' you see on the armor. It eats steel. It eats stone."

Leon thought of the Plague Bear. He thought of the necrotic vines at his
farm.

"The Rot is aggressive," Leon murmured. "It functions like an invasive
weed species."

"Whatever it is, we're losing," Grolm said bluntily. "Ironhold is
pumping out weapons as fast as we can, but we're running out of iron.
The mines in the West are shut down because of bandit raids. The grain
from the East is being price-gouged by the nobles."

Grolm looked at Leon with serious, dark eyes.

"I know you're saving up for that ruin of a farm. And I know you got a
girl down South. But take a word of advice from an old smith: Build your
walls high, Leon. And stock your cellar deep. This winter isn't going to
be just cold. It's going to be hungry."

Leon absorbed the information.

- *Fact:* Supply lines are fracturing.
- *Fact:* Central leadership is absent.
- *Fact:* The threat is necrotic and expanding.

Most people would panic. Most people would flee to the capital or hide.

Leon's **Wisdom 11** processed the threat. His **Agrarian Arts 99**
processed the solution.

When a storm is coming, you don't run. You reinforce the barn. You dig
the drainage ditches. You ensure the herd is safe.

"Thank you, Foreman," Leon said. His voice was calm, steady as a
heartbeat. "That is valuable data."

"You gonna run?" Grolm asked.

"No," Leon said. he stood up, dusting off his trousers. "I just bought a
house. I have a mortgage. And I have ten acres of potential food
production that needs to be brought online."

He looked at the stack of armor.

"If the Kingdom is hungry, Foreman, then the most valuable thing I can
be is a Farmer."

He picked up his staff.

"I will continue the night shifts," Leon promised. "But I need to
negotiate a trade. Instead of silver... can I be paid in scrap iron and
limestone? I need to reinforce my perimeter."

Grolm laughed, a short, barking sound. "You want garbage instead of
coin? You really are a crazy bastard."

"Scrap is only garbage if you lack imagination," Leon smiled.

"Fine. Take what you can haul. Just keep fixing the damn shields."

"Deal."

Leon walked out of the smithy into the bright morning light. The city of
Ironhold was waking up, noisy and oblivious to the rot creeping up from
the South.

Leon patted the pocket where he kept his deed.

"Inspector," Leon said to the cat on his shoulder. "The forecast calls
for heavy storms. We need to speed up the renovation."

The cat looked South, its green eyes narrowing. *Then we need more
hands. Two is not enough.*

"I know," Leon agreed, thinking of a certain bridge-building crew back
in Silt-River. "But first... let's go see about some seeds."
