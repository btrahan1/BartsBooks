# Chapter Fifty: The Taxonomy of Pests

The irrigation ditch was a mile long.

Normally, digging a trench of that magnitude required a team of
laborers, a team of oxen, and a week of back-breaking work.

Leon had done it since breakfast.

He stood waist-deep in the earth, his shovel moving in a blur of motion
that was less "digging" and more "earth displacement." **Strength 19**
meant the dirt offered about as much resistance as water. He scooped,
turned, and packed the embankment in a single fluid motion, his rhythm
unbroken for four hours.

Lyra sat on the edge of the trench, her boots dangling. She was
whittling a stick, watching him.

She knew him well enough now to know that the excessive physical output
wasn't just productivity. It was processing power. He was thinking about
something heavy.

"You're going to hit the water table if you go any deeper," Lyra noted
softly.

Leon stopped. He leaned on his shovel, breathing evenly despite the
mountain of displaced soil behind him.

"The grade is crucial," Leon said, staring at the mud. "If the water
flows too fast, it erodes the roots. If it flows too slow, it
stagnates."

He looked up at her. His eyes were troubled.

"Lyra."

"Yes, Leon?"

"Undead are easy," he said abruptly. "They are husks. Magic trapped in
calcium. Releasing them is... it feels like popping a dislocated
shoulder back into place. It is a relief."

He wiped a smudge of clay from his cheek.

"But the South. You said there are Goblins. Ogres. Trolls."

Lyra stopped whittling. She sensed the shift in the air. "There are.
Thousands of them."

"They are alive," Leon said. The words tasted like ash. "They breathe.
They eat. They have... families? Hierarchies?"

"They do," Lyra admitted. "Goblins have clans. Ogres have tribes."

"Then they are not weeds," Leon murmured. "Weeds are invasive plants out
of place. Goblins are... fauna. They are animals."

He gripped the shovel handle so hard the wood groaned.

"I can compost a zombie, Lyra. I can crush a skeleton. But if an Ogre
stands before me... a living, breathing creature that is simply
following its nature... I do not know if I can kill it."

He looked at her, his expression raw and vulnerable.

"I am Level 16. I have the strength to tear a house down. But if I go to
this war... and I hesitate... I will be a liability. I cannot protect
you if I am debating the moral value of the thing trying to kill you."

Lyra hopped down into the ditch. She landed in the soft mud, ignoring
the mess it made of her boots. She walked up to him and placed her hands
on his chest, right over the **Neutron Star** core.

"Leon," she said firmly. "Look at me."

He met her gaze.

"You aren't a liability," she said. "You're a Farmer. And what do
Farmers do with predators?"

"We build fences," Leon said. "We use dogs. We scare them off."

"And if the wolf gets into the pen?" Lyra asked. "If the wolf is tearing
at the throat of the lamb?"

Leon's eyes darkened. "Then we put the wolf down. Quickly. Without
cruelty."

"Exactly," Lyra said. "The Hollow Legion... the rot in the South... it
drives them mad, Leon. The Goblins aren't just living their lives. They
are infected. They are rabid. Killing them isn't murder. It's mercy.
Just like the bear."

Leon absorbed this. He processed the data.

Rabid Wolf Protocol.

It didn't make him *like* it. But it categorized it. It moved the action
from "Senseless Violence" to "Necessary Sanitation."

"Mercy," Leon repeated. "I can do mercy."

"And," Lyra added, stepping closer, "you don't have to be a killer,
Leon. You can just be the Wall. You hold the line. You keep them off us.
Let the Rangers and the Mages do the killing. You just... handle the
crowd control."

Leon nodded slowly. The tension in his shoulders eased.

"Crowd control," he agreed. "I am very good at herding."

He looked at her. She was covered in road dust, standing in a muddy
ditch, looking at him with absolute faith.

"Lyra," he said. "Before you go... I have something for you."

"Is it fertilizer?" she teased gently.

"No. It is... structural reinforcement."

He reached into his pocket and pulled out a small cloth bundle. He
unfolded it.

Inside lay the **Mythril Mesh**.

It shimmered like liquid moonlight. Leon had spent three nights knitting
it, pouring his **\[Metal Tinkering\]** and **\[Ki Infusion\]** into
every loop. It wasn't just chainmail; it was a fabric made of the
strongest metal in the world, woven with the care of a grandmother
knitting a sweater.

He had fashioned it into a cowl---a scarf-like loop that could be worn
around the neck or pulled up to protect the lower face.

"It is Mythril," Leon explained shyly. "I extracted the filaments from
the slag at the factory. I knitted it."

Lyra touched the material. It was cool and impossibly light. It felt
like silk, but when she squeezed it, it didn't give.

"You... knitted Mythril?" Lyra whispered.

"It protects the jugular and the carotid arteries," Leon said, his voice
turning clinical to hide his nervousness. "It is slash-resistant,
pierce-resistant, and I imbued it with a \[Temperature Regulation\]
weave, so it keeps you warm in the mountains and cool in the desert."

He took a breath.

"I know I cannot go with you today. I have to finish the harvest. But...
I worry about your neck. It is a very important structural point."

Lyra looked at the cowl. It was priceless. A piece of armor like this
would cost a King's ransom. And he had made it because he worried about
her neck.

"Leon," she said, her voice thick. "This is... too much."

"It is not enough," Leon countered. "But it is what I can do right now."

He hesitated.

"If... if you accept it... it means I am claiming that neck. It means I
intend to come South and ensure it stays attached to the rest of you."

Lyra laughed, a wet, teary sound. She took the cowl and looped it over
her head. It draped perfectly, shimmering against her green tunic.

"You're claiming my neck?" she smiled, touching the metal silk.

"I am optioning the whole Ranger," Leon corrected. "But the neck is the
priority."

Lyra stepped in and kissed him. It was fierce and desperate and full of
promises.

"It's a deal, Farmer," she whispered against his lips. "You finish your
harvest. You build your walls. And then you come find me."

"I will," Leon promised. "I'll follow the ribbon."

Leon saw the notification. He smiled.

"Now," Leon said, picking up his shovel. "You should step back. I have
another half-mile of ditch to dig before lunch. And the water table is
waiting."

Lyra climbed out of the ditch. She touched the cowl around her neck. She
watched him work---the rhythmic, unstoppable motion of a man who changed
the world one shovel-load at a time.

"Goodbye, Leon," she whispered.

"See you soon, Lyra," Leon called back, not stopping his rhythm.

He threw a shovel of dirt.

*To the South,* he thought. *Soon.*

But first, the wheat needed water. And the Kobold needed cheese.
