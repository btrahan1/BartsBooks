# Chapter 1: The Unseen Giant

Steven finished the last row with the sun climbing higher in the sky,
casting a warm, golden light across the freshly turned earth. *His final
time, he noted with a grim satisfaction, was four hours and fifty-one
minutes.* He had beaten his estimate by just under nine minutes, a small
victory in a day he wished was already over. He unhitched himself from
the plow, his shoulders screaming in protest, and left it standing like
a lonely sentinel at the edge of the field.

The water from the well was a breathtaking shock, washing away the sweat
and grime in a frigid cascade. He scrubbed himself down quickly, the
cold seeping into his bones, and dried off with a rough-spun towel. His
town clothes were simple but clean: a linen shirt that was tight across
the shoulders and a pair of dark woolen trousers. They felt foreign and
constricting after the freedom of his work rags.

He set off at a steady, ground-eating trot. The five miles to town were
a familiar path, a ribbon of dirt winding through fields and small
copses of trees. As his body settled into the rhythm of the run, his
mind, as it always did, began to churn. *He thought about the day ahead,
about the necessity of being present in a place he wanted nothing to do
with. And he thought about the way people would look at him.*

Steven was not self-unaware. He knew his size was the first thing people
registered, the sheer physical presence of him. But it was more than
that. There was a little something else, something unnerving about him
that he couldn\'t quite name. He'd seen it in the flicker of a
merchant's eyes, in the way other children would fall silent when he
approached them as a boy. It wasn\'t just respect for his strength or
fear of his size; it was a deeper, more instinctual caution, as if they
sensed a coiled spring within him, a tightly wound intensity he himself
did not fully understand.

Over the years, he had learned to "tone it down." It was a conscious
act, a series of subtle adjustments to make himself seem smaller, less
threatening. He would stoop his shoulders just so, rounding the powerful
slabs of muscle. He would keep his gaze lowered, avoiding direct eye
contact unless necessary. He learned to soften his voice, to speak in
shorter, simpler sentences than his thoughts would naturally form, lest
the quiet complexity of his mind leak out and create another layer of
unease in those around him. He learned to be the gentle giant, the
slow-moving, simple farmer people expected him to be. *It was a
performance, a suit of armor worn in reverse, not to protect himself
from others, but to protect others from their perception of him.*

As he neared the town of Oakhaven, the road widened and the foot traffic
increased. Families dressed in their best clothes were all heading
toward the town square. The air buzzed with a mixture of nervous
excitement and parental pride. Steven slowed to a walk, automatically
adopting his toned-down posture. He pulled his metaphorical cloak of
invisibility around his massive frame and melted into the crowd.

The town square was already teeming with people. Banners bearing the
crest of The Order---a golden crown pierced by a silver sword---hung
from the eaves of every building. At the center of it all was the town
fountain, a large, circular stone basin from which a statue of the
kingdom's founder, King Theron, stared out with stony resolve. This was
the designated meeting point.

Steven's eyes scanned the throng until he found what he was looking for.
Through the open doorway of Ye Olde Tavern, he caught a glimpse of
Sarah, her hair tied back, a tray balanced expertly on one hand as she
navigated a sea of boisterous patrons. She looked overstressed but
determined. *A brief, fierce wave of protectiveness washed over him. She
was the reason for all of this. The plowing, the scraping by, the
endless work---it was all to keep her and their mother safe and fed.*

He gave a nearly imperceptible nod in her direction, a silent promise he
knew she wouldn't see, and then turned his attention back to the task at
hand. Young men, all his age, were beginning to form a ragged line near
the fountain, shuffling their feet and trying to look braver than they
felt. Steven took a deep breath, lowered his head, and moved to join
them, a giant trying his best to be just another face in the crowd.
