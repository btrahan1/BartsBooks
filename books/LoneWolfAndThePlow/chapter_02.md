# Chapter 2: The Inspection

The line of eighteen-year-olds snaked from the fountain towards the
steps of the Town Hall. There were twenty-eight of them in total, a
nervous, fidgeting collection of farmers, craftsmen's sons, and a few
well-dressed boys from the wealthier merchant families. Steven
positioned himself near the end, a hulking shape of feigned placidity.
*He kept his eyes on the worn cobblestones, counting them, calculating
the area of the square, doing anything to keep his mind occupied and his
gaze averted.*

At precisely the twelfth bell, a side door of the Town Hall opened and a
man strode out, his polished black boots clicking sharply on the stone
steps. He was flanked by two guards in the gleaming silver and gold
armor of The Order. This was Commander Elias Vance, the local head of
The Order and the man who would conduct the inspection.

Commander Vance was a man carved from granite and discipline. His face
was a mask of stern authority, with a jaw that looked like it could
crack walnuts and eyes that missed nothing. He was not a large man, but
his presence was immense, radiating an aura of absolute command that
silenced the murmuring crowd. He held a small, leather-bound book and a
quill.

The process began, and *it was as swift and brutal as Steven had
imagined*. Vance walked down the line, his gaze flicking over each boy
for no more than a few seconds. He would assess their posture, the look
in their eyes, the set of their jaw. He was looking for a spark, a hint
of the fire required for an officer of The Crown.

\"Dismissed,\" he\'d say, his voice a sharp crack, and a soldier would
tap the boy on the shoulder, sending him back to his anxious family. One
after another, they were turned away. A cooper\'s son, strong but with a
nervous tic. A baker\'s boy, soft around the middle. A weaver\'s
apprentice, too slight of build. The commander made no comments, offered
no explanations. His judgment was absolute.

The merchant\'s sons, standing taller and wearing finer clothes,
received a moment more of his attention. Steven watched from under his
lowered brow as Vance paused before them. Their parents had likely paid
for private tutors in swordsmanship and strategy, grooming them for this
very day. The commander would look them over, make a small, precise tick
in his book, and murmur, \"Stand aside.\" These were the chosen few, the
\"possibles\" who would now become \"probables.\" Five of them, just as
expected, were selected and moved to a separate group near the Town Hall
steps, their faces a mixture of pride and relief.

The line dwindled rapidly. Dismissed. Dismissed. Stand aside. Dismissed.

Steven's heart began to beat a little faster, a heavy, rhythmic thud
against his ribs. He focused on his breathing, on keeping his shoulders
slumped, his hands hanging loosely at his sides. He made himself a
statue of insignificance. Just a big, dumb farmer's boy. Nothing to see
here. Move along. This was the performance of his life.

Finally, there was only one boy between him and the commander. Vance
dismissed the boy, a wiry farmhand with calloused hands and a sun-burnt
face, without a second glance.

And then, the commander's cold, analytical eyes fell upon Steven.

For a moment, nothing happened. The commander's gaze, which had been a
quick, dismissive flicker for so many others, simply... stayed. It was
as if a hawk, scanning a field for mice, had suddenly spotted a wolf.
The air grew thick and still around them. The casual dismissal died on
Vance's lips.

Slowly, deliberately, Commander Vance walked towards Steven, his steps
measured and silent. The crowd, sensing the shift in atmosphere,
murmured expectantly. Vance circled him, a predator assessing its prey.
His eyes raked over Steven's frame, and for the first time, Steven felt
the full weight of an expert's scrutiny. This was not the casual glance
of a town merchant; this was the gaze of a man who understood the
mechanics of power, of muscle and bone and the will that drove them.

Vance saw past the slump of the shoulders to the broad, powerful back
beneath the linen shirt. He saw the column of his neck, thick and
strong. He saw the hands, which Steven had tried to make look idle, and
recognized the ingrained strength in fingers that could likely crush
stone. He saw past the lowered gaze and sensed the coiled stillness, the
unnerving calm that Steven had so carefully cultivated.

The commander came to a stop directly in front of him, forcing Steven to
either crane his neck uncomfortably or meet his gaze. After a long,
tense moment, Steven slowly lifted his head.

When their eyes met, Commander Vance did not see a simple farmer. He saw
intelligence. He saw a deep, unyielding resolve. He saw the fortress of
numbers, the recited passages, the quiet weight of responsibility. He
saw the very thing Steven had spent years learning to hide.

A slow, thin smile spread across Commander Vance's face. It was not a
smile of warmth, but of discovery.

\"Well, now,\" the commander said, his voice low but carrying clearly in
the suddenly silent square. \"What have we here?\"
