# Chapter 3: The Gaze from the Crowd

The commander\'s words hung in the air, a hook cast to snag the truth.
Steven felt the collective gaze of the town settle upon him, heavy as a
shroud. *His plan was unraveling. The invisibility he had so carefully
woven was tearing at the seams.*

Instinct, honed by years of quiet observation and self-control, took
over. He offered a slight, deferential bow of his head, breaking the
commander\'s intense gaze. \"Steven, son of Alistair, from the northern
farms, Commander,\" he said, his voice deliberately pitched low and
even, the simple, respectful tone of a commoner addressing his superior.

Internally, he was a whirlwind of activity. He clamped down on the aura,
the presence, the unnamed intensity that Vance had somehow glimpsed. He
imagined it as a bright, burning lantern within his chest, and with a
conscious act of will, he began to draw a thick, heavy shade over it. He
pulled the vibrant, complex thoughts from the forefront of his mind and
pushed forward the mundane: the weight of the plow, the feel of the
soil, the ache in his shoulders. He became, in his own mind, nothing
more than the beast of burden he pretended to be.

\"Look at me when I speak to you, boy,\" Commander Vance commanded, his
voice sharp, cutting through Steven\'s performance.

Slowly, reluctantly, Steven raised his head again. He softened his eyes,
attempting to project a simple, bovine compliance. He held the
commander\'s gaze for a heartbeat, showing nothing but humble obedience.

It was in that moment of forced connection that he felt it.

It wasn\'t a sound or a sight, but a sudden, distinct shift in the very
texture of the air behind the commander. A new awareness brushed against
his own. It was a sharp, focused pinpoint of immense will, like the tip
of a perfectly honed dagger suddenly pressed against the edge of his
consciousness. It was a power fundamentally different from Commander
Vance\'s trained authority; this was something innate, ancient, and
vast.

Someone\'s eyes in the back of the crowd had swerved his way. Someone
powerful.

A jolt, cold and electric, shot through him. His first instinct was to
turn, to find the source of this incredible presence. But he fought it
down with brutal efficiency. To react would be to reveal. To show that
he had sensed her---for he knew, with an unshakeable certainty, that it
was a \'her\'---would be a catastrophic failure. It would prove that he
was far more than the simple farmer he appeared to be.

While his eyes remained locked on Commander Vance, placid and
unremarkable, Steven sank further into his own mind. *He didn\'t just
pull the shade over the lantern; he locked it in an iron box and sank it
to the bottom of a mental ocean. He built walls within walls, a fortress
of quiet humility around the core of his being. The unnerving stillness
that the commander had sensed became a vacant calm. The intelligence
became a dull placidity. He locked it all down, harder and tighter than
ever before, becoming a void, an empty space where a giant of a man
happened to be standing.*

He gave no indication. Not a flicker of an eyelash, not a subtle shift
in weight. His focus on the commander became absolute, his performance
of simplicity now a desperate act of survival against a new, unseen
observer.

Commander Vance's eyes narrowed. He saw the change, the retreat. He saw
the intelligence he had glimpsed vanish, replaced by the very dullness
Steven was projecting. For a moment, a flicker of doubt crossed the
commander's face. Had he imagined it? Had he mistaken a moment of
defiance for something more?

He studied Steven for another long, silent ten seconds, the entire
square holding its breath. Then, with a curt, dismissive shake of his
head, as if annoyed at himself for wasting time, he finally spoke.

\"You have the build for the army\'s infantry, boy. But The Order
requires more than brute strength.\" He raised his voice for the crowd
to hear. \"You are all dismissed.\"

A collective sigh of relief went through the remaining boys in line. But
as Steven began to turn, to lose himself in the dispersing crowd and
escape the suffocating weight of the twin gazes, the commander\'s voice
cut through the air one last time.

\"Not you,\" Vance barked, his eyes fixed on Steven. \"You stay.\"
