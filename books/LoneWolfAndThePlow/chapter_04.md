# Chapter 4: The Unveiling

The Commander's words, \"You stay,\" were still hanging in the air when
the world shifted on its axis.

From the open door of Ye Olde Tavern came a sound that was sharp, ugly,
and unmistakable. A loud, stinging *slap* of flesh on flesh.

It was followed an instant later by a cry of pain and surprise that
lanced through Steven's heart with the force of a physical blow. *It was
his sister's voice.* Then, a burst of cruel, masculine laughter rolled
out from the tavern, loud and mocking.

In the space of a single, frozen heartbeat, everything changed.

Gone was the veil. Gone was the humble farm boy. Gone was the gentle
giant and the fortress of quiet humility. The lantern in his chest did
not just flare to life; it exploded, vaporizing the iron box he had
locked it in. The crushing weight of his presence, the raw, unnerving
aura he had so painstakingly suppressed for years, flooded the town
square with the force of a burst dam.

*It was a pressure, a physical weight in the air that made the hairs on
the back of every neck stand up. It was the silent, absolute certainty
of violence.* His shoulders, which had been stooped in a permanent
apology, straightened and squared, revealing their true, formidable
breadth. His head came up, and the placid dullness in his eyes was
replaced by a cold, clear fire that promised retribution. *He was no
longer a farmer. He was a force of nature about to be unleashed.*

Commander Elias Vance, a man who had faced down charging cavalry and
stared into the maw of death without flinching, felt a primal jolt of
fear. He saw the transformation, the sudden inferno in the eyes of the
boy before him, and his military training screamed at him, but his
instincts screamed louder. He took an involuntary step backward. Then
another. His hand twitched towards the hilt of his sword before he
froze, realizing with a sickening lurch that a sword would be useless.

Across the square, in the shadow of the apothecary's awning, the woman's
gaze sharpened. The wave of raw power that washed over the square was
unmistakable, a clarion call to anyone sensitive enough to hear it.
Vanera, who had been observing with detached interest, paused. The
sheer, untamed magnitude of the boy's will was staggering. Her head
tilted, a gesture of pure, analytical curiosity. This was not the
trained, disciplined power of The Order. This was something else
entirely.

Steven's entire world had narrowed to the open door of the tavern. The
Commander, the crowd, the mysterious woman---they had all ceased to
exist. There was only the sound of his sister's cry echoing in his soul.

He turned his head slowly, his fiery gaze leaving the stunned commander
and locking onto the tavern. The words were spoken softly, yet they cut
through the stunned silence of the square like a razor.

\"Excuse me.\"

He did not run. He walked. Each step was a measured, deliberate beat of
a drum counting down to a final, terrible conclusion. The crowd parted
before him, a sea of frightened faces receding from an unstoppable tide.
They scrambled to get out of his way, not because he shoved them, but
because to be in his path felt like standing before an avalanche. He
moved with a terrible, focused grace, a giant who had finally, and
irrevocably, woken up.
