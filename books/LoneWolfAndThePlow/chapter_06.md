# Chapter 6: The Standoff

The tavern did not erupt in a brawl. There was no chaos, no prolonged
fight. There was only a single, brutal sound---a wet, solid thud, like a
side of beef being dropped onto a butcher's block---followed by the
splintering crack of wood.

Shortly thereafter, the body of a Soldier, still alive, but slightly
stunned, crashed through the tavern doors. He didn\'t just stumble out;
he was propelled, sailing backward through the doorway to land in a heap
of stunned limbs and bruised pride in the dirt courtyard where the
troops had gathered.

The soldier, a burly man who had been sharing the merchants\' sons\'
table, scrambled to his feet. Dust and humiliation clung to him.
Indignant fury burned in his eyes, *the murder he had intended to
inflict now reflected back at him.*

But that fire faltered, then died, replaced by a cold, creeping dread.

Steven made his way through the ruined tavern doors. He moved with the
same unhurried, deliberate pace. The silence that followed his emergence
was heavier than any sound. He stopped just inside the doorway and
turned to the tavern owner, who was peering over his bar, his face pale
with terror.

\"My apologies for the mess,\" Steven said, his voice calm and even. He
reached into a small pouch at his belt and flipped a single silver coin
onto the bar. It spun, a flash of impossible wealth for a simple farmer,
before settling. \"This should cover the door.\"

He then turned and continued out into the courtyard, his eyes locking
onto the soldier who now held a sword with trembling hands.

The other soldiers, who had instinctively moved to form a line, saw the
look on their comrade\'s face. They saw the absolute lack of fear in the
farmer who approached him. They took a collective, instinctive step back
and looked at their Commander hesitantly. Their hands rested on the
hilts of their swords, but their eyes were full of a question for which
*they feared the answer.*

Commander Vance's face was a thundercloud of rage. *This was
insurrection. An assault on a uniformed soldier, a blatant defiance of
his authority in the middle of the town square.* \"Take him!\" he
roared, his discipline overriding his fear.

But before the first soldier could move, before Vance's order could
galvanize them into action, a calm hand settled on his sword arm. He
turned, startled, to find Vanera beside him. He hadn\'t even seen her
move.

Her eyes, dark and ancient, were not on him. They were fixed on Steven
with an unreadable, intense focus. She slightly shook her head, a
minute, almost imperceptible gesture that was nonetheless an absolute
command.

\"Commander,\" she said, her voice a low murmur that was for him alone,
yet it carried the weight of unquestionable authority. \"Let this play
out.\"

Vance stared at her, his jaw working silently. He looked from her to the
scene unfolding in the courtyard: the lone, terrified soldier, the
unwavering farmer, and his own hesitant men. With a visible effort that
cost him dearly, the commander clenched his fist and held his ground.
The order to attack died in his throat.

The courtyard was now a silent stage. The soldier, trapped between his
duty to fight and his instinct to flee, stood alone. And Steven, a
pillar of cold, righteous fury, continued to walk toward him.
