# Chapter 7: The Debt

Steven stopped.

He was a mere ten paces from the soldier, a distance that felt both
intimate and as wide as a chasm of dread. The soldier's sword, which had
seemed so deadly moments before, now felt impossibly heavy in his hand.
The point wavered, dipping toward the dirt. Sweat trickled down the
man\'s temples, tracing paths through the grime of his sudden fall. *He
was a soldier of The Order, sworn to uphold the law and fear no man. Yet
the man before him was not a man; he was a reckoning.*

Steven\'s voice was not loud, but it filled the courtyard, each word a
perfectly weighted stone dropped into a silent pool.

\"I think you owe my sister an apology, and you owe me a silver coin.
How do you wish to pay?\"

The question was simple, direct, and utterly terrifying. *It was not a
threat of violence. It was a statement of transaction.* The soldier
understood, with sickening clarity, that *Steven was not talking to
him.* His gaze flickered past the terrified soldier to the dark maw of
the tavern doorway, where the true debtor was hiding. *The soldier was
merely the gatekeeper, the first part of the bill to come due.*

The demand was chillingly precise. An apology for the transgression
against his sister. And a single silver coin---the exact price Steven
had just paid for the destroyed door. *It was a perfect, terrible
symmetry. A soul for a soul, a debt for a debt.*

For the soldier, the choice was impossible. *To attack was suicide. To
stand down was to surrender his pride and his duty before the entire
town.* His breath hitched. The metallic taste of fear filled his mouth.
He looked at the unmoving giant, then at the hard eyes of his commander,
and finally at the useless sword in his hand. *It was a fool\'s burden.*

With a clatter that sounded as loud as a thunderclap in the dead
silence, the soldier dropped his sword. It hit the packed earth,
bouncing once before lying still. *He had made his choice.*

Commander Vance's jaw tightened so hard a muscle pulsed in his cheek.
*He saw it not as pragmatism, but as cowardice. A stain on the honor of
The Order.* Vanera, however, watched with an unblinking, analytical
calm. *The boy had a code. He was not a mindless berserker. He was an
executioner presenting a bill. This was far more interesting.*

Steven paid the disarmed soldier no mind. *The man had ceased to be
relevant.* He stepped past him without a glance, his boots crunching
softly in the dirt, and came to a stop facing the tavern.

He did not shout. He did not need to. He simply stood there, a silent,
immovable monument of pending consequence, his eyes fixed on the shadows
within the doorway. His stillness was a summons. And in the breathless
silence of the Oakhaven town square, everyone---the soldier, the
commander, the witch, and the townspeople---waited for the true debtor
to emerge and pay his price.
