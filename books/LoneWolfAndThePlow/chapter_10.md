# Chapter 10: The Law of All Men

The air in the courtyard grew impossibly thin, charged with the tension
of the moment. Steven\'s words, spoken with such calm, deliberate
weight, were more shocking to the commander than any physical threat.
*They were a fundamental challenge to his worldview.*

\"I know the law,\" Steven said, his voice resonating with a certainty
that had no place in a common farmer. \"It applies to all. Soldiers,
Commanders, and simple commoners like me.\"

He took a step closer. The movement was fluid, shrinking the space
between them, a silent escalation that was felt by every person in the
square. The tip of Vance\'s sword was now only a few feet from his
chest.

\"Your soldier seems to have forgotten that,\" Steven continued, his
eyes unwavering. \"Have you?\"

It was the final spark.

For Commander Vance, this was the ultimate heresy. *The law was not a
set of rules to be debated by farmers; it was a hierarchy, a structure
of power and obedience that held the kingdom together. He was the law\'s
instrument, and this boy was spitting on the very foundation of their
world.*

A vein pulsed in the commander\'s temple. The disciplined mask of the
officer shattered, replaced by a mask of pure fury. \"Insolence!\" he
snarled, the word a guttural rip from his throat. \"You will learn your
place!\"

There was no more warning. There was only an explosion of motion.

Commander Vance lunged. He was not a brawling soldier; he was a master
of his craft. The attack was a blur of silver, a textbook thrust aimed
directly at Steven's throat, designed to end the confrontation in a
single, fatal instant. It was fast, precise, and utterly lethal. The
crowd cried out, a collective shriek of horror, certain they were about
to witness an execution.

Vanera leaned forward, her eyes wide, absorbing every micro-expression,
every subtle shift of muscle. This was the moment of truth.

Steven did not retreat. He did not flinch. As the point of the sword
closed the final inches, his body moved with a speed that seemed
impossible for his size. It was not the desperate scramble of a man
avoiding death, but an act of sublime, instinctive economy.

He turned his torso a fraction of an inch, just enough for the blade to
skim past his throat. At the same time, his left hand, which had been
hanging loosely at his side, came up. It was not a wild swing, but a
short, precise chop, like a blacksmith striking a hot piece of iron.

Instead of flesh, the commander\'s blade met the unyielding edge of
Steven\'s hardened hand. The sound was not the wet impact of steel on
skin, but a sharp, ringing *CLANG*, as if the sword had struck a stone
anvil. A shockwave of vibration shot up the commander\'s arm, and with a
flick of Steven's wrist that seemed to defy the laws of physics, the
sword was wrenched from his grasp. It flew through the air, spinning end
over end, before burying itself deep into the wooden wall of the tavern,
where it quivered like a captured bird.

The courtyard fell into a silence so profound it was as if the world had
stopped breathing.

The commander stood, disarmed, his hand numb and tingling, his face a
canvas of pure, uncomprehending shock.

And Steven, his hand unmarked, took one final step forward.
