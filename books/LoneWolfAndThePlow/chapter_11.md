# Chapter 11: An Invitation for Tea

The commander's sword quivered in the tavern wall, a testament to the
impossible moment that had just transpired. The square was a vacuum, all
sound, all motion sucked into the void of collective disbelief.
Commander Vance stood frozen, his empty hand still held before him, his
mind struggling to bridge the gap between what he knew to be possible
and what he had just experienced.

Steven did not press the advantage. He did not raise a hand to the
disarmed commander. The man was no longer an obstacle. The path was
clear.

\"I have a farm to attend to,\" he said into the silence, the words
simple and mundane, yet utterly surreal in the context of what had just
happened.

With that, he turned and began to walk away, his back to the commander,
his destination the road that led back to his fields. It was an act of
such profound confidence, such absolute dismissal of the threat posed by
a Commander of The Order, that it was more stunning than the disarming
itself.

He took three steps before he stopped and turned to look back. His eyes
swept over the shocked faces in the crowd, over the pale and terrified
merchant\'s sons cowering in the tavern doorway, and over the humbled
soldier. His gaze finally settled on the commander, who was still rooted
to the spot.

\"I would strongly suggest that no one gives my sister any more
trouble.\" The statement was delivered without heat, a cold, hard fact
laid bare for all to see.

Then his eyes shifted, moving past the commander, and for the first
time, he looked directly at the woman standing in the shadows. His gaze
met Vanera\'s across the courtyard, a silent acknowledgment that he had
known she was there all along.

\"If you wish to further our discussion, I invite you,\" he said, his
voice carrying clearly to the commander. Then, his gaze still locked on
the woman, he added, \"And you, to visit in the morning. I will make us
some tea and we can have a pleasant conversation about The Law, The
King, The Order, Commoners, Taverns and Small Towns.\"

A low murmur rippled through the onlookers. He had not only bested the
commander but had just summoned him---and the strange, powerful woman at
his side---as if they were neighbors being invited for a chat.

Steven held their gazes for a final moment, ensuring his terms were
understood.

\"My offer is one of peace,\" he concluded, his voice the very
definition of finality. \"My family is non-negotiable.\"

Without another word, he turned his back on them all and walked away. He
did not look back. He simply walked, his broad shoulders disappearing
into the dispersing crowd, leaving behind a shattered sword, a shattered
sense of order, and two of the most powerful people in the region
standing in stunned silence in the middle of the Oakhaven town square.

Commander Vance stared at the space where the farmer had been, his face
a complex storm of humiliation, fury, and a terrifying sliver of doubt.
But Vanera\... Vanera watched the giant\'s retreating form with an
expression of pure, unadulterated fascination. The slightest of smiles
touched her lips. A farmer who knew the law, who could disarm the local
commander without breaking a sweat, and who had the audacity to invite
them for tea.

The Year of The Crown, 823, had just become infinitely more interesting.
