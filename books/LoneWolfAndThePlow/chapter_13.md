# Chapter 13: At the Gate

The cool, clean light of early morning spilled across the valley, but it
did little to cool the heat of Commander Vance's mood. He rode his
warhorse stiffly, a storm cloud in full uniform, the humiliation of the
previous day a hot coal in his stomach. Beside him, Vanera rode with a
relaxed, almost serene posture, her dark eyes taking in the landscape
with a calm, observant curiosity, as if on a trip to inspect a rare
specimen.

As they crested the final hill, the farm came into view. It was not an
estate, nor was it the hovel Vance might have expected. It was a modest
but meticulously kept plot of land, its stone fences built by a steady
and patient hand, its fields of freshly turned earth lying in perfect,
straight lines. It was a place of order and hard work.

And at the simple wooden gate that marked the entrance to the property,
Steven was waiting.

He was not armed. He wore the same simple clothes from the day before.
He was not flanked by anyone. He was simply there, at the gate, as if he
had grown from the very soil he farmed.

Commander Vance and Vanera reined in their horses a few yards from the
gate. The six soldiers in their escort clattered to a halt behind them,
a stark and shining intrusion of military might into the peaceful
morning.

Steven's eyes met theirs. He did not speak. He did not smile. He stood
at the gate in silence, a pillar of quiet strength. His gaze moved from
the commander's stiff posture to Vanera's placid curiosity. Then,
slowly, deliberately, it swept over the six soldiers who sat uneasily on
their mounts behind their commander. His eyes lingered on them for a
moment, *a clear, silent accounting of their presence.*

He looked at the two he had invited, and the six that he didn\'t.

And he waited.

The silence stretched, thick with unspoken questions. The jingle of a
horse's bridle was the only sound. The gate was closed. Steven was the
gatekeeper. The invitation had been for tea and conversation, not for an
armed delegation. His stillness was a question, more potent than any
spoken word. The first move of their \"pleasant conversation\" belonged
to the commander, and the presence of his uninvited soldiers was a
statement that required a response.

Steven waited. Calmly.

The Commander bristled. *The silent, unyielding stance of the farmer was
a direct challenge to his authority, a continuation of the previous
day\'s humiliation. He had brought the soldiers as a clear and necessary
statement of power. To be held at a gate by an unarmed commoner was an
intolerable insult.* His hand tightened on his reins, his jaw clenching.

But before he could issue a command that would have irrevocably
escalated the situation, Vanera simply smiled. It was a small, knowing
smile that acknowledged Steven\'s unspoken terms and found them\...
appropriate. She turned slightly in her saddle to face the escort.

\"You six will wait here,\" she said. Her voice was quiet, yet it
carried an authority that was absolute. The soldiers, who had been
tensing for a command from Vance, immediately relaxed, their expressions
a mixture of relief and confusion. They exchanged glances, but none
questioned the order.

Only when Vanera's command had been given and implicitly obeyed did
Steven move. With a smooth, efficient motion, he unlatched and opened
the wide wooden gate, swinging it inward. The hinges, well-oiled, made
no sound. He stood to the side, a silent invitation for them to enter
his domain.

As their horses passed through the gate, the feeling was unmistakable.

It struck them both at the same instant, a palpable shift in the
atmosphere. *To the Commander, it was a sense of profound and unsettling
order. Every stone in the low walls that lined the path seemed perfectly
placed. The furrows in the distant field were not just straight; they
were lines of perfect geometry drawn upon the earth. There was not a
stray weed, not a single tool left out of place. It was a discipline
that mirrored, and perhaps even surpassed, that of his own legion.*

For Vanera, the sensation was deeper, more elemental. She could feel the
very life force of the land. The soil beneath her horse's hooves felt
more vital, the air cleaner, the low hum of life that pervades any
natural space was somehow stronger, more focused here. It was as if the
farm itself had an aura, a powerful, benevolent presence that was
intrinsically and unshakably linked to the man who now closed the gate
behind them.

This wasn\'t a farm; a chaotic, messy place where a man struggled to
tear a living from the stubborn earth.

This was a Farm. A place of perfect, willed harmony. A domain.

There was a difference, a profound and undeniable difference, and both
the Commander and Vanera knew it. They had just crossed a border,
leaving the kingdom behind and entering a territory ruled by a different
kind of law altogether.

As they dismounted in the farmyard, the front door of the small stone
house opened. It was Sarah who emerged, carrying a simple wooden tray.
On it were three steaming ceramic mugs. She was the epitome of a
gracious host, her movements calm and welcoming, betraying none of the
terror she had felt the day before. By her side, her mother stood, a
silent, watchful presence, her hands clasped before her.

Sarah approached, and the savory, herbaceous aroma of a fresh tea wafted
towards them. It was a unique scent, a blend from the leaves she and
Steven often gathered together in the high meadows. She had a \"sense\"
for herbs, a quiet, intuitive knowledge of which leaves to pick and
when. She thought little of it, but most of those who drank her tea
enjoyed it immensely, finding it both calming and invigorating.

\"Welcome to our home,\" Sarah said, her voice steady as she offered the
tray first to Vanera, then to the Commander. After they had each taken a
mug, she accepted the reins of their powerful warhorses. With a
familiarity that suggested she\'d handled animals far more
temperamental, she led them away towards the barn, her mother following
to help.

As Sarah passed him, Steven stopped in front of his sister and gave a
single, deep nod. It was a gesture of profound gratitude and trust. Then
he turned to his guests.

He walked to the center of the yard, where a flat, circular stone base
was set into the earth. Bending down, he gripped the edges of what
looked like a nearby boulder and, with a smooth application of power
that made the Commander's eyes widen, rolled out a tremendous round
stone. It was a single, massive slate disc, three feet thick and
perfectly shaped, worn smooth by time and purpose. He lifted it and
placed it on top of the pedestal. There was a soft grinding sound as
grooves in the tabletop settled perfectly into their counterparts on the
base, locking it into place. It was not furniture; it was an altar to
the earth itself.

From beside the house, he brought two fine whicker chairs, their
delicate construction a stark contrast to the primeval table. He placed
them opposite each other, then gestured with an open hand.

\"Please,\" he invited his guests. \"Sit.\"
