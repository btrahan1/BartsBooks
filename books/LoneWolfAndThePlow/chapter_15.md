# Chapter 15: On the Road

As the farm disappeared behind the rolling hills, a subtle but profound
change came over Steven. The immense, protective aura that had been a
palpable weight in the farmyard receded, sinking back beneath his skin.
The mantle of the absolute ruler of his domain was laid aside. He was no
longer the Protector of the Farm; *he had vassals to take care of that.*

His posture changed. He was not the stooped, invisible giant from his
first trip to Oakhaven, nor was he the unshakeable pillar of authority
from that morning. He was something new: a quiet observer. He rode his
borrowed horse with an easy, natural grace, his body moving with the
animal, not fighting it. His eyes, no longer burning with protective
fire or veiled in humility, were constantly moving, taking in
everything. He noticed the way the wind bent the long grasses, the
subtle changes in the soil from one valley to the next, the flight
patterns of birds startled by their passage. *Steven was a farmer in a
new, vast field, and he was reading the land.*

He also watched the ten soldiers of their retinue. They were good men, a
disciplined and professional squad. They rode with ramrod-straight
backs, their eyes scanning the horizon for threats. They were sharp, but
they were also rigid. They saw the road and the hills, but they did not
see the story the land was telling. *Steven saw unused potential, a
fallow field waiting for a plow. An idea began to form, a simple,
logical extension of the work he had begun on the farm. Time, like
sunlight and water, was a resource. To waste it was a sin against
nature.*

That evening, as they made camp around a small, efficient fire, Steven
knew he had to speak. *This was not his farm. He could not command here.
The man across the fire was a Commander of The Order, a man Steven had,
surprisingly, come to respect.*

He waited until the simple meal was done. Vanera was watching the
flames, a placid smile on her face, but Steven knew she was listening to
the silence.

\"Commander Vance,\" Steven began, his voice calm and respectful. \"May
I ask a question of your experience?\"

Vance, who had been checking a saddle strap, looked up, intrigued by the
deferential approach. \"Of course.\"

\"Your men are good soldiers,\" Steven said, a statement of honest fact.
\"Disciplined. Loyal. But a soldier\'s life, it seems to me, is mostly
waiting. You wait for orders, you wait for a battle, you wait on the
road.\"

He paused, choosing his next words with the care of a man planting
seeds. \"On the farm, idle time is when things grow. It is an
opportunity. I wonder if it could be the same for a soldier.\"

He leaned forward slightly, his earnestness clear. \"With your
permission, I would like to offer your ten men\... a different kind of
work during our idle time. Not drills with a sword. Something else.
Exercises in balance, in awareness. In feeling the ground beneath their
feet as an ally, not just a road.\"

He leaned back, his proposal made. \"I believe I can make them better.
Stronger in a way that is not just muscle. But they are your men, and I
would not presume to do so without your full blessing. I would only do
so as your guest, under your command.\"

The Commander was silent for a long moment, his gaze distant. Vanera,
intrigued as always, watched him, a slight, knowing smile touching her
lips. She had known Steven would not remain a passive passenger for
long.

Vance\'s eyes shifted, looking over at his men as they set the watch for
the night. Then his mind went back to the gate of the farm, to the four
men who had been utterly transformed, their eyes burning with a purpose
he had never been able to instill. He looked back at the farmer sitting
across the fire.

\"Steven,\" the Commander said, his voice low and serious. \"I saw what
you did with four of my men in a week. You did not just train them. You
gave them a soul.\"

He straightened up, his decision made. \"You have my blessing. You have
my full permission. They are not my men to command for this journey;
they are your students.\" He offered a small, respectful nod. \"I
confess, I am eager to see these methods for myself.\"
