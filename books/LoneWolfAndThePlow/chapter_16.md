# Chapter 16: The First Lesson

Late that afternoon, with the sun casting long, golden shadows across a
grassy clearing, Commander Vance assembled the ten men. They formed a
crisp, disciplined line, their faces impassive, their posture perfect.
They were the image of professional soldiers.

The Commander stood before them, a figure of absolute authority.
\"Men,\" he began, his voice carrying in the still air. \"For the
duration of our journey to the Academy, you will be receiving
supplementary instruction. This man,\" he gestured to Steven, who stood
calmly to the side, \"is Steven of Alistair. He will be your instructor.
You will give him your full attention and your complete obedience.
Listen to him. Do as he says.\"

With that, the Commander gave a curt nod, a formal transfer of
authority. He then stepped back, moving to stand beside Vanera under the
shade of a large oak tree. He was as intrigued as she was now, a
spectator to his own command.

Steven walked forward and stood before the line of soldiers. He did not
speak. He simply stood there, his hands clasped behind his back, and
looked at them. One by one, his gaze traveled down the line, a slow,
deliberate assessment. The soldiers held their positions, but under the
weight of his silent scrutiny, a flicker of unease began to creep into
their perfect discipline. A man shuffled his feet. Another cleared his
throat.

After a full minute of profound silence, Steven spoke, his voice quiet,
yet it carried to every man.

\"Take off your boots.\"

The soldiers blinked. A few exchanged confused glances. Their sergeant,
a grizzled veteran named Corben, took a half-step forward. \"Sir, if I
may---\"

\"As you were, Sergeant,\" Commander Vance\'s voice cut sharply from the
sideline. Corben snapped back into line, his face a mask of confusion.

Slowly, hesitantly, the ten soldiers knelt and unlaced their heavy,
hobnailed boots. They placed them in neat lines on the grass and stood
again, the unfamiliar feeling of cool earth and soft grass beneath their
feet a strange and vulnerable sensation.

\"Good,\" Steven said. \"Now. Stand on your left foot. Raise your right
knee.\"

It was a simple command, but the result was a mess. Men wobbled. Some
hopped to maintain their balance. One nearly fell over, catching himself
at the last moment. They were strong, disciplined men, but their balance
was built for the brace of armor and the solid plant of two feet on the
ground.

Steven walked slowly down the line, not barking corrections, but
observing. He stopped in front of a young soldier who was swaying like a
sapling in a high wind.

\"You are fighting the ground,\" Steven said softly. \"You are thinking
of the sky. Stop.\" He lightly touched the soldier\'s back. \"Feel the
earth holding you up. It is not your enemy. Let it support you.\"

He moved to the next man. \"You hold your breath. Breathe. Your breath
is a current. Let it flow.\"

He spent ten minutes walking among them, offering quiet, individual
corrections. Finally, he returned to the front. Most of the men were now
standing still, though their muscles trembled with the effort.

\"Your center of strength is not in your shoulders, which you use to
carry your armor,\" he said, his voice calm and instructive. \"It is not
in your head, where you carry your orders. It is here.\" He placed a
hand flat on his own abdomen. \"It is the root that connects you to the
ground. The rest of you is just branches in the wind. Find your root.
Hold it. Now, put your foot down.\"

A collective sigh of relief went through the men as they stood on two
feet again.

\"That is all for today,\" Steven said. He gave a respectful nod to the
Commander and then walked to the fire, leaving the ten soldiers standing
barefoot in the grass, utterly bewildered and strangely energized.

Under the oak tree, the Commander turned to Vanera, his face a mixture
of confusion and awe. \"What was that? He taught them\... how to
stand.\"

Vanera smiled, her eyes fixed on Steven\'s retreating back. \"No,
Commander,\" she corrected softly. \"He is teaching them how to be
mountains.\"
