# Chapter 17: The Law of the Fence

The next day, the road widened, and the thatched roofs of a new town,
Stonebridge, appeared in the valley below. Smoke curled from chimneys,
and the distant sound of a blacksmith\'s hammer echoed in the air. It
was another recruiting stop for The Order, another town square to be
filled with hopeful boys and proud families.

As they began their descent, Steven guided his horse alongside the
Commander\'s. He watched the ten soldiers of their retinue, seeing how
their posture stiffened, a subtle, ingrained arrogance emerging as they
neared a place where their uniforms would grant them status and fear.

\"Commander,\" Steven said quietly, his voice a respectful murmur.
\"Before we enter the town, perhaps you would address the men regarding
their conduct?\"

Commander Vance looked at Steven, a slow, genuine smile spreading across
his face. He shook his head. \"They are your men now, Steven. I am an
interested spectator at this point.\"

Steven nodded, accepting the responsibility. He signaled for the column
to halt. The ten soldiers pulled up, forming two neat lines on the road.
Vanera watched, intrigued as always.

Steven did not stand before them like a commander. He simply faced them,
his expression serious.

\"In a moment,\" he began, his voice calm and steady, \"we will enter
that town. Look at it.\" He gestured with his hand. \"There are bakers
in there who get up before the sun. There are masons with calloused
hands and aching backs. There are women tending to children and men
tilling small gardens. They are the ones who make everything else
possible. They are the soil from which the kingdom grows.\"

He let his gaze pass over each man, ensuring he had their complete
attention. \"We, as soldiers, are the fence. We are here to protect the
field from the wolves and the blight. But a fence that tramples the
crops it is meant to guard is worse than useless. It is a curse.\"

His tone hardened slightly, the quiet instruction replaced by an
absolute command.

\"You will not be a curse. You will enter this town with respect. You
will not swagger. You will not demand. You will pay for what you take,
and you will say \'please\' and \'thank you\' when you do it. Any man
who starts a drunken brawl or insults a commoner will answer directly to
me.\"

He paused, letting the weight of his law settle. \"You are not here to
be served. You are here to serve. You are the shield. A shield does not
bully the person it protects.\"

He looked at each of them again. \"If you dishonor these people, you
dishonor me. You dishonor the Farm. And you will find you are no longer
welcome in either.\"

The soldiers sat taller in their saddles, but the arrogance was gone. In
its place was a look of dawning understanding. They were not just being
given rules; they were being given a code, a higher purpose.

\"Let\'s go,\" Steven said simply, and nudged his horse forward.

The column entered Stonebridge not as a conquering force, but as quiet
guardians, their eyes holding a new and profound respect for the common
folk they passed.

Vanera rode a few paces behind, a silent observer to the sermon on the
road. As the column moved into Stonebridge, she gently shook her head, a
slow, bewildered motion.

*Even away from the Farm, he had Power. She just didn\'t understand it.*
She was a master of power in all its forms. The raw, elemental forces
that could be bent to a sorcerer\'s will. The intricate, subtle power of
political maneuvering. The brutal, straightforward power of an army. She
understood these things as a master weaver understands her loom. But
this\... this was a thread she had never seen before.

*It was just words, for The Creator\'s Sake!* There was no incantation,
no mystical energy, no threat of immediate violence. He had simply
spoken. He had told a group of hardened, professional soldiers that they
were a fence and that the people were the crops. And in the space of a
heartbeat, he had fundamentally altered their perception of themselves
and their duty. It was like watching a man tell a river to flow uphill,
and then seeing the water obey.

How did he do it?

As they entered the town square, a bustling hub of commerce and daily
life, she realized something fundamental had changed, not just in the
soldiers, but in the very air around them. Normally, when a uniformed
patrol of The Order entered a town, there was a subtle shift. Merchants
would watch their goods a little more closely. Mothers would pull their
children a little nearer. A quiet, low-level fear, a deference born of
intimidation, would settle over the square.

But not today.

The ten soldiers moved with a quiet, respectful purpose. They dismounted
and secured their horses without shouting, their movements economical
and unobtrusive. They moved through the crowd not as occupiers, but as
guests. The townspeople, sensing no threat, no arrogance, simply went
about their business. The atmosphere was not one of fear; it was one of
simple, peaceful coexistence.

Vanera sat on her horse, watching this impossible scene unfold. The
power Steven wielded wasn\'t just over his men. It was a power that
radiated outward, a calming, ordering influence on the world around him.
He hadn\'t just changed the soldiers; he had changed the very nature of
their interaction with the world.

She looked at Steven, who was quietly speaking with the town elder, his
posture deferential, his voice calm. He was not casting a spell. He was
simply *being*. And she, the woman who knew everything about power, was
beginning to suspect that *she had been studying the wrong kind her
entire life*.
