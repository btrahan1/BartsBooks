# Chapter 18: The Right Question

The town crier\'s bell rang, and soon a line of nervous young men, all
eighteen years of age, formed in the center of the square. It was a
familiar scene, a ritual Vanera had witnessed a hundred times. The boys
stood, shuffling their feet, trying to look brave for their families who
watched from the edges of the square.

Steven, the Commander, and Vanera sat on their horses, observing. The
Commander, by tradition, was meant to conduct the inspection. But he
made no move to dismount. He simply sat on his horse and waited, his
eyes on Steven.

After a moment of silence, Steven gave a slight nod of acknowledgment to
the Commander, accepting the deferred responsibility. He swung his leg
over the saddle and slid to the ground, his boots making a soft thud on
the cobblestones. He handed his reins to one of his men.

He walked to the front of the line of boys, but he did not pace before
them like a predator. He stood still, his presence calm and reassuring.
He addressed them not with the authority of a recruiter or the power of
a commander, but with a quiet grace and respect that immediately put the
nervous boys at ease.

\"My name is Steven,\" he began, his voice no louder than needed to be
heard. \"I am here today with The Order. I know what you\'re thinking.
You\'re wondering if you\'re strong enough, if you\'re brave enough, if
you have what it takes to be chosen.\"

He let his gaze pass over their faces, seeing the hope, the fear, the
parental pressure.

\"Those are all good questions,\" he continued. \"But they are not the
first question you should be asking.\" He paused, letting them lean in,
letting them wonder. \"The first question is simpler. Do you want to be
here?\"

A confused murmur went through the line of boys.

\"This is not a punishment,\" Steven said. \"Service to the kingdom is a
duty, but it is a duty that must be taken up with a willing heart. If
you are standing here because your father told you to, or because you
think it is the only way to earn a wage, or because you seek glory\... I
am telling you now, that is not enough. The path of a soldier is hard.
It is lonely. And it requires more from you than you can possibly
imagine.\"

He made a simple, open-handed gesture to the side. \"If you do not truly
wish to be here, you may leave now. There is no shame in it. There are a
hundred other ways to serve the kingdom. Go home. Be a good son. Be a
good baker, or a good farmer. That is a noble service in itself. No
questions will be asked.\"

He then simply stood, his hands clasped behind his back, and waited.

For a long moment, no one moved. Then, a boy near the end of the line,
his face a mask of profound relief, gave a hesitant bow and walked away,
melting back into the crowd where a woman who was surely his mother
embraced him. A moment later, two more boys followed, their shoulders
losing a tension they had been carrying all morning. In the end, five of
the twenty boys left the line.

Only when they were gone did Steven nod. He looked at the fifteen who
remained, seeing a different light in their eyes now. They were not just
hopefuls; they were volunteers.

Now, he could begin his own assessment.
