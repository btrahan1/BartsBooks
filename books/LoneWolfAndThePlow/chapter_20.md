# Chapter 20: Weeding the Garden

Steven smiled. It was an honest smile, wide and full of a quiet pride.
He KNEW his land, his people, had HEART. But THIS---this unified,
unhesitating step forward---was more than he had expected. A warm
feeling, like the first sun of spring on the soil, spread through his
chest.

He looked at the ragtag group of individuals before him. Farmers\' sons
with strong backs, a blacksmith\'s apprentice with calloused hands, a
few thin but eager boys from the town. He knew they all *wanted* to
prove themselves worthy in this moment of inspiration. But alas, not
everyone had what it takes in the long run. A momentary fire in the
belly could light a lamp, but it couldn\'t keep a forge hot through the
winter. The Order needed a fire that would last.

It was time to do some weeding.

His smile faded, replaced by a look of calm seriousness. \"Your heart is
in the right place,\" he said, his voice returning to a more instructive
tone. \"But the heart is a muscle. It must be tested. It must be
trained.\"

He walked to the center of the group. \"I am going to ask you to do
something very simple. But it will not be easy.\"

He demonstrated, standing before them. \"You will stand on your left
foot. You will raise your right knee, like so. You will place your hands
behind your back. You will look straight ahead. And you will hold it.\"

The boys, confused but willing, mimicked his stance. A few wobbled, but
they all managed it.

\"The test is not to see who can stand the longest,\" Steven explained,
his voice calm and clear. \"The test is to see *how* you stand. I will
be watching. The Commander will be watching. There is only one rule: you
may put your foot down whenever you wish. The moment your foot touches
the ground, you will quietly walk away and rejoin your family. That is
all.\"

And with that, he said no more. He simply stood, a quiet observer, and
let the test begin.

The first five minutes were easy. The boys were full of determination,
their faces set with resolve. But as the minutes stretched into ten,
then fifteen, the reality of the test began to set in. Muscles began to
burn. Ankles started to ache. The initial fire of inspiration was being
consumed by the slow, grinding reality of discomfort.

A boy grimaced, his leg trembling violently. He fought it for another
minute, then, with a sigh of frustrated defeat, he put his foot down and
walked away, his head hung low.

Another boy began to mutter under his breath, complaining about the
pointlessness of it all. He lasted another five minutes before quitting
in a huff.

Another tried to distract himself by looking at the crowd, only to lose
his focus and topple, disqualifying himself.

Steven watched them all. He wasn\'t looking for the ones with the best
balance. He was looking for the ones who, when the pain came, went
inward. The ones who found a quiet, still place inside themselves and
simply\... endured. He was weeding out the impatient, the complainers,
the easily distracted. He was looking for the ones who understood,
without being told, that this was not a test of the body, but of the
will.
