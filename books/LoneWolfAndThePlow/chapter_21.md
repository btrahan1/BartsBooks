# Chapter 21: The Carrot

The sun climbed higher, beating down on the town square. One by one, the
boys reached their limits. Some quit with a frustrated curse, others
with a quiet sigh of resignation. Each time a boy put his foot down, he
was met not with scorn, but with a respectful nod from one of Steven\'s
men, who would quietly escort him back to his family. There was no shame
in finding one\'s limit, only in pretending you didn\'t have one.

In the end, after nearly an hour had passed, there were five still
standing.

Their faces were pale with strain. Sweat beaded on their foreheads and
trickled down their temples. Their supporting legs trembled with a
fatigue so deep it was a constant, agonizing tremor. There were tears of
determination in their eyes, blurring their vision, but behind the
tears, the fire burned strong. They had gone past the point of mere
physical endurance and had entered a place of pure, unyielding will.

Yes, Steven thought with a deep, profound satisfaction. These would do
nicely.

It was time for the carrot.

\"You may put your foot down,\" he said, his voice gentle but clear.

The five boys lowered their legs with a collective, shuddering sigh of
relief, nearly collapsing where they stood. They had done it. They had
passed.

Steven walked to stand before them. He did not look at them as a
recruiter examining new assets. He looked at them with the pride of a
farmer who had just found five healthy, strong saplings that would one
day grow into mighty oaks.

\"Look at me,\" he said, and waited until all five had met his gaze.
\"Today, you found a strength you did not know you had. You found a
place inside yourself where the pain could not reach. Remember this
feeling. This is the root from which all your courage will now grow.\"

He then smiled, a warm, genuine smile that washed away their exhaustion.
\"The Order can give you a uniform and a sword. It can give you food and
a wage. But what you have just earned for yourselves is something far
more valuable.\"

He looked at each of them in turn, his voice filled with a quiet,
sincere respect.

\"You have earned a chance. You have earned a beginning. And you have
earned my attention.\" He gestured to the Commander, who stepped
forward, his face a mask of deep approval. \"Go now,\" Steven said.
\"Say goodbye to your families. Your new life starts today.\"

And there it was.

As Steven spoke those final words, \"You have earned my attention,\"
Vanera felt it. A flicker. A resonance in the air around him that was
almost physical. For a fleeting instant, *she thought she saw a glimpse
of that impossible power.*

He was somehow projecting his will. It wasn\'t a command or an
incantation. It was as if his own standards, his own unshakeable morals,
his own deep, rooted strength were emanating from him like heat from a
forge, and the five boys who were attuned to it, who had proven
themselves worthy, were being tempered in its fire. It wasn\'t magic.
She would have recognized the tell-tale shimmer of arcane energy. She
didn\'t know what it was, but she had seen a glimpse of its shape, and
it was as awesome as it was terrifying.

She looked at the five remaining boys. They stood straighter, the
exhaustion on their faces now mingled with a fierce, quiet pride. *They
were no longer just boys from a backwater town. They were his.* They had
been chosen by a standard that had nothing to do with wealth or
breeding, and even she, who had judged thousands, was impressed. *Had
they---The Order, The Crown, her---been looking for the wrong qualities
all along?*

Her gaze swept around the townspeople at the edge of the square.
Usually, this was a moment of mixed emotions: pride for the chosen,
sadness for the departure, quiet relief for those not taken. But today
was different. There was a collective pride she had never seen before.
They were not just proud of the five boys who had endured. They were
proud of the process itself. They had witnessed a test of will, not of
brute strength. They had heard a speech about love and duty, not just
glory and gold. The farmer had not just recruited five boys; he had
validated the quiet, everyday strength of the entire town. *That was as
interesting as everything else.*

Vanera felt a subtle shift in her own ancient, settled worldview. Even
though she had caught a glimpse of his power, a hint of its nature, she
knew, with a certainty that chilled her to the bone, that there was so
much more she didn\'t see.

And to her, the thought of not seeing something was\... *disturbing.*
