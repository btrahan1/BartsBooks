Lord Tomas The Farmer

Bart Trahan

### 

The rich soil of the Cresting Hills was the only world Tomas had ever
known. At twelve, his hands were already calloused from the plow and his
face freckled by the sun. Life was a simple, satisfying rhythm of sowing
and reaping alongside his mother and father. He desired nothing more.
This contentment, however, was a luxury they could no longer afford.

The problem arrived on the back of a royal decree, nailed to the village
notice board. On a young man's sixteenth birthday, he would be
conscripted into the Royal Army if he held no formal apprenticeship. A
blacksmith, a tanner, a cooper---any trade with a certified master would
exempt a boy from service. Farming, the lifeblood of the kingdom, was
inexplicably not considered a qualifying skill. It was a peasant's lot,
not a trade.

Panic rippled through the farming families. Masters of the trades,
already few and far between, were suddenly inundated with desperate
requests. Doors were politely but firmly closed. There were no
apprenticeships to be had. With four years until his conscription, time
was already running short for Tomas.

That left one option, an unspoken dread in the household: Uncle Silas.

His father's brother was a man spoken of in whispers, a figure who lived
on the rocky outskirts of the territory where the soil was too mean for
farming. He was a retired adventurer, some said a former legionnaire
captain. The only thing anyone knew for certain was that Silas could
take a guppy and forge it into a predator of the deep. But the process
was agonizing for the fish.

The journey to his uncle's home was a silent, somber affair. The rolling
green hills gave way to jagged stones and thorny scrub. Silas's house
was more of a small, fortified stone hovel, a plume of smoke rising from
its chimney like a defiant finger.

Silas met them at the door. He was a wall of muscle and scars, his face
a roadmap of old battles. His eyes, sharp and severe, immediately fell
on Tomas.

A transparent blue screen, visible only to Tomas, flickered into
existence.

**Name:** Silas **Level:** ?? **Class:** ?? **Title:** The Grinder

Tomas's own screen felt pitiful in comparison.

**Name:** Tomas **Level:** 3 **Class:** Farm Boy **Title:** None

**Attributes:** \* **Strength:** 6 \* **Agility:** 5 \* **Stamina:** 12
\* **Intelligence:** 7 \* **Wisdom:** 8

**Skills:** \* \[Basic Husbandry Lv. 5\] \* \[Tilling Lv. 4\] \* \[Rock
Picking Lv. 8\] \* \[Endurance (Passive) Lv. 9\]

Silas grunted, and for a terrifying moment, Tomas thought his uncle
could see his stats. "So, this is the boy. You want me to keep him out
of the army."

"We have no other choice," Tomas's father said, his voice strained.

Silas circled Tomas like a wolf inspecting livestock. "Look at him.
Soft. He has the 'Farm Boy' class. The army will eat him alive. They'll
put him on the front line with a sharpened stick and call it a
promotion." His gaze lingered on Tomas. "But that stamina... that
endurance... from picking rocks all day, I imagine. There's something to
work with. A foundation under all that dirt."

He turned to Tomas's parents. "Leave him. Come back in four years. If
he's still alive, he won't be a farmer anymore."

The words hung in the air, cold and final. Tomas's mother held back a
sob as she hugged him, a hug that felt like a farewell. Then, his
parents were gone, leaving Tomas alone with the man known as "The
Grinder."

Silas stared at him, his expression unreadable. "Your training starts
now. Your *\[Endurance\]* skill is high because your body is used to
long, repetitive labor. We will build on that. The army values those who
don't die easily."

He led Tomas not to a training yard, but to a steep, rocky hill behind
his hovel. At the bottom was a large, empty barrel. At the top was a
small, gushing spring.

"Your first task," Silas said, his voice flat. "Fill the barrel. Use
your hands."

Tomas looked at the distance, the steep incline, and the pitiful amount
of water he could carry in his cupped hands. It was an impossible task.
"But... that will take days. I'll..."

"Yes," Silas interrupted. "It will. And you will do it without sleep.
Every time you collapse, I will wake you. Every time you stop, you will
start again. Your life is no longer about the rhythm of the seasons. It
is about the notifications in your head. Now, begin."

As Tomas took his first reluctant steps up the hill, a new screen
blinked into his vision.

**\[Quest Initiated: The First Lesson - A Test of Will\]**
**Objective:** Fill the barrel with water from the spring. **Reward:**
?? **Failure Condition:** Giving up. **Note:** Your new life begins now.
Welcome to the grind.

The sun beat down on him. The rocks cut into his bare feet. His hands,
slick with water and dirt, could barely hold a few mouthfuls at a time.
Hours bled into one another. His stamina bar, once a source of quiet
pride, drained to empty, refilled with agonizing slowness, and drained
again. He fell, exhausted, more times than he could count. Each time,
Silas was there, prodding him awake with the butt of a spear.

He was no longer Tomas, the boy who loved the smell of rain on dry
earth. He was a machine of agony, a vessel of endless, pointless motion.
Sometime during the second night, as he collapsed near the top of the
hill, his body screaming in protest, a new sound chimed in his mind,
sharp and clear.

\[Repetitive, strenuous action under extreme fatigue has created a new
skill!\]

**\[Skill Gained: Grit Lv. 1\]** **Effect:** Passively increases all
attribute generation through physical training by 1%. You can now push
your body 1% beyond its previous physical limits.

Through the haze of pain and exhaustion, Tomas saw his uncle watching
him from the bottom of the hill. There was no praise in his eyes, no
sympathy. There was only the cold, hard glint of a blacksmith looking at
a piece of raw iron glowing in the forge, ready to be hammered into
shape. The process had begun.

The chime of the quest completion was the only pleasant thing Tomas had
experienced in what felt like an eternity. He lay in a heap on the damp
earth, the half-full barrel looming over him like a tombstone. His body
was a symphony of agony.

**\[Quest Complete: The First Lesson - A Test of Will\]** **Reward:** +1
Stat Point, Title: The Tenacious. **\[New Title Equipped: The
Tenacious\]** **Effect:** +5% to all experience gains for
Stamina-related skills.

He tried to appreciate the small victory, to feel the warmth of the
notification, but his exhaustion was a cold, heavy blanket smothering
everything else. He didn't have the energy to even assign the stat
point.

"Boy."

Tomas flinched at the sound. He didn't even have a name of his own
anymore. He pushed himself up, his arms trembling violently, and faced
his uncle.

"Let me tell you something about the army." Silas looked off into the
distance, his scarred face unreadable, his eyes seeing something far
beyond the rocky hills. "It sucks. And the fewer skills you have, the
more it sucks."

Silas's gaze snapped back to Tomas, a cold, analytical light in his
eyes. He looked the boy up and down, taking in the mud-caked clothes,
the raw, bleeding hands, the sheer depletion. "They love fresh meat like
you. Strong. Lacking ambition. Hearty. You'll be on the front line,
without a doubt."

He took a step closer, his shadow falling over Tomas. "Is that what you
want?"

The question cut through the fog of pain. Tomas thought of his mother's
smile, the smell of the fields after a rain, the solid weight of an axe
in his hands as he split wood with his father. He thought of home. The
life he was born for. The life they were trying to take from him.

A raw, hoarse sound escaped his throat. "No."

Silas raised an eyebrow. "No? Then what do you want, boy?"

"I want to go home," Tomas whispered, the words cracking with emotion
and exhaustion. "I... I just want to be a farmer."

For a moment, the world was silent save for the wind whistling through
the rocks. Silas's harsh expression didn't soften, but a flicker of
something---not pity, but perhaps understanding---passed through his
eyes.

"Good," Silas said, his voice a low growl. "That's the best answer you
could have given. Ambition makes you a target. A desire for glory gets
you killed. But wanting to go home? Wanting to see your farm again?
That's a reason to live when you're bleeding out in some gods-forsaken
ditch. It's an anchor in the storm."

As he spoke, a new notification shimmered in Tomas's vision.

**\[Motivation Solidified! New Trait Gained!\]** **\[Trait: Farmer's
Resolve\]** **Effect:** When your Health or Stamina drops below 25%,
your will to return home grants you a temporary +10% bonus to all
regeneration rates and a minor resistance to pain effects.

Silas pointed a thick finger at Tomas. "But the army doesn't care what
you want. They care about what you can do. Your endurance is a start. It
means you die slower. But it won't stop a spear from finding your gut."

He turned and strode toward a flat, open patch of ground littered with
stones of all sizes. "Get up. Your next lesson begins."

Groaning, Tomas forced his protesting limbs to obey. He stumbled after
his uncle, his new title and trait feeling like abstract concepts
compared to the overwhelming reality of his aching body.

Silas stopped in the middle of the field and turned. "The front line is
a storm of steel and arrows, of spells and shattered earth. You will
learn to feel the wind before the rain. You will learn to move before
you think."

A new quest window blinked open.

**\[Quest Initiated: Dance of the Stones\]** **Objective:** Survive for
10 minutes. Do not get hit. **Bonus Objective:** ?? **Reward:** New
Skill: \[Dodge\]. **Failure Condition:** Getting knocked unconscious.

"What do I do?" Tomas asked, his voice trembling.

Silas bent down, his hand closing around a rock the size of his fist.
"You dance."

He didn't throw it. He *flicked* it. The stone shot through the air with
terrifying speed. There was no time to react, no time to even process
the threat. It slammed into Tomas's shoulder, sending a shockwave of
pure agony through his system.

\[-5 HP\]

Tomas cried out and staggered back, clutching the fresh, throbbing
bruise.

"Too slow," Silas grunted, already picking up another rock. "On your
feet. The next one is already in the air. Dance, boy. Or die."

And so Tomas danced. It was a clumsy, desperate dance at first, a series
of panicked flinches and painful impacts. But slowly, agonizingly, it
changed. The dance became his life. He danced while mucking out the
small pen for Silas's goat. He danced while fetching water---no longer
with his hands, but with a bucket that felt heavier than a boulder. He
learned to feel the subtle shift in the air, the tightening of his
uncle's shoulders, the faint whistle of a stone cutting through the
wind. He could be attacked at any moment, and his body began to react
without his mind's permission.

A week after the lesson began, he was tending the fire when Silas
appeared at the edge of his vision. Tomas didn't look. He felt the
movement, a familiar predatory stillness, and as the stone was thrown,
he simply swayed his torso back, a smooth, economical motion. The rock
zipped past his nose, close enough for him to feel the displacement of
air.

He held his position, muscles coiled, waiting for the next one. And the
next. For ten solid minutes, stones flew from every direction, and Tomas
moved like a reed in the wind, bending and swaying, but never breaking
his rhythm. Then, as suddenly as it began, it stopped. The silence was
deafening.

A cascade of notifications filled his vision, bright and beautiful.

**\[Quest Complete: Dance of the Stones\]** **Reward:** New Skill:
\[Dodge Lv. 3\]

**\[Bonus Objective Complete: Endure a full week of continuous threat
assessment.\]** **Bonus Reward:** +1 Perception, New Skill: \[Danger
Sense Lv. 1\]

**\[Danger Sense Lv. 1 (Passive)\]** **Effect:** You have a primal
awareness of immediate threats. A faint pressure or instinct will alert
you to incoming attacks a split second before they occur.

Tomas stared, mesmerized, at the glowing blue text. The world around him
faded into a blur. A week of bruises, of constant fear, of sleeping with
one eye open, had forged something new inside him. He wasn't just
reacting anymore; he was *anticipating*. The *\[Danger Sense\]* skill
gave a name to the knot in his stomach that had been his constant
companion, turning it from a source of anxiety into a tool.

"Took you a damn week to go 10 minutes without being hit." Silas's voice
was a gravelly intrusion, shattering the moment. "What are you staring
at, boy!"

Tomas blinked, the UI fading slightly as he focused on his uncle.
"Nothing. I was just..." He trailed off. How could he explain the
glorious text that promised a slightly better chance of survival?

"You were staring at empty space with your mouth hanging open," Silas
corrected him harshly. "The fight isn't over when the arrows stop. It's
over when you're sure your enemy is dead. Complacency is a luxury for
kings and fools. You are neither."

Before Tomas could respond, Silas disappeared into his hovel. He
returned a moment later and threw a heavy, crude object at Tomas's feet.
It landed with a solid thud. It was a round, wooden shield, banded with
rusty iron and scarred with old cuts. It looked impossibly heavy.

"Your next lesson," Silas announced. "Dodging is for rogues and cowards.
It keeps you alive, but it doesn't win the field. An army fights in a
line. It holds. And a man in a shield wall who dodges is a man who just
got his neighbor killed."

He gestured to the shield. "Pick it up."

Tomas bent and strained, his muscles protesting as he lifted the shield.
It was rough, splintery, and weighed a ton. His arm, already sore from a
week of abuse, trembled under the load.

\[New Skill Detected: Basic Shield Use Lv. 1\]

Silas nodded, a grim look of satisfaction on his face. "Now, stand
there. Don't move your feet. Don't dodge. The next rock that comes, you
will not avoid it. You will meet it."

A new quest window shimmered into existence, its text radiating a sense
of profound dread.

**\[Quest Initiated: The Unmovable Post\]** **Objective:** Block 100
incoming projectiles with the shield. **Restriction:** You may not use
the \[Dodge\] skill. Your feet must remain planted. **Reward:** New
Skill: \[Block\]. **Failure Condition:** Dropping the shield.

Silas was already gathering more stones, his movements efficient and
merciless. "The shield is not a piece of wood, boy. It is an extension
of your will. It is a promise to the men beside you that you will not
break. Now, show me your will."

And show him he did. Tomas "willed" a lot of things, none of which he
would mention to Uncle Silas. Willing his Uncle to disappear didn't seem
like a wise thought to voice aloud.

The first rock hit the shield like a smith's hammer striking an anvil.
The impact was a brutal, physical thing that shot up his arm, rattled
his teeth, and sent a jolt of pure agony through his shoulder.

**\[-1 HP\]** **\[Stamina -3\]** **\[Your shield arm has suffered a
jarring impact.\]**

He grunted, his feet digging into the dirt as he fought to stay upright.
His will was a frantic, desperate thing. *I will not drop this shield. I
will not fall. I will... wish that rock had hit Uncle Silas instead.*
The treasonous thought was a small, warm spark in the cold sea of his
misery.

"Straighten your back!" Silas roared. "Absorb with your legs, not your
arm! You're a post, not a hinge!"

Another rock slammed into the wood. **THUD.**

**\[-1 HP\]** **\[Stamina -3\]**

Another. **THWACK.**

**\[Your \[Basic Shield Use\] has leveled up to Lv. 2!\]** **\[You now
have a better understanding of how to brace for impact.\]**

The notification was a drop of water in a desert. A tiny change
occurred. The next time a rock hit, he instinctively bent his knees
slightly, grounding himself. The shock was still jarring, but less
severe.

By the 17th rock, his arm was a numb, trembling limb. He was operating
on muscle memory and sheer, stubborn refusal to fail.

By the 50th rock, splinters had worked their way into his forearm. His
stamina bar was a flickering red line, and his health had been chipped
away to just over half. The world had shrunk to the space between his
eyes and his uncle's throwing arm. With every impact, he willed it.
*Hold on. Just hold on. I want to go home. I want to feel the soft dirt,
not this cursed stone.*

Around the 80th projectile, his grip finally failed. The shield dipped,
his fingers spasming open. The next rock was already in the air, aimed
squarely at his chest.

**\[Trait Activated: Farmer's Resolve!\]** **\[Health and Stamina
regeneration temporarily increased!\]**

The thought of failure, of being sent to the army as a defenseless
grunt, of never seeing his parents again, flooded his mind. A surge of
desperate energy, warm and potent, flowed into his arm. He roared, a
sound of pure frustration and pain, and forced his fingers to close
around the splintered handle, jerking the shield up just in time. The
stone smacked into the iron banding with a deafening clang.

**\[Through extreme adversity, your will has been tempered!\]**
**\[Basic Shield Use has evolved into a new skill: Block!\]**

**\[Block Lv. 1\]** **Effect:** Allows you to negate or reduce incoming
physical damage by intercepting it with a shield. Effectiveness is based
on your Strength and the quality of the shield.

The final twenty rocks were different. He was no longer just enduring.
He was *blocking*. He learned to angle the shield, to meet the stone's
force and deflect it, turning a bone-shattering impact into a glancing
blow. The notifications for HP loss stopped. The stamina drain lessened.
He felt the subtle shift as he imposed his will not just on his own
body, but on the objects hurtling toward him.

The 100th rock skipped off the top of his shield and spun harmlessly
into the dirt. Silence descended once more. Tomas stood, swaying on his
feet, his arm locked in place, the shield feeling like it had been fused
to his bone.

**\[Quest Complete: The Unmovable Post\]** **Reward:** New Skill:
\[Block\]. (Already Awarded)

He finally allowed his arm to drop. The shield hit the ground with a
heavy thud. He could barely feel his fingers.

Silas walked over, his expression as hard as ever. He kicked the shield
with his boot, inspecting the fresh dents in the wood.

"You bled. You cried a little," Silas stated, pointing to a small, clean
track on Tomas's dusty cheek. "But you didn't break. Good."

It was the closest thing to praise Tomas had received. It felt better
than any skill notification.

"You can hold it," Silas continued, his brief moment of approval
vanishing as quickly as it came. "Now you learn to hit back."

"Knowing when to block, and knowing when to strike is the difference
between living and dying, boy." Silas circled Tomas slowly, holding a
long, smooth stick and twirling it nonchalantly in his fingers. "It's
all about rhythm. Understanding the flow of battle."

Tomas held his shield on his left arm, and it felt like a friend by now.
The weight was familiar, the balance an extension of his own body. But
the crude wooden sword in his right hand felt like a foreign object. It
was heavy, poorly balanced, and clumsy. He was a post, a wall. He wasn't
a hammer.

One he was destined to learn, according to his next Quest.

**\[Quest Initiated: The Rhythm of Battle\]** **Objective:**
Successfully block an attack and land a counter-attack 10 times.
**Reward:** New Skill: \[Basic Swordplay\], New Skill:
\[Counter-Strike\]. **Failure Condition:** Dropping your sword or
shield. **Note:** A good defense is an opportunity for a great offense.

"The pause after a block," Silas continued, his voice hypnotic as he
moved. "That's where you find your opening. A breath. A heartbeat.
That's your window. If you're too slow, the window closes. If you're too
fast, you overextend and die. You must feel the rhythm."

Without warning, he lunged. The stick was a blur, whistling through the
air as it aimed for Tomas's head.

Instinct, honed by a week of pain, took over. The shield came up.
**CLACK!** The sharp sound of wood on wood echoed in the clearing.
Tomas's *\[Block\]* skill flared in his mind, and he held his ground,
absorbing the impact through his legs just as he'd been taught.

For a split second, he felt pride. He had blocked it perfectly.

That second was one too long. Silas had already recoiled and the stick
smacked hard against Tomas's exposed ribs.

\[-3 HP\]

Tomas wheezed and stumbled back, the wooden sword in his hand feeling
useless.

"You see?" Silas said, not a hint of exertion in his voice. "You
blocked. You stopped. You thought. The rhythm ended. You died. Again."

He reset his position. "This time, don't stop. The block is not the end
of the movement. It is the beginning of the next one."

He attacked again, a low swing aimed at Tomas's legs. **THWACK!** Tomas
lowered his shield and blocked it. This time, he didn't pause. He swung
his wooden sword in a clumsy, desperate arc. It was slow and
telegraphed. Silas simply took a small step back, and the sword sliced
through empty air, throwing Tomas off balance.

A quick rap from Silas's stick on the back of his head was his reward.

"Pathetic," Silas spat. "Are you trying to chop wood or fight for your
life? Feel the flow!"

They went on like that for what felt like hours. Block. Clumsy swing.
Pain. Block. Wild swipe. Pain. Tomas's HP bar dipped lower and lower.
His frustration mounted, a hot, useless thing in his chest. The wooden
sword was a club, an anchor, anything but a weapon.

Then, something shifted. He stopped thinking about his home. He stopped
thinking about the army. He stopped thinking about the quest
notifications. He focused only on the stick in his uncle's hand and the
rhythm of his movements.

In, out. Attack, retreat. Block...

Silas lunged with a quick jab to the chest. **CLACK!** Tomas met it with
his shield. But this time, as the stick rebounded, Tomas's own arm was
already moving. He didn't swing for the fences. He didn't try to
overpower his uncle. He just pushed the wooden sword forward in a
straight, simple line, using the momentum from his block.

It connected. A dull *thud* as the tip of his wooden sword poked
squarely into Silas's sternum. It wasn't powerful. It wasn't elegant.
But it landed.

A beautiful sound chimed in his mind.

**\[Successful Counter-Attack: 1/10\]** **\[New Skill Created: Basic
Swordplay Lv. 1\]**

Silas took a half-step back, a flicker of surprise in his cold eyes. He
rubbed his chest. "There. You felt it. Once. Do it again."

And the dance resumed. But now, Tomas had a single note of the song.
Block. Strike. *Thwack-Thud.* It was a brutal, punishing lesson, but he
had found the rhythm. *Thwack-Thud.* His movements became more
efficient, less desperate. He learned that a short, quick jab was better
than a wild, powerful swing. *Thwack-Thud.*

Finally, after a dozen more bruises and a near-empty stamina bar, he
blocked a final, sweeping strike and landed the tenth counter-attack on
his uncle's thigh.

**\[Quest Complete: The Rhythm of Battle\]** **Reward:** \[Basic
Swordplay\] (Already Awarded), New Skill: \[Counter-Strike\].

**\[Counter-Strike Lv. 1\]** **Effect:** After a successful block, your
next immediate attack deals 5% additional damage and has a slightly
increased chance to hit.

Tomas stood panting, sweat and tears stinging his eyes. He had done it.
He hadn't just survived the attack; he had answered it.

Silas lowered his stick. He looked at Tomas, then at the crude wooden
sword in the boy's hand, and gave a single, sharp nod. "The rhythm can
be learned. Good. Because tomorrow, the rhythm gets faster."

About a week later, Tomas was inspecting his screen. The bruises had
faded to a dull yellow, his muscles were no longer screaming but hummed
with a constant, thrumming ache that felt almost like strength. He
allowed himself a small moment of pride as he reviewed his progress.

**Name:** Tomas **Level:** 4 **Class:** Farm Boy **Title:** The
Tenacious

**Attributes:** \* **Strength:** 8 \* **Agility:** 7 \* **Stamina:** 14
\* **Intelligence:** 7 \* **Wisdom:** 8 \* **Perception:** 2 \* **Stat
Points Available:** 1

**Skills:** \* \[Basic Husbandry Lv. 5\] \* \[Tilling Lv. 4\] \* \[Rock
Picking Lv. 8\] \* \[Endurance (Passive) Lv. 11\] \* \[Grit Lv. 2\] \*
\[Dodge Lv. 4\] \* \[Danger Sense (Passive) Lv. 2\] \* \[Block Lv. 5\]
\* \[Basic Swordplay Lv. 4\] \* \[Counter-Strike Lv. 2\]

**Traits:** \* \[Farmer's Resolve\]

The numbers were a testament to the pain. Each point was a scar, each
level a memory of exhaustion. The boy who had arrived a few weeks ago
couldn't have blocked a single one of Silas's strikes. Now, he could
almost keep up. A thin, genuine smile touched his lips.

"Boy, wipe that smile off of your face."

The voice cut through his reverie like a cold knife. Tomas straightened
up, his smile vanishing instantly as Silas approached.

"All you have accomplished so far is that you probably won't die in the
first five minutes of your first battle." Silas stopped in front of him,
his shadow a familiar weight. "Let's see if we can increase that to ten
minutes, yes?"

Tomas's heart sank. He knew what that tone meant. A new, more painful
lesson was coming.

Silas led him to the training ground and, with the heel of his boot,
scraped a wide circle into the dirt, perhaps ten feet across. "You can
block an attack from the front. A stationary target is easy. But a
battle is not a straight line. It is a swirling chaos of death coming
from all directions."

He gestured to the circle. "This is your world now. You do not leave it.
I will come at you from all sides. You will turn, you will block, you
will strike. You will control this space. If you step out of the circle,
you have been outmaneuvered. You have surrendered the field. You have
died."

The dreaded blue screen appeared, its text stark and unforgiving.

**\[Quest Initiated: The Wolf's Circle\]** **Objective:** Survive for 5
minutes within the circle. **Bonus Objective:** Land 5 successful
counter-attacks. **Restriction:** Do not step outside the marked circle.
**Reward:** New Skill: \[Shield Bash\]. **Failure Condition:** Stepping
out of the circle or being knocked down.

Tomas gripped his shield and wooden sword, his knuckles white. He
planted his feet in the center of the circle, his eyes locked on Silas.

"The rhythm is no longer a simple *thwack-thud*," Silas said, beginning
to circle him like a predator sizing up its prey. "Now, the dance has
footwork."

He darted in, not from the front, but from the side. Tomas pivoted, his
feet clumsy, and barely got his shield up in time to block the blow. The
force of it almost made him stumble back. He instinctively took a step
to brace himself---a step that would have taken him right out of the
circle. He caught himself at the last second, his back foot hovering
over the line.

While he was off balance, Silas was already moving, a blur of motion. A
sharp rap from the stick struck the back of Tomas's knee.

**\[-2 HP\]** **\[You have been struck in a vulnerable joint. Agility
temporarily reduced.\]**

His leg buckled. He cried out, catching himself with his sword hand
before he collapsed completely. He was on one knee, exposed and
vulnerable.

Silas stood over him, stick resting on his shoulder. "On the
battlefield, you are now dead. Your focus was on the line behind you,
not the enemy in front of you. Get up. We go again. The dance doesn't
stop just because you're tired."

A few weeks later, something finally "clicked". The Wolf's Circle was no
longer a prison but a stage. Tomas moved within it with a grim fluidity,
his feet no longer clumsy but rooted and precise. The shield and sword
were extensions of his own will, two parts of a single, defensive whole.
The dance was still brutal, but he was learning the steps.

He was in the middle of a particularly intense exchange, the sound of
wood on wood a constant, percussive beat. He ducked under a wide swing,
blocked a follow-up jab, and felt a familiar chime in his mind.

\[Your constant analysis of your opponent's movements has increased your
Perception by 1!\]

For the briefest of moments, his focus shifted to the welcome blue text.
It was a fatal hesitation. Silas, ever the opportunist, saw the opening.
His stick blurred, reversing its path in a vicious, decapitating sweep
aimed at Tomas's neck. There was no time to block, no space to dodge.

And then, it happened. Tomas's body moved without his command. He didn't
lift his heavy shield. Instead, the wooden sword in his right hand rose
in a fluid arc, not to meet the blow head-on, but to intercept it at an
angle.

ZING.

The sound was completely different from the dull *thuds* and *clacks* of

other hardened veterans Silas had grudgingly brought in completed the
circle.

This "optional" exercise had become the cornerstone of the Aegis Hold's
routine. To miss this opportunity was anathema to them. The benefits
were palpable enough that to miss one session felt like missing
breakfast... and lunch... and sometimes dinner.

Silas watched from the workshop door, a whetstone and a dagger forgotten
in his hands. He had taught men to fight by breaking them. By pushing
them until their bodies screamed and their minds went numb, forging
endurance through pure, brute-force agony.

Tomas was teaching them to build from within.

"It is not in your arms or your legs," Tomas's voice continued, calm and
instructive. "It is the wellspring of your stamina. The anchor of your
will. The root that connects you to the earth. Feel it, in your gut,
just below your navel. That is your core. That is your center."

One of the younger recruits, the skinny boy Tomas had first corrected,
suddenly gasped. A faint blue screen, visible only to him, flickered
into life.

**\[Through focused meditation, you have temporarily improved your
mind-body connection!\]** **\[Stamina regeneration rate increased by 5%
for the next hour.\]**

A murmur of understanding passed through the other recruits as they felt
similar, subtle shifts within themselves. This was the "palpable
benefit." It wasn't just a feeling of calm; it was a quantifiable,
game-changing buff that made the grueling physical training that
followed more effective and less draining.

Even Gregor, the cynic, closed his eyes and focused, his brow furrowed
in concentration. He'd complained loudly the first time Tomas had
suggested this "navel-gazing nonsense." Then, after one session, he
found he could last an extra ten minutes in a full-speed spar before his
lungs started to burn. He hadn't complained since.

"The body is a fortress," Tomas said, his own eyes closed. "And a
fortress with an unguarded center is just a wall waiting to crumble.
Know your center. Strengthen it. And you will never be truly broken."

For Tomas, the notifications were different.

**\[Your \[Instruction\] skill has leveled up to Lv. 3!\]** **\[Through
repeated practice and teaching, you have developed a new specialization
for the \[Instruction\] skill!\]** **\[Specialization Gained: Centered
Will\]** **Effect:** Those who follow your meditative guidance gain
temporary bonuses to Stamina and Willpower-based stats.

Silas finally shook his head and went back to his dagger. It was
hopeless. He couldn't even begin to comprehend what he was seeing. He
had created a grinder to sharpen swords. Tomas had taken that grinder,
dismantled it, and was now using the parts to build a watch, a complex,
intricate machine of interlocking parts and profound purpose.

He had no idea how it worked. He just knew that his men were becoming
stronger, faster, and more resilient than any soldiers he had ever seen.
And it was all because of the quiet, focused "nonsense" happening in the
yard.

"Uncle, my time draws near."

The words, spoken softly, carried more weight than any war hammer in the
forge. It was a cool evening, and the sounds of Camp Aegis were winding
down. The rhythmic *thump* of a practice dummy being struck, the low
murmur of men cleaning their gear, the scent of roasting meat from the
mess hall---it was a symphony of readiness that they had built together.

Tomas, a month shy of his sixteenth birthday, stood on the porch of the
original hovel. He was a giant, his frame filled out with the dense,
powerful muscle of a master warrior and a tireless laborer. He looked
around Camp Aegis, his creation, and a deep, paternal pride was etched
on his face.

"There is much for them still to learn," he said, his gaze settling on
the newest batch of recruits drilling in the twilight. "We will not send
our young men to war unprepared."

He turned to Silas, who was quietly observing from his customary spot.
The years had been kind to the old warrior. The good food, the sense of
purpose, had smoothed some of the harder lines on his face.

"Come," Tomas said, his voice gentle but firm. "Sit with me. I have some
things to show you. Things you didn't know you knew. Depths you didn't
know you had. Prepare yourself."

A chill, completely unrelated to the evening air, ran down Silas's
spine. He, The Grinder, being told to "prepare himself" by the boy he'd
once tried to break? Yet, he had learned long ago that when Tomas spoke
with that tone, the world was about to change. He nodded grimly and
followed Tomas to the center of the yard, the place of meditation.

They sat facing each other, the camp quieting around them as the men
sensed the gravity of the moment.

"Close your eyes, Uncle," Tomas instructed. "Breathe. Listen not with
your ears, but with your core. I have spent years learning the rhythm of
others. Now, I will show you your own."

Silas closed his eyes, feeling the familiar skepticism war with the
hard-earned trust he had in his nephew. He followed the breathing
pattern, the one he'd seen Tomas teach dozens of times. He reached
inward, searching for the "center" he'd always dismissed as metaphysical
nonsense.

And then, Tomas's voice seemed to echo not in his ears, but inside his
own mind. "You have spent a lifetime building walls around your soul,
Uncle. You closed the door on the man you were. But the foundation is
still there. Look."

The darkness behind Silas's eyelids shifted. A faint, blue light began
to bleed at the edges of his perception. It was the light he had seen in
Tomas's eyes a thousand times but never understood. It grew brighter,
sharper, coalescing into structured text. For the first time in his
life, Silas the Grinder was seeing his own interface.

**\[You have been guided into a state of profound introspection.\]**
**\[Your perception has been opened to a new layer of reality.\]**

**Name:** Silas **Level:** ?? **Class:** Legionnaire (Veteran)
**Title:** The Grinder

**Attributes:** \* **Strength:** 68 \* **Agility:** 55 \* **Stamina:**
72 \* ...

**Skills:** \* \[Spear Mastery Lv. 32\] \* \[Unarmed Combat Lv. 28\] \*
\[Intimidation (Passive) Lv. 18\] \* \[Battle Tactics Lv. 25
(Atrophied)\] \* \[Leadership Lv. 15 (Dormant)\]

The breath hitched in Silas's throat. He saw it. The blueprint of his
life. The power he possessed, the skills he had honed, and more
shockingly, the skills he had abandoned. *Atrophied*. *Dormant*. The
words were a judgment, a testament to the decades he had spent hiding
from his own potential.

While Silas was reeling, a notification of immense significance appeared
before Tomas.

**\[Your \[Aegis Mentorship\] skill has been used to its absolute
limit.\]** **\[You have successfully guided a master to rediscover their
own dormant power.\]** **\[Your understanding of the fundamental nature
of growth and potential has reached a new pinnacle!\]** **\[TITLE
GAINED: The Master Builder\]**

Silas opened his eyes, a storm of emotion swirling within them: shock,
regret, and a terrifying, long-forgotten spark of ambition. He looked at
Tomas, who was watching him with a calm, steady gaze.

"They are not just skills, Uncle," Tomas said softly, knowing exactly
what Silas had seen. "They are parts of you. You are more than a
grinder. You were a leader. You still are."

Silas stared at his own hands, seeing them not as the tools of a lonely
exile, but as the instruments of a Legionnaire Captain. The army wasn't
a looming threat to be survived. It was a broken institution he had once
belonged to, a world he understood.

A slow, dangerous smile spread across Silas's face.

"My time draws near..." he repeated his nephew's words, his voice a low,
rumbling growl. "You're right, Tomas. But they won't be coming to draft
our boys."

He looked around at the camp, at the fiercely loyal men, at the fortress
they had built, and at the Master Builder who stood at its heart.

"They'll be coming to beg for them."

A week before his sixteenth birthday, his "coming of age," Tomas stood
before the assembled men of Camp Aegis. The sun was setting, casting
long, dramatic shadows across the training yard. There were no boys
here. The thirty-odd men who stood in a perfect, disciplined formation
were a mix of hardened veterans and young warriors forged in the camp's
unique crucible. They were lean, strong, and moved with a quiet
confidence that was more potent than any shout.

"Gentlemen," Tomas's voice boomed across the yard, rich with the
authority he had earned through sweat and steel. "It has been a pleasure
to work with you. To train with you. To toil with you. To GROW with
you!"

He walked up and down the line, his eyes meeting each man's gaze. He saw
not recruits, but brothers-in-arms. He saw Gregor's cynical smirk, which
was now more of a genuine grin. He saw Valerius's stoic nod of respect.
He saw the fierce loyalty burning in the eyes of the young men they had
saved from becoming cannon fodder.

"My first deployment is for two years," Tomas continued, his tone
shifting from camaraderie to command. "By law, I must answer the call.
And I will. I am eager to share with you what I learn out there, in the
heart of the Royal Army. But I am more eager..."

Tomas paused, turning to wave a hand that encompassed the entire
compound---the sturdy barracks, the humming forge, the meticulously
organized workshop, the fertile fields. "...to see what YOU can make of
our HOME!"

A murmur of pride rippled through the ranks. It was their home. Not a
temporary training camp, but a place they had built with their own
hands.

"Silas is the Master of this Hold," Tomas declared, his voice ringing
with finality. Silas, standing to the side, straightened his back, the
dormant *\[Leadership\]* skill within him flaring to life. "His word is
law. Gregor, you are his second. Valerius, you are Master of the Forge.
Finn, you will continue to expand our resources."

He was not abandoning them. He was empowering them. He was giving the
fortress a purpose beyond his own survival.

A final, magnificent notification, bordered in shimmering silver, filled
Tomas's vision.

**\[The Legendary Chain-Quest \[The Aegis Hold\] has reached a new
milestone!\]** **\[Stage 1: The Groundwork\] has been completed!\]**
**\[Your vision has been realized and a self-sustaining community has
been forged.\]**

**\[Stage 2: The Living Fortress\]** **Objective:** While you serve your
term, ensure the Aegis Hold not only survives, but thrives. **Tasks:**
\* Successfully train and graduate two new squads of recruits. \*
Establish a formal trade agreement with a local village. \* Research and
develop a unique combat technique or piece of equipment. **Reward:**
Title: Lord of the Aegis Hold, Unlocks Faction Management options.

Tomas smiled, a genuine, brilliant smile. His personal quest for
survival was over. He had not just avoided his fate; he had rewritten
it. He wasn't a farm boy being sent to the slaughter. He was the founder
of a new power in the land, a Master Builder on his way to inspect the
flawed foundations of the Royal Army itself.

He drew the first real sword he had ever forged, a simple, perfectly
balanced blade, and held it aloft. The setting sun glinted off the
polished steel.

"I go to serve the Kingdom," he called out, his voice a clarion call of
defiance and purpose. "But I will return to serve our home! Make me
proud!"

A single, unified roar erupted from thirty throats, a sound of loyalty
and unbreakable will that echoed through the hills. It was the sound of
a living fortress, its foundations solid, its future bright, and its
master ready for the next stage of the grind.
