The journey to the Royal Army's southern recruitment depot would have
taken two days by horseback. Yes, Tomas didn't own a horse. The thought
had never come up. Horses were for merchants and nobles, a luxury that
required more resources to maintain than they provided for a
self-sufficient hold.

So he walked.

Well, "walk" doesn't really describe his week of travel. For a normal
person, the seventy-league journey would be an arduous trek. For Tomas,
it was a mobile training ground. The road, the woods, the fields---they
were all learning opportunities. He didn't see a path to his
destination; he saw a week-long, self-directed training quest with
varied and challenging terrain.

He started on the main road at a steady, ground-eating jog, a pace he
could maintain for hours. His *\[Endurance\]* skill, the very foundation
of his being, ticked up slowly, but the sustained, rhythmic motion
unlocked something new.

**\[Continuous, paced exertion over long distances has created a new
skill!\]** **\[Skill Gained: Running Lv. 1\]** **Effect:** Increases
stamina efficiency while moving at a sustained pace faster than a walk.

When the road passed through dense forest, he would melt into the trees.
Here, the objective changed. He crept, his large frame moving with a
preternatural silence, his feet finding purchase on moss and soil
without snapping a twig. He focused on his breathing, on minimizing his
presence, on becoming just another shadow in the dappled light.

**\[You are actively attempting to move unobserved in a viable
environment.\]** **\[New Skill Created: Stealth Lv. 1\]** **Effect:**
Reduces the chance of being seen or heard. Effectiveness is based on
Agility, environment, and equipment.

When he emerged into wide-open fields, he would train speed. He ran. He
would pick a landmark---a distant copse of trees, a large boulder---and
sprint, pushing his body to its absolute limit in a controlled explosion
of power. His stamina bar would plummet, his lungs would burn, and his
*\[Farmer\'s Resolve\]* would kick in, pushing him through the pain. He
would then jog to recover, his regeneration rates humming, before
sprinting again.

**\[Your \[Running\] skill has leveled up to Lv. 5!\]** **\[Repetitive,
maximum-exertion bursts of speed have created a new skill!\]** **\[Skill
Gained: Sprint Lv. 1\]** **Effect:** Provides a short, intense burst of
speed at a high stamina cost.

Night was not for rest. It was for honing his *\[Perception\]* and
*\[Danger Sense\]*, for practicing *\[Stealth\]* in the deep shadows,
for meditating under the stars to find his center in an unfamiliar
environment. He ate from the rations he'd packed and occasionally
foraged, his old *\[Basic Husbandry\]* skills giving him an eye for
edible plants.

By the time the walled recruitment depot came into view on the seventh
day, Tomas was not weary. He was honed. He felt like a blade that had
been held to a whetstone for a week straight---sharper, keener, and
humming with latent energy.

He walked the final mile at a normal pace, letting his body cool, his
presence settle. He joined a line of other boys, all nervous, soft-faced
sixteen-year-olds who looked either terrified or filled with a foolish,
unearned bravado. They saw a giant of a young man, his face calm and his
eyes ancient, fall in line behind them. They smelled of sweat and fear.
He smelled of pine, earth, and discipline.

The army wasn't getting a farm boy. It wasn't even getting a trained
warrior. It was getting a living system of relentless self-improvement,
and it was about to be audited.

"Fall in!"

The voice was a physical force, a crack of thunder that rolled across
the dusty recruitment yard. Drill Sergeant Williams sounded like a bear
crossed with an ogre that ate gravel for breakfast. He was a
barrel-chested man with a face that looked like it had been used to stop
a rockslide.

As he walked up and down the line of terrified sixteen-year-olds, he
shook his head in disgust. "Pathetic," he snarled, his voice a low
growl. "I asked the capital for men, and they sent me a fresh crop of
milk-sops and fools. You're soft. You're weak. You will be broken, and
if you are very, very lucky, we will rebuild you into something that
doesn't die the first time it hears a war-horn."

His predatory gaze swept down the line, lingering for a moment on each
trembling face, each poorly-held posture. And then he stopped.

He stopped in front of Tomas.

The Drill Sergeant was a large man, but Tomas was taller by a head and
broader at the shoulder. Williams had to crane his neck slightly to meet
the boy's gaze. He saw no fear. He saw no bravado. He saw only a calm,
placid stillness that was more unnerving than any defiance.

"What have we here?" Williams's voice lost some of its general-purpose
roar, becoming a focused, dangerous rumble. He circled Tomas slowly, his
eyes narrowing as he took in the sheer physicality of the young man. The
powerful set of his shoulders, the calloused hands, the perfect, rooted
posture. This was not the soft flesh of a farm boy or the unearned bulk
of a rich merchant's son. This was dense, functional muscle, forged in a
fire hotter than any army drill.

He stopped in front of Tomas again. "What have you been eating, boy?
Whole cows?"

A few of the other recruits snickered nervously, then fell silent as
Williams shot them a look that could curdle milk.

Tomas's expression didn't change. His eyes, which had analyzed every
detail of the Sergeant's posture, the wear-patterns on his boots, and
the tension in his shoulders, finally met his gaze.

"Vegetables and lean protein, Drill Sergeant," Tomas replied, his voice
a calm, even baritone. "And a great deal of physical exertion."

The absolute sincerity and straightforwardness of the answer threw
Williams off for a fraction of a second. There was no "sir," no
stammering, just a simple statement of fact. This boy wasn't playing the
game. He was answering the question.

As he spoke, a notification only Tomas could see appeared.

**\[You are being assessed by a high-level individual.\]** **\[Name:
Roric Williams\]** **\[Level: 38\]** **\[Class: Drill Sergeant\]**
**\[Title: The Wall Breaker\]**

Williams leaned in close, his face inches from Tomas's. His voice
dropped to a menacing whisper. "Is that so? Well, we have plenty of
physical exertion here. Let's see if we can't wear some of that cow off
you." He raised his voice to a full-throated bellow. "Recruit! Give me
one hundred press-ups! Now!"

Without a word, Tomas dropped to the ground, his back perfectly
straight, and began. He didn't rush. He didn't strain. Each press-up was
a perfect, measured repetition, his form flawless.

The rest of the recruits stared in stunned silence. One hundred
press-ups was a punishment meant to break a man on the first day, to
establish the pecking order.

Williams watched, a grim smile on his face, expecting the boy to start
shaking around twenty, to collapse around forty.

At fifty, Tomas's rhythm was unchanged. At seventy, his breathing was
still deep and even. At one hundred, he pushed himself up one last time
and held the position, waiting for the next command.

The Drill Sergeant's smile faded. He had used a hammer, and he had
struck solid granite. This wasn't a boy to be broken. This was something
else entirely.

"On your feet, recruit," Williams said, his voice dangerously quiet.
"What's your name?"

"Tomas, Drill Sergeant."

"Well, Tomas," Williams said, a new, predatory light in his eyes.
"Welcome to the army. I do believe you and I are going to have a great
deal of fun."

Running the recruits through drills was not what Tomas expected to be
doing on his second day in the army.

After the press-up incident, Drill Sergeant Williams had personally
supervised every moment of Tomas's processing. He'd watched him clear
the initial strength and stamina tests with scores that broke the
depot's records, his expression growing grimmer with each new feat.

By the second day, a new, malicious plan had formed. Williams had
assembled the fifty trembling recruits in the pre-dawn chill.

"Listen up, you maggots!" he had roared. "Your squad leader for the day
is the big one. Recruit Tomas. His job is to make you run the basic
drills until you puke. My job," he'd added with a vicious grin, "is to
watch *him*. Every time one of you pathetic whelps messes up---drops a
shield, trips over your own feet, breathes too loud---your squad leader
here does twenty press-ups. Let's see how long his form stays perfect."

It was a cruel, brilliant strategy designed to isolate the strong, to
make the pack turn on its leader. But it backfired spectacularly.

Tomas simply accepted the command with a nod. "On the line!" he'd called
out, his voice calm but carrying an innate authority that made the
recruits jump. "We will begin with the foundational stance. Your feet
are your roots. If your roots are weak, the tree will fall."

He moved down the line, adjusting a shoulder here, a foot there, using
the exact same methods Silas had used on him. "No, your shield is not a
wall. It is a conduit..."

When a boy inevitably stumbled, Tomas would calmly say, "Hold your
positions. Correct your stance," and would drop to the dirt, flawlessly
executing twenty press-ups before rising, not even breathing heavily.

By mid-morning, he'd done over three hundred press-ups, yet the recruits
were the ones sweating and exhausted. But they weren't resentful. They
were in awe. They saw a leader who took their punishment without
complaint, whose own standards were impossibly high. His calm competence
was a rock in the chaotic sea of their first days. They found themselves
trying harder, not out of fear of Williams, but out of a desire not to
fail the quiet giant leading them. The quality of their drilling,
despite their fatigue, was improving at a visible rate.

Williams just glared from the sidelines, his arms crossed, a thunderous
expression on his face as he observed the process. The plan was failing.
The recruits weren't turning on Tomas; they were coalescing around him.

"Sergeant Williams!" barked a new voice, sharp and laced with
aristocratic disdain. "What the hell is going on here?"

A man in the pristine uniform of a Captain, his chest bare of battle
ribbons but gleaming with polished brass, strode onto the training yard.
He was followed by two guards who looked more like ceremonial
decorations than soldiers.

"Captain Valerius," Williams grunted, snapping to a stiff, resentful
attention.

"I asked you a question, Sergeant," the Captain said, sneering at the
sweaty, pathetic recruits. "Why is a recruit leading drills? And why are
you letting him run them into the ground? This is an assessment period,
not a punishment detail. We're looking for officer candidates, not
breaking pack mules."

His dismissive gaze fell on Tomas, who had paused the drill and now
stood silently at the head of the formation.

"And who is this one?" the Captain asked, a note of amusement in his
voice. "He seems a bit old for the new levy."

"Recruit Tomas, sir," Williams said through clenched teeth. "Sixteen
years old. Came in with this batch."

The Captain laughed, a short, barking sound. "Sixteen? Preposterous.
Sergeant, get this oaf back in line and run your drills properly. I'm
here to identify men with potential, not... farm animals with good
posture."

Before Sergeant Williams could even process the Captain's order, Tomas
spoke. His voice wasn't a shout, but it was soft and clear, seeming to
carry for miles across the dusty yard, cutting through the tension.

"Good. Now, it seems we have an audience." He didn't look at the
Captain. He addressed the recruits. "Men! Line up!"

There was a scramble, but it wasn't the clumsy shuffling of frightened
boys. It was the sharp, efficient movement of soldiers who knew their
leader. In seconds, a near-perfect double rank formed, facing Tomas.
Williams stood frozen, a silent observer to the mutiny of competence
unfolding before him.

Tomas's voice became a rhythmic cadence, the words a familiar drill.
"Shields up!" *SHUMP.* The sound of fifty wooden shields rising as one
was sharp and unified. "Swords, ready!" *SHINK.* Fifty wooden blades
cleared their imaginary scabbards. "Block... thrust!" The front rank
took a half-step, shields absorbing a phantom blow, and their swords
punched forward with a collective grunt. "Drop back one step, shield
low. Parry! Thrust!" The line flowed back, shields dipping to cover
their legs, then blades flickered out in a different sequence, a more
complex maneuver. "Again! Good! ... At ease!"

The recruits relaxed from their ready stance, their chests heaving but
their eyes locked on Tomas.

"Stow shield! Sheath swords! Right face!"

With a clatter of wood and a shuffle of boots, the entire formation
turned as one to face the officers.

"Salute the Captain!"

Fifty hands snapped up in a perfect, synchronized salute to the man who
had just insulted them. They stood there, a silent, disciplined fighting
force, awaiting his command.

Captain Valerius stood with his jaw slack. His face, once a mask of
arrogant disdain, was now a canvas of pure, unadulterated disbelief. He
had seen veteran legions with less cohesion. These were supposed to be
soft, untrained farm-hands on their second day.

He finally found his voice, the word escaping as a choked whisper.

"What the hell?"

Drill Sergeant Williams didn't move a muscle. His face was granite, but
for the first time that day, the corner of his mouth twitched into
something that might have been a smirk. He had just witnessed the
impossible.

Tomas, standing at ease, finally met the Captain's gaze. A subtle,
brilliant notification shimmered in his mind.

**\[You have demonstrated leadership under pressure and inspired a unit
to act as one.\]** **\[New Skill Created: Leadership Lv. 1\]**
**Effect:** Those who recognize you as a leader receive a minor bonus to
morale and discipline while under your command.

"Recruits! Fall out! Assume half-lotus position!"

The command was so bizarre, so utterly alien to any military doctrine,
that Captain Valerius stared at the Drill Sergeant in disbelief,
expecting him to intervene. But Williams remained silent, his expression
unreadable, watching the unfolding spectacle.

The fifty recruits moved without hesitation. They broke formation,
stowed their gear neatly, and settled onto the dusty ground, folding
their legs into the familiar meditative posture. The practiced ease of
the movement was another shock to the Captain's system.

Tomas moved to the center of the group, settling into a full lotus, his
back ramrod straight.

"Now, close your eyes," he commanded, his voice dropping to the calm,
hypnotic cadence of their morning sessions at Camp Aegis. "Search for
your center. Let everything else fall aside. Your pain. Your weariness.
Your thirst. Your hunger. THEY DO NOT EXIST!"

His voice rose slightly, not in a shout, but with an intensity that
vibrated through the air. "They are simply weakness leaving your body."

A strange, profound silence filled the training ground. The usual sounds
of a military depot---distant shouts, the clang of steel---seemed to
fade away, leaving only the sound of fifty men breathing in slow,
measured unison.

"Good!" Tomas's voice was a soothing balm. "Breathe in the air, breathe
out the pain. Breathe in nature, breathe out weariness. Good! Now let it
all go. Look within yourselves, AND SEE YOUR GAINS!"

As he spoke the final words, a wave of palpable energy seemed to wash
over the recruits. For the men in the circle, it was a familiar, welcome
warmth. Subtle blue screens flickered to life before their eyes, visible
only to them, each one a testament to their morning's hard work.

**\[Your strenuous drilling has increased your \[Endurance\]!\]**
**\[Your \[Block\] skill has gained experience!\]** **\[Your Stamina has
increased by 1!\]**

A collective sigh of satisfaction rose from the group. They weren't just
being told they were getting stronger; they were being shown irrefutable
proof. It was a dopamine hit of pure, quantifiable progress, the secret
ingredient to the Aegis Hold's rapid growth.

Captain Valerius saw none of this. He saw a group of raw recruits being
led in some sort of mystical ritual by a sixteen-year-old giant. He saw
his authority being completely and utterly subverted. He saw the grim,
silent approval on his most notoriously brutal Drill Sergeant's face.

He turned to Williams, his voice a furious hiss. "Sergeant, what is the
meaning of this insubordination? Arrest that man!"

Williams finally moved. He took a slow, deliberate step forward,
planting himself between the Captain and the meditating recruits. He did
not look at Tomas. He looked directly at his commanding officer.

"With all due respect, Captain," Williams's voice was a low, dangerous
rumble. "I have never seen recruits progress this fast in twenty years
of service. I am not arresting a damn thing."

His eyes narrowed. "I am *observing*."

"Recruits! Dismissed! PT at 5 A.M. Rest well. You have earned it!"

With a final, authoritative command, Tomas rose from his meditative
pose. The fifty recruits opened their eyes, a visible sense of
refreshment and renewed vigor on their faces. They scrambled to their
feet, organized their gear with practiced efficiency, and jogged off
towards the barracks, a low, excited chatter breaking out among them.
They moved with the purpose of a veteran unit, not a gaggle of first-day
conscripts.

The training yard was suddenly empty, save for the three men at its
center. The tension was a palpable thing.

Tomas turned and walked directly over to the simmering Captain and the
stoic Drill Sergeant. He stopped a respectful three paces away, his
large frame seeming to shrink as he adopted the perfect, humble posture
of a subordinate. He snapped a salute so sharp, so precise, that it made
the Captain's own feel sloppy by comparison.

"Recruit Tomas, at your service, Captain!" he said, his voice clear and
formal, devoid of any of the messianic calm from moments before. He was
no longer the instructor, the guru. He was a recruit reporting to his
officer.

The sudden shift in persona was jarring. It was also a masterful
tactical move, instantly reframing the situation. He had dismissed *his*
men, but now he was reporting for duty. It was an act of deference that
also subtly highlighted who had been in real command moments earlier.

Captain Valerius's face was a mottled shade of purple. He was caught
between fury at the insubordination and utter bewilderment at the
results. He had been publicly undermined, then saluted with impeccable
military courtesy. The Drill Sergeant at his side was in a state of
silent, borderline mutiny. He had no idea how to process the situation.

"You..." Valerius sputtered, pointing a trembling finger at Tomas. "You
have a lot of explaining to do, recruit. What was that? Some sort of
hedge-magic? A peasant ritual?"

"It is a meditative technique, sir," Tomas replied, his gaze fixed
respectfully on a point just over the Captain's shoulder. "Designed to
improve focus, aid in recovery, and allow a soldier to quantify their
own progress, thereby increasing morale and the efficiency of subsequent
training."

He delivered the lines as if reciting from a military textbook. As he
spoke, his *\[Analytical Mind\]* skill was working overtime, parsing the
Captain's anger, his polished but unused gear, and the subtle tells of a
man whose authority was derived from rank, not respect.

"Quantify progress?" Valerius scoffed, seizing on the unfamiliar term.
"Nonsense. Progress is measured in miles marched and dummies broken.
Sergeant," he snapped, turning to Williams, "confine this man to the
stockade for gross insubordination and---"

"I would advise against that, Captain," Williams cut in, his voice
dangerously low.

"Are you refusing a direct order, Sergeant?" Valerius hissed, his hand
instinctively dropping to the pommel of his ceremonial sword.

"I am offering tactical advice, sir," Williams corrected him smoothly, a
predatory gleam in his eyes. "In one morning, that 'recruit' has done
more to forge this batch of nobodies into a cohesive unit than I could
have in a month. He has demonstrated leadership, tactical instruction,
and a novel method for improving troop morale and performance that
actually seems to work. If you confine him, you are actively harming the
readiness of this company."

He paused, letting the weight of his words sink in. "Sir."

The "sir" was an insult, a mere formality tacked onto a statement of
undeniable fact. Valerius was trapped. To punish Tomas now, after his
own Drill Sergeant had publicly vouched for the method's effectiveness,
would make him look weak and foolish.

He stared at Tomas, then at Williams, his mind racing. He was a man who
understood power, and a fundamental shift in power had just occurred on
his training yard. He had come to find officer candidates. It seemed he
had stumbled upon something far more rare, and far more dangerous.

The tension in the yard was a drawn bowstring, ready to snap. Captain
Valerius's face was a storm of conflicting emotions, his authority
challenged, his options limited.

Then, Tomas spoke again, his voice calm and deferential, a sudden splash
of cool water on a grease fire.

"My apologies, Captain. I did not mean to overstep my boundaries. I am
here to follow orders."

He took a half-step forward, his posture remaining perfectly
subordinate. "I am not familiar with the stockade. Please direct me, and
I will report there as ordered."

The statement was a masterstroke of de-escalation. It was an apology
without admitting fault. It was an acceptance of punishment without a
shred of defiance. He wasn't resisting; he was *complying* with an order
that hadn't even been fully given, placing the final decision---and the
full weight of its consequences---squarely back on the Captain's
shoulders.

It completely disarmed Valerius. How could he punish a man who was not
only willing to accept the punishment but was politely asking for
directions to it? It made the act of punishment seem petty and absurd.

Williams's respect for the boy---the man---ratcheted up another notch.
Tomas hadn't just won the physical and tactical battle; he was now
effortlessly winning the political one.

Captain Valerius stared at Tomas, the fury draining from his face to be
replaced by a complex, calculating expression. He was an officer from a
noble house, a man trained in the subtle arts of courtly maneuvering. He
recognized the move for what it was: a brilliant checkmate.

"That..." Valerius finally said, forcing the anger from his voice.
"...will not be necessary. Your methods are... unorthodox, Recruit
Tomas. Highly unorthodox. But the results... the results are
intriguing."

He smoothed the front of his pristine tunic, regaining his composure. He
was shifting tactics. If you can't beat them, analyze them. And if they
are useful, *use* them.

"Sergeant Williams," he said, his voice now crisp and professional. "You
will grant Recruit Tomas provisional authority to continue his training
regimen with this unit. I want a full report on his methods and their
quantifiable results on my desk by the end of the week. I want to know
everything. The stances, the drills, the... meditation." He said the
word as if it tasted strange.

He turned his sharp, intelligent gaze back to Tomas. "As for you,
Recruit. You have my attention. Do not make me regret this. Carry on."

With a curt nod, Captain Valerius turned on his heel and strode from the
training yard, his two ceremonial guards scrambling to keep pace. He had
come to the depot looking for clay to mold into junior officers. He had
found a master potter who was already turning the clay into perfectly
formed vessels. And he was determined to find out how.

As the Captain departed, a new notification, shimmering with a faint
bronze light, appeared in Tomas's vision.

**\[You have successfully navigated a complex social and political
conflict.\]** **\[Your \[Leadership\] skill has proven effective in a
non-combat situation.\]** **\[New Skill Created: Diplomacy Lv. 1\]**
**Effect:** Increases the chance of achieving a favorable outcome in
social negotiations and conflicts.

"Recruits. Today is your third day!"

Tomas's voice boomed across the training yard at 5 A.M. sharp. The fifty
recruits stood in perfect formation, their posture crisp, their eyes
alert. The aches and pains of the first day were gone, replaced by the
thrumming energy of quantifiable progress and a shared, profound respect
for the man standing before them.

"There is a war going on out there!" Tomas's voice was no longer the
calm instructor or the deferential subordinate. It was the voice of a
commander, sharp and resonant with purpose. "There are creatures from
your childhood nightmares rampaging our lands! Orcs! Goblins! Ogres!
Trolls!"

He paced before them, his presence filling the yard. Each step was
deliberate, each gesture imbued with meaning.

"They will not care that you are a farmer's son. They will not care that
you miss your mother. They will see you as nothing more than meat for
their cook-fires and a momentary obstacle on their path of destruction!"

The recruits' faces were grim. The abstract threat of the army had been
replaced by a visceral, terrifying reality.

"So I ask you," Tomas roared, his voice reaching a crescendo as he
stopped and locked eyes with the men in the front rank. "Are you ready
to get serious!"

"SIR, YES, SIR!" The response was a single, explosive sound, a roar of
unified intent that sent a flock of birds scattering from the barracks
roof. It was a sound that had never been heard from third-day recruits
in the history of the depot.

Williams simply stood in the corner of the training grounds, arms
crossed over his massive chest, grinning like a madman.

This was it. This was the magic. It wasn't just the drills or the
strange meditation. It was the *why*. For twenty years, he had broken
boys down with fear and pain, then rebuilt them into soldiers who fought
because they were ordered to.

Tomas was building them from the foundation up with purpose. He gave
them the tools to succeed, showed them the proof of their own growth,
and then hammered home the terrible, glorious reason for their struggle.
He wasn't creating soldiers. He was forging defenders.

"Good!" Tomas's voice returned to a calm, instructional tone. "Because
today, we introduce a new rhythm. The rhythm of the shield wall. You
will learn to move not as fifty men, but as one. One shield. One
purpose. One unstoppable force. Valerius! Gregor! Finn!"

From the side, three figures emerged. The recruits gasped. They were the
legendary veterans who worked for Tomas's uncle, the men who had been
spotted around the camp but never approached. Valerius, built like an
ox, carried a massive tower shield and a heavy war hammer. Gregor, wiry
and scarred, held a pair of wickedly sharp short swords. Finn, silent
and hooded, had a longbow in his hand.

"These men will be your opponents," Tomas announced calmly. "They will
show you what a real attack looks like. Your job is not to beat them.
Your job is to *survive* them. As one. Now, form the wall!"

Williams's grin widened until it felt like it would split his face.
Live-fire exercises. With veteran mercenaries. On day three. The
Captain's report was going to be an absolute masterpiece. He was
witnessing a revolution, and he had a front-row seat.

One week later.

Captain Valerius sat in his impeccably neat office, the morning sun
glinting off the polished wood of his desk. In his hand, he held a
multi-page report, bound neatly with twine. His other hand was massaging
his temple, a gesture that had become increasingly common over the past
seven days.

He looked across the desk at the stone-faced Drill Sergeant standing at
attention.

"Are you serious?" the Captain asked, his voice a strained whisper of
disbelief. He tapped a paragraph in the report with a trembling finger.
"Do you expect me to believe this report? What sort of nonsense is
this?"

Williams's expression didn't flicker. "It is my official observation,
Captain. Every word is true."

"True?" Valerius's voice cracked. "You're telling me that this... this
*batch* of fifty raw recruits, after one week of training under another
recruit, can form a shield wall with a 90% cohesion rating against mock
charges from veteran mercenaries?"

He flipped a page. "You're telling me their average stamina scores have
increased by 35%? That's statistically impossible! The best I've ever
seen is a 10% increase after a month of brutal conditioning!"

He flipped another page, his eyes wild. "And what is this? 'Meditative
Core Attunement'? 'Quantifiable Skill Progression'? 'Provisional Squad
Designations based on Aptitude Analysis'?" He threw the report down on
the desk. "Williams, this reads like a philosopher's fever dream, not a
military training log!"

"The results are on the training field, sir," Williams said, his voice a
low rumble. "Not just on the paper. Recruit Tomas has them sparring with
blunted steel now. He's identified five potential squad leaders, ten
heavy shield specialists, and a cohort of skirmishers. They move, think,
and fight like men who've been training for a year, not a week."

Valerius stared at his Drill Sergeant, searching for any sign of
deception or madness. He found only granite certainty. He had seen
glimpses of it himself, making discreet observations from the
battlements overlooking the yard. He had seen the impossible discipline,
the flawless drills, the way the recruits moved with a purpose that
bordered on fanatical.

"And his... mercenaries?" the Captain asked, his voice softer now, laced
with a grudging curiosity. "They are on the payroll? Whose?"

"They are listed as 'external training consultants'," Williams replied,
a hint of a smirk touching his lips. "They take their payment in
repaired gear from the depot forge---which Tomas has also somehow
optimized---and three square meals a day. Apparently, the food is better
than anything in the officer's mess."

The Captain leaned back in his chair, the report lying between them like
a gauntlet thrown down against reality itself. He was a man of logic, of
military doctrine passed down through generations. Everything in this
report, everything he had witnessed, defied that logic.

But the evidence was undeniable. The army was in the midst of a brutal,
grinding war. They were losing ground, their freshly-levied troops
breaking against the monstrous hordes. A method that could produce
veteran-level soldiers in a fraction of the time wasn't just unorthodox;
it was a miracle. A miracle that had landed, quite literally, in his
lap.

"Sergeant," Valerius said, his decision made. "Get Recruit Tomas. And

Three weeks later. In the courtyard. In front of a fort?

Captain Valerius stood on the battlements, his knuckles white on the
stone railing, and wondered where the hell the fort had come from. A
week ago, it had been an empty patch of ground used for overflow drills.
Now, a sturdy, defensible wooden palisade with a gate and firing steps
stood there, looking like it had been part of the depot for years. He
had authorized the use of "training materials." He hadn't authorized the
construction of a permanent military installation by a single platoon of
recruits in their spare time.

Below him, the yard was a sea of tension.

"Recruits, form up!" Tomas's voice, now carrying the weight of a
seasoned commander, echoed in the enclosed space. Fifty young men,
barely a month in service, moved with the precision of a royal guard,
forming a tight, interlocking phalanx before the fort's gate. Their
shields were painted with the simple, stark insignia of a single,
unadorned shield: the sigil of Camp Aegis.

"Behind you is your home," Tomas spoke, his voice soft but reaching
every man. "Your farm, your shop, your bakery, your grandfather's
cabin." He pointed to the new fort. "Inside there are innocents!"

Valerius grunted. They weren't exactly innocents. It was a sizable group
from Camp Aegis---led by a grim-faced mountain of a man Tomas called
Silas---that had arrived the previous week to "help out" and deliver
"supplies." The fort was the result.

"Across from you is a group of trainees, one hundred strong," Tomas
continued, his gaze sweeping over the opposing force. "They are eighteen
months ahead of you. A demonstration was requested. All participants,
dip your training swords in the barrels of red paint before you!"

As one, Tomas's fifty recruits dipped their blunted steel swords into
barrels of crimson paint. At a nod from Williams, who stood officiating
with an impossibly wide grin, the hundred-man veteran platoon did the

"If you are struck in your sword arm, you will drop your sword,"

Tomas paced slowly before HIS recruits, his eyes burning with an

"I expect your shields to be bathed in red," he said, his voice a low,

MYSELF CLEAR!?"

"SIR, YES, SIR!" The roar was a physical shockwave, a declaration of
absolute faith and lethal intent.

On the battlements, Captain Valerius was no longer alone. He was flanked
by the depot's Commander, a grizzled old General named Marcus, and
several other high-ranking officers who had come to witness the
"unorthodox methods" Valerius had risked his career to report.

"Impressive discipline, Captain," General Marcus said, his voice a
gravelly rumble. "But they are outnumbered two to one by men with a year
and a half of experience. This will be a slaughter."

Valerius said nothing. He had seen the impossible for three weeks
straight. He was no longer certain what a slaughter looked like.

"BEGIN!" Williams roared.

The veteran platoon surged forward with a standard battle cry, a wave of
confident aggression. They were bigger, more experienced, and there were
twice as many of them.

Tomas's unit did not move. They braced. Fifty shields became one solid
wall. Fifty pairs of eyes watched from the narrow slits, calm and
focused. The demonstration had begun.

There was a lot of laughing from the veteran platoon's ranks as they
advanced. They saw fifty green boys, fresh off the farm, and a quick,
glorious victory. They charged, their formation loose and chaotic, each
man eager to be the first to mark an enemy and claim bragging rights.

The initial clash was a thunderous *WHUMP* as one hundred men crashed
against fifty shields. The impact was staggering. The veterans expected
the line to buckle, to shatter into a panicked mob.

It didn't even tremble.

Then, a voice rang out, clear and resonant above the chaos. "HOLD THE
LINE!"

The fifty shields, bathed in the first splashes of red paint, remained
locked together, an unbreachable crimson wall. The veterans, their
initial momentum spent, found themselves jammed up against an immovable
object, their formation now a disorganized, crowded mess.

"NOW, THRUST!"

Fifty swords, moving as one, flashed forward from the narrow gaps
between the shields. They didn't swing wildly. They were short,
economical, brutal jabs aimed at torsos and arms. A score of veterans
grunted in surprise, looking down at the sudden red blossoms on their
chests and limbs. They dropped their swords or fell to the ground,
confusion warring with the sting of the mock blow.

"BLOCK, STEP BACK! THRUST!"

The phalanx moved. It wasn't a rout; it was a controlled, rhythmic
retreat. One step back, absorbing the renewed, more desperate push from
the veterans, and then another coordinated wave of lethal thrusts. It
was a meat grinder.

On and on it went. The veterans, frustrated and unable to bring their
superior numbers to bear against the disciplined front, began to fall
into disarray. Their attacks became sloppier. Tomas's men, however,
never broke their rhythm. They were a single organism, a fifty-headed
hydra of wood and steel, guided by the calm, constant commands of their
leader. When a recruit's sword arm was marked, he would drop his weapon
but keep his shield locked in the wall, protecting the man next to him.
His job was not over.

After almost an hour of grueling, relentless combat, there was silence.

Well, except for the heavy, ragged breathing of one hundred and fifty
men.

The training yard was a sea of red-marked bodies. Half of Tomas's
recruits were without a sword, their right arms hanging limp at their

there were no more "living" enemy combatants. Every single one of the

stunned disbelief.

On the battlements, the Depot Commander was silent. His cigar had gone
out, forgotten between his fingers. The other officers were speechless,
their faces pale. General Marcus turned his head slowly, his eyes wide,
to look at Captain Valerius.

He finally spoke, his voice a hoarse, reverent whisper.

"What in God's name did I just witness?"

"Recruits! FALL OUT!"

Tomas's voice cut through the stunned silence of the courtyard. The
command was the key that unlocked the disciplined machine. The perfect
shield wall dissolved. The fifty young men, panting and covered in red
paint, moved with practiced efficiency. They lowered their shields,
helped their "dead" comrades to their feet, and began retrieving the
discarded wooden swords from the field. There was no boasting, no
cheering. It was the calm, professional aftermath of a job well done.

Meanwhile, the hundred veterans were picking themselves up, a dazed,
humbled look on their faces. They had been systematically dismantled,
not by superior strength or speed, but by a form of tactical cohesion
they had never encountered.

Within minutes, Tomas's fifty recruits had stowed their gear and
reformed into their perfect, double-rank formation, facing the
battlements.

"Face Front!" Tomas barked, his eyes now on the assembled officers
above. "Salute the Depot Commander!"

SNAP.

Fifty hands moved as one, a crisp, unified salute directed at General
Marcus. They stood there, silent and steady, awaiting the judgment of
the highest authority in the depot.

On the battlements, General Marcus was still processing the impossible
outcome. He had just watched a platoon of one-month recruits,
outnumbered two-to-one, annihilate a company of seasoned trainees
without suffering a single "fatality." They hadn't just won; they had
demonstrated a new doctrine of warfare.

He looked at Captain Valerius, whose expression was a mixture of
vindication and awe. He looked at Drill Sergeant Williams, who was
standing at the edge of the field, looking for all the world like a
proud father at his son's graduation. And finally, he looked down at the
architect of it all: the sixteen-year-old giant who stood at the head of
his men.

The General took a deep breath, the gears of a lifetime of military
command turning in his mind. He saw not a breach of protocol, but a
solution. A desperate, brilliant solution to a losing war.

He returned the salute with a slow, deliberate motion.

"Captain Valerius," the General said, his voice a low, commanding rumble
that carried across the yard. "My office. Now." He then turned to one of
his aides. "Get me every file we have on 'Camp Aegis'. I want to know
everything. The location, the personnel, the supply chain. Everything."

He gave one last look down at Tomas. "And someone get that recruit a
proper officer's uniform. His 'recruitment' period is officially over."

One month later, 2nd Lieutenant Tomas surveyed the compound.
Unofficially, the men had taken to calling it "Camp Eagle," a respectful
nod to the depot's official insignia, but everyone knew its heart and
soul belonged to the Aegis.

The facility was now three times as large. The original palisade fort
stood at one end, but it was now flanked by newly constructed siege
towers, obstacle courses, and mock-village streets. It was no longer a
simple training yard; it was a full-fledged war simulation center.

At 5 a.m. sharp, one hundred and fifty men stood assembled in the main
yard. Tomas's original fifty stood as a solid, veteran core, their
movements imbued with a quiet, dangerous confidence. The hundred
veterans they had humbled a month prior were now integrated into the
formation, their initial resentment forged into a fierce, grudging
respect. The precision of their combined formation was undeniable. The
anticipation in the air was unmistakable.

General Marcus looked on from the main watchtower, his expression
unreadable. For the past month, he had approved everything Tomas
requested. Though, "request" was a strong word. Tomas simply directed,
and people, from the quartermaster to the engineering corps, obeyed. He
had a way of stating a need as a self-evident fact that made refusal
seem illogical. Paperwork was filled out as an afterthought, a mere
formality to document the whirlwind of progress.

Today was the culmination of that progress. They would be running a
variety of "games." Capture the Flag. Escort the Noble. Assault the
Fort. Each scenario was designed by Tomas to test not just combat
prowess, but adaptability, strategy, and small-unit leadership. General
Marcus wanted to see the results of his investment, to see if the
miracle of the first month could be scaled up.

Tomas walked before the assembled companies. He wore the simple,
unadorned uniform of a 2nd Lieutenant, but he carried the presence of a
seasoned commander.

"Men!" his voice rang out. "For the last month, you have learned the
Aegis doctrine. You have learned to be a wall. You have learned to be a
spear. Today, you will learn to be the mind that wields them both."

He pointed to the far side of the compound. "Company Alpha,"---the
hundred veterans---"your objective is to capture the flag from the
fort's tower. Simple and direct."

He then gestured to his original fifty. "Company Beta. Your objective is
to defend that flag. You are outnumbered two-to-one. You know the
drill."

A low, confident murmur went through Beta Company's ranks.

"However," Tomas added, a sharp glint in his eye, "there is a
complication." He pointed to Captain Valerius, who was standing
nervously beside a covered wagon. "A 'noble dignitary' and his retinue
must be escorted from the east gate to the fort. Alpha Company, you may
choose to intercept this convoy to create a diversion. Beta Company, you
must decide how many men you can spare from the fort's defense to ensure
his safe arrival. Your 'noble's' life is a secondary victory condition."

On the tower, General Marcus grunted in approval. It was a classic
strategic dilemma. A test of resource allocation and risk assessment
under pressure.

"The rules are the same," Tomas concluded. "Paint marks the kill.
Leaders will be designated. Your sergeants will command. I will be
observing. Prepare for battle!"

As the companies broke off to strategize with their sergeants---leaders
that Tomas, not the official army roster, had appointed based on
merit---General Marcus turned to an aide.

"Get me a runner to the capital," he said, his voice a low, decisive
rumble. "Tell them Operation Aegis is ready for field testing. It's time
to see how this new doctrine holds up against real monsters."

The results of the exercise were exemplary. The hundred veterans of
Alpha Company, employing standard military doctrine, launched a
full-frontal assault on the fort, expecting to overwhelm the defenders
with sheer numbers. They were systematically dismantled.

Beta Company, under the command of a young sergeant Tomas had personally
mentored, split their forces with calculated precision. Ten men were
detached in a flexible screening force that harried, delayed, and
ultimately repelled the veterans' attempt to intercept the "noble,"
leading them on a frustrating chase through the mock village. The
remaining forty defenders used the fort's choke points and their
superior shield-wall tactics to hold the main assault at the gate,
bleeding the attackers of men and morale. By the end, the flag was
secure, the noble was safe, and Alpha Company was a sea of red paint and
humbled pride.

No one would believe these men had never seen "real" battle. But that
was about to change.

As the companies were debriefing, there was one part of the
demonstration that was unplanned. A small group of soldiers stood
observing from the side, their armor scarred and their expressions
cynical. They were a squad of five-year veterans, battle-hardened scouts
from the northern front, brought back to the depot to share their
experiences. Three of them watched, unimpressed.

"It's a fancy drill," the squad leader, a wiry man with a nasty scar
across his lip, muttered to his companions. "Impressive, but can that
boy-officer even fight? He's great at giving orders, but a commander who
can't hold his own is just a liability. What happens if he falls?"

Williams, who had been overseeing the paint distribution, was watching.
His hearing was excellent. "Why don't you go 'ask' him for a
demonstration?" he said, his voice a low, challenging rumble as he
approached the scouts. Williams had to admit, he too, was curious. He
had seen Tomas as an instructor and a commander, but he hadn't seen him
in a one-on-one fight since that very first day.

The scarred scout leader smirked. He saw it as a challenge and a chance
to put the prodigy in his place. He and his two men strode onto the
field, their movements loose and predatory. They approached Tomas, who
was reviewing the results with his sergeants.

"Lieutenant," the scout said, his tone dripping with false deference.
"That was a fine display. But some of the men were wondering if you'd
honor us with a demonstration of your personal prowess. A commander
should lead from the front, after all. A friendly spar. Three against
one, to make it sporting."

A hush fell over the training yard. The recruits, who revered Tomas,
bristled at the challenge. Tomas's sergeants stepped forward
protectively.

Tomas simply raised a hand, silencing them. He looked at the three
veterans, his *\[Analytical Mind\]* skill dissecting their confident
stances, their scarred gear, the subtle tells of men who had survived
real, bloody combat.

He gave a small, calm smile. "Of course. It is always an honor to learn
from veterans of the front."

He unbuckled his officer's belt and handed it to a nearby sergeant. He
walked to the barrels of paint, picking up not a sword and shield, but
two blunted short swords, dipping both in the red paint.

The veterans smirked, grabbing their own painted weapons---a sword and
shield for the leader, a short spear, and an axe. Three armed and
experienced killers against one unarmored boy with two practice blades.

Williams grinned. The scouts thought they were testing the boy. They had
no idea they were the test.

"Whenever you're ready, gentlemen," Tomas said, his voice calm, as he
settled into a low, balanced stance. The demonstration was not over yet.

What happened next had to be seen. Speaking of it afterward would just
get one called a liar. For years to come, it was spoken of only in
whispers by the one hundred and fifty men who witnessed it.

The three veterans, confident in their numbers and experience, spread
out to flank him. The leader with the shield charged forward to pin him,
while the spearman and the axeman circled to the sides for the killing
blow. It was a standard, effective tactic for overwhelming a single
opponent.

It should have worked.

The leader lunged, his shield forward for a bash, his sword ready to
follow up.

Tomas did not retreat. He did not block. He *flowed*.

In a movement so fast it seemed to blur, he took a single, precise step
to the side, his body turning like a hinge. The shield boss missed him
by an inch. As the veteran stumbled past, his momentum carrying him
forward, Tomas's right-hand sword drew a perfect, clean red line across
the man's exposed back. At the same time, his left-hand sword flickered
out and tapped the veteran's sword hand.

**\[Parry\]** **\[Counter-Strike\]**

One down, disarmed and "dead" in less than three seconds.

The spearman, surprised by the sudden collapse of his leader, thrust his
spear at Tomas's chest.

Tomas didn't even look at the spear tip. His *\[Danger Sense\]* had been
screaming a warning for a full second. He slapped the shaft of the spear
down with his left blade, redirecting it harmlessly into the dirt. He
didn't pause. He used the momentum of the parry to spin, his right-hand
blade whipping around in a tight, vicious arc that left a crimson splash
across the spearman's ribs.

Two down. Five seconds.

The axeman, seeing his comrades fall with impossible speed, let out a
roar of rage and charged, swinging his axe in a wide, powerful blow
meant to cleave Tomas in two.

This time, Tomas met the attack. Not with force, but with finesse. He
crossed his two short swords into an 'X', catching the handle of the axe
just below the head.

\[Block\]

The impact was immense, but Tomas's stance was a perfect root, his
*\[Aegis Initiate\]* core absorbing the shock. For a fraction of a
second, the two fighters were locked, strength against strength.

And in that fraction of a second, every man in the yard saw it. They saw
the calm focus in Tomas's eyes. They saw the flicker of panic in the
veteran's.

Tomas *pushed*. He didn't shove the man back. He twisted his blades,
using the veteran's own strength against him, wrenching the axe from his
grip. The weapon spun through the air. Tomas released his right sword,
snatched the axe out of the air, and continued the weapon's original
trajectory, tapping the now-unbalanced veteran neatly on the chest with
the flat of the axe head.

Three down. Less than ten seconds.

Silence.

Tomas stood in the center of the training yard, breathing evenly. In one
hand he held a painted short sword, in the other, the veteran's painted
axe. The three battle-hardened scouts stood frozen, looking at the red
marks on their bodies and then back at the calm, unbloodied young
officer. They hadn't just been beaten. They had been surgically,
effortlessly, and humiliatingly dismantled.

Tomas dropped the axe and the sword. He walked over to the scout leader
and offered a hand to help him up.

"Thank you for the lesson," Tomas said, his voice genuine and calm, as
if they had just concluded a friendly dance. "Your flanking maneuver was
well-executed. I will incorporate it into our training."

The veteran stared at the offered hand, then at the boy's face, and
finally understood. This wasn't a boy playing soldier. This was
something else entirely. He took the hand, humbled to his very core.

From the watchtower, General Marcus slowly, deliberately, put his unlit
cigar into his mouth and began chewing on the end of it. His aide stood
beside him, his mouth hanging open.

Williams just stood where he was, grinning from ear to ear. His
curiosity was satisfied. The revolution had its king.

Six months later. On a windswept hill overlooking the blood-soaked
valley of the Gray Marches, stood Duke Alaric, Warden of the Northern
Front, astride a brilliant, armored black stallion. Before him, arrayed
in the pre-dawn gloom, stood Division Aegis. The "experiment."

Three hundred and one strong. Six companies of fifty men, plus a single
officer. A farm boy. The Duke snorted, the sound a plume of white in the
frigid air. He had read the reports from General Marcus, of course.
Preposterous, unbelievable documents detailing impossible training
results. But reports were one thing. The reality of a goblin horde
numbering in the thousands was another.

He had expected a rabble of over-disciplined but untested boys. What he
saw was... impressive.

They were in formation, a perfect checkerboard of six compact phalanxes.
The morning light glinted off their polished, functional steel helmets
and the tops of their identical tower shields, each bearing the simple
sigil of the Aegis. They stood at perfect attention, not a fidget or a
shuffle in the entire division. They could have been statues, carved
from the very rock of the hill they stood on.

The Duke, a veteran of a hundred battles, could appreciate the
discipline. It was the foundation of any army. But this was different.
There was no fear in their stillness. No nervous tension. There was only
a profound, unsettling calm. They looked less like soldiers waiting for
a battle and more like a surgeon waiting for an operation to begin.

His eyes fell on their commander. Lieutenant Tomas stood at the front,
not on a horse, but on his own two feet. He wore the same functional
armor as his men, distinguished only by the unadorned officer's cloak
that billowed slightly in the wind. He held no sword. His hands were
empty, resting calmly at his sides. He was staring down into the valley,
where the green tide of the goblin horde was beginning to stir, their
crude banners a chaotic smear against the gray landscape.

The Duke had expected to see a prodigy, a fierce young warrior eager for
glory. Instead, he saw a master craftsman surveying his materials. The
valley was his workshop, the battle his forge, and the lives of three
hundred men were his tools.

An aide rode up to the Duke's side. "My Lord, the horde is forming. The
scouts estimate at least five thousand, with ogre support. Standard
chaotic advance is expected."

The Duke grunted, his eyes still fixed on Tomas. "And our 'boy-wonder'?
What is his plan? A glorious last stand?"

The aide hesitated. "He... he has given only one order, my Lord. 'Hold
the line. Trust the rhythm.'"

The Duke snorted again, a sound of pure aristocratic disdain. He had
given this experimental division the honor of holding the center, the
position where the goblin charge would be heaviest. It was a test, and a
brutal one. He expected them to break. He wanted to see how long they
would last before he committed his own veteran legions to clean up the
mess.

The sun finally broke over the distant mountains, its light spilling
into the valley. A guttural roar, the sound of a thousand bloodthirsty
throats, rose from the goblin horde. The ground began to tremble as the
green tide surged forward.

Division Aegis did not move.

But on the hill, Tomas finally raised his hand. It wasn't a signal for a
charge or a volley of arrows. It was a slow, deliberate gesture, like a
conductor raising a baton. And across the field, three hundred men took
a single, synchronized breath, and settled into their stances. The
operation had begun.

A lone figure stepped forward from the horde. An ogre. Eight feet of
solid, reeking muscle, weighing at least five hundred pounds. It carried
a crude, two-handed axe that was nearly as tall as the creature itself,
its edge chipped and stained. It was a living siege engine, a
line-breaker meant to sow terror and shatter formations before the main
charge hit.

The beast let out a guttural roar, a challenge that echoed across the
valley.

On the hill, Tomas gestured to his right without taking his eyes off the
creature. Valerius, his face a grim mask, stepped forward and handed him
two perfectly balanced, unadorned short swords. Tomas accepted them
without a glance, his grip sure and familiar, and strode forward to meet
the challenge alone.

A murmur of disbelief went through the Duke's retinue. To face an ogre
in single combat was not bravery; it was suicide.

"PUNY HUMAN! PREPARE TO DIE!" the ogre bellowed, its voice a wet,
rasping sound.

Tomas kept walking, his pace steady, his expression calm. He was closing
the distance, shortening the battlefield, turning the ogre's powerful
charge into a liability.

When they were less than twenty meters apart, the ogre roared in
frustration at the human's lack of fear, then charged. The ground shook
with its pounding footsteps. It raised its massive axe high, preparing
to bring it down in a single, skull-shattering blow.

What happened next put the Duke on edge.

Tomas didn't try to meet the charge. He didn't brace or dodge. As the
ogre entered its swing, Tomas *accelerated*. He exploded forward in a
*\[Sprint\]*, moving inside the arc of the descending axe. The massive
weapon whistled through the air where he had been a split second before,
crashing into the earth with enough force to send clods of dirt flying.

The ogre was now overextended, its weapon buried in the ground, its
balance compromised. For Tomas, it was the opening. The pause in the
rhythm.

He didn't go for a killing blow. He moved like a surgeon. His left sword
flashed, scoring a deep, precise red line across the back of the ogre's
thick knee. His right sword followed, slicing through the massive tendon
of the creature's weapon arm.

There was a wet, snapping sound. The ogre bellowed, not in rage, but in
agony. Its right arm went limp, its grip on the axe vanishing. Its left
leg buckled, sending the behemoth crashing to its knees with a
ground-shaking thud. It was crippled, disarmed, and helpless in less
time than it took the Duke to draw a breath.

Tomas didn't gloat. He didn't pause. He spun, his swords a blur of
motion, and with a series of brutally efficient strikes, ended the
creature's life.

Silence descended upon the battlefield. The goblin horde, which had been
moments from a full-scale charge, faltered in confusion and shock. Their
champion, their living battering ram, had been dismantled with the
contemptuous ease of a butcher jointing a carcass.

Tomas stood over the fallen ogre, breathing evenly. He looked up, not at
the enemy, but back at his own men. His message was clear: *This is what
we face. And this is how we will defeat it.*

On the hill, the Duke Alaric unconsciously tightened his grip on his
reins, his armored stallion shifting nervously beneath him. He had seen
champions fight monsters before, a clash of heroic might and brute
force. He had never seen a monster so utterly and efficiently
*deconstructed*. It wasn't a fight. It was a dissection.

"That boy is dangerous," the Duke whispered, his voice filled with a
newfound, profound respect.

From behind him came another whisper, so quiet he almost missed it. It
was the grizzled scout leader from the depot, now attached to the Duke's
personal guard.

"Especially if you are an ogre."

In a furious and deafening collective yell, five thousand goblins, their
shock curdling into a rage-fueled bloodlust, charged up the hill. With
them came the remaining twelve ogres, now spread throughout the horde,
their massive forms rising above the green tide like monstrous islands.

Those expecting Tomas to flee back to his lines were disappointed. That
was never the plan.

As the horde surged forward, Division Aegis moved. With a series of
sharp, barked commands from their sergeants, five of the six companies
dispersed, not in retreat, but into a wide, flexible semi-circle. They
flowed backwards and sideways, giving ground but never breaking
formation, creating a massive, U-shaped pocket in the center of the
battlefield.

A single company remained at the top of the hill, an unmovable anchor
guarding the Duke's position. It was the original fifty. The first
recruits. They planted their feet, locked their shields, and built a
wall of steel and grit, their faces grim and determined.

The Duke snorted again, a sound of nervous habit now rather than
disdain. He was never in any danger; his warhorse was one of the finest
in the land, capable of outrunning any goblin. He stayed, for now, his
curiosity having transformed into a morbid, all-consuming fascination.

Tomas did not rejoin his men. He became a ghost. He flowed around the
edges of the chaotic goblin charge, disrupting but never fully engaging.
He was a flash of steel in the flank of a charging ogre, a quick,
precise strike to a leg tendon that would send the beast stumbling into
its own troops. He was a sudden, terrifying presence that would appear
before a goblin chieftain, forcing the creature's personal guard to peel
off from the main advance to deal with him, only for him to melt away
into the chaos seconds later.

He was disorganizing the enemy, breaking their charge into smaller, more
manageable waves. He was a master shepherd, herding the terrified,
enraged goblins towards the waiting formations of his companies, a few
hundred at a time.

To the untrained eye, it was total chaos. A swirling, screaming vortex
of green skin and flashing steel.

The Duke's eye was not untrained. Not in the least.

He saw the genius in the madness. He saw the goblin horde, which should
have hit his center line like a tidal wave, being funneled and broken
apart. He saw Tomas creating pockets of disorder, which the individual
Aegis companies then systematically and efficiently annihilated. A
company would engage a wave of two hundred goblins, hold them on their
shield wall, grind them down with disciplined thrusts, and then pull
back, letting another fresh company take their place.

It was a battle of rhythm. Tomas was the conductor, disrupting the
enemy's chaotic rhythm while his own army maintained its perfect, lethal
cadence. They weren't fighting one battle against five thousand. They
were fighting twenty-five smaller battles against two hundred, over and
over again. It was a strategy of exhaustion and annihilation, and it was
working with terrifying efficiency. The Duke sat on his horse, his
disdain long forgotten, and watched a master at work.

Tomas was tiring. The initial adrenaline had faded, replaced by the
deep, gnawing ache of sustained, high-intensity combat. His stamina bar,
vast as it was, was no longer full. He flowed and struck, parried and
dodged, a whirlwind of controlled violence, but for every goblin
chieftain he baited, ten more seemed to take its place. The numbers
seemed endless, but he knew they weren't.

More than his own fatigue, he worried for his men. He could feel the
rhythm of his companies beginning to slow. The disciplined steps back
were a fraction late, the thrusts losing some of their snap. They were
holding, but the sheer weight of the horde was a physical and mental
grind.

He needed to remind them of their foundation. He began a chant, his
commander's voice cutting through the din of battle.

"HOLD THE LINE!"

From the five embattled phalanxes, a ragged but defiant roar answered
him. "HOLD THE LINE!"

"BEND, BUT DO NOT BREAK!" he bellowed, his voice straining.

"BEND, BUT DO NOT BREAK!" The reply was stronger this time, a surge of
shared will.

An eerie aura seemed to form over the battlefield. It was a shimmer in
the air, a pressure felt in the bones. It was palpable, identifiable,
unmistakable. The Duke felt the hairs on his arms stand up.

Tomas took a deep, centering breath, tapping into the core of his power,
the very essence of the Aegis doctrine. His voice rolled across the
field, not as a shout, but as a profound, resonant instruction that
seemed to bypass the ears and speak directly to the soul of every man in
his division.

"BREATHE IN! YOU ARE NOT TIRED! YOU ARE NOT IN PAIN! YOU FEEL WEAKNESS
LEAVING YOUR BODY, NOTHING MORE! BREATHE OUT!"

And they did. Across the battlefield, three hundred men, in the midst of
the blood and chaos, took a single, synchronized, meditative breath. It
was the same breath they had taken every morning in the training yard.
It was the breath that centered them, that rooted them to the earth.

Their postures strengthened. Shields that had begun to waver were locked
firmly back into place. Swords that had grown heavy were held steady
once more. A wave of renewed vigor, visible and shocking, washed through
the ranks of Division Aegis.

What was happening was not possible.

On the hill, the Duke watched, his mind struggling to comprehend the
phenomenon. Men tire. Men falter. Men die. That was the fundamental,
immutable truth of warfare. They did not get *stronger* in the middle of
a battle.

But he could see it. He saw a man from the third company, his arm
bleeding from a stray cut, grimace not in pain, but in renewed focus as
he took the breath. He saw his stamina bar, a concept he didn't
understand but could feel the effects of, tick visibly upward.

For Tomas, the notifications were a torrent of golden light.

**\[Your \[Leadership\] skill has been pushed to its absolute limit in a
large-scale combat scenario!\]** **\[Your \[Instruction: Centered Will\]
ability has been activated under extreme duress!\]** **\[A new
Division-Wide Buff has been created!\]**

**\[Aura of the Aegis (Active)\]** **Effect:** While this ability is
active, all allies within the sound of your voice who share the Aegis
doctrine gain significantly increased stamina regeneration and
resistance to pain and fatigue. The effect is amplified by the number of
disciplined allies. **Cost:** Continuous, severe drain on the user's
personal stamina and willpower.

Tomas felt a crushing weight descend upon him, as if he were personally
shouldering the fatigue of all three hundred of his men. His own stamina
bar plummeted. But as he watched his men regain their footing, their
line solidifying once more, he knew it was a price worth paying. The
line would hold.

As the main goblin assault faltered, dying in waves against the
resurgent shield walls, a final, desperate gambit was launched. A
single, guttural roar cut through the chaos. One of the last remaining
ogres, larger and more cunning than the others, had held back. Seeing
the human commander on the hill, it gathered a reserve of five hundred
goblins and launched a direct, targeted charge at the Duke.

But the Duke didn't flee. Not yet. The man he was an hour ago would have
wheeled his stallion and galloped to safety without a second thought.
But the man he was now, the man who had witnessed the impossible, would
not dishonor the fifty men standing as a wall of steel before him.

He pulled his own ancestral sword, a magnificent blade that hummed with
latent power, and to his own profound shock, he shouted.

"HOLD THE LINE!"

His voice was a pale imitation of Tomas's, but it was filled with a fire
he hadn't felt in decades. The fifty men of the Aegis guard, their eyes
forward, seemed to draw strength from it nonetheless.

"DO NOT BLOCK, DEFLECT!" he roared, the tactical language of the Aegis
doctrine spilling from his lips as if he'd known it his entire life.
"THRUST! PARRY! MOVE! STRIKE!"

The Duke had no idea where those words came from. In the next moment,
driven by an impulse he couldn't explain, he spurred his black stallion
forward, not away from the charge, but directly into its flank. He
became a dervish of devastation. The disciplined movements he had been
observing for hours were now his own. He was no longer just a commander;
he was a part of the rhythm. His powerful blade deflected a crude spear,
his horse trampled a goblin, his follow-up strike was a perfect,
economical thrust.

He carved a path of destruction through the goblin ranks, his veteran
skill merging with the new, efficient doctrine, culminating in a
desperate, valiant duel with the final ogre. The beast's club swung
wide, and the Duke, moving with a speed that defied his age, drove his
sword through the creature's eye, ending the final, desperate charge.

The rush he felt was indescribable. He sat panting on his horse, his
blood singing, and he looked for more enemies, but there was nothing
left to kill. The valley floor was a carpet of goblin dead. The battle
was over.

Impossible! Three hundred men still stood, weary and blood-splattered,
but alive. A victory against overwhelming odds with almost no
casualties.

But one fell.

As the last goblin fell and the cheers of his men began to rise, Tomas,
the anchor of their will, the source of their impossible endurance,
swayed on his feet. The *\[Aura of the Aegis\]* had faded, the cost of
his power coming due crashing down upon him. His stamina and willpower
utterly depleted, his vision tunneled to black. He fell to the ground in
a heap.

"NO!" The roar came from a dozen throats at once. Valerius, Silas,
Williams, and even the Duke, his face a mask of horror, all spurred
their horses or began to run towards the fallen commander. The architect
of their miraculous victory lay motionless on the field.

The world swam back into focus in a haze of blue light and concerned
faces. He was lying on the ground, the smell of blood and churned earth
filling his nostrils. Strong hands were checking him for wounds, turning
him over gently.

He felt... empty. A vessel drained to the last drop. His *\[Stamina\]*
bar was a single, flickering red pixel. A notification, stark and
alarming, pulsed in his vision.

\[WARNING: Willpower has fallen below critical levels. Bodily functions
compromised. Forced shutdown imminent.\]

Through the fog, he saw the face of the Duke, a visage of aristocratic
hauteur now streaked with dirt and goblin blood, his eyes wide with a
frantic, unfamiliar concern. He saw Silas, his uncle's face a terrifying
mask of worry. He saw Williams, the tough-as-nails Drill Sergeant,
looking genuinely scared.

Tomas was spent. Utterly and totally spent. The weight of three hundred
men's fatigue had been a crushing, absolute burden. But as he looked
past the worried faces to the valley floor, where his men stood
victorious and the goblin horde was no more, a huge, triumphant grin
spread across his face.

He looked up at the Duke, his voice a ragged, breathless whisper.

"Did we win?"

The Duke stared down at the pale, exhausted face of the young man, at
the impossible, triumphant grin, and the sheer absurdity of the question
struck him like a physical blow.

A roar of laughter erupted from his chest. It was not a sound of mirth
or amusement. It was a raw, booming, cathartic explosion of adrenaline,
relief, and utter, profound disbelief.

"Did we win?" he bellowed, the laughter still shaking his massive frame.
He looked around at the silent, awestruck officers, at the weary but
living soldiers of Division Aegis, at the valley floor littered with
thousands of slain enemies. "Did we win!?"

He leaned down, his face close to Tomas's, his eyes blazing with a wild,
ecstatic fire that Tomas had never seen before.

"No, boy," the Duke roared, his voice echoing across the now-quiet
battlefield. "We didn't *win*."

He swept a gauntleted hand across the scene of their impossible victory.

"We *conquered*!"

Two years later, Commander Tomas knelt on a velvet cushion in the grand
throne room of the capital. The cold stone floor was a stark contrast to
the familiar packed earth of a training yard. The air, thick with
incense and the scent of polished marble, was a far cry from the sweat
and steel he was used to.

His two years were up.

The time had passed in a blur of blood and strategy. The "experiment" of
Division Aegis had become the tip of the Royal Army's spear. The victory
at the Gray Marches was just the beginning. Battle after battle, Tomas
and his men had achieved the impossible, shattering goblin warbands,
breaking orcish legions, and holding ground against odds that would have
broken any other unit.

His deeds were now the stuff of legend, being written of in history
books, sung by bards in every tavern, and dreamed of by a new generation
of would-be recruits who now aspired not to be knights in shining armor,
but disciplined soldiers of the Aegis. Tomas's training methods---the
focus on foundation, the meditative core attunement, the rhythm of
combat---were being taught, albeit imperfectly, in academies across the
country.

The Horde, once an unstoppable tide of destruction, was now in complete
disarray, its leadership broken, its armies shattered by a doctrine it
could not comprehend. Peace, which seemed like an impossibility two
years ago, now seemed less a dream and more of simply a goal. One that
could be achieved through superior tactics and indomitable will.

Tomas knelt, his head bowed. He wore a formal officer's uniform, the
dark blue wool feeling foreign and restrictive. His short swords were
not at his hip; they had been surrendered at the door as per courtly
protocol. He felt exposed, more vulnerable than he had ever felt on a
battlefield.

The King, a man whose weary face showed the long years of a losing war,
sat upon the throne. He looked down at the young man who had
single-handedly turned the tide. He had read the reports, heard the
songs, and still found it hard to believe.

"In the two years since your conscription," the King's voice boomed,
amplified by the hall's acoustics, "you have saved the northern front,
reclaimed the western territories, and broken the back of the greenskin
horde. You have redefined the very nature of our army and given this
kingdom something it had lost: hope."

The King rose from his throne and descended the dais, a heavy, ancestral
sword in his hand. He stopped before the kneeling commander.

"For valor beyond measure, for leadership that has reshaped a nation,
and for service that will be remembered for a thousand years, I, King
Theron, grant you the highest honor of this realm."

He lightly tapped Tomas on each shoulder with the flat of the
greatsword.

"Rise, Sir Tomas."

At the grand banquet that evening, the air was thick with the sounds of
celebration. Wine flowed freely, and bards sang the now-famous "Ballad
of Aegis Hold." Tomas sat at the King's right hand, an uncomfortable
guest of honor amidst the silks and jewels of the royal court. He had
politely eaten his meal, answered the fawning questions of nobles, and
endured the endless toasts to his health.

Finally, during a lull in the festivities, King Theron leaned over, his
expression serious. He asked a simple question that the entire kingdom
was wondering.

"What will you do now, Sir Tomas?"

It was the offer of a lifetime. A duchy in the reclaimed territories?
Command of the entire Royal Army? A permanent position on the Royal
Council? Any of these would be his for the asking.

Tomas pretended to ponder the question, taking a slow sip of wine. But
he had made up his mind long ago, on a dusty training field, watching a
young recruit finally master the perfect block.

"I would like to open an Academy, Your Majesty," he said, his voice calm
and clear, cutting through the nearby chatter. "With your blessing, of
course."

The King raised an eyebrow, intrigued. "An academy? We have academies."

"You have academies that teach boys to swing a sword," Tomas corrected
him gently. "I have been training men to be soldiers. I think it's time
I started training leaders of men."

He set down his goblet and turned to face the King, the full force of
his quiet, unshakable conviction in his eyes.

"You've seen what one disciplined division can do. Imagine a dozen such
divisions. Imagine an army where every sergeant, every captain, every
commander understands the Aegis doctrine not as a set of drills, but as
a philosophy. A philosophy of building from the foundation up, of
knowing one's center, of turning a simple line of soldiers into an
unbreakable, living fortress."

He leaned in slightly, his voice dropping. "Give me the worst recruits,
the cast-offs from the other academies. Give me a plot of land and five
years. I will not give you an army. I will give you a generation of
leaders who will secure this kingdom's peace for the next century."

He leaned back, his proposal laid bare. "Would you agree?"

King Theron stared at the young knight, at the burning intelligence in
his eyes. He saw no personal ambition, no lust for power or land. He saw
only the focused, unwavering dedication of a master builder who, having
perfected a single fortress, now wanted to redesign the entire kingdom's
defenses.

A slow smile spread across the King's weary face. He had offered Tomas a
reward. Tomas had countered by offering him a future.

"Yes, Sir Tomas," the King said, raising his goblet in a toast that
silenced the entire hall. "I believe I would."
