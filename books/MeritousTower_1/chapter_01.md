# Chapter 1: The Drop

Here is the setup for the **"Unseen"** class (the Nobodies) and the beginning of Jace's climb.

### The Setup

**The Stratification of the 12-Year-Olds:**
1.  **The Highborn (Elites):** They arrive healthy, tall, and confident. They know the "Meta." They have memorized the maps of Floor 1. They know the monster weaknesses. They have secret handshakes to find each other instantly.
2.  **The Stewards (Professionals):** Children of merchants and scribes. They are anxious but literate. They try to form committees immediately.
3.  **The Labor (Workers):** Strong but lost. They look for someone to tell them what to do.
4.  **The Dregs (The Unseen):** Jace’s people. Malnourished, stunted growth, scarred. They don't group up. They scatter. They know that in a crisis, the "weak" are the first to be eaten—or sacrificed by the strong.

**The System's "Fairness":**
Everyone arrives in **The Silt** (Floor 1) wearing identical grey hemp tunics. No weapons. No items.
*   **The Elite Advantage:** They have 12 years of proper protein, martial arts tutors, and strategy guides.
*   **Jace's Advantage:** He knows what it feels like to starve. He knows how to kill with a rock. He has no hesitation.

### The Opening Scene: The Drop

The smell hit Jace before his vision cleared. It smelled like sulfur, dead fish, and fear.

A moment ago, he had been standing on the Induction Platform in the city below, surrounded by crying mothers and stoic fathers. Now, there was only the grey mist and the sucking sound of mud.

**[SYSTEM INITIALIZATION...]**
**[WELCOME TO THE SPIRE, ASCENDANT.]**
**[CURRENT FLOOR: 1 (THE SILT)]**
**[OBJECTIVE: SURVIVE. CLIMB. PROVE.]**

Jace blinked, shaking the vertigo from his head. He was ankle-deep in cold, black muck. Around him, hundreds of other twelve-year-olds were materializing in flashes of blue light.

The noise started instantly. Screaming. Crying. Panic.

"Mother!?" a girl shrieked to his left. She was chubby, rosy-cheeked. A Worker's kid. She’d probably never missed a meal in her life.

Jace didn't scream. He dropped into a crouch. His eyes darted left, right, up. *Threat assessment.* It was a habit from the streets. When you wake up in a new alley, you check for exits, and you check for the bigger dogs.

He saw the cliques forming already.

Thirty yards away, on a patch of dry, elevated stone, a group of boys and girls were gathering. They didn't look scared. They looked annoyed. Their tunics were the same grey hemp as Jace’s, but they wore them differently—shoulders back, chins high.

"Valerius Group! On me!" a blond boy shouted. He had the height of a fourteen-year-old and muscles defined by expensive gymnasiums. "Formation Delta! Check your perimeters!"

**The Highborn.**

While the Worker kids were sobbing in the mud, the Highborn were securing the high ground. They began kicking the "Professional" kids off the dry stone, shoving them down into the muck to make room for their own.

"Make way," the blond boy sneered, pushing a bespectacled boy into the water. "The dry ground is for the capable."

Jace narrowed his eyes. *Same old world,* he thought. *New place, same rules.*

He checked his own status. A blue semi-transparent box hovered in his vision.

**[Name: Jace]**
**[Origin: Dreg]**
**[Level: 1]**
**[HP: 40/40] (Malnourished Penalty applied)**
**[Stamina: 100/100]**
**[Strength: 4]**
**[Agility: 9]**
**[Constitution: 4]**
**[Intelligence: 7]**
**[Willpower: 12]**

*Four Strength.* The Highborn kids probably started with ten. Jace looked at his hands. They were scarred, the fingernails bitten down to the quick. He was small for his age, his growth stunned by years of scavenged meals.

But then he looked at the **Willpower**. The System note flickered.
*([Note: High Willpower detected. Source: Trauma/Survival. Resistance to Fear effects increased by 20%.])*

A scream cut through the chaotic noise. Not a cry for mom—a scream of pain.

The mud rippled. Something moved beneath the surface, tracking the vibrations of the panicked children.

"Quiet!" Jace whispered, though nobody heard him.

A massive shape erupted from the silt near the sobbing girl. It looked like a cross between a crocodile and a cockroach, its chitinous shell slick with oil. A **Silt-Reaver**.

**[Combat Initiated]**

The Worker kids froze. The Professional kids scrambled back, tripping over each other.

The Highborn boy on the rock, the leader, pointed. "Target spotted! Left flank! Use the distraction to secure the perimeter!"

*Use the distraction.*

They weren't going to help the girl. They were going to let the monster eat her so they had time to set up their camp. It was a cold, calculated, military decision.

The Reaver lunged, its mandibles clicking open.

Jace didn't think about honor. He didn't think about being a hero. He thought about the time a stray dog had cornered him behind the butcher shop when he was eight. If you run, you die. If you hesitate, you die.

Jace scooped up a handful of the thick, gritty mud.

As the Reaver reared back to strike the frozen girl, Jace whistled. A sharp, piercing sound he used to use to signal the other street rats the cops were coming.

The Reaver’s head snapped toward him.

Jace threw the mud. It wasn't a noble weapon. It was sludge. But his aim—honed by years of throwing rocks at rats—was true. The mud splattered across the Reaver’s multiple eyes.

The beast hissed, blinded and confused, thrashing sideways.

"Move!" Jace roared at the girl, his voice cracking. "Get to the rocks!"

He didn't wait to see if she listened. He scrambled toward the nearest threat—not the monster, but the environment. He saw a jagged piece of rusted rebar sticking out of the mud, a remnant of some ancient structure.

The System flagged it.
**[Rusty Iron Shard (Trash Quality)]**
**[Damage: 1-3]**

Jace ripped it free. The metal bit into his palm, but he held tight.

The Reaver had wiped the mud from its eyes. It ignored the girl now. It wanted the annoyance. It wanted Jace.

"Come on," Jace breathed, his heart hammering against his ribs like a trapped bird. "Come and get it."

From the high rocks, the Highborn boy watched, his arms crossed. "Fool," he muttered. "He's wasting energy. Let him die."

But Jace wasn't planning on dying. He was planning on doing the one thing the Highborns had never learned to do.

He was going to fight dirty.

***

The Silt-Reaver lunged, displacing a wave of black sludge.

**[WARNING: LEVEL 4 HOSTILE DETECTED]**
**[AGILITY CHECK: PENDING...]**

A blue box flashed directly in the center of Jace’s vision, obscuring the monster’s jaws.

"Get out of the way!" Jace yelled, not at the girl, but at the floating text. He swiped his hand through the hologram, shattering the pixels just in time to see the serrated teeth snapping inches from his nose.

He threw himself backward. The mud sucked at his legs, acting like an anchor. He didn't have the strength to jump, so he simply collapsed, letting gravity take him. The Reaver’s jaws snapped shut on empty air where his chest had been a split second before.

Jace hit the water, the foul taste filling his mouth. He scrambled on all fours, crab-walking sideways as the beast thrashed, trying to reorient itself.

**[COMBAT TUTORIAL: WOULD YOU LIKE TO ENABLE AUTO-ASSIST?]**
**[YES / NO]**

"No! Shut up!" Jace gagged, spitting out muck. The Highborn kids on the rocks were probably using Auto-Assist—a ghostly overlay that showed trajectory lines and weak points. Jace didn't know that. He just knew the blue light was blinding him in the darkness.

The Reaver recovered faster than he did. It whipped its tail, catching Jace in the ribs.

*Crack.*

The wind left him. The pain was blinding, white and hot.

**[HP: 28/40]**
**[STATUS EFFECT: BRUISED RIBS (-10% Agility)]**

Jace wheezed, clutching his side. The Reaver hissed, sensing weakness. It stalked forward, its many legs churning the mud. It was slow now, confident. It was playing with its food.

Jace gripped the rusted rebar. His hands were shaking. Not from fear—well, mostly from fear—but from the adrenaline dump. He looked at the monster. Thick chitin on the back. Harder than stone. The underbelly was submerged. The eyes were small, beaded targets.

But the mouth...

The beast opened its jaws again, letting out a rattling hiss. Inside, the flesh was pink, soft, and wet.

*Bait it,* his instincts whispered. *Like the rats in the vents. Give them something to bite that isn't you.*

Jace stopped moving. He stood up, shaking, in the knee-deep water. He lowered the rebar, hiding it behind his leg. He looked small. Defeated.

The Reaver took the bait. It surged forward for the kill shot, jaws wide to swallow him whole.

Jace didn't try to dodge this time. He stepped *into* the lunge.

As the top jaw came down, Jace threw his left arm up—not to block, but to feed it. The teeth clamped down on his forearm.

**[HP: 15/40]**
**[CRITICAL DAMAGE DETECTED]**

Jace screamed, a raw, animal sound, but he didn't pull away. The pain was absolute, grounding him in the moment. With the beast’s jaw clamped on his left arm, its head was locked in place. It couldn't thrash.

"Got you," Jace snarled through gritted teeth.

He drove the rusted rebar upward with his right hand, putting every ounce of his meager 4 Strength into the thrust. He didn't aim for the shell. He aimed for the soft palate, the roof of the mouth, driving the metal straight up into the brain.

*Squelch.*

The beast stiffened. It tried to scream, but only gurgled.

Jace didn't let go. He twisted the rebar. "Die!"

The Reaver convulsed once, twice, and then went limp, its heavy bulk dragging Jace down into the water.

Jace yanked his bleeding arm free from the teeth and fell back against a mossy stone, gasping for air. The silence that followed was deafening. Even the Highborn on the rocks had stopped talking.

Then, the System exploded into life.

**[TARGET ELIMINATED: JUVENILE SILT-REAVER (LVL 4)]**

**[CALCULATING MERIT...]**
*   **Base XP:** 40
*   **Solo Kill Bonus:** x2
*   **Level Disparity (Lvl 1 vs Lvl 4):** x4
*   **Equipment Quality (Trash):** x1.5

**[TOTAL XP GAINED: 480]**
**[LEVEL UP!]**
**[LEVEL UP!]**

Jace blinked, his vision swimming. The pain in his arm was throbbing in time with the pulsing notifications.

**[SYSTEM ANALYSIS: ANOMALY]**
**[User refused Tutorial Assistance.]**
**[User sacrificed limb to secure kill.]**
**[User utilized environmental debris.]**

**[ACQUIRING SKILL...]**
**[New Skill Created: Gutter-Fighting (Passive)]**
*Description: You fight with zero regard for form or fairness. The System acknowledges that survival is the only rule.*
*Effect: +10% Damage when using Improvised Weapons. +5% Critical Hit chance when injured below 50% HP.*

**[ACQUIRING ATTRIBUTE...]**
**[Bravery (Hidden Stat) Unlocked: +1]**
*You stood when others ran.*

Jace stared at the floating text. He felt different. The exhaustion was still there, but a sudden warmth washed over him—the sensation of a Level Up. The worst of the pain in his arm dulled to a manageable ache as his HP bar refilled slightly with the level increase.

He looked over at the girl he had saved. She was still cowering behind a rock, staring at him like he was a monster.

"Check the body," Jace rasped, his voice hoarse.

"W-what?" she stammered.

"The monster," Jace said, using the rebar to prop himself up. "The tutorials... my dad told me once. Monsters have cores. Or loot. Check it."

"I... I can't," she wept.

Jace scoffed. He limped over to the dead Reaver. He didn't have a knife, so he used the rebar to pry open the shattered skull. Inside, amidst the gore, was a small, dull grey pebble.

**[Lesser Beast Core (Grey)]**
*Value: 5 Merit Points.*

Five points. A loaf of stale bread cost 2 points in the slums. He had just earned two and a half loaves of bread in thirty seconds of near-death terror.

He pocketed the stone. He looked up at the Highborn group on the dry rocks. The leader, the blond boy, was looking down at him. There was no respect in his eyes, only calculation.

"You're bleeding, Dreg," the blond boy called out. "Blood attracts the Swarm. Get away from our perimeter."

Jace looked at his dripping arm. The boy was right.

He looked at the chubby girl. "If you stay here, they won't let you up on the rocks. And if you stay in the water, you die."

Jace turned and began wading toward a dense tangle of mangrove roots in the distance. It looked dark and tight—uncomfortable for a group, but perfect for a rat.

"Come on," he muttered to the girl, not looking back. "Try not to splash."
