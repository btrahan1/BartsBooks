# Chapter 17: The Amalgam

The exit to Floor 4 was a grand archway of polished obsidian, but it was completely blocked. The thing standing in front of it was a grotesque mockery of a man, fifteen feet tall and built from the Sepulcher itself.

Its body was a misshapen torso of cracked sarcophagi. One arm was a colossal tombstone, held like a tower shield. The other was a writhing cluster of rusted iron spikes and sharpened femurs. Skulls of varying sizes were embedded in its chest and shoulders, and where its head should be, a single, eerie green lantern—a grave-light—burned with cold fire. It stood motionless, a silent, unholy guardian.

Jace instinctively took a step forward, his knuckles white around his rebar. This thing was big. It was ugly. And his first, primal instinct was to charge it. But that was the old Jace.

He stopped, took a breath, and turned his head slightly.

"Bella... tell me."

The two words were an order, a plea, and an expression of absolute faith.

Bella was already at work, her eyes glowing faintly. She was crouched behind a half-crumbled wall, her focus absolute as she processed the flood of information the System was feeding her.

**[Identify: Active]**
**[Mausoleum Amalgam (Lvl 12 - Floor Guardian)]**
**[Type: Golem/Undead]**
**[Armor: Extremely High]**
**[Speed: Very Slow]**
**[Weakness: The Grave-Light Core in its chest is the source of its animation. The Core is heavily protected by the Tombstone Shield.]**
**[Special Ability: [Grave Reconstruction] - Absorbs nearby stone and bone debris to regenerate severe damage.]**

"Okay," Bella whispered, her mind racing. She then activated her other skill.

**[Tactical Survey: Active]**

The battlefield lit up in her vision. The Amalgam was a huge red threat. But so were the two massive, crumbling pillars on either side of the chamber. A section of the floor near the back wall, directly over a deep burial shaft, glowed with an even more intense, critical red.

"We can't break it," Bella said, her voice low and steady. "Not head-on. That tombstone arm is its shield. We hit it, we'll just break our weapons. But its own strength is our weapon."

She pointed to the pillar on the left. "That pillar is unstable. Jace, I need you to make it angry. Use [Goad]. Get it to swing that massive tombstone arm at you, but stand right in front of that pillar."

Jace looked from the pillar to the monster. "So I play matador, and it hits the pillar instead of me?"

"Exactly," Bella confirmed. "Its own strength will bring the ceiling down on its arm, crushing the shield. That should expose the Grave-Light."

"And then we just hit the light?"

"Then it gets tricky," Bella warned. "It can heal. It'll start pulling pieces of the walls to rebuild itself. We won't have much time. Once the core is exposed, we both hit it with everything we've got. Slings, rebar, whatever we can."

She took a deep breath. "And if that's not enough... there's a weak spot in the floor over there." She pointed to the glowing red patch. "If we can lure it onto that spot... we can end this the same way we ended the beetle."

Jace processed the plan. It was elegant, brutal, and relied on perfect timing. He gave a slow, predatory grin.

"So, Phase One: Break the arm. Phase Two: Break the core. Phase Three: Break the floor." He hefted his rebar, the weight familiar and comforting in his hand. "Tell me where to stand."

Bella pointed to a specific flagstone a few feet in front of the crumbling pillar. "Right there. Don't let it push you back."

Jace nodded and stepped out into the open, the scrape of his boot on stone echoing in the silent chamber. The Amalgam's head, the eerie green lantern, swiveled to face him.

"Alright, you ugly pile of junk," Jace shouted, focusing his will. "Let's dance."

The plan worked with terrifying perfection.

Jace’s taunts, amplified by the magical compulsion of [Goad], sent the Mausoleum Amalgam into a lumbering, single-minded rage. The green Grave-Light in its chest flared, and it swung its colossal tombstone arm in a crushing arc meant to obliterate the annoying Dreg.

Jace, light on his feet, dove aside at the last possible second.

The sound was apocalyptic. A deep, grinding **CRUUUNCH** echoed through the Sepulcher as the tombstone slammed into the crumbling pillar. The pillar didn't just crack; it exploded. Tons of rock and debris rained down, burying the golem's shield arm in an avalanche of its own making.

"Phase Two!" Jace roared, scrambling to his feet.

The Amalgam was stunned, its arm trapped. The Grave-Light, its core and its only eye, was now a wide-open, stationary target.

Jace and Bella unleashed a volley of stones from their slings. The pebbles, which would have shattered uselessly against the shield, now *tinked* and *pinged* against the green lantern, creating small, spidery cracks in its surface.

But the golem was already healing. It began to tug its arm free, the stones of the floor groaning as its [Grave Reconstruction] ability tried to pull the rubble back into itself.

"Not fast enough!" Jace snarled. He knew they needed one big, decisive hit.

He charged. Using a jutting sarcophagus lid on the golem's leg as a springboard, he leaped into the air, driving his rusted rebar forward like a spear. He aimed for the largest crack in the lantern.

The impact was a sickening crunch of piercing metal and shattering magic. Jace plunged the rebar deep into the Grave-Light, and the green energy within arced and spat violently. The golem let out a silent, psychic roar of pure agony.

Jace tried to pull his weapon free, but it was stuck fast, wedged deep in the core. The golem, its rage now absolute, reached up with its free, spiky arm. It ignored Jace, who was now clinging to its chest. Its entire focus was on the object causing it such immense pain.

With a horrendous screech of metal on metal, it gripped the rebar and ripped it from its own "eye." Green light bled from the wound, but the golem wasn't dead yet. It was furious.

And then it made a mistake.

Its Grave-Light swiveled, its hateful attention shifting from the boy on its chest to the girl in the distance—the one who had been directing the assault, the source of the plan. The Analyst.

It saw her as the true threat.

With a surge of supernatural strength, the Mausoleum Amalgam drew its arm back and hurled the rebar. The rusty metal spike became a deadly missile, a green-tinged blur of vengeance aimed directly at Bella.

*Big mistake.*

Bella didn't flinch. She didn't run. This was the exact scenario she had trained for, the one Jace had bruised her for. The world seemed to slow down as her mind processed the trajectory.

She planted her feet, pouring every last dreg of her mana into a single, defiant point in the air before her.

A hexagonal shield of pure, silver light shimmered into existence. It wasn't the small, flimsy barrier she had first created. This one was solid, bright, and curved with perfect, lethal geometry.

**[Riposte Barrier: Active]**

The rebar hit the shield with a deafening **CLANG** that echoed like a blacksmith's hammer striking the soul of a bell.

But the rebar didn't stop. It didn't fall. It struck the concave surface of the shield and, with all the furious momentum the golem had given it, was instantly sent flying back along its exact original path.

The Amalgam had no time to process what had happened. It had just thrown its weapon.

The reflected rebar, now a silver-and-green missile, slammed directly back into the gaping, bleeding wound in its Grave-Light core.

There was no explosion. Just a final, sharp *CRACK*.

The green light in the lantern didn't just go out. It shattered into a million tiny shards of emerald dust. The animating magic was gone.

The Mausoleum Amalgam froze for a single, silent second. Then, it simply crumbled. The sarcophagi split, the bones clattered to the floor, and the tombstone arm fell with a ground-shaking thud. In moments, the terrifying guardian was nothing more than a pile of inert rubble and a single, dark, shattered lantern.

Silence returned to the Sepulcher. Jace slid down the pile of debris, landing beside the dark lantern. He looked across the room at Bella, who was leaning against a pillar, her legs trembling, her mana bar completely empty.

They had won. And they didn't have to break the floor. The boss had broken itself.
