# Chapter 35: Floor 8 and the Store

The portal to Floor 8 opened onto a landscape of stark, desolate beauty. They stood on a vast plain of polished black obsidian, crisscrossed with fissures that glowed with a soft, internal cherry-red light. The air was thin and cool, and the sky was a permanent, starless twilight.

As they took their first steps onto the volcanic glass, Bella suddenly stopped, her head tilted. "Jace... Carol... do you see that?"

In the distance, a solitary, glowing structure pulsed with a gentle, welcoming blue light. It was a small, elegant pavilion, seemingly made of pure energy, an oasis of calm in the stark landscape.

"Is that a store?" Carol asked, her voice filled with a hopeful reverence.

Bella was already using her skill. "It is," she confirmed, a note of relief in her voice. "An active System Store."

Jace let out a breath he didn't realize he'd been holding. *Lord be praised.* After the relentless teasing and his own mortifying embarrassment on the library floor, a moment of quiet, productive transaction was exactly what he needed.

As they approached the pavilion, he watched the two girls, their faces lit by the blue glow of the store. They were animated, already discussing potential upgrades for their armor and sharpening oils for Carol's blade. He felt a now-familiar pang in his chest. A mix of pride, affection, and a lingering, potent guilt.

*The ladies deserved some gifts,* he thought, the idea solidifying with a grim sense of purpose. This was his chance. *What could he buy to make amends?*

They cashed in their loot from the last two floors, their Merit Point balance soaring to a healthy 2,500. While Bella and Carol were excitedly browsing the `Armor` and `Materials` sections, Jace quietly opened his own menu, his focus sharp and targeted. He wasn't looking for himself. He was looking for *them*.

He found Bella's first. It was perfect. Not flashy, but a force multiplier for her greatest asset: her mind.

**[Item: Scholar's Periapt (Rare)]**
*Description: A silver amulet inlaid with a shard of a Silent Scribe's core. It hums with focused energy, aiding in the rapid processing of information.*
*Effect: -15% Mana cost for all [Identify] and [Tactical Survey] skills. +3 Intelligence.*
**[Cost: 450 MP]**

He bought it without a second thought. Next, he searched for Carol. He needed something that spoke to her new title, something that wasn't just a piece of armor, but a symbol of her courage. He found it in the `Gloves` sub-category.

**[Item: Gauntlets of the Valiant (Rare)]**
*Description: Plated gauntlets that seem to shine brighter when the wearer is facing overwhelming odds. They reward courage with power.*
*Effect: When your [Duel] skill is active, your attack speed is increased by 20%. +2 Strength.*
**[Cost: 500 MP]**

He purchased them, the transaction draining a significant chunk of their funds. Two small, glowing items materialized in his hands: a delicate silver amulet on a thin chain and a pair of beautifully crafted steel gauntlets.

He walked over to where the girls were debating the merits of light leather versus chainmail. He cleared his throat awkwardly. They both turned to look at him.

He didn't make a big speech. He just held the items out.

"Here," he mumbled, not quite meeting their eyes. "For... you know. The team. To... make up for the... nickname."

They stared, speechless, at the offerings. Bella was the first to react, her analytical curiosity overriding her surprise. She took the amulet and immediately used [Identify]. Her eyes widened as she read the description. It was a gift that showed he understood her role perfectly, that he respected and wanted to enhance her greatest strength. A slow, genuine smile touched her lips.

Carol took the gauntlets, her fingers tracing the intricate plating. She, too, identified them. Her breath hitched. The gift wasn't an apology; it was a coronation. It was Jace acknowledging her as "The Valiant," the team's champion.

She looked up at him, and the last of her teasing anger melted away. She smirked, but it was a warm, friendly expression now.

"Trying to buy our forgiveness, Lover Boy?" she asked, her voice soft.

Jace just shrugged, a faint red tinge on his cheeks. "Is it working?"

Carol laughed, a clear, happy sound that echoed across the obsidian plain. "It's a good start, Casanova. A very good start."

Jace let out a slow, relieved breath as the tension finally broke. Carol's laugh was infectious, and even Bella's smile lost its analytical edge and became genuinely warm. He had done it. He had navigated the treacherous minefield of his own stupidity and come out the other side. The gifts were accepted. The nicknames were now terms of endearment. They were a team, stronger and more cohesive than ever.

It was in this perfect, quiet moment of mutual respect and budding friendship that the Spire, with its impeccable and utterly sadistic sense of comedic timing, chose to make its presence known once more.

The ancient, booming voice echoed across the obsidian plains, a psychic shockwave that shattered the peace.

**[ATTENTION!]**

**[TEAM CASANOVA IS THE FIRST TO REACH FLOOR 8!]**

The broadcast was, if possible, even louder and more definitive than the last one.

The laughter died in Carol's throat. Her eyes, which had been sparkling with amusement a second before, flew wide with a look of pure, unadulterated horror. She stared at Jace, then at the new gauntlets in her hands, as if they had somehow betrayed her.

Bella's warm smile didn't just vanish; it inverted. Her eyes, which had been soft and forgiving, narrowed into razor-sharp slits of cold, calculating fury. She looked from Jace to the Scholar's Periapt hanging around her neck, a muscle in her jaw twitching.

"Oh, really?" Bella’s voice was a soft, deadly whisper, colder than the starless twilight sky. The analytical genius was gone, replaced by something far more terrifying. She was no longer processing data; she was pronouncing a sentence.

Jace froze, the color draining from his face. He stared at the two girls, his mind a frantic, screaming void. He hadn't said a word. He hadn't even *thought* it. The System... it had taken Carol's forgiving, teasing nickname and blasted it across the entire tower as their official team name.

He held up his hands, palms out, in a universal gesture of complete and utter surrender.

"I didn't do it," he pleaded, his voice a strangled squeak. "I swear on my mace, I didn't do it! It wasn't me this time!"

Carol just pointed a trembling, accusing finger at him. "You are a curse, Jace! A walking, talking, reputation-destroying curse!"

Bella just took a slow, deliberate step forward, cracking her knuckles with a sound that was unnervingly loud in the sudden silence.

Jace did the only thing a Sentinel faced with two furious, magically-enhanced women could do. He abandoned all pretense of courage and strategy.

He turned and ran. Again. The legend of the Forerunner, the terror of Floor 4, the hero of the Redeemed, was once again sprinting for his life across the desolate plains of a new and hostile world.

The chase across the obsidian plain was frantic and utterly silent, save for the pounding of their boots on the volcanic glass. Jace ran with the desperation of a man who knew he was doomed, his powerful Sentinel build unfortunately not optimized for outrunning two highly agile, highly motivated women.

He risked a glance over his shoulder. They were gaining. Their faces were still set in masks of cold fury. He pushed harder, his lungs burning in the thin, cool air. In his panic, his foot caught on an unseen fissure in the glass. He went down, not in a graceful roll, but in a sprawling, undignified heap, skidding across the sharp ground.

He scrambled to his knees, covered in fine black dust, and turned to face his fate.

But the killing blow never came.

Carol, who had been running on pure, righteous anger, saw him there. The legendary Forerunner, the fearsome Mr. Dreg, the newly-christened Casanova, was kneeling in the dirt looking like a panicked squirrel that had just fallen out of a tree. A strange, unfamiliar sound escaped her lips. A snort.

It was followed by a choked giggle.

Bella, still radiating a glacial fury, heard the sound and faltered in her stride. Carol's giggle was infectious, a ridiculous sound in the desolate landscape. It was the first crack in the icy wall of her anger. She looked at Jace, truly looked at him, and saw not the source of her humiliation, but a terrified, dust-covered idiot who had the worst luck in the entire Spire.

The sheer, cosmic absurdity of their situation finally hit her. The Spire, their personal god and tormentor, was actively trolling them. It was taking their smallest, stupidest moments and broadcasting them as legendary deeds. Her cold fury didn't just break; it shattered into a million pieces. A small, reluctant laugh escaped her, then another, until she was leaning against a glowing fissure, tears of laughter streaming down her face.

Jace stared at them, utterly bewildered. The threat was gone, replaced by a sound he hadn't expected: mirth. The energy drained out of him, and he just slumped onto the obsidian floor, his head in his hands.

By the time they caught up to Jace, they were all laughing.

It was a deep, cleansing, and much-needed release of all the tension and fear they had been carrying.

"Look at him," Carol finally managed to gasp out between peals of laughter. "Our great 'Casanova'... brought down by a crack in the floor."

"System analysis complete," Bella added, wiping a tear from her eye, her voice still trembling with amusement. "The name is... catastrophically stupid." She paused, then a wicked, analytical glint returned to her eyes. "However, it seems to have a 100% success rate at making us famous and infuriating our enemies. From a purely logical standpoint... we should probably keep it."

Jace just groaned, his face still in his hands. He looked up, his expression a mixture of profound relief and utter defeat. "Fine," he grumbled, but a smile was finally breaking through. "Fine. We're Team Casanova."

He looked at the two of them, laughing together under the starless twilight sky, and realized that for all the System's trolling, it had accidentally given them something precious. A shared, ridiculous identity. It was a nice bonding moment.
