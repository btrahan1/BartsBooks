# Chapter 36: The Corbin Fight

The laughter faded, replaced by a comfortable, shared silence as they continued their trek across the obsidian plains. The bonding moment, however brief, had solidified them. They were no longer just three survivors. They were a team, forged in battle and now tempered by shared, cosmic absurdity. They were Team Casanova.

It was then that they saw the figures blocking the path ahead. There were four of them, clad in sleek, dark armor that seemed to absorb the faint light of the fissures. They stood with an arrogant, practiced ease, their weapons—long, cruel-looking polearms—held at the ready.

At their lead was a tall, powerfully built boy with a cold sneer on his face. He took a step forward, his eyes scanning them with a predatory gaze.

"Well, well, well... what have we here?" His voice was a low, arrogant drawl. He wasn't from the Induction. He was older, his face hardened with the confidence of someone who had survived for a long time. His gaze settled on Carol, a flicker of cruel recognition in his eyes.

"I have heard of you," he said, his sneer widening. "Carol the Coward." He said the name like a curse, deliberately ignoring her new, server-wide title of 'The Valiant'. "Left your team to die. No wonder you teamed up with these misfits." He gestured dismissively at Jace and Bella. "Heard about you, too. 'Team Casanova'. What a joke."

He took another step forward, his polearm held loosely, not bothering to analyze them at all. His confidence was absolute. He was a higher level, better geared, and in his mind, from a superior generation of climbers.

"Well, no fancy tricks and cowardly actions will save you this time," he declared, his team fanning out, their polearms creating an impassable wall of sharp steel.

Bella and Jace instinctively moved into a defensive formation, ready for a brutal, head-on fight.

But Carol held up a hand, stopping them. She looked at the arrogant boy, and her face, which had been so full of newfound confidence, seemed to... crumble. Her shoulders slumped. The fire in her eyes dimmed. Her Heartwood blade drooped, its tip scraping against the obsidian floor. She looked, for all the world, like the broken, terrified girl they had found on Floor 5.

"Please," Carol whispered, her voice a pathetic, trembling plea. "Don't... don't hurt me. I'll... I'll do whatever you want."

Jace and Bella exchanged a look of pure confusion. *What is she doing?*

The lead boy laughed, a harsh, grating sound. "See? Pathetic. A coward is always a coward. Get her. We'll take her gear first."

Two of his cronies stepped forward, their polearms lowered to prod her.

Carol let out a small, terrified squeak and stumbled backward, tripping over her own feet and falling in a heap. It was a perfect, pitiful display of utter incompetence. The two cronies smirked, sharing a look of contemptuous victory. They stepped closer, their guards completely down.

And that's when the trap sprang.

The moment they were within ten feet of her, Carol's entire demeanor shifted. The fear vanished, replaced by a cold, predatory light in her eyes. The slumped shoulders squared. The trembling stopped.

"NOW!" she roared.

She didn't get up. She swept her leg out in a low arc, knocking the feet out from under the closest attacker. At the same moment, she activated her new skill, her [Valiant] title glowing with a brilliant, holy light.

**[Duel: Active!]**

She pointed her sword not at the man she had just tripped, but at the leader. A golden thread of light connected them, and the leader's sneer turned into a look of stunned confusion as the System forced his attention onto her.

Jace and Bella, who had been watching in stunned silence, finally understood. This wasn't a retreat into fear. It was a weaponization of it. Carol was faking being a coward and using her old reputation to her advantage.

The moment the [Duel] activated, Jace charged, his mace a blur aimed at the *other* two climbers who were momentarily forgotten. Bella's sling was already singing, her stones aimed not to kill, but to distract, to harry, to break their formation. JJ was a shadow, zipping past the chaos to bite at the heels of the man Carol had dueled.

The battle devolved into instant, perfect chaos. The leader, forced by the Duel to focus on a cowering girl who was suddenly a whirlwind of aggressive defense, was completely wrong-footed. His two cronies, suddenly beset by a raging Sentinel and a tactical Analyst, were thrown into disarray. And the one on the floor was still trying to figure out how he'd been so thoroughly duped.

Their perfect, arrogant formation had been shattered in less than three seconds, not by a fancy trick, but by a masterful, psychological gambit. Carol wasn't a coward anymore. She was something far more dangerous: a warrior who knew how to think like one.

The leader, whose name they would later learn was Corbin, was completely baffled. The [Duel] skill was a psychic vise, forcing his attention, his very combat instincts, onto the girl he had just dismissed as pathetic. But she wasn't pathetic.

She fought with skill. She fought with purpose.

She didn't try to match his strength. She was a ghost, a blur of motion, her Heartwood blade a flickering green light. She used her old skills—[Evasion], [Feint], [Disengage]—not to run, but to create openings. She'd feint an attack to his left, and as he moved to block, she'd already be on his right, the flat of her blade smacking against the back of his knee.

"What's wrong?" she taunted, her voice no longer trembling, but a cold, sharp blade of steel. "Can't even beat a coward?"

Corbin roared in frustration and swung his polearm in a wide, decapitating arc. Carol dropped, sliding under the attack, and came up inside his guard, her shield slamming into his ribs. The blow had no real power, but it broke his stance, leaving him open. She didn't press the attack; she just created distance again, a confident, predatory smirk on her face.

She was fighting a battle of a thousand cuts, not with her sword, but with his own pride. Every missed swing, every failed attack, every taunt that hit its mark was another small wound to his ego.

Meanwhile, the rest of his team was being systematically dismantled. Jace was a walking nightmare, his [Aura of Dread] sowing confusion while his mace shattered armor and bone. The crony Carol had tripped was still on the ground, being relentlessly harried by a sparkling rat he couldn't seem to hit. The other two were being picked apart by Bella's tactical genius, her sling stones seeming to come from every direction at once, forcing them to constantly turn, never letting them set their feet.

Corbin knew he was losing. He needed to break the [Duel]. He gathered his energy for a powerful, area-of-effect skill, a concussive blast designed to knock back all nearby enemies.

But Carol saw it coming. The tell-tale shimmer of mana gathering around his hands. She didn't try to stop it. She used it.

Just as he was about to unleash the blast, she did something new. She channeled her own mana, not into her sword, but into her title. The word 'Valiant' seemed to glow on her soul.

**[User Intent Analyzed: To Rebuke an Enemy's Arrogance.]**
**[Synergy Detected: [Duel] Skill + [The Valiant] Title]**
**[New Skill Created: Valiant's Decree (Active)]**
*Description: You issue a challenge that cannot be ignored. For the duration of a [Duel], your target is unable to use area-of-effect abilities or switch targets. All their focus must be on you.*

Corbin's concussive blast fizzled, the mana dissipating harmlessly. A look of pure, uncomprehending shock spread across his face. He was trapped. He was locked in a one-on-one fight with a "coward" who was now dictating the very terms of their reality.

The fight was over a minute later. His team was broken, disarmed, and huddled in a defeated heap under Jace's watchful gaze.

Corbin stood alone, his polearm held in a trembling grip, his face a mask of sweat and disbelief. Carol walked calmly toward him, her Heartwood blade held loosely at her side. She stopped a few feet away, her expression not one of anger, but of cold, hard-won wisdom.

"Next time," she said, her voice ringing with the authority of a victor, "you should do an [Identify], fool."

She turned her back on him and walked away, the duel, and the battle, won not by a final, killing blow, but by a single, irrefutable truth: she was no longer the person he thought she was.
