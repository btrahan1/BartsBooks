# Chapter 39: The Plan for Floor 12

They gathered around a table in the corner of the bustling tavern, the "Laughing Hydra," the air thick with the smell of ale and roasted meat. The initial awe of the city had worn off, replaced by a focused, professional determination. They were here to work.

Jace laid his Gauntlets of the Valiant on the rough-hewn wooden table, the sound a dull thud that drew their attention. He looked at Bella, then at Carol, his gaze intense and serious under the dim tavern light.

"Here's the plan," he said, his voice a low, commanding rumble that cut through the surrounding noise. "We clear this level—Floor 12—all the way up to the final Boss. But we don't engage."

He paused, letting the unconventional idea sink in.

"We find the boss room," he continued, "and we do what Bella does best. We analyze. We use [Identify] from a distance. We use [Tactical Survey] to map the entire arena. We learn its weaknesses, its attack patterns, its environmental traps. We gather every single piece of data we can. Then, we come back here."

He tapped the table for emphasis. "We use the Merit we've earned from clearing the floor to upgrade our weapons. We buy the right potions. We craft the right gear. Then... we go back up, clear the level again on a speed run for extra XP, and we walk into that boss room knowing everything. And we teach it who the real bosses are."

It was a brilliant strategy. Cautious, intelligent, and brutally efficient. It minimized risk and maximized their advantage. It was a plan that leveraged Bella's analytical genius and their new ability to use Floor 11 as a home base. It was the plan of a veteran team, not a trio of fresh-faced newcomers.

Bella's eyes lit up, the sheer, beautiful logic of the plan appealing to the very core of her being. "Perfect," she breathed. "A reconnaissance run followed by a targeted extermination. We'll have a complete threat assessment before we ever risk a single point of HP."

Carol leaned back in her chair, a slow, predatory grin spreading across her face. "So we're going to punch it in the face, run away, and then come back and punch it even harder?" she asked, perfectly summarizing the plan in her own, more direct terms. "I love it."

Jace nodded, a grim smile on his face. "Get some rest. Eat up," he said, pushing a plate of food toward Carol. "Tomorrow, we go to work."

The portal to Floor 12 opened into a howling, windswept expanse of floating islands, connected by shimmering, unstable bridges of pure energy. The "Sky-Reaches of the Tempest" was a vertical battleground, where a single misstep meant a thousand-foot fall into the swirling storm below.

The floor was a bit harder than they thought it would be.

The "trash" mobs, as Jace had called them, were vicious packs of **Gale-Feathered Harpies**, agile creatures that could attack from any angle, their shrieks disorienting and their claws sharp as razors. There was a pretty big difference between these intelligent, coordinated hunters and the lumbering beasts of the lower floors.

But Team Casanova was different, too.

Their new Warden's Resolve armor paid itself off in spades. The Harpies' claws, which would have torn through their old leather, screeched and scraped against the solid steel, the damage minimal. Jace's Sentinel class was the perfect anchor in the chaotic, three-dimensional fights. His [Aura of Dread] made the Harpies hesitant to swarm, and his [Gaze of the Warden] could lock down the entire pack, forcing them to focus on his impenetrable shield while Bella and Carol picked them apart.

The fights were grueling, a constant test of their coordination and awareness, but they were never in real danger. They advanced methodically, island by island, their teamwork a well-oiled machine.

After clearing a particularly large island and defeating the Harpy Matriarch, a shower of light and loot rained down. Amidst the beast cores and feathers, a single, leather-bound book fell with a soft thud.

Bella, naturally, identified it first. Her eyes widened. "Jace!"

**[Item: Primer of the Master Leathersmith (Rare)]**
*   **[Type: Recipe Book / Skill Tome]**
*   **[Description: A detailed guide to the advanced techniques of leatherworking, from curing exotic hides to imbuing them with magical properties.]**
*   **[Effect: Grants the user the [Leatherworking] Crafting Skill at Level 3. Unlocks ten rare recipes for leather and hide armor.]**

It was a rare drop, a leathercrafting primer. Sweet!

Jace picked up the book, a feeling of profound satisfaction settling over him. He had been relying on his innate [Jury-Rigging] skill, but this... this was a path to real craftsmanship. He could use all the tough hides and rare monster parts they had been collecting in their new shared Stash. He could create specialized gear, tailored to their exact needs.

"Well," he said, a slow, determined grin spreading across his face as he absorbed the tome, the knowledge flowing into his mind. "Looks like I know what I'm doing when we get back to town."

They now had a new objective for their return trip: not just to upgrade, but to *create*. The Spire had just given them another tool to forge their own destiny.

The town on Floor 11, which the veterans called "Sanctuary," had everything they needed. After their reconnaissance run to the boss gate of Floor 12, they returned not with loot, but with a wealth of priceless data. The next few days were a blur of intense, focused preparation.

They were all gathered in the public workshop, a massive, echoing hall filled with the sounds of industry. Anvils rang, alchemical vials bubbled, and the air was thick with the scent of hot metal, strange herbs, and curing leather. They had decided that they would splurge on two more books they found in the store: one on alchemy for Bella, and one on blacksmithing for Jace.

The workshop proved itself extremely valuable. Jace, covered in soot, was at a forge, hammering a newly smelted ingot of beast-metal into a greave to replace a dented piece of his Warden's armor. His [Jury-Rigging] skill was slowly, through trial and error, morphing into true [Blacksmithing].

Bella was at an alchemy station, her Grimoire propped open beside her. She carefully ground a Harpy feather into a crystalline powder, creating a Potion of Haste. Her [Identify] skill allowed her to see the exact properties of every ingredient, making her a natural [Alchemist].

Carol sat at a large table, poring over Bella’s Grimoire, which was now filled with detailed entries on the Sky-Reaches' monsters. She was cross-referencing the data with her own combat experience, mapping out attack patterns and formulating new dueling strategies.

It was in this den of focused creation that they may have found their healer.

She was working at the station next to Bella's, a delicate setup for weaving and enchanting. She was petite, in a cute way, with a cascade of mousy brown hair that kept falling into her face. She seemed to be in a state of constant, quiet panic, her hands fumbling with a spool of silver thread. Her shyness, Jace noted from the corner of his eye, made her even more cute.

She let out a tiny, frustrated squeak as a small, glowing enchantment rune she was trying to weave into a bandage fizzled out, the silver thread turning to dust. She slumped in her stool, the picture of defeat.

Carol, ever the newly-minted empath, was the first to notice. "Rough day?" she asked, her voice gentle.

The girl jumped, startled, and blushed furiously. "Oh! Um... yes. Sorry. The mana weave is unstable. I'm... not very good at this yet."

Bella looked over, her analytical gaze softening with a hint of sympathy. "The ambient mana in Sanctuary is very dense. You have to adjust your flow rate. Try projecting half of what you think you need."

"Oh," the girl said, her eyes wide. "Th-thank you."

Jace, wiping soot from his brow with the back of his hand, walked over. "Don't worry about it. We're all new to this crafting stuff." He looked at the medical-looking bandages. "You're a healer?"

The girl nodded shyly. "Sort of."

Bella, unable to resist, focused her intent. **[Identify]**.

The information that flowed into her mind made her eyebrows shoot up in surprise. She immediately shared it with the others.

**[Name: Lia]**
**[Level: 12]**
**[Class: Field Chirurgeon]**
**[Title: The Mender]**

**[Class Description: A rare, non-divine healing class focused on the physical manipulation and reallocation of life force. A Field Chirurgeon cannot create new vitality, but can mend wounds with supernatural speed and transfer existing life force between allies.]**

**[Key Skill: [Vitality Transfer] - Target an ally with high HP and channel their life force into a wounded ally. Efficiency is based on the [Intelligence] stat.]**

But her class... that was interesting. Very interesting.

Jace, Carol, and Bella exchanged a silent, instantaneous look through the Nexus Stone.

*Jace: She doesn't make health. She moves it. She could pull from my HP pool to heal you two without me needing to use [Sanguine Offering].*
*Carol: That's... perfect. You're the battery, she's the power cable.*
*Bella: The synergy is optimal. She is the exact force multiplier we identified a need for.*

The shy, clumsy girl fumbling with her bandages wasn't just a healer. She was the missing piece of their perfect, unstoppable team.

Jace, seeing the perfect strategic synergy laid bare before him, immediately shifted from grimy blacksmith to what he imagined was a suave, charming leader. He wiped his hands on a rag, sauntered over to Lia’s station, and leaned against the table with an exaggerated casualness that fooled absolutely no one.

He put on his best smile, a lopsided, slightly sooty grin he probably thought was disarming.

"So," he began, his voice a low, gravelly attempt at smooth. "You come around here often?"

The line, ancient and terrible, hung in the air. Lia just stared at him, her shy confusion deepening into pure, unadulterated bewilderment. JJ, who had been napping in Carol's bag, poked his head out and let out a small, judgmental squeak.

Before Jace could dig himself any deeper, Bella shoved him away with a firm, exasperated push to the shoulder.

"Don't mind him," she said to Lia, her voice a model of calm, collected sanity. "He's an idiot." She shot Jace a look that could have frozen the forge. "His social skills are a work in progress."

She then turned back to Lia, her expression softening into a genuine, welcoming smile. It was a rare sight, and it instantly put the shy girl at ease.

"I'm Bella," she said, gesturing to herself. She then gestured to Carol, who had walked over, a look of profound second-hand embarrassment on her face. "And this is Carol. The loud one is Jace."

Carol offered a friendly, apologetic wave. "Sorry about him. The System gave him a really dumb nickname a few floors back, and it's gone to his head."

Lia blinked, looking between the three of them—the polite, intelligent Analyst, the friendly, confident warrior, and the slightly-singed idiot who had just tried to use the worst pickup line in the history of the Spire. A tiny, hesitant smile touched her lips.

"I'm... Lia," she whispered. "And I... I'm here most days. Trying to level up my enchanting." She looked down at her failed bandage, a fresh wave of embarrassment washing over her. "Not very successfully."

"We're new here," Bella continued, seamlessly taking control of the conversation. "We were just admiring your work. A Field Chirurgeon is a very rare class. Your focus on vitality transfer is... fascinating."

Bella had instantly shifted to a topic Lia was comfortable with: her skills, her work. The shy girl's eyes lit up, just a little.

"Oh! You know what a Chirurgeon is?" she asked, surprised. "Most people just think I'm a bad Cleric."

Jace, recovering from being so thoroughly shut down, watched the interaction with a dawning sense of awe. Bella wasn't just smart; she was brilliant. She was building a rapport, establishing a connection, and recruiting their new healer, all without a single, cringe-worthy pickup line.

He decided then and there to leave the talking to her from now on.

Jace, thoroughly chastened, watched Bella and Carol work their magic. They weren't just talking to Lia; they were including her, drawing her out of her shell with genuine interest and a shared frustration for the difficulties of crafting.

He saw his opening. Not for another disastrous attempt at charm, but for a simple, honest gesture. He quietly slipped away from the workshop and headed for the plaza. A few minutes later, he returned, a small, wax-paper bag in his hand.

He walked up to the group, deliberately avoiding the swagger of his previous approach. He stopped beside Lia, who flinched slightly, expecting another terrible line.

Instead, Jace just looked at her, his expression surprisingly sincere, a light dusting of soot still on his cheek.

"Sorry about that," he said, his voice quiet and devoid of any bravado. "I am an idiot. No doubt."

He held out the small bag. "Would you like a pastry?"

Lia stared at the offering, bewildered. The bag smelled warm, of cinnamon and sweet bread. It wasn't a strategic move or a charming ploy. It was just... nice.

Bella and Carol exchanged a look of stunned surprise. Their idiot was learning.

Lia hesitantly reached out and took the bag. She peeked inside. A warm, freshly baked cinnamon roll, still sticky with icing, sat nestled within. It was a simple, comforting luxury.

"Thank you," she whispered, a genuine, shy smile finally breaking through her nervousness. It was the first time they had seen her truly smile, and it was a brighter light than any of the forges in the workshop.

Jace just gave a small, almost imperceptible nod and stepped back, letting the girls continue their conversation. He had offered a truce, an apology, and a snack, all in one. He had learned his lesson: sometimes, the most effective strategy wasn't a grand, sweeping gesture, but a simple, quiet act of kindness. And a well-timed pastry.

The pastry worked. Over the next hour, Lia slowly opened up, her shyness receding in the face of Bella’s calm intelligence and Carol’s easy-going confidence. They learned she’d been in Sanctuary for months, having made it to Floor 11 with a party that had since disbanded, leaving her too timid to seek out a new one.

The proposition was inevitable. Bella, with Jace and Carol's silent, psionic agreement, laid out the plan.

"Consider it a trial run," Bella said, her tone professional but kind. "We're heading back to Floor 12 to test our new gear. We'd like you to come with us. We'll take on a few mobs... you do your health transfer thing when you think best. Jace will do his 'smoldering self-heal' when Carol pulls aggro to top himself back off."

Lia’s eyes went wide with a familiar panic. The words Bella used were a foreign language to her.

"'Self-heal'?" she stammered. "'Aggro?' Uhm... I usually just do 'down time healing,' after the fight is over. And I use myself as the battery. You're sure I can heal in battle? What if I mess up?"

Jace stepped forward, his expression patient. "Aggro is just... who the monster is angry at. Usually me," he explained simply. "And my self-heal," he tapped his chest, "is my [Internal Furnace]. I regenerate health slowly all the time. But I can also transfer my health directly to you or Carol using [Sanguine Offering]. The problem is, that costs me a lot of health."

He looked at Lia, his meaning clear. "You're the bridge. You can use your [Vitality Transfer] to pull health from my large, constantly regenerating pool and give it to Carol or Bella when they get hit. It's more efficient. And it keeps me in the fight."

The concept was terrifying to her. Healing on the fly, managing multiple health pools in the middle of a chaotic battle...

Carol placed a reassuring hand on Lia’s shoulder. "Hey. It's okay. The first time I fought with these two, I fell on my face and nearly got eaten. This is a trial run, not a final exam. We've got your back."

Lia looked at the three of them. The terrifyingly intense Sentinel, the brilliant, calm Analyst, and the fierce, smiling Valiant. They weren't just asking her to be a healer. They were offering her a role, a strategy, a team.

She took a deep, shaky breath, clutching the half-eaten pastry in her hand like a talisman.

"Okay," she whispered, her voice barely audible but firm with a newfound resolve. "I'll... I'll try."
