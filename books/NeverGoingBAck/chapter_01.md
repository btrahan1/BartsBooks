# Chapter 1: Release Day

The sound of the gate buzzing was the first thing Pedro Rodriguez would never forget. For five years, that sound meant lockup. Head count. Lights out. It was the sound of his life being paused.

Today, it was the sound of it hitting play again.

"Rodriguez," the guard said, not unkindly but without warmth. "Step through. Don't look back."

Pedro didn't. He gripped the handle of the clear plastic bag that held his worldly possessions: a cheap pair of jeans that were tighter than he remembered, a grey t-shirt, and forty-two dollars in cash. Five years for a '67 Mustang that wasn't his. A joyride that turned into a felony when he panicked and crossed state lines.

He stepped out into the Texas sun. It was brighter out here. Unfiltered by the mesh wire that covered the rec yard. It hit his face like a physical weight, hot and blinding.

He took a breath. It smelled like asphalt and dry grass. It smelled like choices.

The bus stop was a hundred yards down the road. A simple metal bench baking in the heat. Pedro began to walk, his prison-issue shoes crunching on the gravel shoulder. He was thirty-two years old, but he felt like a newborn, blinking against the glare, unsure if his legs would carry him all the way to the city.

One foot in front of the other. That was the rule inside. It had to be the rule outside, too.

He sat on the bench, the metal burning through his thin jeans. He didn't mind the pain. It was real. It was different than the dull ache of the cot.

As he waited for the Greyhound that would take him back to San Antonio, he made a mental list. Not a written one—paper could be found, paper could be taken. A list in his head, burned in deep.

*Things I want:*
A shower without shower shoes.
A steak, medium rare, with no gristle.
To walk into a room and not have to scan for the exits or the biggest guy.
To pay rent. It sounded stupid, but he wanted the dignity of a bill with his name on it.

*Things I don't want:*
To see Hector.
To touch a steering wheel. Not yet. Maybe not ever.
To feel that rush, that electric spike in his chest when the engine turned over on a car that didn't belong to him. That rush was a lie. That rush cost five years.

He looked at his hands. Calloused, scarred from the prison laundry detail. They were strong hands, but they were idle now. He clenched them into fists, then released them, spreading his fingers wide against his knees.

"Never again," he whispered to the empty road.

A cloud of dust on the horizon signaled the bus. Pedro stood up. He picked up his plastic bag. He checked his reflection in the plastic: gaunt, short hair, eyes that had seen too much grey.

He wasn't the kid who stole the Mustang anymore. He just hoped the world would let him be the man he was trying to become.
