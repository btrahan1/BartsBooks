# Chapter 2: The Return

The Greyhound smelled of stale coffee and diesel fumes. It was a smell Pedro used to associate with escape, with the open road. Now, it just smelled like transition. Like the waiting room between two lives.

He found a window seat near the back. He kept his bag on his lap, his arms crossed over it like a shield. The bus wasn't full—a young mother with a sleeping toddler three rows up, a few students with headphones, and a man across the aisle who looked like he had been carved out of old leather.

The engine rumbled to life, a deep vibration that traveled up through the floorboards and into Pedro's boots. As the bus pulled away from the station, leaving the prison in the dust, Pedro didn't look back. He kept his eyes on the horizon.

"Just got out, huh?"

The voice was gravelly, worn smooth by time. Pedro stiffened, his eyes snapping to the man across the aisle. The old man was watching him, not with judgment, but with a calm, knowing curiosity. He wore a faded trucker hat and a plaid shirt that had seen better decades.

Pedro didn't answer immediately. Inside, you didn't answer questions. You deflected. You assessed. But he wasn't inside anymore.

"Is it that obvious?" Pedro asked, his voice rough. He hadn't spoken much in the last few days.

The old man chuckled, a dry sound like boots on dry leaves. "The haircut gives it away, son. High and tight aside. And the clothes. They fit you like they were made for someone else."

Pedro looked down at his jeans. "Yeah. Maybe."

The countryside rolled by—scrub brush, barbed wire fences, the endless Texas sky. It was big. Too big.

"You got a look about you, though," the old man said after a mile or so. He leaned slightly into the aisle. "Most guys fresh out, they got that 'deer in the headlights' look. Scared. Or angry. Like they want to fight the world for what it took from them."

He paused, studying Pedro's profile.

"You don't look scared. You look determined. What's on your mind, son?"

Pedro tightened his grip on the plastic bag. He thought about the list in his head. The shower. The steak. The rent. The *never again*.

"Focus," Pedro said quietly. "Just trying to stay in my lane."

"Staying in the lane is good," the old man nodded. "Harder than it looks, though. Especially when the road gets curvy."

"I drove off the road once," Pedro said, surprising himself with the admission. " wrecked everything. I ain't doing it again."

The old man held his gaze for a long moment, then nodded, a slow gesture of respect. "Good. Keep that fire. The world out here... it's slippery. It tries to pull you back. But a man who knows where he's going is harder to stop."

He tipped his hat and leaned back, closing his eyes. "Wake me when we hit San Antonio, kid. And good luck."

Pedro turned back to the window. The reflection ghosting against the glass looked determined, just like the old man said.

*Harder to stop,* Pedro thought. He liked the sound of that.
