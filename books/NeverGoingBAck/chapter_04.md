# Chapter 4: N.G.B.

Sal leaned against the doorframe of the office, nursing a lukewarm coffee. He watched the kid work.

Pedro was sweeping. It wasn't just sweeping; it was a rhythmic, meditative demolition of dust. And he was humming. A low, tuneless vibration that barely rose above the sound of the traffic on the access road.

Sal had been in this business for forty years. He knew engines, and he knew men. Engines were easy—they broke because of friction or heat. Men broke for a thousand different reasons. But this kid, Rodriguez... he seemed put back together. The way he organized the tool bench without being asked. The way he looked at the engines like they were puzzles, not chores.

Sal wanted to keep him. He needed a guy who actually cared. But then he looked at the insurance papers on his desk. *Felony conviction. Grand Theft Auto.*

It was the kiss of death. His liability premiums would triple. Or worse, the policy would just be dropped.

Sal sighed, the sound heavy with regret. He had to cut him loose. It was barely noon on the first day, but there was no point dragging it out.

"Pedro," Sal called out.

The humming stopped instantly. Pedro froze, then turned, the broom held like a staff. "Yeah, boss?"

"Come in here a minute."

Pedro leaned the broom against the wall and wiped his hands on his pants. He walked into the small, air-conditioned office. He didn't sit down. He saw the papers on the desk. He saw the look in Sal's eyes.

"You're a good worker, kid," Sal said, not burying the lead. "Better than the last three guys I fired. But I made some calls." He tapped the paper. "Insurance. They won't touch you. Not with a GTA on your sheet. I can't hire you."

Pedro didn't flinch. He didn't look devastatingly crushed, which surprised Sal. Instead, a strange, calm focus settled over his face.

"I figured," Pedro said. "That's why you don't hire me."

Sal blinked. "I just said that."

"No," Pedro said, taking a step forward. "You don't hire *Pedro Rodriguez, the felon*. You hire a vendor."

Pedro reached into his back pocket and pulled out a folded piece of paper. It looked official, stamped with the seal of the Secretary of State. He smoothed it out on Sal's desk.

**Certificate of Formation: NGB, LLC.**

"NGB?" Sal asked, squinting at the paper.

"Never Going Back," Pedro said. "I set it up six months ago from the library terminal. I got my G.E.D. inside. I took the ACTs just to see if I could. I read every book on small business law they had in the unit."

Pedro tapped the document.

"You aren't hiring an employee, Sal. No W-2. No background check required for a vendor contract. You're hiring NGB, LLC to provide 'Automotive Support Services' on a contract basis. I handle my own taxes. I handle my own liability."

Sal looked up from the paper to Pedro's face. The kid wasn't just determined; he was prepared. He had spent five years in a cage and built a key out of paper.

"You filed for an LLC from prison?" Sal asked, a grin slowly spreading across his face.

"I had a lot of time," Pedro said simply. "And I'm good at puzzles."

Sal laughed. It was a genuine, belly-shaking laugh. He picked up his pen and pointed it at Pedro.

"NGB, huh? Never Going Back."

"That's the promise," Pedro said.

"Alright," Sal said, sweeping the insurance rejection into the trash. "I'll draw up a contract. But you charge reasonable rates, you hear me?"

"I charge fair market value," Pedro smiled. "But the first week is discount."
