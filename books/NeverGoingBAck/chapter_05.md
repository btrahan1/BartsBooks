# Chapter 5: Freedom

The sun had long since dipped below the horizon, painting the Texas sky in bruised purples and charcoal greys. Inside the shop, the fluorescent lights hummed, casting long shadows across the concrete floor.

Pedro was still moving.

He had swept the bays three times. He had organized the socket wrenches by size, then by drive type. He had taken the trash out to the dumpster and then scrubbed the empty cans. It was 6:00 p.m., and he hadn't stopped, hadn't sat, hadn't even looked up when the delivery car rattled into the parking lot.

Sal watched him from the office doorway. He had seen hardworking men before—guys trying to pay off debts, guys trying to impress the boss. But this was different. This wasn't hustle; it was exorcism. The kid was trying to sweat out five years of cage time in a single afternoon.

The pizza delivery guy, a teenager with a pimply face and a bored expression, handed Sal two large boxes. Sal paid him and tipped him five bucks.

"Pedro," Sal called out.

Pedro was wiping down the lift controls. He didn't turn around. "Almost done, boss. Just wanted to get this grease off the handle."

"Pedro." Sal's voice was firmer this time. Not angry, just heavy.

Pedro stopped. He lowered the rag and turned. He looked exhausted. The adrenaline of the release, the bus ride, and the job pitch had worn off, leaving him hollow-eyed and shaky.

"Dinner time," Sal said, lifting the boxes.

Pedro looked at the pizza boxes, then at his dirty hands. "I can finish up first."

"You're finished," Sal said. "Tools down. That's an order from your client."

Pedro hesitated, then set the rag on the bench. He walked over to the small break table Sal had set up in the corner of the office.

"Pepperoni and jalapeño," Sal said, opening the lid. "Best in the county, or so they tell me."

The smell filled the room—hot cheese, spicy peppers, yeast. It wasn't the medium-rare steak Pedro had dreamed about, but looking at it now, steaming in the box, it looked like a feast.

"Thank you," Pedro said.

"Sit," Sal commanded.

They ate in silence for a few minutes. Pedro ate carefully, savoring the texture, the heat. It tasted real. It tasted like outside.

Sal wiped his mouth with a paper napkin. "So. NGB, LLC. You got a corporate headquarters for that enterprise?"

Pedro paused, a slice halfway to his mouth. He lowered it. "I'm working on it."

"Uh-huh," Sal grunted. "That means you got no place."

"I got cash," Pedro said defensively. "I can get a motel."

"Not tonight you can't. Not around here. They'll eat you alive or overcharge you for the privilege." Sal leaned back in his creaking chair. "I got a cot in the back storage room. It ain't the Ritz. It's got boxes of air filters stacked next to it. But it's lockable, and it's free. For a few nights."

Pedro stared at him. The knot in his chest, the one that had been there since the gate buzzed this morning, loosened just a fraction.

"And," Sal added, wrinkling his nose pointedly, "there's a shower in the locker room. Hot water, too. I know you know it, kid, but we need to wash the state off you."

Pedro looked down at his grey t-shirt, stained with sweat and the grime of the shop. He smelled like recycled air and anxiety.

"A shower," Pedro whispered. "Without shoes."

"Preferably," Sal chuckled.

Pedro looked at the old man, then at the half-eaten pizza, and finally out the window where the stars were starting to poke through the twilight. He wasn't rich. He wasn't totally free of his past. But he had a full stomach, a job contract, and a door he could lock himself.

"Deal," Pedro said.

Sal nodded and grabbed another slice. "Good. Eat up, partner. We got a transmission to pull in the morning."

Pedro took a bite. It was the best damn pizza he had ever had.
