# Chapter 6: The Dipstick Test

The sun wasn't up yet, but Pedro was.

He folded the thin blanket Sal had given him and stacked it neatly on the corner of the cot. He smoothed the wrinkles out of the canvas. It was a habit from the inside—inspection ready, always. Even if the only person inspecting was a stack of air filters.

He walked into the small bathroom attached to the locker room. The shower was hot. He stood under the spray for five minutes, letting the steam loosen the stiffness in his back. He scrubbed with a bar of orange industrial soap that smelled like pumice and citrus.

When he walked out into the shop bay, the lights were already humming. Sal was there, drinking coffee from a Styrofoam cup, reading the morning paper on the hood of a '67 Chevelle.

"Morning," Sal grunted, not looking up.

"Morning," Pedro said. He felt exposed without his jumpsuit. He was wearing his same grey t-shirt, washed in the sink the night before and dried over a fan. It was wrinkled, but clean.

Sal folded the paper. He gestured with his chin toward Bay 3.

"Your first client," Sal said.

Pedro looked. It was a 1995 Honda Accord. The paint was peeling on the roof like a sunburn, and the rear bumper was held on with what looked like hope and duct tape. It was a beater. A commuter car that had seen better decades.

"Oil change," Sal said. "Customer says it's running rough. Wants the cheapest option. Five quarts of conventional, new filter. That's it."

Pedro walked over to the car. He circled it once, hands clasped behind his back. He wasn't just looking at the car; he was feeling it. The way it sat on its suspension. The smell of unburnt fuel hanging around the exhaust pipe.

"Keys?" Pedro asked.

Sal tossed them. Pedro caught them out of the air.

He got in. The seat was torn. The cabin smelled of stale cigarettes and vanilla air freshener. He turned the key.

The engine coughed, then caught. It idled with a rhythmic *tick-tick-tick*.

Pedro listened. He closed his eyes.

He drove it onto the lift. He got out, set the arms, and hit the button. The hydraulics whined as the beige sedan rose into the air.

Pedro walked underneath. He grabbed the drain pan and positioned it. He reached up for the drain plug.

It was tight. Stripped tight. The last guy had used an impact gun on an oil pan bolt. Rookie mistake. Or a lazy one.

Pedro didn't curse. He just grabbed a breaker bar. He applied steady, even pressure. *Click.* The bolt turned.

Hot black oil poured out. It wasn't amber. It was the color of midnight and smelled like burnt toast. It was thick, sludgy.

"Five thousand miles overdue," Pedro whispered.

While the oil drained, Pedro didn't stand around. He moved.

He checked the front tires. The tread on the inside was bald, wire showing on the left front. *Alignment issue.*

He checked the CV joints. The boots were cracked, grease splattered on the inside of the wheel well. *Axle danger.*

He lowered the car halfway to check the engine bay. He pulled the dipstick for the transmission fluid. It was brown and smelled burnt. *Transmission service needed.*

He checked the belts. The serpentine belt had cracks you could stick a fingernail into. *Time bomb.*

He finished the oil change. He replaced the plug with a new crush washer (Sal kept a box of them). He spun the new filter on, hand-tight, then a quarter turn more. Perfect. He poured in the fresh oil.

He started the car. The *tick-tick-tick* was quieter, but still there.

Pedro took a rag and wiped his hands. He walked over to the office where Sal was watching him through the glass.

Sal raised an eyebrow. "Done?"

"Oil is done," Pedro said. He pulled a small notepad from his back pocket. He had found it in the desk drawer.

"And?" Sal asked.

"And," Pedro flipped the page. "Customer asked for the cheapest option. That was a lie."

"How so?"

"Cheapest option is just the oil. But if he drives off like this, he's walking home in a month."

Pedro handed Sal the pad.

*   **Priority 1:** Front tires are dead. Wire showing. imminent blowout risk. Alignment is off -2 degrees camber on driver side.
*   **Priority 2:** Serpentine belt is dry-rotted. If that snaps, he loses power steering and alternator.
*   **Priority 3:** Transmission fluid is burnt. Needs a flush, or he's looking at a rebuild in 10k miles.
*   **Observation:** Valve cover gasket is weeping oil onto the exhaust manifold. That's the burning smell.

Sal looked at the list. He looked at the neat, block handwriting. He looked at Pedro.

"You checked all this while the oil was draining?"

"Gravity does the work," Pedro said. "I just looked around."

Sal tapped the paper. "Most guys would just change the oil and let him drive off. Guaranteed work when the belt snaps and he has to tow it back."

"That's not fixing it," Pedro said. "That's trapping it. *'A blind mechanic gets rich, but a honest one gets sleep,'* my... someone told me that once."

Sal stared at him for a long moment. Then, a slow smile spread across his face.

"Leroy," Sal said, testing the name he had seen on the old intake form years ago, the one Pedro never mentioned. "You got a good eye."

Pedro stiffened slightly at the name, but he didn't correct him. "Pedro," he said softly. "The name is Pedro."

"Right. Pedro." Sal stood up and grabbed the phone. "I'm gonna call the customer. I'm gonna read him this list. And I'm gonna tell him that my new specialist, NGB, recommends we handle the belt and the tires today so he doesn't die on the highway."

Sal dialed the number. He looked at Pedro and winked.

"Go clean up Bay 4. I think we got a transmission job coming in after all."
