# Chapter 7: The Scout

The rest of the day was a blur, but it was the good kind. The kind where time doesn't drag; it evaporates.

Sal had worked his magic on the Accord owner. It wasn't a hard sell, just a brutally honest one. "You can pay me three hundred now for the belt and the fluid, or you can pay a tow truck two hundred later, *then* pay me six hundred to fix the engine because the snapped belt took out your water pump."

The guy grumbled, but he paid. He even took the "First Time Customer Multi-Repair Discount" Sal invented on the spot.

Pedro did the work. He flushed the transmission fluid until it ran bright red again. He wrestled the new serpentine belt into place, his knuckles scraping against the firewall, leaving a streak of blood that he barely noticed.

It felt... good.

Beneath the lift, with grease on his face and the smell of ATF in his nose, Pedro felt a strange sensation in his chest. It wasn't the tight, anxious knot of prison. It was a clean, heavy warmth. Satisfaction.

*Why did I walk away from this?* Pedro thought, tightening the tensioner bolt. *I had good hands. I had a brain. I could have been doing this for five years instead of counting bricks in a wall. I was so stupid.*

He wiped the sweat from his eyes. Regret was a useless tool. It didn't fix anything; it just stripped the bolts. *Focus on the turn,* he told himself. *Righty-tighty.*

Around 2:00 PM, while Pedro was mounting the front tires after the rotation, a sound cut through the hum of the shop fans.

It was a deep, guttural roar. A V8 engine, with glasspack mufflers and a cam that gave it a choppy, aggressive idle.

Pedro froze. His hand tightened on the impact wrench. He knew that sound. That wasn't a commuter car. That was a street fighter.

It rolled past the shop slowly. Too slowly.

Pedro looked up through the open bay door. It was a black Dodge Charger, matte finish, tinted windows so dark they looked like oil slicks. The bass from the stereo was thumping so hard it rattled the tools on Pedro's cart. A heavy reggaeton beat that vibrated in his teeth.

The car didn't stop. It just prowled by, the driver clearly scanning the lot, the office, the bays.

Sal walked out of the office, wiping his glasses. He stood next to Pedro, watching the taillights fade down the access road.

"Friends of yours?" Sal asked. His voice was casual, but his eyes were sharp.

Pedro stood up slowly. He put the impact wrench down. He didn't look at the road anymore. He looked at the concrete floor, stained with the fluids of a dozen different cars.

"No," Pedro said. "Not friends."

The Charger turned around at the light a quarter-mile down. It came back. Same slow speed. Same thumping bass. This time, it revved as it passed—a sharp, challenging bark of the throttle.

*VROOM-Pop-Pop.*

It was a peacock move. A "look at me" move.

Pedro looked at Sal. The old man wasn't flinching. He was just watching, memorizing the plate, assessing the threat level like a mechanic diagnosing a rattle.

"Once upon a time," Pedro said quietly, wiping his hands on a rag until the skin was raw. "I would have been hanging out of one of those windows, being a fool. Thinking I was the king of the road because I could make the most noise."

"And now?" Sal asked.

Pedro looked at the Honda Accord. It wasn't flashy. It wasn't loud. But it was fixed. It was safe. It would get a father home to his family without breaking down.

"Now," Pedro said, picking up the torque wrench to finish the lug nuts. "I just want to torque these to spec. Eighty pound-feet. No more, no less."

Sal nodded. "Good answer. But keep the bay door closed tonight, yeah?"

"Yeah," Pedro said.

He went back to work. But the rhythm was broken. The knot in his chest was back, just a little loose thread of anxiety. The past had driven by. And in this neighborhood, the past usually circled back around.
