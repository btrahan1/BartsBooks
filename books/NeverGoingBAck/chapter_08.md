# Chapter 8: The Form

The sun was just a rumour on the eastern horizon, painting the sky in pale, watery greys. The air behind the shop was cool and smelled of damp asphalt and old tires.

It was 6:00 AM. An hour before opening.

Pedro stood in the small, fenced-in lot behind the garage. He was barefoot on the concrete. His eyes were half-closed, his breathing slow and rhythmic.

He moved through the dawn like he was moving through water.

He shifted his weight to his right leg, sinking low, his left leg extending slowly, silently. His arms floated up, palms facing outward, pushing against the empty air with a gentle, relentless pressure.

*Inhale. Expand.*
*Exhale. Sink.*

Sal opened the back door, a mug of coffee in his hand. He stopped mid-step. He watched the new guy—the guy who could diagnose a transmission by smell—moving in a way that didn't belong in a grease-stained auto shop. It looked like a dance, but it was too controlled. Too deliberate.

"What are you doing?" Sal asked, his voice rough with morning gravel.

Pedro didn't startle. He finished the movement, bringing his hands down in a slow arc, centering his energy. Only then did he turn.

"It's called Tai Chi," Pedro said quietly.

Sal took a sip of coffee, watching him. "That some kind of kung fu?"

"Sort of," Pedro said. He picked up his socks and shoes from the pile of pallets where he'd set them. "This form... it's the Yang style. It's soft."

"Soft?" Sal snorted. "You look like you're pushing a ghost."

"It relaxes me," Pedro said, sitting on a crate to lace up his boots. "It helps me focus. And it's really good exercise. Keeps the joints oiled."

Sal watched him tie the laces. He saw the scars on Pedro's knuckles again. He remembered the look in Pedro's eyes yesterday when the Charger rolled by.

"You expecting a fight, kid?" Sal asked.

Pedro stood up. He stomped his heel to settle the boot.

"I'm always expecting a fight, Sal," Pedro said. "That's why I practice not fighting."

He looked at the fence, at the razor wire coiled at the top.

In the unit, the guys who fought every day didn't last. They got stabbed, or they got thrown in the hole, or they just burned out. Pedro had survived differently. He survived by being the guy who *could* hurt you, but chose not to. It was the threat of violence, held in check by absolute discipline, that created space.

But not everyone on the outside understood that language. Some people, like the driver of that Charger, only spoke in loud noises and heavy hands.

"The things that got me through," Pedro murmured, almost to himself, "weren't violence. It was the willingness to be still when everything else was chaos."

Sal nodded slowly. He didn't fully get it—Sal was a tire iron and baseball bat kind of guy—but he respected the discipline.

"Well," Sal said, turning back to the door. "As long as you can still turn a wrench. Coffee's on."

"I'll be right there," Pedro said.

He waited until the door closed. He took one last deep breath, filling his lungs with the cool morning air. He centered himself, pushing down the anxiety, the anger, the fear of the black car.

"Never going back," he whispered his mantra to the rising sun.

Then he picked up his shop rag and went to work.
