# Chapter 9: The Pedro Analysis

The bay door rolled up at 9:00 AM sharp, but this time, Sal didn't drive the car in. He pushed it. Or rather, he guided it while Pedro helped push from the rear bumper.

It was a 1963 Corvette Sting Ray. The "Split-Window."

It was painted a deep, lustrous Sebring Silver that caught the shop lights and threw them back like liquid mercury. The chrome bumpers were flawless. The knock-off wheels gleamed.

"This," Sal said, wiping a microscopic smudge of dust from the fender with a microfiber cloth, "belongs to a friend of mine."

Pedro stepped back, keeping his hands well away from the paint. He knew what this was. This wasn't a car; it was a retirement fund on wheels. It was American art.

"It's beautiful," Pedro said.

"It is," Sal agreed. "And I am the only one he lets work on it. He usually brings it in once a year for a fluids check and a detailing. He breathes on this car, Pedro. He treats it better than his kids."

Sal looked at Pedro. His expression was serious, devoid of the usual morning banter.

"I want you to run the 'Pedro Analysis' on it."

Pedro blinked. "You want me to touch it?"

"I want you to inspect it," Sal corrected. "Don't fix anything. Don't tighten a single bolt. Just look. Smell. Listen."

Sal checked his watch. "The owner, Franklin Summers, is coming by at noon to pick it up. He's... particular. I'd like to introduce you by having you give him the diagnosis."

Pedro felt a spike of adrenaline. This wasn't just a job; it was an audition. Sal was throwing him into the deep end with a client who probably knew every scratch on the chassis.

"You really trust me with this?" Pedro asked.

"I trust your eyes," Sal said. "Don't make me regret it. And don't tell me what you find. Surprise me."

Sal walked back to the office, leaving Pedro alone with the silver ghost.

Pedro took a breath. He went to his tool cart and put away his wrenches. He washed his hands three times. He put on a pair of fresh nitrile gloves.

He approached the car like it was an unexploded bomb.

He spent the first hour just looking. He checked the panel gaps. Perfect. He checked the weather stripping. Supple, conditioned.

He popped the hood. The 327 cubic-inch V8 sat there like a jewel in a box. The valve covers were polished to a mirror shine. The air cleaner was pristine.

Pedro leaned in. He didn't touch. He inhaled.

*Old fuel? No, stabilizer. Good.*
*Rubber? Fresh hoses.*

He got out his flashlight and peered into the dark corners of the engine bay. He checked the wiring harness routing. He checked the hose clamps.

Then he put it on the lift. He didn't use the standard pads; he used the soft rubber blocks Sal kept locked in a drawer, placing them carefully on the frame rails.

He raised it up.

The undercarriage was cleaner than Pedro's kitchen table. The frame was satin black. The exhaust was stainless steel.

But Pedro kept looking. He wasn't looking for broken things. He was looking for *tired* things. Things that were about to whisper before they screamed.

At 11:55 AM, a black Lincoln Town Car pulled into the lot. A driver got out and opened the rear door.

A man stepped out. Franklin Summers looked like he owned the bank that owned the shop. He was in his sixties, wearing a linen suit that cost more than Pedro’s entire life earnings. He had silver hair and eyes that looked like they appraised diamonds for fun.

Sal met him at the door. They shook hands—the warm grip of old friends.

"Franklin," Sal said. "Good to see you."

"Salvatore," Franklin replied. His voice was rich, a baritone that commanded attention. "Is she ready?"

"She is," Sal said. "But I had my new specialist take a look. I wanted a second opinion."

Franklin’s eyebrows went up. "A specialist? I thought *you* were the specialist, Sal."

"I'm getting old," Sal joked. "Come meet him."

They walked into the bay. Pedro was standing by the car, holding his notepad. He had taken off his gloves. He stood straight, shoulders back, hands clasped in front of him. The "At Ease" stance.

"Franklin Summers," Sal said. "This is NGB. Pedro."

Franklin looked Pedro up and down. He saw the grey t-shirt, the prison-quality tattoos on the forearms that Pedro hadn't fully covered. He didn't sneer, but he was guarded.

"Pedro," Franklin said coolly. "Sal tells me you have a sharp eye. What did you find? And be careful, I know this car better than my wife."

Pedro nodded. He didn't look at his notes. He had memorized them.

"Mr. Summers," Pedro began, his voice steady. "The car is immaculate. The ignition timing is set perfectly to 10 degrees before top dead center. The idle mixture is spot on."

Franklin nodded, impatient. "I know. I tuned it myself."

"However," Pedro continued. "The lower radiator hose clamp on the water pump side is rotated about fifteen degrees off-center. It's rubbing slightly against the inner fender well when the engine torques under load. It hasn't worn through yet, but there's a scuff mark on the rubber."

Franklin frowned. He leaned in, peering into the engine bay.

"Also," Pedro said. "The left rear sway bar bushing has a hairline crack on the top side. You can't see it from below; you have to feel it. It will start to squeak in about five hundred miles."

Franklin looked at Sal. Sal shrugged, smiling.

"And finally," Pedro said quietly. "The positive battery cable has a slight bit of corrosion inside the terminal boot. It's hidden, but I measured a 0.2-volt drop when cranking. It might hesitate to start on a very cold morning."

Silence filled the bay.

Franklin Summers stared at the engine. He reached down and touched the radiator hose. He felt the scuff. He straightened up and looked at Pedro with a completely different expression. It wasn't just respect; it was delight.

"A voltage drop test?" Franklin asked. "On a routine inspection?"

"Electricity never lies," Pedro said. "It just hides."

Franklin laughed. It was a loud, booming sound. He turned to Sal.

"You were right, Salvatore. He's a bloodhound."

Franklin turned back to Pedro and extended his hand. "Good to meet you, Pedro. Fix the clamp. Replace the bushing. Clean the terminal. Bill me for the hours."

"Yes, sir," Pedro said, taking the hand.

"And Pedro?" Franklin added, opening the door of his Corvette. "Next time, check the glove box latch. It rattles at 60."

Pedro smiled. "I tightened that first, sir."

Franklin beamed. He got into his car, fired up the engine—which roared to life instantly—and looked at Sal.

"Keep him," Franklin shouted over the exhaust.

As the Corvette rumbled away, Sal walked over and slapped Pedro on the shoulder.

"Pedro Analysis," Sal chuckled. "Passed with flying colors."
