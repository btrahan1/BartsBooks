# Chapter 10: The First Draw

It was Friday afternoon. The shop air was thick with the smell of orange cleaner and the anticipation of the weekend.

Pedro was wiping down his tools. It was a ritual. Every socket, every wrench, every screwdriver was cleaned, inspected, and returned to its specific slot in the drawer. He didn't own the tools yet—they were Sal's spares—but he treated them like they were platinum.

Sal walked out of the office. He held a folded piece of paper.

"Pedro," Sal said. "Your pay."

Pedro wiped his hands on a rag and took the paper. It wasn't a check. It was a printed confirmation of an electronic transfer.

**Payee: NGB, LLC**
**Bank: River City Credit Union**
**Amount: $1,250.00**

Pedro stared at the number. He blinked. He looked at the week's dates. Five days. Forty hours, maybe forty-five.

He did the math in his head.

"Sal," Pedro said, frowning. "This is wrong."

"It's correct," Sal said, leaning against the tool cart. "I double-checked the hours."

"It's too high," Pedro insisted. "Even efficiently, this is..."

Then he remembered. **Gross amount.**

In prison, he made forty cents an hour. Before prison, when he worked odd jobs, it was under the table cash or minimum wage with taxes stripped out.

 This was a vendor payment. No Social Security. No Medicare. No Income Tax withheld. It was the raw, unbled number.

"Right," Pedro murmured. "Contract work. Nothing taken out."

"You gotta save thirty percent for the IRS, partner," Sal reminded him. "Don't go buying a jet ski. Uncle Sam will come for his cut in April."

Pedro nodded. "I know. I verified the tax tables in the library. Schedule C form."

He looked at the number again. Even minus the thirty percent, it was more money than he had legally made in a month, ever.

"Still," Pedro said, tapping the paper. "It seems... a bit high. It seems more than for a full week's work. Even at the shop rate."

Sal shrugged. He pulled a cigar out of his pocket but didn't light it. He just rolled it between his fingers.

"You did five transmission flushes. You did the brakes on Mrs. Gable's minivan in half the book time. You saved the radiator job on the Ford."

Sal pointed to the empty bay where the silver Corvette had stood yesterday.

"And Franklin Summers called me this morning. He said his glove box is silent as a tomb. He insisted I add a 'Consulting Fee' to your draw."

Pedro looked up. "I didn't ask for that."

"You didn't have to," Sal grinned. "That's how it works out here, Pedro. You do good work, you get paid. You do *excellent* work, you get tipped."

Pedro looked at the deposit slip. $1,250.

He could pay for a month at the motel. He could buy real food. He could buy his own torque wrench.

He folded the paper carefully and put it in his pocket.

"Thank you, Sal."

"Don't thank me," Sal said, pushing off the cart. "Thank NGB, LLC. Now go home. Or go to the motel. Just get out of here. I'm locking the gate in ten minutes."

"Yes, sir," Pedro said.

He walked out of the bay door into the gathered dusk. He touched the paper in his pocket. It was just paper, just numbers. But it felt heavy. It felt like an anchor, holding him steady in a world that tried to wash him away.

He had a job. He had money. He had a name.

*Never going back.*
