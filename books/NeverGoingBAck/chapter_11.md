# Chapter 11: The Arrival

Saturday morning started slow. The rush of the work week had faded into a lazy heat that hung over the asphalt. Pedro was working on a brake job for Mrs. Ramirez's sedan, specifically the caliper mounting bracket.

Then the shadow fell over the bay.

It wasn't a cloud. It was the black Dodge Charger.

The deep, thumping bass preceded it, vibrating the tools on the metal bench. It didn't drive by this time. It slowed, the tires crunching heavily on the gravel, and turned sharply into the lot.

The engine revved once—a sharp, aggressive bark—before cutting off. The silence that followed was heavier than the noise.

Pedro didn't flinch. He didn't drop his wrench. He kept his eyes on the caliper bolt he was tightening. *Righty-tighty. Eighty pound-feet.*

He felt the car doors open. Four of them.

Four men stepped out. They were Latinos, young but with eyes that looked old and hard. They wore the uniform of the street: pristine sneakers, oversized shirts, and an air of ownership that Pedro knew well. It was the walk of men who expected people to move out of their way.

Sal walked out of the office. He held his clipboard, acting as if this were just another customer, though Pedro noticed he had left the office door slightly ajar—the one where he kept the baseball bat behind the filing cabinet.

"Morning, gentlemen," Sal called out, his voice steady. "Can I help you?"

The driver, a guy with a neck tattoo of a scorpion and a gold chain that glittered in the sun, leaned against the fender of the Charger. He didn't look at Sal. He was scanning the shop. His eyes locked onto Pedro's back.

"Nice shop, old man," the driver said. He picked at a fingernail. "We heard you got a new mechanic. Someone... talented."

Sal stood his ground. He didn't look back at Pedro. "I got a few guys. We do good work. You need an oil change?"

The other three men fanned out slightly. It wasn't an attack formation, not yet, but it was a perimeter. They were blocking the exits without making it look like they were blocking the exits.

Pedro finished the nut he was tightening. He felt the click of the torque wrench. *Click.* Perfect spec.

He placed the wrench gently on the cart. He wiped his hands on his rag, taking his time to get the grease off his knuckles.

Then he turned.

He didn't walk toward them aggressively. He didn't retreat. He walked to a spot about five feet behind Sal and two feet to the right. The devastatingly polite "support" position. Close enough to intervene, far enough to not be the aggressor.

Pedro looked at the driver. He looked at the three men. He read them instantly. The one on the left was nervous; he kept touching his waistband. The one on the right was a brawler; thick neck, knuckles scarred. The driver was the shot-caller.

Pedro didn't speak. He just stood there, arms loose at his sides, feet planted.

The driver smiled, a cold expression that didn't reach his eyes. "There he is. The Ghost."

Pedro looked at Sal, then back at the driver.

He nodded once.

It wasn't a submission. It wasn't a greeting. It was an acknowledgment. *I see you. I am here.*

"I'm working," Pedro said softly. "State your business."
