# Chapter 13: The Diplomat

Pedro took his time. He didn't rush the inspection. He treated the Charger with the same reverence he had shown the Split-Window Corvette, even though this beast was a different species entirely.

The Corvette was refined power. This was brute force.

He opened the hood. The V8 was dressed up—chrome valve covers, braided hoses, an oversized air cleaner that cost more than Pedro’s first car. But as he leaned in, he saw the truth.

The plug wires were routed messily, touching the hot headers. The carburetor linkage was sticky. The valve tick wasn't a major mechanical failure; it was just sloppy lash adjustment.

*It’s a peacock,* Pedro thought. *Bright feathers, loud noises. It’s meant to impress on the outside and make noise.*

He felt the eyes of the four men on his back. They were waiting for him to criticize it. To tell them that their chariot was a mess.

Pedro wiped his hands and stood up. He thought about diplomacy. In the yard, you didn't tell a man his shank was dull; you offered to sharpen it.

*You don't insult a man's ride,* Pedro thought. *You escalate it.*

He turned to the driver.

"She's got a lot of heart," Pedro said. "Strong pull. But she's fighting herself."

The driver frowned. "What do you mean?"

"The timing is too advanced," Pedro explained, keeping his voice respectful. "She's shouting when she should be singing. And that tick? That's just a rocker arm that needs love. It's like a pebble in a running shoe. It slows you down."

Pedro gestured to the engine.

"You built this car to be King of the Street, right?"

"Damn straight," the driver said.

"Right now," Pedro said, "it’s a sledgehammer. And sledgehammers are scary. But I can make it a scalpel."

He stepped closer, lowering his voice, inviting the driver into the vision.

"I can tune this lash so tight, and adjust the carb so precise, that at red lights, people won't even know it's running. It will be so smooth you could balance a nickel on the air cleaner while you rev it. Silent power. The kind that doesn't need to shout because everyone already knows it's the boss."

The driver imagined it. He pictured rolling up to a light, the car vibrating with restrained energy, silent and deadly, then exploding off the line.

"Smooth," the driver repeated. "Like ice?"

"Smoother," Pedro promised. "Like oil on glass."

The driver looked at his crew. He saw them nodding. They liked the sound of that. *Scalpel.*

"Do it," the driver said. "Whatever she needs."

Pedro nodded. He had successfully walked the line. He hadn't insulted the car; he had promised to unleash its potential.

"Give me an hour," Pedro said. "Go get some lunch. When you come back, she'll be a new woman."

The driver tossed him the keys again. "We'll be at the taco truck down the street. Don't scratch the paint."

"I treat every car like it's my own," Pedro said.

As the four men walked away, their swagger just a little bit lighter, Sal walked up to Pedro. He let out a long breath he had been holding for ten minutes.

"A scalpel?" Sal asked, raising an eyebrow.

"It sounds better than 'fixing the sloppy idle'," Pedro shrugged.

"You just became the most protected mechanic in San Antonio," Sal chuckled. "You know who that is, right?"

"I know he's a customer," Pedro said, popping the hood latch. "And NGB protects its customers."

Sal shook his head, smiling. "Get to work, Diplomat. I'll watch the gate."
