# Chapter 14: Quiet Strength

Pedro eased the Charger out of the shop lot. The difference was night and day. Before, the car had lurched and shouted, fighting its own mismatched timing. Now, it glided.

The idle was a low, resonant thrum—a predator purring in its sleep.

He drove down the block to the taco stand. It was a converted trailer with a few picnic tables under a red awning. The four men were sitting at the corner table, laughing, eating tacos al pastor from paper plates. The music from a portable speaker was loud.

They didn't hear him approach.

That was the test.

Pedro rolled the matte black beast right up to the curb, ten feet from their table. The engine was so balanced, so rhythmically perfect, that it blended into the ambient noise of the street.

He shifted into neutral. He looked at the driver, who hadn't even looked up from his soda.

Pedro tapped the throttle.

*THRUM-ROAR.*

It wasn't a bark. It was a growl. A deep, baritone surge of power that vibrated the soda cans on the table.

The four heads snapped up instantly.

Pedro cut the engine. The silence that followed was heavy, impressive.

He stepped out, tossing the keys in the air and catching them. He walked over to the table.

"Delivery," Pedro said.

The driver stood up. He walked over to the car, listening to the ticking of the cooling metal. He ran a hand over the fender.

"I didn't even hear you roll up," the driver said, shaking his head. "She used to scream like a banshee."

"She was screaming because she was in pain," Pedro said. "Now she's breathing."

The driver hopped in. He turned the key. The V8 caught instantly—no churning starter, no gas pedal needed. Just a solid *WHAM* of ignition settling into that perfect, dangerous idle.

"Wow," the driver said. He revved it again, feeling the response. 

"That took some effort," Pedro said, leaning against the doorframe.

The driver looked at him, a wide grin breaking his hard face. "Five years for Grand Theft Auto, and you just hand me the keys to this beauty?"

Pedro grinned back. It was a real smile this time. "I served five years for taking cars. I figured I owed the universe a few good returns."

He tapped the roof.

"Rule number one: never steal from family."

The driver laughed. He shut the door and reached into his pocket. He peeled off three crisp hundred-dollar bills from a thick roll and slapped them into Pedro's hand.

"Three hundred?" Pedro asked. "The labor was barely an hour."

"That ain't for the hour," the driver said, stepping closer. He looked Pedro in the eye, his expression serious again.

"I wasn't sure about you at first, Pedro. The Ghost. I thought maybe you were just... broken. Prison does that to men. Makes them quiet because they're scared."

The driver shook his head.

"But I can tell you're strong, homie. But like... a quiet strong. You know what I mean?"

He tapped his own chest.

"You got gravity. That's worth more than noise."

Pedro pocketed the cash. "I appreciate that."

"We'll be seeing you," the driver said. "And if anyone gives you grief... you tell them Leo sends his regards."

"Thanks, Leo," Pedro said.

He turned and walked back toward the shop. He didn't look back as the Charger peeled out, the tires chirping once—a controlled, precise launch that sounded like a rocket leaving the pad.

Pedro walked with his head up. He had gravity.
