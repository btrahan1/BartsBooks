# Chapter 15: The Clean Sweep

Back at the shop, the silence returned. But it was a different kind of silence. It wasn't the heavy, humid quiet of a Texas afternoon; it was the pristine silence of a church after the service has ended.

Sal leaned against the doorframe of his office, nursing a lukewarm Dr. Pepper. He watched Pedro.

The kid was sweeping. Again.

Most mechanics swept the floor because they had to. They pushed the dust around, leaving streaks of grease and grey piles in the corners. Pedro swept like he was painting.

He moved in long, rhythmic arcs, the broom bristles hissing softly against the concrete. Push. Step. Push. Step.

The floor was spotless. Not just "shop clean." It looked like it belonged in a mall. You could eat a sandwich off Bay 3.

Sal shook his head, evaluating, then re-evaluating.

He had watched the entire exchange with the Charger crew. He had seen men like Leo before. He had seen them break bones over a spilled drink or a wrong look. That conversation could have gone differently. Way differently. It could have ended with a tire iron through a windshield or a shop burning down at midnight.

Instead, Pedro had walked out there, unarmed, and turned a wolf pack into a customer base.

*Rule number one: never steal from family.*

Sal had heard him say it. It was a line that sounded like it came from a movie, but when Pedro said it, it sounded like a law of physics.

Pedro reached the end of the bay, gathered the microscopic pile of dust into a dustpan, and tapped it gently into the trash. He didn't slam it. He placed the broom back on its hook, aligning it perfectly vertical.

Sal took a sip of his soda, watching the dust motes dance in the light.

"Tie Chee?" Sal muttered to himself, testing the shape of the word in his mouth. "Is that what he called it?"

He looked at his own reflection in the glass of the office window. Old, tired, carrying the weight of forty years of grease and negotiations. Then he looked at Pedro, who was now wiping down the sink in the corner.

The kid was a weapon. A precision instrument wrapped in a grey t-shirt. And for some reason, he was sweeping Sal's floor.

Sal chuckled softly, a dry, rattling sound.

"Well," Sal whispered to the empty office. "At least the floors are clean."
