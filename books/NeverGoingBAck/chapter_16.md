# Part Two

# Chapter 1: The Trap

The morning light in the shop was different now. It didn't feel like an interrogation spotlight; it felt like a workspace.

Pedro was sweeping. It was his meditation. The rhythm of the bristles against the concrete was the metronome of his new life.

Sal was behind the counter, flipping through a parts catalog, but his eyes weren't on the pages. He was watching Pedro. He had been watching him for weeks now, trying to solve the equation.

"Pedro," Sal said, his voice cutting through the soft *swish-swish* of the broom. "I want to ask you something that's been on my mind."

Pedro didn't stop sweeping, but he slowed the tempo. "Ask away, Boss."

"You've been here a month," Sal said. "You got a bank account. You got an LLC. You got a routine."

Sal closed the catalog.

"But you don't have a shadow. You don't have a parole officer."

Pedro stopped then. He rested his hands on the top of the broom handle, analyzing the question. It wasn't an accusation; it was honest confusion.

"No, sir," Pedro said calmly. "I don't."

"Why?" Sal asked. "With a five-year stretch usually comes two years of paper. Mandatory supervision."

"That's a trap," Pedro said. "It's a fast track to recidivism."

He resumed sweeping, moving a small pile of dust toward the pan.

"I did my time," Pedro continued. "Paid off in full. I declined all Parole Reviews. I waived my hearings."

Sal was stunned. He took off his glasses and rubbed the bridge of his nose. "You waived them? Why? You could have probably got out earlier! You could have been out in three."

Pedro set the broom aside. He walked over to the counter, wiping his hands on a rag.

"I wasn't ready," Pedro said.

He looked out the bay door at the traffic passing by.

"I didn't even start to learn who I was, who I could be, until the third year. The first two years? I was just angry. I was busy planning how to get back at the world. If I had gotten out then... I would have been back inside in six months."

Pedro looked Sal in the eye.

"And even if I had changed," Pedro said, "The System is designed to catch you on the rebound. If I had gotten out early, I'd have a P.O. breathing down my neck. I'd have missed a meeting at some point, most likely. Maybe the bus breaks down. Maybe work runs late."

He shook his head.

"Or, I could get locked back up on some minor charge. Running a red light. Jaywalking. Not paying a bill on time. Stupid stuff like that. When you're on paper, 'stupid stuff' is a felony violation."

Pedro picked up the broom again.

"No," he said firmly. "I didn't want a leash. I wanted a door. I stayed until the clock hit zero. The warden tried to talk me out of it. Said I was wasting state resources."

Pedro smiled, a thin, sharp expression.

"But when I walked out that gate, I walked out clear. No tail. No check-ins. No drug tests."

He looked at the clean floor, then back at Sal.

"I am free, Sal. And I'm never going back."

Sal looked at him for a long moment. He saw the logic in it. It was brutal, calculated, and terrifyingly disciplined. To choose prison over a chance at freedom, just to ensure the freedom was absolute?

"You're a hard man, Pedro Rodriguez," Sal whispered.

"I tried being soft," Pedro said, turning back to his work. "Soft got me five years."
