# Chapter 2: The Artist

Three days later, another car rolled into the lot.

It was a 1964 Chevy Impala. Candy apple red. Gold spoke rims. Hydraulics that hissed softly as it settled onto the gravel. It was a lowrider masterpiece, the kind you see on calendars.

Four men stepped out. They wore pressed khakis and white tanks. Their leader, a guy with a thick mustache and sunglasses, leaned against the door. Let's call him Rico.

"Heard you was some hot shot mechanic," Rico said, spitting on the ground. "Thought we'd show you what a real car looks like."

Pedro walked out. He didn't look at Rico. He looked at the car.

He saw the flaws instantly. He heard the slight exhaust leak at the manifold. He smelled the burning transmission fluid—the hydraulics were overtaxing the pump. The suspension geometry was a nightmare.

But the paint...

The paint was liquid fire. It had depth. It had soul. The pinstriping on the hood was so fine it looked like it had been painted with a single hair.

Pedro just nodded.

"Pop the hood," Pedro said softly.

Rico smirked. "You wanna check my oil, grease monkey?" But he popped it.

It was... unexpected.

It wasn't mechanically perfect—the carburetor was too big for the manifold, and the headers were restrictive—but it was beautiful. The spark plug wires were routed in perfect, symmetrical arcs, held in place by custom loom clips painted gold. The vacuum lines were braided steel, running in parallel lines like a circuit board.

It wasn't efficient. But it told a story. It was engine bay as jewelry.

And the mural on the underside of the hood... it was a sprawling, intricate scene of Aztec warriors and angels. It was breathtaking.

Pedro stared at it. He caught his breath. He traced the line work with his eyes. He wasn't looking at a machine; he was looking at a canvas.

Suddenly, a shadow fell over the group.

The black Charger rolled into the lot. It moved so silently, thanks to Pedro's "Scalpel" tune, that no one had heard it approach.

*VROOM.*

Leo revved the engine once. A single, terrifying growl that made Rico and his crew jump.

Startled, Rico spun around.

Leo stepped out of the Charger. Three of his guys stepped out with him. They didn't look at the Impala. They looked at Rico. They started posturing, squaring their shoulders, moving to prop up their mechanic.

"You got a problem with NGB?" Leo asked, his voice low. "Because if you got a problem with the Ghost, you got a problem with the Southside."

Rico’s crew tensed up. Hands moved toward waistbands. Sal stepped out of the office, the bat in his hand, though he held it low.

The air was electric. Violence was a spark away.

Pedro finished creating the mental image of the mural. He turned around. He walked right into the middle of the standoff. He didn't look at Leo. He didn't look at Rico.

He walked up to the guy standing next to Rico—a younger kid, maybe twenty, with paint-stained fingernails. The one who had been quietly watching Pedro admire the hood.

Pedro stuck his hand out.

"You," Pedro said.

The kid blinked, terrified. "Me?"

"You did the mural," Pedro said. "And the wiring. The symmetry."

The kid nodded slowly. "Yeah. I did."

Pedro took the kid's hand and shook it. It wasn't a prison grip. It was a gentleman's shake.

"You win," Pedro said loud enough for everyone to hear.

Rico frowned. "What?"

"Mechanically? It needs breath," Pedro said honestly. "But that?" He pointed to the engine bay. "That is spirit. I can fix a transmission. I can tune an engine. But I can't do that."

He looked the kid in the eye.

"You are not good, son. You are an artist."

The tension in the lot snapped. Rico looked at his car, then at his quiet cousin who painted it. He looked at Pedro, who wasn't fighting, but acknowledging.

"An artist?" Rico asked, his chest puffing out slightly. "Yeah. Yeah, he is. My cousin Chuy. Best brush in the city."

"Respect," Leo said, nodding at Chuy.

Pedro stepped back, bridging the gap between the two crews. He looked at Rico, then he turned and looked at Leo and the South Side boys.

"You guys come too," Pedro said, engaging both leaders. "Neither of you has any work to do right now anyway. Come back near the end of the day. We can all talk."

He paused, a small smile playing on his lips.

"I have an idea."
