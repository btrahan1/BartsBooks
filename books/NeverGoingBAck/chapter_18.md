# Chapter 3: The Proposition

The sun was beginning to dip, casting long, golden shadows across the lot. Both crews—Leo's crew with the matte black Charger, and Rico's crew with the candy red Impala—were standing in a loose semi-circle. It was a rare sight. Usually, these two groups occupied different orbits. Today, they were sharing the same gravity.

Pedro stood in the center. Sal was to his right, leaning on the bat but relaxed.

"Sal, you too," Pedro said, looking at his boss. "We all family here."

Leo crossed his arms, looking at Rico. Rico adjusted his sunglasses, looking at the Charger. The truce was holding, but it was fragile.

"Okay, guys," Pedro said, his voice calm and authoritative. "Here's my idea. Two weeks from today, there is a show. The River City Rumble."

He saw heads nod. Everyone knew the Rumble. It was the biggest meet in the county. Custom rods, lowriders, muscle cars.

"Both of you have sweet rides," Pedro continued. "But right now, you're competing for scraps. Rico, you look good but run rough. Leo, you run fast but..." Pedro gestured vaguely at the matte paint. "You're all muscle, no suit."

Leo frowned, but didn't argue.

"I think if I work with Chuy," Pedro nodded to the artist, "we can elevate them both. With Chuy's artistry under the hood of the Charger, and my humble mechanical skills under the hood of the Impala..."

He chuckled.

"I bet both teams take home a trophy. Best in Class Muscle. Best in Class Lowrider."

Rico looked at Leo. "You let my cousin touch your car?"

Leo looked at Chuy. "If he makes it look half as good as that mural... yeah. Besides, Pedro vouched for him."

"And Sal," Pedro turned to the old man. "I would like for you to ask Franklin Summers to stop by with his car as well."

Sal raised an eyebrow. "Franklin? He doesn't do car shows. He thinks they're pedestrian."

"Ask him," Pedro said. "Tell him it's a private consultation. All of us. Wednesday afternoon, the day after I work my skills on that beauty out there."

He pointed to the Impala.

"We'll line the cars up. The Corvette. The Charger. The Impala. We'll talk shop. We'll talk trophies. And maybe some prize money, who knows?"

Pedro looked around the circle. He saw the ambition lighting up in their eyes. He wasn't just offering them a tune-up; he was offering them glory.

"Wednesday," Rico said. "We'll be here."

"Wednesday," Leo agreed.

As the crews dispersed, engines rumbling—one roaring, one hissing—Sal looked at Pedro.

"You're building an army," Sal said quietly.

"No," Pedro said, picking up his broom to sweep the gravel back into place. "I'm building a fleet."
