# Chapter 4: The Summit

Wednesday afternoon. The shop lot was buzzing, but the vibe was different. It wasn't the tension of a standoff; it was the energy of a block party.

Sal had ordered five extra-large pizzas from the place down the street—the good stuff, with the crispy pepperoni cups. Rico’s crew and Leo’s crew were mingling, leaning on the hoods of their cars, eating slices and arguing about suspension geometry.

The Charger sat on the left, its engine bay now sparkling thanks to Chuy’s detailing work. The Impala sat on the right, its idle now smooth and rhythmic thanks to Pedro’s tuning. They were complementary beasts: one built for speed, one built for style, but both now touching perfection.

Then, the low rumble of a 327 V8 cut through the chatter.

Every head turned.

The Sebring Silver Corvette Sting Ray pulled into the lot. It moved slowly, careful of the gravel, gleaming like a piece of jewelry dropped into a sandbox.

"Damn!" was the unanimous exhalation from everyone there. Even Rico, who thought anything without hydraulics was boring, took his sunglasses off.

Franklin Summers drove right up to the center, parking between the muscle car and the lowrider. He stepped out, wearing a crisp linen suit that looked dangerously out of place in a Southside auto shop. But he didn't check his watch, and he didn't look uncomfortable.

He smiled at the group.

"Gentlemen," Franklin said. "Sal tells me we have a quorum."

He didn't wait for an introduction. He walked to the front of his car and popped the hood.

The crowd surged forward. It wasn't tentative. It was a magnetic pull.

What followed wasn't Gang vs. Gang vs. Guy in a Suit. It was just Car Guys.

Leo was pointing at Franklin’s mechanical fuel injection unit, asking about the flow rate. Franklin was listening intently to Chuy explain the gold-leaf process on the Impala’s firewall. Rico was nodding respectfully as Franklin explained the history of the split-window design.

There was no posturing. No checking of watches or waistbands. Just the universal language of horsepower and chrome.

Franklin ran a hand along the fender of the Charger. "Matte black," he mused. "It absorbs light. Very menacing. Like a stealth fighter. Who did the detailing? The texture is remarkably even."

"Chuy did," Leo said, slapping the artist on the back. "Kid's got hands."

"Remarkable," Franklin said. He reached into his jacket pocket and pulled out a business card. He handed it to Chuy. "I have a Jaguar E-Type that needs a very specific touch. Call me."

Chuy looked at the card like it was a winning lottery ticket.

Back near the office door, Sal and Pedro stood watching the scene.

"You realize," Sal said quietly, "that you have the heavy hitters of the Southside and the Chamber of Commerce eating pepperoni pizza together on my property."

"It's just cars, Sal," Pedro said, wiping his hands on a rag. "Metal doesn't care where you came from. It only cares if you respect it."

Sal looked at Pedro. He looked at the fleet they had assembled. A muscle car, a lowrider, and a classic.

Sal extended his fist.

Pedro bumped it.

"Hell of a fleet," Sal said.

"We're gonna sweep the Rumble," Pedro smiled.
