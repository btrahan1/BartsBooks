# Chapter 5: The Fleet

By Friday, the shop didn't look like a shop anymore. It looked like a hive.

"Pedro," Sal shouted over the sound of a pneumatic drill. "You've taken over my shop!"

He was exasperated, throwing his hands up, but the smile on his face betrayed him. He wasn't angry; he was amazed.

Pedro was under the Impala, finalizing the transmission linkage. He slid out on his creeper, wiping grease from his cheek.

"Just delegating, Boss," Pedro said.

Sal looked around. Three members of "The Fleet"—as they were now calling themselves—were moving through the bays with surprising efficiency.

One of Leo's guys, a towering man named Tiny, was sweeping the floor with the same meditative focus Pedro used.

One of Rico's cousins was in Bay 1, performing an oil change on Mrs. Gable's minivan. He was being supervised by Chuy, who was treating the oil filter like it was a delicate art restoration project.

In Bay 2, another one of the Southside boys was doing a brake job on a Ford F-150.

Sal watched them. They were working hard. They were respectful. They were following the "Pedro Standard"—tools cleaned, floors spotted, customers treated like royalty.

But as Sal watched the brake job—the guy fumbling slightly with the caliper clip, then asking for help—he realized something.

It took three of them to keep up with the work.

One sweeping. One on oil. One on brakes.

Pedro usually did all three before his morning coffee break.

"You got a whole crew in here," Sal noted, leaning down to hand Pedro a 10mm socket. "And they're barely matching your output."

Pedro took the socket. "They're learning, Sal. They used to solve problems with their fists. Now they're solving them with wrenches. It takes a different kind of muscle."

"And what are you solving?" Sal asked, looking at the Impala.

"Efficiency," Pedro said, sliding back under the car. "And maybe a little bit of peace and quiet on the streets."

Sal watched the "Fleet" work. Tiny finished sweeping and immediately started organizing the tire rack by tread pattern.

"Well," Sal grunted, walking back to the office. "At least I don't have to sweep anymore."
