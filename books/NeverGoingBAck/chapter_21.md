# Chapter 6: The Rules of Engagement

The sun was setting on the Tuesday before the River City Rumble. The shop lights were off, but the lot was illuminated by the golden hour glow.

Three cars sat in a line, gleaming under protective covers that had been peeled back halfway for a final inspection.

The matte black Charger.
The candy red Impala.
The silver Corvette Sting Ray.

Franklin Summers was there, standing next to his Split-Window. He had decided, after seeing the work Chuy put into the engine bays, that merely observing wasn't enough. He had entered the "Classic Original" category.

The crews—Rio’s lowriders and Leo’s street racers—were mingling, wiping down rims that were already spotless, checking tire pressures that were already perfect.

Pedro stood on a wooden crate. It was the only way to be seen over the crowd of twenty men.

"All right, Fleet, listen up," Pedro said.

The chatter died down instantly. It wasn't just respect for a mechanic anymore; it was respect for a captain.

"We leave in the morning, 7:00 A.M. sharp," Pedro announced. "We roll in convoy. Franklin leads. Impala in the middle. Charger brings up the rear. We intimidate them with alignment before we even park."

A few guys chuckled. Leo nodded, liking the formation.

"And..." Pedro paused, looking at each of them. He looked at the tattoos, the scars, the heavy eyes of men who had lived hard lives. "We go clean."

Raised eyebrows and murmurs rippled through the group. Usually, car shows were an excuse for a party. Coolers of beer. Something stronger in the vape pens. A little posturing with rival crews.

"That's right, clean," Pedro said, his voice cutting through the murmurs. "No drugs. No alcohol. No ruckus. No mean-mugging the judges. No fighting with the guys who trailered their cars in."

Rico frowned. "Pedro, come on. It's a party."

"No," Pedro said firmly. "Tomorrow, it's business. We aren't going there as gangs. We aren't going there as 'The Southside.' We are going there as professionals."

He stepped down from the crate and walked over to the Impala. He touched the fender.

"This work? This art? It deserves respect. And if you act like hooligans, they won't see the art. They'll just see the hood."

He looked at Leo. He looked at Rico. He looked at Franklin, who gave a subtle nod of approval.

"We are a team," Pedro said, raising his voice. "We are The Fleet!"

The murmurs stopped. The chests puffed out a little. They liked the sound of that. It wasn't a gang name; it was a military unit. A force.

"We go in clean. We win their trophies. We take their prize money. And we drive home while they're still trying to figure out how a bunch of street racers built better cars than the millionaires."

Leo grinned, a wicked, sharp expression. "I like it."

"They won't know what hit them!" Pedro shouted.

"THE FLEET!" the men shouted back, a roar that startled the pigeons off the telephone wires.

Sal, standing in the doorway of the office, just shook his head and smiled.

"God help the River City Rumble," Sal whispered.
