# Chapter 7: The Procession

For the first time in twenty years, Sal walked to the front door of the shop on a Saturday morning and flipped the sign.

*CLOSED.*

He locked the door. He felt a strange lightness in his chest. Usually, Saturday was the busiest day—oil changes, inspections, emergency repairs for people who needed their cars for Monday. But today, the bays were empty.

Word had gotten around.

It wasn't spoken loudly. It was in the murmurs at the grocery store, in the nods between drivers at stoplights. There were fewer people revving their engines at 2 A.M. There was less screeching of tires leaving the parking lots.

Less noise. More pride.

The streets of the Southside felt... tuned.

Sal walked out to the truck he kept parked around the back—an old, reliable Ford. He pulled it around to the front.

The Fleet was waiting.

At 7:00 A.M. sharp, the engines fired.

First, Franklin’s Corvette. A crisp, high-pitched mechanical whir that settled into a steady hum.
Then, the Impala. A deep, rhythmic lope, the cams chopping the air with precision.
Finally, the Charger. A low, subterranean rumble that you felt in your teeth.

Pedro sat in the passenger seat of the Charger, next to Leo. He gave the signal.

Franklin pulled out. The Impala followed. The Charger took the rear.

They moved at exactly the speed limit. No speeding. No swerving. They moved like sharks in formation.

As they turned onto Main Street, heading for the highway that would take them to the River City Rumble, Sal looked in his rearview mirror.

He blinked, thinking it was a trick of the light.

Behind the Charger, a sedan pulled out. Then a pickup truck. Then a beat-up Honda.

They weren't racing. They were following.

A dozen cars. Then twenty. Cars with primer spots, cars with dents, cars with child seats in the back. The community.

They were curious, yes. But they were also... patriotic. They were escorting their champions.

Sal watched the line of cars stretching back two blocks, following the matte black bumper of the Charger at a respectful distance.

"Look at that," Sal whispered to himself, gripping the steering wheel.

The Southside wasn't invading the Northside. It was arriving.
