# Chapter 8: The Rumble

The River City Rumble was already in full swing when they arrived. The air was thick with the smell of unburnt fuel, funnel cakes, and tire shine. Loud rock music blared from the PA system, competing with the cacophony of hundreds of engines revving.

Then, The Fleet arrived.

People stopped and stared. Not because they were loud, but because they were silent.

It was the quietest trio of cars anyone had ever heard. They moved through the crowded lanes like a school of sharks closing in on a school of fish—fluid, predatory, and inextricably linked.

Franklin’s Silver Corvette cut the path.
The Candy Red Impala flowed behind it.
The Matte Black Charger anchored the rear.

They found three empty spots near the center of the show grounds. As one, they turned. As one, they backed in.

The crowd hushed, watching the synchronization. It wasn't the chaotic parking of weekend warriors; it was a tactical maneuver.

Then, the signal.

*VROO-OOOM.*

A single, unified rev. It wasn't a scream; it was the rumble of predators clearing their throats. Deep, resonant, and terrifyingly healthy.

Then, silence. The engines cut simultaneously.

The doors opened. The cars emptied efficiently, professionally.

Sal watched from the passenger seat of his truck, parked a few rows back. He blinked. He wasn't sure where they had gotten the clothes, but they didn't look like a gang. They looked like a factory race team.

They all wore matching charcoal grey work shirts with a small, understated logo stitched on the chest: *The Fleet.* No colors. No bandanas. No sagging pants. Just clean lines and serious faces.

Pedro stepped forward. He nodded to Leo. He nodded to Rico. He nodded to Franklin.

All three drivers turned and popped their hoods.

They stepped back, crossing their arms.

For a second, nobody moved. The contrast between the silent, disciplined crew and the chaotic show around them was stark.

Then, the gravity took over.

The crowd converged.

They swarmed the three cars, ignoring the million-dollar builds next to them. They were drawn to the silence, the precision, and the impossible alliance of American Muscle, Lowrider Art, and Classic Heritage sitting side by side.

"Showtime," Pedro whispered.
