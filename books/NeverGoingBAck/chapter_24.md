# Chapter 9: Respect

The perimeter around the three cars was three deep. The questions came in a barrage.

"How did you get that candy paint to look like liquid?"
"Is that a factory fuel injection unit on the Vette?"
"How the hell is that 440 idling so smooth? My Charger shakes the fillings out of my teeth."

The Fleet—Leo, Rico, Tiny, and the rest—stood by their machines. Instinctively, they started to puff out their chests, the old habit of intimidation rising to the surface. But then they remembered the rule. *Go clean.*

They let the air out. They smiled.

"The paint?" Rico said, gesturing to the quiet kid next to him. "That's Chuy. He's the artist. I just drive it."

"The idle?" Leo said, pointing to the man in the charcoal shirt standing by the fender. "That's Pedro. NGB, LLC. He tuned the lash to a coherent frequency. Ask him about it, man, it's wild."

The crowd turned to Chuy and Pedro, but the respect lingered on the crew.

It was a feeling none of them had ever experienced before.

On the street, respect was currency, but it was counterfeit. It was printed on fear. People respected you because you were louder, harder, or more violent.

This? This was different.

This respect was heavy. It was solid. It came from effort. It came from talent. It came from building something beautiful instead of breaking something weak.

Tiny, the giant who usually acted as Leo’s enforcer, was showing a suburban dad how the trunk latch was shaved and smoothed. The dad was nodding, impressed. Tiny wasn't scaring him; he was teaching him.

"Feels good, doesn't it?" Pedro whispered, stepping up beside Leo.

Leo looked at the crowd, then at his own hands. "Yeah. It feels... real."

Even the judges, usually stone-faced men in polo shirts holding clipboards, were stopping. They walked around the cars, checking boxes they rarely checked. Fit and finish. Originality. Attention to detail.

One of them, an older man with a clipboard, looked at the engine bay of the Charger, then at the paint of the Impala. He looked up at Pedro and just nodded. A slow, respectful nod.

He didn't need to say anything. The checkmark on his page said it all.

Back at the truck, Sal watched it all. He saw Rico shaking hands with a cop who was admiring the Impala. He saw Leo laughing with a guy in a polo shirt. He saw Pedro orchestrating it all, the conductor of a symphony of steel and redemption.

Sal took off his glasses and wiped his eye with the back of his hand.

"Damn dust," Sal muttered, though the air was still. "That's all it is. Just dust."
