# Chapter 10: The Walk

Once the initial wave of the crowd had subsided, and the judges had moved on to the next row, Pedro signaled the team.

"Let's walk," he said.

They left one guy from each crew to watch the cars (Tiny and a cousin named Manny), and the rest of The Fleet moved out.

They walked as a unit, but relaxed. They weren't patrolling; they were appreciating.

They looked at the other cars. A '69 Camaro with a blower. A pristine '55 Bel Air. A custom mercury sled that scraped the asphalt.

They met the other competitors.

"Nice paint," Rico told a guy with a pearl-white Cadillac. And he meant it.
"Solid build," Leo told a Mustang driver. "That roll cage is tight."

They sampled the food—corn dogs, turkey legs, funnel cakes dusted with powdered sugar that ended up on their charcoal shirts.

It was a cycle. They got respect, so they gave respect. It was an energy that fed on itself, breaking down the usual barriers of "My Crew vs. Your Crew."

As they moved through the crowd, they started to hear the whispers. The wake of their arrival was still rippling through the show.

"That's them? Over there in the grey shirts?" a teenager whispered to his friend. "The team with those cars?"

"Yeah, that's The Fleet," his friend whispered back. "Did you see that Vette? It's like a time machine."

"The Vette is clean," an older guy in a lawn chair said, leaning in. "But did you see the damn mosaic under the hood of that Impala? And the way he did the wiring? It's like jewelry, man."

They kept walking, pretending not to hear, but smiling.

Then, they passed a group of guys arguing near the beer tent.

"I'm telling you, it was electric!" one guy was insisting. "Don't forget about that other beauty, the Charger. I can't even tell it's running half the time? Is it an EV?"

"It ain't an EV, stupid," his buddy laughed. "It's got a Hemi."

"Then why is it silent? A Hemi don't whisper!"

Leo nudged Pedro. "An EV?"

Pedro chuckled, biting into a corn dog. "Silent power, Leo. Keeps them guessing."

They walked on, surrounded by the smell of gasoline and sugar, carried by a wave of admiration they had built with their own hands.
