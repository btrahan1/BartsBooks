# Chapter 11: The Hardware

The sun was low, casting long shadows across the fairgrounds as the crackle of the PA system announced the awards ceremony. The team gathered near the main stage. They were tired, smelling of funnel cake and exhaust, but the energy was high.

First up was the Lowrider category.

"First Place," the announcer boomed, "For 'Best Paint' and 'Best of Show Lowrider'... The 1964 Chevy Impala!"

Rico let out a whoop. He shoved Chuy forward. "Go get it, artist!"

Chuy walked up the steps, shy and terrified, to accept the trophy that was nearly as tall as he was. The crowd cheered—not politely, but loudly. They had seen the mural. They knew.

Next was the Modern Muscle category.

"First Place, Modified," the announcer said. "The Matte Black Dodge Charger."

Leo walked up with a swagger that he tried to suppress but couldn't quite manage. He shook the judge's hand and held the trophy up to The Fleet. Pedro nodded. *Silent power.*

Then came the Classic Original category. The big one. The purist category.

Franklin Summers straightened his linen jacket.

"In Second Place," the announcer read, "The 1963 Corvette Sting Ray... Franklin Summers."

There was a murmur of surprise from The Fleet. Tiny looked like he wanted to go have a word with the judges.

Franklin just smiled. He walked up, accepted the red ribbon and the smaller trophy, and shook the winner's hand—a man who had trailered in a Mercedes SL.

When he came back down, Leo looked furious. "Second? That Mercedes didn't even drive here!"

 "It's fine, Leo," Franklin said, examining the ribbon. "The Mercedes is perfect. My car has stone chips on the valence because I drive it. Second place for a driven car is a victory. It has character."

He handed the ribbon to Sal. "Put this in the office."

The announcer tapped the microphone. The crowd quieted down. This was the final award. The Team Award.

"And finally," the announcer said. He looked at the card in his hand, then looked out at the parking lot where three cars sat in perfect formation.

He chuckled. He shook his head, wondering why he even had to read it.

"For Best Multi-Car Entry... honestly, guys, everyone knew who won this five minutes after they rolled in."

He pointed to the grey-shirted crew standing together in the sunset.

"The Fleet!"

The roar was deafening. It wasn't just the Southside guys cheering. It was the whole show. It was the acknowledgement that something new had arrived.

Pedro looked at Sal.

"Go get it, Boss," Pedro said.

"Me?" Sal asked. "I didn't do anything."

"You built the shop," Pedro said. "We just fill the bays."

Sal swallowed the lump in his throat. He walked up the stairs, his bad knee aching, to accept the massive gold cup. He held it up, catching the last rays of the Texas sun.

The Fleet wasn't just a car club anymore. It was a legend.
