# Chapter 3: The Scrivener's Snark

The silence that followed the messenger girl’s departure was heavy enough to make the floorboards groan. Or perhaps that was just Tobin’s ego, settling into the dust alongside the wood shavings and the fragments of his shattered confidence.

Tobin sat back down on his stool, his movements ginger and stiff. His jaw was throbbing—a slow, rhythmic pulse that felt like a tiny blacksmith was hammering on his bone. He touched his lip, winced, and looked at the smear of red on his fingers.

"Dad?" Tobin asked, his voice sounding thick through his swollen mouth. "What's a Nom?"

Alaric didn't answer immediately. He was busy carefully re-arranging the scrolls on his desk, his fingers moving with a deliberate precision that spoke of deep thought. He took a long, steadying breath, the kind usually reserved for dealing with the most difficult tax-collectors.

"A Nom?" Alaric finally said, looking up. He sounded genuinely perplexed. "Never heard of it before today. Sounds like one of those terms they use in the capital. Or maybe some special classification for the Master of the South Road’s people."

Tobin continued to rub his jaw, the skin already beginning to feel hot and tight. "She hit me pretty hard, Dad. Almost like she was using a physical enhancement spell, but... I didn't see any mana-shimmer. I didn't feel anything except her knuckles."

He leaned back, trying to reclaim some of his dignity. He looked at his status window again, the '12' in Intelligence still shining there, though it felt slightly dimmer now, like a candle in a drafty room. 

"When I become a mage," Tobin said, his voice gaining strength as his imagination took over, "I'll have a magic shield. I've read about them. The *Sphere of Aegis*, or the *Gale Shroud*. If someone tries to hit me like that, it'll bounce right off! They’ll be the ones lying in the dirt, wondering what happened."

Alaric stopped his work. He set his quill down—a rare occurrence in the middle of a crate—and turned fully to face his son. The warm light of the afternoon hit his ink-stained face, highlighting the lines of worry and the deep, practical knowledge of a man who had spent his life recording the hard realities of others.

"Oh really?" Alaric asked, his voice suspiciously neutral. "And what if they hit you twice?"

Tobin blinked. "Twice? Well, the shield would stay up. I’d maintain the mana-flow. I’d be prepared."

"Preparation cost mana, Tobin," Alaric said, ticking off points on his violet-stained fingers. "And mana is a resource, just like ink or parchment. How long do you think it will be before you develop a shield spell strong enough to stop a warrior from harming you until you can defeat them with spells?"

Tobin opened his mouth to cite *Magus Valerius’s Treatise on Deflection*, but his father didn't give him the chance.

"A warrior doesn't hit once and then wait for you to chant your three-syllable focus," Alaric continued. "They hit, and they keep hitting. They hit the shield until it cracks, or they hit around it, or they just throw a handful of sand in your eyes while you're busy making pretty lights. Do you honestly think a dozen weeks at the Academy will turn you into an iron fortress?"

Tobin scowled, his swelling lip making the expression look particularly lopsided. "Well, then, I'll get them from a distance!" he insisted, his hands moving as if tracing an imaginary targeting sigil. "I’ll see them coming. I’ll cast a *Frostbolt* or a *Kinetic Blast* before they even get within ten paces. That’s the whole point of magic! It's the superior reach! A sword is only as long as an arm, but a spell is as long as my sight!"

Alaric didn't argue. He didn't even look angry. He just leaned back against his desk and let a small, sharp smirk play across his face.

"Like you did today?" he asked.

The words hit Tobin harder than the girl's knuckles had. He felt the heat of a flush rising up his neck again, but this time it wasn't arrogance—it was the cold, stinging burn of a truth he couldn't deflect.

He looked at the floor, where he’d been lying just minutes ago. He hadn't seen the girl coming. He hadn't seen the punch. He’d been so busy shouting about his destiny and his twelve that he hadn't noticed the way she shifted her weight. He hadn't noticed the stillness in her eyes. He’d been at point-blank range, talking about distance.

"She... she was a messenger," Tobin muttered, but the defense felt hollow even to him.

"She was a messenger who walked through the South Road alone," Alaric corrected. "She was a messenger who delivered a message while you were still trying to find the first page of your own story. Magic isn't a distance weapon, son. It's a tool of the mind, and minds can be slow. Bodies? Bodies are fast. Bodies move while you're still thinking about your second syllable."

Alaric turned back to his scrolls, clicking his tongue as he picked up his quill again. "You have a twelve in Intelligence, Tobin. That's a fine number. But don't let those numbers blind you to the fact that you're currently a boy with a sore jaw and a desk full of uncopied land deeds. If you want to be a mage who lives long enough to wear the silver robes, you might want to start thinking about the space between the spells."

Tobin sat in silence for a long time after that. The shop was quiet again, the only sound the steady *skritch-skritch* of his father’s quill. He looked at his own quill, its nib dry and crusty.

He thought about the girl. The Nom.

She hadn't cast a spell. She hadn't used a staff. She’d just used her fist. And at that moment, her fist had been more 'magical' than every book on Tobin’s shelf. 

He dipped his quill into the inkwell, the black liquid swirling like a miniature storm. He thought about the "twice-hit." He thought about the "distance." He thought about the smirk on his father’s face.

"I need to know," Tobin whispered to himself, his voice lost in the scratch of his work. "I need to know what a Nom really is."

The deeds were still waiting. Three crates of old, brittle reality. But as Tobin began to write, his hand wasn't just copying names and boundaries. He was thinking about the velocity of the world. He was thinking about the punch.

And for the first time in his life, he wasn't looking at the stars. He was looking at his own two hands, wondering if they were ready to hold anything more than a pen.

He worked until the shadows in the shop grew long and the ink began to dry in the pots. The festival was coming. The System had arrived. But as Tobin closed the first crate of completed scrolls, he realized the '12' in his mind wasn't a destination. It was just the starting line for a very long, very dangerous race.

And he was already a lap behind.
