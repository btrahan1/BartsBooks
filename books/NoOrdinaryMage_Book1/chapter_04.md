# Chapter 4: The System and the Classes

The following few days in the shop were more productive than Alaric had expected. Tobin, for once, didn't mind the tedious work of copying old land deeds. In fact, he seemed to throw himself into the labor with a strange, quiet intensity. He didn't complain about the ink-smears or the stiffness in his back; he just kept his head down and his quill moving. 

It wasn't that his ego had been completely erased by the punch—if anything, it had simply been compressed, like a coiled spring. He still knew he would be a great mage someday. That was a given. That was written in the '12' that haunted his peripheral vision. But the punch had added a new variable to his calculations. He wasn't just thinking about power anymore; he was thinking about the *types* of power.

It was mid-afternoon on the Thursday before the Festival of the System when Tobin finally broke the silence. The shop was warm and gold-lit by the slanting sun, and the smell of drying parchment was particularly pungent today.

"Dad," Tobin said, pausing to wipe a drop of black ink from his thumb. "Can you tell me more about classes and the System? I mean, really tell me."

Alaric looked up, his glasses perched nervously on the tip of his nose. He took a moment to finish the sentence he was writing, then sighed. "You've been reading the primers for years, Tobin. You know the basics."

"I know the theory," Tobin insisted, leaning forward. "I know there are the four pillars of the Kingdom. Warriors for the front lines, Strength and Constitution. Healers for the back, Wisdom and Spirit. Rogues for the shadows, Agility and Luck. And Mages... for the stars. Intelligence and Charisma. Everyone knows about those. They’re the foundation of every legion and every guild."

"They are," Alaric agreed. "They are the roles the System assigns to provide order to our world. They are the shapes we are meant to fit into."

"But *she* didn't really seem to be any of those," Tobin said, his hand unconsciously drifting to his jaw, which was now a fading yellow-green bruise.

Alaric paused. "She?"

"That messenger," Tobin clarified, his eyes narrowing as he replayed the scene for the hundredth time. "She had a staff, but she didn't use it like a Mage’s focus. She had the Strength of a Warrior, but she moved with the speed of a Rogue. And her Agility—she said it was fifteen! That's higher than most adult town guards. But she didn't *feel* like a Rogue. Rogues are... twitchy. They look for the gap. She was just still. Like a stone."

He looked at his father, searching for a clue. "I wonder what class she was. She said she was a 'Nom'. But that's not in any of the books. Is there a hidden class? A prestige role for the Master of the South Road’s retainers?"

Alaric sat back, his fingers interlacing over his ink-stained tunic. He looked at the rows of scrolls on the walls, thousands of records of lives that had been neatly categorized by the System. He looked at the ledger where "Tobin - Potential Mage" would soon be officially inscribed.

"The System is a map, Tobin," Alaric said softly. "But the territory is always larger than the map. Most people are content to stay on the roads. They find a class, they follow the skills, they earn their levels, and they live a predictable life. A Warrior knows his shield, a Healer knows his poultices."

"And a Mage knows his spells," Tobin added.

"True," Alaric said. "But then there are those who don't care for maps. They don't look for a class to tell them who they are. They look at the world and they see what's needed. They don't want to be a 'Warrior' or a 'Mage'. They just want to be... effective."

Tobin frowned. "But the System *forces* a choice. At the Festival, you touch the Stone, and the Interface locks in your path. You can't just 'not' have a class."

"That is the law," Alaric said, his voice dropping an octave. "But I’ve lived long enough to know that laws have a way of fraying at the edges. Especially near the South Road, where the Master of the South Road keeps his own counsel."

"Do you think she’s unclassed?" Tobin asked, the idea both terrifying and exhilerating. "Is that possible? To have stats like that without the Class Bonuses?"

"I think," Alaric said, standing up to stretch, "that you’re spending a lot of time thinking about a girl who hit you once. Maybe you should spend that same energy thinking about what *you* are going to be when you touch that Stone in two days' time."

"I'm going to be a Mage," Tobin said, his voice firm. "I'm going to take the path. But... I'm going to keep my eyes open, Dad. I'm going to look for the things that aren't on the map."

Alaric smiled, a genuine, tired smile. "Then you'll already be ahead of most of the children in this village. Now, finish that crate. The Miller needs these deeds by sunset, or he’ll start claiming the stream belongs to the birds."

Tobin went back to his work, but his mind stayed on the messenger. *Nom.* It sounded short, sharp, and final. Like the punch.

As the sun dipped below the horizon and the shop filled with long, violet shadows, Tobin realized that his birthday hadn't just given him a status window. It had given him a question. And he had a feeling that the answer wasn't going to be found in any of the silver towers he’d dreamed of.

He looked at his hands, still stained with ink, and then at the door. The Festival was approaching. The music was already starting to drift from the village square—the flutes and the drums of the System's Arrival.

Tobin of Oakhaven was going to be a Mage. He was sure of it. But as he wrote the final names of the day, he found himself wondering if he would ever be a "Nom." And whether he’d have to get hit a second time to find out.
