# Chapter 10: The Echo of the System

The small, sputtering fire Tobin had managed to coax from the frozen dampness of the Ironwood Ridge was a pathetic thing. It flickered weakly against the dark, a fragile orange flower in a desert of grey stone. But to Tobin, whose hands were raw from thorns and whose bones felt like they’d been hollowed out by the cold, it was a triumph equal to any high-arcane feat. He sat huddled on a flat rock, his arms wrapped around his knees, breathing in the scent of cedar-smoke and his own singed hair.

He felt hollowed out. The "12" in his mind, once a beacon of arrogance, now felt like a heavy anchor he was barely dragging through the mud. 

Just as he reached out to poke a stray twig back into the embers, the world didn't stop, but it shimmered. 

A familiar, translucent veil of text flickered into existence in the center of his vision. It wasn't the violent, intrusive strobe of a Level-up or a Class-assignment. It was a subtle, almost apologetic pulse of light.

**[Skill Up: Survival Tactics (Unassisted) - Level 1]**
**[Attribute Gain: Though pathetic, you have demonstrated a modicum of agility. Agility +1]**

Tobin froze. The bit of dry oatcake he’d been about to chew remained caught in his teeth. 

"What?" he whispered, the sound lost in the crackle of the fire. 

The text hung there for a heartbeat longer before fading back into the indigo night. Tobin felt a strange, phantom sensation—a ghost of the System's touch. But it wasn't the warm, supportive 'assistance' Gregory had described. It felt more like a ledger-entry being etched into stone, a distant observation of a fact that had already occurred.

He looked at his hands. They were still scratched. His shoulder still ached from the log Elana had dropped. He didn't feel 'faster' or 'more agile' in the way the books promised. He just felt... like himself.

"I still have the System?" Tobin asked, his voice cracking. He looked toward the shadows where Elana was sitting, her mountain-pony tethered nearby. She was sharpening a small, wicked-looking knife with a whetstone, the *shhh-shhh* of the steel providing a rhythmic counterpoint to the wind.

Elana didn't look up, but the rhythm of the sharpening didn't slow. 

"I thought Gregory said..." Tobin trailed off, struggling to find the right words through his confusion. "I thought being Interdicted meant the System was gone. I thought I was a ghost. But it just told me I gained a skill. It just gave me a point of Agility."

Elana scoffed, a short, sharp sound that was more informative than a thousand village elder speeches. She finally paused, the knife catching the orange light of the fire.

"Of course you have the system, Tobin," she said. Her voice was flat, but not unkind for once. "The System isn't some god you can hide from. It’s the air we breathe. It’s the language the world speaks to keep track of itself. You can’t 'lose' the System any more than you can lose your shadow."

She resumed her sharpening. *Shhh-shhh.*

"It's always there," she continued. "It's providing you feedback. It’s a mirror, not a tutor. When you moved those branches without snapping your ankles, your body learned. The System simply recorded the fact. That 'Agility +1'? That’s the System admitting that you actually got better at moving. It didn't *make* you better. It just noticed that you made *yourself* better."

Tobin looked back at his status window. 

**Agility: 11**

He’d gained it. He’d actually earned a point. In Oakhaven, a point of Agility meant weeks of 'Agility Training' under a paid instructor, with the System assisting the muscle-growth every step of the way. He’d done it in one afternoon, through sheer, bloody-minded friction.

"Anything you earn on the NOM path is from your efforts alone," Elana said. She set the knife down and looked at him, her eyes reflecting the dying embers of the fire. "Gregory told you about Assistance, didn't he? The stencils? The scaffolding?"

Tobin nodded.

"Well, the difference is simple," Elana said, leaning forward. "For a Warrior, the System sees the intent and provides the power. It 'assists' the growth. For us, the System is purely observational. It provides the feedback, but it doesn't give you the help. If you want a skill, you perform the action until your body masters it. If you want a stat, you push your limits until your potential expands to accommodate the growth. The System doesn't help you lift the log; it just tells you how much it weighed after you’re done."

Tobin felt a surge of something that wasn't exhaustion. It was a terrifying, exhilarated clarity. The System wasn't his enemy, but it wasn't his friend either. It was just an accountant. A cold, impartial recorder of his own self-directed struggle.

"Survival Tactics," Tobin muttered. "Level one."

"A commoner with that skill would be assisted in finding water or identifying edible berries," Elana said, her voice dropping back into its usual professional tone. "You? You just know what you know. If you forget your lesson, the System won't nudge your hand away from a poison root. It’ll just record the time of your death."

She stood up, sheathing her knife in a single, fluid motion. She walked over to the horse and pulled out a heavy wool blanket, tossing it toward Tobin. It hit him in the chest, the weight of it surprising him.

"Sleep," she commanded. "Tomorrow we start the climb. Ironwood Ridge doesn't care about your Intelligence of twelve. It cares about whether your '11' in Agility is enough to keep you from falling off a cliff."

Tobin wrapped the blanket around himself, the scratchy wool a luxury he hadn't expected. He lay down on the hard, uneven ground, using his pack as a pillow. The silver-capped inkwell inside poked him in the back of the head, a reminder of the mark he still intended to leave.

He looked at the stars, which felt closer and sharper here than they ever had in Oakhaven. He didn't have a Mage's focus. He didn't have a shield spell. He was a boy with a sore jaw and a single, unassisted point of Agility.

But as the fatigue finally pulled him toward sleep, he realized that the '11' in his mind felt realer than the '12' ever had. The '12' was a potentiality, a gift of birth. The '11' was a trophy. It was a record of thorns, logs, and a fire that he’d built with his own two hands.

The System was watching. It was recording. 

And Tobin vowed that before he reached the end of the South Road, he would give that cold accountant something truly spectacular to write down.

He fell asleep to the sound of the wind through the Twisted Ironwood, the '1' he’d earned tonight feeling like the first word in a new book. A book where he was the author, and the System was merely the ink.

No Ordinary Mage. 

The path was unassisted. The growth was slow. But the power... the power was his.
