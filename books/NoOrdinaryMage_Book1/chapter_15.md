# Chapter 15: The Summit Town

The final few hundred yards of the Ironwood Ascent were less of a climb and more of a vertical negotiation. Tobin’s world had narrowed to the space of three feet ahead of him—a kaleidoscope of jagged grey slate, twisted ironwood roots that felt like iron bars, and the rhythmic, rasping sound of his own breath. His lungs felt like they were being scraped by sandpaper, and the +1 Wisdom he’d earned the previous day didn't make his legs any less hollow.

But as he pulled himself over a final, weather-beaten ledge, the grinding incline abruptly vanished.

Tobin collapsed onto his hands and knees, his chest heaving as he drank in the thin, ice-cold air. He expected to see a secluded fortress, perhaps a cluster of silver towers similar to the ones he’d dreamed of in Oakhaven, or at the very least, a walled monastery where the 'ghosts' of the South Road hid from the Empire’s gaze.

Whatever he was expecting at the Summit wasn't what he saw.

Before him lay a bowl-shaped plateau, nestled between four towering peaks that looked like the teeth of a giant. And within that bowl sat a town. It wasn't a military outpost or a scholarly retreat; it was a living, breathing community. Smoke drifted lazily from stone chimneys, the scent of baking bread and coal-fires mingling with the sharp tang of the mountain air. There were gardens terraced into the slopes, children playing in the central square, and the rhythmic *clink-clink* of a busy smithy.

"Is... is this it?" Tobin panted, pushing himself into a sitting position. "The Academy?"

Elana snorted, her mountain-pony moving past him with its usual tireless gait. "Academies are for people who like to sit in circles and talk about mana-density. This is the Summit."

Sir Gregory dismounted, his silver armor catching the midday sun. He looked out over the town with a gaze that held a rare hint of warmth—the look of a man who had finally come home.

"Is everyone here a Nom?" Tobin asked, his eyes wide as they began to walk down the gentle slope toward the town’s entrance. He looked at a group of men hauling a heavy cart of ore, their muscles bulging with unassisted power. He looked at a woman tending a terrace of mountain-herbs, her movements efficient and precise.

"Of course not!" Elana said, her voice holding that familiar edge of clinical disdain. "We have plenty of Common classes here as well. Bakers, smiths, weavers. You think we grow our own grain and weave our own wool between lessons?"

She gestured toward a group of men in leather armor practicing with wooden staves in a nearby yard. "And we have quite a few regular Classes as well. Fighters, Rogues, even a few Mages who realized the towers weren't all they were cracked up to be."

Tobin stilled. "Wait, I thought we had to learn all of our skills on our own? I thought the NOM path was about being unassisted?"

A group of teenagers sitting on a nearby stone wall let out a series of snickers as Tobin passed. One of them, a boy with sun-bleached hair and a jagged scar across his knuckles, winked at him.

Sir Gregory spoke, his voice carrying that same resonant depth that made the village square feel small. "We do learn the skills on our own, Tobin. That is the fundamental Law. But that doesn't mean we don't need teachers."

He grinned—a genuine, wolfish expression that made his weathered face look years younger. "The System grants skills like a gift. You touch the Stone, you accept the class, and suddenly you know how to swing a mace or channel a flame. The 'arithmetic' is handled for you. The muscle-memory is infused into your nerves by the Interface."

They reached the central square, where a large fountain carved from a single block of ironwood stood. The water was crystalline and cold, splashing over the dark, polished wood in a rhythmic cadence.

"In the Summit," Gregory said, gesturing toward a tall, broad-shouldered man who was currently demonstrating a complex sequence of sword-strikes to a rapt audience of children. "We learn by observing, doing, and absorbing. The man over there is a Warrior—a real Class, with all the assistance the Empire can provide. He is here to show us the 'Map.' He shows us where the strikes go, how the weight shifts, how the mana-flow *should* feel."

"But he doesn't give us the skill," Elana added, her voice dropping into a rare moment of instruction. "We watch him until our eyes bleed. We copy him until our muscles scream. We fail a thousand times, and the System doesn't nudge our hands or sharpen our focus. And then, finally, when our body understands the territory because it has walked every inch of it... the System notices."

Tobin looked at his status window. He thought about his Agility of 11 and his Survival Tactics skill. He’d earned those. They hadn't been infused; they’d been forged.

"Observation and action," Gregory said, stopping in front of a modest stone house near the square's center. "That is the NOM pedagogy. We use the specialized classes as mirrors. We see what they can do with their 'assistance,' and then we find the way to do it through raw, unassisted brilliance. We don't hate the Classes, Tobin. We study them. We dismantle them. And then we build something better from the pieces."

Tobin looked around the town with new eyes. He saw a 'Commoner' baker who moved with a grace that suggested an Agility higher than any villager back home. He saw a 'Mage' in a simple grey robe talking to a group of young children about the way the wind moved through the peaks.

It wasn't a town of outcasts. It was a town of masters—people who had realized that the System's gifts were a tether, and that true power lay in the unassisted grind.

"So I'm not here for an infusion," Tobin whispered, the realization finally settling in his chest.

"You're here for an education," Gregory said. "And believe me, after three days on the South Road, the 'arithmetic' of mana is going to feel like a holiday compared to the arithmetic of survival."

He looked at the boy with the '12' in Intelligence. "Welcome to the Summit, Tobin. Try not to let the Commoners out-work you. They’ve been drawing their own lines since before you were born."

Tobin stood in the center of the square, the cold mountain air feeling like a challenge he was finally ready to face. He didn't have a class. He didn't have a stencil. He didn't have a map.

But as he looked at the children practicing strikes and the masters watching the world with un-goggled eyes, he realized that he was no longer a ghost. He was a student of the territory. 

And as he walked toward the first barrel, his mind already beginning to calculate the physics of the lift, he knew he was just getting started.
