# Chapter 16: The Baker's Five

The Ironwood Summit was a high-altitude paradox. The air was thin and frigid, yet the activity of the town generated a strange, vibrant warmth. Tobin stood by the central fountain, his hands hovering over the first of the five barrels. It was made of thick, iron-banded oak—dark, heavy, and already half-full of the icy mountain water that surged from the heart of the plateau.

"Haul five," Sir Gregory had said. "Unassisted."

Tobin took a deep breath, his 'Intelligence 12' brain automatically trying to calculate the most efficient center of gravity. At home in Oakhaven, a task like this would have been handled by a cart, a pack animal, or perhaps a simplified *Levitation* cantrip by a bored apprentice. Here, it was just Tobin, his 11 Strength, and a debt of survival that needed to be paid.

He gripped the rim of the first barrel. The cold water bit into his fingers like a thousand tiny needles. With a grunt that felt like it originated in the soles of his boots, he heaved.

The weight was staggering. It wasn't just the mass of the water; it was the way the liquid shifted, a dynamic, sloshing burden that threatened to pull his center of gravity into the grey slate of the square. Maintaining balance was tricky, every step a frantic correction of the ankles and core.

"Water for you, Sir Baker!" Tobin managed to gasp as he reached the bakehouse, the barrel's rim biting into his shoulder. "I'll be back... with more!"

The Baker, a man whose forearms were the size of Tobin’s thighs and dusted with white flour, looked up from a heavy slab of dough. He didn't smile, but he gave a sharp, appreciative nod. "Empty it into the cistern, boy. The yeast doesn't wait for your breath to return."

Tobin dove into the task with an enthusiasm that was partly genuine pride and partly a desperate need to prove he wasn't just a 'ghost' from a dusty village shop. He wasn't just hauling water; he was hauling his own future.

As he made his second trip, he noticed he wasn't alone. The town square was a theater of observation. Several other trainees—young men and women with the lean, predator-like builds of more advanced NOM students—were watching him. Some wore smirks of amusement, others a look of clinical curiosity.

"Well, look at the new ghost," one young man commented, leaning against a stack of crates. He was a bit taller than Tobin, a bit stronger looking, with a shock of dark hair and eyes that seemed to have the 'System goggles' permanently engaged. "I'll see how he feels after the third barrel."

The comment was followed by a few scattered snickers from a group sitting on the fountain's edge. Tobin paid them no mind. He couldn't afford to. His 11 Agility was currently focused entirely on the treacherous slope between the well and the bakery door.

By the time he finished the second barrel, the " God among boys" brain was starting to send frantic signals of distress. His shoulders felt like they were being crushed by hot iron, and his calves were screaming in a language he hadn't known existed.

But it was the third barrel that almost broke him.

Halfway to the bakery, his foot slipped on a patch of mountain moss. The barrel tilted dangerously, the water sloshing over the edge and drenching his tunic in freezing liquid. For a heartbeat, Tobin was sure he was going to drop it. He could almost hear the derisive laughter of the crowd, the final confirmation that he was just "Ordinary" after all.

*Resilience,* Gregory’s voice echoed in his mind. *The specialist is a hammer. You are the territory.*

Tobin bit his lip until he tasted copper, shifted his weight, and forced his legs to lock. He didn't drop the barrel. He wrestled it back into balance, his breathing a ragged, whistling sound in the thin air.

When he finally delivered the third barrel, he didn't exchange nods. He just dumped the water and turned back toward the square, his eyes fixed on the pavement. He could feel the smirks of the other trainees fading, replaced by a strange, quiet tension. They were no longer watching a joke; they were watching a process.

The fourth barrel was a blur of gray stone and white-knuckled pain. He didn't feel his hands anymore. He didn't feel the cold. He only felt the rhythmic thrum of his own pulse, a steady, unassisted cadence that refused to stop.

When he reached for the fifth and final barrel, the square had gone silent. Even the tall boy with the shock of dark hair was standing now, his arms crossed over his chest, his eyes narrowed as if trying to calculate the arithmetic of Tobin’s persistence.

Tobin heaved. The world tilted. His thighs were burning, his lungs were ragged, and every step felt like a victory against the entire mountain. He didn't walk; he conquered the space between the well and the bakehouse.

When he delivered the last drop into the cistern, he didn't say a word. He simply let the empty barrel thud to the floor and then lay down on the cool stone ground, his chest heaving as if he were trying to swallow the sky.

A shadow fell over him.

The Baker stood there, his flour-dusted hands resting on his hips. He wasn't nodding now; he was actually smiling, a small, begrudging expression of respect. He reached into a basket and pulled out a large, steaming loaf of dark bread and a thick wedge of yellow cheese.

"Nice work, young man," the Baker said, his voice booming in the quiet bakery. "Most stop at three. They think they’ve proven enough. But the bread needs five barrels, whether the ghost is tired or not."

He dropped the bread and cheese beside Tobin’s head. "Eat. You’ve earned the caloric debt."

Tobin didn't move for a long time. He just lay there, the smell of fresh bread filling his senses, more intoxicating than any mana-scent he’d ever imagined. 

Suddenly, the gold-veiled text chimed in his mind.

**[Attribute Milestone Approaching]**
**[Endurance +1 Progress: 85%]**
**[Strength +1 Progress: 70%]**
**[New Stat Unlocked: Will 11]**
**[System Note: You'll definitely need more of this.]**

It wasn't a gain yet, but it was a ledger. A confirmation that the arithmetic of his labor was being recorded with impartial accuracy. 

Tobin sat up slowly, his muscles trembling, and took a bite of the bread. It was coarse, heavy, and tasted like life itself. He looked out the bakery door toward the square, where the other trainees were dispersing. The tall boy looked back once, his expression no longer mocking, but thoughtful.

"I finished," Tobin whispered to himself.

He wasn't a Mage yet. He wasn't a Warrior. He was just a boy who had moved five barrels of water. 

But as he sat there in the warm, flour-dusted air of the bakery, eating the bread he had earned with his own two hands, Tobin of Oakhaven realized that the Summit wasn't just a place to learn skills. It was a place to learn that the only limit to a person's potential was the moment they decided they had "proven enough."

And Tobin, despite the burning in his thighs and the ache in his spirit, knew that he was a long, long way from being done.

"Tomorrow," he muttered through a mouthful of cheese. "I'll do six."

The Baker laughed, a deep, hearty sound that echoed through the stone walls. "We’ll see, Grasshopper. We’ll see."
