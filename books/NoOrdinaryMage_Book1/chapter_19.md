# Chapter 19: The Arithmetic of Mana

If the first three days at the Ironwood Summit had been a trial for Tobin’s muscles, the fourth day promised to be a trial for his soul. 

He walked beside Elana as they left the Mess Hall, his 12 Endurance and his 11 Will fueling a stride that was finally starting to feel at home on the mountain slate. He had sorted Thurgood’s pile of slag—a task that required more patience than strength—and his mind was already racing ahead to the afternoon. 

"The first spell," Tobin whispered, the words a rhythmic mantra in his mind. 

He’d spent twelve years dreaming of this moment. In his father’s shop, he’d read every scrap of parchment that mentioned magecraft. He’d imagined himself cloaked in shimmering blue robes, standing atop a mountain peak as he summoned a storm of fire or a wall of impenetrable light. *Fireball.* That was the classic. Or perhaps a *Lightning Bolt* that would crack the sky. Even a *Shield* spell, something to turn away the punches that had so often found his jaw.

"Where are we going?" Tobin asked as Elana led him toward a quiet, narrow ledge that overlooked the vast, misty valley to the west. 

"To the classroom," Elana replied without looking back.

The 'classroom' was nothing more than a flat slab of granite, shielded from the wind by a massive protrusion of ironwood. Sir Gregory was already there, his silver armor muted in the afternoon haze. He was sitting on a low stone stool, holding a piece of chalk and a small, blackened iron plate.

"Seat yourself, ghost," Gregory commanded.

Tobin sat on the cold stone, his heart hammering against his ribs. He felt like a coiled spring, ready to unleash the mana he’d been hoarding in his '12 Intelligence' brain since Oakhaven.

"You’ve built the body," Gregory said, his voice carrying that same resonant depth. "You’ve understood the organism. Now, you must learn to speak the language of the territory. You want to be a Mage. So, we shall start with your first spell."

Tobin leaned forward, his eyes wide. *Fireball?* No, probably too dangerous for a first attempt. *Flame Jet?* Maybe *Frost Beam?*

"The spell," Gregory said, holding up the blackened iron plate, "is *Spark*."

Tobin froze. The excitement that had been fueling him for three days vanished as if someone had doused it with ice water. 

"Spark?" Tobin repeated, his voice flat. "Just... a spark? Like the kind I made with the flint and steel on my first night?"

Elana let out a short, sharp snort. "A bit better than that, hopefully. But yes. *Spark*."

"But... I’m an Interdicted!" Tobin blurted out, the 'God among boys' ego flaring up for a final, desperate moment. "I have a 12 in Intelligence! I’ve read the theory on mana-resonance! I want to learn something... something significant!"

"Significant?" Gregory mused, a faint, dangerous smile touching his lips. "You think *Spark* is insignificant because the System grants it to every twelve-year-old who touches the Stone. You think it’s small because the 'Ordinary' Mages use it as a cantrip to light their pipes."

He tossed the chalk to Tobin. "Tell me, Tobin. How many variables are required to manifest a single ember of light without a System-granted stencil?"

Tobin looked at the chalk, then at the blackened plate. "I... I don't know."

"An 'Ordinary' Mage doesn't need to know," Gregory said, his voice dropping into a clinical tone. "When they cast *Spark*, they invoke a stencil. The System handles the mana-density, the resonance-frequency, the atmospheric pressure corrections, and the containment geometry. They provide the 'intent,' and the System provides the arithmetic. It’s like a child pointing at a picture of bread and expecting a loaf to appear."

He leaned in, his silver armor reflecting the dull grey of the clouds. "A NOM has no stencil. To make a spark, you must calculate the exact vibration of the mana in the air around you. You must account for the humidity, the altitude of the Ironwood Ridge, and the specific resistance of your own mana-conduits. You must perform the math of creation itself, in real-time, with nothing but your own mind as the engine."

"Calculate?" Tobin whispered. 

"Arithmetic, Tobin," Elana said, sitting cross-legged on the stone beside him. "The 'Ordinary' Mage uses a hammer. We build the hammer, from the molecular level up. A *Fireball* is just a *Spark* with more math and more mana. If you can't calculate the resonance of a single candle-flicker, you’ll never handle the roar of a furnace."

Gregory pointed to the plate. "Look at the mana-mist, Tobin. Really *look* at it. Don't look for the spell. Look for the numbers."

Tobin took a deep breath and closed his eyes. He activated his *Perception* skill, focusing not on the world around him, but on the invisible currents he’d felt since he was a child. In Oakhaven, the mana had been a vague, shimmering warmth. Here, in the high silence of the Summit, it felt... different.

It felt like a sea of static.

Tiny, prickling waves of energy washed over his skin. They weren't smooth; they were jagged, irregular, and terrifyingly complex. He realized that the "shimmering blue light" of his dreams was just a crude simplification. The real territory was a storm of mathematical noise.

"I see it," Tobin whispered, his brow furrowed in concentration. "But it’s... it’s moving too fast. I can't find a pattern."

"Because you're looking for a pattern that doesn't exist," Gregory said. "You must *create* the pattern. You must find the resonance-frequency of the air in front of your finger and hold it until the mana collapses into heat."

Tobin began to draw on the iron plate with the chalk. He recalled the few fragments of higher-order mana-theory he’d read in Alaric’s shop. *Density over Volume equals Resonance...* No, that wasn't right. *Resonance is a function of Will over Intelligence multiplied by the atmospheric constant...*

His '12' in Intelligence, which had always felt like a giant among pygmies, suddenly felt like a very small, very overwhelmed child in the face of the mountain's static.

**[Skill Unlocked: Mana Perception (Unassisted) - Level 1]**
**[Intelligence Milestone Approaching: 5%]**

The hours bled into the evening. Tobin didn't make a spark. He didn't even make a wisp of smoke. He spent the entire afternoon staring at the iron plate, his chalk-covered hands trembling, as he tried to solve the arithmetic of a single, tiny point of light.

He realized that the "disappointing" *Spark* was actually a masterpiece of calculation. To manifest a single ember without a System stencil was like trying to catch a specific drop of rain in a hurricane using nothing but a pair of tweezers.

"It's impossible," Tobin muttered, his head throbbing with a dull, sharp ache.

"It's not impossible," Elana said, her voice softer than usual. "It’s just... unassisted. You're learning to think in a way the human mind wasn't designed for. You're building the conduits in your brain at the same time you're using them."

Gregory stood up, the silver armor catching the last rays of the sun. "Enough for today. You’ve seen the arithmetic, Tobin. You’ve felt the weight of the territory. Go back to the Barracks. Sleep. Let your mind knit."

He looked at the iron plate, which was covered in half-finished equations and frantic scribbles. "You didn't quit. And you didn't look for a shortcut. That is the first step of a NOM's magic."

Tobin stood up, his legs feeling heavy, his mind feeling like it had been scraped across a stone. He wasn't excited anymore. He was terrified. He was fascinated. And he was, for the first time, truly humble in the face of the magic he’d always thought he deserved.

"Master Gregory?" Tobin asked as they began to walk back toward the town.

"Yes, ghost?"

"How long... how long did it take Elana to make her first spark?"

Gregory looked at Elana, who looked away toward the darkening sky. "Three weeks," the Warden said. "And she had a higher Perception than you."

Tobin looked at his '11' in Perception and his '12' in Intelligence. He felt a sudden, fierce pride. Three weeks.

"I’ll do it in two," Tobin muttered, more to himself than to them.

Elana smirked. "We’ll see, Grasshopper. We’ll see."

Tobin walked back to the Barracks, his head still ringing with the static of the mountain’s mana. He didn't have a fireball. He didn't have a shield. He only had a blackened iron plate and a growing understanding of just how much math lay between him and the fire.

As he fell into a deep, dreamless sleep, the equations began to dance in his mind, the first, faint whispers of the power he was actually earning.
