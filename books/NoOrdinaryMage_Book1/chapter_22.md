# Chapter 22: The Weaver's Pattern

The next morning was a beautiful day. The sun was barely up, a thin sliver of pale gold cutting through the cold mountain mist. The air was sharp, smelling of wet slate and the first faint wisps of woodsmoke from the bakery. 

Tobin stood on the Barracks steps, his lungs drinking in the thin, ice-cold atmosphere. He felt... different. The '12' in Endurance and the '11' in Agility were no longer just numbers on a screen; they were a physical sensation of integrated capability. The sluggish, heavy feeling of the Oakhaven scrivener's shop was a memory fading as fast as the mist.

"You know what," Tobin said to himself, his voice small in the quiet morning. "I feel like running. I think my new routine will be to do a few laps in and around town. I haven't even seen everything yet!"

He didn't wait for permission. He didn't check the square for Sir Gregory or Elana. He simply started to move.

It wasn't a sprint—he wasn't a Warrior with a System-granted burst of Speed. It was a fast jog, a rhythmic, self-directed pace that required him to constantly adjust his balance on the uneven mountain paths. He ran past the silent fountain, his boots echoing on the grey slate. He ran down the terraced garden slopes, where the soil was dark and the mountain herbs were starting to wake.

He had to stop a few times, his breath coming in ragged gasps that felt like needles in his chest. The altitude was a silent adversary, a reminder that his '12' Endurance was still a work in progress. But as he stood there, hands on his knees, watching the sun illuminate the peaks, he felt a surge of genuine joy. 

He was moving his own body. He was exploring his own territory.

The System noticed, but so did the town. As he passed the smithy, he saw Thurgood already at the bellows; the man didn't look up, but his hammer missed a single beat—a blacksmith’s version of a nod. Near the square, he saw a few of the other trainees—Kaelen among them—standing near the Barracks. Kaelen watched him with a look of genuine surprise. NOMs didn't usually run laps; they usually hoarded their energy for the chores.

Tobin finished his third lap near the eastern gate, his tunic damp with sweat and his face flushed with the kind of heat that regeneration potions couldn't simulate. He slowed to a walk, his legs feeling heavy but solid.

He realized he was standing in front of a building he hadn't noticed before. It was a long, low structure of light-colored stone, with large windows that caught the morning light. The sound coming from within wasn't the *clink* of iron or the *thud* of dough; it was a rhythmic, complex clicking—the mechanical music of a dozen looms.

Tobin stepped inside, and the temperature immediately shifted. It was warm, but not with the roar of a forge. It was a dry, comfortable warmth, smelling of raw wool, dyed silk, and the faint, sweet scent of lavender.

The Weaver's shop was a forest of threads. Dozens of vertical looms lined the walls, their wooden frames looking like the skeletons of forgotten giants. Men and women moved between them with a grace that was almost meditative, their fingers dancing across the strings with a speed that made Tobin’s 'Perception 1' dizzy.

He walked toward a woman at the center of the room. She was older, with silver hair woven into a complex braid and eyes that seemed to be looking at a dozen things at once. She was currently working on a tapestry that looked like a map of the Ironwood Ridge, but the detail was so fine it looked like it had been painted with thread.

"Master Weaver?" Tobin asked, his voice sounding rough after his run.

The woman didn't stop. Her hands were a blur of motion, throwing a wooden shuttle back and forth with a rhythmic *chunk-clack*. "Mistress Elara," she corrected, not looking up. "And you're the one Gregory brought. The boy with the iron pebble and the empty mana-plate."

Tobin blinked. The news in the Summit traveled faster than a *Spark* spell. "How did you...?"

"Thurgood complains about the noise of your boots. The Baker complains about the speed of your appetite. And the mountain speaks for itself," she said, finally stopping the shuttle. She looked at him, her gaze sharp and analytical. "You're early for a guest. And you're late for a student. What are you here for?"

Tobin looked around at the looms, at the intricate patterns of the tapestries. He thought about the mana-mist on the ledge—the static he was trying to solve. He saw a similarity here. The threads were like the vibrations of mana. The shuttle was the will. The pattern was the spell.

"I haven't seen everything yet," Tobin said, holding out a hand to clarify. "Anything I can do to help out this morning? I... I'm good with my hands, and I can follow a rhythm."

Mistress Elara leaned back, her silver braid catching the light. She looked at his soot-stained fingers and his sweaty tunic. She looked at the '11' in Agility and the '12' in Intelligence. 

"You want to help?" she asked, a small, knowing smile touching her lips. "The looms produce the fabric, but the bobbins are the lifeblood. I have a hundred spindles of mountain-silk that need winding. It's a task for people with patience, steady hands, and an eye for the way a thread tensions."

She gestured toward a large wooden wheel in the corner. "You wind. You watch the tension. You watch the way the fibers interlock. If you snap a single thread, you're back to hauling water."

Tobin walked to the wheel. It was a simple device, but the raw silk—a shimmering, pale blue thread—looked as fragile as a spiderweb. 

"I'll be careful," Tobin said.

He sat on a low stool and began to wind. It was a different kind of labor than the forge or the bakery. It didn't require raw strength, and it didn't require the brute-force endurance of the ascent. It required a fine, delicate application of pressure, a constant awareness of the 'territory' of the thread.

As he worked, his 'Intelligence 12' began to dissect the process. He saw the way the twist of the silk provided its strength. He saw the way the tension adjusted the resonance of the thread when the loom struck it.

He wasn't just winding silk. He was learning how a pattern was built. He was learning that complexity wasn't a storm to be conquered, but a series of individual threads to be understood and integrated.

"The pattern is always there, boy," Mistress Elara said from her loom, even though she wasn't looking at him. "The System gives you a completed cloak. We learn to weave the thread ourselves. If you understand the weave, you'll understand why the cloak holds together."

Tobin didn't answer. He was too focused on the shimmering blue silk, his mind already beginning to map the 'pre-spark' resonance he’d felt onto the structure of the weaving. 

He had started the morning with a run to see the town. He was ending it by seeing the foundation of reality.

Tobin finished the final bobbin, the blue silk mountain-silk glowing softly in the afternoon light. He stood up, his legs a bit stiff but his mind feeling unusually sharp—like a blade that had just been honed to a perfect edge.

"Thank you for the lesson, Master Weaver!" Tobin said, giving a respectful bow. "The water won't haul itself and that blacksmith can get pretty ornery sometimes! Best to catch him in a good mood!"

Mistress Elara chuckled, her silver braid swaying. "Go on then, ghost. Tell Thurgood if he snaps another needle for my looms, I'll start charging him in silver-ink."

As Tobin set out from the shop, the cool mountain air hitting his face, he felt a strange, humming clarity in the back of his mind. It was the "pre-spark" resonance, still vibrating in the silence he'd built during meditation, but it felt... denser. Larger.

Tobin stood in the middle of the square, quietly. He didn't pump his fist, he didn't celebrate wildly. The '13' in Intelligence was a shimmering light in his mind, but it was just one part of the pattern.

"A Step," Tobin whispered to himself, the cool mountain air tasting like potential. "Just another step. It's not all about intelligence. It's about balance."

He gripped the iron slag in his pocket, his mind already beginning to re-calculate the *Spark* resonance with the new architecture of his brain. But he didn't head for the ledge just yet. Instead, he turned his gaze back toward the well by the bakery.

"Those barrels aren't going to move themselves!"

He began to walk toward the water-task, his pace steady, his focus absolute. He was a student of the territory, and at the Summit, every muscle and every thought had to pull its own weight.
