**Book One: The Aspirant**


**Chapter 1: The Fool\'s Dream**

The Graywall Orphanage smelled of boiled cabbage and damp stone. It was
a smell that clung to everything---the thin wool blankets, the
rough-spun tunics, and the very skin of the thirty-odd children who
called it home. For sixteen-year-old Gabriel, it was the only world he
had ever really known. He was quiet, quick, and practiced in the art of
being forgettable---a necessary skill when the bigger boys like Roric
enforced the primary rule of Graywall: what was yours was only what you
were strong enough to hold onto.

The one reprieve from the dreary routine, the one day the cabbage smell
seemed to fade, was the yearly arrival of Master Valerius, the Queen's
own storyteller. He was a man who seemed to carry the warmth of the
Queen\'s own hearth with him, his fine but well-traveled clothes a stark
contrast to their own drab grays.

The other children would listen with wide eyes to tales of mighty
Fighters cleaving through goblin hordes or cunning Rogues slipping past
the watchful eyes of dragons. For them, the stories were simple and
direct. Strength meant joining the City Guard. Dexterity meant you might
be a good messenger. Those were achievable goals, tangible rungs on a
ladder that could lift them out of Graywall.

But Gabriel listened differently. He didn\'t just hear the stories; he
dissected them. He memorized every word, fascinated by the tales
Valerius told of the advanced classes, the ones that required a person
to walk two paths at once.

One year, after the main stories were done, Gabriel, then about twelve,
dared to ask a question. \"Master Valerius,\" he'd said, his voice small
but clear in the quiet hall. \"Are there any heroes who walked\... all
four paths?\"

Master Valerius had paused, a flicker of surprise in his ancient eyes.
He looked down at the small, underfed boy with an intense gaze. \"A
sharp question,\" he\'d mused. \"The legends speak of such a thing. Not
a class, but a Scion. A being who embodies all four disciplines in
perfect balance. But the cost\... the experience required is a mountain
ten times higher than any other. It is a path so arduous it is
considered a myth. A fool\'s dream.\"

Roric had shoved Gabriel\'s shoulder, whispering, \"Fool\'s dream for a
fool.\"

But for Gabriel, it was anything but. The fool\'s dream became his
secret obsession. While the other boys practiced clumsy sword swings
with sticks in the yard, Gabriel pursued balance. He would sneak up to
the steep, slate-shingled roof of the orphanage and practice walking the
ridgepole, his heart pounding, for a sliver of **Dexterity**. He would
sit with Matron Elara as she tended to the sick, watching her mix herbs
and offer quiet words of comfort, hoping to grasp an ounce
of **Wisdom**. He would replay Master Valerius\'s stories in his mind,
trying to understand the strategies of the great Mages, fighting for a
spark of **Intelligence**. And every day, he endured the gnawing hunger,
the back-breaking chores, and the cold that seeped through the walls,
turning his suffering into a silent lesson in **Constitution**.

From a place beyond the veil of Carnadium, Selyra, the Goddess of
Twilight and Balance, watched him. While other gods saw a scrawny,
unremarkable orphan, she saw a spirit. He did not pray for strength. He
did not pray for skill. He prayed for a *chance*. Bound by The System\'s
laws, she could not reach down and change his stats or grant him
unearned power. But the rules said nothing about nudging a traveler
towards a forgotten map.

On the last night of his fifteenth year, as Gabriel slept fitfully,
keenly aware that tomorrow his life would be measured and judged, Selyra
sent him that nudge.

He fell into a deep, unnaturally vivid sleep. He was underwater, the
pressure immense, his lungs screaming for air. A calmer feeling pushed
the terror aside---a sense of purpose. *Swim down,* a thought that was
not his own whispered in his mind. He obeyed, kicking deeper into the
crushing black. Just as his vision began to fade, a faint, silvery light
bloomed below. He swam towards it and broke through a shimmering,
invisible barrier.

Air. He gasped, his lungs burning with sweet, breathable air.

He was in a vast cavern, the only light coming from glowing moss on the
impossibly high ceiling. In the center of the cavern, rising from the
smooth stone floor, was a door. It was made of a seamless, black
material that seemed to drink the light, and etched across its surface
in glowing, silver runes were words he had never seen, yet understood
with perfect clarity.

**TIME DILATION DUNGEON**

Gabriel\'s eyes snapped open.

He was back on his straw mattress. The first gray hints of dawn were
filtering through the grimy windows. The dream was fading like mist, but
the image of the door and its inscription was burned into his memory. He
didn\'t know where it was. He didn\'t know how to get there. But he knew
it was real.

As he heard the heavy, rhythmic footsteps of the Ministry Guard
approaching the dormitory door, he felt not despair at the numbers he
was about to receive, but a flicker of defiant, unquenchable hope. He
had his quest.
