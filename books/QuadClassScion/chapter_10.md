**Chapter 10: The First Spark**

The road was a harsher teacher than the farm. By the fourth night, they
were deep in the King\'s Wood, a sprawling forest that swallowed the
road for days at a time. Here, Silas\'s true lessons began. He taught
Gabriel how to read the language of broken twigs, which berries would
fill your stomach versus which would knot it with poison, and the stark
reality of hunting for their supper.

On the fifth day, Silas handed him a simple leather sling. \"A warrior
needs to be able to strike from a distance.\"

Hours later, Gabriel stalked through the underbrush and saw it: a plump
rabbit nibbling on clover. The number screamed in his head. *Twenty
thousand.* This was his chance. An easy kill. He loaded a stone, his
movements silent, his aim steady. He drew back his arm, muscles tensed
for the killing blow.

And he stopped.

He looked at the small, living creature. Killing not for food, but for a
single, solitary experience point\... it felt cheap. It felt wrong.
Slowly, carefully, he lowered his arm and melted back into the woods.

When he returned to camp empty-handed, Silas simply raised an eyebrow.

\"I had one,\" Gabriel admitted. \"I let it go.\"

\"We have food?\" Silas asked.

\"Yes.\"

\"Then you made the right choice,\" the old man said. \"A hunter kills
for need, not for sport. Never let The System turn you into a butcher
who only sees the world as a sack of experience points.\"

That night, Gabriel took the second watch. The fire had burned down to a
bed of glowing, hypnotic embers. He thought of Silas\'s flame tattoo. *I
can light a fire with a word and a spark of will.* He had no words, but
he had will.

Holding out his left hand, he stared at his fingertips. He imagined a
thread of warmth being pulled from the fire, pooling in his index
finger. He focused all his intent, his **INT** 10 mind, on a single,
simple command: *Ignite.*

Nothing.

He tried again, closing his eyes, picturing a tiny version of the fire
wanting to be born. He pushed, not with his muscles, but with his mind.
He felt a strange, tingling pressure.

*Ignite!*

A tiny, brilliant orange spark sputtered to life on his fingertip. For a
glorious half-second, he was a Mage. Then, the spark flared and
vanished, leaving behind a sharp, stinging pain. \"Ow!\" he hissed. A
small, angry red blister was already forming. He stared at it, and
instead of dismay, a wide, triumphant grin spread across his face. It
had *worked*.

The thought came immediately. *Balance.*

He held his throbbing finger in his right hand. He focused on the memory
of watching the matron tend to sick children, of the feeling of
compassion. He willed the pain to fade, for the burn to be soothed. A
gentle, warm energy flowed from his hand, and he felt the stinging in
his finger begin to subside, replaced by a dull, pleasant thrumming. The
angry red of the blister faded to a light pink, the pain almost gone.

On a single night, alone by a dying fire, Gabriel had touched all four
paths. He looked down at his slightly burned, slightly healed finger, a
testament to his first, fumbling steps on the road to becoming a Scion.
