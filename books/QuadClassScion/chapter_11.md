**Chapter 11: The Price of a Single Point**

The campfire was a small island of warmth in a vast, dark ocean of
trees. Tonight, Gabriel\'s mind was on the numbers.

\"Silas,\" he began, \"can we talk about stats? How much stronger is a
person with a Strength of 11 compared to a 10?\"

Silas set his whetstone down, giving the question his full attention.
\"Most people think the scale is a straight line. They\'re wrong. A
Strength of 1 to 10 is the peak of what a man can achieve through
natural effort alone. It\'s learning to carry a bucket of water without
spilling a drop.\"

He picked up a fist-sized rock. \"This is a STR of 10. To get to 11\...
you don\'t just add a bit more water. You have to add a second bucket.\"

Gabriel\'s eyes widened. \"So the increase from 10 to 11 is as big as
the increase from 1 to 10?\"

\"Bigger,\" Silas corrected. \"It\'s a curve that gets steeper. Going
from 10 to 11 doesn\'t make you ten percent stronger. It makes you
half-again as strong. The jump from 11 to 12 is even more significant.
Pushing past that \'soft cap\' requires an almost supernatural
effort\... or the experience points to force The System to grant it to
you.\"

The immensity of his task settled on Gabriel with new weight. He wasn\'t
just trying to add a few points. He was trying to add four second
buckets, and then four third ones.

\"Now you see the madness of our path,\" Silas said grimly. \"We aren\'t
just trying to climb one steep curve. We\'re trying to climb four of
them, all at once.\"

The lesson hung in the air. Silas stood. \"Your watch. Keep your senses
sharp.\" He disappeared into the shadows, leaving Gabriel alone with the
fire and the unsettling new mathematics of his own existence.

The moon was hidden by clouds. Gabriel settled into his watch, expanding
his senses beyond the ring of firelight. He heard the hoot of a distant
owl. Then he heard something else. The soft *snap* of a dry twig.

He froze, silently drawing the short sword Silas had given him and
melting into the deeper shadows at the edge of the camp. He saw
movement. Three hunched, grotesque shapes slinking from tree to tree.
Goblins. Their beady eyes glowed faintly, fixed on the campfire and
Silas\'s sleeping form.

His first instinct was to cry out. But Silas\'s words echoed in his
mind. *Your watch.* This was his responsibility. His test.

He sheathed his sword and reached for his sling, loading a stone. He
chose his target---the goblin at the rear---and took aim. The stone flew
with a sharp *whizz*, striking the goblin square on the temple. It
collapsed without a sound.

The other two spun around, spotted his silhouette, and charged with
shrieks of rage. Gabriel drew his sword as the first goblin swung its
rusty blade in a wide, clumsy arc. He sidestepped and thrust, but the
blade skittered off its tough hide. The second goblin closed in from his
other side. He was trapped.

Thinking fast, he thrust his free hand forward at the face of the first
goblin, his mind screaming the command. *Ignite!*

A brilliant spark, larger this time, erupted in the goblin\'s face. It
shrieked and staggered back, clawing at its eyes. That was the opening
Gabriel needed. He spun, parried the second goblin\'s attack, and drove
his short sword deep into its gut. He pulled the blade free just as the
first, half-blinded goblin charged again. He met the charge and drove
his sword upward under its jaw.

Silence descended. A moment later, Silas stepped out of the shadows,
sword in hand. He hadn\'t been sleeping. He surveyed the scene. \"Good
work,\" he said.

Just then, a faint blue light pulsed from the bodies of the goblins and
flowed into Gabriel.

A fight for his life had earned him just enough experience to kill four
and a half bunny rabbits. He felt no disappointment. Instead, a grim,
unshakable resolve settled over him. The journey had begun.
