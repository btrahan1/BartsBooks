**Chapter 12: The Nature of a Skill**

The scent of salt was a new, exciting tang in the air, a promise that
their long trek was nearing its end. Weeks on the road had honed
Gabriel. Encounters with goblins, oversized wolves, and the occasional
bandit had become more frequent, and his experience points had crawled
into the low hundreds---a pitiful number that nonetheless represented a
dozen life-or-death struggles.

One evening, while cleaning a well-made dagger he\'d taken from a bandit
leader, a thought that had been bothering him for days finally surfaced.

\"Silas,\" he asked, \"what about skills? I know I\'m getting better
with the sword and sling. I can feel it. But unlike experience, The
System never tells me anything. Is there a way to check?\"

Silas nodded, understanding dawning on his face. \"You\'ve stumbled upon
the third pillar of power, boy. Stats are your body\'s raw potential.
Experience is The System\'s measure of your advancement. But skills\...
skills are *yours*. They are born from the marriage of your stats and
your actions.\"

He pointed a stick at Gabriel. \"The System doesn\'t grant you a \'Sword
Skill\' just because you pick up a sword. It only pays attention when an
action moves from conscious effort to unconscious competence. You have
to do it, and do it right, over and over, until it\'s as natural as
breathing.\"

\"So\... how are they measured?\" Gabriel pressed.

\"Not with numbers,\" Silas said. \"With tiers. Apprentice, Jouryman,
Adept, Master. You won\'t get a notification for every swing. You\'ll
think it\'s all just guesswork. Then, one day, you\'ll do something
you\'ve done a hundred times before, but you\'ll do it perfectly,
without thinking\... and The System will finally acknowledge it.\"

That night, Gabriel took the first watch, his mind abuzz. He thought
about the fire he\'d summoned, the healing he\'d performed. They were
clumsy, singular acts. Not skills yet, just possibilities.

His thoughts were interrupted by a faint rustling in the high branches
of a tree. He squinted. A shadow-cat, a nocturnal predator known for its
silence, was perched on a limb, its yellow eyes fixed on their camp\'s
rations.

Weeks ago, he would have woken Silas. Tonight, he felt a surge of quiet
confidence. He picked up his sling, the leather worn and familiar in his
hand. He chose a stone. He didn\'t think about the arc, the wind, or the
distance. He didn\'t calculate.

He simply looked at the target, and his body *knew* what to do. His arm
was a seamless extension of his will. The motion was a single, fluid,
perfect release.

The stone flew true, a silent streak in the moonlight. It struck the
shadow-cat with a dull *thump*, sending it tumbling from the branch.

In that exact moment, a new, different kind of blue screen flashed in
his vision.

**\[New Skill Acquired: Sling Proficiency (Apprentice)\]**

Gabriel stared at the notification, a slow, triumphant smile spreading
across his face. It wasn\'t guesswork. It was work. And the work was
rewarded.

A moment later, he heard a grunt from the darkness. \"Good shot, boy.\"

Silas had been awake the whole time. Gabriel finally understood. The
path to becoming a Scion wasn\'t just about grinding experience points.
It was about forging his own abilities, one perfect, instinctive action
at a time.
