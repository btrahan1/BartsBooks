**Chapter 14: The Jester\'s Wager**

From a place that was neither heaven nor hell, The Jester, a cosmic
entity of pure, chaotic potential, watched the boy\'s progress with
growing amusement. The story was good, but a bit predictable. A hero, to
be truly forged, needed to be tested in a fire of his own, without his
grizzled guardian.

\"Oh, Selyra,\" The Jester chuckled to himself, his form shifting like
heat haze. \"You nurture him like a delicate flower. But flowers don\'t
fell monsters. For that, you need an axe. And axes must be tempered.\"

He leaned forward, his grin a slash of joyful malice. The rules were the
rules. He couldn\'t just drop a demon in the boy\'s path. Direct
intervention was so clumsy. But manipulating the existing pieces? That
was an art form. He scanned the bustling streets of Port Seraphine
below. He had to separate the boy from the old man. His gaze fell upon
the worn leather scroll-tube tucked into Silas\'s pack. \"Ah, the map.
The linchpin of the whole grand adventure. Perfect.\"

Now, for the catalyst. His eyes scanned the rooftops until he found her:
a slip of a girl, no older than fifteen, with eyes that were far too old
and hands that moved with the speed of a striking viper. A pickpocket
named Pip.

The Jester didn\'t command her. He simply\... nudged.

As Gabriel and Silas descended into the chaotic maze of Port Seraphine,
the quiet solitude of the road was replaced by a roaring river of
humanity. The air was thick with the smells of fish, tar, and sweat.

\"Stay close,\" Silas growled. \"A city like this can swallow a boy
whole.\"

As they pushed through the main thoroughfare, a crowd gathered around a
fire-eater. Silas, for a moment, was captivated, a flicker of nostalgia
in his eyes. It was the perfect distraction.

Pip, perched on a nearby awning, was about to move on when a glint of
light caught her eye. It came from the old man\'s pack. The leather
scroll-tube seemed to shimmer, to pulse with a light that made it look
like it was crafted from spun gold. A surge of pure, undiluted greed,
sharper than any she had ever felt, seized her. *The score of a
lifetime.*

She dropped from the awning, landing as silently as a cat. A bump here,
a jostle there. Her fingers, impossibly quick, danced at the straps of
Silas\'s pack. It took less than a second. By the time the fire-eater
took his final bow, Pip was gone.

Silas, clapping with the rest of the crowd, reached back to adjust his
pack. His hand met empty air.

His blood ran cold. \"The map,\" he choked out, his face paling. He spun
around, his eyes scanning the crowd. \"The map is gone!\"

Gabriel\'s heart leaped into his throat. He scanned the dispersing
crowd, and for a split second, he saw her---a flash of movement, a small
figure scrambling up the side of a building two streets down. \"There!\"

\"Go!\" Silas commanded, his age and heavy armor making a rooftop chase
impossible. \"Get it back! I\'ll cut through the streets! Don\'t lose
her!\"

There was no time to think. Gabriel sprang into action, shoving his way
through the crowd. He leaped onto a stack of crates and pulled himself
up onto a roof. Below, the city was a chaotic jumble. Ahead, the thief
was a fleeting shadow, leaping from one rooftop to the next.

The Jester settled into his cosmic seat, propping his chin on his hands.
\"And\... the chase begins,\" he said with a delighted grin. \"Let the
real test commence.\"
