**Chapter 15: The Rooftops of Port Seraphine**

The world became a blur of slate tiles, precarious ledges, and the
yawning gaps between buildings. Gabriel ran, his focus narrowed to the
single, fleeting figure ahead. The thief was impossibly agile, moving
with a fluid grace that spoke of a lifetime spent navigating this
treacherous, vertical world. This was her home turf.

Pip glanced over her shoulder. The boy was still there. He was clumsy,
lacking the refined economy of a true roof-runner, but he was powerful
and relentless. He wasn\'t leaping across the gaps; he was launching
himself with raw, athletic force. And he was gaining. A flicker of pity
stirred in her chest. He was just a farm boy, out of his depth. He
didn\'t know the rules of this game. She wasn\'t just running; she was
leading. Every turn was taking them further into the Docks District, a
place where the City Guard never patrolled and life was cheap.

She vaulted a chimney stack and dropped lightly onto a rickety balcony
before hopping to the cobblestones of Fishgut Court below. It was a
notorious dead-end, the unofficial headquarters for her \"friends.\"
Three of them were down there now: a hulking brute named Cudgel and the
Vorn brothers, a pair of wiry thugs who fought with fish-gutting knives.

She held up the scroll-tube. \"Look what I got,\" she announced, her
voice a little too loud.

Just as Cudgel caught the map, Gabriel appeared at the edge of the roof
above them. He paused, taking in the scene. A trap. His heart hammered
in his chest. Silas was nowhere in sight. He was utterly, completely
alone.

He could retreat. It was the smart move.

\"Well, well,\" one of the Vorn brothers sneered, looking up at Gabriel.
\"Looks like the little mouse brought a friend.\"

\"He don\'t look like much,\" Cudgel grunted, tucking the map into his
belt. \"Give us your coin, boy, and we might let you walk away.\"

Pip watched Gabriel, a knot of tension in her stomach. She saw the fear
in his eyes, but she also saw something else flickering beneath it: a
stubborn, defiant fire. *Run, you fool,* she pleaded silently.

But the map\... the map was everything. His dream, Silas\'s hope, the
key to the Sunken Door. Retreat was not an option.

Gabriel dropped from the roof, landing in a crouch that absorbed the
impact, his hand already on the hilt of his sword. He stood, his posture
shifting from that of a frightened boy to that of a cornered animal.

\"I\'m not here for a fight,\" Gabriel said, his voice steady. \"Just
give me the map, and I\'ll be on my way.\"

The Vorn brothers erupted in laughter. \"Hear that, Cudgel?\" one of
them cackled. \"He wants the map!\"

Cudgel cracked his massive knuckles, a cruel grin spreading across his
face. \"If you want it, farm boy,\" he said, taking a heavy step
forward. \"You can come and get it.\"

Pip leaned against the wall, her face a mask of resignation. The farm
boy hadn\'t run. She felt a pang of guilt, quickly suppressed. This was
the way of the city. You did what you had to do to survive.

And the farm boy was not going to survive.
