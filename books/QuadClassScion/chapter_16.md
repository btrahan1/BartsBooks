**Chapter 16: The Price of a Map**

The air in Fishgut Court was thick with the threat of violence. Gabriel
stood alone, but as he looked at the three advancing figures, he saw
what others wouldn\'t: the frayed edges of their clothes, the hunger in
their eyes. He saw survivors, backed into a corner by a world that had
given them nothing. And he saw Pip, half-hidden in the shadows, her face
a pale mask of conflict.

He knew he had to fight. But he refused to believe he had to kill.

Taking a slow, deliberate breath, Gabriel did the last thing they
expected. He sheathed his short sword. The soft *shing* of the blade
returning to its scabbard was shockingly loud in the tense silence.
Cudgel and the Vorn brothers stopped, confused.

\"I told you, I don\'t want to fight,\" Gabriel said, holding up his
hands, open and empty. \"The map is just parchment, but it\'s important
to me. I have thirty-two copper pieces. It\'s everything I have. Take
it. Let me have the map, and we all walk away.\"

He tossed the small coin purse onto the cobblestones between them. \"I
know what it\'s like,\" he added, his gaze sweeping over them. \"To do
what you have to do to get by.\"

For a moment, his gamble seemed to work. The bravado in their faces
faltered. But the moment passed. Cudgel looked at the small purse, and a
look of contempt twisted his features. To him, this wasn\'t peace; it
was weakness.

\"You think we\'re beggars?\" he spat, kicking the purse and sending
coppers scattering. \"We\'ll take the map, the coin, *and* a few of your
teeth for the trouble.\"

He lunged. The Vorn brothers fanned out, their gutting knives flashing.

Gabriel\'s calm resolve hardened into cold reality. He had offered
peace. They had chosen violence.

He didn\'t stand his ground; he moved. As the Vorn brothers closed in,
he kicked over a nearby crate of rotting fish heads. The first brother,
caught by surprise, slipped on the slimy mess. Gabriel closed the
distance in two quick steps, reversed his sword, and brought the heavy
pommel down in a swift, precise strike on the man\'s temple. The thug
went limp. One down. Disabled, not dead.

The second brother was more cautious, slashing wildly with his knife.
Gabriel ducked under the swing, grabbed the man\'s outstretched arm,
twisted him off-balance, and drove his shoulder hard into the man\'s
chest. They crashed against the brick wall of the courtyard. The thug\'s
head connected with the bricks with a hollow thud, and he slid to the
ground, dazed.

Two down.

Now it was just Cudgel. The big man had paused, his dull eyes wide with
shock and a new, burgeoning rage. He roared and charged, swinging his
fists like stone hammers.

The Jester, watching from his chaotic perch, clapped his hands in
silent, delighted applause. A hero who tries to negotiate. A fighter who
uses his wits before his steel. A warrior who deliberately chooses not
to kill.

\"Oh, Selyra,\" he giggled. \"Your little flower has thorns. How
wonderfully, beautifully inefficient!\" He leaned in, his grin widening.
The real test, the one of pure, brute force, was about to begin.
\"Let\'s see how your \'balance\' holds up against a hammer, boy.\"
