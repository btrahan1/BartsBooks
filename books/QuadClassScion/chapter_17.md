**Chapter 19: The Salt-Stained Gryphon**

The tavern, called the Salt-Stained Gryphon, was a microcosm of Port
Seraphine: loud, chaotic, and smelling vaguely of spilled ale and
desperation. Gabriel sat across from Silas in a dark, sticky booth, a
mug of watered-down cider in front of him. He took a sip and grimaced.
It was awful.

The noise faded into a dull roar as his mind replayed the events of the
day. *The Merciful.* The title felt strange. And the
skill, **Intimidation (Apprentice)**, felt like a tool he didn\'t know
how to use. Was he supposed to scowl at people?

\"I\'ve never heard of The System just *giving* a skill,\" Silas had
grumbled as they\'d walked away from Fishgut Court. \"It makes no
sense.\"

Gabriel\'s thoughts were a swirling vortex, but beneath it all was a
new, solid layer of confidence. He had been tested, alone, and he had
prevailed on his own terms.

He looked over at Silas, who was scanning the tavern\'s entrance. The
old warrior had spent an hour with a sailor in a wharf-side office,
making arrangements. Now, they were waiting for their \"contact,\" the
captain of the ship that would take them south. The idea of the sea was
both terrifying and exhilarating. He pictured the map, the tiny island
in a sea marked **HERE BE SERPENTS**. The journey was no longer an
abstract goal; it was about to begin.

A new figure entered the tavern, and the boisterous noise seemed to dip
for a moment. He wasn\'t a large man, but he moved with a fluid,
predatory grace that commanded attention. He wore the salt-stained
clothes of a lifelong sailor, a sheathed cutlass at his hip. His face
was a roadmap of scars, and his eyes, a startlingly pale blue, missed
nothing. He scanned the room, and his gaze locked onto their booth.

\"Silas Croft,\" the man said, his voice a low, gravelly sound like
rocks grinding together. \"It\'s been a long time.\"

Silas looked up, and a rare, genuine smile touched his lips. \"Kaelen.
You old sea dog. I thought you\'d be kraken-bait by now.\"

Captain Kaelen laughed, a short, sharp bark. \"The krakens know better
than to tangle with me.\" He slid into the booth and finally turned his
piercing gaze on Gabriel. \"So this is the boy. You\'re causing quite a
stir, Silas. The message you sent ahead was\... intriguing.\"

Gabriel felt himself shrink a little under the captain\'s intense
scrutiny. This was their contact. And he looked more dangerous than all
three of the thugs in Fishgut Court combined.
