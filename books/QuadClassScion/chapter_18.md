**Chapter 20: The Echo in Fishgut Court**

The Salt-Stained Gryphon was a world away from Fishgut Court, but the
events of the afternoon still echoed there, a silent hum that had
fundamentally altered the grimy desperation of the alley.

Cudgel and the Vorn brothers sat in a sullen, bewildered silence. The
scattered coppers from Gabriel\'s purse lay untouched. The Vorn brother
Gabriel had healed kept touching his temple, a look of profound,
superstitious awe on his face. The knot was gone. The headache was gone.

Pip was not silent. She paced back and forth, her quick mind grappling
with an impossible equation.

\"Did you see it?\" she asked for the tenth time, her voice a sharp,
insistent whisper. \"He put his sword away.\"

\"He had magic,\" the second Vorn brother mumbled. \"He was a mage.\"

\"No,\" Pip shot back. \"A mage would have just thrown fire. He moved
like a roof-runner, fast and smart. And then\...\" She looked at the
first brother. \"He *healed* you. Mages don\'t heal. Healers don\'t
fight. Fighters don\'t use magic. It\'s\... it\'s not possible.\"

She stopped pacing and stared at her own hands. For her entire life, the
world had operated on a simple, brutal set of rules. The strong take
from the weak. You do what you must to survive another day. It was a
miserable life, but it was predictable.

That farm boy had shattered her understanding of the world.

He had the power to kill them all. But he hadn\'t. He had offered them
his own money first. He had used precise, non-lethal force. He had
terrified them with a power they couldn\'t comprehend, and then he had
shown them a kindness that was even more alien. He hadn\'t treated them
like thugs. He had treated them like people who had made a bad choice.

The question that had been circling in her mind finally landed with
devastating force. *Who am I?*

She looked at Cudgel\'s brutish anger, at the Vorn brothers\' petty
cruelty. This was her life. This was her future. Was this all she was
ever going to be?

The farm boy was different. He was strong, but he wasn\'t cruel. He was
powerful, but he wasn\'t a bully.

*Is there a better way?*

\"The coin,\" she said suddenly.

Cudgel looked up. \"What about it?\"

\"We\'re keeping it,\" Pip stated, her voice taking on a new, unfamiliar
authority. \"But we\'re not spending it on ale.\" She walked over and
began picking up the scattered coppers.

\"What are you doing, Pip?\" the healed Vorn brother asked, confused.

She dropped the coins into her pouch, the small weight feeling different
today. Heavier. \"I don\'t know,\" she said, looking toward the mouth of
the alley, toward the bustling city beyond. \"But I\'m done being a
nail.\"

The farm boy was gone, a ghost who had passed through their miserable
lives. But he had left something behind that was more powerful than a
memory of fire or a miraculous heal. He had left behind a question. And
for Pip, that question was the beginning of a new, and far more
dangerous, path.
