**Chapter 2: The Grindstone**

The Ministry Sergeant was a man encased in polished leather and duty.
His boots clicked with unnerving authority on the stone floor of the
common hall, where all the orphans over the age of fourteen had been
assembled. Matron Elara stood beside him, her face a mixture of hope and
weary resignation.

\"As per the Queen\'s Decree,\" the Sergeant began, his voice devoid of
warmth, \"all citizens are to be registered with The System upon their
sixteenth year. As the exact dates of birth for many of you are unknown,
the Ministry has determined this to be the appropriate day.\"

He held up a smooth, black slate. \"When I call your name, you will step
forward and place your hand upon the System Slate. Your core attributes
will be revealed. This is not a judgment. It is a measurement.\"

A nervous shuffle went through the small crowd. For all his bluster,
even Roric looked pale. He was called first. He swaggered forward,
trying to project confidence, and slammed his hand onto the slate. A low
hum filled the air, and glowing green numbers materialized on its
surface.

- **STR:** 9

- **DEX:** 6

- **WIS:** 5

- **INT:** 5

- **CON:** 8

- **CHA:** 6

The Sergeant grunted, a sound that could almost be mistaken for
approval. \"Strength 9. Adequate for enlistment in the City Guard. You
have a path. Step aside.\"

Roric puffed out his chest, a wide grin splitting his face. A Strength
of 9 was better than most orphans could hope for. He shot a triumphant,
mocking look back at the others.

\"Gabriel.\"

The name hung in the chilly air. Gabriel felt a dozen pairs of eyes on
him as he walked forward. His heart hammered against his ribs, a frantic
drumbeat of terror and anticipation. He placed his hand on the slate. It
was cool and unnervingly smooth.

The slate hummed, louder and longer than it had for the others. The air
crackled slightly. Then, the numbers appeared.

- **STR:** 7

- **DEX:** 7

- **WIS:** 7

- **INT:** 7

- **CON:** 7

- **CHA:** 7

Silence. Then, a snort from the back. It was Roric. \"A perfect set of
7s! Looks like you\'re balanced, all right. Equally weak everywhere!\"

A wave of stifled laughter rolled through the orphans. The Sergeant
stared at the slate, his brow furrowed with a kind of clinical pity.
\"Unfortunate,\" he stated flatly. \"Barely adequate. No clear aptitude.
You have two years until you must choose a class.\"

The shame was a physical blow, hot and sharp. Gabriel felt his ears
burn, and he clenched his fists to stop them from shaking. But as he
stared at the glowing green sevens, the image of the dungeon door pushed
through the despair. His mind, the one the slate rated as a mere 7,
began to calculate. *I need six points in Strength. Six in Dexterity.
Six in Wisdom. Six in Intelligence. Twenty-four levels\' worth of
experience\... just to qualify.*

The laughter behind him faded to a dull buzz. The Sergeant\'s dismissive
gaze lost its sting. Gabriel pulled his hand back from the slate, his
gaze steady. The scorn of the world was just fuel for the fire.

An hour later, he was gone. He exchanged quiet goodbyes with two of the
younger boys, collected a small pouch with ten copper pieces from the
Matron, and walked out the heavy oak door without looking back.

He already had a plan. While the other boys had gossiped about joining
the guard, Gabriel had listened to the cart drivers. He'd heard them
speak of a man named Silas, a farmer on the town\'s edge whose last two
farmhands had quit, calling him a \"crotchety old bastard\" who worked
you to the bone for a pittance. The others saw a dead-end job. Gabriel
saw an opportunity.

It took him an hour to walk to the edge of town, where the cobblestones
gave way to a muddy track. Silas\'s farm was easy to spot: the one with
the sagging fence and the fields choked with weeds. Gabriel found the
old man splitting logs, his movements efficient and powerful.

\"What do you want?\" Silas grunted, not stopping his work.

\"I heard you might need a hand,\" Gabriel said.

Silas paused, sizing Gabriel up with sharp, pale blue eyes. \"Heard I\'m
a crotchety old bastard, you mean. You don\'t look like you\'ve got much
\'half\' to spare.\"

\"I\'m a hard worker,\" Gabriel said simply. \"I don\'t need much. A
place to sleep and food to eat.\"

The old man squinted. \"You\'re from Graywall. Let me guess, your stats
weren\'t anything to write home about.\"

\"They\'re all 7s,\" Gabriel admitted, without shame.

Silas let out a short, barking laugh. \"7s. The System\'s way of calling
you useless. And you think you can handle this farm?\"

\"I know I can,\" Gabriel said, his voice ringing with a certainty that
was at odds with his appearance. \"Give me one week. If you\'re not
satisfied with my work, I\'ll leave and you\'ll owe me nothing.\"

The old farmer stared at him for a long, silent moment. \"Fine,\" he
finally growled. \"One week. The barn\'s got a loft. The work starts
before the sun comes up. Don\'t be late.\"

Without another word, he turned back to his log pile. Gabriel allowed
himself a small, internal smile. He walked to the barn and climbed the
ladder to the loft. Looking out the opening, he could see the entirety
of the farm spread before him. Fields to be tilled, fences to be mended,
animals to be fed. To anyone else, it was a mountain of back-breaking
labor.

To Gabriel, it was his new training dungeon. The grind was about to
begin.
