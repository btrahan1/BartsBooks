**Chapter 22: The Endless Blue**

The sea was nothing like Gabriel had imagined.

He had thought of it as a bigger version of a lake, a vast, flat plain
of water. He had not been prepared for the reality. It was not a plain;
it was a living, breathing mountain range in constant, violent motion.
The ship, the *Sea Serpent\'s Pride*, which had seemed so massive and
solid at the dock, felt like a tiny, fragile leaf tossed on an endless,
churning expanse of deep, intimidating blue.

The first three days were a special kind of misery. Gabriel\'s body, so
accustomed to the unyielding solidity of the earth, rebelled. \"Finding
his sea legs,\" Captain Kaelen had called it with a booming laugh,
clapping him on the back as Gabriel leaned over the railing for the
fifth time, his stomach churning in revolt.

Silas was unsympathetic. \"Your body is a vessel, boy. Your will is the
captain. Seize control of the ship.\"

It was a harsh lesson, but it was the only kind Gabriel understood.
During his waking hours, he forced himself to stay on deck, clinging to
the railing, facing the relentless, rolling horizon. He didn\'t just
endure the nausea; he focused on it. He used his will, the same tool he
used to spark a flame or soothe a burn, and turned it inward. He focused
on the concept of balance, not on a narrow rooftop, but inside his own
core. He felt the pitch and roll of the ship, and instead of fighting
it, he began to move with it, making a thousand tiny, unconscious
adjustments.

By the fourth day, the sickness was gone. He could walk the deck with a
steady, rolling gait, his body now in tune with the rhythm of the ocean.
He could finally appreciate the terrifying, magnificent beauty of his
new world. He saw pods of sleek, silver-skinned dolphins racing the
ship\'s bow. He watched a colossal whale breach in the distance, sending
a plume of spray into the air that created a fleeting rainbow. He
learned the names of the constellations from the old sailors, a new and
far more vital map than any he had seen on land.

The journey was also a new training ground. Every day, he and Silas
would spar on the deck, the rolling of the ship adding a new,
unpredictable dimension to their practice. Footwork was no longer about
a stable stance, but about constant, fluid adaptation---the ultimate
lesson in **Dexterity**.

He also found a new way to practice his nascent magic. Staring out at
the endless expanse of water, he found it far easier to tap into the
Healer\'s art. The ocean was a vast, primal font of life, and he could
feel its energy. He learned to draw that gentle, restorative power into
himself, mending the crew\'s minor cuts and bruises, earning him the
nickname \"The Charm\" among the sailors. The fire, however, was harder.
Surrounded by the overwhelming power of water, conjuring a flame felt
like trying to light a candle in a hurricane. It required a new level of
focus, a fiercer application of will. His sparks were small and
fleeting, often sputtering out before they could truly catch.

On the ninth night of their voyage, as he stood the late watch near the
ship\'s bow, staring into the inky, star-dusted water, he felt it. A
change. A deep, resonant thrumming that seemed to vibrate up through the
hull of the ship and into the soles of his feet. It wasn\'t the familiar
rhythm of the waves. It was something else. Something massive.
Something\... hungry.

The sea, which had become a place of wonder, suddenly felt like a place
of profound and ancient dread. He was a tiny speck of dust floating on
the surface of a dark, hungry, and awakening world. And he had the
distinct, chilling feeling that they were being watched from below.
