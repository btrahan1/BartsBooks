**Chapter 23: The Hunger from Below**

The thrumming intensified. The sea around the *Sea Serpent\'s
Pride* grew unnaturally calm, the waves flattening into an eerie, glassy
surface. Seasoned sailors, men who had spent their lives on the water,
began to look around with wide, fearful eyes, their hands gripping
charms and the hilts of their weapons.

\"All hands!\" Captain Kaelen\'s voice boomed across the deck, sharp
with a new, urgent tension. \"Something\'s coming!\"

Silas was instantly at Gabriel\'s side, his hand on his sword. \"Stay
sharp, boy. The sea\'s gone wrong.\"

Then, the water began to boil. A shape, impossibly vast, rose from the
depths. It was a serpent, but to call it a serpent was to call a
hurricane a summer breeze. Its head, larger than the ship\'s forecastle,
was a nightmare of armored, barnacle-encrusted plates and rows of teeth
like obsidian daggers. A single, colossal eye, glowing with a cold,
malevolent light, fixed upon their ship. The Scyllan Sea-Drake had
arrived.

For a century, it had slept in the crushing, lightless dark, dreaming of
ancient, titanic battles. For a century, its hunger had been a dormant
thing. But the call, the scent it had followed up from the abyss, was a
banquet for its senses. It was not the simple, fleeting taste of flesh
it was used to. This was the flavor of potential, the rich, intoxicating
aroma of a nascent power that resonated with the very fabric of the
world. It was a meal that would not just fill its belly, but nourish its
ancient, hateful soul.

The tiny wooden thing floating on the surface was the source. The morsel
it had been promised. Its hunger was a physical presence, a pressure
that could be felt in the air, a deep, primal need that had drawn it
across leagues of ocean and up from a mile of black water. It would have
its meal.

A wave of pure, primal terror washed over the deck. Men screamed. This
was not a creature to be fought. This was an extinction event.

Gabriel felt the fear, a cold, sharp spike in his gut. He should have
been paralyzed. But as the initial shock subsided, his unique mind took
over. The terror was replaced by a strange, exhilarating clarity. He
wasn\'t just seeing a monster. He was seeing a system. He analyzed the
armored plates, the single massive eye, the overwhelming aura of ancient
power. And a thought, utterly insane and completely logical, sliced
through his fear.

*The experience\...*

Twenty thousand rabbits. Two thousand goblins. He looked at the Scyllan
Sea-Drake, and his mind, for the first time, comprehended a way to climb
the impossible mountain in a single, terrifying leap.

\"What is that thing?\" he asked, his voice steady.

\"A Scyllan,\" Silas\'s voice was grim. \"A beast of legend. They\'re
not supposed to come to the surface. Something drew it here.\"

The Captain\'s voice cut through the chaos. \"Archers, to the rails! Aim
for the eye!\"

Arrows rained against the creature\'s hide, sparking harmlessly. The
beast let out a roar, a sound not of air and lungs, but of the deep
earth grinding against itself. It was a sound of pure, ravenous hunger.

It was an impossible fight. But Gabriel\'s resolve hardened. This was
the test The Jester had wanted. The beast reared back, its colossal head
blotting out the stars, preparing to strike.

\"Silas,\" Gabriel said, his voice ringing with a new, cold authority.
\"Keep it distracted. I have a plan.\"

He didn\'t wait for a reply. He ran, not away from the monster, but
towards it, leaping up onto the ship\'s forecastle. He needed to get
closer. He needed to find a weak point. And he needed to hit it with
something more powerful than an arrow or a sword. He needed to finally,
truly, become a Mage.
