**Chapter 26: The Aftermath**

The shriek of the wounded Scyllan faded into the ocean\'s vastness as
the beast dove back into the abyssal depths. The sudden, profound
silence it left behind was almost as deafening as its roar.

On the deck of the *Sea Serpent\'s Pride*, no one moved. The sailors,
Silas, and Captain Kaelen could only stare at the spot where the monster
had been, then back at the lone, drenched figure on the ruined
forecastle.

Gabriel's vision was swimming. A cascade of blue screens, one layered on
top of the other, pulsed insistently at the edge of his sight.

**\[SPECIAL FEAT ACHIEVED: Leviathan\'s Bane\]**\
**\[You have successfully defeated a Legendary-tier creature far beyond
your current level.\]**

**\[New Skill Acquired: Cryomancy (Apprentice)\]**

**\[INT increased by 1.\]**

**\[WIS increased by 1.\]**

**\[EXP GAINED: +50,000\]**

**\[TOTAL EXP: 50,123.5 / 2,000\]**

The numbers were staggering, nonsensical. He had enough experience to
buy twenty-five stat points. The System had rewarded him with two points
directly. He ignored it all.

The world snapped back into focus. He wasn\'t a hero in the glow of
victory. He was a boy on a broken ship, surrounded by injured people.
The Scyllan\'s thrashing had turned the deck into a disaster zone. The
main mast was a jagged stump, and men were groaning, their limbs twisted
at unnatural angles.

The effort had taken more than a bit out of him. He felt hollowed out,
as if he had poured his very soul into that spear of ice. His limbs felt
like lead, and a deep, bone-aching exhaustion settled over him. But
there were wounded. And he was the only Healer around.

\"Report!\" Captain Kaelen\'s voice finally boomed, shaking the crew out
of their stupor. \"Damage! Injuries!\"

Gabriel pushed his exhaustion aside. He stumbled down from the
forecastle and went to the most grievously injured sailor, a man with a
deep, bleeding gash in his arm. \"Hold still,\" he said, his voice
raspy. He placed his hands over the wound. He was too drained for a true
miracle, but he could feel the ambient, life-giving energy of the sea.
He drew on it, a gentle pull, and channeled it into the wound. The
bleeding slowed, the edges of the gash knitting together slightly. It
would hold.

He moved from one sailor to the next, a specter of quiet competence in
the chaotic aftermath. He set a broken leg, soothed massive bruises, and
diagnosed a concussion. He was not a master healer, but he was a beacon
of hope on a ship that had none.

As he worked, a thought pricked at his conscience. *I didn\'t kill
it.* He had defeated it, driven it away, but he had not ended its life.
He hoped The System, or the gods, wouldn\'t hold that against him. The
creature hadn\'t been evil. It had been drawn to him, a force of nature
acting on an unnatural stimulus. It had been a part of the world that
simply\... *was*. To have destroyed it felt, somehow, like a crime. He
hoped his choice had been the right one.

He didn\'t know that his act of mercy was causing a more profound chaos
in the heavens than the spear of ice itself. He just knew that there
were people who needed him, and for now, that was all that mattered.
