**Chapter 29: The Wolf in the Harbor**

The tavern in Maridia was called The Drunken Kraken, a step down even
from the Salt-Stained Gryphon. The air was thick with the smoke of cheap
pipe-weed and the reek of stale fish. They had secured their supplies
and were waiting for the tide to turn, sharing a quiet meal in a dark,
secluded corner.

Silas was a million miles away. He was seeing a boy conjure a spear of
impossible ice. He was seeing a screen with a number---50,000---that had
no business existing. For sixty years, he had believed he understood the
rigid, unforgiving laws of The System. Now, he felt like a man who had
spent his life studying the rules of chess, only to watch his opponent
summon a dragon and burn the board.

He was so lost in his thoughts that he didn\'t notice the shadow detach
from the tavern\'s entrance. He didn\'t see the figure in dark leather,
moving with a silent, deadly purpose. He didn\'t see the silver wolf\'s
head insignia.

Gabriel saw him. A cold prickle of dread, a whisper from his unsettling
dreams, slithered up his spine. \"Silas,\" he said, his voice a low,
urgent hiss.

It was almost too late. The assassin was upon them, a longsword
appearing in his hand as if from thin air. He lunged, not at Gabriel,
but at Silas, the perceived guardian and the greater threat.

Silas reacted on pure, instinctual memory. He threw himself backward,
overturning their table, the assassin\'s blade hissing through the air
where his throat had been.

The tavern erupted into chaos. Patrons screamed and scattered. The
assassin was relentless, a whirlwind of deadly grace, his skill far
beyond any bandit they had faced.

Gabriel drew his own sword and flanked the attacker. The assassin,
without looking, parried Gabriel\'s thrust with his second sword, the
screech of metal on metal deafening. He was a master of the dual-blade
style, a true professional.

But he had underestimated them. He found himself facing a seasoned
veteran and a prodigy whose balanced stats made him preternaturally fast
and strong. Working together, Gabriel and Silas forced the assassin onto
the defensive. Gabriel, using a clever feint, created an opening, and
Silas drove his boot into the back of the assassin\'s knee.

The man fell to one knee, his swords clattering to the floor. Gabriel
was on him in an instant, the point of his short sword pressed against
the man\'s throat.

\"Do it, whelp,\" the assassin sneered. \"Send me to my master.\"

Gabriel hesitated. This was not a goblin. This was a man. A killer, yes,
but a man. He had sworn to himself he would not take a human life unless
absolutely necessary.

The assassin\'s eyes flickered with triumph. \"You hesitated, and that
is why you will d---\"

He never finished. Silas, his face a grim mask, ran him through from the
side. The assassin\'s eyes went wide with shock, and he slumped to the
floor, dead.

\"He was palming a dagger,\" Silas growled, his voice filled with a
teacher\'s frustration. \"Poisoned. He was begging for mercy to create
an opening. You hesitated, and that almost cost you your life!\"

Shame, hot and sharp, washed over Gabriel. Silas was right. His mercy
had almost gotten him killed.

But the shame was instantly vaporized by a new, all-consuming horror.

As Silas turned, a second assassin, who had been hidden among the
panicked crowd, lunged from the shadows. His movements were silent, his
face a blank canvas of lethal intent. Before anyone could react, he
plunged a long, thin stiletto deep into Silas\'s back.
