**Chapter 4: The Puzzle of Silas Croft**

The seasons turned. Summer\'s oppressive heat gave way to the crisp,
golden days of autumn. The scrawny orphan from Graywall was gone,
replaced by a lean, wiry youth. The farm, once a symbol of neglect, was
slowly coming back to life under their combined, relentless effort.

Gabriel\'s days were a grueling but sacred routine. He no longer just
performed his chores; he attacked them with a calculated fury. He
didn\'t just carry one bucket of water from the well; he carried two. He
didn\'t just till the soil; he sought out the largest rocks to haul to
the farm\'s edge, each one a victory for his burgeoning **Strength**. He
ran where he could have walked, pushed where he could have rested,
turning every moment into an opportunity to grind.

But the true change was in his focus. Heeding Silas\'s words, he trained
his mind as hard as his body. While mending harnesses, he forced his
fingers to move with precision, a quiet pursuit of **Dexterity**. While
tending the animals, he learned to read their moods and needs, a silent
lesson in **Wisdom**. And most of all, he observed Silas.

He was discreet, but the old man was a living puzzle Gabriel was
determined to solve. The most telling moment came one afternoon when a
trio of loud, drunken youths stumbled onto the property, looking to
cause trouble. Gabriel instinctively reached for a pitchfork. But Silas
simply walked out to meet them, holding nothing but his gnarled walking
staff. He just stood there, his weight perfectly centered, his posture
radiating a quiet lethality that was utterly out of place on a humble
farm. The youths took one look at the old man\'s eyes, at the way he
held the staff not as a crutch but as a weapon, and their drunken
bravado evaporated. They muttered apologies and scrambled away.

Silas was no mere farmer. He was a warrior.

One evening, as they sat by the fire in the small cottage, Silas finally
spoke, his voice a low growl that cut through the crackling flames.
\"You\'re a quiet boy, but your mind is loud. For months now, you\'ve
watched me like you\'re trying to dismantle a machine to see how it
works. I\'m tired of the staring. Spit it out. What\'s on your mind?\"

This was it. The moment of truth. Gabriel took a deep breath and decided
to take the leap.

\"When I was twelve,\" he began, his voice quiet but steady, \"the
Queen\'s storyteller spoke of the advanced classes. He mentioned a
legend. A path so difficult it was called a myth.\"

Silas\'s expression didn\'t change, but his eyes narrowed almost
imperceptibly.

\"He called it the path of the Scion,\" Gabriel continued. \"A warrior
who walked all four paths. Fighter, Rogue, Mage, and Healer. I\... I
want to do that. I want to become a Quad-Class Scion.\"

He braced himself for the laugh, for the scornful dismissal. But Silas
just sat there, the firelight dancing in his ancient eyes. Finally, the
old man leaned forward slightly. \"A fool\'s dream,\" he murmured, and
Gabriel\'s heart sank.

\"But,\" Silas added, his voice a low, serious rumble that held not a
trace of mockery, \"only a fool would dismiss a dream just because it is
difficult. Tell me, boy\... why?\"

The simple question caught Gabriel off guard. He looked into the old
warrior\'s eyes and gave the only answer he had. \"Because\... everyone
says it\'s impossible. And I need to prove them wrong.\" Then, taking
one more, even greater risk, he added, \"And because I had a dream. Of
an underwater cavern, and a door. A door to a place called the Time
Dilation Dungeon.\"

At the mention of those words, the mask of the crotchety old farmer
finally fell away. A look of profound, undisguised shock washed over
Silas\'s face. He stared at Gabriel as if seeing him for the very first
time.

\"By the gods\...\" Silas whispered, his voice raspy with an emotion
Gabriel couldn\'t identify. \"You actually saw it.\"
