**Chapter 6: Echoes of a Blighted Scion**

In the celestial spheres, the game had changed. Gabriel was no longer a
private project for the Goddess of Twilight. He was a piece on the
board, and the other gods were now watching.

\"The boy shows promise,\" boomed Kael, the God of War, with grudging
respect. \"But the path he has chosen is a fool\'s errand.\"

\"Balance is not a myth,\" Selyra\'s calm voice countered. \"It is the
foundation of all things. And it is about to be tested.\" Her gaze
shifted from the small farm to a dark, festering wound on the map of
Carnadium---a blighted land in the far north where shadows clung even at
noon.

In that blighted land, a being sat upon a throne of twisted wood. He was
Malakor, the Blighted Scion. Centuries ago, he too had dreamed of the
Quad-Class, but he had lacked the patience for the grind. He had found
other paths, trading his humanity for power, breaking the shackles of
time not with diligence, but with atrocity. He had long since surpassed
Level 10, unlocking his own terrifying advanced class: the World-Ender.

A flicker of light, a pinprick of pure, defiant potential from the
south, disturbed his dark meditations. Annoying. \"A new light seeks to
kindle,\" Malakor\'s voice rasped, a sound like dying leaves on
gravestones. A shadow detached itself from his throne. \"Send a whisper
south. Find this flicker. Snuff it out before it becomes a flame.\"

Meanwhile, in the capital city of Argent, King Theron listened to the
grim reports from the northern border. His most elite unit, the Argent
Hand, was holding the line against Malakor\'s Blight, but the cost was
high. \"We are not a sword,\" his captain had told him, \"we are a dam,
and the cracks are beginning to show.\" The King, chained to his throne
by duty, could not face the threat himself. \"We need more than a dam,\"
he murmured after she had left. \"We need a tide to wash the Blight
away.\"

Selyra felt the King\'s prayer, the Blighted Scion\'s dark intent, and
the boy\'s unwavering focus. The time for subtlety was ending. The map
had to be found. She sent a whisper, not to Gabriel, but into the deep,
forgotten memories of the old warrior.

The morning after his confession, Gabriel found a different Silas
waiting for him. The farmer was gone. The commander had returned.

\"Today\'s lesson: memory,\" Silas said, leading Gabriel to his cottage.
He opened a large, iron-banded chest at the foot of his bed. \"For sixty
years, I have tried to forget the man I was.\"

Inside was the life he had left behind: a suit of well-oiled steel
armor, a simple longsword, and a dented shield. As he lifted the shield,
a strange, nagging feeling---Selyra\'s whisper---made him run his
fingers along the inside rim. His thumb caught on a small, almost
invisible latch.

\"Well I\'ll be\...\" he murmured. He\'d forgotten the hidden
compartment he\'d built half a century ago. He pried it open. Inside,
nestled in faded velvet, was a tightly rolled scroll of sea-worn
parchment.

\"The last treasure I ever found,\" Silas said, unrolling it on the
table. It was a nautical map, detailing the coastline hundreds of miles
to the south. \"Worthless. We searched that area for weeks. Found
nothing.\"

Gabriel leaned over it, his heart beginning to beat faster. Just then,
the first rays of the morning sun slanted through the cottage\'s window.
The light passed through a small, faceted crystal hanging in the
frame---a cheap trinket---and cast a tiny, perfect rainbow onto the map.

Where the rainbow touched the parchment, a single, silver rune,
invisible moments before, began to glow with a faint, ethereal light. It
was the same rune Gabriel had seen above the door in his dream.

Silas stared, his breath catching. He angled the map in the light. More
runes appeared, a constellation of hidden marks that formed a path,
leading away from the coastline into a section of the ocean marked only
with the words: **HERE BE SERPENTS**. The destination was a tiny,
unmarked island.

The fool\'s dream was no longer a dream. The myth was real. And the map
to its gate was in his hands.

\"Well, boy,\" Silas said, his voice thick with a lifetime of buried
hope. \"It seems you have a journey to make.\"
