**Chapter 7: Mentor and Aspirant**

The glowing map lay on the table, a bridge between a forgotten past and
an impossible future. The world, which for two years had been the size
of Silas\'s farm, had suddenly expanded to a terrifying, unknown
horizon.

\"Come with me,\" Gabriel said, the words a raw, earnest plea. He looked
at Silas, not as an employee to a boss, but as a student to a master.
\"Please.\"

Silas looked away from the map, his face settling back into its
familiar, weary lines. \"My journeys are done, boy. I\'m an old man. My
place is here.\"

\"No!\" Gabriel insisted, stepping closer. \"You\'re a warrior. I\'ve
seen it. I don\'t know anything, Silas! I know this farm, and I know the
orphanage. I don\'t know the road, or the forests, or what to do when
the sun goes down and there\'s no roof. I\'ll die before I even find the
coast!\"

He gestured wildly at the map. \"And what about the dungeon itself? You
heard the name. Time Dilation. What does that even mean? What if it has
rules? Limits? I need your wisdom. I need your training!\"

He took a breath, his gaze softening as he looked at the old man\'s
tattooed wrist. \"This isn\'t just my dream anymore, Silas. It was yours
first. You said the only thing that stopped you was time. This is the
answer! What if you could level again? What if we *both* could?\"

Silas was silent. He walked slowly to the cottage door and looked out at
the fields he had tended for sixty years. It was his life\'s work. A
testament to a long, quiet failure. He had made his peace with it. Or so
he had thought.

\"My bones ache in the morning,\" he said, his back still to Gabriel.
\"My sword arm is slow. The world has moved on.\"

\"I need you!\" Gabriel\'s voice was firm, cutting through the old
man\'s excuses. \"I don\'t need a relic. I need a mentor. I need the
Quad-Class warrior who spent twenty years fighting just to gain one
level. That\'s the man I need to teach me how to fight. How to
survive.\"

The plea hung in the air, an undeniable challenge. It held up a mirror,
forcing Silas to see not the stooped old farmer he had become, but the
indomitable warrior he had been.

For a long time, the only sound was the faint morning birdsong. Silas
turned from the door, his face a mask of profound conflict. He looked at
the boy, whose eyes burned with a fire he hadn\'t seen in anyone---least
of all himself---for more than half a century. He looked at the
impossible map glowing on his table. He looked at his own hands,
callused from the plow, but still remembering the feel of a sword hilt.

Finally, Silas let out a long, slow sigh, the sound of sixty years of
resignation giving way.

\"You\'re a bigger fool than I was,\" he grumbled, but the gruffness in
his voice couldn\'t hide the new spark in his eyes. He walked over to
the old chest and nudged it with his foot.

\"The armor will need polishing,\" he said. \"And the sword needs a new
edge. It\'s a long walk to the coast.\"
