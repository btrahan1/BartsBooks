**Chapter 9: The Weight of Twenty Thousand Rabbits**

The sun was climbing the morning sky as they walked the muddy track away
from the farm. For the first time in two years, Gabriel was leaving it
behind, the sight of the familiar fields shrinking in the distance.

Silas, clad in his simple steel armor, walked with a purpose Gabriel had
never seen. The stoop in his shoulders was gone, his stride long and
measured. He was no longer Silas the farmer; he was Silas the warrior.

\"I chose it,\" Gabriel said, breaking the comfortable silence.

A slow grin spread across the old man\'s weathered face. \"Happy
birthday, boy. Welcome to the loneliest club in the kingdom.\"

Gabriel\'s eyes were still seeing the faint afterimage of the blue
screen. \"It said I need 2,000 experience points. Is that for Level 2?\"

Silas let out a hearty, genuine laugh, a sound of a man who knew the
absurdity of their path all too well. \"Gods, no,\" he chuckled.
\"That\'s for your first stat point. The System is generous. For your
trouble, you get one point to place in your lowest primary attribute.\"

\"So\...\" Gabriel reasoned, \"once I get 2,000 EXP, I can raise one of
my stats to 11?\"

\"That\'s the way of it,\" Silas confirmed. \"It\'s The System\'s cruel
little bargain for Aspirants like us. You can\'t level, but
you *can* crawl your way to the starting line.\"

\"So\... is 2,000 experience a lot?\" Gabriel asked, the number feeling
vast and abstract.

Silas stopped and looked at him, his eyes twinkling with a kind of grim
amusement. \"Let\'s put it this way. The average goblin gives about 10
experience points. A normal adventurer would need to kill about two
hundred goblins to reach Level 2.\"

\"So I need to kill two hundred goblins to get one point in Strength,\"
Gabriel said.

\"No,\" Silas said, the single word landing with quiet force. \"You\'re
forgetting the jester\'s price. The ten-times penalty isn\'t just for
leveling up later. It starts now. You only *receive* one-tenth of the
experience for every kill.\"

Gabriel\'s mental calculation stuttered, then rebooted with the new,
horrifying variable. \"So\... that goblin that gives 10 EXP\... I would
only get 1 EXP from it?\"

\"Exactly,\" Silas confirmed, starting to walk again. \"The System is
fair, you see. It gives everyone the *opportunity*. It just makes sure
that for some of us, that opportunity is buried under a mountain of
impossibility.\"

A wave of cold dread washed over Gabriel. The number was no longer
abstract. It was a monster.

\"So, to get that 2,000 experience\...\" Gabriel\'s voice trailed off.

Silas finished the thought for him. \"Well, you could get 2,000
experience if you killed 2,000 of the world\'s weakest creature, the
common bunny rabbit, which gives 1 EXP each. But in your case, with the
jester\'s tax, you\'d have to kill *twenty thousand* of them.\"

Twenty. Thousand. Rabbits.

The number was so ludicrous that Gabriel couldn\'t help but let out a
short, sharp laugh of his own. All his work on the farm had just been
the prologue. The real grind, the one that broke men and turned legends
into cautionary tales, had just begun.

\"Let\'s hope,\" Silas said, clapping a heavy, gauntleted hand on
Gabriel\'s shoulder, \"that dungeon of yours has something a bit more
challenging---and a lot more rewarding---than a bunny rabbit, yes?\"

Gabriel looked ahead, down the long road that stretched toward the
horizon. The weight of twenty thousand rabbits was on his back, but for
the first time, he wasn\'t carrying it alone.
