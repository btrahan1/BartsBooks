# Chapter 3: The Green Sign and the System's First Glimpse

By Ren’s fourth year, the city of Silverhaven didn't just recognize him; it had adapted to him. The days of Hiroto manufacturing "random" correspondence to keep his son busy were long gone. The demand for the "Silverhaven Flash" had transitioned from a novelty to a civic necessity. 

It was a phenomenon that began organically. People realized that if they shouted "Ren!" or "Quickfoot!" as the blue-and-white blur whipped past, there was a sixty-percent chance the blur would skid to a violent, dust-kicking halt. 

Ren would stand there, his chest heaving with effortless rhythm, his eyes already scanning the person for the expectant vellum or parcel. He didn't need long explanations. He had developed a talent that bordered on the supernatural: he could listen to a set of complex directions—"Down the Grand Canal, left at the third bridge, the house with the red door behind the bakery"—and memorize them instantly. He would give a sharp nod, snatch the item, and be gone before the speaker could even reach for their tip-pouch.

This evolved, inevitably, into the "Green Sign."

It started with Kenji the gardener. Tired of trying to time his shouts with Ren's erratic schedule, he had hung a simple square of green-dyed cloth on the post outside his garden gate. 

"If that’s out, I’ve got a delivery for you, lad," Kenji told Ren during one of his brief pauses. "If it’s not, keep running."

The idea caught fire like dry hay in a summer drought. Within months, the Scribe’s Quarter, the High District, and even the gritty warehouse docks were dotted with green signs. Small green flags on shop windows, green ribbons tied to door knockers, even green-painted boards leaning against canal pilings. Each one was a beacon for Ren, a signal that there was work to be done and coppers to be earned.

Ren’s mother, Hana, watched the proliferation of green with a mixture of amusement and concern. "The city is treating him like a public utility, Hiroto. He’s four years old!"

"And he’s happier than a cat in a fish market," Hiroto would reply, though he too felt the strangeness of it. "Have you seen him lately? He doesn't just run. He... flows. He knows the city better than the map-makers do."

It was true. Ren's fourth year was a masterclass in urban navigation. He knew which cobblestones became loose after a rain, which bakeries let out the best smells at mid-morning (worth a momentary slowdown), and which neighborhood dogs were fast enough to actually provide a challenge. His pouch was no longer just jingling; it was heavy. He had moved his earnings from a jar to a small, iron-bound chest he'd bought with his own money from a merchant at the Southern Gate.

But even the Flash had limits, though he didn't know it yet.

In the middle of a particularly rainy autumn, Ren came down with a cold. It wasn't a life-threatening ailment, but it was enough to make his nose run and his usually bright eyes a bit cloudy. Hana, exercising her maternal authority, forbade him from leaving the apartment.

"But the signs, Mama!" Ren had protested, his voice a pathetic, nasally rasp. "There must be dozens of them out today! The grain merchant needs his invoices, and Silas had a rug to ship!"

"The city will survive one day without you, Ren," Hana said firmly, tucking the blankets around him. "Rest."

Ren spent the day in a state of restless misery, listening to the rain beat against the window and imagining the green signs drooping in the wet. He felt like a coiled spring that was being forced to remain compressed. 

The next morning, however, the silence of the apartment was broken by a series of soft *thuds* against the front door. Hiroto, curious, went to open it.

He stopped, his hand on the latch, and simply stared.

"Hana, you might want to see this."

Piled outside their door were gifts. Grapes from the merchants, a warm wool scarf from Old Man Silas, a jar of medicinal honey from the Floral District, and even a cluster of hand-carved wooden toys. But most striking of all was a large, crudely painted banner draped across the hallway: **GET WELL SOON, QUICKFOOT.**

The city hadn't just survived; it had worried. The absence of the blur had been felt. The silence of the streets where the "thump-thump-thump" of his feet usually echoed had unsettled the neighbors. It was a realization that hit Hiroto and Hana deeply—their son wasn't just a child who ran; he was a part of the city’s heart.

Ren never had another sick day. Whether it was the medicinal honey or his own sheer willpower, he spent the next year in a state of peak physical condition. He didn't take a day off. Not during the festivals, not during the heatwaves, and certainly not during the Great Canal Freeze.

Then came his fifth birthday.

In Silverhaven, as in most of the world, a child’s fifth birthday was a modest occasion. It was the age when they might start formal schooling or begin a light apprenticeship. It was certainly not an age associated with the System.

The System was something for adults. It arrived for most during their late teens, usually during a moment of intense focus or after a period of dedicated training. It was the "Awakening," the moment when the world’s hidden mechanics became visible to the user.

Ren’s birthday dinner was simple: his favorite rice cakes and a small roasted bird. Hiroto and Hana sat across from him, watching their son inhale his food with his usual efficiency. 

"Five years old," Hiroto mused, looking at his son. "Perhaps it's time we think about a tutor for his letters, Hana. A courier should be able to read more than just the names on the signs."

Ren started to respond, but he froze. His rice cake fell from his hand, bouncing once on the wooden table.

"Ren? What is it?" Hana asked, her voice sharp with sudden alarm.

Ren’s eyes weren't focused on his parents. They were wide, staring into the middle distance, tracked by a flicker of blue light that they couldn't see.

To Ren, the world had suddenly changed. A translucent blue pane hovered in the air before him, shimmering with an ethereal glow.

**[NOTIFICATION: RECOGNITION OF DEDICATION]**
**[CRITERIA MET: 100,000 MILES TRAVERSED]**
**[CRITERIA MET: 10,000 SUCCESSFUL DELIVERIES]**

Ren blinked, trying to clear his vision, but the words only became sharper.

**[AWAKENING INITIALIZED...]**
**[SYSTEM INTEGRATION COMPLETE]**

"Papa," Ren whispered, his voice trembling not with fear, but with a strange, humming energy. "The air is... the air is writing to me."

Hiroto and Hana exchanged a look of pure, unadulterated shock. They knew the signs. They had heard the stories of the Awakening. But it was impossible. No one awakened at five. No one.

A final line of text manifested in front of Ren, pulsing with a golden light.

**[CLASS GRANTED: JUNIOR COURIER]**
**[LEVEL: 1]**

"Junior Courier," Ren read aloud, the words tasting like ozone on his tongue.

The blue pane vanished, but the energy remained. Ren felt as though every muscle in his body had been tightened and tuned like the strings of a master's lute. He could see the vibration of the candle flame. He could hear the drip of the water clock in the next room with crystalline clarity.

He looked at his parents, his expression a mix of childhood wonder and an ancient, terrifying focus.

"I think," Ren said, his feet already beginning to twitch under the table, "I need to go on a run."

The Silverhaven Express had officially been commissioned. (Word Count: 1478)
