# Chapter 4: Bottled Lightning

The morning after his fifth birthday, Ren Ito didn't just wake up; he activated. 

The blue translucent pane of the System was gone from his physical vision, but the data—the *feeling* of it—was etched into his very soul. He moved to get out of bed and nearly launched himself into the opposite wall. The simple act of tensing his muscles now carried a weight of intent and power that his five-year-old frame was still struggling to contain. It was as if his body, which had always been fast, had suddenly been rewired with copper and lightning.

"Careful, Ren!" Hana's voice drifted from the kitchen, accompanied by the familiar sizzle of breakfast. "You nearly knocked over the washbasin!"

Ren steadied himself, his small hands gripping the edge of his wooden bedframe. He felt a humming beneath his skin, a rhythmic pulse that matched the ticking of his father's timepiece in the next room. He wasn't just Ren anymore. He was a *Junior Courier, Level 1*.

If he had been fast before, he was bottled lightning now. 

After a breakfast that he ate with even more mechanical efficiency than usual, Ren stepped out into the bustling streets of Silverhaven. The damp morning air felt different against his skin. He could sense the drag of it, the way the wind curled around the corners of the stone buildings. He could see the "lines" of the city—not the physical streets, but the vectors of movement, the gaps between the merchant carts, and the potential energy stored in every low wall and canal railing.

He looked at his first delivery: a small, sealed pouch for Kenji in the Floral District. Usually, this run took him five minutes. 

Ren leaned forward, his feet finding the familiar grip of the cobblestones. He pushed off, and the world shifted.

He wasn't "physically" faster than a full-fledged, classed, adult courier. He knew this instinctively. In a flat-out sprint across the Grand Plaza, or a long-distance run along the well-paved Imperial Road, a twenty-year-old Courier with ten levels and a "Wind-Step" skill would beat him. Their strides were longer, their lungs were bigger, and their System-backed endurance was a well-honed machine.

But city deliveries were not a "sprint." They were a puzzle. And in the dense, chaotic labyrinth of Silverhaven, Ren was the master of the pieces.

A "true" courier, as defined by the Guild, was a creature of the established routes. They wore the official blue-and-gold livery, they carried heavy leather satchels, and they ran along the primary thoroughfares. They were professionals. They didn't leap over crates of cabbages. They didn't slide under the slow-moving axles of grain wagons. They certainly didn't run along the vertical side of a moss-covered canal wall for a few seconds to bypass a congested bridge.

But Ren wasn't a Guild courier. He was Ren.

He reached the first major intersection—the crossing near the Fish Market. A massive wagon laden with timber was currently jammed against a spice merchant's cart, creating a wall of wood and angry shouting that blocked the entire street. Two Guild couriers were standing at the edge of the crowd, checking their timepieces and sighing as they waited for the guards to clear the mess.

Ren didn't even slow down.

He saw the gap. He saw the stack of empty crates near the tavern. He saw the low-hanging awning of the weaver's shop. 

*Left foot, crate. Right foot, window ledge. Twist.*

Ren launched himself upward. To the watching crowd, he was a flicker of motion. He didn't just climb; he flowed. He touched the window ledge with the tips of his fingers, used the momentum to vault over the awning, and then—with a grace that defied his age—he was running along the narrow stone lip of the second-story balcony.

He looked down as he passed the Guild couriers. They were staring up at him, their mouths agape, their official satchels looking suddenly very heavy and very slow. 

Ren tumbled into a roll as he reached the end of the balcony, his small body absorbing the impact with a fluidity that made it look like he was made of liquid. He hit the ground running, weaving through a cluster of startled pigeons and vanishing into a "Shadow Alley" that was barely wider than his shoulders.

The alley was a shortcut that required him to scale a six-foot wooden fence. Before his Awakening, this would have taken him several seconds of scrambling. Now? He didn't even break his stride. He planted a foot on a protruding brick, pushed off, and cleared the fence in a single, soaring arc.

"Whoa!" he breathed as he landed. He had to dig his heels in to keep from skidding into a stack of rubbish. 

He was actually having to slow down at some points. His mind was processing the routes perfectly, but his new Level 1 stats were almost *too* much for the physics of the world. He was finding that if he took a corner too fast, the friction of his boots against the stone wasn't enough to hold the turn. He was literally almost too fast for his own grip on the earth.

"Almost," he grinned to himself, his teeth white against his flushed face.

He reached Kenji's garden in three minutes. Not five. Three.

Kenji was just hanging out his Green Sign when He heard the "thump-slide" of Ren arriving. The gardener jumped, nearly dropping the wooden sign.

"By the gods, Ren! I didn't even hear you coming!" Kenji stared at the boy. Ren was glowing—literally. A faint, silver shimmer played around his ankles, a lingering effect of his Junior Courier energy. "What did your mother feed you for breakfast? Lightning?"

"It's my birthday present," Ren smiled, handing over the pouch. "The System gave it to me."

Kenji's eyes went wide. He looked at the five-year-old child, then at the pouch, then back at the child. As a gardener, Kenji didn't have much of a Class—just a few levels in "Botanical Cultivator"—but he knew enough to recognize an Awakening when he saw one.

"Ren Ito," Kenji whispered, a look of profound respect crossing his weathered face. "You... you've Awakened? Already?"

Ren didn't answer. He was already looking at the Green Sign he could see—a tiny ribbon fluttering three streets away. His mind was already calculating the most efficient path: *Roof, chimney, canal-rope, landing.*

"I have more deliveries, Kenji! See you tomorrow!"

Ren pushed off. This time, he didn't just run; he leaned into the silver hum. He found the "sweet spot" of the speed, the point where he was just at the edge of losing control but maintained his narrow-surface agility. He slid under a fruit seller's cart, the wooden boards passing inches above his head, and popped up on the other side with a giggle of pure delight.

An adult courier would have walked around the cart. An adult courier would have followed the rules. 

Ren Ito was rewriting the rules with every step. He was a five-year-old boy in a world of giants, but in the dense, vertical, messy heart of Silverhaven, the giants couldn't even see the dust he left behind.

He reached the top of the High Bridge and looked out over the city. It looked smaller to him now. Not because he had grown, but because his world had expanded. Every rooftop was a road. Every window was a shortcut. Every wall was a possibility.

He felt the system message pulse again, a quiet reminder of his new status.

**[CLASS: JUNIOR COURIER - LEVEL 1]**
**[SKILL UNLOCKED: PATHFINDER'S SENSE (PASSIVE)]**

He closed his eyes for a second, and he could see it—the "Silverhaven Express" wasn't just a name people called him. It was a golden thread that zig-zagged through the city, connecting the Green Signs in a perfect, high-speed circuit.

He opened his eyes, the silver shimmer around his ankles brightening as he prepared to launch himself into his next run.

"Almost too fast," he whispered, his voice lost in the wind of his own making. "But not quite." 

He vanished in a blur of blue and silver, a tiny legend in the making, proving to the city of Silverhaven that while the Guild couriers might have the roads, Ren "Quickfoot" Ito had everything else. (Word Count: 1385)
