# Chapter 5: The Official Seal

Pride, in the city of Silverhaven, was often proportional to the height of one’s collar and the weight of one’s leather satchel. The professional couriers of the Silverhaven Guild were men of immense pride. They were the lifelines of the city, the runners who kept the gears of commerce and government turning. They were classed individuals, hallowed by the System, and they did not take kindly to competition—especially competition that was three feet tall and possessed a vocabulary heavily featuring the word "Papa."

The morning after Ren’s first day as a Level 1 Junior Courier, the Ito household was visited by a representative of this pride.

A sharp, rhythmic rapping echoed through the apartment, louder than the usual neighborly knocks. When Hiroto opened the door, he was met with the sight of a man dressed in the crisp blue-and-gold livery of the Guild. The courier stood with his chest puffed out, a leather tube held in one gloved hand, and a look of such concentrated smugness on his face that it seemed to take up more space than the man himself.

"Clerk Hiroto Ito?" the courier asked, his voice a practiced baritone.

"I am," Hiroto replied, his brow furrowing as he noticed the official seal on the tube.

The courier handed it over with a flourish that was meant to be imposing but came across as merely pretentious. "A formal communication from the High Master of the Silverhaven Courier Guild. It would be in your best interest to read it immediately. Some... irregularities have been noted in the Scribe’s Quarter."

Without waiting for a response, the man turned on his heel and marched down the hallway, his boots clicking with an self-important rhythm.

Hiroto returned to the dining table, where Hana was pouring tea and Ren was currently occupied with testing the friction of his new boots against the wooden floorboards. Hiroto broke the wax seal, unrolled the vellum, and began to read.

As his eyes moved across the page, his expression shifted from curiosity to a deep, simmering frown.

"What is it, Hiroto?" Hana asked, setting down the teapot.

"A cease and desist," Hiroto muttered, his voice dropping an octave. "The Guild is claiming 'unauthorized and unlicensed delivery of sensitive municipal and private correspondence.' They’re threatening to bring charges before the Registry. They say Ren is disrupting the 'standardized flow of communication' and creating a 'dangerous precedent for unclassed labor.'"

Hana’s eyes widened. "Unclassed? But Ren Awakened! He has a Class!"

"They don't know that," Hiroto said, his fingers tightening on the edges of the vellum. "And even if they did, they’d argue that a five-year-old cannot hold a license. They’re afraid, Hana. They’ve seen him outrunning their veterans in the alleys, and they’re trying to use the law to kill the 'Silverhaven Express' before it becomes a threat."

Ren stopped his friction test and looked up, his dark eyes sharp. He didn't understand all the big words, but he understood "stop." He understood that the men in the blue-and-gold suits didn't want him to run.

"Papa?" Ren asked, his voice quiet. "Do I have to stop?"

Hiroto looked at his son—at the silver shimmer that still played around the boy’s ankles, at the pure, uninhibited joy that radiated from him every time his feet hit the cobblestones. He looked at the heavy chest of coppers under the bed, the fruit of his son’s hard work and the city’s genuine affection.

"No," Hiroto said, his voice hardening with a sudden, clerk-like resolve. "You don't have to stop. But we are going to change the game."

Hiroto grabbed a fresh sheet of vellum, his quill moving with a speed that rivaled Ren’s legs. He was a senior city clerk. He spent his life navigating the Byzantine structures of Silverhaven’s bureaucracy. The Guild might have the roads, but Hiroto Ito had the rules.

He wrote with a ferocious precision, citing Registry Bylaw 42-C regarding 'Early Awakening Exceptionalism' and Municipal Code 12 regarding 'Non-Contractual Civic Service.' He didn't just write a response; he wrote a legal ambush. He concluded by formally registering the "Silverhaven Express" as a subsidiary training entity under the Scribe’s Quarter, with Ren named as its primary (and only) Junior Carrier.

The ink was barely dry when Hiroto applied his official clerk’s seal.

"Ren," Hiroto said, looking up.

The word hadn't even finished leaving his mouth when the letter was gone. There was a faint *whoosh* of air, a blur of motion, and a flash of blue-and-white tunic out the open window. 

Hana blinked. "You didn't even tell him where to go."

"He knows," Hiroto said, a small, satisfied smile playing on his lips. "He’s been watching those 'snobs' for months. He knows exactly where the High Master’s office is."

Exactly fifteen minutes passed. For Hana, it was fifteen minutes of nervous pacing. For Hiroto, it was fifteen minutes of calm, methodical tea-drinking. 

Then, the window-sill rattled.

Ren landed lightly on the floor, his chest barely rising. He looked as if he’d just taken a leisurely stroll through the garden, despite having just traveled twice across the city’s heart. In his hand, he held a response.

"The man with the big hat looked very surprised, Papa," Ren grinned, his eyes sparkling. "He tried to tell me I couldn't come in, so I went through the skylight."

Hiroto took the letter, broke the new seal—this one not arrogant, but hurried—and read it. His smile deepened.

"What does it say?" Hana asked, leaning in.

"It says," Hiroto started, his voice thick with triumph, "that the Registry, having reviewed the 'unprecedented circumstances' and the 'impeccable record of the individual in question,' has decided to grant a temporary, probationary license for a new category of municipal service."

He reached into the envelope and pulled out a small, jingling collection of items. They were silver-threaded patches, intricately embroidered with the image of a stylized silver wing over a depiction of Silverhaven’s Grand Canal.

"Junior Carrier patches," Hiroto said, holding them up. "Officially identifying the bearer as an authorized agent of the Silverhaven Express."

Ren’s jaw dropped. He reached out, his small fingers tracing the silver threads of the wing. It was the first time he’d seen his nickname rendered in such a formal, beautiful way.

"They had them ready?" Hana asked, surprised.

"The Guild keeps them for the children of the High Masters who they *plan* to Awakening in ten years," Hiroto chuckled. "But my letter made it clear that if they didn't recognize Ren now, I would take the 'Early Awakening' data directly to the Mayor, and the Guild would lose its monopoly on educational grants. They folded like a paper boat in a storm."

Ren grabbed the patches, his feet already doing a celebratory dance. "Does this mean I'm a real courier now, Papa?"

"It means," Hiroto said, looking down at his son with a pride that finally outweighed his clerkly Reserve, "that you are the *only* Junior Carrier in the history of Silverhaven. It means that when those Guild couriers see you flashing past, they have to treat you as a colleague, not a nuisance."

Hiroto looked at the patches, then at the window where Ren had departed just minutes ago. "And it means we have some sewing to do. Tomorrow, the Silverhaven Express delivers with the full weight of the Registry behind it."

Ren looked at the patches, then at his father, his face splitting into a grin that was half-mischief and half-destiny.

"Take that, you snobs!" Ren whispered to the empty air of the city, his silver shimmer flaring with a new, sanctioned intensity.

The Silverhaven Express wasn't just a boy anymore. It was an institution. (Word Count: 1412)
