# Chapter 6: The Gauntlet of the Guild

Official recognition was one thing; street-level respect was quite another. While Hiroto’s bureaucratic maneuvering had secured Ren the "Junior Carrier" patches and a temporary license, it had also painted a target on the boy’s back. To the seasoned veterans of the Silverhaven Courier Guild, Ren wasn't just a prodigy; he was a loophole. He was an affront to their years of training and their carefully guarded monopoly.

If the Guild couldn't stop him with vellum and wax, they would stop him with bone and stone.

The first month of Ren’s official status became known in the Scribe’s Quarter as "The Gauntlet." It started subtly—a "missed" step by a senior courier that sent a shoulder into Ren as he blurred past, an "accidental" spill of slippery fish-gut water in a narrow alleyway, or a heavy cart suddenly losing a wheel just as Ren was preparing to vault over its axle.

The Guild couriers were clever. They knew the routes Ren frequented. They knew his timing. They began to treat the city itself as an obstacle course designed specifically to trip him up.

One Tuesday morning, Ren was navigating the High Bridge route, a silver-tipped message for the Master of Locks tucked into his belt. He was moving at Level 1 speed, the familiar hum in his ankles giving him that extra edge of grip as he rounded the corner near the Treasury.

A senior courier, a man named Harken who was known for his massive frame and even larger ego, was "standing guard" near a narrow archway. As Ren approached, Harken didn't move. Instead, just as Ren was three feet away, Harken stepped out, his heavy leather boot positioning itself perfectly to catch Ren’s leading foot.

In the old days, Ren would have tumbled. He would have skidded across the stones, losing skin and time. But the System had changed the way Ren perceived the world.

To Ren, Harken’s movement didn't look like a mistake. It looked like a *challenge*. 

*Pathfinder’s Sense* flared in Ren’s mind. A subconscious calculation—Angle: 45 degrees. Vector: Impending. Adjustment: Necessary.

Ren didn't trip. Instead, he used Harken’s boot as a launching pad. He planted his own small foot squarely on the senior courier’s instep, pushed off with the silver hum of his Junior Courier energy, and performed a mid-air twist that carried him over Harken’s head.

"Thanks for the boost, Mr. Harken!" Ren shouted back, his voice bright and unaffected as he vanished into the next alley.

Harken stood there, his jaw dropping, his boot throbbing with the unexpected force of a five-year-old’s high-speed takeoff.

But the Guild didn't give up. If trips didn't work, they tried "impromptu obstacles."

In the Shadow Alleys, where Ren often saved minutes by taking vertical routes, he began to find that things were... different. Ropes that usually hung loose were suddenly taut, creating clothesline traps. Boxes were stacked in ways that blocked his landing zones. Once, a group of apprentices "accidentally" dropped a crate of fragile glass bottles right in front of him, creating a field of jagged shards that should have forced him to stop.

Ren’s response wasn't frustration. It was fascination. 

To him, these weren't malicious attempts to hurt him; they were "High Difficulty Modifiers." Every push, every shove, and every obstacle was a puzzle to be solved at forty miles per hour. He started looking forward to them. He began to intentionally seek out the routes where he knew the Guild members were hiding, eager to see what new "test" they had prepared.

The System, as it turned out, agreed with this perspective.

**[NOTIFICATION: ADAPTIVE AGILITY RECOGNIZED]**
**[CRITERIA MET: 50 SUCCESSFUL OBSTACLE EVASIONS]**
**[CRITERIA MET: 5 SUBSTANTIAL PHYSICAL ADVERSITY OVERCOMES]**

On a rainy Thursday at the end of the first month, just as Ren was returning from a particularly difficult delivery involving a very angry dog and a very slippery roof, the blue pane manifested again.

**[LEVEL UP: 1 -> 4]**
**[STAT POINT ALLOCATION: AGILITY (+5), PERCEPTION (+3), STAMINA (+2)]**

Ren felt the shift instantly. The silver hum in his ankles didn't just get louder; it became more complex. He felt a new lightness in his bones, a sense that gravity was more of a suggestion than a law. 

But the System wasn't done.

**[NEW SKILL GRANTED: BALANCE MASTERY (ACTIVE)]**
*Effect: For 30 seconds, the user Gains 100% friction on any solid surface, including vertical walls and ceilings.*

**[NEW SKILL GRANTED: PREDICTIVE TWITCH (PASSIVE)]**
*Effect: Subconsciously detects physical interference from external sources 0.5 seconds before impact.*

Ren stood in the rain, the water dripping off his Junior Carrier patches. He felt... immense. He wasn't just a boy running; he was a system-backed entity that was evolving in direct response to the pressure of his environment.

"They're helping me," Ren whispered, a slow grin spreading across his face. "The Guild is making me level up!"

The next day, the Guild tried their boldest move yet. Harken and two other senior couriers coordinated a "Box-In" at the Grand Canal Bridge. It was a narrow span with high railings. Harken would block the front, the other two would block the rear, and Ren would be forced to stop and "discuss" the Guild's grievances.

Ren saw them coming from two streets away. *Predictive Twitch* hummed in the base of his skull, alerting him to the coordinated movements of his surroundings. 

He didn't slow down. He accelerated.

As he reached the bridge, Harken stepped out, arms spread wide, a smug grin on his face. "Nowhere to go, little brat. Give us the bag and let's talk about licenses."

Ren didn't target the gaps between them. He didn't even target the bridge.

He activated *Balance Mastery*.

The silver shimmer around his ankles flared into a brilliant, crystalline blue. Ren veered sharply to the right, his feet hitting the vertical stone of the canal's retaining wall. 

Harken’s eyes bugged out. "What the—!"

Ren wasn't just running on the wall; he was running *horizontally* to the ground, his boots sticking to the mossy stone as if he were on a flat road. He bypassed the bridge entirely, passing six feet beneath the feet of the stunned couriers, and then—with a final burst of speed—he ran *up* the vertical side of a parked gondola and flipped onto the opposite bank.

He hit the ground in a perfect tumble, popped up, and offered a cheeky salute to the three frozen couriers on the bridge.

"See you at the Registry!" he shouted, his voice echoing off the canal stones.

The word spread through the city like a wildfire. The "Gauntlet" hadn't broken Ren. It had tempered him. The Guild’s harassment had backfired so spectacularly that many of the younger apprentices were starting to secretly admire the "Silverhaven Express." 

"Hiroto," Hana asked that evening, watching Ren practice handstands on the back of the sofa with impossible stability. "What did you do to that boy? He doesn't look like he’s running anymore. He looks like he’s playing with the world."

Hiroto adjusted his spectacles, a proud glint in his eyes. "I didn't do anything, Hana. The Guild tried to put a wall in his way, and they forgot that Ren’s never seen a wall he couldn't climb."

Ren looked down from his handstand, his eyes bright with the blue light of his new skills. He had three more levels, two new skills, and the knowledge that every trip and every shove was just another step toward the top. 

The Courier Guild had tried to give Ren a lesson. Instead, they had given the Silverhaven Express a boost. And Ren was already looking for the next "lesson." (Word Count: 1354)
