# Chapter 7: The Twinge of Truth

The defeat at the Grand Canal Bridge had left a bitter taste in Harken’s mouth, one that no amount of tavern ale could wash away. To the senior couriers of the Guild, Ren Ito was no longer just a nuisance; he was a walking, wall-running existential threat. Every time the blue-and-white blur bypassed an official checkpoint or outperformed a veteran in the Scribe’s Quarter, the Guild’s prestige took another hit.

"The law won't stop him, and the streets won't break him," Harken growled, slamming his tankard down on the scarred wood of the 'Musty Parchment' tavern. Two other couriers, their faces shadowed by the flickering torchlight, nodded in glum agreement. "If we can't trip his feet, we have to trip his reputation. We need something that even the 'Amiable Clerk' can't talk his way out of."

A sinister plan began to take shape in the smoky gloom of the tavern. If the Silverhaven Express was so dedicated to delivering whatever was asked of him, the Guild would simply ask him to deliver something... unforgivable.

The next morning, the city of Silverhaven woke to a subtle change in its urban landscape. Ren, currently Level 4 and buzzing with the excitement of his new *Balance Mastery* skill, noticed it almost immediately as he exited the Ito apartment.

His *Pathfinder’s Sense* flared, but not with its usual golden efficiency. This time, there was a discordant note, like a string on a lute that was slightly out of tune.

There, just a few streets away in an area of the Fish Market he rarely visited, was a Green Sign. It was a fresh piece of cloth, the dye so vibrant it looked almost aggressive. And it wasn't the only one. His sense detected another in a dark corner of the Warehouse District, and a third near the back entrance of a prestigious apothecary in the High District.

"New clients!" Ren chirped to himself, his feet already beginning their rhythmic "thump-thump-thump" against the cobblestones. "The Express is expanding!"

He reached the first sign in the Fish Market in record time. It was tucked behind a brine-soaked stall where the smell of old scales and salt was particularly pungent. A small, nondescript wooden box sat on a crate beneath the green banner. No note. No instructions. Just the box.

Ren skidded to a halt, his hand reaching out to snatch the package with his usual professional flourish.

But just as his fingertips were inches from the rough wood, it happened.

A sharp, electric "tingle" shot up his arm, settling in the center of his chest. It wasn't the silver hum of his courier energy, nor was it the warning buzz of *Predictive Twitch*. This was deeper, colder. It felt like a drop of ice-water sliding down his spine.

Ren pulled his hand back as if the box were white-hot. 

He stared at the wood. To any normal eye, it was just a package. But to Ren, the light around it seemed to dim. His innate ability, *Sense Illegitimacy*, which had been a quiet background hum since his birth, had just let out a scream.

"Something’s... wrong with this," Ren whispered, his brow furrowing. He leaned in closer, his nose wrinkling. There was a faint, cloyingly sweet scent coming from the seams of the box—the smell of *Star-Silt*, a highly illegal and addictive refined sap from the Deepwoods. To deliver this was to deliver a death sentence or a lifetime of misery for someone.

Ren didn't touch it. He backed away, his heart hammering against his ribs. The Green Sign, which usually felt like a beacon of welcome, now looked like a trap.

He shook his head, trying to clear the cold feeling from his chest, and pivoted toward the second sign in the Warehouse District. "Maybe it’s just that one," he told himself. "Maybe the fisherman made a mistake."

He reached the Warehouse District in a blur of blue, his *Balance Mastery* allowing him to take a shortcut along the top of a stone wall to avoid the morning bustle. He found the second sign in a dead-end alley, leaning against a pile of rotting grain sacks. Another box. Smaller this time, wrapped in black silk.

Again, Ren reached out. And again, the tingle flared. This time it was stronger, a jolt of alarm that made him stumble back. The silk wrap felt like it was woven from shadows. 

*Sense Illegitimacy* wasn't just suggesting something was wrong; it was forbidding him from completing the act. It was as if his very Class—the core of his identity as a Courier—was being threatened by the contents of the package.

"It's a trick," Ren realized, his eyes widening. He looked at the rooftops, sensing the presence of onlookers. He couldn't see them, but he felt the weight of their gaze. 

Harken and his cohorts were indeed watching from the shadows of a nearby granary, their breath held. 

"Take it, you brat," Harken hissed under his breath. "Just one touch, and we'll have the guard on you before you hit the next street."

But Ren didn't take it. He stood in the alley for a long moment, his chest heaving. He was only five years old, but the System and a lifetime of running had given him an intuition far beyond his years. He felt the threads of a web being spun around him, and he knew that the next delivery would be his last if he made the wrong choice.

He turned and raced toward the third sign in the High District. He needed to be sure. He needed to know if the entire city had turned into a minefield of "wrong" things.

The third package was at the apothecary’s back gate. It was a glass vial, carefully padded in wool, sitting beneath a perfectly executed Green Sign. 

Ren didn't even reach for it this time. He stopped five feet away and felt the tingle. It was a high-pitched vibration now, a warning bell that seemed to echo in his very teeth. The vial didn't contain medicine. It contained *Ghost-Sap*, a poison that could silence a heart without leaving a trace.

Ren didn't hesitate anymore. The time for running was over; the time for reporting had begun.

He didn't head back to the Scribe’s Quarter. He didn't go to his father. He knew that if he went to Hiroto, the Guild would claim he was just hiding his illicit cargo. He needed an authority that even the Guild couriers feared.

He headed for the Magister’s Office.

The Office of the High Magister was a stark, grey stone building situated at the very center of Silverhaven’s administrative heart. It was a place of iron-clad laws and cold, unyielding justice. The Magister himself was a man of Level 40 "Justice Dealer" class, a person whose very presence could force a confession from the guilty.

Ren arrived at the heavy iron doors like a thunderclap. The guards, seasoned men with heavy halberds, started to push him back, but Ren didn't stop.

"I need to see the Magister!" Ren shouted, his voice high and shrill with adrenaline. "There are wrong things in the city! Green signs are lying!"

The guards exchanged a look of amusement that quickly faded as they saw the intensity in the boy’s eyes—and the silver-winged patches on his tunic. They knew the Silverhaven Express. They knew the "Amiable Clerk’s" son.

"Ren Ito?" one of the guards asked. "What are you talking about, lad?"

"Dangerous things!" Ren pointed back toward the High District. "Illegal things! Someone is using my signs to hide bad packages! I felt it! My System told me!"

The doors to the inner sanctum creaked open. A tall, gaunt man with hair like silver wire and eyes that seemed to burn with an inner light stepped out. He wore robes of midnight blue, embroidered with scales of justice.

This was Magister Valerius.

Valerius looked down at the five-year-old boy. He didn't see a child. He saw a Level 4 Junior Courier with a silver shimmer around his ankles and a soul that was vibrating with a terrifyingly pure sense of illegitimacy.

"You felt them, did you?" Valerius asked, his voice like the grinding of ancient stones.

"They bit me, sir," Ren said, holding his hands behind his back as if they were still stinging. "The packages. They bit my heart."

Valerius’s eyes narrowed. He possessed a skill called *Sight of the Law*, and right now, he could see the residue of the Guild’s treachery clinging to the air around Ren. He could see the shadows of the traps that had been laid.

"Soldiers," Valerius commanded, his voice echoing through the plaza. "Follow the boy’s directions. Secure the packages at the Fish Market, the Warehouse District, and the Apothecary’s gate. And find out who placed those signs."

He looked back at Ren, a flicker of something resembling a smile crossing his stern face. "You did well to come here, Ren Ito. Many men would have taken the coppers and ignored the 'bite'."

Ren nodded, his breath finally coming back to him. He felt the cold tingle in his chest begin to fade, replaced by a warm, solid sense of rightness. The System chimed softly in his mind, acknowledging a different kind of achievement.

**[NOTIFICATION: MORAL FORTITUDE RECOGNIZED]**
**[CRITERIA MET: REFUSAL OF ILLICIT ENGAGEMENT]**
**[CRITERIA MET: UPHOLDING THE LAW OF THE OFFICE]**

**[LEVEL UP: 4 -> 5]**
**[NEW SKILL GRANTED: TRUTH-RUNNER’S AURA (PASSIVE)]**
*Effect: Increases the user’s 'Sense Illegitimacy' range and allows the user to leave a faint, visible trail of 'Justice light' on any illicit item they encounter but do not touch.*

Ren looked at his hands. They were no longer stinging. They were glowing.

"I'm a Truth-Runner now," Ren whispered.
