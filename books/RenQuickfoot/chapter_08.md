# Chapter 8: The Price of Justice

The interior of the High Magister’s chambers was a stark contrast to the vibrant, chaotic streets that Ren Ito called home. Here, the air was still, heavy with the scent of old parchment, cold stone, and the metallic tang of the two-story-tall iron scales that loomed behind the Magister’s desk. Light filtered in through high, narrow windows, casting long, dramatic shadows across the floor—shadows that seemed to weigh more in this room than anywhere else in Silverhaven.

Ren stood between his father, Hiroto, and the two massive guards who had escorted him in. He felt tiny, his feet barely taking up and space on the vast stone slabs of the floor. His silver-winged patches felt unusually bright in the dim light, a stark reminder of the responsibility he had inadvertently claimed.

Opposite them stood the three 'former' couriers. Harken, once so puffed with pride and smugness, now looked like a deflated balloon. His blue-and-gold livery had been stripped of its Guild insignia, leaving only raw, frayed threads. His two companions, younger men who had followed Harken’s lead in a desperate attempt at relevance, stared at their boots, their faces pale and drawn.

Magister Valerius sat behind his desk, his fingers steepled. He wasn't wearing his formal court robes, but his presence was no less formidable. The air around him seemed to hum with the low-frequency vibration of his *Sight of the Law* skill, a sensory pressure that made the guilty sweat and the innocent stand a little straighter.

"The evidence is irrefutable," Valerius’s voice cut through the silence like a sharpened blade. "The packages recovered from the Fish Market, the Warehouse District, and the Apothecary’s gate were meticulously laced with Truth-Runner’s residue—a light that only manifests when the law is upheld against the illicit. Your own signatures were found on the acquisition logs for the crates. Your motive was petty, but your actions were catastrophic."

He picked up a heavy scroll and began to read the formal charges.

"Trafficking of Class-A Forbidden Substances: Star-Silt and Ghost-Sap. Attempt to corrupt a minor for the purposes of illicit transport. Conspiracy to defame a municipal official. Endangerment of public safety through the placement of malicious decoys."

Valerius looked over the top of the scroll, his burning gaze settling on Harken. "In Silverhaven, the flow of information is sacred. To use a courier—and a Junior Carrier at that—to poison the city's arteries... there is no mercy for such a crime. You are hereby sentenced to ten years of hard labor in the Deepwood Silt-Pits. Your Classes are suspended, and your Guild membership is permanently revoked."

Harken let out a choked sound, his knees buckling. The guards grabbed his arms, lifting him with bruising force. As they were dragged toward the iron-bound door, Harken’s eyes caught Ren’s for a fleeting second. 

Ren didn't see hatred in those eyes anymore. He only saw fear.

"I didn't want to get them in trouble," Ren mumbled, his voice small and echoing in the large room. He looked down at his hands, which were still faintly glowing with the silver-blue light of his *Truth-Runner’s Aura*. "I just wanted them to stop being mean."

Hiroto placed a hand on Ren’s shoulder, a firm, grounding presence. "They didn't just 'mean' to be mean, Ren. They chose to be dangerous. There’s a difference."

Magister Valerius stood up, his tall, gaunt frame casting a shadow that reached the Ito’s feet. He walked around the desk and stood directly before Ren.

"You have done this city a great service, Ren Ito," the Magister said, his voice softening ever so slightly. "We have been trying to crack the Star-Silt ring for months. The dealers were clever, hiding their movements behind the chaos of the Guild’s logistics. By refusing those packages, you did more than just save yourself. You severed a primary vein of the trade."

"But... what happens now?" Ren asked, looking up. "The bad things are gone, but... I saw the people near the Warehouse District. The ones who look like they’re made of shadows. They were waiting for those boxes."

Valerius’s expression became somber. "You are more perceptive than many of my senior investigators. That is the true price of justice, Ren. When we remove the poison, the body suffers from its absence before it can heal. Star-Silt is a cruel master. Those who are addicted to it will face days of agony now that the supply is cut. Some will not survive the withdrawal."

Ren felt a pang of guilt that was sharper than any Guild-trip or shove. He thought of the heavy boxes, the sweet smell of the sap, and the hollowed-out eyes of the people he’d seen lurking in the alleys. 

"Is there anything we can do?" Ren asked, his feet twitching with a new kind of urgency—not the urge to run away, but the urge to run *toward* the problem. "To help those people who will suffer?"

Valerius looked at the boy with a profound respect. "Most men would be content with the reward money and the praise. But you... you are a Truth-Runner. You see the ripples of your actions."

He leaned closer, his voice dropping to a confidential whisper. "If you truly wish to help, go see the Apothecary Velda in the High District. She is a woman of... difficult temperament, but she has the knowledge to create the *Silt-Soothe* tea. It is a medicine that can ease the pain of the addiction, but she is always short on the ingredients that must be harvested from the most difficult corners of the city."

He stood back up, his silver hair catching the light. "She may have a task for you, Ren. A task that requires speed, precision, and a heart that doesn't shy away from the shadows."

"Velda," Ren repeated, the name etching itself into his *Pathfinder’s Sense*. A new golden thread began to form in his mind, snaking through the High District to a shop tucked behind the great library.

"I’ll go now!" Ren said, already leaning toward the door.

"Wait, Ren," Hiroto started, but Valerius raised a hand.

"Let him go, Clerk Hiroto," Valerius said. "The city doesn't just need messengers. It needs a conscience. And I suspect your son is the fastest conscience Silverhaven has ever seen."

Ren didn't wait for a second invitation. He was gone in a blur of blue, his feet hitting the stone stairs of the office with a rhythmic velocity that sounded like a message of hope.

He bypassed the Grand Canal, using *Balance Mastery* to run along the underside of the Merchant’s Bridge to avoid the midday traffic. He felt the silver hum in his ankles shifting, becoming deeper, more resonant. He wasn't just running for coppers anymore. He was running for the shadows.

He arrived at the High District, skidding to a halt before a shop with a sign that featured a gnarled root curled around a mortar and pestle. The air here was different—sharp with the scent of dried herbs and the acrid tang of boiling chemicals.

Ren took a deep breath, straightened his Junior Carrier patches, and pushed open the heavy wooden door.

Inside, the shop was a jungle of glass and greenery. Jars of preserved specimens lined the walls, and bundles of drying lavender hung from the ceiling. Behind the counter sat a woman whose hair was a wild nest of copper curls and whose eyes were hidden behind thick, circular spectacles. She was currently occupied with grinding something that smelled like burnt toast.

"We’re closed for the mid-day inventory," the woman barked without looking up. "Come back when the bells strike three."

"Magister Valerius sent me," Ren said, his voice steady. "He said you might have a task for a Junior Carrier. For the people who hurt from the Star-Silt."

The grinding stopped. The woman looked up, her spectacles magnifying her eyes to a comical, yet intimidating size. She looked at Ren—at his small size, at his silver-winged patches, and at the unmistakable light of a Level 5 Truth-Runner.

"So," Apothecary Velda said, a slow, predatory grin spreading across her face. "You're the little Flash everyone’s talking about. The one who broke the ring."

She set down her pestle and leaned over the counter. "You want to help, do you? You want to see the dirty side of the medicine, not just the coppers in the pouch?"

"I want to help," Ren insisted.

"Good," Velda said, pulling out a map of the city’s sewer system and the abandoned dock-works. "Because I need someone who can get to the *Moon-Moss* at the bottom of the Old Docks before the tide comes in. It’s the only thing that will stop the heart-shakes from the Silt-withdrawl. And it’s in a place where even the rats are afraid to go."

Ren looked at the map. He saw the dark, winding paths, the vertical drops, and the timing required. 

"I can do it," Ren said, his feet vibrating against the shop floor.

"Then run, little Express," Velda whispered, her eyes burning with a reflected blue light. "Because the tide doesn't wait for justice, and neither does the pain."

Ren turned and launched himself out the door, the bell chiming a frantic accompaniment to his departure.
