# Chapter 10: The Yellow Sign

The triage center in the Warehouse District was a place where the scent of hope struggled against the lingering stench of rot and despair. It was a cavernous, drafty warehouse that had been quickly cleared of its crates of spice and timber, replaced by rows of low cots and moth-eaten blankets. The air was thick with the sound of labored breathing, the low moans of those in the throes of withdrawal, and the frantic, hushed whispers of the city’s healers.

Ren Ito practically materialized in the center of the room. One moment, the space near the main door was empty; the next, a blue-and-silver streak had solidified into a small, determined boy carrying a heavy wooden crate and wearing a backpack that looked twice as large as he was.

"The Express is here!" Ren shouted, his voice ringing through the warehouse with a clarity that cut through the gloom.

The mood in the center brightened almost instantly. Healers who had been hunching over their patients with looks of grim resignation stood up, their eyes widening as they saw the gold-tinted liquid shimmering in the vials.

"The Silt-Soothe," one of the junior healers whispered, reaching out with trembling hands. "By the gods, he actually did it. He brought the cure."

Ren moved through the triage center with a focused, surgical speed. He didn't just drop off the crates; he assisted the healers in distributing the vials. He saw the first doses delivered—the way the frantic pacing of the addicted slowed, the way the violent tremors of their limbs began to subside as the medicine hit their systems. The sighs of relief from the patients were palpable, a collective exhalation of a city that had been holding its breath.

"This is the first dose, Ren," Magister Valerius said, appearing from the shadows of a far corner. His silver hair looked duller in the warehouse light, and for the first time, he looked every bit his age. "It's a victory, but a tenuous one. They will have to take a dose each day for the next week before the poison is truly purged from their systems. If they miss even one, the heart-shakes will return, and they may not survive a second bout."

Ren looked around the room. There were perhaps fifty people on the cots—men and women from every district, their faces hollowed out, their pride stripped away by the Star-Silt. 

"Is this everyone?" Ren asked.

Valerius shook his head, his expression becoming somber once again. "No, Ren. Not even close. For every person here, there are five more in the shadows. Many do not dare to come. Some are too sick to move farther than their own doorways. Many are too proud to admit they were taken by the sap. And many... many are simply scared. They think the Magister’s Office is here to punish them, not to heal them."

Ren sat down on an empty crate, his feet still for the first time in hours. He thought of the Old Docks, the Dock-Creepers, and the cold, dark water. He thought of the way the city hid its wounds. He looked at his satchel, still half-full of Velda’s tinctures.

"If they won't come to the cure," Ren said, his dark eyes sharpening with a new kind of intent, "then the cure has to go to them."

"We don't have enough healers for home visits, Ren," Valerius sighed. "And the Warehouse District isn't safe at night. Even my guards are hesitant to enter the back-alleys once the sun goes down."

Ren paused for a long moment, his mind racing through the map of the city. He didn't see the danger. He saw the routes. He saw the rooftops and the window-ledges. He saw the ways in that the Guild couriers didn't know existed.

"I have an idea," Ren said, standing up. "Spread the word through the healers and the street-urchins. Tell them that the Silverhaven Express is running a night shift."

Valerius narrowed his eyes. "A night shift? Ren, you're five years old."

"I'm a Junior Courier, Level 5," Ren corrected him, the silver hum in his ankles flaring with a sudden, blue intensity. "And I'm a Truth-Runner. I can see where the wrong things are, but I can also see where the people are hurting."

He grabbed a piece of yellow cloth from a pile of bandages and a small, unlit candle. "Tell them this: if they're sick and they can't come here, they just need to put up a **Yellow Sign** and a candle in their window or on their doorstep. If I see the yellow and the light, I will deliver the dose. Every night for the next week."

"They'll be afraid to open their doors to anyone at night," Valerius noted.

"I don't need them to open the door," Ren grinned, a flash of his usual mischief returning. "I'll leave it in the gaps. I'll slide it under the sill. I'll drop it on the balcony. They don't even need to see me. I won't be there for more than a second or two before I'm gone again."

The Magister looked at the boy—at the silver-winged patches, the stained hands of an alchemist, and the unwavering gaze of a five-year-old who was currently carrying the weight of a thousand lives.

"The Yellow Sign," Valerius whispered. "A signal for those who are afraid of the light."

"It's a secret delivery," Ren said, already checking the straps of his backpack. "Like the Green Signs, but only for the help. No need to even retrieve the medicine until the sun comes up and I'm long gone."

The idea was brilliant in its simplicity. It bypassed the pride, the fear, and the logistical nightmare of the triage center. It turned the city’s darkness into a cloak for the cure.

"Very well, Ren," Valerius said, striking his staff against the cold stone floor. "I will have my runners spread the word. We will call it the 'Express of the Shaded Hearts'."

"No," Ren said, heading for the door. "It's just the Silverhaven Express. And the first run starts tonight."

As Ren burst out into the evening air, his *Pathfinder’s Sense* shifted. The golden threads of the city pathways were beginning to be punctuated by tiny, flickering points of light in his mind's eye—potential candles, potential lives. 

He didn't go home. He headed back to Velda’s. He needed more Silt-Soothe. He needed more canisters. He needed to prepare for the fastest, most secretive week of his life.

The Silverhaven Express wasn't just delivered for coppers or for truth anymore. It was delivering for the shadows that everyone else had forgotten. And as the sun dipped below the horizon, the first yellow sign appeared in a window in the Shadow Alleys, a single candle flickering beside it.
