# Chapter 12: The Milestone and the Mirror

The sixth birthday of Ren Ito was never supposed to be a spectacle. 

In the quiet, orderly house in the Scribe’s Quarter, Hiroto and Hana had planned for a small tea service, perhaps an extra strawberry tart, and a new set of durable leather soles for Ren’s lightning-quick feet. But Silverhaven, it seemed, had other plans.

It started with Hiroto’s guest list. He had felt it only polite to invite Master Thorne, the merchant who had first recognized Ren’s potential. Then came the healers from the triage center, and then Apothecary Velda (who arrived early and stayed in a corner, scowling at the lack of medicinal herbs on the appetizers). Then Magister Valerius expressed an interest in attending, which meant he had to bring his honor guard.

By the time the day arrived, the guest list had mutated into a civic event. The guests had guests, the merchants had brought their families, and the people of the Warehouse District had practically declared a public holiday. The Ito apartment was abandoned for the sprawling, columned magnificence of the Mayor’s House, which the Mayor himself had offered with a look of slightly panicked generosity.

The Grand Ballroom, usually the site of somber tax negotiations and trade summits, was now filled with the chaotic energy of children, the smell of fresh cake, and the countless heart-signs that had been turned into festive banners.

Ren was at the center of it all, dressed in his finest Junior Carrier tunic—complete with the silver wings and a new, gold-stitched border that Velda had insisted upon. He was all smiles, hopping from foot to foot with a restive energy that the ballroom floor struggled to contain. But for every beaming grin he gave to a well-wisher, there was a small, embarrassed shrug. He didn't quite know what to do with a city that wanted to celebrate him for just doing his job.

"You look like a king, Ren!" Old Man Silas shouted, leaning on a new, finely carved cane. "A king of the alleys!"

"I'm just a courier, Silas!" Ren replied, his voice bright. "I just go where the signs are!"

The Mayor, a man who usually preferred the company of ledgers to children, stepped forward as the clock neared the hour of Ren’s birth. "Citizens of Silverhaven," he began, his voice echoing off the high ceilings. "Six years ago, a light was born in the Scribe’s Quarter. Today, that light has touched every corner of our city. Ren Ito, the Silverhaven Express, has shown us that speed is nothing without a heart, and that justice is the fastest message of all."

He raised a glass of sparkling cider. "To Ren! To the Express! And to the future!"

"TO REN!" the room roared, a thousand voices joining in a crescendo of genuine affection.

And then, it happened.

A chime, crystal-clear and resonant, echoed through the ballroom. It wasn't the sound of a bell or a musical instrument. It was a sound that seemed to originate from the air itself, vibrating in the marrow of everyone present. It was the sound of the System.

The conversations died instantly. A silence, profound and expectant, fell over the Grand Ballroom. 

Ren froze. To his eyes, the air wasn't empty. A massive, translucent blue pane had manifested directly in front of him, twice the size of any he had seen before. It shimmered with a golden filigree that pulsed in time with his heartbeat.

Ren looked at the pane, his brow furrowing. He saw rows of numbers, words he didn't quite recognize, and a set of bars that glowed with a soft, inner light.

"Papa?" Ren whispered, his voice small in the sudden stillness. "What’s a... a status sheet?"

Hiroto stepped forward, his eyes widening as he realized that, for the first time, some of the System’s information was leaking into the physical world. He could see the faint outline of the header: **STATUS: REN ITO**.

"It's a mirror, Ren," Hiroto said, his voice hushed with awe. "It's the System showing you exactly who you are."

Ren reached out, his small finger touching the blue light of the pane. As he did, the text sharpened, and the information became clear to him—and to those standing closest.

***

**[LEVEL 7 JUNIOR COURIER: REN ITO]**
**[TITLE: HEART-BEAT OF SILVERHAVEN]**

**[ATTRIBUTES]**
- **Agility:** 45 (Peak Human: 10)
- **Perception:** 28 (Heightened Urban Awareness)
- **Stamina:** 22 (Endless Run)
- **Intelligence:** 15 (System Logic Proficiency)
- **Charisma:** 35 (Civic Darling)

**[ACTIVE SKILLS]**
- **Balance Mastery (Level 3):** 100% friction on any surface. Vertical and inverted movement enabled.
- **Pathfinder’s Sense (Level 4):** Real-time route optimization. Detects shortcuts and hazards.
- **Truth-Runner’s Aura (Level 2):** Detects illicit items within 50 meters. Leaves justice trails.

**[PASSIVE SKILLS]**
- **Sense Illegitimacy:** Innate moral compass and trap detection.
- **Predictive Twitch:** 0.5s reaction bonus to physical interference.
- **Urban Agility:** Increases speed by 25% when within city limits.
- **Herbalism (Novice):** 95% identification accuracy of city flora.
- **Alchemy (Novice):** 5% potency bonus to crafted medicine.

**[CURRENT STATUS: EVOLVING...]**

***

Magister Valerius leaned in, his own *Sight of the Law* vibrating in response to the sheet. "An Agility of forty-five? At six years old?" He looked at Hiroto with a mixture of terror and wonder. "The 'Peak Human' limit for a seasoned soldier is ten. Ren isn't just fast. He’s... he’s becoming a force of nature."

Velda peered over her spectacles, her eyes fixed on the Alchemy skill. "Manual stirring bonus? Well, at least the System recognizes good labor when it sees it."

Ren didn't care about the numbers. He was looking at the bottom line—the word *Evolving*.

"Papa, it says I'm changing," Ren said, looking up at Hiroto. "Am I not a boy anymore?"

Hana stepped forward, taking Ren’s hand in hers. She felt the incredible, buzzing heat of his skin, the energy that was literally radiating from his Level 7 body. "You'll always be our boy, Ren. But the System is recognizing that you've outgrown the 'Junior' part of your world."

The chime echoed one last time, a deep, gong-like sound that seemed to signal the end of one era and the beginning of another. The blue pane faded, leaving only the afterimage of Ren’s incredible stats burned into the air.

"Ren," Magister Valerius said, his voice grave. "A status sheet like that doesn't just come with power. It comes with a calling. The city loves you, but the world... the world is much larger than Silverhaven. And it is much darker."

The birthday cake was forgotten. The festive hearts seemed to pulse with a new, more serious light. Ren looked around the Grand Ballroom, at the faces of the people he had saved, and at the vast, unknown city that lay beyond the windows.

"I'm ready," Ren said, his feet hitting the polished floor with a sound like a starting pistol.

The Silverhaven Express wasn't just a child's game anymore. It was a documented, system-validated reality. And as the bells of the city struck the first hour of his sixth year, Ren Ito didn't just walk to his next slice of cake.
