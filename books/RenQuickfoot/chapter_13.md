# Chapter 13: The Iron Gauntlet

The Silverhaven Guard Training Grounds were not designed for children. They were a grim, functional landscape of gray stone, heavy iron, and the pervasive sound of shouting and clashing steel. Situated just outside the High District’s northern wall, the grounds were a testament to the city’s defensive might—a sprawling complex where the "Iron Bastions" of the Guard were forged.

Ren Ito stood at the edge of the central parade ground, his eyes wide, his neck craned back so far he was almost horizontal. Beside him, Hiroto and Hana stood with a mixture of pride and trepidation. They had been invited here by a formal messenger, a man clad in the heavy plate and crimson tabard of the Captain’s personal guard.

"It’s... it’s very big," Ren whispered, his feet already beginning a nervous, high-speed tap against the hardened dirt.

"It’s the Iron Gauntlet, Ren," a voice boomed from the center of the grounds. 

Captain Kaelen stepped forward. He was a mountain of a man, his armor scarred by a decade of service, his eyes hard but not unkind. He was Level 35, an "Iron Bastion," and his very footsteps seemed to vibrate with a weight that commanded respect.

"Magister Valerius tells me you've been doing our job for us, little Express," Kaelen said, his voice carrying across the grounds. "The Star-Silt ring was a rot we couldn't find, and you cut it out in a week. On behalf of the Guard, I wanted to thank you."

He gestured to the hundreds of guardsmen who were gathered around the perimeter. They weren't scowling like the Courier Guild veterans. They were watching with a quiet, professional interest. They had seen the Heart Signs. They knew the score.

"But more than that," Kaelen continued, a glint of challenge entering his eyes. "I heard a rumor. I heard that the Silverhaven Express thinks there isn't a wall in this city that can stop him."

Ren’s gaze drifted back to the massive construction behind the Captain. It was a three-story-tall monstrosity of timber and iron. It featured vertical climbing walls with shifting handholds, swinging pendulums of spiked wood, narrow balance beams over pits of freezing water, and a final, nearly vertical ramp that ended in a bell twenty feet above the ground.

"Can I try it?" Ren asked, the silver hum in his ankles spiking to a frantic, blue frequency.

"Ren!" Hana started, reaching for his shoulder, but Hiroto caught her hand.

"Let him," Hiroto said softly. "The System gave him a status sheet for a reason, Hana. He needs to see where he stands."

Captain Kaelen laughed, a sound like gravel in a grinder. "It’s meant for elite recruits, Ren. Men four times your size and weight. If you fall, the water is cold and the mud is deep."

"I don't plan on falling," Ren said, stepping forward. He adjusted his Junior Carrier patches, felt the familiar weight of his *Truth-Runner’s Aura*, and took a deep breath.

**[NOTIFICATION: HIGH-STAKES COMPETITION INITIATED]**
**[OBJECTIVE: COMPLETE THE IRON GAUNTLET]**
**[TIME TO BEAT: 2 MINUTES, 45 SECONDS (RECORD HELD BY CAPTAIN KAELEN)]**

Ren didn't wait for a starting pistol. He didn't even wait for Kaelen to finish his warning.

He blurred.

The first section was a series of five-foot-tall stone hurdles designed to test a recruit’s lunging power. Ren didn't lunge. He activated *Balance Mastery* and ran *over* them, his feet hitting the vertical faces of the stones and propelling him forward with the momentum of a released spring. He crossed the hurdle field in three seconds.

The guards let out a collective gasp.

Then came the swinging pendulums. Heavy logs, wrapped in iron, swung back and forth across a narrow bridge. To a man, it required timing and strength to push past. To Ren, it was just another calculation.

*Predictive Twitch* flared. *Left. Right. High. Low.*

Ren didn't just dodge the pendulums; he used them. As a log swung toward him, he vaulted onto its side, used its own momentum to carry him across the gap, and flipped onto the next platform before the log could swing back. 

He was moving so fast that his blue-and-silver light was leaving a streak across the training grounds.

"Look at his feet!" one of the recruit-sergeants shouted. "He’s not even touching the boards for more than a heartbeat!"

The third section was the Shift-Wall. A vertical wall of wood where the handholds moved on a mechanical track. It was meant to test grip and endurance. Ren hit the wall at full speed, activated *Balance Mastery*, and simply ran up it. He ignored the handholds entirely, his boots sticking to the vertical timber as if he were on a flat road. 

He reached the top of the three-story wall in six seconds, performed a perfect handspring off the top ledge, and dove toward the balance beams.

The balance beams were only four inches wide, suspended over a pit of muddy water. Most recruits crawled across them. Ren sprinted. His *Agility* of 45 was a cheat-code in this environment. To him, the narrow beam felt like a highway. He crossed the thirty-foot span in a single, sustained burst of speed, his arms outspread for balance, his eyes fixed on the final obstacle.

The Iron Ramp.

It was a twenty-foot-tall curve of polished wood, angled at nearly eighty degrees. At the very top hung a silver bell. To reach it, a recruit had to build up enough speed on the flat run to carry their momentum up the curve.

Ren skidded around the corner, his boots throwing up a cloud of dust. He saw the bell. He saw the Captain’s record timer ticking down in his peripheral vision: *1 Minute, 12 Seconds.*

He was on track to shatter the record.

"Go, Ren!" Hiroto’s voice rose above the crowd.

Ren didn't just run toward the ramp. He activated everything. *Pathfinder’s Sense* showed him the optimal line of ascent. *Balance Mastery* ensured his friction was absolute. He hit the base of the ramp with a sound like a thunderclap.

The guards stood up, their shouting silenced by the sheer impossibility of what they were seeing. 

Ren didn't struggle up the ramp. He didn't slow down as gravity tried to pull him back. He ran up the ninety-degree curve as if it were a gentle hill. He reached the top, his hand shooting out to grasp the rope of the bell, and he swung it with a violent, joyful intensity.

*GONG!*

The sound echoed across the Training Grounds, silencing the clashing steel of the distant practice bouts. 

Ren sat on the very top of the bell housing, his chest heaving, his silver-blue light slowly fading to a dull hum. He looked down at the Training Grounds, at the stunned faces of the hundreds of guardsmen, and at Captain Kaelen, who was staring at his stopwatch with an expression of pure, unadulterated shock.

**[LEVEL UP: 7 -> 8]**
**[CRITERIA MET: RECORD-BREAKING PERFORMANCE]**
**[CRITERIA MET: DEMONSTRATION OF ELITE AGILITY BEFORE PROFESSIONALS]**

**[TIME: 1 MINUTE, 22 SECONDS]**
**[NEW RECORD ESTABLISHED]**

Ren looked down at the Captain. "Was that... was that okay?"

Kaelen didn't answer for a long moment. He looked at the bell, then at the ramp, then at the small five-year-old boy who had just cut his own legendary record in half. 

The Captain stepped forward into the center of the grounds. He raised his massive, gauntleted fist toward the sky.

"EYE OF THE STORM!" Kaelen shouted, the traditional Guard salute.

"EYE OF THE STORM!" the hundreds of guardsmen roared back, their voices a thunderous acknowledgement of the new master of the Iron Gauntlet.

Ren smiled, a massive, face-stretching grin. He flipped off the bell housing, landed in a perfect tumble on the ramp, and skidded to a halt before his parents.

"I think I like the Guard," Ren said, his eyes sparkling. "They have really fun toys."

Hiroto hugged his son, his own heart still racing. "They're not toys, Ren. But you... you are certainly something else."

Kaelen walked up to them, his expression now one of profound respect. He reached into a small pouch at his belt and pulled out a silver pin—the mark of a "Friend of the Guard." He pinned it to Ren’s tunic, right beside the silver wings.

"You're not just a courier, Ren Ito," Kaelen said softly. "You're a warning. A warning that the old ways of doing things... they’re just too slow. If you ever want a job that involves more than just letters, the Guard has a place for a runner like you."

"I'll think about it," Ren said, already eyeing the obstacle course for a second run. "But for now, I have a delivery for Old Man Silas. He said he’d have a new pair of laces for me if I beat the sun to the Southern Gate."

Ren blurred before the Captain could even reply. The Silverhaven Express was back on the clock, a new record in his pocket, a new level in his soul, and the knowledge that in a city of iron and stone, there wasn't a gauntlet he couldn't run. (Word Count: 1485)
