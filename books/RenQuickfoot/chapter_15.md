# Chapter 15: The Head Start

The morning air at the Guard Training Grounds was crisp, flavored with the scent of damp earth and the lingering metallic tang of the previous day’s weapon drills. Ren Ito stood in the center of the parade ground, feeling remarkably small. Around him stood forty other recruits—men and women in their late teens and early twenties, most of them twice his height and triple his weight. They wore the standard gray training tunics, heavy leather boots, and expressions that ranged from nervous to deeply skeptical.

Ren adjusted his own small tunic. He hadn't brought his silver-winged patches or his "Friend of the Guard" pin. Today, he was just Recruit Ito.

"Listen up, you lot!" 

The voice was like a landslide—deep, abrasive, and impossible to ignore. A man who looked like he had been carved out of a single block of granite marched toward them. This was Sergeant Goram, a man whose reputation for discipline was so fierce it was said he could make a stone wall sweat. He was a Level 30 "Drill-Master," and his presence felt like a physical weight on the grounds.

Goram skidded to a halt in front of the formation, his hands clasped behind his back, his eyes scanning the row of recruits with the intensity of a hawk looking for a field mouse.

"Today is your first day of the most intense training you will ever endure!" Goram roared. "In these grounds, you are no longer sons of merchants or daughters of bakers. You are raw material. And it is my job to forge that material into something that won't break when the monsters come knocking on the Southern Gate!"

He stopped in front of Ren. The Sergeant was so tall that Ren had to look almost vertical to see the man’s chin.

"You may notice," Goram continued, his voice dropping to a deceptively quiet tone that carried to every corner of the grounds, "that one of our recruits is a bit smaller than the rest of you."

A few of the recruits couldn't help themselves; a muffled snort of laughter escaped from the back row.

Goram’s head snapped toward the sound. "Who laughed? Stand out!"

A tall, broad-shouldered youth with a cocky grin stepped forward. "Recruit Jarl, sir. It’s just... the kid’s barely five, sir. Is he lost? The nursery is in the Floral District."

"Recruit Jarl," Goram said, walking until he was inches from the youth’s face. "If you think Recruit Ito is a pushover, let me direct your eyes to the Iron Gauntlet."

He pointed to the massive obstacle course looming in the distance. "Recruit Ito holds the all-time record for that course. He ran it in one minute and twenty-two seconds. My own time—and the Captain's time—was nearly double that. If you think his size is a weakness, I suggest you rethink your entire understanding of combat, or you'll find yourself dead before the first bell strikes."

Jarl’s grin vanished. He looked at Ren, then at the Gauntlet, his face turning a shade of pale that matched the morning fog.

Ren tried to keep a low profile, shifting his weight slightly, but Goram wasn't finished.

"To start off this glorious morning," the Sergeant shouted, addressing the whole group again, "you will do ten laps around the campground! It's a two-mile circuit. Mud, stones, and stairs included."

The recruits groaned, but Goram raised a hand. "Wait. There is a catch."

He looked at Ren. "Recruit Ito, you will sit on that crate for the first five laps. You will not move until the last recruit finishes their fifth circuit. If any of you finish ten laps before he does... if any of you beat the 'Silverhaven Flash' on his first morning... you get the rest of the day off. No weapon drills. No kit cleaning. Just tavern-time and sleep."

The recruits’ eyes lit up. Ten laps was a lot, but a five-lap head start? On a five-year-old? It was the easiest victory they would ever see.

"Take your marks!" Goram barked. "Go!"

The forty recruits launched themselves forward in a chaotic scramble of leather and heavy breathing. Jarl led the pack, his long legs eating up the distance, his eyes fixed on the distant tavern.

Ren sat on the wooden crate as instructed. He didn't look bored. He looked focused. He closed his eyes, feeling the silver hum in his ankles. At Level 8, his *Agility* of 50 (having allocated his latest points) felt like a coiled spring. His *Stamina* of 25 meant that a two-mile run wasn't even a warm-up.

He watched them go. Lap one. The pack was tight.
Lap two. The lines began to fray.
Lap three. The heavy breathing was now a chorus of gasps.
Lap four. Jarl was still in the lead, but his face was the color of a ripe beet.
Lap five. 

As the last recruit, a stout girl named Mara who was gasping for air, rounded the corner of the fifth lap, Goram checked his stopwatch.

"Ito!" Goram shouted. "Run!"

Ren didn't just stand up. He exploded.

He didn't activate *Balance Mastery*—the ground was flat enough. He just let his raw *Agility* take over. To the recruits, it didn't look like he was running. It looked like a blue-and-silver blur was skipping across the surface of the earth. His feet barely seemed to touch the dirt before he was twenty feet further along.

He finished his first lap before Mara had even reached the three-quarter mark of her sixth.

By Ren’s third lap—the recruits’ eighth—he was already weaving through the back of the pack. The guards watching from the barracks began to gather, cheering not for the recruits, but for the "Express."

"Coming through! Left side!" Ren’s voice was clear, unaffected by the exertion. He zipped past a group of three recruits who were practically crawling, their eyes glazed with exhaustion.

By the time Jarl entered his tenth and final lap, Ren was finishing his seventh. The five-lap handicap was evaporating at a terrifying rate.

Jarl looked back, his lungs burning, his legs feeling like they were made of lead. He saw the silver blur. It was closer. Much closer. 

"I... won't... lose..." Jarl wheezed, pushing his body into a desperate, staggering sprint.

Ren hit his ninth lap. He saw the recruits ahead of him—a long line of staggering, sweating bodies. He didn't just pass them; he used them as markers, a series of milestones to be conquered.

He passed the middle of the pack. He passed Mara. He saw the final bend.

Jarl was fifty yards from the finish line, his feet dragging through the mud, his vision tunneling. He could see Sergeant Goram standing there, arms crossed, the stopwatch in his hand.

Then, a sound. *Thump-thump-thump.*

It was a light, rapid tapping, like a drum played by a master.

Ren bypassed Jarl on the left, his feet hitting a dry patch of stone and propelling him forward with a burst of speed that shouldn't have been possible after two miles of sprinting. He didn't look tired. He looked like he was just getting started.

Ren crossed the finish line three seconds before Jarl.

The recruits collapsed. They didn't just sit down; they fell where they stood, forty bodies sprawled across the dirt of the parade ground like a fallen army. Mara was face-down in a puddle. Jarl was on his knees, his head hanging low, his chest heaving with a violent, rhythmic desperation.

Ren skidded to a halt beside Sergeant Goram. He wasn't even breathing hard. A few beads of sweat stood on his brow, but his silver-blue light was glowing with a steady, vibrant health.

"Laps complete, Sergeant!" Ren said, giving a sharp, crisp salute he had practiced with his father.

Goram looked at the stopwatch, then at the carnage of his new recruit class. He looked at Ren, and for a fleeting second, a genuine smile—the first anyone had seen in years—touched the Drill-Master’s lips.

"Time: Eight minutes, forty-two seconds," Goram announced. "For ten laps. With a five-lap delay."

He looked at Jarl, who was currently attempting to not vomit on the Sergeant's boots. "Well, Recruit Jarl. It seems the nursery is closed today. And since no one beat Recruit Ito... it looks like everyone gets to stay for the kit-cleaning and the shield-drills."

A collective moan of despair rose from the dirt, but it was a weak, exhausted sound.

"Ren," Mara wheezed from her puddle, looking up at him with a mixture of awe and hatred. "What... are you... made of?"

"Letters and coppers, mostly!" Ren grinned, reaching down to help her up. 

But as Ren pulled her to her feet, he felt the weight of her. She was heavy. She was solid. She was something his speed couldn't easily overcome if she didn't want to move.
