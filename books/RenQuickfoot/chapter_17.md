# Chapter 17: The Anchor's Burden

The silence that followed Ren’s triple-set of PT was only broken by the heavy, ragged breathing of the forty recruits who were still attempting to peel themselves off the dirt. Ren stood at attention, his face slightly flushed, his silver-blue light humming with a quiet, vibrant intensity.

Then, the air chimed.

It was a soft, crystalline sound, one that Ren had learned to associate with the System’s subtle refinements.

Ren stared at the translucent blue pane in a daze. He was officially Level 8, but these weren't Level-up points. These were *earned* points—the result of pushing his body past its perceived limits.

The rest of the recruits, who had been watching the interaction with Sergeant Goram, paused. Even Jarl, who was currently draped over a water-trough like a wet blanket, raised his head.

"Did... did the kid just level up?" Jarl wheezed. "Again?"

Ren shook his head, looking down at his hands, which felt a fraction more solid, a fraction heavier in all the right ways. "No," he muttered softly, almost to himself. "Just a bit of Strength and Constitution. The training... it’s changing me."

Sergeant Goram didn't acknowledge the chime with a smile. He didn't even blink. He just paced the formation, his hands behind his back, looking at the exhausted recruits with a profound lack of sympathy.

"All right, you sorry waste of time and energy!" Goram roared. "Listen up! I’ve decided that you’ve had enough running. No more track time. No more PT. No more jumping jacks."

There was a ragged, pathetic cheer from the dirt. Mara let out a sigh of relief that sounded more like a whistle.

"Instead," Goram continued, his grin widening into something that can only be described as predatory, "you now just get to stand around for an hour."

"Stand around?" Jarl whispered. "That... that doesn't sound too bad. I can stand. Standing is my best thing."

"Oh, it's very easy," Goram said, a terrifyingly evil glint in his eyes. "All you have to do is hold a standard-issue Silverhaven Guard shield and a heavy iron-wood spear. One in each hand. Arms extended at ninety degrees. Without letting them touch the ground. For one hour. While soaking up the afternoon sunshine. Fun, right?"

The cheers turned to ashes.

The guardsmen from the barracks began to bring out the equipment. These weren't 'junior' shields. They were full-sized Tower Shields, made of reinforced oak and rimmed with heavy iron. They weighed nearly forty pounds on their own. The spears were ten-foot shafts of seasoned ash, tipped with a pound of hammered steel.

Ren stood in his position as the equipment was handed out. When the two guardsmen approached him, they hesitated.

"Sergeant?" one of the guards asked. "The boy is... well, the shield is taller than he is. Maybe the practice buckler?"

"Standard issue!" Goram barked. "He wanted to learn how to stand. A storm doesn't care how tall you are when it hits the wall. Give him the iron."

Ren reached out as the shield was lowered toward him. As the handle touched his arm, his knees buckled. 

The shield was massive. The bottom rim hit the dirt, but Ren grabbed the inner grips, his small muscles straining instantly. Then came the spear. It felt like holding a fallen tree.

"Begin!" Goram shouted, checking his stopwatch.

The first five minutes were a chorus of groans and the sound of shields clattering to the floor. Jarl and the larger recruits fared the best initially, their massive shoulders and thick arms absorbing the weight with ease. They looked at Ren, who was shaking visibly, his small arms vibrating like harp strings.

Ren was struggling mightily. At his size, the center of gravity for the shield was all wrong. It wanted to pull him forward, to pin him to the earth. The spear was even worse, a long lever that multiplied every ounce of its weight against his shoulder. His *Strength* of 6 and *Constitution* of 6 (after the morning’s boost) was being tested by the equivalent of a giant trying to hold a house in each hand.

"Focus, Recruit Ito!" Goram’s voice was close, sounding right in Ren’s ear. "You're fast, but a wall doesn't move. Be the wall!"

Ren’s vision began to tunnel. The sweat he had promised to break was now pouring down his face, a salt-sting in his eyes. His biceps felt like they were being carved with hot knives.

But as the ten-minute mark passed, Ren’s *System Logic* and *Balance Mastery* began to interact.

He realized that he was trying to hold the weight with his *arms*. To a courier, arms were for balance, for carrying letters. His power had always come from his *legs*.

Ren shifted his stance. He didn't lock his knees; he bent them slightly, sinking his weight into a low, athletic crouch. He realized that if he tilted the shield just a fraction of a degree toward his body, he could rest the top edge against his shoulder, turning his entire skeleton into a support beam.

He began to 'cheat'—not by breaking the rules, but by optimizing them.

He redistributed the weight across his entire form. He moved his arms and legs into precise, diagonal positions, creating a sort of internal momentum where the weight wasn't settled on any one muscle group. He used the silver hum of his *Balance Mastery* to 'stick' his feet to the earth, creating an anchor point that defied the spear’s leverage.

It was still very, very difficult. His small frame was still being crushed by gravity. But the shaking stopped. He became a tripod of iron and blue light.

The larger recruits were starting to fade. Jarl’s arms were trembling now, his massive muscles beginning to cramp under the static load. Mara had already dropped her spear twice, her face a mask of frustration.

"Look at him," Mara whispered, staring at Ren. "He’s... he’s angling himself. He’s turning his body into a lever."

Ren didn't hear her. He was too busy feeling 'the burn.' It wasn't just pain anymore; it was a rhythmic pulse of growth. He could feel the System working, recording the strain, validating the effort. To him, this wasn't just a drill; it was a delivery—a delivery of strength.

The hour felt like a lifetime. The sun climbed, the sweat soaked Ren’s tunic until it was a second skin, and the iron of the shield became a part of his very bone structure.

Finally, the bell rang.

"Rest!" Goram commanded.

The sound of forty shields and forty spears hitting the stone was like a massive, metallic collapse. Jarl fell forward, his arms useless, his face hitting the dirt. Mara sat down and simply stayed there, staring at nothing.

Ren lowered the shield with a slow, agonizing control. He didn't drop it. He set it down. Then he leaned the spear against the rim. He stood up, his arms feeling three times longer than they should be, his shoulders burning with a delicious, heavy heat.

The blue pane manifested again, right in front of his sweating, determined face.

"Reward enough," Ren whispered, a massive, exhausted grin spreading across his face. He looked at his hands. They felt different. They felt like they belonged to a boy who could do more than just run. They belonged to a boy who could *hold*.

Goram walked over to him, standing so close that Ren was in the man’s shadow. The Sergeant looked down at the puddle of sweat Ren had left on the stones.

"I think I see sweat, Recruit Ito," Goram said, his voice surprisingly gentle. "Lots of it."

He reached down and tapped the top of the tower shield. "Good to know you're human, Ren. A fast human, but a human nonetheless."

He looked at the rest of the recruits, who were watching Ren with a new, quiet kind of reverence. They didn't feel superior because of their size anymore. They felt like they were in the presence of something that couldn't be measured with a ruler.

"Tomorrow," Goram announced, "we learn how to move *with* the shields. But for now... Recruit Ito, since you're the only one who didn't drop his kit... you get to lead the way to the mess hall."
