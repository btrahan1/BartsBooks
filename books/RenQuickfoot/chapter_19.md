# Chapter 19: The Art of the Paring Knife

The exodus from the mess hall was a slow, groaning affair. Forty recruits, their bellies full of Chef Bram’s heavy beef-and-barley stew, dragged their feet toward the barracks. The sounds of clattering wooden bowls and rowdy conversation faded into the distance, replaced by the soft, rhythmic sounds of a kitchen settling into its evening routine.

Ren Ito did not join the exodus. 

While Jarl and Mara were already dreaming of their thin straw mattresses, Ren had stepped to the side, pressing himself against the cool stone wall of the kitchen entrance. His feet were finally quiet, his silver-blue light dimmed to a faint, resting hum, but his eyes were wide and fixed on the prep station.

*Chop. Chop. Scrape.*

Chef Bram was no longer dicing carrots for a hundred. He was seated on a low wooden stool before a massive vat of mud-caked potatoes. In his hand was a paring knife—a small, unassuming piece of steel with a wooden handle, worn smooth by years of use. Compared to the massive kitchen-cleaver he had used earlier, it looked like a toy. 

But as Ren watched, he realized the paring knife was the more dangerous of the two.

Bram’s thumb was positioned with terrifying precision. He would rotate a potato with his left hand while the blade, driven by a subtle flick of his right wrist, stripped away a single, continuous spiral of brown skin. The potato emerged perfectly white, with not a sliver of edible flesh wasted.

Ren drifted closer, his *Pathfinder’s Sense* and *System Logic* analyzing the movement. It wasn't about speed here. it was about the path. The knife was tracing the exact topography of the tuber, adjusting for every bump and eye with a micro-correction that shouldn't have been possible for hands that large.

"Can I help?"

The rhythm stopped. Bram didn't look up, but his large knife remained held at an angle that looked remarkably like a defensive posture. He let out a long, slow sigh that made his heavy apron ripple.

"You again?" Bram growled, dropping the peeled potato into a bucket of water with a wet *plonk*. "Be gone, little Flash. I’ve seen enough of your energy today. I have a hundredweight of potatoes to peel and a kitchen to clean. This isn't a show; it's a chore."

Ren’s eyes never left the paring knife. He could see the way the light caught the edge—a thin, silver line that spoke of absolute sharpness. "It's not a chore, sir. It's... it's the same as the Gauntlet. You're finding the fastest route around the potato."

Bram smirked, finally looking up. He saw the five-year-old boy staring at his knife with a level of intensity usually reserved for master swordsmen. "You want to peel potatoes? You actually *want* to peel them? To most recruits, that's a punishment. I give it to the ones who fall asleep during pike drills."

"I don't think it's a punishment to learn how to be precise," Ren said, his voice quiet. "I can run fast, but sometimes I run too far. I want to learn how to keep the knife on the skin."

Bram leaned back, the stool creaking under his massive frame. He looked at the vast, messy landscape of the empty mess hall—hundreds of dirty bowls, spilled broth on the tables, and the floor coated in a layer of muddy footprints from the afternoon rain.

"Tell you what, little Flash," Bram said, a wicked glint entering his eyes. "Bus the tables. Clear every bowl, wipe every inch of wood, and sweep the floor until I can see my beard in the reflection. If you can do that before I finish this first bucket... I might consider letting you touch a knife."

He gestured to the ballroom-sized hall. "The recruits usually take an hour for the whole crew to clean it. You're just one boy."

Ren didn't even wait for the Chef to finish his sentence.

He was gone.

To Bram, it looked as if a blue-and-silver whirlwind had suddenly ignited in the center of the mess hall. Ren wasn't just running; he was *choreographing*. He activated *Pathfinder’s Sense*, and the room became a web of glowing blue lines, connecting every dirty bowl to the central wash-tub.

*Zip-clack-clack.*

Ren moved between the tables with a speed that defied the laws of friction. He stacked the wooden bowls ten high, using his *Balance Mastery* to keep them perfectly centered even as he banked around the corners. He didn't carry them one by one; he used the trays as if they were skateboards, sliding them across the wet floors and catching them at the edge of the wash-station.

*Swoosh-swish.*

A bucket of soapy water and a rag became a blur of motion. Ren didn't scrub; he *flowed*. One pass across the table, his *Agility* of 50 ensuring that every inch was covered in a single, sustained movement. 

Bram stopped peeling. He sat with a half-peeled potato in his hand, his mouth slightly open, as he watched the "Express" work. It was a masterclass in urban logistics applied to a dining room. Ren used the benches as springboards, vaulting over the tables to reach the far corners, his silver light leaving a trail of "Justice Light" that illuminated the dust as it was swept away.

The rhythm of the broom was even faster than the knife. *Thump-shhh. Thump-shhh.*

In fifteen minutes—exactly a quarter of the time it took forty grown men—the entire mess hall had been cleared. The plates were stacked, the tables gleamed with a fresh-scrubbed shine, and the floor was so clean it looked like it had been polished with silk.

Ren skidded to a halt in front of Bram’s station, his chest barely heaving, a strand of hair stuck to his damp forehead. 

"The kitchen is clear, Chef," Ren said, his voice bright. "And you still have ten potatoes left in the first bucket. You mentioned something about teaching me how to peel?"

Bram looked at the hall, then at the bucket, then at the small, glowing boy. He let out a low, rumbling laugh that shook the very rafters of the kitchen.

"By the gods," Bram whispered. "Captain Kaelen wasn't exaggerating. You're not just fast; you're a disaster for anyone who likes a slow pace."

He picked up a second paring knife from a magnetic strip on the wall and held it out to Ren, handle-first. "Sit. But listen well, Recruit Ito. Speed is your friend out there in the streets. In here, at this board, speed is the thing that will cost you a finger."

Ren took the knife. It was heavy, perfectly balanced, and felt like a natural extension of his arm. 

"The secret isn't the knife," Bram said, his voice becoming the low, steady drone of a master teacher. "The secret is the thumb. You don't push the blade. You pull the potato. Your left hand is the engine; your right hand is the rudder. If you try to force it, the blade will bite too deep. If you let it float... it will found the truth."

Ren touched the blade to the first potato. He felt the resistance of the skin. He tried to move fast, to zip around the tuber like he was rounding a canal corner.

*Snip.*

The blade bit deep, taking a chunk of the white flesh with it.

"Too fast," Bram said, not even looking. "You're trying to win a race. The potato isn't a race. It's a conversation."

Ren took a breath. He slowed down. He closed his eyes for a second, feeling the *Effortless Flow* skill he had gained earlier. He stopped thinking about the destination—the peeled bucket—and started thinking about the *contact*. 

He pulled the potato against the blade. He felt the grain. He felt the eyes. He adjusted his thumb, mimicking Bram’s grip exactly.

A thin, translucent spiral of skin began to curl away from the knife. It wasn't as fast as the Chef’s, but it was smooth. It was precise.

The System chimed, a soft, encouraging tone.

**[NOTIFICATION: PRECISION MANIPULATION INITIATED]**
**[CRITERIA MET: ADAPTATION OF HIGH-SPEED KINETICS TO FINE MOTOR CONTROL]**
**[CRITERIA MET: SUCCESSFUL INTEGRATION OF CHEF'S LOGIC]**

**[SKILL GRANTED: THE STEADY EDGE (PASSIVE)]**
*Effect: Increases precision and accuracy with small blades and tools by 10%. Reduces the risk of accidental injury when performing delicate tasks.*

Ren didn't stop. He peeled the second potato. Then the third. He found a new kind of rhythm, one that wasn't based on how fast his feet moved, but on how still his mind could be. 

Bram watched him, a look of profound respect crossing his face. "You're a strange one, Ren. Most kids your age want to swing a sword and play at being heroes. You... you want to know how the stew is made."

"The stew is what keeps the heroes standing," Ren said, dropping a perfectly peeled potato into the water. "If the engine doesn't have fuel, it doesn't matter how fast the wheels are."

They sat in the quiet kitchen for the next hour, the only sound the soft, rhythmic *shrrrip* of the knives. By the time the bells of Silverhaven struck the final hour of the night, the vat was empty, the kitchen was spotless, and Ren Ito had learned that the smallest blade in the Guard was sometimes the one that required the most strength to master.

"Go home, Ren," Bram said, patting him on the shoulder as he walked him to the gate. "Come back tomorrow. I have a shipment of leeks coming in, and they require a very... refined touch."

"I'll be here, Chef," Ren said, his feet already beginning to vibrate again as he hit the open road. 

He vanished into the night, a blue-and-silver streak heading for the Scribe’s Quarter. He was a courier. He was an anchor. And now, he was a boy who knew the secret of the thumb. 

The Silverhaven Express was becoming more than just a name; it was becoming a complete system. And as Ren ran past the darkened shops and the sleeping canals, he realized that every potato he peeled was just another way of finding his way home. (Word Count: 1472)
