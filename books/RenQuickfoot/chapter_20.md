# Chapter 20: Early Bird's Grist

The bells of Silverhaven Cathedral had barely struck the third hour of the morning when Ren Ito's feet hit the cobblestones of the Northern Wall. The city was still swaddled in a thick, brackish fog that tasted of canal salt and woodsmoke. For most, this was the hour of deepest sleep, the time when dreams were at their heaviest. 

For the Silverhaven Express, it was the perfect time for a delivery.

Ren didn't have a letter or a vial of *Silt-Soothe* in his bag today. He had something far more valuable: a restless energy that refused to wait until the evening. He moved through the fog with *Pathfinder’s Sense* guiding him past the sleeping guards and the heavy portcullis. He wasn't sneaking—his silver-winged patches were tucked into his belt, but his presence was as quiet as the dawn itself.

He reached the Guard Training Grounds and headed straight for the kitchen.

Inside, the heat was already rising. The massive iron stoves were roaring, stoked by a pair of heavy-eyed kitchen assistants who were moving with the sluggishness of men who would rather be dead. But at the center of the kitchen, bathed in the orange glow of the embers, stood Chef Bram.

The Chef was currently staring at a mountain of crates filled with eggs, tubers, and massive sides of salted bacon. He looked like a man preparing for a siege.

"Can I help?"

Bram stopped. He didn't drop the heavy iron ladle he was using to stir a vat of porridge, but he did close his eyes for a long, painful second. He turned slowly, the orange light making his braided beard look like flickering flame.

"I meant tomorrow *evening*, Little Flash!" Bram roared, his voice echoing off the vaulted stone ceiling. "The sun isn't even up! The recruits are still snoring in their grease! Why in the name of the High Magister are you here?"

Ren just smiled, his silver-blue light glowing with a soft, pre-dawn luminescence. "I couldn't sleep. And you said you had leeks coming in. Also... what's for breakfast?"

Bram stared at him. He looked at the five-year-old boy who seemed to have more energy than the entire Guard combined. He looked at the mountain of work ahead of him—the breakfast that had to be ready for five hundred hungry men in exactly two hours.

"You're a menace, Ren Ito," Bram grumbled, but he set the ladle aside. He pointed to a crate of eggs—hundreds of them, their shells pale and delicate in the firelight. "We need four hundred eggs cracked and whisked. We need them fluffier than a High District pillow. And we need the bacon sliced thin enough to see through, but thick enough to satisfy a Sergeant's temper."

He smirked, crossing his massive arms. "I thought you'd be resting those 'anchor' legs of yours. Goram told me he was going to make you stand for two hours today."

"I can stand while I whisk," Ren said, already reaching for a bowl.

"Sit," Bram commanded, pointing to the same stool from the night before. "If you're going to be in my kitchen at this hour, you do it my way. Efficiency isn't just about moving fast, Ren. It’s about *pacing*. If you burn all your fuel on the eggs, you'll have nothing left for the leeks."

Ren sat. He picked up the first egg. He remembered the 'secret of the thumb' from the night before—the way the hands have to communicate with the material. 

*Crack. Plop. Crack. Plop.*

Ren began to move. His *The Steady Edge* skill flared, giving his fingers an uncanny precision. He didn't just crack the eggs; he deconstructed them. One hand held the shell, the other applied the exact amount of force to the rim of the bowl. Not a single fragment of shell fell into the yellow pool.

Bram watched him for a moment, then turned to the bacon. He picked up a long, thin slicing knife. "Watch the wrist, Ren. Whisking isn't a struggle. It's a vibration. If you use your whole arm, you'll tire in ten minutes. Use the flicker. Turn your wrist into a bird's wing."

Ren adjusted his grip on the whisk. He closed his eyes, visualizing the blue lines of his *Pathfinder's Sense* not on the floor, but inside his own forearm. He saw the path of the energy. He slowed down, then accelerated into a tight, focused blur.

*Whsh-whsh-whsh-whsh.*

The eggs transformed. They went from a flat pool of yellow to a frothy, aerated cloud of gold. 

The System chimed, its sound harmonizing with the crackling of the stove.

**[NOTIFICATION: DISCIPLINED PREPARATION INITIATED]**
**[CRITERIA MET: EARLY ADAPTATION TO LABORIOUS ROUTINE]**
**[CRITERIA MET: SUCCESSFUL APPLICATION OF KINETIC VIBRATION]**

**[SKILL GRANTED: MORNING’S GRACE (PASSIVE)]**
*Effect: Increases Stamina regeneration by 10% during the first four hours after sunrise. Reduces the "Groggy" or "Fatigued" status effects upon waking.*

Ren felt a wave of clarity wash over him. The early morning fog in his own mind cleared, replaced by a sharp, crisp focus. The sunrise hadn't even truly begun, but to Ren, it felt like high noon.

"He's doing it again," Bram whispered to himself, watching Ren whisk the second hundred eggs with even more speed than the first. "He's finding the rhythm of a kitchen before he's even old enough to reach the top shelf."

For the next two hours, the kitchen was a hive of silent, lethal productivity. Bram handled the heavy searing and the meat, while Ren became a blur of prep work. He sliced tomatoes with a precision that bordered on the magical, his small blade leaving uniform circles that looked like red coins. He whisked until the bowls were overflowing with golden foam. He even helped the kitchen assistants, who were now wide awake and staring at him in terrified amazement, by clearing the dirty prep-stations in a series of fifteen-second whirlwinds.

By the time the first breakfast bell rang, the mess hall was prepared. The tables were set, the smells were divine, and Chef Bram was leaning against the counter, wiped his brow with a towel that was already soaked.

"You're a freak of nature, Express," Bram said, handing Ren a small plate of the first eggs—perfectly seasoned and light as air. "But you're a useful one. If the Guard knew how much work you did this morning, they'd fire half my staff."

"I just wanted to help," Ren said, digging into the eggs. The *Gourmet's Eye* bonus made them taste like the best thing he had ever eaten. "And I wanted to see the sun come up over the stoves."

Just then, the doors to the mess hall swung open. Jarl and Mara led the first wave of recruits, their eyes bleary, their shoulders hunched with the anticipation of another day of Goram's torture. They looked like ghosts in the morning light.

They stopped dead when they saw Ren.

Ren was sitting on the prep-stool, wearing a small white apron Bram had found for him, a clean plate in his lap and a bright, well-rested smile on his face.

"Recruit Ito?" Mara wheezed, rubbing her eyes. "Are you... are you serious? Did you sleep here?"

"I've been here since the third bell!" Ren chirped, pointing to the massive bowls of eggs. "Breakfast is ready. Chef Bram says the bacon is particularly good today."

Jarl stared at the clean, orderly kitchen, then at his own trembling hands. He looked at Ren, who looked like he had already lived an entire day while Jarl was still trying to remember his own name.

"I... I can't," Jarl muttered, turning around and walking back toward the barracks. "I'm dreaming. I have to be dreaming. Kid’s a literal monster."

"Eat your breakfast, Jarl!" Goram’s voice boomed from the entrance, more awake than any human had a right to be. The Sergeant walked in, smelled the air, and looked at Ren. He saw the apron. He saw the clean tables. He looked at Bram.

Bram just nodded once toward Ren.

Goram’s expression didn't change, but he walked over to Ren and patted him on the head—a gesture of such uncharacteristic affection that the kitchen assistants dropped their ladles.

"Seems the Express makes more than just deliveries," Goram said. "He makes a damn good breakfast. Eat up, you lot. You're going to need the energy. Today... today we learn how to run in formation. And since Ito is already warmed up... he’s going to be the pace-setter."

Ren’s smile didn't waver. He finished his eggs, stood up, and untied his apron. He felt the *Morning's Grace* pulsing through him, his stamina higher than it had ever been. He looked at Mara, who was currently face-down in a bowl of porridge, and felt a surge of genuine warmth.

"Ready when you are, Sergeant!" 

The Silverhaven Express wasn't just a courier anymore. He was the city's heart-beat, the guard's friend, and the kitchen's secret ingredient. And as the sun finally broke through the fog, painting the training grounds in gold, Ren Ito stepped out onto the track, ready to lead the way into the dawn. (Word Count: 1462)
