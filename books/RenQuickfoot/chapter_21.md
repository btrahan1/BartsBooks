# Chapter 21: The Pulse of the Line

The morning sun had finally burned through the canal fog, revealing a training ground that was still damp and unforgiving. Sergeant Goram stood at the head of the forty recruits, his shadow stretching long and sharp across the dirt. In his hand was a heavy iron-wood baton, which he used to tap rhythmically against his thigh.

*Tap. Tap. Tap.*

"A formation isn't just people walking in line!" Goram roared, his voice echoing off the barracks walls. "It is the heart of any action! Out there, in the winding alleys or the open fields, you aren't forty recruits. You are one unit! One shield! One blade! If one of you falters, you all falter! If one of you is too fast, you're a target! If one of you is too slow, you’re a hole in the wall!"

He turned his predator’s gaze toward Ren, who was standing at the very front of the formation. Ren was still wearing his Junior Carrier tunic, but it was now stained with the sweat of the morning kitchen shift and the dust of the grounds. His silver-blue light was pulsing at a low, expectant frequency.

"Recruit Ito!" Goram barked. "You are the pace-setter. Your job isn't to run. Your job is to *lead*. You will set the pulse of this unit. If you outrun them, I will make you do the laps again with a second shield. If they fall behind, I will make them do it with *you* on their shoulders. Begin!"

Ren took a breath. This was a different kind of challenge. Usually, his life was defined by the singular pursuit of speed—finding the shortest path, the fastest blur. But now, he had forty people behind him. Jarl, whose legs were still trembling; Mara, who was gritting her teeth against the soreness; and thirty-eight others who were looking at his back as their only guide.

"Forward... MARCH!" Ren shouted, his voice high but steady.

They began to move. *Left-right-left-right.*

Ren kept his steps short and measured. His *Agility* of 50 was screaming at him to accelerate, to turn the march into a sprint, but he suppressed the urge. He focused on the sound of the boots behind him.

*Crunch-crunch-crunch-crunch.*

At first, it was a mess. Jarl’s long stride was too much for the recruit beside him. Mara was trying to compensate for a slight hitch in her hip from the previous day’s lunges. The line was waving like a ribbon in the wind.

"Tighten it up!" Goram’s voice lashed from the sidelines. "Jarl, you're a gap! Mara, you're a tail! Ito, you're disconnected! Find the pulse!"

Ren closed his eyes for a fraction of a second, activating *Pathfinder’s Sense*. Usually, the blue lines showed him the fastest route. But now, he willed the System to show him something else. He wanted to see the *rhythm*.

The blue lines shifted. They didn't point forward anymore; they radiated outward from his feet, connecting to every recruit in the formation. He saw the distance between each set of boots, the angle of each shoulder. He saw the 'friction' where the lines were breaking.

He slowed his pace by a fraction of a second. He felt the unit compress. He adjusted his stride, making it slightly more rhythmic, more percussive.

*Thump. Thump. Thump.*

His feet hit the earth with a sound that coordinated with the heartbeat of the unit. He began to use his *Truth-Runner’s Aura*, not to detect illegitimacy, but to leave a faint, silver-blue trail in the air—a visual marker for the others to follow.

"Watch the light!" Mara shouted from the second row. "Follow the Express!"

The recruits focused. They stopped looking at the ground and started looking at the silver-blue trail Ren was weaving. They saw the 'pulse' of his movement.

The crunch of the boots began to synchronize.

*THUMP-THUMP-THUMP-THUMP.*

Forty pairs of boots hitting the ground at the exact same instant. It was a powerful, intimidating sound—the sound of a single, forty-legged beast moving across the grounds.

Goram stopped tapping his baton. He watched the formation as it rounded the first corner. They didn't fray. They didn't lose the line. They moved as a solid block of gray and iron, Ren at the tip like the point of a spear.

**[NOTIFICATION: UNIT COHESION RECOGNIZED]**
**[CRITERIA MET: SYNCHRONIZATION OF VARIED KINETIC OUTPUTS]**
**[CRITERIA MET: LEADERSHIP THROUGH RHYTHMIC STABILIZATION]**

**[SKILL GRANTED: UNIT HARMONIC (PASSIVE)]**
*Effect: Increases the coordination and stamina of those following the user by 5%. The user can 'sense' the fatigue and positioning of allies within a formation.*

Ren felt the connection deepen. He could 'feel' Jarl’s breath evening out. He could 'feel' Mara’s determined stride finding its strength. He wasn't just lead; he was the conductor of a battlefield orchestra.

"Shields UP!" Ren commanded as they entered the second lap.

Forty Tower Shields rose in a single, metallic wave. The weight that had crushed them the day before felt lighter today, because they weren't holding it alone. They were holding it with the pulse.

They marched for the full hour. When Goram finally called the halt, the recruits didn't collapse into the mud. They stood at attention, their chests heaving in unison, their eyes bright with the realization that they had done something impossible. They had become a unit.

Jarl looked down at his hands, then at Ren’s back. "We didn't falter," he whispered, his voice full of a new kind of pride.

"We were one shield," Mara added, her voice thick with emotion.

Goram walked to the front of the line. He looked at Ren, then he looked at the forty recruits who were standing straighter than they ever had in their lives.

"A formation is a heart," Goram said, his voice unusually quiet. "Today, for the first time, this heart beat. And it beat because its pace-setter knew when to slow down."

He tapped Ren on the shoulder with his baton. "Good work, Recruit Ito. You showed them that speed isn't about being first. It's about ensuring no one is last."

Ren gave a sharp salute, his silver-blue light glowing with a calm, steady warmth. He looked at the forty recruits behind him and realized that his 'call to stand' was being answered. He wasn't just an anchor for himself; he was the rhythm for the whole city’s defense.

"Dismissed for fifteen minutes!" Goram barked. "Then, we move on to pike-work! And since the Express has such a good eye for rhythm... he gets to demonstrate the first thrust!"
