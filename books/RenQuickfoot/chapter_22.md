# Chapter 22: The Point of the Spear

The formation drills had left the recruits with a new sense of cohesion, but also with a profound ache in their legs that no amount of Chef Bram’s stew could quite erase. They were back on the parade ground, the midday sun now high and hot, beating down on their gray tunics. Before each recruit stood a ten-foot pike, its ash shaft polished to a dull sheen and its steel tip wrapped in protective leather for the drill.

Sergeant Goram walked the line, his hands clasped behind his back, his eyes scanning the upright weapons with the critical gaze of an architect inspecting a foundation.

"Millennia ago, if the stories are to be believed," Goram began, his voice dropping into a low, resonant rumble that demanded attention, "a man picked up a rock and turned it into a weapon! He used it to smash, to crush, to break the things that wanted to eat him."

He stopped in front of Jarl, who was standing at a rigid, trembling attention. "Then at some point, someone picked up a stick and started smacking things on the head with it!"

A few of the recruits couldn't help themselves; a muffled ripple of laughter went through the line. Even Mara’s lips twitched.

"Laughter, eh?" Goram barked, though there was no anger in it. "Go ahead. It sounds ridiculous. But that stick was the first extension of the human arm. It was the first time we decided that getting close enough to punch a wolf was a bad idea."

He continued down the line toward Ren. "Then some enterprising person found a sharp stone—maybe a piece of flint, maybe a splinter of obsidian—and they put a point on the end of that stick! And that, recruits, was called a spear!"

The laughter vanished. The recruits looked at the pikes in their hands with a new, somber kind of thought. They weren't just holding pieces of wood; they were holding the cumulative history of human survival.

"From that point on," Goram said, pausing for effect, "no pun intended... man learned to conquer beasts!"

"Huraah!" a few of the recruits shouted, caught up in the Sergeant’s rare moment of storytelling.

"As I said," Goram thundered, his voice rising to drown them out, "they *began* to conquer beasts! Being armed is not the same as being a warrior. A spear in the hands of a fool is just a long way to trip yourself. But a spear in the hands of a unit? That is a wall that breathes. That is the death of kings and the end of monsters."

He stepped into the center of the grounds and picked up a pike of his own. He held it vertically, then with a movement so fast it was almost a blur, he snapped it down into a horizontal position. The sound of the wood catching in his hand was like a gunshot.

"Today, you begin to learn how they did it!" Goram announced. "The spear is about three things: distance, timing, and the point. You do not swing a spear. You do not club with a spear. You *are* the spear."

He looked at Ren. "Recruit Ito! Step forward."

Ren stepped out of the line, his feet light on the dirt. He had already spent an hour holding this weapon during the 'anchor' drill, but holding it for endurance was vastly different from holding it for action.

"You have the fastest hands I've ever seen on a courier," Goram said, tossing Ren a practice pike. "But can those hands find the truth of the point?"

Ren caught the weapon. It felt familiar, but different. His *The Steady Edge* skill flared, his fingers finding the exact balance point of the ash shaft. His *Pathfinder’s Sense* didn't show him a route across the grounds; it showed him a straight, unwavering line through the air, terminating exactly in the center of a straw-filled target twenty feet away.

"The first thrust," Goram commanded. "It starts in the hips, flows through the shoulder, and terminates in the snap of the wrist. It is a single, unbroken pulse."

Goram demonstrated. *Step-thrust-retract.*

It was a mechanical, lethal precision. The pike didn't wobble. It didn't arc. It moved on a rail of invisible iron, puncturing the target with a sound like a drumbeat.

"Your turn, Ito. Set the rhythm."

Ren took his stance. He didn't think about smacking or smashing. He thought about the potato. He thought about the continuous spiral of skin and the way the knife found the path. He realized that the pike was just a very long paring knife, and the air was the tuber.

He felt the *Effortless Flow* in his joints. He felt the *Unit Harmonic* pulse in his mind, connecting his movement to the ground beneath him.

*Step.*
*Thrust.*

Ren didn't just push the wood. He *sent* it. His silver-blue light spiked into a sharp, narrow needle of energy that extended from his hands, down the shaft, and into the very tip of the blade. 

The pike moved with a speed that made the guardsmen on the walls lean over to see. It didn't just hit the target; it vanished into the straw, the leather-wrapped tip punching through the other side with a muffled explosion of dust.

*Retract.*

Ren was back in his starting position before the target had even finished vibrating.

**[SKILL GRANTED: STRIKING FLASH (ACTIVE)]**
*Effect: Increases the speed of the first thrust or strike in an engagement by 20%. Ignores 5% of the target's physical armor due to precision alignment.*

The recruits stared. Jarl looked at his own pike, then at the hole Ren had just put through a target meant for heavy-infantry practice.

"He... he didn't even use his muscles," Mara whispered. "He just... happened."

Goram walked over to the target, inspected the exit wound in the straw, and then looked back at Ren. The Sergeant’s face was still a mask of granite, but his eyes were burning with a terrifying approval.

"A point on the end of a stick," Goram said, repeating his earlier words. "In the hands of Recruit Ito, it seems that point is also a bolt of lightning."

He turned back to the rest of the recruits. "Pick up your weapons! If a six-year-old can find the heart of the straw, you grown men can at least find the target! One thousand thrusts! Begin!"

The sounds of wood hitting wood and the grunts of exertion filled the grounds. Ren returned to the line, but he didn't stop focusing. For every thrust he made, he felt the *Striking Flash* refining the movement, turning his speed into a tool for more than just deliveries.

He realized that the spear wasn't just a weapon for defense. It was a bridge. A bridge between his speed and the things that needed to stay in one place. And as the afternoon wore on, and the calluses on his small hands began to harden, Ren Ito knew that the 'Silverhaven Express' was starting to carry a message that no one would want to intercept.

Man had learned to conquer beasts with a sharp stone on a stick. Ren was learning to conquer his own limitations with a silver light on a heart of ash. (Word Count: 1422)
