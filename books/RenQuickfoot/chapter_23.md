# Chapter 23: The Dual Edge

The Training Grounds were beginning to slip into their evening lull. The sun, now a deep orange orb hanging over the western canal walls, cast long, distorted shadows of the training dummies and the heavy iron racks. Most of the recruits had already retreated to the barracks to nurse their various bruises, but Ren Ito was still moving.

He had finished his thousand thrusts with the pike, his small shoulders burning with a clean, honest heat. He had an hour before he was due in Chef Bram’s kitchen for the evening prep, and the idle energy in his feet wouldn't let him sit still. 

He found himself wandering toward the back of the parade grounds, where the secondary weapon racks stood. These weren't filled with the standard-issue pikes or the heavy kite-shields of the line. These were specialized tools, many of them dusty and forgotten.

Ren’s eyes alighted on a pair of wooden practice blades at the bottom of a rack. They were long—nearly eighteen inches—but too short and heavy to be called a sword. They had a slight curve to the edge and a thick, sturdy spine. They looked like oversized paring knives that had been forged for a giant.

"What are these called, Sergeant?"

Goram, who was in the middle of oiling a training harness near the gate, looked up. He wiped his hands on a greasy rag and walked over, his heavy boots crunching on the gravel. He looked at the blades, then at Ren, a strange, thoughtful expression crossing his granite face.

"Those?" Goram said, his voice a low rumble. "Those are *Seaxes*. Or at least, the practice version of them. Some call them short-blades, others call them 'gut-biters'. They’re a relic of the old scout-corps, before the Guard became all about the pike and the wall."

He picked one up. It looked tiny in his massive hand, but the weight of it was clear. "Long for a knife, short for a sword. They’re meant for work where a ten-foot pole is nothing but a hindrance. Close-quarters. Alleys. The kind of places where you don't have room to breathe, let alone swing."

He looked at Ren’s hands—the hands that had spent the morning whisking eggs and the night before peeling potatoes with the precision of a master. 

"They require a different kind of logic than the spear, Ren," Goram said, tossing the wooden blade to the boy.

Ren caught it effortlessly. His *The Steady Edge* skill flared instantly. The balance was perfect—the weight was centered just above the hilt, allowing for a rapid, flicking motion that felt like an extension of his own pulse. He picked up the second one with his left hand.

"They're dual-blades?" Ren asked, his silver-blue light beginning to shimmer at a new, more intricate frequency.

"In the right hands, they’re a whirlwind," Goram said, crossing his arms. "The style was called the 'Viper's Kiss'. One blade to find the opening, the second to deliver the message. It's about speed and precision, using your momentum rather than your mass."

Ren took a stance. He didn't use the 'anchor' crouch he had learned with the shield. He went into a low, athletic spring-load, the wooden blades held in a reverse grip—the same way Bram held the paring knife when he was stripping the skin from a difficult tuber.

He moved. 

He didn't run across the grounds. He *danced*. He used *Balance Mastery* to pivot on a single heel, his body spinning like a top. The wooden blades blurred, tracing complex, overlapping ovals in the air. 

*Swish-clack. Swish-clack.*

To Ren, it wasn't a combat drill. It was a route. He was mapping the air around him, his *Pathfinder’s Sense* showing him the gaps in an imaginary opponent's guard. He wasn't stabbing like the spear; he was *carving*. 

"Watch your left," Goram commanded.

Ren didn't think. He didn't even process the command with his conscious mind. *Predictive Twitch* fired. He spun low, his left-hand blade sweeping through the space where an ankle would have been, while his right-hand blade snapped upward in a defensive arc.

The silver-blue light was no longer a trail behind him. It was a sphere, a cocoon of energy that hummed with the sound of a hundred hummingbirds.

**[NOTIFICATION: MULTI-DIRECTIONAL SYNCHRONIZATION INITIATED]**
**[CRITERIA MET: SIMULTANEOUS UTILIZATION OF DUAL INDEPENDENT ARCS]**
**[CRITERIA MET: INTEGRATION OF KITCHEN PRECISION AND SCOUT AGILITY]**

**[SKILL GRANTED: AMBIDEXTROUS FLOW (PASSIVE)]**
*Effect: Eliminates the 20% penalty for actions performed with the non-dominant hand. Increases dual-wielding coordination by 15%.*

Ren came to a halt, the wooden blades held perfectly still, their tips pointing toward an imaginary foe. He wasn't breathing hard, but his eyes were wide with a new kind of realization.

"They... they fit me," Ren whispered. "The spear is for the stand. But these... these are for the Express."

Goram walked over and took the blades back, nesting them into the rack with a slow, deliberate movement. "They fit you too well, Recruit Ito. A boy with your speed and those blades... it’s a terrifying combination. Most men use a shield because they're afraid of being hit. You use speed because you intend to be the one doing the hitting."

He looked Ren in the eye. "But remember the lesson from this morning. A unit is a heart. Those blades befit a loner, a shadow in the night. If you’re going to master the 'Viper's Kiss', you must never forget that even the fastest viper needs a den to return to."

"The Guard is my den, Sergeant," Ren said, giving a sharp, crisp salute. "And Chef Bram’s kitchen."

Goram let out a rare, gruff chuckle. "Indeed. Now, get moving. If you're late for prep because you were playing with old scout-toys, Bram will have my head for breakfast."

Ren blurred before the Sergeant could even finish the sentence. He wasn't just running for the kitchen; he was running toward a new understanding of himself. He was a courier who could fight, a chef who could lead, and now, a boy who knew that his two hands didn't have to be the same to be equal.

As he reached the mess hall and began the potato-peeling rhythm, he felt his *Ambidextrous Flow* at work. His left hand was now as precise as his right. The spirals of skin came away half as fast but twice as thick. 

He realized that the System wasn't just giving him skills; it was building a bridge between every part of his life. The paring knife, the spear, the shield, and the seax—they were all just different ways of delivering the same message.

And Ren Ito was determined to be the fastest messenger Silverhaven had ever seen. (Word Count: 1412)
