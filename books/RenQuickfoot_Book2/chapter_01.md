# Book 2: Chapter 1: The Four-Fold Pulse

"Bye Mom! Bye Papa! See you at the second bell!"

The front door of the Ito apartment didn't just close; it vibrated on its hinges, the sound following a blur of blue and silver that had already cleared the first flight of stairs before the echo could settle.

Hana Ito stood in the center of the living room, a half-folded tunic in her hands and a look of lingering bewilderment on her face. She turned to the window, catching a final, fleeting glimpse of a small figure leaping over a merchant’s cart two streets away. 

She let out a long, slow sigh that seemed to carry the weight of the entire morning’s chaos. "I barely saw him any longer, Hiroto. He was in for ten minutes to inhale the porridge, and now he’s gone to the northern wall. Is he... is he spreading himself too thin?"

Hiroto, seated at his desk with a cup of lukewarm tea and a stack of Registry ledgers, let out a soft, rhythmic chuckle. He didn't look up, but the way his shoulders relaxed spoke of a profound, clerk-like peace.

"Yes, yes he is, Hana," Hiroto said, his quill finally touching the parchment. "By every law of physics and municipal logic I know, our son is currently trying to be four different people at once. He’s a soldier, a chef, a student of the shadows, and an alchemist’s gopher. It’s an administrative nightmare."

He paused, a small smile playing on his lips. "But look at him. Did you see his eyes when he talked about the leeks? Or the way his feet were vibrating while he was putting on his boots? He’s having a blast right now, so let him be. He’ll figure out the limits on his own."

Hana walked over to the desk, her brow furrowing. "Four mentors, Hiroto. Sergeant Goram, Dusk, Chef Bram, and Apothecary Velda. How did he get so lucky—or so unlucky—to have the four busiest people in the city fighting over his hours?"

"He didn't get lucky, Hana," Hiroto said, finally looking up. "He made it impossible for them to say no. You don't say no to the Silverhaven Express when he offers to deliver your future. You just... try to keep up."

***

To Ren Ito, now officially seven years old and a Level 8 "Omni-Apprentice," the city of Silverhaven didn't feel like a place of limits. It felt like a perfectly synchronized clockwork mechanism, and he was the gear that connected all the others.

As he blurred through the Scribe’s Quarter, his **[The Omni-Apprentice]** unique passive was already at work. He wasn't just running; he was calculating. 

A pulse of gold-and-silver light hummed in his mind, showing him the 'Four-Fold Pulse' of his day. 

**[NOTIFICATION: OMNI-RESOURCES ENGAGED]**
- **DAWN:** Guard Formation & Pike Drills (Goram) - *Status: Active*
- **MORNING:** Kitchen Logistics & Precision Prep (Bram) - *Status: Pending*
- **AFTERNOON:** Alchemy & Herbalism Research (Velda) - *Status: Pending*
- **DUSK/NIGHT:** The Viper's Kiss & Shadow-Movement (Dusk) - *Status: Pending*

Ren hit the vertical face of a canal wall, his **[Balance Mastery]** allowing him to take the "Express Route" over the Grand Canal Bridge. He didn't feel tired. The **[Morning's Grace]** skill from Chef Bram’s kitchen was flooding his system with stamina, clearing the lingering fog of sleep and turning the cool morning air into pure fuel.

He reached the Guard Training Grounds just as the first sunbeams struck the iron-bound gates. Sergeant Goram was already there, his granite face set in a mask of expectation.

"Three seconds late, Recruit Ito!" Goram roared, though the glint in his eyes was anything but angry. 

"I was helping Mama with the tunic, Sergeant!" Ren chirped, skidding to a halt and going straight into a perfect, high-speed salute.

"Tunic-duty isn't a military objective!" Goram barked. "Shields up! The line is waiting, and Jarl has been trying to find his balance for the last twenty minutes. Show him what it means to be an anchor."

Ren snatched his Tower Shield from the rack. A year ago, it had been a crushing weight. Now, it felt like a part of his own body. He fell into the front-rank position, the **[Unit Harmonic]** pulse radiating outward as the other forty recruits—his Shield-Brothers and Sisters—found their rhythm against his back.

"One-two-one-two!" Ren led the chant, his voice clear and resonant.

For the next two hours, the Guard grounds were a symphony of iron and wood. Ren set a pace that was punishing but precise, his **[Omni-Apprentice]** skill allowing him to 'record' every stance and every thrust with a 10% learning boost. He wasn't just doing the drills; he was deconstructing them, seeing how the pressure of the shield-wall mirrored the pressure of a kitchen rush.

As the second bell of the morning echoed across the city, the Guard drill ended. Usually, the recruits would collapse, but Ren didn't even break his stance.

"Dismissed!" Goram shouted.

Ren didn't head for the barracks. He didn't even head for the water trough. 

He handed his shield to a dazed Jarl, gave the Sergeant a final, lightning-fast salute, and vanished toward the mess hall before the dust of the march had even settled.

"He's going to the kitchen?" Mara wheezed, leaning on her pike. "We just did five miles in full kit... and he's going to *work*?"

"He's the Express, Mara," Jarl muttered, watching the blue-and-silver blur vanish. "He's just on his way to his next delivery."

Inside the kitchen, Chef Bram was already in a state of controlled explosion. A massive shipment of river-fish had arrived, and the air was thick with the scent of salt, scales, and urgent hunger.

"Ren! About time!" Bram shouted, pointing a heavy cleaver at a pile of silver-bellied trout. "Scale 'em. Gut 'em. Fillet 'em. If I see a single bone in the breakfast stew, I'll make you clean the grease-pits with a toothbrush!"

Ren grabbed a scaling knife. He didn't look annoyed at the threat. He looked delighted. He sat on his stool, his **[The Steady Edge]** skill turning his hands into a mechanical blur. 

*Zip-shhh-zip-shhh.*

The scales flew like silver snow. Ren wasn't just cleaning fish; he was practicing his micro-motor control. Every flick of the wrist was a miniature **[Striking Flash]**. Every movement to clear the board was a lesson in **[Pathfinder's Sense]** on a six-inch scale.

By the time the third bell rang, the fish were processed, the porridge was bubbling, and Ren was already untying his apron.

"Fish are done, Chef! I'm off to the High District!"

Bram wiped his brow, staring at the perfectly empty crates. "Seven years old," he whispered to the boiling porridge. "If he survives Velda's fumes this afternoon, the boy’s going to be a force of nature."

Ren hit the streets again. He was tired now, but it was a different kind of tired—a heavy, satisfying heat that pulsed in his muscles. He stopped at a canal fountain, splashed cold water on his face, and checked his bag. Inside were the rare *Barnacle-Blooms* he had harvested on his run to the grounds—a gift for Velda.

He looked up at the High District tower, where the Apothecary’s sign hung proud and sharp. The afternoon was for the herbs and the glass. The evening was for the shadows of the Dusk.

"Spreading too thin?" Ren whispered to himself, his feet already finding the vertical stone of the High Bridge. 

He looked at his hands—steady, strong, and glowing with the blue light of the Omni-Apprentice. 

"I'm not thin," Ren grinned, launching himself into a high-speed vault over a startled merchant’s awning. "I'm just wide!"

The Silverhaven Express wasn't just a delivery service anymore. It was a multi-disciplinary pulse, and for the first time in his seven years, Ren Ito felt as if the city was finally big enough to hold him. (Word Count: 1478)
