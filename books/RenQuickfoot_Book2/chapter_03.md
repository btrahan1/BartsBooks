# Book 2: Chapter 3: The Verge of Shadows

Ren’s fingers brushed the hilts of the blades at his sides. They weren't the worn, iron-wood practice seaxes he had been using in the Guard grounds. These were the real deal—short-swords forged from Silverhaven steel, their edges honed by Dusk herself during their midnight sessions. The leather grips felt tacky against his palms, a tactile reminder that the time for games had passed.

"The delivery must be made, fiends!" Ren shouted, his voice ringing out across the silent verge.

The shadow in the deep green didn't answer with words. It answered with a low, clicking sound—the sound of many legs on dry wood. A Wood-Stalker, a nightmare of chitin and moss-covered fur, lunged from the gloom. It was the size of a large hound, but with the elongated, segmented limbs of an insect and eyes that glowed with a sickly, emerald light.

Ren didn't wait for it to reach him. He launched himself.

He didn't just run; he ignited. The silver-blue light of his **[Omni-Apprentice]** aura didn't trail behind him like a ribbon; it condensed into a sharp, aerodynamic shell. He moved so fast the air itself let out a high-pitched whistle.

From a high branch of a white-bark oak, hidden in a pocket of absolute silence, Dusk sat with her legs crossed. She shook her head, a faint, dangerous smile playing on her lips. 

"All right, kid," Dusk whispered to the wind. "Show me what you've got when the steel is live."

Ren hit the first Stalker with the **[Striking Flash]**. He didn't use a wild swing; he used a surgical thrust. The live steel slid into the creature’s chitinous neck with a sickening *squelch*.

The Stalker screeched, but Ren was already gone. He used **[Ambidextrous Flow]** to spin his left blade into a defensive arc, catching the spear-like leg of a second attacker. The metal-on-chitin impact sent a jar of vibration up his arm, but his **[Reactive Twitch]** guided the parry into a sliding redirection.

The Stalker overshot, its own momentum carrying it into the path of Ren’s right-hand blade.

*Slice.*

The green blood of the forest-dweller sprayed across the dirt. Ren rolled, his **[Balance Mastery]** keeping his center of gravity perfectly centered even as he tumbled through the leaf-litter.

"Too slow," Ren whispered, his eyes flashing with the white intensity he had shown during his graduation. 

He could feel it now—the way the real blades changed the logic of the fight. The wood was for the stand, for the endurance. The steel was for the delivery. It didn't push; it *delivered* kinetic energy exactly where it needed to go.

Three more Stalkers emerged, clicking their mandibles. They were moving in a pincer formation, trying to surround the "Flash". 

Ren didn't retreat. He used **[Pathfinder’s Sense]** to map the gaps between their twitching limbs. He saw the blue lines not as a road, but as a series of fatal vectors.

He accelerated into a **[Viper's Kiss]** whirlwind. 

To Dusk, watching from above, it looked like a silver-blue tornado had suddenly erupted in the center of the clearing. Ren wasn't just fighting; he was choreographing a massacre. His **[Effortless Flow]** ensured he didn't waste an ounce of stamina, and his **[The Steady Edge]** kept his cuts precise even at high-frequency.

*Clack-clink. Squelch. Snap.*

In under ten seconds, the clearing was silent again. The five Stalkers lay in various states of ruin, their emerald eyes fading to black.

Ren stood in the center, his chest heaving slightly, the real blades held in a low, respectful guard. His silver-blue light was steady, pulsing with a deep, combat-hardened frequency.

Dusk dropped from the tree, landing as silently as a falling leaf. She sheathed her own black blades and walked toward him, her expression one of grim approval.

"You missed the sixth one," Dusk said, her voice cool as the morning mist.

Ren didn't even turn his head. His **[Predictive Twitch]** flared. He flicked his right hand backward, the steel seax flying from his hand like a bolt of lightning.

A final Stalker, which had been preparing to pounce from the root of the oak, was pinned to the wood by its throat. Its legs twitched once, then went still.

Ren walked over, pulled the blade free with a wet *shluck*, and wiped it on the creature’s fur. He looked at Dusk, his face a mask of focus.

"The delivery must be made, Dusk," Ren said, his voice lower than it had been a year ago. "No matter how many 'fiends' are on the road."

Dusk reached out and adjusted the silver-winged patch on his shoulder. "Indeed, Express. Indeed."
