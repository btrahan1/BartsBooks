# Book 2: Chapter 5: The Threshold of the Wall

Even Ren Ito, a boy whose soul was forged from silver-blue lightning and an inexhaustible supply of Hiroto’s optimism, had to admit that his current schedule was... manageable. 

The removal of the four-hour kitchen shift had opened a strange, silent gap in his day—a pocket of time where the smell of trout was replaced by the clean, metallic tang of the Guard’s training grounds. For the first time since the "Omni-Apprentice" status had settled on his shoulders, Ren didn't feel like a letter being pulled in four directions. He felt like a spear being sharpened on three different stones.

He was standing at the edge of the parade ground, watching the morning sun illuminate the high stone walls of Silverhaven. His new **[Chef's Blade]** was a heavy, comforting weight on his left hip, balanced by the live-steel seaxes on his right.

"Every man has his limits, Ren."

The voice was a low, granite rumble. Sergeant Goram was standing beside him, his massive arms crossed, his gaze fixed on the forty recruits who were currently attempting a series of high-speed pike maneuvers.

"Knowing what those limits are," Goram continued, not looking at Ren, "and knowing when you can exceed them, and for how long... that is a lesson many men rarely learn. Most men never even approach their limits. They live in the comfortable middle, where the air is thick and the legs never burn."

He finally looked down at Ren, his eyes narrow and assessing. "You, on the other hand, have been living on the thin edge for three months. I watched you in Bram's kitchen. I watched you on the Verge. You were red-lining, boy. You were a boiler one second away from an explosion."

Ren looked at his hands, which were steady for the first time in weeks. "I felt like I could do it, Sergeant. The System said I could."

"The System provides the tools, Ren, but you provide the engine," Goram said, a rare, gruff note of pride in his voice. "Mastering the kitchen taught you efficiency. Mastering the Verge taught you reactive speed. But mastering *yourself*... that's the drill that never ends. You make me proud, boy. Truly."

He reached out and gave Ren a heavy pat on the shoulder—a gesture that felt like a mountain settling into place. 

"But pride doesn't win the stand," Goram barked, his voice suddenly returning to its full, drill-master volume. "Now, what are you waiting for? Those recruits are getting fatter and lazier with me just staring at them! Jarl is currently holding his pike like it’s a lady’s parasol, and Mara looks like she’s about to take a nap on her shield!"

Ren’s smile broke across his face like a sunrise. "Yes, Sergeant!"

He launched himself from the standing position. He didn't use a blur-sprint. He used a controlled, high-frequency glide—a movement that utilized his **[Effortless Flow]** to the absolute maximum. 

**[NOTIFICATION: REFINED FOCUS RECOGNIZED]**
- *Passive Skill Interaction: Omni-Apprentice (3/4 Paths Active)*
- *Effect: Learning Speed for remaining paths increased from +10% to +15%*
- *Effect: Stamina drain reduced by an additional 5% during cross-discipline training.*

Ren hit the center of the recruit formation, his silver-blue light pulsing with a deep, authoritative warmth. He didn't just join the line; he became the pivot-point for it.

"Pikes UP!" Ren commanded, his voice carrying the weight of his **[Kitchen Graduate]** precision. "Find the truth of the point! Jarl, your shoulder is dipping—you're losing the lever! Mara, watch the pulse—follow the light!"

The recruits, who had been struggling under the heat, suddenly felt the shift. The **[Unit Harmonic]** pulse was stronger today, clearer. It wasn't just a guide; it was an infusion of Ren’s newly focused energy.

For the next four hours, the Guard grounds became a ballet of iron and ash. Ren didn't just lead the drills; he deconstructed them in real-time. He showed Jarl how a slight flick of the wrist—the same one Bram used for the paring knife—could turn a clumsy thrust into a **[Striking Flash]**. He showed Mara how to use the weight of the Tower Shield as an anchor, a physical manifestation of the **[Bio-Flow Anchor]** he had learned on the Verge.

He was seeing the intersections now. The Guard’s defense was the kitchen’s structure. The kitchen’s efficiency was the shadow’s movement. 

By the end of the shift, the recruits weren't just exhausted; they were transformed. They stood in a perfect, breathing wall, their pikes held with a lethality that hadn't been there yesterday.

Goram walked the line, his baton tapping rhythmically against his thigh. He stopped in front of Ren, who was standing at the head, not a single bead of sweat on his brow despite the intensity.

"Better," Goram grunted. "Much better. It seems a boy with three paths is twice as dangerous as a boy with four."

Ren gave a sharp salute. "The delivery is getting faster, Sergeant."

"Good. Because Apothecary Velda just sent a runner," Goram said, his expression darkening. "There’s a fever breaking out in the Scribe’s Quarter—not the kind you cure with soup and rest. It’s a magical blight, Ren. And she needs the Express to deliver the 'Bitter-Sweet' antidote to forty houses before the sun sets."

Ren’s feet began to vibrate instantly. His **[Morning's Grace]** was still pulsing, his stamina near its peak. 

"I'm ready, Sergeant."

"I know you are," Goram said, looking at the city gates. "Go. Show this city what its heart-beat can really do."
