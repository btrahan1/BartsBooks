# Book 2: Chapter 6: The Intent of the Infusion

The High District air was cooler than the bake-ovens of the Guard grounds, but inside Velda’s tower, the atmosphere was thick enough to chew. A violet haze hung over the laboratory, smelling of ozone and bruised lavender—the distinct, slightly metallic scent of a magical blight meeting its alchemical match.

Ren arrived at the balcony in a silver-blue blur, his **[Morning's Grace]** still humming in his veins. He didn't even wait for the dust to settle before landing beside Velda’s primary workstation.

"So, you've found some time for old Velda I see?" the Apothecary said, not looking up from a bubbling glass phial. Her bird-like hands were moving with a frantic, precise energy. "I was beginning to think you forgot about your humble mentor in favor of Sergeant Goram’s mud-drills."

Ren rolled his eyes, a playful grin breaking across his face. "Never, Lady Velda. Never! You're the one who keeps my system from melting when I eat the wrong forest-berries."

Velda paused, her grip on the glass momentary tightening. A faint, dusty pink rose to her cheeks—the closest the stern woman ever came to a blush. She adjusted her spectacles with a sharp, defensive click.

"No 'ladies' here, boy! Enough of that high-district nonsense," she scolded, though her voice lacked its usual bite. "If the city officials hear you calling me that, they’ll expect me to start wearing silk and attending garden parties, and I have far too much dignity for that."

She gestured toward the laboratory table, where forty empty vials stood in a perfect, crystalline line. Beside them were the **[Dusk-Petals]** Ren had gathered, now crushed into a vibrant, glowing slurry.

"Now, watch carefully as I prepare this antidote," Velda commanded, her voice turning clinical. "This isn't like whisking eggs or filleting trout, Ren. This is about more than kinetic efficiency. This blight is an echo—a magical dissonance that has settled into the Scribe’s Quarter. To cure it, the antidote needs a specific resonance."

She picked up a silver stirrer and began to thread it through the purple slurry. As she moved, Ren’s **[Perception]** of 34 flared. He didn't just see the liquid; he saw the invisible threads of light connecting Velda’s fingers to the mixture.

"This isn't about speed, Ren," Velda said, her focus absolute. "This is about intent. If you move too fast, the mana-threads will snap. If you move too slow, they’ll stagnate. Watch my mana flows, then try to replicate them."

To Ren’s **[System Logic]**, the laboratory suddenly became a web of glowing blue and violet lines. He saw the way Velda was 'pushing' her own internal energy into the infusion—not with the explosive force of a **[Striking Flash]**, but with the steady, unwavering pressure of the **[Bio-Flow Anchor]**.

"I see it," Ren whispered, his silver-blue light beginning to hum in harmony with her violet threads.

"Good. I will do the first few, then you join me," Velda said. "You are the Omni-Apprentice, Ren. You have the speed to process forty vials in the time it takes most to do one, but if the intent isn't there, you’re just delivering expensive water."

Ren stepped up to the second workstation. He picked up his **[Chef's Blade]** to finely dice a root of *Bitter-Thread*, but he didn't use the high-frequency blur. He used a slow, deliberate stroke, visualizing the mana-flow as a delivery route that had to be traveled with absolute reverence.

He felt the shift in his own light. The silver-blue spiked, then smoothed out into a deep, resonant hum—a "Mana-Anchor."

**[SKILL GRANTED: RESONANT INFUSION (ACTIVE)]**

"Excellent," Velda murmured, watching as Ren’s first vial began to glow with a steady, stable violet light. "You're not just a runner, Ren. You're starting to understand the destination before you even leave the gate."

For the next two hours, the "Express" was silent. Ren worked in perfect synchronization with Velda, their hands moving in a dance of glass and intent. The forty vials were filled, sealed, and packed into Ren's delivery bag—a bag that now felt surprisingly heavy, not with weight, but with the significance of the cure.

"The Scribe's Quarter," Velda said, handing him the final bottle. "Go. The fever is fast, but you are faster. Just remember... the intent in the vial is only as strong as the intent in the boy who carries it."

Ren didn't say anything. He didn't need to. He gave Velda a final, sharp salute—not as a recruit, but as a student—and vanished through the balcony. 

He hit the High District streets, but he didn't blur-run. He moved with a focused, rhythmic momentum that felt like the pulse of the infusion itself. He wasn't just delivering a cure; he was delivering a resonance.
