# Book 2: Chapter 7: The Shadows' Schedule

The Scribe’s Quarter was finally quiet, the violet haze of the magical blight receding into the stone like a bad dream after a long night. Ren skidded to a halt in front of Velda’s tower, his silver-blue aura dimmed to a resting hum. His bag, once heavy with the resonance of forty vials, was now nearly empty.

Ren reached into the pouch and pulled out the small, crystal bottles one by one, checking them against the registry in his mind. 

"39 bottles delivered, Mistress Velda," Ren said, his voice carrying the fatigue of the run but none of the illness. "One left. Does someone else in the High District need a cure?"

Velda, who had been waiting on the balcony with a lantern that glowed with a soft, protective light, stepped forward. She looked at Ren, her sharp eyes scanning his face for any sign of the gray, ashy pallor of the blight.

"That's for you, Ren," Velda said, her voice unusually gentle. "Drink it now. As fast as you are, not even you can outrun a fever of this magnitude. You have been exposed to the heart of the resonance for hours, boy. Better safe than sorry."

Ren didn't argue. He popped the cork, the scent of the violet slurry flooding his senses with its bitter, arcanic promise. He tilted the vial back, the liquid tasting of cold mountain air and old copper.

"There," Velda said, taking the empty vial from his hand. "Now, go home and get some sleep. Your parents have been pacing the floor since the sun hit the zenith."

"I was going to, but..."

Dusk emerged from the shadows of the balcony doorway as if she had been made from the darkness itself. Her black silk clothes were perfectly still, her eyes reflecting the silver light of Ren’s aura.

"Go home and get some sleep, boy," Dusk echoed, her voice a cool vibration in the night air. "But don't get too comfortable. Now that you don't have to be at the kitchens before the sun rises, you and I can spend more time together before the morning sky lightens. It's the perfect time to dance in the shadows."

Ren looked at her, then at the moon, which was already past its peak. "More time?"

Dusk’s smile was a thin, silver line—unnerving in its predatory joy. "The 'Viper's Kiss' requires more than just midnight drills, Ren. It requires the deep quiet of the pre-dawn hours, when the city’s heart-beat is at its lowest. That is when you truly learn how to move without a path."

She stepped closer, her gaze lingering on the live-steel seaxes at his waist. "I hear you have been getting in some practice with those blades of yours on the Verge. I will expect a full demonstration after you've taken the vial and have had a few hours of rest."

She reached out and tapped the hilts of his weapons, her touch as light as a shadow. "Wood is for learning the rhythm. Steel is for learning the truth. We shall see how much truth you have gathered while I wasn't looking."

And then, she was gone. She didn't vault or run; she simply dissolved into the darkness of the stairwell, leaving only the scent of ozone and the feeling of a new, more intensive delivery schedule.

Velda let out a long, weary sigh. "See? I told you she was hovering. Go home, Ren. Before she decides that 'rest' is a relative term."

Ren gave Velda a final, respectful bow, his silver-blue light sparking with a renewed, if slightly nervous, energy. He was tired, yes, but he was also Level 12, a Kitchen Graduate, and now, a combatant who had survived the Verge.

"I'm going, I'm going!" Ren chirped, launching himself from the balcony.

He blurred through the sleeping streets, the violet-tasting antidote still singing in his blood. He was heading for home, for a few hours of sleep in a bed that wasn't made of grass or stone. But in his mind, the "Viper's Kiss" was already starting its dance. 

The Silverhaven Express had finished his deliveries for the day. But the night... the night was just beginning to open its route. (Word Count: 1462)
