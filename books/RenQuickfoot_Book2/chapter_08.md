# Book 2: Chapter 8: A Different Kind of Cutting

The Ito apartment was filled with the warm, reassuring scent of baking bread and the soft, rhythmic scratching of Hiroto’s quill. It was the hour before the true dawn, that thin slice of time where the city of Silverhaven was caught between the exhaustion of the night and the anticipation of the morning.

Ren had managed exactly three hours of sleep. He had woken up before the first bell, his body vibrating with the resonance of the blight-antidote and the electric expectation of Dusk’s challenge. He was currently in the small kitchen, checking the straps of his steel seaxes and the edge of his **[Chef's Blade]**.

Hana Ito walked into the room, her hair still sleep-mussed, a look of stern motherly disapproval on her face as she saw her seven-year-old son adjusting a weapons-belt.

"I thought you were done with the kitchens, Ren!" Hana scolded, her voice low to avoid waking the neighbors, but carrying the full weight of her concern. "Master Bram told us himself—he fired you! You're supposed to be sleeping until the sun is high, not sneaking out while the moon is still visible."

Ren looked up, his silver-blue light sparking with a mischievous, high-energy intensity. He didn't look tired; he looked like a spring that had been wound to its breaking point.

"I am done with the kitchens, Mom!" Ren chirped, giving her a quick, glancing hug that lasted only a fraction of a second. "Master Bram said I'm only allowed back in to eat."

He headed for the window, his feet already beginning to find the "Express" rhythm. 

"But Dusk is waiting," Ren added, his hand resting on the hilt of his right-hand seax. "I'll be practicing a different kind of cutting this morning!"

And then, he was gone. He didn't use the door; he simply tilted forward and vanished into the gray pre-dawn mist, a silver-blue streak heading for the rooftops.

Hana stood in the quiet kitchen, staring at the empty window-frame. She turned to Hiroto, who was watching from the doorway with a look of quiet, scholarly amusement.

"What does that even mean?" Hana whispered. "A different kind of cutting? Hiroto, he’s seven! He should be cutting paper for school-crafts, not... whatever 'Dusk' is teaching him."

Hiroto walked over and placed a hand on her shoulder. "It means, Hana, that our son is no longer just a courier. He’s becoming the blade that protects the message. Let him be. He’s the only boy I know who can deliver a joke and a revolution before breakfast."

***

The rooftops of the Scribe’s Quarter were slick with pre-dawn dew, turning the slate tiles into a treacherous landscape of low-friction hazards. To most, it would have been a nightmare of broken ankles and vertical drops. To Ren, it was a playground.

He activated **[Balance Mastery]** and **[Urban Agility]**, his feet finding the exact 'truth' of the roof-line. He didn't just run; he flowed, his movement a silent, high-speed vibration that left a trail of "Justice Light" in the mist.

He reached the Old Archive tower, the highest point in the Quarter. Dusk was waiting there, perched on a gargoyle that looked remarkably like her own silent, predatory stance.

"Three minutes early, Ren," Dusk said, her voice a cool shadow in the air. "Rest agreed with you. The 'Mana-Anchor' in your pulse is steady."

She dropped from the gargoyle, her black blades drawn in a single, fluid motion. She didn't offer a greeting or a tip. She simply attacked.

"The demonstration begins!" Dusk commanded.

This wasn't the Verge, and these weren't Wood-Stalkers. This was a duel of high-frequency agility. Dusk moved with a localized speed that Ren’s **[Pathfinder’s Sense]** struggled to map. She was a series of overlapping arcs, her steel blades hissing through the mist.

Ren didn't retreat. He drew his steel seaxes, the live metal singing as it left the leather sheaths. 

*Clack-clink. Clink-zip.*

Ren used **[Ambidextrous Flow]** to engage both blades simultaneously. He didn't just parry; he used the "Viper's Kiss" rhythm he had learned from his mentors. He integrated the precision of the kitchen—the 'secret of the thumb'—into the way he redirected Dusk’s strikes. 

He wasn't fighting like a soldier. He was fighting like a chef who was deconstructing a difficult recipe.

"Better," Dusk whispered, her blade sliding along his left-hand seax. "You're starting to see the gaps between the heartbeats."

Ren’s silver-blue light flared, turning the pre-dawn gray into a vibrant, electric blue. He used **[Reactive Twitch]** to catch her heavy downward strike, but instead of holding the weight, he used his **[Effortless Flow]** to slide around it.

He stepped *in*, his **[Chef's Blade]** flashing from his belt in a sudden, high-precision feint. 

Dusk’s eyes widened. She had expected the seaxes, but the inclusion of the Master-Chef's tool broke her rhythm. It was a blade that 'knew its owner,' and in Ren’s hand, it was an extension of his own impossible speed.

Ren didn't cut her. He stopped the blade exactly one centimeter from the collar of her black silk tunic.

"A different kind of cutting, Dusk," Ren said, his voice steady despite the adrenaline. "The kind where you don't waste any energy on the windup."

Dusk stood perfectly still, the silver-blue light reflecting in her dark eyes. She slowly lowered her blades and sheathed them. A long, slow smile spread across her face—one that lacked the predatory edge and held a flicker of genuine wonder.

"You integrated the kitchen into the Kiss," Dusk murmured. "The precision of the prep work... used as a combat vector. Goram would call it madness. I call it... remarkable."

She reached out and tapped the Chef's Blade. "Master Bram’s gift suits you. It’s a blade of intent, Ren. Like the infusion you made for Velda. You're no longer just following the paths we give you. You're starting to build your own."

Ren sheathed his blades, his silver light settling into a calm, triumphant glow. The sun was just beginning to break over the Eastern Wall, painting the city in shades of gold and violet. 

"Does this mean I'm done with the 'dance'?" Ren asked.

"Hardly," Dusk said, turning back toward the gargoyle. "It means we can finally move on to the difficult part. But for now... go. Sergeant Goram is waiting for his most focused recruit. And I suspect he has a mission for you that requires more than just a pike."

Ren grinned, his feet already beginning to vibrate for the next run. "A mission? Like a delivery?"

"Like a revolution, Ren," Dusk said, her voice vanishing into the morning light. "Like a revolution."

Ren launched himself from the tower, a blue-and-silver streak heading for the Training Grounds. He was Level 12, an Omni-Apprentice who had finished one path and was rewriting the others. The "Silverhaven Express" was no longer just a name—it was a promise that the city would always arrive at its best. 

***

By the time Ren reached the Training Grounds, the morning drills were already in full swing. The sound of clashing pikes and the bark of orders echoed off the stone walls. In the center of the yard, Sergeant Goram stood with his arms crossed, his gaze fixed on Ren as he skidded to a halt in a cloud of dust.

Goram didn't say anything at first. He simply began to circle Ren like he was inspecting a suspect piece of meat at the market. 

"Hmmm," the Sergeant grunted, his boots crunching on the gravel. He moved to Ren's left. "Huh."

He completed the circle, pausing to squint at Ren’s silver-winged patches. "Hmmm ... maybe."

"Sir?" Ren questioned, his feet still twitching with the residual energy of the run. 

"I'm thinking, Ren, I'm thinking!" Goram snapped, though there was more contemplation than anger in his voice.

Suddenly, a shadow flickered. Dusk appeared right behind the Sergeant's shoulder, her movement so silent that even Ren's **[Pathfinder’s Sense]** barely registered her arrival.

"Boo!" she whispered.

Goram jumped a good six inches, his hand instinctively flying to the hilt of his heavy drill-sword. "Damnit Dusk!" he roared, his face turning a shade of red that matched the morning sun.

"What's the hesitation, Sarge?" Dusk asked, her voice light and teasing. She leaned against the air as if it were a solid wall. "He's perfect for the mission!"

"He's just so ... young!" Goram countered, gesturing vaguely at Ren’s height. "And small!"

"And fast, and nimble, and quick, and innocent looking!" Dusk finished for him, ticking the points off on her gloved fingers.

"Well yeah, but, damnit..." Goram turned back to Ren, his eyes narrowing. "How old are you now, seven?"

"And a half, sir!" Ren corrected, standing as tall as his small frame would allow. He looked from the granite-faced Sergeant to the shadowy Instructor. "Whatever it is, I can do it!"

Goram looked at Dusk, then back at Ren. He let out a long, weary sigh that seemed to deflate some of his military posture. "Innocent looking. Right. If this goes south, Dusk, I'm telling the Magister it was your idea."

"It usually is, Sarge," Dusk smirked. "It usually is." (Word Count: 1654)
