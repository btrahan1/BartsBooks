# Book 2: Chapter 11: A New Mentor

The briefing room in the heart of the Guard's Training Grounds felt different now. The heavy iron-bound door remained closed, sealing in the secrets of the mission, but the atmosphere had shifted from the static weight of planning to the kinetic tension of departure. The specialized gear—the traction-enchanted boots, the shadow-cloak, and the alchemical vials—was now packed away into the magical silence of Ren's new silver ring. 

Ren stood by the massive wooden table, his hand resting on the hilt of his Master-Chef's blade. He felt more prepared, certainly, but also more vulnerable. The "several hundred miles" that Goram had mentioned felt like a physical weight, a distance that his mind, accustomed to the tidy boundaries of Silverhaven, was still struggling to comprehend.

Sergeant Goram and Dusk were watching him, their professional masks firmly in place, though there was a lingering softness in Dusk’s expression that she hadn't quite managed to tuck away. They weren't finished with him, however. Not yet.

"Ren," Goram said, his voice echoing in the small, windowless space. "I'd like to introduce you to your mentor."

Ren looked up, confused. "My mentor? But sir... I already have three. You, Instructor Dusk, and Master Bram. And Velda, if you count the Apothecary paths. I thought..."

"You thought you were leaving the nest on your own?" Dusk finished, a playful but sharp edge to her voice. She stepped away from the shadows of the wall. "For the next several weeks, at least, if not months, you will be away from the three of us. We have our duties here—the Guard needs its Drill-Master, and the shadows of Silverhaven don't police themselves."

Goram stepped back, gesturing toward the darkest corner of the room, a spot that Ren’s **[Perception]** had previously marked as empty. "We don't want you slacking off just because we're not there to bark orders or throw knives at your heels. Master Loamwalker will be your travel companion for the first couple of weeks. Don't worry, he won't slow you down."

As the Sergeant spoke, the shadows in the corner didn't just move; they seemed to breathe. A figure stepped forward, draped in a cloak that looked more like living moss and mottled bark than any fabric Ren had seen. The man—if 'man' was the right word—was tall and lean, his movement so fluid that he appeared to flow across the stone floor rather than walk. 

His skin was the color of seasoned oak, and his eyes were a piercing, vibrant emerald that seemed to hold the reflected light of a thousand forests. His ears were tapered to delicate, sharp points, and his hair was a silver-gold that mimicked the pre-dawn light.

Goram smirked, a look of genuine amusement crossing his granite features. "He's an Elven Ranger. One of the best the Borderlands have ever produced. He will be your guide, more or less, until he is convinced you can survive for weeks at a time on your own on the road, and in the woods, and wherever else you find yourself."

Ren stared, his **[Sense Illegitimacy]** providing a strange, pleasant hum of 'truth' from the newcomer. Elves were rare in the city center, and Rangers even rarer. These were people who lived in the silence between the heartbeats of the world, keepers of the wild paths that connected the great civilizations.

Master Loamwalker stopped a few feet from Ren. He didn't offer a hand or a salute. He simply tilted his head, his emerald eyes scanning Ren from his traction-boots to the silver-blue light pulsing in his pulse.

"You are small," Loamwalker said. His voice was melodic, carrying a resonance that reminded Ren of the wind through the high pines. It lacked the harshness of the city, the grating quality of the markets and the docks. "Small, and loud. Your mana-signature is like a bell ringing in a quiet valley."

Ren bristled slightly. "I'm a courier! I'm supposed to be noticed! Or at least... I'm supposed to be fast. And I'm an Omni-Apprentice."

"Speed in the city is not speed in the wild, little Express," Loamwalker murmured. He looked at Goram. "You are certain of this, Sergeant? He smells of baking bread and old ink. The Iron Mountains do not care for either."

"He's the fastest conscience in Silverhaven, Loamwalker," Goram grunted, though he didn't argue with the Ranger's assessment. "And his instincts are unlike anything I've seen in twenty years of training. He won't just follow you; he'll be pushing you to keep up."

The Elf’s emerald eyes flickered back to Ren. "A bold claim. But one I look forward to testing. Survival is not a sprint, Ren Ito. It is a long, deliberate conversation with the earth. You are accustomed to the cobbles giving you friction and the walls giving you shelter. Out there, the earth will challenge your every step, and the sky will be the only roof you have."

Ren looked at the new mentor, feeling a sudden, intense surge of curiosity. His **[Intelligence]** and **[Pathfinder's Sense]** were already trying to categorize Loamwalker's movement patterns. The way the Elf stood—relaxed, yet perfectly balanced—suggested a level of agility that made Ren's own **[Balance Mastery]** feel like a clumsy imitation.

"I can survive," Ren said, his feet already beginning to vibrate against the stone floor. "I’ve been throughout the city. I’ve handled the Wood-Stalkers in the High District."

"The High District is a garden, Ren," Dusk added, leaning against the briefing table. "Master Loamwalker is here to make sure you don't treat the Iron Mountains like a walk in the park. He's forgotten more about the wild paths than all the maps in this room combined."

Loamwalker turned slightly, his cloak swirling with a sound like dry leaves. "We leave at first light. Not a second later. The city gates open for the merchants at five, but we will not be using the gates. The road begins where the walls stop, and I intend to see how you handle the transition."

He looked at Ren's Ring of Holding. "Do not rely too heavily on the magic. Space can be folded, but time cannot. A hundred miles is still a hundred miles, regardless of how light your satchel feels."

Ren nodded, the weight of the new mentorship settling around him. He had spent months learning the 'Steady Edge' of the kitchen, the 'Viper's Kiss' of the shadows, and the 'Iron Bastion' of the Guard. Now, he was being handed over to a man who lived in the green spaces on the map, a man whose very existence was a challenge to the city-bound life Ren had always known.

"A few hundred miles," Ren whispered, looking at the Elf. "You really won't slow me down?"

Goram’s smirk widened. "Ren, the only thing that slows down an Elven Ranger is a bad philosophy. Everything else is just a scenery-change."

Loamwalker didn't smile, but there was a flicker of something in his emerald eyes—a hint of the predatory grace that defined his class. "The road is a long teacher, boy. I hope you are a fast student."

Ren grinned, his silver-blue light flaring in the dim room. "I'm the fastest student Silverhaven's ever seen, Master Loamwalker. Let's find out if the woods can keep up."

The preparations were finally over. The briefing had concluded. The mentors had done all they could within the safety of the stone walls. As the mana-lamps in the room began to dim, Ren felt the pull of the East—a resonant, magnetic tug that transcended the city’s maps and pointed toward the jagged teeth of the Iron Mountains. He wasn't just a boy anymore. He was a traveler, and for the first time in his seven-and-a-half years, the horizon was the only limit he was willing to accept. (Word Count: 1464)
