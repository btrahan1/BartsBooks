# Book 2: Chapter 17: Shadows of the Canyon

The descent into the Obsidian Maw was a plunge into another world. 

As Ren and Master Loamwalker moved away from the sun-drenched plateau of the Foothills, the light didn't just fade; it seemed to be actively consumed by the canyon walls. The red-orange glow of the late afternoon was replaced by a heavy, oppressive purple, and then by a darkness so dense it felt like a physical weight against Ren's skin. 

The air changed too. The crisp, thinning oxygen of the peaks was gone, replaced by a damp, stagnant chill that smelled of ancient stone, cold minerals, and something else—a faint, metallic tang that made the hair on the back of Ren’s neck stand up.

The canyon walls were sheer, rising hundreds of feet on either side, their surfaces jagged and unforgiving. Here, the 'Ranger’s Gait' was no longer an elective skill; it was the only thing keeping Ren from a plummet into the abyss. Every step required a perfect flex, a perfect arrival of the sole on a surface that was often no wider than a finger’s breadth. 

Ren moved with a silent intensity, his 'iron buckets' echoing slightly no matter how hard he tried to find the resonance. He was hyper-aware of his surroundings, his **[Perception]** and **[Intelligence]** working at full capacity to map the treacherous path.

"Be wary, Little Express," Loamwalker’s voice suddenly cut through the gloom, low and sharp. "We are being stalked."

Ren didn't stop, but his body tensed, his hands hovering instinctively near the hilts of his seaxes. He closed his eyes for a micro-second, sending out a pulse of his **[Sense Resonance]**, using the canyon walls as a massive acoustic mirror.

"I feel them, Master," Ren replied, his voice a steady whisper. "I sense at least six of them. They are moving in the upper shadows, using the crevices. Three on the left wall, three on the right."

He paused, his brow furrowing as he processed the data coming back from the stone. It wasn't the clean, predatory hunger of the Grass-Stalker. This was different. It was a cold, calculated pressure—a frequency that felt jagged and wrong.

"They are hungry," Ren continued, his voice dropping an octave. "But... it feels wrong. It is not only hunger, but a type of malice even. It’s like they aren't just looking for a meal; they are looking for the 'break.' What are they? I sense they will not be deterred by a few hard slaps."

Loamwalker didn't look back, but Ren could see the Ranger’s shoulders tighten. The Moss-Cloak seemed to darken, its green mana-flicker dying out as it shifted into a stealth-mode that Ren’s eyes could barely track.

"You are learning to read the frequency, boy," Loamwalker said. "That malice is the mark of the Shadow-Gaunts. They are not animals of the Reach. They are scavengers of the deep stone, born of the mountain’s dark marrow and fed on the fear of those who lose their way. They do not hunt for survival; they hunt for the resonance of suffering."

Ren felt a shiver that had nothing to do with the canyon chill. His **[Sense Illegitimacy]** was starting to howl now, the wrongness of the stalkers becoming clearer with every step. They were moving closer, their presence becoming a heavy, oily smear against the clean frequency of the stone.

"They won't be deterred by mercy," Loamwalker confirmed, his hand finally reaching for the bow strapped to his back. "A Shadow-Gaunt sees mercy as a weakness of the spirit, a crack in the armor they can exploit. If they pounce, Ren, you do not use the flat of the blade. You use the edge. And you use it quickly."

The canyon grew narrower, the path descending into a steep, rubble-filled gut where the walls seemed to lean inward, threatening to crush anything that passed between them. The sound of their own footsteps—even Loamwalker’s whispered tread—seemed to echo for miles, a rhythmic dinner bell for the things in the dark.

Ren adjusted his breathing, the silent nasal rhythm now a desperate necessity to calm his hammering heart. He could 'hear' the stalkers now—the dry scuttle of multi-jointed limbs against the granite, the faint, clicking sound of chitonous mandibles, and a low, guttering hiss that sounded like steam escaping a dying pipe.

"They are closing the circle," Ren reported, his **[Target Acquisition]** flicking between the six signatures. "They are timing the lunge for the narrowest part of the gut. Four seconds. Maybe five."

"Then let us greet them," Loamwalker said.

The Ranger didn't stop, but his movement changed. He didn't just run; he 'accelerated' through the silence. He whipped his bow around, nocking three arrows with a speed that defied Ren's **[Perception]**. 

At the same moment, the shadows exploded.

The Shadow-Gaunts didn't pounce with the heavy grace of the feline Stalker. They were spindly, grey-skinned nightmares with too many joints and eyes that glowed with a sick, oily yellow light. They dropped from the crevices like heavy, wet stones, their long, needle-thin claws extended.

One of them launched itself directly at Ren’s head. 

Ren didn't hesitate. The 'mercy' of the meadow was gone, replaced by the cold, tactical efficiency of the Omni-Apprentice. He felt the 'malice' of the Gaunt—a wave of psychic sludge that tried to dampen his resolve—and he pushed back with the full weight of his **[Intelligence]**. 

He snapped his wrists, drawing his seaxes in a single, fluid motion. He didn't use the flat. He didn't aim for a non-lethal strike. He stepped into the Gaunt’s lunge, his 'iron buckets' providing a solid, unyielding pivot on the rubble.

The steel passed through the Gaunt’s neck with a sickening, wet crunch. The creature didn't even have time to scream before its head was severed, its body dissolving into a cloud of grey, putrid ash that smelled of ozone and rot.

Ren didn't stop to admire the kill. Two more Gaunts were landing behind him, their clicking mandibles inches from his heels. He spun, his **[Balance Mastery]** allowing him to keep his footing on the shifting slope. He used the 'Ranger’s Gait'—the arrival of the sole—to find a solid stone, and then he launched himself.

He was a blur of steel and silver-blue light. He didn't use the complex flourishes of the Academy; he used the short, brutal efficiency of a courier who had learned that the fastest way through an obstacle was to remove it. He severed a limb, then a torso, then a head. 

To his left, Loamwalker was a symphony of lethal precision. His arrows hissed through the air, finding the yellow eyes of the Gaunts with impossible accuracy. The Ranger didn't even seem to be aiming; he was simply 'placing' death wherever the shadows moved.

In less than a minute, the gut was silent again. The six malicious entities were gone, reduced to heaps of grey ash that the damp wind was already beginning to scatter.

Ren stood in the middle of the path, his chest heaving, his blades still extended. His **[Sense Resonance]** was slowly returning to its baseline, the 'wrongness' of the Gaunts fading from the stone. He looked at the ash on his boots, then up at Loamwalker.

"They... they weren't alive, were they?" Ren asked, his voice shaking slightly.

Loamwalker sheathed his bow, his moss-cloak slowly regaining its faint green glow. "They were alive in their own way, Ren. But they were not of the light. They were the shadows' attempt to have a voice. And they will not be the last."

He looked deeper into the canyon, where the path disappeared into an even more profound darkness. "The Obsidian Maw is a long road, Little Express. And it seems your feet have finally learned that not everything in the wild deserves a name. Some things only deserve the steel."

Ren nodded, his face hardening as he sheathed his seaxes. He felt the silver ring on his finger pulse—a steady, encouraging beat. The journey through the meadow had been a dream of connection. This, he realized, was the reality of the mission. 

"I'm ready, Master," Ren said, his voice quiet but devoid of hesitation. "Let's keep moving. The package doesn't deliver itself."

Loamwalker smirked, a genuine expression of approval that finally reached his emerald eyes. "Spoken like a true Ranger. Now, run. The next six are already waiting." (Word Count: 1463)
