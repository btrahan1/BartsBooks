# Book 2: Chapter 25: The Iron Stand

For ten minutes, the High-Hall of the Iron Mountains was a theater of the impossible. Ren move like a silver-blue ribbon of smoke, a flicker of white tunic that existed everywhere and nowhere at once. Krag, the Master of the Earth-Stance, was a force of nature—a tectonic plate in motion—but he was find it impossible to pin down the "Little Express."

Every time Krag lunged, his massive shield leading the way like a battering ram, Ren was already gone. He used the polished obsidian floor as a springboard, his bare feet finding purchase where others would slide. He utilized the **[Ranger's Gait]** to pivot mid-air, and his **[Sense Resonance]** to anticipate the exact micro-second the air pressure changed before a strike.

The Dwarven crowd watched in a growing, frustrated silence. They had expected a slaughter; instead, they were watching a dance. 

Krag stopped in the center of the Arena of Foundation, his breathing heavy and ragged, his matte-grey armor covered in a fine layer of sweat. He lowered his shield, his face twisted in a mask of genuine disgust. He spat onto the obsidian tiles, the sound echoing through the pressurized quiet of the hall.

"Enough!" Krag roared, his voice a gravelly rumble. "You move like the wind, boy, but you fight like a fairy! What good is a courier if he can't stand to meet the threat? You skip and hop like a mountain-goat, but the Stone doesn't dance. The Stone *stands*. If this is the best the surface has to offer—a fly that's too fast to swat—then Silverhaven is already lost."

The King of the Iron Mountains leaned forward, his emerald throne glowing with a dangerous, expectant light. He didn't speak, but his slate-grey eyes were fixed on Ren, waiting for the pebble's reply.

Ren stopped exactly ten paces from Krag. He didn't look tired; if anything, the "Silent Resonance" breathing had kept his heart rate steady and his mind sharp. He looked at the irritated Dwarf, then slowly inclined his head in a respectful, shallow bow.

"You are right, Honorable Krag," Ren said, his voice carrying that new, metallic weight. "The Stone does stand. And a bridge must be more than just a path; it must be a foundation."

Ren reached back and efficiently sheathed his twin seaxes. The silver-steel blades vanished into their scabbards with a soft, final *snick*. 

Then, Ren did something that made the entire High-Hall gasp. 

He didn't reach for another blade. He reached for the silver ring on his finger—the **[Ring of Holding]**. 

There was a sudden, violent distortion in the air—a ripple of localized gravity that made the nearby torches flicker. With a grunt of effort, Ren pulled two massive objects from the ring's interior space.

The first was a heavy infra-structure shield, a slab of reinforced steel and enchanted oak that was nearly five feet tall and three feet wide. The second was a heavy-infantry pike, a six-foot shaft of tempered iron tipped with a broad, leaf-shaped blade that shimmered with a faint, blue aura.

The weapons seemed impossibly huge in Ren's seven-year-old hands. The shield was taller than he was, and the pike looked like it weighed more than his entire body. The visual was absurd—a small boy in a simple tunic, holding gear designed for a front-line legionnaire.

"Round Two?" Ren asked, his voice calm.

A collective murmur of shock rippled through the Council of Stone. Even the King's flagon of ale stopped halfway to his mouth. No one had expected the "Little Express" to carry heavy ordinance, let alone have the strength to wield it.

Ren didn't wait for a reply. He sank his center of gravity, but this time, he didn't look for lightness. He looked for mass. 

He used his **[Intelligence]** and his **[Mana Manipulation]** to execute a complex technique he had been practicing in the quiet hours of their mountain camps. He didn't just hold the shield; he 'fused' his resonance with it. He used his mana to create a series of structural 'anchors' between his boots and the obsidian floor, effectively turning his small frame into a tripod for the massive weight.

He wasn't holding the pike with his muscles alone; he was using **[Leverage]** and **[Balance Mastery]** to find the exact center of gravity where the weapon became an extension of his own skeleton. It was a masterpiece of technical application—a seven-year-old using the laws of physics and the flow of mana to bypass the limitations of his age.

"The 'Iron Stand'," Ren whispered to himself.

Krag stared at the Pike and Shield, his eyes wide. The disgust was gone, replaced by a sharp, professional wariness. He knew the weight of that gear. He knew the strength required to keep it steady. And Ren was keeping it as steady as the mountain itself.

"So be it," Krag growled, his hand tightening on his own shield. "If you want to be an anvil, boy... I shall be the hammer."

The King slammed his flagon onto the table. "Greet him again, Krag! Show him the weight of the Marrow!"

This time, Krag didn't just lunge. He roared, his armor flaring with a deep, earthen mana-glow. He used a technique called **[Crush of the Deep]**, his weight seemingly doubling as he charged across the arena. Each step cracked the obsidian tiles beneath his feet. He hit Ren's shield with the force of a falling boulder.

The collision was deafening. A shockwave of air and mana blew outward, extinguishing several of the nearby torches.

Ren didn't fly backward. He didn't even slide. 

He stood his ground, his bare feet digging into the stone, his teeth gritted in a snarl of pure effort. He felt the 'resonance' of the impact travel through the shield, through his arms, and down into the floor. He didn't try to absorb the blow; he 'channeled' it. He let the mountain take the force that Krag had intended for his body.

The "Little Express" had vanished. In his place was a "stationary storm"—a small, unyielding point of silver-blue light that refused to be moved.

Krag pulled back, his eyes narrowing in shock. He had never felt a surface-born stand so solid. It was like hitting the foundation-stone itself.

Before Krag could reset for a second strike, Ren's pike whipped out. It wasn't a fast, flicking strike like his seaxes; it was a heavy, deliberate thrust that carried the momentum of his consolidated mass. The pike-tip struck the center of Krag's shield with a sound like a tolling bell.

Krag was forced back half a step, his boots screeching against the obsidian.

"I am a Courier," Ren said, his voice echoing through the stunned hall. "I carry the weight of a city. I carry the hope of the Guard. And I carry the iron of those who came before me. Do you still think I fight like a fairy, Honorable Krag?"

The King exploded in a roar of raw, genuine pride—the kind of sound a Dwarf only made when he recognized a peer. He wasn't laughing at the boy anymore; he was cheering for the warrior.

"He stands!" the King bellowed, his fist slamming against his chest. "By the Stone, the boy stands!"

Ren centered his pike, his focus absolute. His mana-spirit was no longer a flicker; it was a steady, burning flame of industrial blue. He felt the 'resonance' of his oversized gear settling into his bones, the 'Iron Stand' becoming a part of his very essence.
