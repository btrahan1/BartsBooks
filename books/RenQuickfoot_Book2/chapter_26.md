# Book 2: Chapter 26: The Stone's Respect

What followed was something never before seen by the Stone-Kin.

For thirty minutes, the High-Hall of the Iron Mountains was transformed into a laboratory of kinetic resonance. The audience of thousands—Dwarven lords in silver-etched mail, soot-stained smiths from the lower districts, and even the stern Council of Stone—stood in a silence so thick it felt like the mountain itself was holding its breath. They were watching a child do the impossible: survive, and eventually match, the unyielding fury of a Master of the Earth-Stance.

Ren's fighting style was a revelation, a fluid, beautiful, and terrifying marriage of opposites. One moment, he was the Wind—a silver-blue blur that utilized the 'Flex Philosophy' to wrap around Krag's explosive charges. He would twist mid-air, his white tunic fluttering like a banner, his bare feet barely touching the obsidian floor before he was gone again. He was too fast to be pinned down, a phantom that navigated the spaces between Krag's strikes with the precision of a master courier.

But then, just as Krag would adjust for the evasion, Ren would become the Stone. 

He would drop his center of gravity, his resonance anchoring him to the marrow of the mountain, and meet Krag's massive shield-strikes with his own oversized infantries gear. The 'Iron Stand' wasn't just a defensive posture; it was a defiant statement. Ren didn't just absorb the blows; he 'echoed' them. He used his **[Mana Manipulation]** to catch the frequency of Krag's impact and feed it back into the floor, creating shockwaves that cracked the obsidian tiles in perfect geometric patterns.

It wasn't a one-sided struggle by any means. Ren was sent flying multiple times—thrown backward by the sheer kinetic mass of a Dwarf who weighed four times as much as he did. He would hit the floor, skidding for twenty feet, but he wouldn't break. He would roll with the momentum, his **[Balance Mastery]** turning a potential disaster into a graceful recovery, his feet finding purchase even as he was still sliding.

But Krag was also sent flying. 

In a moment that made the King leap from his emerald throne, Ren timed a thrust of his heavy pike perfectly. He didn't just push; he 'leveraged' Krag's own momentum against him. As Krag charged with the **[Crush of the Deep]**, Ren planted the butt of his pike into a micro-fracture in the obsidian, using the six-foot shaft as a fulcrum. He caught the edge of Krag's shield and, with a surge of mana-reinforced strength, redirected the Dwarf's charge upward.

Krag, the immovable object, was launched three feet into the air, spinning like a massive, armored top before landing with a bone-jarring *thud* ten feet away.

The disdain that had filled the High-Hall only thirty minutes ago had vanished, replaced by a profound, professional respect. The Stone-Kin valued weight, but they also valued the 'Marrow'—the internal strength that allowed a structure to keep its integrity under impossible pressure. And Ren was demonstrating more 'marrow' than any surface-dweller they had ever encountered.

After fifteen minutes, the sparring had stopped being a test and had become a conversation. They were no longer 'greeting' each other; they were exploring the limits of their respective philosophies. Krag was no longer lunging with the intent to crush; he was testing Ren's foundations, searching for the fracture-points in the boy's 'Iron Stand'. Ren, in turn, was learning the rhythm of the Dwarf's gravity, finding the exact micro-seconds of stillness between Krag's explosive bursts of energy.

The air in the arena was hot and thick, saturated with the smell of iron, sweat, and ionized mana. Ren's white tunic was stained with soot and blood where he had skidded across the tiles, but his eyes remained twin beacons of silver-blue light. He was beyond exhaustion, operating on a level of pure, instinctual flow where his **[Intelligence]** and **[Perception]** were no longer separate from his muscles.

Finally, at the thirty-minute mark, the sparring reached its natural conclusion. 

Krag stopped. He didn't drop his shield, but he didn't raise it either. He stood in the center of the Arena of Foundation, his chest heaving like a freight train, his face a vivid, healthy red. Sweat poured from his thick beard, dripping onto the cracked obsidian. He looked at Ren, his slate-grey eyes no longer hard with skepticism, but bright with a hunter's respect.

"He'll do, my King," Krag rumbled, his voice a ragged, breathless sound that carried to the furthest corners of the hall. He looked at the boy, then slowly, with a deliberate, stiff-backed motion, he lowered his shield to the floor and pounded his fist against his chest—the Dwarven salute of an equal. "By the Heart of the Mountain... he'll do."

Ren stood opposite him, his pike held steady, his shield angled perfectly. He didn't collapse. He waited until Krag had finished his salute, then he slowly retracted his weapons into the **[Ring of Holding]**. The heavy gear vanished with a soft ripple in the air, leaving Ren standing small and solitary once again.

He bowed to Krag—not a shallow, diplomatic bow this time, but a deep, respectful acknowledgment of a master. Then, he turned to the emerald throne.

The King of the Iron Mountains was silent for a long moment. He looked at the cracked obsidian of the arena, then at the exhausted Krag, and finally at Ren. The King's face, usually set in a permanent scowl of royal indifference, had softened into something resembling a grim, appreciative smile.

"Thirty minutes," the King whispered, a sound that carried a weight of its own. "Thirty minutes against the Stone-Guard, and the Pebble still stands. Silverhaven may be a city of wood and light, but it seems they have found some iron in their marrow after all."

The King stood up, his massive frame casting a long shadow over the Council of Stone. 

"Ren Ito," the King's voice boomed through the High-Hall. "You came here as a Courier, a 'sprout' carrying the words of those who live under the sun. But you have greeted the mountain according to the old ways. You have shown us that the 'Bridge' is not just a path, but a foundation."

The King gestured to the Council. "By the laws of the Stone-Kin, I acknowledge you. You are no longer just a messenger; you are an **[Acknowledged Messenger]** of the Iron Mountains. You shall have your audience. You shall speak of the Blight, and we shall listen with the attention that iron deserves."

A thunderous sound erupted—not the industrial roar of the forges, but the rhythmic pounding of fists against shields and tables. It was the Stone-Kin's applause, a rhythmic, tectonic vibration that welcomed Ren into their society.

Ren felt the resonance of it washing over him, a warm, heavy acknowledgement that made the cold metal of his silver ring feel like it belonged. He was still seven years old. He was still a courier. But as he stood in the center of the High-Hall, surrounded by the respect of the mountain, Ren realized that the 'Silverhaven Express' had just completed its most important delivery.

He had delivered the truth of his own worth. 

"I am ready to speak, Your Majesty," Ren said, his voice steady even as his legs began to tremble with the release of the adrenaline. "Whenever the mountain is ready to hear."
