# Book 2: Chapter 27: The Shadow's Silhouette

The High-Hall of the Iron Mountains felt different now. The pressurized silence that had initially greeted Ren was gone, replaced by a low, simmering hum of energy. The Dwarven dignitaries weren't just watching him; they were *studying* him. The cracked obsidian in the center of the hall, still vibrating from the aftershocks of the spar, served as a jagged monument to the fact that the surface-dweller was no longer an outsider. He was a variable that had been calculated and found to have significant mass.

Ren stood before the emerald throne, the sweat from his 30-minute duel with Krag cooling against his skin. He had retracted his heavy gear, and he stood small once more, his simple tunic stained with the dust of the mountain. But his posture remained that of the "Iron Stand"—grounded, balanced, and ready.

The King of the Iron Mountains gestured for a servant to bring Ren a stool. It was a simple, stone seat, but the gesture was monumental. For the first time in memory, a surface-born child was being invited to sit in the presence of the Council of Stone.

"Sit, Acknowledged Messenger," the King rumbled, his voice now devoid of any mock-disdain. "You have earned your breath. Now, use it to tell us what Master Goram and your Council of Five are so worried about. Tell us of this 'Blight'."

Ren sat, his hands resting on his knees. He didn't jump into a dramatic tale; he spoke with the clinical precision of a courier reporting a road hazard.

"From what little I know, Your Majesty, it is a disease," Ren began, his voice clear and steady. "But not a disease of the flesh like a fever. It is a corruption of the essence—a rot that starts with the very foundation of the living world. It begins with the plants."

He paused, letting the words sink in. The Council of Stone leaned forward, their faces illuminated by the amber glow of the quartz veins.

"The soil changes first," Ren continued, his **[Sense Resonance]** helping him find the right frequency for his words. "The mana-signatures of the roots become... discordant. They begin to wither, but not from lack of water. They turn black, a slick, oily darkness that smells of cold iron and old death. And once the plants are taken, the corruption moves to the wildlife."

"Wildlife?" one of the elder Councilors asked, his voice sharp with technical interest. "A fever of the beasts?"

"No, Councilor," Ren replied. "It is a corruption of the mind. Animals eat the infected plants, or they drink from the streams that carry the rot. At first, they simply stop acting like themselves. Normally docile creatures—the grass-browsers and the forest-fowl become aggressive. They forget their fear of predators. They forget their fear of *man*."

Ren's mind flickered back to the Grass-Stalker in the high meadows, the predator that had fought with a desperate, suicidal intensity.

"And the aggressive creatures?" Ren asked, his eyes meeting the King's. "They become feral. They become monsters that attack with no thought for their own safety. They don't fight to eat, Your Majesty. They fight to destroy. I have seen nature turn its back on its own balance, transformed into a weapon of pure, unthinking rage."

The King took a slow pull from his flagon, his gaze fixed on the glowing crystals of the ceiling. He wasn't looking at Ren; he was looking into the deep memory of the mountain.

"A corruption of the essence," the King muttered, the name sounding like a technical diagnosis. "We have seen similar things in the deep-veins, but that was ages ago—when the Shadow-Gaunts first crawled from the void. You are saying this rot is on the surface?"

"It is, Your Majesty," Ren said, leaning forward. "And I suspect you have already noticed similar occurrences on the 'Skin' of your mountain. The high-pastures, the vents that breathe the surface-air... have you not seen the moss turning black? Have you not heard of the mountain-cats attacking your scouts without provocation?"

The silence that followed was heavy with the weight of unsaid truths. The King's hand tightened on the arm of the emerald throne, the translucent stone flaring with a deep, defensive green.

"The Skin-Walkers have reported... anomalies," the King admitted, his voice low. "Black-rot in the lichen-beds. Goats jumping from cliffs for no reason other than to strike at a patrol below. We thought it was just the mountain’s mood—the stones shifting, the seasons being difficult. But if this is a disease..."

"It is a silhouette of something larger," Ren said, playing the final card of his briefing. "Silverhaven is the first barrier, Your Majesty. But if the woods fall, the mountain is next. This corruption doesn't care about the height of your walls or the strength of your iron. It eats the life that sustains the stone. If the surface dies, what happens to the vents that bring you air? What happens to the water that runs through your deep-marrow?"

The Council began to murmur, a sound like a landslide in the distance. They were no longer skeptical; they were analyzing the logistical implications. They were Dwarves, after all. They didn't fear monsters; they feared structural failure.

Ren watched them, his **[Intelligence]** mapping the flow of the room. He saw the shift from 'disdain' to 'defensive planning'. He had successfully delivered the message, but more importantly, he had delivered the *threat* in a language the Stone-Kin could understand.

"You speak of the 'Iron Accord' as a necessity," the King noted, his gaze returning to Ren. "You want us to open the Under-Roads. You want us to commit our Stone-Guard to the surface-borders. You are asking for a lot from a people who prefer the safety of the basalt."

"I am asking you to protect your foundations, Your Majesty," Ren replied. "Silverhaven doesn't want your charity. We want your partnership. We have the light of alchemy, the speed of the Express, and the wisdom of the High-Guard. You have the iron, the resonance of the marrow, and the strength of the earth. Together, we can find the source of this rot before it reaches your heart."

The King stood up, his massive frame radiating a sense of absolute authority. The audience was over, but the deliberation was just beginning.

"You have given us much to chew on, Acknowledged Messenger," the King said. "We shall consult with the Council of Stone and the Master-Smiths. We shall verify your 'silhouette' with our own reports from the Skin-Walkers."

He looked at Thrain and Balin, who were still standing at the edge of the dais, their faces etched with a mix of awe and relief.

"Take the boy to the High-Passage Guest-Cells," the King commanded. "Treat him as a guest of the throne. Let him have meat, ale, and all the rest he requires. He has earned it."

Ren stood and bowed deeply—the bow of an equal, not a servant. 

"One more thing, Your Majesty," Ren said, as he prepared to follow the scouts.

"Speak," the King grunted.

"The rot isn't just a disease," Ren said, his voice dropping to a whisper that somehow carried through the entire hall. "It has a frequency. It has a will. It is choosing its targets. And I think it knows I am here."

The King's eyes widened, a flicker of genuine alarm crossing his granite features. He didn't answer, but the look he gave Ren was one of deep, unsettling recognition.

Ren walked out of the High-Hall, his steps tired but steady. He had delivered the word. He had shown the iron. Now, he just had to survive the silence that was coming. (Word count: 1307)
