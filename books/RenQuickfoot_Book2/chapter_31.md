# Book 2: Chapter 31: The Forest's Welcome

The descent from the Iron Mountains was a transition of color and density. The stark, monochromatic grey of the basalt pillars and the soot-stained obsidian slowly gave way to the muted, dying greens of the high mountain scrub. Ren moved with a speed that felt different now—less like a frantic dash and more like a controlled avalanche. Every time his boots struck the stone, he utilized his new **[Kinetic Redirection]**, turning the downward impetus of the mountain into a flat, horizontal velocity that blurred the world around him.

He was a hundred miles from the heart of the mountain when his **[Sense Resonance]** picked up a discordance that made his teeth ache. It wasn't the rhythmic, industrial hum of the Stone-Kin, nor was it the clean, rustling song of a healthy forest. It was a jagged, static-filled scream—the sound of the Blight.

As he reached the place where the mountain ended and the Great Forest began, the trees didn't offer a welcoming shade. Instead, they stood like skeletal sentinels, their leaves withered and black, their trunks covered in that slick, oily rot he had described to the King. The air was thick with the scent of damp earth and something metallic, a smell that triggered an instinctual "fight-or-flight" response in Ren's marrow.

Then, he saw him.

Master Loamwalker was standing in a clearing of twisted grey-wood, his longbow humming with the release of an arrow every two seconds. But he wasn't exactly waiting for Ren. He was surrounded.

A pack of blighted creatures—distorted versions of the Grass-Stalkers and lean, feral wolves—were circling the Ranger. They moved with a jerky, unnatural cadence, their eyes glowing with a sickly, void-like purple. They didn't growl; they made a sound like dry branches snapping. Normally, Loamwalker would have picked them off before they even scented him, but these creatures were coming from every direction at once, their numbers and aggression seemingly fueled by the very corruption that had taken the trees.

Ren didn't hesitate. He didn't even slow down.

With a mental command, he accessed his **[Ring of Holding]**. In a ripple of gravity and mana, the heavy infantry pike and the slab-like shield materialized in his hands. He didn't need to sink his center of gravity; the momentum of his descent already provided the necessary power.

"The Iron Stand'!" Ren yelled, a sound that carried the weight of the mountain.

He launched himself from a basalt shelf, a streak of white and silver-blue light. He didn't just land; he 'arrived' in the center of the clearing, his shield-edge striking a blighted wolf with the force of a falling anvil. The creature was flattened instantly, its distorted bones snapping like glass.

One of the Grass-Stalkers, its hide a mangled mess of black-rot and matted fur, prepared to pounce on Loamwalker's exposed flank. Ren's pike whipped out, the six-foot shaft moving with a precision that his **[Blade Fluidity]** bonus made look effortless. He didn't just thrust; he 'leveraged' the weapon through the creature's chest, the leaf-shaped point exiting the other side in a spray of dark, viscous ichor.

Ren pivoted on his bare feet, his shield coming up to catch the charge of another feral beast. The impact was massive, but Ren didn't move. He stood his ground, his resonance anchored to the forest floor as firmly as it had been to the obsidian of the High-Hall. 

He bashed the creature aside, the heavy steel of the shield edge cracking its skull, and stepped into the space next to Loamwalker. He lowered the pike to a defensive horizontal, the blue aura of the weapon casting a protective circle around them.

The clearing went silent for a micro-second, the remaining creatures hushing their snaps and clicks as they processed the arrival of this new, heavy variable.

Ren turned his head slightly, giving the Ranger a wide, adrenaline-fueled grin. He could feel the **[Resonant Rapport]** bonus kicking in—a subtle, harmonic alignment that made him feel Loamwalker's next move before the Elf even made it.

"Miss me much, Master?" Ren asked, his voice steady despite the hammer-pulse of his heart.

Loamwalker didn't stop moving. He released one more arrow, pinning a leaping Grass-Stalker to a tree, before lowering his bow. He looked at Ren—at the massive pike, the heavy shield, and the way the boy's posture had settled into something immovable. The Elven Ranger's usual calm, observant eyes widened in a look of profound, genuine shock.

"Ren?" Loamwalker whispered, his voice catching on the name. He looked at the oversized gear, then at the silver-blue glow of Ren's mana-signature, which was no longer a flicker but a steady, burning flame. "By the roots of the First Tree... what did the Stone-Kin do to you?"

"They showed me the marrow," Ren replied, his eyes scanning the surrounding trees. "And they gave me a few things to carry. We have the Iron Accord, Master. But first, we have a few 'anomalies' to clear out."

The blighted pack, sensing the shift in the battle's resonance, didn't retreat. They didn't care about the iron or the light. These were creatures that had lost the ability to calculate risk. One of them, a wolf that had been warped until its spine carried jagged bone-spurs, lunged from the shadows of a decaying oak.

Ren didn't dodge. He sank his weight, his **[Anchored Foundation]** locking his feet into the mulch-heavy soil. He caught the wolf's throat on the edge of his shield, then twisted, using the move he'd practiced against Krag to redirect its own weight into the ground. There was a sickening *crunch* as the creature's momentum shattered its own reinforced ribs against the unyielding steel.

"Stay close, Ren!" Loamwalker commanded, his voice regaining its edge. "The Blight in this sector is deeper than when we left. They don't just hunt now; they swarm. They’re sensitive to your resonance—that light is like a beacon to them!"

"Then let them come to the light," Ren said, his pike whipping out to catch a second wolf mid-air. 

He moved forward, his shield held high, his pike-point searching for the next target. He wasn't just a courier anymore. He was the vanguard. He realized that the 'Silverhaven Express' didn't just deliver messages—it delivered consequences. His every move was a physical rejection of the corruption, the blue aura of his weapons leaves a trailing phosphor in the stagnant air.

Loamwalker moved in perfect sync with him, the Ranger's arrows providing the 'Wind' that complimented Ren's 'Stone'. It was a beautiful, terrifying display of two different philosophies merged into a single, lethal cadence. 

The forest was about to learn that the boy who entered the mountain was not the boy who had come out. Ren Ito had brought the weight of the Iron Mountains back with him, and he was just getting started. (Word count: 1212)
