# Book 2: Chapter 32: The Burden of Evidence

The aftermath of the clearing battle was a tableau of silence and decay. Around Ren and Loamwalker, the "mountain of corpses"—distorted, oily-furred shapes that had once been proud forest-predators—lay scattered across the soot-stained mulch. The air was thick with a heavy, metallic ozone, the lingering resonance of Ren's pike-strikes and the Ranger's mana-infused arrows.

Ren stood in the center of the carnage, his heavy shield resting on the ground, the tip of his pike still shimmering with a fading blue light. He was breathing heavily, his heart-rate elevated not just from the physical effort, but from the realization of how close the forest was to complete structural failure. The "Shadow's Silhouette" wasn't just a distant threat anymore; it was here, spilling its dark, discordant blood into the roots of the world.

Master Loamwalker stepped over the carcass of a blighted wolf, his eyes scanning the remains with a clinical, diagnostic intensity. He didn't look victorious; he looked like a doctor who had just discovered the first signs of a terminal plague.

"Ren," Loamwalker said, his voice a low, somber vibration. "We cannot leave this place with just a story. The Council of Five and the High-Guard will need more than the word of a courier and a scout. They will need proof of the biological corruption."

He pointed to one of the Grass-Stalkers that Ren had impaled. The creature's hide was almost entirely black, its eyes frozen in a state of void-like obsidian.

"We need a sample," Loamwalker continued. "A complete specimen. Can you fit one of these corpses into the ring?"

Ren looked at the distorted beast. It was nearly five feet long, a mass of muscle and matted fur that carried a palpable aura of wrongness. He looked at his **[Ring of Holding]**, the silver band feeling momentarily heavy on his finger.

"I can try, Master," Ren said. 

He stepped toward the corpse, his bare feet sinking into the damp earth. He placed his hand on the cold, oily hide of the blighted stalker. He took a deep breath, centered his mana, and "willed" the object into the ring's interior space.

Usually, the **[Ring of Holding]** handled its contents with a seamless, weightless efficiency. Items simply transitioned from the physical world into the localized gravity of the ring's pocket-dimension. But as the blighted corpse vanished in a ripple of blue-violet light, Ren felt something he had never experienced before.

It was a "drag."

A sudden, sharp tension pulled at his hand, a metaphysical weight that seemed to bypass the ring's enchantments. It wasn't that the physical weight of the corpse had transferred to him; it was as if the *corruption* itself was resisting the storage. The discordant resonance of the Blight was fighting against the order of the ring's space.

Ren grunted, his knees nearly buckling as the sensation traveled up his arm and settled into his chest. It felt like he was suddenly carrying a leaden lead, not in his muscles, but in his very soul.

"I... I have it," Ren gasped, his hand trembling as he pulled it back. 

He looked at the ring. It was still silver, still smooth, but his **[Sense Resonance]** told him it was no longer 'clean'. It was carrying a shadow now, a localized pocket of distortion that felt like a cold, heavy stone in his pocket.

"The ring took care of much of the weight," Ren said, wiping a bead of sweat from his forehead. "But I can still feel it. It’s like carrying a pocket of winter, Master. It’s... it’s heavy."

Loamwalker's expression softened into one of deep, protective concern. He knew the cost of carrying such things. He knew that Ren was effectively acting as a containment vessel for a piece of the world's rot.

"It will be a burden, Ren," Loamwalker said. "The corruption will try to 'speak' to your resonance. You must keep your [Iron Stand] active, even while you run. Use your foundations to keep your mind separate from the weight."

Ren nodded, his jaw set in a line of grim determination. He adjusted his boots, shifted the straps of his tunic, and took a final, steadying breath. He felt the Level 24 surge in his **[Stamina]** kicking in, providing the resilient core he needed to manage the drag.

"I can manage, Master," Ren said, his voice regaining its metallic edge. "Let us make haste! The Iron Accord is useless if it’s delivered to a city that’s already been taken. I have a message to deliver, and I intend to deliver it at speed!"

Loamwalker nodded, a flicker of pride crossing his features. "Then run, Silverhaven Express. I will provide the wind. You provide the path."

Without another word, Ren dissolved into a starting crouch. He didn't just push off; he 'exploded' from the clearing. 

utilizing his new **[Kinetic Redirection]**, he turned every impact with the decaying forest floor into a horizontal burst of mana-reinforced speed. He moved with a cadence that was different from his journey to the mountain—it was heavier, more deliberate, but somehow even more persistent. 

Behind him, Loamwalker moved with the liquid grace of an Elven Ranger, his feet barely touching the ground as he kept pace with the boy. Together, they were a blur of motion through the grey, skeletal trees.

The forest was different now. The silence was absolute, broken only by the rhythmic thud of their footsteps and the occasional, distant scream of a blighted creature. The green of the woods had been replaced by a spectrum of ash and charcoal, the very mana of the soil feeling 'thin' and exhausted.

Ren could feel the "Shadow's Silhouette" watching them. It was a pressure on his back, a directed awareness that seemed to follow him through the brush. He realized that the System's warning hadn't been an exaggeration. He was no longer just a courier; he was a target. He was carrying the treaty that could unite the worlds, and he was carrying the evidence that could prove the threat.

To the Shadow, Ren Ito was the most dangerous variable in the entire forest.

"Faster!" Loamwalker urged, his voice a sharp command that cut through the whistling wind. "The sun is setting, and the Maw is not the only place where the shadows grow long!"

Ren didn't reply. He simply shifted into his highest gear, his mana-spirit burning with a fierce, industrial-blue light. He ignored the cold, leaden drag in his ring, focusing instead on the frequency of the path ahead. He was the Express. He was the courier. And he was going home. (Word count: 1146)
