## Chapter 12: Mingle

Arthur moved through the crowd not like a guest, but like a scanner.

He stood near a pillar, holding a glass of sparkling water he had no intention of drinking, and observed. The room was a sea of jewels, silk, and power. But Arthur saw the cracks.

A Duke from Bavaria approached him, led by curiosity about the stranger in the midnight blue tuxedo.

"I don't believe we've met," the Duke said, puffing on a cigar that violated at least three fire codes. "Von Hapsburg."

Arthur didn't shake the hand immediately. He looked at the Duke’s wrist.

"The winding stem on your Breguet is loose," Arthur said calmly. "You over-wind it every morning at 7:00 AM because you are anxious about the market opening. That anxiety is stripping the thread."

The Duke froze. He stared at his watch. He stared at Arthur.

"How... who are you?"

"Arthur Penhaligon. Consultant."

A famous opera singer drifted by, complaining of a scratchy throat. Arthur leaned in slightly.

"It is not a virus, madam," he said softly. "It is the HVAC system in your penthouse. The filtration unit has a micro-fissure in the HEPA seal. You are inhaling construction dust from the renovation next door. Check the intake vent in the master bedroom."

She gaped at him. "My... my neighbor *is* renovating. How did you know?"

"I heard the particulate in your respiratory friction," Arthur lied smoothly. It was actually the faint trace of drywall dust on her velvet wrap, but the lie felt more mystical.

By the time the main course was served, a small orbit had formed around Arthur. They whispered. They pointed. *Is he a psychic? A spy? A doctor?*

"He told Count Orlov that his vintage Ferrari is misfiring because of ethanol separation in the fuel line, not the spark plugs!" one woman hissed. "And he hasn't even seen the car!"

Arthur found it amusing. Humans were just machines with messy wiring. If you looked at the inputs (dust, stress, bad habits), the outputs (coughs, loose watches, stalled cars) were inevitable.

He caught Vance’s eye across the room. Vance raised his glass, suppressing a laugh. Arthur offered a tiny, microscopic shrug. *You asked me to mingle.*

Then, a deep, resonant sound filled the hall.

*GONNNNNNG.*

It was a large brass gong, struck by a mallet. The vibration was slightly flat—Arthur noted the gong needed to be annealed—but it did the job. The room fell silent.

Vance stepped onto the dais. The lights dimmed, leaving only a spotlight on the glass case.

"Ladies and Gentlemen," Vance began, his voice smooth as oil. "We live in an age of disposable miracles. We throw away our phones when the screen cracks. We discard our history when it gathers dust."

He walked around the case.

"But tonight, I want to show you something that cannot be thrown away. A machine that has slept for a century. A machine that has been woken up."

He gestured to Arthur.

"Mr. Penhaligon? If you would do the honors."

Arthur stepped out of the crowd. The whispers intensified. *That’s him. The Seer.*

Arthur walked up the steps to the dais. He didn't smile. He didn't bow. He approached the machine with the gravity of a surgeon.

He reached into his pocket and produced a key—an ornate, brass key that he had polished until it shone.

He inserted the key into the automaton’s back.

*Click.*

The sound echoed in the silent hall.

He wound it. One. Two. Three. Four. Five.

The spring tensioned. The potential energy built up, coiled and ready.

Arthur stepped back. He reached inside the case and flipped the start lever.

The room held its breath.

The boy moved.

His head turned slightly to the left. His eyes blinked. His chest rose and fell, mimicking a breath.

He dipped the quill into the inkwell. He lifted it. He paused, as if thinking.

Then, the hand descended.

*Scritch. Scratch.*

The sound of the quill on parchment was amplified by the silence. The cams rotated. The levers pushed and pulled. The boy wrote.

Arthur watched the ink flow. Perfect. Consistent. No blotches.

The boy finished the first line. He dipped the pen again. He wrote the second line.

He stopped. He lifted the pen. He turned his head back to center. And he smiled—a mechanical quirk of the jaw that Arthur had lubricated with a drop of clock oil.

Vance stepped forward. He opened the case and picked up the parchment. He held it up to the light.

"Read it!" someone shouted from the back.

Vance smiled.

"It says..." Vance read aloud.

*"I am not broken. I was merely waiting."*

He lowered the paper.

"And below that..."

Vance looked at Arthur.

*"Restored by Penhaligon."*

The room exploded. Applause. Gasps. People standing on chairs to see.

Arthur stood next to the machine. He felt a flush of pride—not for himself, but for the boy. The boy had performed. The boy was alive.

Vance leaned in close to Arthur, under the roar of the applause.

"I think, Arthur," Vance shouted, "that your waiting list just got significantly longer."

Arthur looked at the crowd. He saw the Duke checking his watch. He saw the opera singer touching her throat. He saw a room full of broken things that wanted to be fixed.

"I'm going to need more tea," Arthur said. "And a bigger sorting tray."
