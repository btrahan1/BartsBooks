## Chapter 13: Sabotage

The applause was a wave of sound, a rhythmic, ordered vibration that Arthur found tolerable. He was just reaching for his tea, satisfied that the mechanism had performed within its specified tolerances, when the pattern broke.

It wasn't a mechanical failure. It was a biological one.

A scream tore through the ballroom. It was high, discordant, and shattered the atmosphere like a hammer hitting a pane of glass.

The applause died instantly. The silence that followed was not the respectful silence of the reveal; it was the terrified silence of the herd.

"Help! Someone help! He's not breathing!"

The voice came from the front row, directly to the left of the dais.

Arthur didn't freeze. Freezing was a waste of time. He turned his head, locking onto the source of the disturbance.

Duke von Hapsburg, the man with the over-wound Breguet watch, was lying on the marble floor. His face was a color not found in any healthy Pantone chart—a sort of mottled, hypoxic purple. His cigar lay smoldering on a priceless Persian runner.

Chaos erupted. People pushed back. Chairs scraped. A waiter dropped a tray of champagne flutes—*crash, tinkle, splash*.

"Entropy," Arthur hissed under his breath. "I detest entropy."

Vance grabbed Arthur’s arm. His face had lost its color. "Arthur, stay back. I’ll call security."

Arthur shook him off. He didn't step back. He stepped down.

He moved through the panicked crowd not like a hero, but like a mechanic walking toward a smoking engine block. The crowd, recognizing the "Seer," parted instinctively.

Arthur reached the body. A woman in emerald silk was kneeling beside the Duke, sobbing.

"Don't touch him!" Arthur ordered. His voice wasn't loud, but it had the absolute authority of a man who knows exactly which wire to cut.

The woman froze.

Arthur knelt on one knee. He didn't check for a pulse immediately. He checked the environment. The cigar. The spilled wine. The angle of the fall.

Then, he looked at the Duke.

"Subject is non-responsive," Arthur murmured.

He peeled back one of the Duke's eyelids. The pupil was blown wide, unreactive to the chandelier's glare.

Arthur placed two fingers on the carotid artery. Not to feel for life—he already knew the machine had stopped—but to feel the *temperature*.

"System shutdown complete," Arthur said flatly. "Cooling cycle has not yet begun."

"Is he... is he having a heart attack?" the woman stammered.

Arthur leaned in closer. He sniffed. No almonds (cyanide). No alcohol overdose. But there was something else. A faint, metallic scent. Ozone? No. *Electricity?*

He looked at the Duke’s neck, just above the starch of his collar.

There, almost invisible against the flushed skin, was a tiny red dot. A puncture wound. No larger than the tip of a sewing needle.

Arthur reached into his tuxedo pocket and pulled out his pocket magnifier. He leaned down, hovering inches from the flesh.

He saw it. A minute burn mark around the puncture. And something else. A glint of metal buried deep in the tissue.

Arthur stood up slowly. He pulled a handkerchief from his pocket and wiped his hands, though he hadn't touched anything messy.

Vance was at his side now. "Arthur? What is it? A stroke?"

Arthur looked at Vance. Then he looked at the room—at the hundreds of wealthy, terrified faces.

"This was not a natural failure, Mr. Vance," Arthur said, his voice clipped and cold. "The visual inspection reveals a foreign object intrusion in the cervical vertebrae. Specifically, a high-velocity micro-projectile."

"In English, Arthur!"

Arthur adjusted his cuffs.

"He wasn't sick, Julian. He was decommissioned."

Arthur pointed to the tiny spot on the Duke's neck.

"Sabotage," Arthur declared. "Or, as the police prefer to call it... Murder."

He turned to the room, his eyes scanning the crowd, looking for the one component that didn't fit the machine.

"Nobody leaves," Arthur announced. "And for the love of God, nobody touch the shattered glass. That is evidence."
