# Chapter 5: The Command Center

The technician hit a final key. A profound, electric silence fell over
the hangar.

And then, a calm, synthetic, and utterly dead voice filled the vast,
subterranean space. It was a text-to-speech program, translating the
pure, unfiltered thoughts of Marcus "Rook" Riley from a world away.

"**CARTER,**" the synthetic voice began, each word a cold, hard,
perfectly spaced block of sound. "**THE** '**CURE**'**. I WANT IT.
NOW.**"

There was a pause, a moment of pure, silent, electronic thought.

"**AND TELL ROSTOVA... IF SHE EVER TRIES TO CHANGE MY DATA-BURST
FREQUENCY AGAIN... I WILL SEND HER BACK A PICTURE OF A GOAT. FOR A WEEK.
DO WE HAVE AN UNDERSTANDING?**"

The final, deadpan threat about the goat hung in the air, a perfect,
calculated insult that was so bizarre and so specific that everyone in
the room knew it was not a joke. Director Rostova's face went from pale
to a deep, blotchy, furious red. She had just been threatened with
digital livestock by her own black-ops asset from another dimension.

Dr. Carter, however, looked like he had just been handed a miracle. "He
wants the cure," he breathed, his voice full of a desperate, relieved
hope. "He's asking for help. He's engaging! It's working!"

But before Rostova could start shouting orders or Carter could start
preparing his therapeutic protocols, Vance stepped forward. His calm,
quiet authority instantly silenced the room.

"Give me the microphone," he said to the lead technician. "How long will
the transmission take to go through?"

The technicians were scrambling. "He's opened a real-time text channel,
sir, but it's not simultaneous. There will be... a thirty-second delay
for him to receive, and another thirty for his response. More or less."

"Fine," Vance said. He took the microphone, his knuckles white. This was
the most important part of the entire operation. This was the moment the
real mission began. He had to tell the truth. The terrible, liberating,
and unbelievably dangerous truth.

He took a breath. "Rook," he began, his voice calm, steady, and
broadcast across the void. "This is Vance. Now, don't get pissed... but
I know you will anyway. The mission you were given was a lie. Your
sister's message was real, but the rest was a fabrication to get you
through the door."

He paused, letting the thirty-second delay tick by, knowing that on
another world, a man was reading these words, his rage likely building
to a supernova.

"But... here's the truth. You already have the cure."

Another thirty-second pause. The hangar was so quiet you could hear the
hum of the portal.

"Going through the portal *is* the cure," Vance continued, laying the
entire gamble bare. "Dr. Carter's theory was that your specific, complex
PTSD was not a purely psychological condition. It was... a resonance. A
feedback loop with the background psychic noise of Earth, of humanity.
He believed that if we could get you to a place with a different,
quieter background frequency... the loop would break."

He looked at the portal, as if he could see Rook on the other side. "It
was a gamble. A massive, unethical, unforgivable one. But judging by the
clarity of your signal... it worked. You are free, Rook. Free of the
static. The buzz you're feeling now? That's just you. All you."

He let that sink in for a final thirty seconds. The greatest betrayal
was also the ultimate gift.

"You don't have to do a damned thing," Vance finished, his voice soft
but clear. "You have your exit. You got what you wanted. We will uphold
our end of the bargain. The trust for your sister is active."

He took one last, deep breath, preparing for the inevitable, violent
response.

"But just in case you change your mind... just in case you decide that
being the only sane man in a world full of monsters is a cause worth
fighting for... we are preparing the full informational packet on
Fortitude and The Grey. The one you refused before you stepped through.
It will be waiting for you. The choice, as always, is yours."

Vance let go of the microphone, his knuckles white. He had laid the
truth bare. Now came the agonizing thirty-second wait for the response.
He expected rage. He expected curses. He expected a promise of a slow,
painful death if Rook ever found a way back.

He did not have to wait thirty seconds.

An impossible fifteen seconds later, a new sound crackled through the
hangar's speakers. It wasn't the calm, synthetic text-to-speech voice.

It was Rook's own voice. Raw. Real. And laced with a dry, tired, and
deeply unsettling amusement.

"Don't bother."

The voice was a ghost, an echo from another world, and it sent a
collective gasp through the entire hangar. Real-time, two-way audio was
a physical impossibility. Yet here it was. All their hopes, their
theories about what he might do now that he was "free," were instantly
dashed by the finality in his tone. He was rejecting them.

"...I already have all of the information," Rook's voice continued, a
slight, staticky chuckle underpinning the words. "Just 'received it',
more or less. All the data on Fortitude. All your failed strategies. All
your pathetic, desperate little hopes."

Another impossible pause, as if he were listening to their shocked
silence from across the universe.

"But... you lied," Rook's voice came again, and the amusement was gone,
replaced by a cold, flat certainty. "You lied about the cure. You lied
about the mission." A pause. "But I already knew you were lying before I
ever stepped through the Portal."

The shock in the room deepened into a profound, chilling awe. He had
known. The entire time. He had known it was a gamble, a one-way trip,
and he had stepped through anyway.

"By the way," the voice from the void continued, its tone now shifting
to one of pure, contemptuous, professional critique, "all your plans
were shit anyway. Your tactical approaches, your containment
strategies... they were all based on the assumption that the Grey are an
enemy to be fought. They're not. They're an environment to be
navigated."

There was another slight chuckle, a sound that was both terrifying and
brilliant.

"Now, let me show you what you *should* have been doing all along."

The main holographic display, which had been showing a static map,
flickered and changed. New data streams began to pour in, not just maps,
but complex, multi-layered real-time analyses of the Grey's psychic
weather patterns, their feeding cycles, their resonant frequencies. It
was a level of intelligence, of understanding, that was decades beyond
anything their own scientists had ever conceived.

"I will send you useful information 'when I feel like it'," Rook's voice
stated, laying out the new, non-negotiable terms of their relationship.
"But you will get a bio-telemetry 'pulse' every five minutes to let you
know I'm still alive. You'll get your proof of life, so you don't do
anything stupid like send in another team of heroes to mess up my
quiet."

The final transmission was a clear, absolute, and deeply insulting
dismissal.

"Now, don't bother me," the voice from another world said, a hint of
something that sounded almost like enjoyment in its tone.

"I'm working here."
