# Chapter 7: The Rescue

Vance stepped through the portal, and the world dissolved into a
maelstrom of silent, screaming noise. The hangar vanished, replaced by
the bruised-purple sky and the rust-colored dust of the alien world. The
low, pleasant hum Rook had experienced was, for a normal human mind, a
deafening, psychic hurricane.

The Faraday Cage Helmet, or FCH, immediately kicked in. A low,
electronic hum filled his ears, and the chaotic, soul-tearing assault of
the Grey's background radiation was dampened, reduced to a bearable,
high-pitched whine, like a thousand tiny needles prickling at the inside
of his skull.

"I should have brought a bigger boat," Vance mumbled to himself, the
absurdity of the thought a small, familiar island in the raging psychic
sea. The FCH was working, but just barely. He could feel the edges of
his consciousness beginning to fray, the tendrils of the Grey searching
for a crack, a weakness, a painful memory to latch onto.

Fortunately, Vance knew more than a little about PTSD, both from his
clinical studies and from his own quiet, well-managed battles in the
dark of the night. And he had come prepared. He had his own anchor.

His ex-wife.

He didn't think of the divorce, the bitter arguments, the loneliness.
That was a feast he would not offer to the Grey. He focused on a single,
perfect, and unassailable loop of memory. He imagined her on their
wedding day, the impossible, brilliant, sun-drenched smile she had given
him. He felt the warmth, the hope, the terrifying, beautiful certainty
of that moment. Then, he shifted the image. He saw her in a sterile
hospital bed, exhausted but radiant, holding their first child, a tiny,
screaming, perfect little miracle.

He left it at that. The wedding. The birth. He played the loop over and
over in his mind, a mantra of pure, uncomplicated joy. The psychic
needles of the Grey pricked and probed, but they could find no purchase
on the smooth, solid surface of his chosen memory.

He was a lighthouse in a hurricane, his beam unwavering. And then he saw
them.

The Team.

They were huddled together in a shallow ravine, just as Rook's data had
indicated. They were a mess. Their eyes were wide, darting at unseen
threats. One man was whispering to himself. Another was aiming his rifle
at a rock, his hands trembling. They were alive, but they were lost,
trapped in the haunted houses of their own minds.

Vance took a deep breath, the psychic whine of the FCH a constant,
irritating reminder of his ten-minute clock. He straightened his
non-existent tie, a force of habit. The hero had arrived.

"Commander Thorne?" he called out, his voice calm, steady, and amplified
by his helmet's external speaker. "My name is Elias Vance. I'm here to
bring you home."

It took twenty agonizing, heart-stopping minutes to coax the eight
broken soldiers across the final hundred meters. Vance was a master of
calm, his voice a steady, reassuring presence, anchoring each of them
with the names of their children, their hometowns, the taste of their
favorite meals---the small, perfect details he had memorized from their
files.

One by one, they stumbled through the shimmering, silver veil of the
portal, collapsing into the waiting arms of the medical teams. They were
home. All eight of them. The hangar erupted in a ragged, relieved cheer.

But Vance was not with them.

Rostova's relief was instantly replaced by a new, sharp, and
surprisingly personal spike of fear. She ran to the edge of the
containment ring, her eyes locked on the swirling portal. He had been in
there for over twenty minutes. His ten-minute FCH window was long gone.

"Vance!" she screamed at the portal, her voice raw, her professional
composure completely gone. "VANCE!"

There was no response. The portal remained a silent, indifferent curtain
of light. Five more minutes passed. Ten. Each second was an eternity.
The cheering in the hangar had died, replaced by a grim, waiting
silence. Dr. Carter was ashen-faced. Vance was gone. Lost.

And then, a figure stumbled through.

It was Vance. His face was pale, his eyes unfocused, and a thin trickle
of blood ran from his nose, a sign of extreme psychic strain. He took
two steps and collapsed to his knees, his mind clearly in a daze, but a
weird, goofy, and completely inexplicable smile was plastered on his
face.

The medical team rushed to him, but before they could reach him, the
speakers in the hangar crackled to life one last time. It was Rook's
voice. The real one. Raw, staticky, and laced with a profound, and very
tired, sense of annoyance.

"When he comes through," Rook's voice from another world rasped, "tell
him he owes me one."

There was a pause, and then the final, baffling, and deeply personal
message was delivered, a cryptic sign-off to a rescue no one had
witnessed.

"...and tell him that we won't be taking any soapy showers together any
time in the near future... that sick bastard."

The transmission ended.

The hangar was silent, save for the low, unhinged chuckle coming from
Elias Vance, who was now being helped onto a gurney. Rostova stared from
the portal to the dazed, smiling analyst.

She had no idea what had just happened. But she knew two things for
certain.

One: Rook had just saved Vance's life.

And two: The story Vance was going to tell about his "heroic, solo
rescue mission" was going to require some very, very creative editing.
