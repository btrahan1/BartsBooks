# Chapter 9: The King

It was Rook. In real-time.

He was crouched on a black, glassy rock, the bruised-purple sky and the
oversized moon hanging behind him like a theatrical backdrop. He was
looking directly at them, as if staring through a one-way mirror. The
image was so sharp, so stable, they could see the dust on his orange
jumpsuit.

But it was his eyes.

They were glowing.

A soft, internal, and deeply unnatural light emanated from them,
shifting slowly from a calm, translucent blue to a hot, angry red, and
then back to blue again. It was the color of a machine's status light.
The color of a soul that was no longer entirely human.

"I'll take care of that."

Rook's voice came through the speakers, as clear as day. The amusement
was gone. The annoyance was gone. It was a voice of pure, cold, and
absolute control. He had heard them. He had been listening to their
entire, panicked conversation.

Dr. Carter let out a small, strangled gasp.

"You have no chance of destroying the portal," Rook's image said, his
glowing eyes fixed on Carter. "Not from your side. The event horizon is
self-stabilizing. You would need a force sufficient to momentarily
disrupt the localized spacetime continuum." He paused, his gaze drifting
off into space for a handful of seconds, his eyes flickering red, then
blue, as he ran an impossible calculation in his head.

He looked back at them. "A nuclear bomb," he stated, the words a simple,
factual analysis. "One... with an explosive yield approximately 723.8
times the power of the one you dropped on Hiroshima."

The sheer, casual precision of the number was more terrifying than any
threat. He had just done the physics in his head, in seconds.

Rostova stared, her blood running cold. He was right. That was the
exact, top-secret calculation from the initial contingency reports. He
couldn't have known that.

"But don't worry, Doctor," Rook's image continued, his glowing blue eyes
settling on Carter's terrified face. "I have no intention of coming
back." A pause. "And I have no intention of letting anything else from
this side cross over, either."

He reached a hand up, not to his comms unit, but to his own temple.
"This connection... it's a security risk. A backdoor. Time to close it."

His glowing eyes flickered a final time from blue, to red, to a
brilliant, blinding white.

"I'll be in touch," he said.

And the screen went black. The voice went silent. The data streams
ceased.

The hangar was plunged back into a profound, and now utterly terrifying,
silence. They were blind. They were deaf. And the most dangerous weapon
in human history was now completely, and totally, off the leash.
