# Chapter 12: The Last Laugh

The finality of the moment was absolute. The portal was gone. The
project was over. A heavy, somber silence settled over the mobile
command center. It was the quiet of a chapter ending, of a book being
closed forever.

And then, something small, white, and utterly impossible fluttered down
from the ceiling of the command tent.

It wasn't a leaf, blown in by the wind. It wasn't a stray piece of
paper. It looked like... a photograph. It drifted down on the still,
recycled air, turning lazy circles, before Director Rostova, with the
lightning-fast reflexes of a woman who missed nothing, snatched it out
of the sky.

She looked at it.

Her face, which had been a mask of grim, weary resolution, went
completely blank. The blankness was then replaced by a look of pure,
unadulterated, slack-jawed disbelief. Her mouth opened, then closed,
then opened again. No sound came out.

"Director?" Carter asked, concerned. "What is it?"

Wordlessly, her movements stiff and robotic, she handed the photograph
to Vance.

Vance took it, his expression one of calm, academic curiosity. He looked
down at the image.

And Elias Vance, the cool, grey, unflappable analyst, the man who had
faced down a super-soldier and a psychic hurricane without breaking a
sweat, began to blush. It started at his collar and spread up his neck,
a rising tide of crimson that turned his ears a startling shade of
purple. He stared at the photograph as if it were a gorgon, petrified in
a state of pure, professional horror.

It was a picture of Rook.

He was in what looked like a crude, makeshift shower, cobbled together
from military salvage and alien rock formations. He was completely,
unapologetically, naked. Water was cascading over his scarred, powerful
shoulders. He was covered in a thick lather of white, soapy suds.

And he was grinning.

It was not a grimace. It was not a sneer. It was a wide, genuine, and
utterly infuriating shit-eating grin, aimed directly at the viewer, at
the impossible camera that had taken the shot. It was a look of pure,
triumphant, and deeply personal mockery.

Scrawled at the bottom of the photograph, in what looked suspiciously
like Rook's own handwriting, was a simple, final, devastating message.

"*To Vance, my biggest fan.*"

The last laugh. From across a void of spacetime he himself had just
sealed. A final, metaphysical middle finger.

Vance stood there, turning several, distinct shades of purple, the
photograph trembling in his hand. Rostova was still speechless.
Dr. Carter peeked over Vance's shoulder, looked at the photo, and then
very wisely decided to go check on the status of the backup generators
on the far side of the base.

The king was in his kingdom. The door was closed. And he had made damn
sure they would never, ever, forget who had won the game.
