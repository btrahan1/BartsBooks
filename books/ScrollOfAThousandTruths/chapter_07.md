# Chapter 7: The Carrot and the Stick

The frantic, clumsy sounds of work began to fill the square. Kael was
halfway up the ladder to the wall, his useless arm making the climb
awkward. The second man was heaving splintered planks into a pile, his
movements unsteady. The third was on his hands and knees, still fighting
waves of nausea.

A faint smile touched Jin-woo's lips. It was not a smile of warmth or
amusement, but of a complex problem finding an elegant solution. The
itch in his soul had been soothed.

With a final, almost casual sweep of his hand, he sent an unseen
essence, a gentle wave of his Ki, flowing from him toward the trio. It
was the inverse of the sharp, corrective pulse he had used before---a
soft, restorative balm.

"I feel much better already," he said, his voice returning to its
normal, placid tone. "Don't you?"

The effect was as immediate as the punishment had been.

On the ladder, Kael felt a warmth spread through his shoulder. The
deadened nerves in his arm sparked back to life. He flexed his fingers,
his eyes widening in shock as they obeyed. The arm was weak, tired, but
it *worked*.

The man clearing the stall felt his leg suddenly find its strength, the
buckling weakness gone. The third man's debilitating vertigo vanished in
an instant, the world snapping back into sharp, stable focus.

They all froze, staring at their own bodies in utter bewilderment. They
looked at Jin-woo, their expressions a mixture of fear, confusion, and
for the first time, a sliver of genuine awe. He had broken them with a
touch, threatened them with a word, and now, healed them with a gesture.
This was a power beyond their comprehension.

Jin-woo simply nodded, as if a minor transaction had been completed. He
turned his back on them for the final time.

As he walked, a memory surfaced from the deepest part of his being, a
lesson from his old master, Elder Hyun. The old scribe had been teaching
him about balance in calligraphy, how too much pressure on the brush
created a clumsy, ugly line, while too little pressure left a weak,
faded one.

"*The same is true for guiding men, Jin-woo,*" the old man's voice
echoed in his memory. "'*All stick and no carrot,*' *as the old proverb
says,* '*makes for a bitter student.*' *A lesson taught only with pain
is a lesson that will be resented. A lesson taught with consequence,
both positive and negative, is a lesson that will be understood.*"

He was beginning to understand. The Way of the Fist was only the stick.
The Way of the Brush, in this chaotic new world, was not just about
understanding. It was about knowing precisely where to apply pressure,
and when to lift the brush from the page. He had delivered the
consequence, and now, he had provided the incentive.

He left the town square behind, the sounds of steady, purposeful work
now replacing the silence of fear. He had not just defeated three thugs.
He had given them a new, terrifyingly clear contract with reality, and
in doing so, had planted the first, uncertain seed of change.
