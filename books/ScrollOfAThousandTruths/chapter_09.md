# Chapter 9: A Stringless Choice

A week passed. The rhythm of the town had found a new beat, one set by
the silent, flowing movements in the square each dawn. The morning
exercise was no longer a spectacle; it was a ritual.

Kael, Roric, and Sten were no longer the town's tormentors. They were,
to the astonishment of everyone including themselves, becoming fixtures
of its nascent order. Kael's sharp eyes from the wall had spotted a
coming rockslide, allowing the path to be cleared. Roric's patrols had
discovered a source of clean water upstream. Sten, with a quiet, focused
fury, had rebuilt the woodcarver's stall twice as strong as it was
before. Their work was grudging, but it was consistent.

Their movements during the morning forms had changed. The clumsy parody
of the first day had been replaced by a rough-hewn grace. The jagged
edges of their Ki, read by Jin-woo's Insight, had been worn smoother,
like river stones in a steady current.

On the eighth morning, as the final form concluded, they stood breathing
in the cool air, a shared, practiced stillness settling over them.
Usually, this was when Jin-woo would simply walk away, leaving them to
their tasks. Today, he remained.

He stood in the center of the square, his eyes still closed, a statue of
perfect calm. The trio waited, sensing a change in the script.

"I do not believe people change," Jin-woo spoke, his voice quiet but
resonant in the morning air. "At least, not easily. The stone of a man's
soul is hard, carved by a lifetime of choices. It does not simply become
clay again."

He paused, letting the weight of his words settle. Kael's jaw tightened.

"However," Jin-woo continued, "I believe sometimes attributes that were
there all along---strength, diligence, a leader's eye---can rise given
the opportunity. They were simply buried under years of rubble."

He finally opened his eyes, and his gaze was not that of a teacher or a
warden, but of something else entirely. It was the clear, analytical
gaze of the scribe, assessing the quality of the ink.

"I have a task for you," he said. "An opportunity. And an escape, if you
so desire."

The word "escape" hung in the air, charged with possibility.

"A caravan of refugees from the south is two days' journey from here.
Raiders have been harrying their trail. The same kind who visited my
home. They are weak, tired, and have little to offer but their lives.
They need an escort through the Blackwood Pass."

He looked at each of them in turn, his Scribe's Insight piercing through
their guarded expressions to the churning uncertainty in their cores.

"This is not a command. It is a choice. You can take supplies and go.
Protect them. Guide them here, or to the next safe haven. It will be
dangerous. You may not return."

He let that sink in before delivering the final, critical stroke.

"Or, you can take those same supplies and simply leave. Head north. Head
west. Never return. The contract I placed upon your Ki," he said,
tapping his own chest, "I will remove it. You will be free. The choice
is entirely your own, and it is without strings."

Silence. The entire square seemed to hold its breath. The trio looked at
each other, a complex, silent conversation passing between them. They
were being offered the one thing they thought they had lost forever:
freedom. They could go back to being the men they were, unburdened by
this strange monk and his impossible lessons.

Kael looked down at his own hands, hands that had taken and broken for
years, but had, for the last week, learned to guard and build. He looked
at the townspeople, who were beginning to watch them not with fear, but
with a fragile, tentative respect. He looked back at the monk, who stood
waiting, his expression unreadable, offering them a chance to prove his
philosophy right, or to shatter it completely.

"Are you interested?" Jin-woo asked.
