# Chapter 10: The Weight of Freedom

Kael's eyes narrowed. The offer was too clean, too simple. Freedom was
never given; it was taken. This had to be a trick. Roric and Sten
exchanged wary glances, their minds racing, searching for the hidden
trap in the monk's words.

Seeing the suspicion in their cores, Jin-woo's expression softened
slightly. "Before you decide," he said, his voice taking on the cadence
of a storyteller. "Let me tell you a tale."

The square remained silent, captivated.

"When I was a young apprentice at the monastery," he began, "my master
grew displeased with my progress. He said my spirit was too light, too
untested. So, one morning, he shackled my arms and legs with heavy iron
weights."

He looked at the trio, and they instinctively felt the memory of their
own powerlessness.

"Every day, I went about my chores. Sweeping the courtyard, tending the
gardens, carrying water from the well. And every morning, my master
would add another weight. The broom became a log I had to drag. The
garden path became a mountain to climb. My forms became a clumsy,
exhausting struggle. For a week, the weight grew, and my world shrank to
the simple, agonizing effort of the next step, the next breath."

He paused, letting them feel the phantom weight.

"At the end of the week, I was on the verge of collapse, my muscles
screaming. My master came to me, not with another weight, but with a
key. He removed the shackles. I have never known a lightness like it. I
felt I could float off the ground. Then, he pointed to a cart, laden
with sacks of rice for a village suffering from a poor harvest. 'The
cart is heavy,' he told me. 'And the journey is long. Enjoy your trip!'"

Jin-woo smiled at the memory. "And I did. The cart felt like a feather.
The journey was a joy. My master had not been punishing me. He had been
preparing me. The weight was not the prison; it was the training. The
freedom from it was the reward."

As he finished his tale, he raised his hand and waved it gently, a soft,
dismissive gesture.

In that instant, Kael, Roric, and Sten felt it. The quiet, orderly knot
of blue Ki that had been tied to their souls, the governor on their
power, simply... dissolved. It vanished without a trace. Their own Ki,
now cleaner and stronger from a week of disciplined practice, surged
through their meridians, unchained and unrestrained. The feeling was
electric, a dizzying rush of their old power returned a hundredfold.
They were themselves again, but *more*.

They stared at Jin-woo, stunned into silence. He had truly let them go.
The stick was gone.

"How do you feel?" Jin-woo asked, his voice calm and genuine. The
Scribe's Insight read the explosive potential now roaring within them,
and he did not flinch.

"Do you think you can handle this task I set before you?"
