# Chapter 13: The Unwritten Page

Dawn broke, and the twenty-four former raiders stood in the town square.
They were a motley, intimidating crew, even without their weapons.
Scars, sullen glares, and the coiled tension of cornered animals defined
them. They stood in a ragged line, a mixture of defiance and weary
apprehension on their faces. Kael, Roric, and Sten stood to the side,
their arms crossed, their expressions unreadable. They were no longer
prisoners; they were prefects.

Jin-woo did not begin with forms or speeches about breathing. He simply
appeared, a still point in the morning light, and began to pace.

He walked slowly, his hands clasped behind his back, his simple robes
whispering against the dirt. He moved down the line of men, circling
them, observing them not as a commander inspects soldiers, but as a
master artisan inspects flawed timber.

His Scribe's Insight was a scalpel, peeling back the layers of bravado
and anger to the raw truth of their Ki. He saw everything.

He stopped in front of a giant of a man, a brute who towered over the
others. Jin-woo circled him once, his eyes tracing patterns in the air
that only he could see. "Hmmm," he murmured, the sound quiet but cutting
through the silence. "Such a powerful river, but it flows into a swamp.
All that strength, wasted in mud."

The giant's face flushed with anger, but he said nothing. The monk's
words struck a chord of truth he didn't even know existed.

Jin-woo moved on to the next, a wiry man with shifty eyes.
"Interesting," he whispered, his gaze fixed on the man's chest. "Your
core is a bird in a cage of your own making. It flutters with fear every
time it tries to fly."

He continued down the line, a quiet, devastating diagnosis for each man.

"A fire stoked too hot. It will burn itself out before noon." "A
meridian blocked by old grief. You fight with a broken arm you cannot
see." "Your Ki is an echo. You have no will of your own, only the shape
of the last man who shouted at you."

The aura he exuded was not the tranquil peace of the previous mornings.
It was something far more unnerving. It was the absolute, dispassionate
focus of a being who was reading the source code of their souls. It was
disarming because it was not aggressive. There was no anger, no
judgment, only a profound and terrifying clarity. It was more dangerous
than any threat they had ever faced, because for the first time in their
violent lives, they were not being threatened; they were being *known*.
Every lie they told themselves, every wall they had built around their
weakness, was being seen, cataloged, and dismissed as a simple, fixable
error.

When he had inspected every man, Jin-woo stopped in the center of the
square. The line of raiders was no longer defiant. They were shaken,
their bravado stripped away, leaving only the raw, flawed men beneath.

"You are not warriors," Jin-woo stated, his voice calm and clear. "You
are poorly written scrolls. Your stories are full of blotches,
crossed-out lines, and flawed characters. You have spent your lives
trying to write over the mistakes with more ink, making the page uglier
and harder to read."

He looked at the collection of broken men before him.

"I am not here to tear your page out of the book. I am here to teach you
how to erase the errors. But first, you must see them for what they are.
You must learn to read yourselves. Your training does not begin with a
punch. It begins with a blank page."
