# Chapter 16: The First Watch

The twenty-four men stood in the square, holding the clean, damp dishes.
The simple objects felt heavy, symbolic of the journey they had just
completed. They had walked out as a fractured collection of individuals
and returned as six functioning teams. They had survived the stick.

Jin-woo observed them, his Scribe's Insight reading the settled dust of
their internal struggle. The chaotic, angry reds in their Ki had
subsided, replaced by the tired but stable blue of resolve. They had
been tested, and they had not broken.

He raised a hand, not in command, but in a gesture of offering. A cool,
clear current of his own Ki washed over them, a visible shimmer in the
afternoon air. It was not a jolt or a shock, but a gentle, pervasive
peace.

The effect was profound. The deep, weary ache in their muscles eased.
The fog of exhaustion and mental strain cleared from their minds. Even
the lingering sting of old wounds seemed to be soothed by the placid
blue wave. It was the carrot---a tangible taste of the inner calm that
was the true goal of his teachings.

As the wave of restorative energy passed, Jin-woo spoke, his voice
carrying the weight of a royal decree delivered with the simplicity of a
farmer's observation.

"You are now the Town Watch. Watch well."

The words landed in the silent square with the force of a physical blow.
Raider, mercenary, thug---these were the labels they had lived by. Now,
in a single sentence, they had been given a new one. Watchman. Guardian.

Before they could fully process the shift in their identity, he added
the final, critical instruction.

"Handle the rotations yourselves."

And with that, he turned and walked toward the center of the town,
leaving them completely to their own devices. The message was clear: The
tests were over. The work had begun.

For a moment, the men stood in stunned silence. They looked at each
other, then at the town around them---the small houses, the market
stalls, the faces of the villagers peeking out from doorways. This was
no longer just a place they were staying. It was a place they were now
responsible for.

Kael was the first to break the silence. He looked at the five other
chosen leaders, then at the men under his own informal command. The
weight of the monk's trust was heavier than any shackle he had ever
worn.

"Alright," he growled, the familiar command in his voice now colored
with a new, unfamiliar sense of duty. "You heard him. The six of us...
we're the captains. We figure this out. Now."

The men didn't grumble. They didn't posture. They gathered in the center
of the square, their dishes forgotten on the ground, and began the
awkward, messy, and absolutely essential business of building their
first watch schedule. The sound of their arguments and negotiations was
no longer the sound of chaos.

It was the sound of order being forged.
