# Chapter 17: The Unseen Forge

The week unfolded with a rhythm of disciplined labor. The new Town Watch
took to their duties with a grim determination. They patrolled the
walls, guarded the gate, and kept the peace with an efficiency that was
both surprising and intimidating. But a strange, creeping weariness
settled over them.

Every step on patrol felt a little heavier than the last. Hoisting a
supply crate felt like lifting a boulder. Even standing watch became a
battle against a leaden exhaustion that settled deep in their bones.
They were warriors, used to hardship, so they simply gritted their teeth
and pushed through it, attributing the profound fatigue to the
unfamiliar strain of responsibility and their new, cleaner diet.

Jin-woo, meanwhile, watched them from the square, from the rooftops,
from the quiet corners of the town. A faint, almost imperceptible grin
was a permanent fixture on his face. As they moved, he moved with them,
not in body, but in will. His Scribe's Insight was locked onto them, and
with a subtle, constant pressure, he was adding weight to their Ki. Each
day, he increased the invisible burden just a little, making the air
around them feel thicker, their own bodies feel heavier. He saw them
struggle, but more importantly, he saw them adapt. Their muscles grew
stronger, their Ki cores burned brighter to overcome the resistance,
their resolve hardened into steel under the constant, unseen pressure.
They were being forged, and they didn't even know it.

At the end of the week, he gathered them in the square. They stood
before him, exhausted but upright, their posture straighter, their
presence more solid than ever before.

"You men eat too much," Jin-woo said. It was not a criticism, just a
statement of fact. The town's stores were dwindling faster with two
dozen new, active mouths to feed.

The men bristled slightly, but held their tongues.

"A feast would be a grand event," Jin-woo continued, a thoughtful look
on his face. "A celebration of this town's new strength. It's a shame we
don't have any meat to prepare such a thing."

A predatory gleam entered his eyes, and his faint grin returned. He
waved his hand, a casual, dismissive gesture, the same one he had used
to dissolve their spiritual contracts.

The release was instantaneous and overwhelming.

It was not a gentle wave of peace. It was a dam bursting. The invisible,
crushing weight they had been fighting all week vanished in a flash.
Power, *their own power*, surged through them with an explosive,
intoxicating force. Muscles that had been straining against a mountain
suddenly had only air to push against. Ki that had been burning fiercely
to keep them moving now roared through their meridions, an untamed
flood. The giant felt as if he could leap over the town wall. The wiry
man felt fast enough to outrun the wind. It was the feeling of the
shackles being removed, magnified twenty-four times over.

They stared at their hands, at each other, a silent, dawning realization
spreading through them. The weariness, the struggle---it had all been a
part of the lesson.

"Remember to be wise as you choose your offerings from the forest,"
Jin-woo said, his voice cutting through their stunned amazement. "Take
no more than we need."

With that, Jin-woo turned and walked away, leaving twenty-four men
buzzing with a power they had never known they possessed, their bodies
feeling as light as feathers, their spirits soaring. The unspoken task
was clear. The hunt was on. And for the first time, they would go not as
raiders to plunder, but as providers to sustain.
