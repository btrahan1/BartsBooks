# Chapter 18: The Taste of Pride

The feast was indeed grand.

A massive bonfire roared in the center of the square, casting a warm,
dancing light on the faces of the entire town. The air, which had once
been thick with fear, was now rich with the smells of roasting meat,
baked bread, and mulled spices. For the first time since anyone could
remember, the sound filling the night was not the quiet murmur of
survival, but the joyous noise of a true celebration.

The former raiders, now the architects of this bounty, were at the heart
of it all. They were not the sullen, brooding figures of weeks past.
They were animated, their faces flushed with firelight and
accomplishment, as they regaled the townspeople with the stories of
their hunt.

The giant, his voice booming with laughter, described not how he had
shattered the charge of a great-tusked boar, but how he had used his
strength to steer it into a ravine, saving two of his companions from
being gored. He spoke of its power with respect, not contempt.

The wiry man, once a creature of selfish darts and dashes, recounted how
he had tracked a herd of deer for half a day, not to ambush them, but to
learn their path and select an old, lone buck, ensuring the herd itself
remained strong for the future.

One of the new captains told a story that drew a hushed silence. His
team had cornered a doe with a fawn. He described the moment of
decision, the instinct to take the easy kill warring with the monk's
final words: "Take no more than we need." He spoke of letting them go,
and the strange, quiet satisfaction that had come with that act of
restraint.

The pride among them was a palpable force, as warm as the fire itself.
It was a new and potent brew, different from the arrogant bluster of
their past lives. This pride was earned not by taking, but by providing.
It was heavier, more solid, and mingled with it was a sensation none of
them had ever truly felt before: humility. They had been given a gift of
immense power and had been trusted to use it wisely. In honoring that
trust, they had discovered a deeper strength.

They liked it. The feeling was clean, invigorating, a straight-backed
posture of the soul.

And they were not the only ones. A few of the town's unmarried women,
who had once hidden from these very men, now listened to their stories,
their eyes bright with interest. They saw not thugs, but protectors.
They heard not boasts, but tales of competence
and---impossibly---wisdom. A shy smile was offered here, a shared laugh
there. The first, tentative threads of a new community, one of shared
respect and not just shared space, were being woven in the firelight.

From the edge of the square, sitting on the steps of the woodcarver's
rebuilt stall, Jin-woo watched. He ate his simple bowl of stew, a quiet
island of calm in the joyous ocean he had created. He saw the pride and
the humility. He saw the budding romances. He saw the twenty-four flawed
scrolls, now authoring a new, better story for themselves.

And he just smiled.
