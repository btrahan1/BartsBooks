# Chapter 19: The Red Hand

The warmth of the feast and the glow of newfound community lingered for
three peaceful days. The town hummed with a purpose it had never known.
The Watch drilled in the square, their movements growing sharper, their
teamwork more intuitive. They were building something, and they could
feel it in their bones.

The peace was shattered by the sound of a desperate, screaming horn call
from the western watchtower.

Moments later, Roric, the scout, burst into the square. He was not the
confident hunter who had returned from the forest; he was a man fleeing
a nightmare. His face was ashen, his breath came in ragged sobs, and a
deep, poisoned-looking gash ran down his arm. He collapsed at the center
of the square, his eyes wild with terror.

Jin-woo, Kael, and the others rushed to his side.

"What is it?" Kael demanded, grabbing his shoulder. "Raiders?"

Roric shook his head, shuddering. "Worse," he choked out. "The Miller's
farmstead... the one by the old woods..." He swallowed hard, fighting
back a wave of nausea. "We saw the smoke. Went to investigate. The door
was broken in. There was... so much blood. But no bodies. Not the
miller, not his wife, not their children."

A cold dread began to seep into the square.

"They didn't just take the food," Roric whispered, his voice trembling.
"They poisoned the well with black-rot fungus. They tore the animals
apart and left them in the fields. And on the wall, painted in blood...
a handprint. A red hand."

Jin-woo knelt beside the scout, his face a mask of profound stillness.
He placed a hand over Roric's wound, a clear blue light flushing the
poison out. But his Scribe's Insight was not focused on the wound. It
was focused on the memory, the echo of the Ki that had inflicted it.

He had expected to see a chaotic, angry crimson, like the thugs he had
first met. He saw nothing of the sort.

What he perceived was a screaming, cancerous static. It was a void, a
hungry, hateful nothingness that did not have a flawed core, but an
anti-core. It did not burn with misguided passion; it emanated a pure,
nihilistic malice. It was the Ki of a creature that did not want to rule
or steal or win. It only wanted to unmake.

For the first time since his transformation, the Scribe's Insight
offered no solution. It presented no flaw to be corrected, no meridian
to be blocked, no insecurity to be soothed. It was like trying to read a
page that had been completely scribbled over with thick, black,
meaningless ink. There was no text to edit.

The rage he had felt when he first arrived in the town, the hot, sharp
anger at the injustice of the thugs, was a flickering candle compared to
the cold, absolute certainty that now settled in his soul. It was the
same feeling he'd had as he watched his monastery burn. The feeling of
being faced with something that could not be reasoned with.

The men of the Watch gathered, their faces grim, their hands already
finding the hilts of their swords. They looked to Jin-woo, their
teacher, their master.

Kael voiced the question that hung in the air. "What's the lesson here,
master? How do we... correct that?"

Jin-woo rose to his feet, his placid blue aura hardening, its edges
sharpening until it seemed to glint like polished steel. He looked at
the faces of the men he had so carefully retrained, the men he had
taught to be builders, guardians, and providers.

"There is no lesson for them," Jin-woo said, his voice quiet, but
carrying a chilling finality. "The Way of the Brush is for those who can
be taught. It is for a flawed page that can be corrected."

He turned his gaze to the west, toward the unseen horror in the woods.

"These creatures... are not a flawed page. They are a fire that wants to
burn the entire library."

He looked back at his students, his eyes holding the cold, hard light of
a winter star.

"The lesson is for us. The lesson is that the scribe must also know how
to protect the scroll. The Way of the Fist is not for teaching. It is
for erasing that which would destroy the text."

He took a deep, centering breath, and the last vestige of the gentle
teacher vanished, replaced by the unyielding will of a man who had made
a terrible, necessary choice.

"Gather your weapons. Fortify the walls. Send the women and children to
the great hall," he commanded, his voice ringing with an authority they
had never heard before.

"There is no teaching them. There is only... erasure."
