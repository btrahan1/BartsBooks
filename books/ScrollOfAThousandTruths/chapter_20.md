# Chapter 20: The Weight of Honor

The air in the square crackled with a sudden, violent energy. The men,
who had spent weeks learning to control and contain their Ki, now let it
flare. Their exhaustion was burned away by a surge of adrenaline and
righteous fury. They were a pack of wolves who had just been shown a
threat to their territory, and they were eager to be unleashed.

Jin-woo saw the boiling crimson in their auras, the raw hunger for
battle. He did the math in his head. A force of twenty-seven disciplined
fighters. His three captains. A small town to defend. A hidden enemy to
hunt.

"I need volunteers," he said, his voice cutting through the rising
tension.

He beamed with a deep, genuine pride as twenty-seven hands shot into the
air without a moment's hesitation. He saw the fire in their eyes, the
eagerness to follow him, to prove themselves, to unleash the power he
had so carefully forged within them. More than that, a dozen
townspeople---farmers and craftsmen who had been quietly joining the
morning exercises---raised their hands as well, their faces grim but
resolved.

And yes, he thought with a profound sense of responsibility, they were
his now.

"No," Jin-woo said, and the single word dropped the temperature in the
square by ten degrees. The eager hands faltered. Confusion replaced
their battle-lust.

"I made a mistake," Jin-woo clarified, his voice softening as he met
their eyes. "I needed volunteers for who will stay and guard the town."

He let that sink in, watching the dawning understanding on their faces.

"We will send two-thirds of our strength to hunt. Two of my leaders,
each with their two quads, will accompany me. That is a force of
eighteen. I need one leader and his two quads---nine of our best---to
defend this town."

He paused, and his gaze became distant, shadowed by a memory of smoke
and screams. "You can never leave your home base undefended. You cannot
sacrifice your foundation for the sake of a single battle. I learned
this the hard way. Once."

The reference to his lost monastery was unspoken but understood by all.
The lesson was no longer theoretical; it was written in the ashes of his
past.

He looked back at the assembled men, his expression shifting from a
commander's calculation to a master's challenge. He was not asking for
the strongest or the fastest. He was asking for something more. He was
asking for the most disciplined. The most patient. The men who could
swallow their pride and their hunger for glory to perform the most vital
task of all.

"The hunt will have its glory," he said, his voice ringing with a new
clarity. "But the true strength of a wall is tested not when it attacks,
but when it stands. Defending our home, our new beginning... that is not
a lesser task."

He raised his voice, his eyes sweeping over the crowd of warriors.

"Who wishes for this honor?"
