# Chapter 21: The Unbreakable Vow

A tense silence fell over the square. The choice Jin-woo offered was a
difficult one, a razor's edge of pride and duty. To ask to go was to
claim strength. To ask to stay was to claim a different, quieter kind of
strength. Who among them could make that claim?

Then, a heavy footstep sounded on the hard-packed earth.

Sten, the giant Jin-woo had once called a "swamp of wasted strength,"
stepped forward. He was no longer a creature of bluster. His feet were
planted with the grounded solidity of a mountain. He placed a fist over
his heart and bowed his head to Jin-woo.

"Please," he said, his voice a low, rumbling bass. "Allow me this honor.
None will pass by me."

It was not a boast. It was a vow. The wasted strength had found its true
purpose: to be a dam against the flood.

Before the weight of his words had even settled, another man stepped
forward. It was one of the men from the first group to form, a man with
a haunted look in his eyes, known for his vicious temper in his past
life.

"I used to be quick to anger, quick to fight. Quick to kill," he said,
his voice rough with a new, unfamiliar humility. He did not look at
Jin-woo, but at the villagers who watched from the edges of the square.
"I think that if any goblins dare to show themselves within a mile of
this town, those 'skills' might come in handy, once again." He finally
looked at Jin-woo, and his gaze was clear. "May you forgive me for my
faults."

He was not just volunteering. He was taking the ugliest parts of his own
story and offering them as a shield for his new home.

A murmur of profound respect rippled through the assembled men. This was
not a choice born of fear or a desire to avoid the hunt. It was a choice
born of true self-knowledge.

The third of the original captains, a quiet, observant man named Borin,
stepped forward. He gestured to Sten, to the reformed killer, and to the
six other men who comprised their two quads.

"Master," Borin said, his voice steady. "My teams are solid. They are
anchors, not arrows. We are the wall. We will hold the town."

Jin-woo looked at the nine men who had volunteered to stay. He saw Sten,
the immovable mountain. He saw the reformed killer, a weapon turned
shield. He saw Borin, the thoughtful anchor. In them, he saw the perfect
foundation for defense. They had not just volunteered for a task; they
had correctly diagnosed their own purpose.

A look of profound, unblemished pride washed over Jin-woo's face. His
students had surpassed his every expectation. They were no longer just
following his lessons; they were authoring their own.

He gave a deep, formal bow to Borin and his nine men. It was not the bow
of a master to a student, but of a leader to his most trusted guardians.

"There is no higher honor," Jin-woo said, his voice filled with a quiet,
unshakable respect. "Hold the wall."

He then turned to Kael and Roric. "Gather your men. We move at first
light. Travel light, hunt fast. We are not just chasing a threat."

His gaze grew cold again, turning towards the western woods.

"We are erasing a mistake."
