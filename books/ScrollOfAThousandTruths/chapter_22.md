# Chapter 22: The Scribe's Aegis

They lay on the ridge overlooking the goblin encampment, hidden amongst
the rocks and bracken. The sight below was a festering sore on the
landscape. Crude tents of hide and stolen canvas were thrown up without
order. The ground was a churned morass of mud and filth. The air itself,
even from this distance, carried a palpable stench of rot and malice.

Jin-woo was silent, his eyes closed, but his Scribe's Insight was a hawk
surveying the battlefield. His men saw a horde of nearly a hundred
goblins---small, vicious creatures with skin the color of dried blood
and eyes that glittered with mindless hate. Kael, beside him, did the
grim math. They were outnumbered five to one.

But Jin-woo saw something far worse. He saw the chaotic, screeching red
static of the goblin warriors, a disorganized swarm of base cruelty. And
in the center, in a large, foul tent decorated with skulls and smeared
symbols, he saw the true enemy. The evil swirling around it was a
physical presence, a vortex of putrid violet and greasy black Ki that
felt like a sickness in the world's script. Inside was the Shaman, an
engine of pure, unmaking evil. And with him, three attendants, their Ki
like sharp, poisoned knives.

"Master," Kael whispered, his voice tight. "The odds are not in our
favor. A direct assault is suicide."

"It will not be a direct assault," Jin-woo replied, his voice calm and
distant. "It will be a correction. I will be the distraction."

He opened his eyes, and they were no longer the eyes of a teacher, but
of a master strategist calculating the cost. "Your teams need to
encircle the camp. Use the chaos I create to take your positions. On my
signal, you collapse the perimeter. The Shaman and his guard are mine."

Before they could protest, he sat back on his heels. "But first..."

He closed his eyes again, and this time, he reached not outward, but
inward. He drew upon the entirety of his core, the still, clear lake of
his Ki. He pulled it through his meridians, amplifying it, refining it.
Then he reached out further, into the world itself, inviting the clean,
ambient Ki of the mountains and the sky to answer his call. A visible,
brilliant blue light began to coalesce around him.

He was not preparing an attack. He was writing a promise.

His Insight found the eighteen souls of his men, reading the unique
script of each one. He saw their strengths, their fears, their very life
force. And into the margins of their spiritual text, he wrote a new
passage.

A wave of pure, benevolent energy washed out from him, not as a
concussive force, but as a gentle, soaking rain. Eighteen motes of soft
blue light detached from the wave and settled upon each warrior, sinking
into their chests.

The men gasped. A profound warmth spread through them, a feeling of
impossible vitality. It was a quiet, steady thrum of life, a promise of
restoration that settled deep in their bones. Jin-woo had woven a
complexKi-construct onto their souls---a heal-over-time that would mend
minor wounds, fight off the poisons the goblins favored, and bolster
their stamina for the coming hour. It was the ultimate carrot. A
Scribe's Aegis.

"You have one hour from the moment the battle begins," Jin-woo stated,
his voice now devoid of all warmth, a tool of pure command.

He rose to his feet.

"Take your positions."

Kael and Roric gave a single, sharp nod, their faces a mixture of awe
and grim resolve. They trusted him completely. They broke their teams
off, melting into the shadows of the forest to begin their silent,
deadly encirclement.

Jin-woo watched them go. Then, he took a deep breath and began to walk.
Alone.

He did not sneak. He did not creep. He walked with a calm, deliberate
stride down the open slope, straight toward the heart of the goblin
camp. His blue aura was not the peaceful glow of the town square; it was
a cold, hard, focused light, the blue heart of a star.

The first goblin sentry spotted him. It gave a screeching cry, a mixture
of alarm and confusion. Others turned. The jeers and guttural chatter of
the camp began to die down, replaced by a growing, uneasy silence. They
had never seen anything like this. A single, unarmed man, clad in simple
robes, walking into their den as if he were taking a stroll through a
garden.

The rancid, hateful energy of a hundred malevolent souls focused on him.
It was a psychic pressure that would have broken a lesser man, but it
washed over his cold, clear Ki like waves against a cliff.

He did not stop until he reached the absolute center of the encampment,
his feet planted in the filth, the entire goblin horde forming a jagged,
snarling circle around him. He could feel the Shaman's vile presence
stir in the main tent, its attention now fixed entirely on this
impossible anomaly.

The trap was set. He was the bait. And for the first time, he let the
Way of the Fist, the cold, hard logic of erasure, fill his soul.

Of course. This is the moment the Scribe puts down the brush and picks
up the eraser---a black, final, and absolute one.
