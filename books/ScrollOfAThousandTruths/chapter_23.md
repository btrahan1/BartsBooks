# Chapter 23: The Final Edit

The goblins stared, their tiny, hateful minds struggling to process the
scene. This single human, radiating an aura of cold, untouchable calm,
was a violation of the natural order. He was prey, yet he walked like a
predator. He was alone, yet he acted like an army.

Jin-woo paid them no mind at all. His entire being, his every ounce of
perception, was focused on the command tent. He saw the three elite
guards---hulking, scarred creatures twice the size of the others, their
Ki a thick, curdled black---take a step forward, their crude iron blades
scraping from their sheaths. He saw the roiling vortex of evil within
the tent flare in response to his presence. He had their complete,
undivided attention.

He stopped just outside the tent's foul, skin-flap door. He stared the
three elite guards in the eyes, his gaze not one of challenge, but of
pronouncement. He raised his hands slowly to the sky.

And he spoke one simple word, a word that was not a spell, but a
judgment.

"Retribution."

It was not shouted. It was stated, a fact being entered into the record
of the world. And in the instant the word left his lips, all hell broke
loose.

It was not a wave of fire or a blast of force. It was something far more
insidious. Jin-woo's Scribe's Insight was connected to the fabric of
reality, and with that single word, he executed a final, damning edit.
He did not create chaos; he simply commanded every existing flaw in the
camp to fail, all at once.

The rusty haft of an axe in a goblin's hand snapped. A poorly tied rope
on a tent gave way, collapsing the structure on those within. The
ground, already unstable, buckled along a dozen unseen fault lines,
sending creatures sprawling. A precariously balanced pile of stolen
armor and shields came crashing down. The very air seemed to turn on
them as sudden, violent dust devils whipped through the camp, blinding
them with grit and filth.

The world itself was his weapon.

The goblins shrieked, their simple minds shattering. They were not being
attacked by the man; they were being attacked by *everything*. The
ground, the air, their own weapons---it was a symphony of catastrophic
failure. Panic erupted. They turned on each other, their mindless rage
needing a target, and the camp devolved into a writhing, screaming
morass of infighting. The perfect distraction was achieved.

Through the cacophony, Jin-woo projected a single, clear, bell-like tone
from his own Ki. It was a sound that cut through the noise, a signal
imperceptible to the goblins but as clear as a shout to the eighteen men
on the perimeter who wore his Aegis.

The response was immediate. From the shadows of the forest, the Watch
descended. They were the perfect counterpoint to the goblins' chaos.
They were silent, disciplined, and utterly lethal. They moved in their
quads, flowing through the pandemonium like ghosts. They did not waste
energy on flashy blows. They executed precise, coordinated strikes---a
shield bash from one, a spear thrust from another---disabling and
dispatching the panicked goblins with terrifying efficiency. Where the
goblins were a panicked mob, the Watch were a scalpel.

But Jin-woo's attention was no longer on the battle. His part in it was
done.

He lowered his hands and turned his cold, analytical gaze back to the
three elite guards. They had weathered the initial chaos, their stronger
Ki grounding them. They now stood between him and the Shaman's tent,
their black, hateful eyes promising a bloody death.

The scribe's work was finished. It was time for the eraser.
