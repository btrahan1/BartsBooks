# Chapter 25: A New Language

The Shaman, seeing its elite guards erased in a heartbeat, felt the
cold, alien touch of true fear. It did not hesitate. It unleashed its
final, most precious weapon. A gnarled ring on its clawed finger,
crafted from a lightning-struck ironwood tree, blazed with furious
energy. This was not the creature's own Ki; it was a captured storm, a
bound and broken piece of the sky's fury.

With a screech of panic and rage, the Shaman thrust its hand forward.

Jin-woo was fast, but not fast enough to dodge lightning.

A bolt of raw, blue-white power erupted from the ring, not a crude
projectile, but a guided spear of pure energy. It crossed the distance
in less than a thought, striking Jin-woo square in the chest.

His entire body seized. The world dissolved into a white-hot agony. The
Scribe's Aegis on his soul flickered and died, its benevolent magic
overloaded and instantly consumed by the raw, chaotic power. He felt his
muscles lock, his nerves burn, his very Ki flash-boil within his
meridians. Smoke, thick with the smell of ozone and burnt cloth, erupted
from his body. He was a man struck by the heavens, and for a long,
silent moment, he stood perfectly still, his body crackling, his head
bowed.

The Shaman let out a triumphant, cackling sneer. The puny human was
finished. The stored spell was an absolute. Nothing could survive it. It
had worked.

But the Shaman was wrong.

It was not pain. Not merely. It was a library of violent, untamed
knowledge being force-fed into his soul in a single, agonizing instant.

His Scribe's Insight, the part of him that was the Scroll of a Thousand
Truths, did not recoil from the blast; it lunged at it. It was presented
with a new language, a syntax of pure, elemental fury. And its one,
fundamental purpose was to *read*.

In the microsecond that his body was being torn apart, his soul was
deconstructing the attack. It read the spell's structure, its grammar of
destruction, the way the Ki was woven to command the very air. It was
not absorbing energy; it was learning a language. The foreign, violent
script of the lightning was being copied, integrated, and instantly,
perfectly, understood.

The smoke began to clear. The seizing stopped. Jin-woo stood, his head
still bowed, a statue carved from charcoal and silence.

The Shaman's sneer began to falter, a flicker of uncertainty in its
black eyes.

Slowly, deliberately, Jin-woo raised his head.

The change was terrifying. His eyes, normally the calm, clear blue of a
mountain lake, were now pools of captured lightning. Arcing filaments of
raw, blue-white energy danced across his irises. The cold, analytical
light of the scribe was now married to the chaotic, untamed fury of the
storm.

A low, guttural sound, half chuckle, half the rumble of distant thunder,
escaped his lips.

"My turn."
