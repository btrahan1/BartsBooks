# Chapter 29: The Anchor and the River

When Roric's team hit the town's perimeter, it was with the force of a
tsunami. They were a torrent of honed, righteous fury, a whirlwind of
spear and shield that slammed into the goblin's flank and tore it
asunder.

The scene that greeted them was chaos incarnate.

The western gate was a charnel house. Less than a dozen men had held the
line against fifty or more goblins. The battle was not a clash of battle
lines, but a desperate, swirling melee fought over the bodies of the
fallen. The town was holding by a single, fraying thread. And that
thread was woven from the souls of two men.

Sten, the giant, was the mountain. He stood in the narrowest point of
the broken gateway, a living wall of flesh and iron. His movements were
not fast, but they were deliberate, heavy, and absolutely final. A
massive smithing hammer, taken from the forge, rose and fell in a
devastating, unstoppable rhythm. Each swing crushed a goblin attacker,
sending it flying back into the press. He was covered from head to toe
in black, oozing wounds, a dozen goblin blades sticking from his back
and shoulders like the quills of a monstrous porcupine. Blood ran in
rivers down his face, but his feet were rooted to the earth. He was
immovable. Unshakeable.

And he was dying. His Ki was a guttering, defiant bonfire, burning its
last fuel in a glorious, final conflagration.

If Sten was the anchor holding the gate, the other man was the river
flowing around it. It was the reformed killer, the man who had offered
his violent past as a shield. He moved like a deadly wind, two short
swords a blur in his hands. While the mountain held the center, the
river flowed along the edges, a whirlwind of precise, lethal strikes.
Goblins died by the handful, their throats slit, their formations broken
before they could even bring their numbers to bear on the giant. He was
everywhere at once, a phantom of vengeance.

But he too, was falling. He was bleeding from a dozen lesser wounds, his
movements growing a fraction slower, his parries a little less perfect.
The unstoppable river was beginning to run dry.

This was the scene Roric's team crashed into. They were the
reinforcements, the breaking wave that the defenders had prayed for.

They swept through the remaining goblin force like vengeance itself.

The goblins, caught between the unyielding mountain, the deadly river,
and the sudden, silent tsunami at their backs, shattered. Their pathetic
morale, built on cruelty and numbers, evaporated in the face of such
focused, disciplined violence. They broke and fled, only to be cut down
by the relentless pursuit of the Watch.

Within minutes, the battle was over. The last goblin fell with a choked
gurgle, and a sudden, ringing silence descended on the gateway, broken
only by the ragged, gasping breaths of the survivors.

Roric and his men stared at the two figures who had held the line. They
saw Sten, the giant, still standing, the great hammer finally slipping
from his grasp to thud on the bloody ground. He looked at them, a faint,
bloody smile on his lips, and then his eyes rolled back, and the
mountain finally, slowly, began to fall.
