# Chapter 30: The Offering

As the mountain fell, a new strength rose to meet it.

A woman, tall and broad-shouldered with the powerful build of the town
blacksmith, surged forward from the crowd of horrified villagers. She
moved with a desperate speed, wedging her own body under Sten's as he
collapsed. She caught him. The impact of the giant's armored weight
drove her to the ground, the air blasting from her lungs in a pained
gasp, but she cushioned his fall. She lay pinned beneath the man who had
saved them all, her arms wrapped around him, her face buried in his
shoulder, not complaining, not crying out, just holding him.

At the same time, the river was tended. As the reformed killer finally
crumpled, his short swords clattering to the ground, no fewer than three
women rushed to his side. They were sisters, the town weavers, and they
moved as one, their faces grim. They began tearing strips from their own
dresses, their movements efficient and practiced as they staunched the
flow of blood from his dozen wounds. Their concern was not just worry;
it was a reflection of a deeper pain, an awe for the man he had become
and the agony of seeing him broken. Their hearts were breaking.

Kael and the rest of the warriors arrived, carrying the litter. They saw
the carnage, the victory, and the terrible, terrible cost.

A weak voice cut through the scene. "Put me down."

It was Jin-woo. He was struggling to sit up on the makeshift litter, his
face a deathly pallor, his eyes barely able to focus. Kael gently helped
him to his feet, and Jin-woo leaned heavily on his student, his body
trembling with the effort.

His Scribe's Insight, though faint and flickering, was enough. He saw
the truth in an instant. Sten's Ki core was not just guttering; it was
fractured, shattered by the sheer force of his final stand. The river's
meridians were torn, bleeding his life force away with every passing
second.

Jin-woo's prognosis was grim and immediate. They were beyond any
conventional healing. They needed a massive, direct infusion of pure
life energy, a miracle of Ki he was in no state to perform. In his
weakened condition, trying to save them would kill all three of them. A
bitter, helpless despair washed over him.

He was startled by a new movement. An old man, the town's woodcarver,
stepped forward and took his wife's hand. She, in turn, took the hand of
the person next to her. One by one, the villagers---the weavers, the
farmers, the merchants, the women, the elderly---linked hands. A silent,
unbroken circle formed, enclosing the fallen saviors, Jin-woo, and the
entire Watch. It was an act of spontaneous, unified will.

The old woodcarver, his face etched with grief but his voice clear and
strong, spoke for them all. He looked directly at Jin-woo.

"Take our life force," he said, the words a sacred offering. "We are not
warriors. We cannot wield Ki as you do. But we are alive because of
them. Our life is their victory."

He looked at the faces of his neighbors, all nodding in solemn
agreement.

"Take as much as you need," the old man finished, his voice breaking
with emotion. "But please... if you can... save our heroes."
