# Chapter 32: The Weight of an Angel

The sacred silence that had fallen over the square was broken by a
sudden, undignified grunt.

"Get off me, you big oaf!"

The voice was gruff, muffled, and came from somewhere underneath the
newly revived Sten. The blacksmith, who had been pinned beneath him, was
now glaring up at the side of his head.

The giant blinked, his consciousness returning like a slow, foggy dawn.
He breathed in, a wheezing, rattling sound in his newly mended chest. He
shifted his immense weight slightly.

"But this is so... comfortable," he rumbled, his voice thick with
confusion. He looked down, trying to see what, or who, was cushioning
him. He looked down into the face of an angel.

She wasn't a classic beauty. Her face was smudged with soot and sweat,
her brow was furrowed in a permanent scowl, and her nose had clearly
been broken at least once. But her eyes, staring up at him with a
mixture of profound relief and utter exasperation, were the kindest he
had ever seen. She had not hesitated. She had not screamed or run. She
had stood her ground and caught a falling mountain. In that moment, he
saw an inner strength that dwarfed his own. He saw a soul as solid and
as true as forged steel.

The words tumbled out of his mouth before he even knew he was thinking
them, a thought spoken with the simple, unvarnished truth of a man who
had just returned from the dead.

"Will you marry me?"

The blacksmith's eyes widened. Her scowl deepened. "Are you concussed? I
just saved your life, and now you want to ruin mine?"

"Please?" Sten asked, his voice softening into a plea. "I'm very good at
lifting heavy things."

A faint, unwilling smile tugged at the corner of her lips. She gave his
chest a solid, resounding thump with her fist. "We'll talk about it
after you learn to do your own damn laundry. Now get *off*!"

A wave of gentle, relieved laughter rippled through the exhausted crowd.
The tension that had held them all in its grip finally, completely,
shattered. The sacred had given way to the profane, the epic to the
touchingly human.

Across the way, the three weaver sisters were helping the reformed
killer to sit up. He looked at his hands, then at the villagers, his
eyes filled with a dawning, wondrous light. He had been a river of
death, but they had filled him with life. The old, violent story of his
past had been washed away, leaving a clean, unwritten page. He met the
eyes of the eldest sister, and for the first time, he didn't see fear or
revulsion. He saw a deep, quiet respect. He simply nodded, a silent
promise to be worthy of the life they had all given back to him.

Kael, supporting the utterly depleted but conscious Jin-woo, watched the
two small scenes unfold. He saw a mountain finding his anchor, and a
river finding its banks. He looked at his master, who was watching with
a tired but deeply satisfied smile.

The battle was won. The heroes were saved. And in the ashes of the
fight, something new and incredibly strong was beginning to grow.
