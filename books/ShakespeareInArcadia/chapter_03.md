# Chapter 3: The Blue Phantasm and the Impish Guide

The transition was not a graceful fade to black, nor was it the dramatic descent into a pit of fire that Dalton had often seen depicted in the moral plays of his youth. Instead, it was like being yanked through a particularly narrow window by a very impatient constable. One moment, he was bleeding into the mud of a London alleyway; the next, he was lying flat on his back in a meadow so vibrant it looked as if it had been painted by a man with far too much green pigment and a complete disregard for realism.

Dalton sat up, his movements stiff and awkward. He expected the sharp, cold bite of the dirk in his side, but there was nothing. No blood, no hole in his threadbare tunic, and no lingering ache of death. He was whole.

“Alas!” Dalton proclaimed, his voice ringing out across the quiet field. “A chance to start anew! I find myself in a realm of verdant splendor, perhaps the very Arcadia of the poets!”

He stood, dusting off his breeches with a practiced, noble flourish. He didn't know where he was, but he knew how to be there. He straightened his back, lifted his chin, and prepared to greet the local nobility.

Instead, he was greeted by the blue glass again. It hung in the air, defiant and glowing, refusing to move even as a soft breeze rustled the nearby clover.

**[ CHARACTER INITIALIZATION ]**
**[ NAME: DALTON ]**
**[ LEVEL: 1 ]**
**[ CLASS: NONE ]**

Dalton frowned, his eyebrows knitting together in a look of profound disapproval. “My good phantom,” he began, addressing the floating pane of light. “You have made a most egregious error in the ledger. My name is not merely ‘Dalton.’ I am Sir Dalton. To omit the title is a slight against my station and my honor. Pray, correct the scroll at once.”

The screen flickered, a sound like static crackling in the air.

**[ ERROR: TITLE 'SIR' NOT RECOGNIZED. NO KNIGHTHOOD RECORDS FOUND. ]**
**[ NAME REMAINS: DALTON ]**

“How rude!” Dalton huffed, crossing his arms. “I have carried that title through the streets of London for a decade! To have it stripped by a piece of luminous window-glass is… well, it is simply uncouth.”

“You’re telling me,” a voice rasped from somewhere near his left boot.

Dalton jumped, nearly tripping over a particularly large clump of daisies. He spun around, his hand instinctively going to where his sword-hilt should have been, only to find empty air. 

Standing on a flat rock was a creature that looked like a gargoyle that had been shrunk in the rain and then left to dry in a chimney. It was barely a foot tall, with bat-like ears that drooped, a tail that twitched with irritable energy, and skin the color of a bruised plum. It wore a tiny, tattered vest and looked as if it hadn't slept since the reign of King Edward.

“What manner of sprite are you?” Dalton demanded, recovering his dignity and offering a cautious, courtly bow. “Are you a servant of the Great Architect? Or perhaps a familiar of this blue phantasm?”

The creature sighed, a sound of pure, unadulterated exhaustion. “I’m an Imp, you giant peacock. Name’s Sammael-Thrax-the-Third-of-the-Ash-Wastes-and-Chronicler-of-the-Lesser-Hells. But you can call me Sam, since I’m pretty sure your brain would leak out your ears if you tried to say the whole thing.”

“Master Sam,” Dalton said, latching onto the shorter name with relief. “I am Sir Dalton. It is a pleasure to make your acquaintance, though I must say, your manners are a bit… unrefined for a celestial guide.”

Sam’s eyes—large, amber orbs with slit pupils—widened as he looked at Dalton. He didn't look impressed. He looked flabbergasted. “Celestial guide? Buddy, I’m here because I accidentally let a Soul-Eater loose in the High Court of the Abyss. My punishment is you. I’m stuck as your ‘System Liaison’ until you either make something of yourself or, more likely, get eaten by a level-two boar.”

Sam hopped off the rock and walked a circle around Dalton, sniffing at his ragged boots. “I was told I was getting a ‘Heroic Reincarnation.’ I was expecting a warlord, or at least a halfway decent pickpocket. But look at you. You look like you’ve spent your life sweeping floors and huffing ink fumes.”

“I am a knight of the spirit!” Dalton declared, his voice rising with theatrical fervor. “I have memorized the words of the greatest bards! I have defended the honor of maidens!”

“Yeah, and how’d that work out for you?” Sam asked, pointing a tiny, clawed finger at the blue screen. “Take a look at your stats, ‘Sir.’ They’re pathetic.”

The screen changed again, displaying a list of numbers that Dalton found deeply confusing.

**[ ATTRIBUTES ]**
*   **STRENGTH: 10**
*   **DEXTERITY: 10**
*   **CONSTITUTION: 10**
*   **INTELLIGENCE: 10**
*   **WISDOM: 10**
*   **CHARISMA: 13**

Sam stared at the screen, his eyebrows shooting up toward his drooping ears. “Tens? Straight tens? I’ve seen cabbage with more Strength than that. You’re the most mediocre thing to ever cross the veil, Dalton. You’re a baseline. A blank slate. A zero.”

“Ten is a most noble number!” Dalton argued, though he had no idea what it meant. “It is the number of the commandments! It is a double-handful of destiny!”

“It’s the score of a common peasant,” Sam groaned, rubbing his temples with his tiny hands. “Except for that thirteen in Charisma. Why do you have a thirteen in Charisma? You look like a drowned rat in a servant’s tunic.”

“Perhaps the phantom recognizes my stage presence,” Dalton said, smoothing his hair. “Thirteen! A baker’s dozen of charm! I am satisfied.”

“Of course you are,” Sam muttered. “Listen, ‘Sir’ Dalton—and stop looking at me like that, I’m the only one who can see or hear you, and vice versa. To everyone else in Arcadia, you’re just a guy talking to a patch of empty air. If you want that ‘Sir’ title to be official, you have to earn it. The System doesn't hand out knighthoods to level-one nobodies.”

Dalton’s eyes lit up. “Earning it? A quest! A trial of arms! Tell me, Master Sam, where is the nearest dragon? Which kingdom requires my service?”

Sam let out a dry, hacking laugh. “Dragon? You’d trip over your own feet if a dragon even looked at you. No, if you want to be a Knight, you first have to survive long enough to reach Level 5. Then you can choose the ‘Warrior’ class. After that, maybe—maybe—you can find a real lord to swear fealty to and earn a proper title. But for now? You’re just a Level 1 Vagrant.”

“A Vagrant?” Dalton recoiled as if struck. “The word is most uncouth.”

“The word is accurate,” Sam countered. “And since you don’t have a copper to your name, you need two things: a job and experience. In that order. If you want to eat tonight, you’re going to have to find work. And if you want to level up, you’re going to have to kill some Slimes.”

“Slimes?” Dalton echoed. “What manner of foul beast is a Slime? Is it a cousin to the Hydra? A spawn of the basilisk?”

“It’s a blob of jelly that wobbles,” Sam said, deadpan. “They’re in the woods just past this meadow. They’re slow, they’re stupid, and they’re the only thing I’m confident won't kill you in the first five minutes. Well, mostly confident.”

Dalton straightened his tunic, his face setting in a mask of grim determination. He looked out toward the woods, seeing not a wobbling jelly, but a foul monstrosity that required the intervention of a hero.

“Very well, Master Sam,” Dalton said, offering a sharp, military nod. “I shall seek out these gelatinous fiends. I shall cleanse the woods of their wobble, and in doing so, I shall begin my ascent to my rightful station. To the woods! For honor! For glory! And for a fresh apple that has not been bitten!”

Sam watched him march off, his tiny shoulders slumped. “This is going to be the longest eternity of my life,” he whispered to the empty air. “I hope those slimes are hungry.”

Dalton, meanwhile, was already three paces into the meadow, shouting greetings to the trees and promising them that their savior had arrived. He didn't see the screen flicker one last time before it vanished.

Sir Dalton didn't need the quest notification to tell him where to go. He followed the sun, his heart light and his head filled with the echoes of a thousand stage-plays. Arcadia was waiting. And he intended to give it a performance it would never forget.
