# Chapter 4: The Righteous Weapon

The meadow was vast, a sea of hip-high grass and wildflowers that swayed in a synchronized dance Dalton found suspiciously well-rehearsed. He marched with his head held high, his boots—miraculously repaired by the same magic that had stolen his life—crunching softly against the damp earth. To Dalton, this wasn't just a walk; it was the opening montage of a great epic.

Sam, however, was not participating in the majesty. The tiny imp was hovering a few inches off the ground, his bat-like wings flapping with a sound like a wet rag hitting a floor. He looked bored, his chin resting in his clawed hand as he drifted alongside Dalton’s head.

“You know, for a guy who just died, you’re awfully chipper,” Sam observed, swatting away a curious butterfly. “Most people spend their first hour in Arcadia screaming, vomiting, or trying to find a way to sue the universe. You’re over here looking like you’re about to break into song.”

“Master Sam,” Dalton said, not breaking his stride, “the air here is crisp, the sun is warm, and for the first time in a decade, I am not being shouted at by Dickon for misplacing a prop. Why should I not rejoice? This is a second act! A chance to play the hero without the constraints of a script!”

“Yeah, well, heroes usually have swords,” Sam pointed out, gesturing to Dalton’s empty belt. “Or at least a sharp rock. You’re currently armed with a high opinion of yourself and a half-eaten apple. It’s quite a walk to the nearest town, ‘Sir Dalton.’ You might want to pick up a stick or something.”

Dalton stopped mid-step, his brow furrowing as he considered the suggestion. He turned his gaze toward a cluster of ancient, gnarled oaks that bordered the meadow. 

“A stick?” Dalton echoed, his voice dropping into a deeper, more heroic register. “To some, perhaps, it is merely a fragment of a tree. A discarded limb of the forest. But to one such as myself, Master Sam, it shall be a righteous weapon. A staff of justice! A lance of the common man! But tell me, kind sprite, why do you urge me to arm myself with such haste? Is there a foe lurking in the tall grass? A spy of some rival house?”

Sam landed on Dalton’s shoulder, his weight no more than that of a large crow. He pointed a tiny finger toward a small clearing about twenty paces ahead. “Because that slime over there looks hungry.”

Dalton squinted. In the center of the clearing, sitting atop a flat grey stone, was a translucent, emerald-green blob. It was about the size of a large pumpkin and was vibrating with a rhythmic, wet sound. It looked less like a monster and more like a bowl of lime jelly that had achieved a very limited form of sentience.

“That?” Dalton asked, his tone skeptical. “That is the gelatinous fiend of which you spoke? It appears to be… wobbling.”

“It’s a Green Slime, Level One,” Sam said, his voice dripping with mock awe. “And it’s currently digesting a beetle. If you don't find a stick, you’re going to be its dessert. Go on, Excalibur is waiting.”

Dalton marched toward the edge of the woods, his eyes searching the ground with the intensity of a man looking for a lost crown. He passed over a dozen perfectly functional branches, dismissing them with a shake of his head.

“Too short,” he murmured, tossing aside a sturdy piece of ash. “Lacks the necessary balance for a parry. This one? Too brittle. It would shatter against the first stroke of destiny.”

Finally, his eyes landed on a branch of fallen oak. It was roughly four feet long, slightly curved at one end like a shepherd's crook, and stripped of its bark by some long-forgotten storm. It was grey, weathered, and looked exactly like a stick.

Dalton picked it up. He didn't just lift it; he drew it from the earth as if he were pulling it from an anvil. He held it aloft, the sunlight catching the smooth, grey wood.

“Behold!” Dalton cried. “The Branch of Chivalry! Though it lacks the bite of steel, it shall serve as my standard in this uncouth wilderness!”

“It’s a stick, Dalton,” Sam sighed, hovering back into the air. “Just hit the thing before it sees us.”

Dalton didn't just "hit the thing." He approached the clearing with the measured steps of a duelist. He held the oak branch in both hands, his weight shifted to his back foot, his eyes narrowed in a gaze of intense concentration.

The Slime, sensing movement, stopped its vibrating. It let out a wet, sucking sound and slowly turned its entire body toward Dalton. It didn't have eyes, but it seemed to possess a keen sense of "thing-about-to-hit-me."

“Foul spawn of the mire!” Dalton boomed, his voice echoing through the trees. “Prepare to meet thy doom! I am Sir Dalton, and I shall not see this fair meadow stained by your gelatinous presence!”

The Slime responded by letting out a small, green bubble that popped with a sound like a wet sneeze.

“Aha! A challenge!” Dalton cried.

He lunged. It was a move he had practiced a thousand times in the shadows of the Globe—a sweeping, overhead strike designed to look spectacular from the back rows of the gallery. He brought the oak branch down with all the strength his 'ten' score could muster.

The stick hit the top of the Slime with a dull *thwack*. 

Instead of shattering like a skull, the Slime simply compressed. It absorbed the blow with a squelch, its green surface rippling like a pond hit by a stone. Dalton’s hands vibrated from the impact, the shock of the blow traveling up his arms and rattling his teeth.

“How… resilient!” Dalton wheezed, his dignity slightly shaken as the stick became momentarily stuck in the Slime’s translucent body.

The Slime, apparently annoyed by being used as a drum, lunged forward. It was a slow, heavy hop, but it caught Dalton by surprise. The green mass slammed into his shins, the cold, slimy texture soaking through his wool stockings.

“Gah! Unhand me, you wobbling knave!” Dalton shouted, hopping backward. 

“It’s a slime, Dalton, it doesn't have hands!” Sam shouted, clutching his sides as he hovered above the fray. “Hit it again! Aim for the core! The little red bit in the middle!”

Dalton regained his footing, his face flushed with exertion. He saw the "core"—a small, marble-sized nucleus of dark red light deep within the green jelly. He adjusted his grip on the Branch of Chivalry, his expression shifting from theatrical to genuinely determined.

“For honor!” he yelled, abandonning the overhead strike for a more practical, if less dramatic, thrust.

He drove the end of the stick into the center of the green mass. The wood pierced the jelly-like surface, meeting some resistance before sliding through. There was a sickening *pop* as the oak branch struck the red core.

The Slime shuddered. Its vibrant green color suddenly turned a dull, sickly grey. A moment later, the entire creature dissolved into a pool of foul-smelling liquid, leaving behind nothing but a few shimmering particles and a small, hard object on the stone.

Dalton stood over the remains, breathing hard, his 'Branch of Chivalry' dripping with green goo.

Dalton stared at the blue screen that had popped into existence. “Level Two?” he asked, wiping a smudge of slime from his tunic. “And it seems the phantom offers me a choice. A single point of… essence? A fragment of my own potential to be bestowed upon my character?”

“It’s an attribute point, Dalton,” Sam said, landing on the rock and picking up the two copper coins with his tiny claws. “Don't make it sound like you’re being ordained. It’s just a numbers game. You can put it into Intelligence if you want to understand why you’re so bad at this, or Dexterity if you want to stop tripping over your own feet. Personally, I’d suggest Charisma. At least then people might believe your lies for a second longer before they kill you.”

Dalton paced the small clearing, the 'Branch of Chivalry' resting on his shoulder like a scepter. He ignored the imp’s cynicism, his eyes fixed on the floating blue list.

“Intelligence?” Dalton mused, his voice dropping into a philosophical hum. “To know the stars and the secrets of the ancients? A noble pursuit, surely. But I have the words of the Bard; what more wisdom could a man require? And Dexterity? To move like a shadow in the night? No. A knight does not skulk. A knight does not dance around his foes like a court jester. A knight is a pillar! A fortress!”

He stopped and looked at his thin, stagehand arms. They were calloused from ropes and brooms, but they lacked the iron of the heroes he had watched from the wings.

“Charisma is a gift I already possess in abundance,” Dalton continued, puffing out his chest. “But to be a knight, Master Sam… to truly stand against the tide of the uncouth… one must have righteous power in his arms! One must be able to strike a blow that rings through the ages! Strength! That is the foundation of the hero!”

He reached out and tapped the word 'Strength' on the blue screen with the solemnity of a king signing a treaty.

“There!” Dalton proclaimed, flexing his arm. He didn't look any different, but he felt as if his muscles were suddenly forged of slightly better quality timber. “I feel the power of the oak flowing through me! I am a Level Two knight-in-waiting, and my arm is strengthened for the trials ahead!”

“You’re a Level Two vagrant with an eleven in Strength,” Sam sighed, hovering back into the air. “You’re still weaker than the average tavern brawler, but at least you might be able to lift that stick without wheezing now. Can we go? The town’s another hour that way.”

“The town!” Dalton cried, shouldering his branch. “Onward, Master Sam! To Oakhaven! Let us see if they have a blacksmith worthy of my new-found might!”

Dalton began to march again, his step a fraction firmer. He wasn't just a stagehand anymore. He was a man of increasing power. And the world of Arcadia was about to learn that Sir Dalton did not take his cue from anyone.
