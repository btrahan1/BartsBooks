# Chapter 7: Rat Vanquisher

The cellar of the Golden Pottle was a subterranean nightmare of sagging timbers, damp earth, and the oppressive scent of sour hops and ancient decay. It was a space designed for the quiet storage of ale-casks and grain-sacks, but in the flickering, guttering light of the single tallow candle Dalton held aloft, it looked like the dark, forgotten dungeon of a tyrant.

“Vile den of the four-legged usurpers!” Dalton’s voice whispered through the gloom, hushed by the weight of the overhead floorboards. He held his ‘Branch of Chivalry’ in his right hand, the grey oak stick looking remarkably small against the shadows.

Sam was hovering just behind his ear, his wings barely moving. “Can we keep it down? Rats have ears, Dalton. And unlike you, they actually know how to use them.”

“A knight does not sneak, Master Sam!” Dalton replied, though he did lower his voice to a resonant murmur. “We bring the light of justice into the dark. If the vermin hear my approach, let them tremble!”

They didn't tremble. Instead, they hissed.

From behind a row of ale-barrels, a pair of eyes caught the candlelight—tiny, red beads of malice. A moment later, the first rat scurried into the open. It was the size of a small dog, with mangy, soot-colored fur and teeth that looked like yellowed needles. 

**[ CELLAR RAT: LEVEL 1 ]**

Dalton lunged. It was a movement that started with a noble intent and ended with him tripping over a loose stone. He swung his stick in a wide, desperate arc.

*Thwack!*

The branch struck the rat’s flank, sending it tumbling into a pile of empty sacks. But unlike the slimes, the rat didn't pop. It recovered with a terrifying speed, letting out a sharp, piercing shriek. It lunged forward, its teeth sinking into Dalton’s calf.

**[ DAMAGE RECEIVED: 2 ]**
**[ HEALTH: 108 / 110 ]**

“Gah! Unhand my limb, you whiskered knave!” Dalton shouted, hopping on one leg and striking at the creature with the butt of his stick. 

He managed to crush the rat’s skull on the third try, the creature dissolving into grey mist and leaving behind a single, shimmering copper coin. Dalton leaned against a barrel, his breath coming in ragged hitches. The bite burned with a dull, throbbing heat.

“See?” Sam said, hovering over the dead rat. “They hit back. And there’s a lot more than one of them. Kill ten and let's get out of here. My wings are getting sticky from the humidity.”

Dalton straightened his back, ignoring the sting in his leg. “Master Sam, look into the shadows. Do you see them? The red eyes? There are a lot more than ten rats here.”

Sam squinted. The darkness beyond the candlelight was alive with movement. Scores of red eyes blinked back at them from every corner of the cellar.

“Yeah, okay,” Sam muttered, his voice dropping an octave. “That’s a lot of rats. Forget the ten. Just kill what you can and we’ll run for it.”

“Run?” Dalton’s voice rose with an indignant, theatrical resonance. “A Knight leaves no job unfinished! Master Mott has entrusted me with the sanctity of his ale! To leave even one of these furry fiends to gnaw upon the casks would be a stain upon my honor that no amount of salt-pork could wash away!”

“Dalton, you’re bleeding!” Sam shouted. “It’s a basement! There’s no honor in a basement!”

“There is honor wherever a man stands for what is right!” Dalton declared.

He dove back into the fray. The next six hours were a blur of desperate strikes, sharp bites, and the rhythmic, wet sound of oak hitting fur. Dalton moved through the cellar like a man possessed, his ‘Branch of Chivalry’ now stained with more than just slime. He was bitten on the arms, the shins, and once, painfully, on the rear, but he didn't stop. He was stoic in his suffering, reciting lines from *Henry V* to drown out the sound of the hissing vermin.

**[ QUEST PROGRESS: 10 / 10 RATS KILLED ]**
**[ QUEST PROGRESS: 25 / 10 RATS KILLED ]**
**[ QUEST PROGRESS: 50 / 10 RATS KILLED ]**

By the sixth hour, Dalton was a wreck. His tunic was in tatters, his face was covered in a layer of cellar-dust and dried blood, and his Health bar was dangerously low. But the cellar was quiet. The small rats were gone.

“Finally,” Sam wheezed, sitting atop a grain-sack. “Can we go now? You’ve killed enough rats to feed a small army of cats.”

Dalton didn't answer. He was staring at a massive hole in the far wall, behind the furthest row of barrels. A low, rhythmic growl was coming from within—a sound like a bellows being worked by a giant.

“The source,” Dalton whispered, his eyes narrowing. “The heart of the infection.”

He stepped toward the hole, his stick held in a white-knuckled grip. As he approached, a figure emerged from the darkness.

It was a rat, but it was a rat that had clearly spent its life feasting on enchanted grain and magical ale-dregs. It was the size of a pony, with fur as thick as a carpet and a crown of jagged, bone-like protrusions growing from its skull. Its eyes weren't red; they were a burning, sickly yellow.

**[ THE RAT KING: LEVEL 5 (BOSS) ]**

Sam fell off his grain-sack. “Level five? Dalton, run! That thing will eat you in one bite! This wasn't in the job description!”

The Rat King lunged with a speed that defied its massive size. It slammed into Dalton, throwing him backward into a stack of empty crates. 

**[ DAMAGE RECEIVED: 12 ]**
**[ HEALTH: 32 / 110 ]**

Dalton coughed, the air knocked from his lungs. He looked up at the beast as it loomed over him, its massive teeth dripping with a foul, glowing saliva. He felt his Strength—his newly acquired point of 'righteous power'—surging in his arms.

“You… you uncouth… overblown… RODENT!” Dalton roared.

He didn't use a theatrical lunge this time. He used the desperation of a man who had nothing left to lose. He swung the ‘Branch of Chivalry’ with every ounce of his eleven Strength, aiming not for the body, but for the bone-crown atop the creature's head.

The oak branch struck the crown with a sound like a thunderclap. The wood finally gave way, shattering into a dozen pieces, but the impact was enough. The Rat King’s head was driven into the damp earth, its yellow eyes flickering and then going dark.

The beast let out one final, earth-shaking shudder and dissolved into a massive cloud of black mist. 

Dalton lay in the ruins of the crates, breathing hard. He was covered in black dust, his stick was a handful of splinters, and he was down to a sliver of health. But in the center of the mist, something was glowing.

He reached out and picked up a heavy, black wooden club. It was carved from the heartwood of a dark-iron tree and studded with what looked like the teeth of the Rat King. It was heavy, brutal, and perfectly balanced.

**[ LOOT ACQUIRED: "RAT SLAYER" (+1 CLUB) ]**
**[ SKILL UP: BLUNT WEAPONS LEVEL 3 ]**
**[ LEVEL UP! DALTON IS NOW LEVEL 3 ]**

Seven hours after he had descended, the cellar hatch creaked open. Mott was standing there, a lantern in his hand and a look of grim resignation on his face. He had already prepared a burial shroud; no one survived seven hours in his cellar.

Dalton emerged first. He didn't climb out; he hauled himself up, leaning heavily on his new black club. He was covered in dust, blood, and the black soot of the Rat King’s remains. His tunic was a rag, and he looked half-dead.

But he was beaming.

“My good Master Mott!” Dalton wheezed, offering a shaky but triumphant bow. “Your cellar is… purified. The usurper is fallen, and your ale-casks are once more under the protection of a True Knight.”

Mott stared. He looked at the black club, then at the man who looked like he had been chewed on by a dragon. He looked down into the cellar, which was now eerily quiet and smelled of nothing but dust.

“You… you killed the King?” Mott asked, his voice barely a whisper.

“A trifle,” Dalton said, his eyes rolling back slightly as his exhaustion finally caught up with him. “Though he was a most… uncouth conversationalist.”

Mott didn't say another word about the barrels. He grabbed Dalton’s arm and hauled him into the taproom. He sat him down by the fire, brought him a bowl of stew that actually contained meat, and a mug of his best ale.

“Sleep on the house tonight,” Mott grunted, his voice thick with a strange, gruff respect. “And take this.”

He handed Dalton a heavy sack. Inside was a mountain of rat fur, tails, and teeth—the loot Dalton had subconsciously gathered during his crusade.

“Take it to Harlen at the north end of town,” Mott said. “He’s a tanner. He’ll buy the fur, and he might be able to craft you some leather armor. You can’t keep wandering around in those rags, ‘Sir’ Dalton. You look like a ghost.”

Dalton nodded, his head drooping toward his chest. “A ghost… with a club,” he murmured, his fingers curling around the grip of ‘Rat Slayer.’

Sam landed on the edge of the stew bowl, looking at his charge with a mix of disbelief and something that might have been pride if it wasn't so buried under annoyance. 

“Level three,” Sam whispered. “A Boss kill. And a free room. You’re either the luckiest idiot in Arcadia, or the Bard is actually looking out for you.”

Dalton didn't hear him. He was already asleep, snoring with the quiet, dignified rhythm of a hero who had won the day.
