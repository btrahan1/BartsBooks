# Chapter 8: The Gearing of the Hero

The morning sun filtered through the narrow, grime-streaked window of the Golden Pottle, casting a slanting beam of light across Dalton’s face. He woke not with a groan of pain, but with a surge of vitality that seemed to hum through his very bones. The lumpy straw mattress no longer felt like a bed of nails; it felt like a staging ground.

He sat up, stretching his arms over his head. The bites from the cellar rats had faded into faint, silvery scars, and the exhaustion that had threatened to pull him into the earth the night before was gone, replaced by a restless energy.

Immediately, the blue screen flared to life, more vibrant than ever.

**[ SYSTEM UPDATE: DALTON ]**
**[ LEVEL: 3 ]**
**[ ATTRIBUTE POINT UNASSIGNED ]**
**[ TITLE EVOLVED: SLIME SLAYER -> VERMIN SLAYER ]**
**[ EFFECT: +1 DAMAGE VS ALL VERMIN AND SLIMES ]**
**[ WEAPON EQUIPPED: RAT SLAYER (+1 CLUB) ]**
**[ WEAPON BONUS: +1 DEXTERITY, +1 DAMAGE VS RODENTS ]**
**[ SKILL LEVEL: BLUNT WEAPONS (LEVEL 3) ]**

Dalton stared at the screen, his fingers tracing the glowing letters. “Vermin Slayer,” he whispered, the words tasting like fine wine. He stood, grabbing the heavy black club from the bedside table. It felt lighter than it had the night before, the balance almost intuitive now. 

“Master Sam!” Dalton cried, striking a pose of quiet, focused intensity. “Behold the evolution of the hero! I am no longer merely the scourge of the meadow; I am the Vanquisher of Vermin! The phantom recognizes my growth, bestowing upon me a blade—well, a club—of darkness and power! And a point of essence remains! I shall bestow it upon my Strength once more, for a knight must have the power to crush the usurper beneath his heel!”

**[ ATTRIBUTE ASSIGNED: STRENGTH ]**
**[ STRENGTH: 12 ]**

Sam, who had been busy trying to catch a sunbeam in his tiny claws, let out a long, dry sigh. “Twelve Strength. Wow. You’re now officially as strong as a particularly motivated farmhand. Congratulations, ‘Vermin Slayer.’ Are you done talking to the wall? Mott’s actually cooked something that doesn't look like wet sawdust, and I’m pretty sure it’s for you.”

Dalton didn't need a second invitation. He descended to the taproom, his ‘Rat Slayer’ club slung over his shoulder. Mott was there, and for the first time, the turnip-faced man offered a curt, respectfull nod.

“Breakfast,” Mott grunted, sliding a plate across the counter. 

It was a hearty meal—fried eggs, a thick slab of ham, and bread that had actually seen a real oven. Dalton ate with a dignified appetite, feeling his newly increased Constitution and Strength absorbing the nutrients. As he finished, he gathered the sack of rat fur and tails he had earned in the darkness.

“To Harlen’s!” Dalton proclaimed, standing and offering a bow to the innkeeper. “Master Mott, your hospitality has been… adequate. I shall return once I have been properly outfitted for the next act of this drama.”

“Just don't come back covered in more rat-blood,” Mott replied, though he didn't sound entirely annoyed.

The walk to the north end of Oakhaven was a short one, through streets that were already bustling with the morning’s uncouth business. Dalton walked with a new-found confidence, his ‘Rat Slayer’ club a visible sign of his status. He reached a building that smelled strongly of lye, old blood, and tanning salts. A sign above the door showed a stretched hide and a set of iron shears.

Harlen the Tanner was a man who looked as if he were made of the very leather he worked. His skin was brown, wrinkled, and tough, and his hands were permanently stained a deep, earthy red. He looked up from a half-finished tunic as Dalton entered.

“Mott said you might be coming,” Harlen said, his voice like sandpaper on wood. “Show me what you got.”

Dalton placed the heavy sack on the workbench with a flourish. Harlen opened it, his eyes narrowing as he inspected the thick, mangy fur of the Rat King and the dozens of smaller pelts.

“Good quality,” Harlen grunted. “Strange quality, actually. This fur… it’s tougher than usual. I can make you a vest and some leggings. It’ll keep the teeth out, at least.”

“I seek armor worthy of a knight!” Dalton announced, gesturing to his own tattered tunic. “Something that speaks of my status! Perhaps a breastplate of reinforced hide, with pauldrons of the King’s own fur!”

Harlen stared at him for a long beat. “I can make you a vest and leggings, lad. It’ll be furry, it’ll be leathery, and it’ll smell like a wet dog for a month. You want it or not?”

“I shall accept your craftsmanship,” Dalton said, his dignity unshaken. “It is but a step in the journey. A knight must start somewhere, and if his first armor is the hide of his foes, then so be it!”

He also traded the remaining scraps—tails and teeth—for a handful of coins.

**[ LOOT SOLD: 10 COPPER ACQUIRED ]**

Dalton sat in the corner of the shop, reciting scenes from *The Tempest* to pass the time while Harlen worked. The tanner didn't say a word, his shears and needles moving with a rhythmic, practiced speed.

Two hours later, the ‘armor’ was ready. It was… unique. 

The vest was a patchwork of dark, coarse rat-fur and thick, boiled leather. It was bulky, slightly lopsided, and possessed a distinct, musky odor that even the tanning salts couldn't entirely mask. The leggings were similarly mangy, making Dalton’s legs look twice as thick as they actually were.

Dalton donned the gear with the solemnity of a king being crowned. He looked in a polished copper plate on the wall and saw a man who looked less like a knight and more like a very confused wolf-man.

**[ ITEM EQUIPPED: RAT-FUR VEST AND LEGGINGS ]**
**[ EFFECT: +5 DEFENSE, +2 CONSTITUTION ]**
**[ PENALTY: -1 CHARISMA ]**

The blue screen flickered with the penalty notification, the red text looking like a small wound on the interface.

“Minus one Charisma?” Dalton mused, sniffing his own sleeve. “It seems the phantom believes my new attire is… lacking in aesthetic appeal.”

Sam, hovering just outside the shop window to avoid the smell, let out a cackle. “Lacking in appeal? Dalton, you look like a walking carpet that’s been used to mop up a brewery! You smell so bad I’m surprised Harlen didn't charge you extra for the air-freshener!”

Dalton straightened the mangy vest, his eyes bright with a stubborn, quiet pride. “Master Sam, you forget the lesson of the boards. A costume does not make the character; it merely informs it. If the world finds my scent… robust, it is only because they have not faced the darkness I have conquered. A minus one to my charm? A trifle! I have thirteen—no, twelve—points remaining! I am still more charismatic than a dozen of these ‘uncouth’ villagers combined!”

He stepped out of the shop and into the sunlight, his 'Rat Slayer' club in hand and his rat-fur armor bristling in the breeze. He looked ridiculous. He smelled worse. But as he marched back toward the center of Oakhaven, he didn't feel like a fool.

“This is but a step in the journey!” Dalton proclaimed to a passing goat. “A transition from the stagehand to the champion! Let the world judge me by my scent if they must; they shall soon judge me by my deeds!”

“Now,” Dalton said, looking at Sam. “Where shall we find our next quest? I find my ‘Vermin Slayer’ title is already yearning for more substantial exercise!”

“The Reeve’s house,” Sam sighed, still keeping his distance. “Just… try to stay downwind of everyone, okay? For my sake.”
