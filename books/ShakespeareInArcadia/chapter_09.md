# Chapter 9: Vermin Slayer

The streets of Oakhaven were not paved with gold, nor were they lined with the appreciative crowds Dalton felt his recent victories deserved. Instead, they were paved with a mixture of half-frozen mud and livestock contributions, and the crowds were currently doing their best to stay at least six paces away from him. Whether it was his newly acquired ‘Rat Armor’ or the lingering musk of the cellar, Dalton found he had been granted a significant amount of personal space.

“Master Sam,” Dalton said, his voice echoing off the timber walls of the village’s main thoroughfare. “I find that while my spirit is soaring, my public recognition is… somewhat lacking. How do I display my Title to those that wish to know of my prowess? Surely there is a way to project my achievements onto the very air, that all might see the character I have built?”

Sam, who was flying a tactical distance behind Dalton to avoid the scent, let out a grumbled sound that was half-cough, half-laugh. “Project it? Dalton, if you project your achievements right now, you’re just going to project the smell of a dead sewer. But fine. There. This is what the ‘world’ will see of your ‘stature’ if they bother to look.”

Sam flicked a tiny, glowing finger, and a new screen appeared—smaller than the usual one, but glowing with a soft, public-facing light.

**[ PUBLIC PROFILE ]**
**[ NAME: DALTON ]**
**[ CLASS: NONE ]**
**[ LEVEL: 3 ]**
**[ TITLE: VERMIN SLAYER ]**

Dalton stopped dead in the middle of the street, causing a passing pig to let out a startled squeal. He stared at the small screen, his brow furrowing.

“‘Dalton’?” he whispered, his voice dripping with a tragic sense of betrayal. “‘Class: None’? And where, pray tell, is the ‘Sir’? Where is the ‘Knight of the Boards’? This is a most uncouth summary! It reads like a list of ingredients for a particularly bland stew!”

“It reads like the truth, Dalton!” Sam snapped, hovering just above a nearby thatch roof. “You’re level three. You don't have a class because you haven't done anything but hit bugs with a stick. And you’re not a ‘Sir’ because nobody has knighted you! The world doesn't care about your internal monologue. It only sees the numbers.”

Dalton didn't look disheartened. He looked… inspired. He began to march again, his ‘Rat Slayer’ club swinging rhythmically by his side.

“Alas, then the world must be educated!” Dalton proclaimed, beginning a monologue that would last the entirety of the walk to the north end of town. “They see a man of level three, but they do not see the fire of the fifth act burning in my eyes! They see a ‘Vermin Slayer,’ but they do not see the protector of the innocent who stands beneath this mangy fur! I shall carry this title as a badge of my early struggles! Like the humble beginning of a great king who once swept the very floors he now rules! The stage is small, Master Sam, and the audience is… well, the audience is mostly livestock and people with very poor hygiene, but the performance must be grand!”

“The performance is making my head ache,” Sam muttered, but he didn't stop him.

They reached the Reeve’s house. Dalton had expected a citadel—a place of stone walls, fluttering banners, and perhaps a herald to announce his arrival. Instead, he found a building that was only marginally more impressive than the Golden Pottle. It was a large, two-story farmhouse with a slate roof instead of thatch, surrounded by a sturdy fence of sharpened oak. Two guards stood at the gate, looking bored and cold in their mismatched leather armor.

“Is this it?” Dalton asked, his voice dropping an octave. “This is the seat of power in this realm? It lacks… verticality. And banners. Where are the banners, Master Sam? How is a man to know he has reached the presence of authority without at least one heraldic lion?”

“It’s Oakhaven, Dalton, not the High Court of the Ash Wastes,” Sam said, landing on a fence-post. “The Reeve is the guy who collects the taxes and makes sure nobody kills each other too much. He’s the law. Now, try to act like a normal person for once. Just tell him you’re looking for work.”

“I shall tell him I am a champion of the meadows!” Dalton declared, stepping up to the guards.

The guards looked at him. Then they looked at each other. One of them—a man with a permanent scowl named Thorne—wrinkled his nose.

“You the lad who cleared Mott’s cellar?” Thorne asked, his voice dry.

“I am Sir Dalton, the Vermin Slayer!” Dalton announced, offering a bow that was slightly hindered by the bulk of his new vest. “I seek the Reeve! I have service to offer, and a spirit that yearns for more… substantial exercise.”

Thorne shared a glance with his partner. “Reeve’s inside. He’s been expecting someone. Didn't think it’d be a guy wearing a rat-king, but… go on in. He’s in the study. Try not to touch the curtains.”

Dalton entered the house, his boots clicking on the clean, wooden floor. The interior was surprisingly well-maintained—polished oak tables, a few faded tapestries depicting local harvests, and a fireplace that actually drew its smoke properly. 

The Reeve, a man named Franklin, was sitting at a heavy desk covered in ledgers and scrolls. He was a thin man with a sharp, bird-like face and eyes that seemed to be constantly calculating the cost of everything they landed on. He didn't look up as Dalton entered.

“Mott says you’re a hard worker,” Franklin began, his voice thin and precise. “Says you don't mind the dark, and you don't mind getting… messy. Most adventurers who come through here want to hunt orcs or find lost gold. They don't want to crawl into holes.”

Franklin finally looked up, and his eyes landed on Dalton. He didn't flinch at the smell or the mangy armor. Instead, he leaned back in his chair, his fingers steepled beneath his chin. 

He saw a man in a ridiculous costume, speaking with the cadence of a lunatic, and clutching a club made of rat-teeth. He saw a man who clearly believed he was the protagonist of a story that didn't exist.

*A fool,* Franklin thought, his mind already spinning with possibilities. *A magnificent, high-minded, entirely disposable fool. Exactly what I need for the North Mine.*

“Sir Dalton,” Franklin said, the title tasting like copper in his mouth, but he said it with a practiced, diplomat’s ease. “I have heard of your… crusade in the meadows. And your victory in the cellar. You are a man who understands that some tasks, while ‘uncouth,’ are vital to the safety of the realm.”

“Indeed, Master Franklin!” Dalton said, beaming. “A True Knight does not shy from the grime of the world if it means the preservation of the proper order!”

“Quite,” Franklin said, his lips curling into a thin, calculating smile. “And it just so happens that I have a task that requires a man of your… unique temperament. Our North Mine has been silent for a week. Not because of orcs, or dragons, but because of a ‘gelatinous infestation’ and a plague of things that crawl in the deep. My usual guards refuse to go down there. They say it’s ‘below their station.’”

Dalton’s heart swelled. A mine! A subterranean labyrinth of silence and shadows! 

“They lack the conviction of the heart!” Dalton proclaimed. “But I, Sir Dalton, the Vermin Slayer, shall not falter! Tell me of this mine! I shall restore its voice!”

Franklin leaned forward, his eyes bright with a cold, predatory light. “The pay is twenty copper. And whatever loot you find is yours. But be warned, Dalton. The things in the North Mine are… stronger than slimes. They have teeth. And they have a hunger.”

“Let them come!” Dalton cried, raising his ‘Rat Slayer’ club. “I shall give them a performance they shall never forget!”

Sam, watching from the windowsill, felt a cold shiver run down his spine. He looked at Franklin, and then at Dalton. He saw the predator and the player.

“Twenty copper?” Sam whispered. “Dalton, he’s sending you to your death. He’s leveraging your insanity to clear out a suicide-hole.”

Dalton didn't hear him. He was already planning his entrance into the mine. The stage was getting larger. The stakes were getting higher. And he was ready for the next act.
