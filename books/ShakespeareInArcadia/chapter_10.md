# Chapter 10: The Descent of the Uncouth

The North Mine lay a mile outside the wooden palisade of Oakhaven, nestled in the rocky foothills that marked the edge of the Ash Wastes. It wasn't a grand, dwarven delving with carved stone pillars and runic archways. It was a jagged hole torn into the side of a grey hill, shored up with rotting timbers and surrounded by piles of discarded slag. It looked less like a mine and more like a wound that the earth had refused to heal.

Two guards stood a safe distance from the entrance, huddled around a small, smoky fire. They were leaning on their spears, looking jumpy and thoroughly miserable. 

"Behold, Master Sam!" Dalton proclaimed, his 'Rat Slayer' club resting easily on his fur-clad shoulder as he marched up the rocky path. "The maw of the underworld! A fitting stage for a hero's trial. It lacks a certain... architectural grandeur, but one must work with the scenery provided."

Sam, who was flying higher than usual to escape the pervasive smell of Dalton’s rat-fur armor, groaned. "It’s a hole in the ground, Dalton. And it smells like acid and dead things. You know, you don't *have* to do everything the shady government official tells you to do. We could just leave town. Go pick flowers. Farm turnips. Turnips are safe."

"A knight does not flee from a contract, my tiny, cynical friend!" Dalton replied, coming to a halt before the two guards. He struck a pose, planting his club on the ground and puffing out his chest. 

"Greetings, sentinels of the deep!" Dalton boomed, his voice startling the guards so badly that one of them dropped his spear into the fire. "I am Sir Dalton, the Vermin Slayer! Sent by Reeve Franklin to cleanse this subterranean labyrinth of its gelatinous and crawling afflictions!"

The guard who had dropped his spear swore, hurriedly fishing it out of the flames. He looked at Dalton, his eyes widening as he took in the patchwork rat-armor, the strange, tooth-studded club, and the expression of absolute, unhinged confidence on Dalton’s face.

"You?" the guard coughed, waving away the smoke. "Franklin sent *you*? Mate, you look like you fell backwards through a tanner's scrap pile. There’s things down there that ate a cart-horse two days ago. Big things. Wet things."

"The size of the beast merely ensures a larger target!" Dalton said, offering a courtly bow that made his fur vest creak. "Fear not. I have vanquished the Rat King of Mott's cellar, and I have slain the slimes of the meadow. The 'things' below shall soon know the sting of my righteous fury."

The second guard simply shook his head, stepping aside to reveal the dark, gaping entrance of the mine. "It's your funeral, 'Sir' Dalton. Don't say we didn't warn you. If you hear a hissing sound, run. If you hear clicking... well, pray."

"I do not pray for mercy, good sir. I perform!" Dalton declared. 

He strode past the guards and into the gloom of the North Mine. The transition from the crisp afternoon air to the stagnant, heavy atmosphere of the tunnel was immediate. The air tasted of copper and ancient dust, and the only light came from the occasional sputtering torch bracketed to the damp walls.

**[ AREA DISCOVERED: NORTH MINE (LEVEL 3-7 ZONE) ]**

The blue text flickered into existence, casting a faint, sickly glow on the rocky walls. 

"Level three to seven," Sam read, his voice tight. "Dalton, you are level three. That means everything in here is either your equal or can squish you like a bug. We need to be careful."

"A True Knight is always careful, Master Sam," Dalton whispered back, his voice dropping into a resonant, theatrical hush. "He carefully measures his strike to ensure maximum dramatic impact."

They ventured deeper. The path angled downward, the timber supports growing thicker and closer together. The sound of their footsteps echoed uncomfortably in the tight space. 

Then, they heard it. 

A hissing sound. Not the hiss of a snake, but the wet, chemical hiss of acid eating into stone. 

Dalton raised his 'Rat Slayer,' his eyes adjusting to the dim light. In the center of the tunnel, blocking the path, was a Slime. But it wasn't the small, cheerful green blob of the meadows. This slime was a sickly, translucent yellow, the size of a wagon wheel, and it was leaving a trail of smoking, bubbling rock in its wake.

**[ CORROSIVE SLIME: LEVEL 4 ]**

"Dalton," Sam whispered, his voice trembling. "Don't touch it. It's corrosive. It'll eat right through that ridiculous rat-suit, and then it'll eat you."

"A villain of a different hue!" Dalton said, his eyes narrowing. "It seems my 'Slime Slayer' title shall be put to a sterner test!"

The Corrosive Slime sensed them. It rippled, a surge of yellow fluid rushing to its front, and it lunged. It didn't hop like the green slimes; it slid forward with terrifying speed, launching a glob of acidic jelly directly at Dalton’s chest.

Dalton reacted not with a dodge, but with a parry. It was an instinct born of a thousand staged duels. He swung the 'Rat Slayer' up, knocking the acidic glob out of the air. The jelly hit the rock wall beside him, immediately burning a hole into the stone with a violent hiss.

The club, however, remained unscathed. The dark-iron wood and Rat King teeth seemed resistant to the acid.

"Aha!" Dalton cried. "My blade holds true!"

He didn't wait for the Slime to launch another volley. He charged, bringing the heavy club down in a sweeping, two-handed strike. The 'Rat Slayer' hit the yellow mass with a sickening *thud*. 

Thanks to his 'Vermin Slayer' title and his recently boosted Strength, the blow hit hard. The Slime shuddered violently, its form destabilizing. But it didn't pop. It retaliated, surging forward and wrapping a corrosive tendril around Dalton’s left leg.

**[ DAMAGE RECEIVED: 8 (ACID) ]**
**[ HEALTH: 102 / 110 ]**

Dalton let out a sharp gasp as the acid burned through the rat-fur leggings and bit into his skin. The pain was sharp and bright, a searing heat that made his vision swim.

But Dalton didn't retreat. He remembered the pain of the dirk in London. He remembered the humiliation. He was Sir Dalton, and he would not fall to a wobbling puddle of yellow filth.

"Release me, you uncouth... acidic... BOOR!" he roared.

He swung again, aiming dead center for the faintly glowing orange core suspended within the yellow mass. The heavy club smashed into the core, shattering it with a loud, popping sound. 

The Corrosive Slime instantly dissolved into a pool of smoking, foul-smelling liquid, leaving behind a single, faintly glowing yellow gemstone.

**[ CORROSIVE SLIME SLAIN! ]**
**[ +15 EXP ]**

Dalton leaned against the rock wall, breathing heavily. He looked down at his leg. The acid had burned a hole through the leather and fur, leaving an angry, red welt on his calf, but the 'Rat Armor' had absorbed the worst of it.

"You're an idiot," Sam said, hovering close to inspect the burn. "A lucky idiot, but an idiot nonetheless. That thing almost melted your leg off."

"It is but a scratch, Master Sam!" Dalton declared, though he winced as he shifted his weight. He bent down and picked up the yellow gemstone. It was warm to the touch.

**[ LOOT ACQUIRED: ACIDIC CORE (CRAFTING MATERIAL) ]**

"A jewel of the deep!" Dalton said, pocketing the stone. "The rewards of the underworld are already making themselves known. Come, my friend. The stage is set, the opening act is concluded, and I believe I hear the... clicking... of the next scene approaching."

From the darkness deeper down the tunnel, a new sound emerged. A rhythmic, unsettling *clack-clack-clack*, accompanied by the scuttling of many, many legs against the stone. 

Dalton tightened his grip on his club, a grim smile spreading across his face. The audience was waiting, and Sir Dalton was ready to deliver his lines.
