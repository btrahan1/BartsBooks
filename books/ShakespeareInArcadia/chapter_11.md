# Chapter 11: Dalton The Undying

The clicking grew louder, echoing off the damp, uneven walls of the tunnel like a cascade of falling stones. It was a rapid, erratic sound that set Dalton's teeth on edge, entirely lacking the rhythmic, predictable beat of a proper stage cue. 

"Master Sam," Dalton whispered, holding his 'Rat Slayer' club in a two-handed grip, "I believe the antagonist of this scene approaches. And it sounds as though it has brought an excess of footwear."

Sam, hovering close to the ceiling to keep a maximum distance between himself and whatever was making that noise, looked terrified. His small, bat-like ears were flattened against his head. "Dalton, that’s not an antagonist. That’s an apex predator. Do you see the level on this zone? We shouldn't be here. Let's just grab that acid core and tell the Reeve the mine is haunted. He's a coward, he'll believe it."

"A knight does not fabricate ghost stories to mask a retreat!" Dalton declared, his voice firm, though he couldn't help but wipe a bead of sweat from his brow. "We stand our ground."

From the shadows, it emerged. 

It was a monstrosity of segmented, dark-grey chitin, roughly the size of a low-slung cart. Dozens of barbed, insectoid legs clattered against the stone floor, propelling the creature forward with a horrifying, fluid speed. Its head was a nightmare of clicking mandibles, each one looking sharp enough to snap a broadsword in half, and above them sat a cluster of four, dead-black eyes.

**[ IRON-JAW CENTIPEDE: LEVEL 8 ]**

The blue text hovered over the creature, the red numbers of its level pulsing with an undeniable threat.

"Level eight!" Sam shrieked, dropping a few inches in altitude. "Dalton, run! That thing is five levels above you! It's going to turn you into a tragic footnote!"

"I am the protagonist, Master Sam!" Dalton yelled over the deafening clatter of the beast. "And a protagonist does not flee from his designated trial!"

The Iron-Jaw Centipede lunged. It didn't bother with a warning hiss; it simply threw its massive, armored bulk forward, its mandibles snapping shut like iron shears. 

Dalton sidestepped, a clumsy but effective maneuver born of pure adrenaline. The centipede crashed into the wall where he had just stood, sending a shower of loose rock and dust into the air. 

"Take that, you multi-legged fiend!" Dalton roared. He swung the 'Rat Slayer' with all the might his twelve Strength could muster. 

*Clang!*

The club struck the creature's carapace, but instead of the satisfying crunch of shattered chitin, the dark-iron wood rebounded with a bone-jarring vibration. Dalton staggered backward, his hands numb. 

**[ ATTACK INEFFECTIVE. TARGET ARMOR TOO HIGH. ]**

The centipede whipped its head around, clearly annoyed by the attempt. It lashed out with its front legs, the barbed hooks catching the edge of Dalton’s rat-fur vest.

**[ DAMAGE RECEIVED: 45 (PIERCING) ]**
**[ HEALTH: 57 / 110 ]**

Dalton cried out, the sheer force of the blow knocking him off his feet. He hit the stone floor hard, tasting blood and copper. 

"Dalton, you idiot! Get up and run!" Sam screamed, darting around the creature's head in a desperate attempt to distract it. "You can't hurt it! Your club is practically a toothpick to that armor! Run!"

"I... shall not... yield!" Dalton wheezed, forcing himself onto one knee. He looked at his health bar, the blue pane hovering stubbornly in his vision. It was half empty. One more hit like that, and he was finished. 

He didn't see an insurmountable obstacle. He saw the climax of the second act. The moment where the hero is tested beyond his limits. 

The centipede reared up, its mandibles clicking furiously as it prepared for a finishing strike. 

Dalton gripped his club, struggling to stand. His legs shook, his vision blurred at the edges, but his dignity remained completely, absurdly intact.

"Come then!" Dalton bellowed, pointing the 'Rat Slayer' at the beast. "Let the bards sing of this day! Sir Dalton stands unbowed!"

He charged. It was a slow, pathetic charge, completely lacking in tactical sense, driven entirely by theatrical bravado. 

The centipede didn't even try to dodge. It met his charge with a brutal, lightning-fast snap of its mandibles. The iron jaws clamped shut around Dalton's torso, crushing through the rat-fur armor as if it were paper.

**[ FATAL DAMAGE RECEIVED: 90 (CRUSHING) ]**
**[ HEALTH: 0 / 110 ]**

The world went instantly, numbingly cold. The pain spiked and then vanished, replaced by a profound, heavy weakness. The centipede tossed him aside like a broken doll, and Dalton hit the stone wall, sliding down into the dirt.

Sam landed beside his head, the imp's face pale under his purple skin. "Dalton... you fool. I told you to run."

Dalton looked up at the stone ceiling, his vision darkening to a pinpoint. He didn't feel fear. He felt a deep, theatrical melancholy. 

"Alas, Master Sam," Dalton whispered, the words bubbling past the blood in his throat. His voice was faint, but still held the cadence of the stage. "My strength... it was not enough for this act. The curtain... falls too soon."

"You didn't have to die!" Sam yelled, tears of frustration gathering in his large amber eyes. 

"A knight... must try," Dalton breathed. His hand went limp, the 'Rat Slayer' clattering onto the stone. "Tell Mistress Anne... I fought... valiantly..."

He closed his eyes, and the darkness took him. 

**[ USER: DALTON ]**
**[ STATUS: DECEASED ]**
**[ INITIATING RESPAWN SEQUENCE... ]**

***

The transition was not a tunnel of light, nor a choir of angels. It was a sudden, violent jolt, like waking from a dream by falling out of bed. 

Dalton gasped, his eyes flying open. He was lying on a patch of hard, trampled dirt. Above him was not the stone ceiling of the mine, but the clear, blue sky of Arcadia. He sat up quickly, patting his chest. There was no blood. There were no puncture wounds. His rat-fur armor was completely intact. 

He looked around. He was standing right outside the entrance to the North Mine, near the small, smoldering fire where the two guards were still huddled. They were staring at him, their mouths hanging open.

"What is this?" Dalton asked, his voice filled with hushed awe. He stood up, looking at his hands, feeling the undeniable beat of his own heart. "Have I moved on to another realm? Is this the Elysian Fields, crafted to mirror the world I left behind?"

"No," a very tired, very annoyed voice rasped from a nearby rock. 

Dalton turned. Sam was sitting there, his arms crossed, looking thoroughly exhausted. 

"You've respawned, you dolt!" Sam snapped. 

Dalton blinked. "Respawned? Master Sam, speak plainly. I felt the breath leave my body. I felt the cold grip of the grave!"

"And then the System put you right back at the nearest bind-point, which happens to be the entrance of this stupid mine," Sam explained, rubbing his temples. "This is Arcadia, Dalton. People don't just 'die' unless they're NPCs or they take a permanent penalty. As an adventurer, you just pop back up, minus some experience points and a bit of pride."

Dalton looked at the blue screen that had helpfully reappeared.

**[ RESPAWN COMPLETE ]**
**[ PENALTY APPLIED: -10% CURRENT EXPERIENCE ]**
**[ CURRENT EXPERIENCE: 13 / 200 ]**

Dalton stared at the screen, and then at the sky. A slow, magnificent smile spread across his face. He wasn't dead. He was immortal. 

"Respawned," Dalton whispered, the word tasting of infinite possibilities. "Death itself is but a temporary setback! A brief intermission between acts!"

He turned back toward the dark entrance of the mine, his eyes burning with a new, terrifying level of conviction. 

"Master Sam!" Dalton cried, raising his 'Rat Slayer' high. "The play continues! Let us return to the stage! I have an appointment with a centipede, and I believe I have thought of a better opening monologue!"
