# Chapter 12: A Temporary Setback

The two guards at the entrance of the North Mine had seen many strange things in their time. They had seen miners driven mad by the dark, they had seen merchants swindled by goblins, and they had seen adventurers return missing various limbs. But they had never seen a man get crushed by a centipede the size of a cart, disappear in a flash of light, reappear five minutes later smelling faintly of ozone, and then immediately turn around to go back inside.

"You're... you're not going back in there, are you?" the younger guard asked, his voice cracking as he leaned away from Dalton. "Mate, you just died. We saw it. The light flashed, and you popped out of thin air."

Dalton stopped at the threshold of the mine, offering the guards a brilliant, utterly unhinged smile. He swept his arm in a grand gesture, his 'Rat Slayer' club leaving a dark arc in the air. 

"Fear not, sentinels!" Dalton boomed. "Death is merely a setback, my dear Sam! A brief intermission between the acts of a grander play! The protagonist must occasionally stumble so that his ultimate victory shines all the brighter!"

He didn't wait for their response. He plunged back into the darkness, his step lighter than before. 

Sam, trailing behind, let out a long, suffering sigh. "Dalton, please. You have ten percent less experience now. If you keep dying, you’re going to de-level. Do you know how embarrassing it is to de-level? It's like un-learning how to read."

"But I have learned the script, Master Sam!" Dalton countered, marching down the tunnel with his club held at the ready. "The centipede is slow to turn! I merely need to improve my footwork. A knight must be as swift as the summer breeze, even if he wears the fur of rodents!"

They reached the spot where Dalton had fallen. There was a scorch mark on the wall from the corrosive slime, and a small pile of shattered rock where the centipede had struck, but of the massive, many-legged beast, there was no sign. The tunnel was eerily quiet.

"Alas!" Dalton cried, his voice echoing disappointingly down the empty shaft. "The cowardly beast has fled the stage! It knows that Sir Dalton has returned, forged anew in the fires of respawn!"

"It probably just went deeper to digest," Sam muttered, floating up to the ceiling. "Thank the Bard. If we see it again, I'm finding a new host. Now, since we're already down here, can you please just focus on things you can actually kill? Like, say, those smaller spiders over there?"

Dalton turned. Clinging to the timbers of a side tunnel were a half-dozen Cave Spiders. They were the size of dinner plates, covered in bristly grey hair, and currently eyeing Dalton with a mixture of hunger and territorial malice.

"Excellent!" Dalton roared. "Supporting cast! Come, my eight-legged friends! Let us dance upon the boards!"

He attacked the denizens of the mine with a gusto that bordered on the manic. The fear of death—a very real, very paralyzing fear that usually kept Level 3 vagrants from delving too deep—had been entirely erased from Dalton's mind. He fought not like a man trying to survive, but like an actor trying to impress the back row of the gallery.

He parried spider venom with theatrical spins. He crushed Cave Centipedes (the smaller, much more manageable cousins of his nemesis) with overhead strikes while reciting lines from *Henry V*. When he took damage—and he took quite a bit—he treated it as dramatic tension rather than physical injury.

For two hours, the North Mine echoed with the sound of snapping chitin, the wet thud of the 'Rat Slayer,' and Dalton's unending soliloquies. He cleared three side tunnels and a small cavern that smelled strongly of bat guano, returning the mine to a relative state of peace.

Finally, as he brought his club down on the skull of a particularly stubborn Level 4 Rock Mole, the familiar, glorious blue light filled his vision.

Dalton leaned against a wooden support beam, wiping spider-ichor from his brow. He was breathing heavily, his rat-fur armor was torn in three new places, and he was down to a quarter of his health, but his eyes were shining.

"Alas! Level four, Sam!" Dalton proclaimed, his chest heaving with pride as he assigned his new point to Strength. "The hero grows! And at level five, if I recall your earlier counsel, you said I may choose a class! I can finally become a Knight?"

Sam fluttered down from a high ledge, landing on Dalton's shoulder. The imp looked exhausted, entirely drained by the sheer volume of Dalton's performance. 

"No," Sam sighed, the word drawn out into a long, mournful sound. 

Dalton’s smile faltered. "No? But you said..."

"I said you get to choose your *base* class at level five," Sam explained, rubbing his temples with his tiny claws. "Warrior, Rogue, Mage, Ranger... the basics. You can evolve that base class at level ten. But like I said before, a Knight isn't a base class. It's a special class. An advanced class."

"A special class?" Dalton repeated, the disappointment fading, replaced by a sudden, intense interest. "Ah! A title not simply handed out to any commoner who swings a sword! It must be earned! Tell me, Master Sam, what must one do to qualify for such an esteemed position?"

Sam pinched the bridge of his nose. "It's not just a matter of hitting things with a stick, Dalton. There are several things you have to do to qualify. First, you have to choose the Warrior base class. Second, you have to build up a high 'Honor' or 'Reputation' rating with a recognized kingdom or faction."

"Honor! The very currency of my soul!" Dalton beamed.

"Third," Sam continued, ignoring the interruption, "you usually have to swear fealty to a recognized Lord or Lady. Someone with the System-granted authority to bestow the title."

"A liege lord! A king to serve! Excellent!"

"And fourth," Sam finished, a small, cruel smile creeping onto his lips, "you need a mount. You can't be a knight without a horse, Dalton. It's literally in the definition. And horses in Arcadia are expensive. They cost silver, not copper. You currently have twelve copper coins and a club made of rat teeth."

Dalton absorbed this information. He didn't look defeated. He looked as if he had just been handed the script for the most epic, multi-act play of his life.

"A lord to serve, a reputation to build, and a noble steed to acquire," Dalton mused, looking out into the dark tunnels of the mine. "The path is steep, Master Sam. But the view from the summit shall be glorious. I shall become a Warrior, and from there, I shall forge a path to Knighthood that shall make the very heavens weep!"

"Can we just focus on getting out of this mine without you dying again?" Sam pleaded. "My nerves can't take another respawn."

"To the surface, then!" Dalton agreed, shouldering his club. "We have cleared the lower stages. Let us return to the Reeve and claim our twenty copper. We are one step closer to my horse!"
