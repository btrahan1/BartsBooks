# Chapter 13: The Silver Commission

The doors to Reeve Franklin’s study did not burst open so much as they were thrown wide with a dramatic, wooden *thud* that caused the Reeve to spill a drop of ink onto a perfectly good piece of parchment. 

Franklin looked up, his sharp features pulling into a scowl that quickly melted into an expression of genuine, almost pleasant surprise. Standing in the doorway, smelling strongly of burnt hair, acid, and spider ichor, was the man he had been certain was currently being digested in the dark.

"Reeve Franklin, I have returned!" Dalton announced, striking a pose that involved pointing his 'Rat Slayer' club toward the ceiling. 

"Sir Dalton," Franklin said, slowly setting his quill down. He looked at the rat-fur armor, which was now sporting several new, impressive holes. "I must admit, I am... surprised. The guards usually report a distinct lack of returning footsteps from the North Mine."

"The steps of a True Knight are not so easily silenced!" Dalton proclaimed, marching into the room. He didn't wait for an invitation to sit. He didn't even stop moving. He simply began to pace the length of the study, the stage set for his recounting. 

"The descent was fraught with peril, my Lord! Scarcely had we crossed the threshold when a gelatinous beast of corrosive yellow bile sought to melt the very flesh from my bones! I parried its foul spit! I struck true at its glowing heart!"

Sam, hovering invisibly above Franklin's desk, rolled his eyes. "You got hit in the leg and almost cried, Dalton."

Dalton ignored the imp, his voice rising in volume. "And then, in the side-tunnels, a legion of eight-legged fiends! Cave Spiders, they were! Dropping from the ceiling like rain, their fangs dripping with venom! I danced among them, a whirlwind of justice, striking them down one by one!"

Franklin listened patiently for the first two minutes, his fingers steepled beneath his chin. By the third minute, his eyes had begun to glaze over. When Dalton started describing the exact arc of his swing against a Level 4 Rock Mole, the Reeve finally held up a hand.

"Well done, Sir Dalton," Franklin interrupted, his voice cutting through the monologue with practiced bureaucratic efficiency. He reached into a drawer and pulled out a small leather pouch. It hit the desk with a heavy, satisfying clink. "You have cleared the upper levels, and for that, Oakhaven is grateful. You have earned your twenty copper. Off with you now. Thank you for your service."

Dalton stopped mid-stride. He looked at the pouch of copper, his brow furrowing in profound confusion. 

"Off with me?" Dalton echoed, the tragic timbre returning to his voice. "But... but I am not done! The grand finale has yet to be staged! That cowardly, many-legged usurper—the Iron-Jaw Centipede—still lives! It hides in the depths, waiting! I must slay it!"

Franklin paused. He looked at Dalton, his calculating mind running the numbers. Clearing the upper levels was good; it allowed the miners to fetch their abandoned tools. But actually *clearing* the mine? Killing the apex predator that had shut down their silver veins for a week? That would be a massive boon to the town's coffers. And his own.

"You want to go back down there?" Franklin asked, his eyes narrowing slightly. "To fight a beast that, by all accounts, is the size of a wagon?"

"It is my designated antagonist!" Dalton declared, placing a hand over his heart. 

The Reeve thought for a few moments. If the fool died, it cost Oakhaven nothing. If he succeeded...

"Tell you what," Franklin said, leaning forward. "If you can actually clear the mine. If you can bring me proof that the centipede is dead... I will pay you one silver."

Dalton gasped. It wasn't a theatrical gasp; it was genuine shock. A silver coin. Sam had told him horses cost silver. A silver coin was the currency of nobility, of true adventurers. It was the first step toward a mount.

"A whole silver!" Dalton breathed, his eyes shining. He swept into a bow that was entirely free of irony. "You are too generous, my Lord! Consider it done. The beast shall not see another sunrise... assuming it could see the sun from down there."

"One silver," Franklin confirmed. Then, he looked at Dalton again. He looked at the club made of rat teeth. He looked at the mangy fur vest. 

The Reeve sighed. He was a ruthless man, but he was a practical one. Sending this enthusiastic idiot against an armored centipede with a piece of wood was a bad investment. 

"Look, Sir Dalton," Franklin said, standing up from his desk. "I think fighting that thing as you are may be... a bit much to ask of you. The beast has armor. Wood will not pierce it. So, I am going to 'lend' you some equipment."

He walked over to a heavy oak chest sitting in the corner of the study. He unlocked it with an iron key and threw back the lid. 

"This is old town-militia gear," Franklin said, pulling out two items and setting them on the desk. "It's not pretty, and it's certainly not magical, but it's metal."

The first item was a shield. It was a simple heater shield, made of thick pine boards and banded with dark, scuffed iron. The leather straps on the back were worn, but sturdy. 

The second item was a sword. 

Dalton stared at it as if Franklin had just laid the Holy Grail on the table. It was an arming sword, straight and double-edged. The crossguard was plain, the leather grip was frayed, and the blade itself had a few minor nicks and a dull, grey sheen that spoke of long years in storage. 

It wasn't a very good sword. But it was a sword.

Dalton reached out, his hand trembling slightly, and picked it up. As his fingers wrapped around the hilt, the blue text flared to life.

**[ ITEM ACQUIRED: MILITIA SHIELD ]**
**[ EFFECT: +10 DEFENSE, +5% BLOCK CHANCE ]**

**[ ITEM ACQUIRED: RUSTED IRON ARMING SWORD ]**
**[ EFFECT: 5-8 PHYSICAL DAMAGE (SLASHING/PIERCING) ]**
**[ SKILL UNLOCKED: BLADES (LEVEL 1) ]**

Dalton was over the moon. He didn't see the word 'Rusted.' He saw 'Arming Sword.' He strapped the shield to his left arm and gave the sword an experimental swing. It was heavier than his club, throwing his balance off slightly, but the sound it made—the sharp *whoosh* of steel cutting air—was the most beautiful music he had ever heard.

"A blade!" Dalton cried, holding the sword aloft. "A true weapon for a True Knight! Master Franklin, your generosity shall be sung of from the highest peaks! The centipede is as good as dead!"

Sam, hovering near the ceiling, let out a low whistle. "A sword and a shield. Well, well. You might actually survive long enough to run away this time, Dalton."

"Run? We do not run, Master Sam!" Dalton laughed, turning toward the door. "We ride! Figuratively, for now. But soon, with the Reeve's silver, we shall ride literally! To the mine!"

Franklin watched the madman march out of his study, the rusted sword swinging at his side. The Reeve shook his head, returning to his ledger. "A fool," he muttered to himself. "But an entertaining one. And if he pulls this off... a very profitable one."
