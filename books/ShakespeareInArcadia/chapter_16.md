# Chapter 16: Monster Slayer

The blue light of the System had not finished its performance. As Dalton adjusted the heavy, rusted rings of his new mail hauberk, the air in front of him shimmered once again, accompanied by a sound like a small, celestial trumpet.

**[ SYSTEM ANNOUNCEMENT: INCREDIBLE FEAT DETECTED ]**
**[ USER HAS DEFEATED AN ELITE ENEMY 3 LEVELS ABOVE THEIR CURRENT LEVEL ]**
**[ TITLE EVOLVED: VERMIN SLAYER -> MONSTER SLAYER ]**
**[ EFFECT: +2 DAMAGE TO ALL MONSTERS, +1 TO ALL BASE ATTRIBUTES ]**

Dalton stared at the screen, his mouth slightly agape beneath the rim of his iron helmet. He felt a sudden, rushing warmth spread through his limbs, soothing the worst of his exhaustion. The ache in his shield arm lessened, and the heavy mail suddenly felt a fraction lighter. 

"Master Sam!" Dalton breathed, turning to the imp. "Do you see? The phantom recognizes my ascent! 'Monster Slayer'! The title of a true champion! And it has bestowed a blessing upon my very essence!"

Sam, who had been busy inspecting the centipede's remains for any missed copper, flew up to look at the screen. His jaw dropped. 

"Plus one to all stats?" Sam whispered, his voice filled with genuine shock. "Dalton, do you know how rare a flat stat boost is from a Title? People grind for months to get those. You got it by being too stubborn to run away."

"I got it by being the protagonist, Master Sam," Dalton corrected, puffing out his chest, which caused the chainmail to clink pleasantly. 

He quickly assigned his Level 6 attribute point to Constitution, feeling that his near-death experience warranted a bit more physical resilience. With the new Title bonus, his character sheet looked significantly more formidable.

**[ CURRENT STATS: ]**
*   **STRENGTH: 18 (BASE: 13 + CLASS: 5)**
*   **DEXTERITY: 14**
*   **CONSTITUTION: 15**
*   **INTELLIGENCE: 11**
*   **WISDOM: 11**
*   **CHARISMA: 14 (PENALTY INCLUDED)**

"I am a fortress!" Dalton declared, picking up his chipped militia shield and his sword. With his free hand, he reached down and heaved one of the massive, scythe-like iron mandibles from the centipede's corpse. It weighed as much as a small dog. "And now, to the Reeve! We have a silver coin to collect, and a stage that awaits its hero!"

The walk back to Oakhaven was a slow, triumphant march. The sun was beginning to set, casting long, dramatic shadows across the rolling hills. Dalton no longer walked like a stagehand trying to act like a knight. He walked like a man who had stared into the abyss and punched it in the teeth.

He approached the Reeve's house just as the guards were preparing to change the watch. Thorne, the scowling guard from before, saw Dalton approaching and nearly dropped his halberd.

"It can't be," Thorne muttered, rubbing his eyes. 

"Greetings, sentinels!" Dalton called out, his voice echoing with metallic resonance from his helmet. He stopped before them, dropping the massive iron mandible onto the dirt with a heavy thud. "The North Mine is cleared! The beast is vanquished!"

Thorne looked at the mandible, which was the size of his torso. Then he looked at Dalton, covered in dark ichor, sporting dented iron, and wearing chainmail that he definitely hadn't walked in with. 

"Go on in," Thorne said, his voice entirely devoid of sarcasm. "He's in the study."

Dalton strode through the halls of the Reeve's house, leaving muddy, ichor-stained bootprints on the polished oak floors. He pushed open the study doors—not bursting them this time, but opening them with a slow, deliberate solemnity. 

Reeve Franklin looked up from his ledgers. When he saw Dalton, and more importantly, when he saw the massive iron mandible Dalton placed onto his pristine desk, the color drained from his face.

"By the Gods," Franklin breathed, leaning back in his chair. "You actually did it. The Iron-Jaw is dead."

"Slain by my own hand, my Lord!" Dalton said, resting his hand on the pommel of his rusted sword. "A grueling duel, to be sure, but justice prevailed. I have come to report the success of my commission."

Franklin stared at the man. He was a fool, undoubtedly. A madman who spoke in verse. But he was a madman who had just single-handedly killed a monster that an entire squad of guards had refused to face. 

Wordlessly, Franklin reached into his desk. He withdrew a small, heavy silver coin and set it on the ledger. 

"Your reward, Sir Dalton," Franklin said, his voice quiet. He looked at the sword and shield he had "lent" the man earlier that day. They were battered, chipped, and stained. "And... keep the armaments. I believe you have earned them."

"Your generosity honors me, Lord Franklin!" Dalton bowed, scooping up the silver coin. It felt heavy in his palm, the weight of true progression. He had two silver coins now, counting the loot from the beast. "The silver shall go toward the purchase of a noble steed! And now, I must seek my rest. The stage is dark, and the actor is weary."

Dalton left the Reeve’s house and made his way through the dimming streets to the Golden Pottle. 

When he entered the tavern, the usual low hum of conversation stopped. The patrons turned to look at the man in the rusted chainmail, his face smeared with dirt and monster blood. 

Mott, the innkeeper, stopped wiping the bar. He took one look at Dalton and reached for a clean mug.

"Master Mott!" Dalton proclaimed, walking to the bar and slapping one of his silver coins onto the wood. The sound of silver hitting oak was loud enough to make three nearby farmers flinch. "A room for the night! A hot bath, if such luxury exists! And a round of your finest ale for the patrons of this establishment! For tonight, we celebrate!"

Mott looked at the silver coin. Then he looked at Dalton. A rare, genuine smile cracked across his turnip-like face. "Done," Mott said.

That night, the Golden Pottle was louder than it had been in years. Dalton sat at the center table, a mug of ale in his hand, holding court. He didn't just tell the story; he performed it. 

He stood on a chair, using his empty mug to demonstrate the parry. He bellowed "Heroic Strike!" with such fervor that a barmaid dropped a plate. He acted out the beast's final, tragic shudder, and spoke of the 'phantom's blessing' that had granted him the title of Monster Slayer.

The villagers—men and women who spent their lives digging in the dirt and fearing the shadows—listened with rapt attention. They didn't understand the strange words he used, but they understood the passion. They understood that the monster in the mine was dead, and that the man standing on the chair in rusted mail was the one who had killed it.

Sam sat on a rafter high above the taproom, a tiny mug of ale in his clawed hands. He watched Dalton perform, listening to the cheers of the uncouth villagers. 

"Monster Slayer," Sam whispered, taking a sip of the ale and shaking his head. "Well, I'll be damned. The idiot is actually pulling it off."
