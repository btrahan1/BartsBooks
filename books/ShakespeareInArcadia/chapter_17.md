# Chapter 17: Party Time

The morning sun streaming through the clouded window of the Golden Pottle found Dalton in a state of rare luxury. His muscles still ached with the memory of crushing iron jaws, but the hot bath and the clean (or at least, recently aired) sheets had worked wonders. He felt the vitality of a Level 6 Warrior pulsing in his veins. 

He descended the stairs to the taproom with a renewed spring in his step, his chainmail clinking a rhythmic tune. The tavern was quiet, the smell of last night's ale replaced by the savory aroma of Mott's morning porridge and frying bacon.

Dalton took his seat at the center table, expecting a quiet meal. However, before he could even raise his wooden spoon, two strangers approached.

They were not villagers. They moved with the quiet, practiced grace of people who knew how to sleep with one eye open. The man wore supple, forest-green leather armor, a longbow slung across his back, and twin short swords at his hips. He had the lean, weather-beaten look of a man who spent his life outdoors. 

The woman beside him wore simple, practical grey robes over light chainmail. She carried no weapons, but a wooden staff topped with a softly glowing blue crystal rested in her hand. 

"Excuse me," the man said, stopping at Dalton's table. "Are you the one they call Dalton? The one who cleared the North Mine?"

Dalton stood immediately, offering a deep, courtly bow that caused his rusted mail to scrape loudly. "I am Sir Dalton, the Monster Slayer! At your service, kind travelers. Pray, sit and partake of Master Mott's hospitality."

The man exchanged a glance with the woman, an expression that was half-amused and half-bewildered. They sat. 

"I'm Cal," the man said, gesturing to himself. "I'm a Ranger. And this is Deb. She's a Healer."

"A Healer!" Dalton beamed. "A most noble calling! To mend the broken and stitch the torn fabric of life! And a Ranger! A warden of the wilds! I am honored by your company. To what do I owe the pleasure of this morning audience?"

Cal leaned forward, resting his elbows on the rough-hewn table. "We’ll get right to the point. We’ve been tracking a problem on the south road. We're a man down—our frontline fighter took an arrow to the knee two towns back and decided to retire and open an inn."

"A tragic loss to the realm," Dalton nodded sympathetically, having no idea what an 'arrow to the knee' implied beyond bad luck.

"Right," Cal continued. "Anyway, we need a solid frontliner. Someone who can hold aggro and take a hit. We heard what you did to the Iron-Jaw Centipede. That takes guts. Or insanity. Either way, it's what we need." 

Cal paused, offering a friendly, professional smile. "You want to join our party?"

Dalton blinked. He looked at Cal. He looked at Deb. He looked back at Cal. The heroic bravado faltered for a moment, replaced by genuine, utter confusion. 

"You want me... to go to a party with you?" Dalton asked, his tone careful.

"Yeah," Cal nodded enthusiastically. "We'd split the loot evenly, of course."

Dalton’s brow furrowed. "Are we speaking the same language, Master Cal? A festival? Dinner? Dancing? I mean, I am not too shabby with the Waltz, my good man, and I have attended many a harvest gala in my youth, but I fail to see how splitting the 'loot' factors into a social gathering. Do we plan to steal the hors d'oeuvres?"

Sam, who was perched on the edge of Dalton's porridge bowl, slapped a tiny hand over his face. "Dalton, you moron. He means an adventuring party. A group. A squad. They want you to join their team."

Deb, the Healer, let out a soft sigh. She placed a restraining hand on Cal's arm before the Ranger could attempt to untangle the misunderstanding. She stood up, her robes rustling, and stepped out from behind the table. 

With surprising grace, she bowed slightly before 'Sir Dalton.'

"We seek bandits, Sir Dalton," Deb said, her voice carrying a quiet, respectful gravity that perfectly matched Dalton's own theatrical wavelength. "A ruthless gang that preys upon the innocent merchants of the south road. We heard of your heroic feats in the mine. Will you join us, Knight of Arcadia, to hunt them down and rid them from our roads?"

Dalton’s eyes widened. The confusion vanished instantly, replaced by a blazing fire of righteous intent. He slammed his hands onto the table, rattling the wooden bowls.

"Bandits!" Dalton boomed, his voice vibrating with joyous outrage. "Well, why didn't you say so! Footpads! Cutpurses! Villains who prey upon the weak! This is a cause worthy of my steel! Alas, let us find these fiends and show them the true meaning of the word Justice!"

As he spoke the words, a new, golden pane of light materialized before him.
