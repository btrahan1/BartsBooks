# Chapter 18: The Mage and The Rogue

With the remnants of breakfast quickly consumed, Dalton insisted on paying the tab with a handful of copper from his centipede-funded fortune. He tipped Mott with a sweeping bow and a theatrical declaration of the innkeeper's "unparalleled culinary mastery," which caused Mott to simply blink and immediately go back to washing mugs.

Stepping out of the Golden Pottle into the cool morning air, Dalton felt the familiar thrill of a new act beginning. He adjusted his rusted iron helmet, the chainmail hauberk clinking solidly against his chest.

"Lead the way, Master Cal!" Dalton proclaimed, resting a hand on the pommel of his sword. "Let us ride—or rather, march—to the south road and meet these footpads in the field!"

"Not so fast," Cal said, adjusting the strap of his bow. "We just need to pick up the other two party members, Ferna and Shade. They're waiting by the south gate."

Dalton’s smile broadened. "Ah, the more the merrier! A true fellowship! A five-pronged force for Justice and Righteousness!"

"Sure," murmured Cal, a wry grin tugging at the corner of his mouth. "Shade will be all over that, being a Rogue and all."

Dalton stopped dead in the middle of the muddy street. The smile vanished, replaced by an expression of profound, offended shock. 

"A Rogue!" Dalton gasped, clutching his chest as if physically struck by the word. "Surely not! You jest, Master Cal! You travel with a knave? A scoundrel of the alleys? A man who would steal the very pennies from a blind beggar's cup?"

Deb sighed, stepping between Dalton and the confused villagers who had stopped to stare at the yelling man in the rusted bucket helmet. "Sir Dalton, 'Rogue' is just a Class. It doesn't mean he's an actual criminal. It just means he specializes in stealth, scouting, and... acquiring things quietly."

"He steals things quietly," Dalton summarized, his eyes narrowing with suspicion. "A true Knight does not associate with thieves! We walk in the light! We do not slink in the shadows like a beaten cur!"

"He picks locks and checks for traps, Dalton," Sam piped up, hovering just behind Dalton's ear. "Unless you want to open every chest with your face, I suggest you get over your moral outrage."

Dalton puffed out his chest, though his indignation wavered slightly at the mention of traps. "Very well. But I shall keep a close eye on this 'Shade.' If he attempts to ply his villainous trade upon the innocent, he shall answer to my blade!"

They reached the south gate of Oakhaven. Leaning against the wooden palisade was a young woman in robes that were a vibrant, unapologetic crimson. She held a gnarled wooden staff and was currently entertaining herself by lazily tossing a ball of crackling orange fire from one hand to the other. 

"That's Ferna," Cal said, waving to the woman. 

Ferna caught the fireball, letting it dissipate into harmless smoke, and walked over. She had a sharp, intelligent face and a smirk that suggested she found the entire world slightly ridiculous.

"So this is the new meat shield?" Ferna asked, looking Dalton up and down. "I heard he killed the Iron-Jaw with a club made of rat teeth. I was expecting someone... bigger. And less rusty."

"I am Sir Dalton, the Monster Slayer!" Dalton corrected, offering a crisp, rattling bow. "And I am a Warrior, Lady Ferna, not a 'meat shield.' Though I shall gladly stand between you and danger, for it is the duty of the strong to protect the scholarly!"

Ferna raised an eyebrow, clearly amused. "Scholarly? Buddy, I set things on fire for a living. But I appreciate the sentiment. Where's the edgelord?"

"Right behind you."

The voice came from the shadows of the gatehouse. A figure detached itself from the gloom with unsettling silence. He was dressed entirely in black leather, his face half-obscured by a dark cowl and a black cloth mask pulled up over his nose. He carried twin daggers at his waist, and he emanated an aura of someone trying very, very hard to be brooding and mysterious.

**[ SHADE (ROGUE, LEVEL 6) ]**

Dalton squinted at the newcomer. "You... you are the Rogue? Why are you dressed as if attending a funeral in a coal mine?"

Shade crossed his arms, leaning against the wooden wall. "I am one with the shadows. I am the blade you do not see. The whisper of death in the—"

"You're a man standing in the morning sun wearing black leather," Dalton interrupted, his tone completely earnest. "You must be sweltering, my good fellow. And the mask! Are you afflicted by a plague? Or perhaps a severe case of the sniffles?"

Shade stiffened, his eyes narrowing furiously above his mask. "It's to conceal my identity! To strike fear into the hearts of our enemies!"

"It makes you look like a footpad who forgot his own face," Dalton stated matter-of-factly. He took a step closer, resting his hand on his sword. "Listen well, Master Shade. I have been informed that your 'Class' requires a certain... flexibility with the law. I shall tolerate your presence for the sake of the quest. But know this: if I catch you pilfering from widows or orphans, the shadows will not save you from my Heroic Strike!"

Shade looked at Cal, his dark, brooding persona completely shattered by sheer exasperation. "Cal, where did you find this guy? He's a walking alarm bell! We're supposed to be ambushing bandits, and he's clanking around like a dropped bag of pots and pans!"

"He killed the Iron-Jaw," Cal reminded him softly. 

"I don't care if he killed a dragon!" Shade hissed. "He's too loud!"

"Justice is rarely quiet, Master Shade!" Dalton boomed, slapping the Rogue on the back with a heavy, chainmail-clad hand. The blow nearly knocked Shade into the mud. "Now, enough of this idle chatter! The road awaits! The bandits tremble! Let the Vanguard of the South Road march!"

Dalton spun on his heel and began marching down the dirt road, his rusted armor clinking with every step. 

Ferna laughed, tossing another small fireball into the air. "I like him. He's got flair."

Deb sighed, adjusting her healing staff, while Cal just shook his head. Shade, muttering darkly about the indignities of amateur hour, melted back into the shadows to follow. 

Sam hovered above them all, pulling a small, spectral flask from his vest. "Five minutes," the imp muttered to himself. "I give this party five minutes before they start trying to kill each other."
