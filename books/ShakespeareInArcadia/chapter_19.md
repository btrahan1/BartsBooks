# Chapter 19: The Broken Wagon

The South Road was less of a road and more of a deeply rutted dirt path that wound its way through a dense, overgrown forest of twisted oaks and thorny underbrush. For a party trying to maintain an element of surprise, the Vanguard of the South Road was doing a spectacularly poor job, primarily due to their newest member.

"And so I said to the beast, 'Release me, you uncouth boor!'" Dalton recounted loudly, gesturing with his rusted sword as he marched at the front of the formation. "And with a single, devastating blow, I pierced its armored hide! A performance for the ages!"

Shade, who was walking three paces behind Dalton and attempting to blend into the dappled shadows of the canopy, let out a noise that sounded like a kettle boiling dry. "Will you please stop yelling? If there are bandits within five miles of here, they already know our names, our classes, and what you had for breakfast!"

"A knight does not whisper his arrival, Master Shade!" Dalton replied, glancing back over his shoulder. "Fear is the weapon of the coward! Let them hear my approach and despair!"

Ferna chuckled, a small spark of flame dancing across her fingertips. "I'm telling you, Shade, he's great bait. We just let him walk into the clearing, the bandits all stare at him because they can't believe what they're seeing, and then I incinerate them."

"I am not bait, Lady Ferna! I am the vanguard!"

"Same thing, really," Cal muttered, his eyes constantly scanning the tree line. The Ranger's hand rested casually near the fletching of his arrows. "Quiet down, both of you. We're getting close to the last spot the merchant caravan was hit."

The party rounded a bend in the road, the dense trees pulling back to reveal a small clearing. Sitting in the center of the muddy path, tilted at a severe angle due to a shattered rear wheel, was a wooden merchant wagon. Sitting on a rock near the broken wheel, her face buried in her hands, was a solitary figure cloaked in tattered brown wool. She appeared to be weeping softly.

Dalton stopped, his heart swelling with immediate, righteous sympathy. 

"Ah!" Dalton exclaimed, his voice ringing out across the clearing. "Travellers in distress! What an opportune moment for us to lend assistance! A chance to prove the nobility of our newly forged fellowship!"

He took a step forward, fully intending to offer the weeping woman his cloak, his protection, and possibly a brief soliloquy on the nature of hope.

Before his boot could hit the mud, a dark, gloved hand shot out from the shadows and clamped down hard on Dalton's shoulder armor.

"Hush, you fool," Shade hissed, his voice dropping to a harsh, urgent whisper. "It's a trap!"

Dalton turned, looking at the Rogue with an expression of profound disappointment. "A trap? Master Shade, your cynicism is a poison to your soul! Surely you jest! I can clearly see a damsel in distress!"

Cal stepped up beside Dalton, his longbow suddenly in his hand, an arrow already nocked to the string. The Ranger's eyes were not on the weeping woman, but on the dense foliage surrounding the wagon. 

"Look more closely, young Warrior," Cal said, his voice calm but tight with tension, "and prepare your shield."

Dalton frowned, squinting through the visor of his rusted helmet. He looked at the broken wagon. He looked at the weeping damsel. 

Now that he was actually looking—and not just reacting to a familiar stage trope—he began to notice a few inconsistencies in the 'damsel's' performance. The shoulders beneath the tattered brown cloak were remarkably broad. The hands covering the 'weeping' face were thick, calloused, and currently clutching what looked suspiciously like the handle of a loaded heavy crossbow hidden beneath the fabric. And the weeping sound itself was less of a tragic sob and more of a rhythmic, forced hacking.

"By the Bard," Dalton gasped, his theatrical outrage instantly overtaking his sympathy. "A false maiden! A ruse! A poorly acted, uncouth deception! The sheer audacity of the performance!"

"They're in the trees," Deb said quietly from the back, her wooden staff beginning to glow with a soft, pulsing blue light. "I count at least six. Maybe more."

The 'damsel' must have realized the ruse had failed. The weeping stopped abruptly. The figure stood up, throwing off the brown cloak to reveal a massive, scarred man in studded leather armor with a terrifyingly large crossbow aimed directly at Dalton's chest.

"Well," the bandit grunted, spitting a stream of dark tobacco juice into the dirt. "So much for the easy way. Kill 'em all! Except the mage, keep her alive!"

"Oh, you are definitely burning first," Ferna said, her eyes flashing with a sudden, dangerous orange light as twin fireballs roared to life in her palms.

The forest erupted. Shouts echoed from the treeline as half a dozen ragged men armed with axes, rusty swords, and shortbows charged out of the brush. 

Shade didn't draw a weapon; he simply vanished, melting into the shadows of the nearby trees with impossible speed. Cal let his arrow fly, the projectile taking a charging bandit in the shoulder before the man could even raise his axe.

Dalton didn't flinch. He didn't look at the odds. He looked at the massive man with the crossbow, the man who had dared to mock the sacred trope of the damsel in distress.

"Villains!" Dalton roared, drawing his rusted arming sword and slamming it against his militia shield. The metallic *CLANG* cut through the shouts of the charging bandits. "You have drawn the curtain on your own lives! Prepare to face the Vanguard!"

He lowered his shoulder, ignoring the warning click of the heavy crossbow, and charged.
