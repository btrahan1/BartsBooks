# Chapter 20: The Aftermath

The clearing smelled heavily of ozone, scorched earth, and the coppery tang of fresh blood. The sounds of combat had faded, replaced by the quiet rustle of the wind through the leaves and the occasional crackle of a lingering, magical ember. 

Five bandits lay dead in the mud. The massive man who had played the weeping damsel was currently pinned to the broken wagon by two of Cal's arrows, while the others bore the unmistakable, charred marks of Ferna's pyromancy and the deep, clean cuts of a Rogue's daggers. 

And then there was Dalton. 

The 'Vanguard of the South Road' was currently sitting on a mossy log, attempting to look dignified despite the fact that he resembled a rusted iron pincushion. Three arrows protruded from the gaps in his chainmail—one in his left shoulder, one grazing his ribs, and one embedded uncomfortably close to his thigh. 

He hadn't killed all five bandits. In fact, he had only managed to land one solid 'Heroic Strike' on a charging axeman. But he had charged with such roaring, theatrical fury that every single bandit had focused their fire entirely on him, completely ignoring the squishy Mage and the Healer in the backline. 

"He's a fool," murmured Shade, cleaning his twin daggers on the cloak of a fallen bandit. He didn't look at Dalton; he just shook his head, his dark cowl hiding his expression. "He charged a loaded heavy crossbow while yelling his own name. It's a miracle he's not pinned to a tree."

"He's a great meat shield," countered Deb, walking over to Dalton with her glowing staff. 

She didn't ask if he was ready. She simply gripped the shaft of the arrow in his shoulder and gave a sharp, practiced pull. 

Dalton’s breath hitched, his jaw clenching tight. He attempted to maintain a stoic, knightly visage, staring straight ahead and trying very hard not to scream. "A mere... scratch," Dalton ground out through gritted teeth. "'Tis not so deep as a well, nor so wide as a church door, but 'tis... 'tis quite annoying."

"Hold still," Deb sighed. She placed her glowing staff against the bleeding wound. 

A warm, soothing blue light washed over Dalton's shoulder. The pain vanished instantly, the torn flesh knitting itself back together in a matter of seconds.

"Fascinating," Sam observed, sitting cross-legged on Dalton's rusted helmet. "Having a Healer around is much better than watching you slowly bleed to death while reciting poetry. I could get used to this."

Deb moved to the arrow in his ribs, pulling it free with another sickening *schlick*. Dalton winced again, but the blue light quickly followed. 

"You did well, Sir Dalton," Cal said, walking over and retrieving one of his arrows from a nearby tree trunk. "You drew all their aggro. Kept them entirely focused on the front line. Ferna didn't even have to put up her mana shield."

"Yeah, thanks for that," Ferna called out, casually stepping over a charred bandit. "Usually I have to spend half the fight running away from axemen. It was nice to just stand still and aim for once."

Dalton puffed out his chest, though he was careful not to move the final arrow in his thigh. "It is the duty of the Knight to draw the ire of the villain! Let their cowardly arrows find my iron, so that your... fiery conjurations may strike true!"

Deb pulled the last arrow, applied the healing light, and stepped back, wiping her hands on a cloth. "There. You're whole again. But we have a problem. There were six of them in the trees. One of the archers bolted when Ferna set the bushes on fire."

"I'll follow the one that escaped," whispered a voice from the shadows. 

Dalton jumped slightly as Shade seemed to detach himself from the trunk of a large oak tree just behind the broken wagon. 

"He's terrified," the Rogue continued, his voice barely a rasp. "He isn't watching his tracks. He will probably lead us straight back to their main camp."

Without waiting for a reply, Shade stepped backward into the deepest patch of shade and simply vanished. It wasn't magic, exactly—it was just an absurdly high stealth skill—but the effect was unnerving. 

Dalton stared at the empty space where the Rogue had just been. He rubbed his newly healed shoulder, remembering the way Shade had dispatched the bandit leader. During the chaos of Dalton's charge, Shade had simply appeared behind the massive, crossbow-wielding man and driven two daggers into his spine with cold, silent efficiency. 

"That still gives me the chills," Dalton admitted, his voice dropping slightly. "To strike from the dark without a word of warning or a formal declaration of intent... and even though that backstab of his is unseemly, it is undeniably effective."

"It's called Sneak Attack, Dalton," Sam said, patting the top of Dalton's helmet. "It does bonus damage. Don't knock it until you've tried it. Though, considering you wear rusted chainmail and yell your attacks, I don't think stealth is in your future."

Cal slung his bow over his shoulder and began to examine the mud near the treeline. "Shade left a marker trail. We give him a ten-minute head start, then we follow. If we're lucky, we can take out the rest of this gang before the sun goes down."

"To the bandit camp!" Dalton declared, raising his sword and pointing it dramatically into the woods. "Let us follow the trail of the coward, and bring the final act to this wretched troupe!"

"Just... try to keep the yelling to a minimum until we actually see the camp, okay?" Deb asked gently. 

Dalton offered her a gallant bow. "For you, my lady of healing, I shall be as quiet as a mouse! A heavily armored, rusted mouse, but a mouse nonetheless!"
