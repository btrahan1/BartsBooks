# Chapter 21: The Bandit Leader

The trail left by Shade was subtle—a scuffed rock here, a uniquely snapped twig there—but to Cal's trained eyes, it might as well have been a paved road. The party moved through the darkening forest with surprising stealth, aided mostly by the fact that Dalton was concentrating so hard on placing his feet quietly that his face had turned a deep shade of crimson. He held his rusted sword tightly against his chainmail to prevent it from clanking, moving with the exaggerated, tip-toeing gait of a pantomime burglar. 

They eventually reached a rocky ridge that dropped off into a shallow ravine. Hidden behind a thick blind of ferns, the Vanguard of the South Road peered down into the bandit camp. 

It was a large operation. Tents made of stolen canvas dotted the ravine floor, centered around a massive, roaring bonfire. The escaped bandit was standing near the flames, panting heavily, his face pale with terror. He was surrounded by a dozen of his compatriots, and more were emerging from the tents.

"I'm tellin' ya, it was a massacre!" the escaped bandit cried, his voice carrying up to the ridge. "A roaring madman in steel, carving through our ranks like wheat! And fire coming down from the heavens! Bows raining death from the shadows! We didn't stand a chance!"

Dalton puffed out his chest behind the ferns, a proud smile forming beneath his visor. "A roaring madman," he mouthed silently to Ferna. "A compelling review of my performance."

Down below, the crowd of bandits parted. A man stepped forward into the firelight. He was a mountain of a human being, built like a brick wall and clad in heavy, studded leather. He had a thick, black beard and carried a two-handed broadsword resting casually on his shoulder. 

**[ GREGOR (BANDIT LEADER) : LEVEL 7 ]**

Gregor listened patiently to the fleeing bandit's panicked tale. He didn't interrupt. He simply stood there, his face an impassive mask. When the man finally ran out of breath, Gregor nodded slowly.

"So," Gregor said, his voice a deep, gravelly rumble. "You left your brothers to die, and you ran."

"I... I came to warn you!" the bandit stammered, taking a step back.

"You did," Gregor agreed. 

In a movement so fast it seemed entirely unnatural for a man of his size, Gregor swung the massive broadsword down from his shoulder. The blade caught the firelight for a fraction of a second before he ran the escaped bandit completely through. 

Dalton gasped. It was a sharp, involuntary intake of air, driven by pure shock at the casual, unprovoked murder of a subordinate. The sound was loud enough to carry, but before it could fully escape his helmet, a black-gloved hand clamped firmly over the iron grill covering his mouth. 

"Quiet," Shade hissed, his mouth pressed right against Dalton's ear. The Rogue had appeared out of nowhere, seamlessly rejoining the group.

Dalton nodded, his eyes wide. Shade slowly removed his hand, wiping it with distaste on his leather breeches. 

Down in the ravine, Gregor pulled his blade free, letting the body fall into the dirt. He didn't even bother to wipe the steel. He turned to the stunned crowd of his remaining men.

"Prepare, men!" Gregor shouted, his voice echoing off the ravine walls. "This idiot has no doubt led them to our camp! String your bows! Sharpen your axes! We have guests coming, and we shall offer them a bloody welcome!"

Dalton sized up the leader from the safety of the ridge. Gregor was huge. He was Level 7. He clearly knew how to use that broadsword. But as Dalton watched the man, he felt a strange sense of calm. Gregor was a mountain, yes, but he wasn't a monster. He didn't have venom, he didn't have acidic spit, and he certainly didn't have impenetrable, chitinous armor. 

*He is but a man,* Dalton thought, his grip tightening on his own sword. *A villainous, uncouth man. He is surely no match for the giant centipede I have already defeated.*

Cal gently tapped Dalton's shoulder and pulled the party back from the edge of the ridge, retreating deep into the shadow of the trees. 

"There are twenty of them down there, counting the leader," Cal whispered, keeping his voice barely above a breath. "A frontal assault right now would be suicide. Even with Ferna's fire, we'd be overwhelmed by sheer numbers."

"We wait until nightfall," Cal continued, outlining the plan. "When the dark settles, Shade will slip into the camp. He'll give them a little taste of poison in their stew pots. Just something to make them drowsy with their evening meal. Thins the herd, slows their reflexes. Then, we strike."

Dalton opened his mouth, the words 'A coward's tactic!' rising instinctively to his lips. To poison a foe before battle? It was the height of unchivalrous behavior! It was the work of assassins and knaves!

He looked at Shade, ready to object, but he paused. 

He thought of Gregor, driving a sword through the chest of his own man. The bandits had no honor. They preyed on the weak, they set traps on the road, and they murdered their own. 

Furthermore, as Sam had pointed out, Dalton was a Level 6 Warrior, not an immortal god. Twenty-to-four were odds that even the bards would struggle to spin into a victory. If the 'uncouth' tactics of the Rogue could level the stage, then perhaps... perhaps it was a necessary evil. 

"I see the wisdom in your design, Master Cal," Dalton whispered back, surprising everyone, especially Sam. "It may not be the most honorable of overtures... but these men have forsaken honor. Let them taste the bitter stew of their own villainy."

Dalton turned his gaze back toward the direction of the ravine. His eyes burned with a righteous fire. The extras could be put to sleep. But the leader?

"That leader, however," Dalton murmured, his voice tight with anticipation. "He and I... we have a date with destiny."
