# Chapter 22: The Reckoning

The night stretched long and cold over the ravine. Shade had returned from his infiltration hours ago, melting out of the darkness with a grim nod. The deed was done. 

The bandits, paranoid from the escaped scout’s tale, were on high alert. They took their meals in strict shifts, eating from the large, communal stew pot at thirty-minute intervals to ensure the camp was never fully occupied with supper. Little did they know, this cautious protocol only sealed their doom. Shade had used a mild, slow-acting paralytic—something designed to induce heavy drowsiness rather than sudden death, ensuring the first eaters wouldn't raise the alarm for the rest.

As the moon reached its zenith and the hours crawled toward 3 A.M., the tension in the camp began to visibly slacken. The sentries leaned heavily against their posts, their heads nodding, while those off-duty snored loudly around the dying embers of the bonfire. 

From their vantage point on the ridge, the Vanguard prepared.

"Go," Cal whispered. 

The attack began in absolute silence. Cal’s bowstring twanged twice in rapid succession; two arrows vanished into the darkness, followed immediately by the dull thud of two sentries hitting the dirt. Simultaneously, Shade appeared behind the opposite pair of scouts, his daggers flashing in the moonlight. 

Four down. Sixteen remaining.

Then, the silence shattered. 

The night ignited as a massive sphere of roaring orange flame materialized above Ferna’s upraised staff. She hurled it like a javelin into the center of the camp. The fireball landed directly amidst a cluster of five bandits who were sleeping off their poisoned stew near the tents. 

The explosion shook the ravine. The five bandits were instantly out of the fight, screaming as the magical fire consumed the canvas and sent them scrambling into the mud.

That was the cue. 

From the shadows of the main path, Dalton strode down into the flickering, chaotic light of the burning camp. He didn't run. He marched with the slow, measured tread of an executioner. 

"Cry 'Havoc!', and let slip the dogs of war!" Dalton roared, his voice magnified by the acoustic bowl of the ravine and the metallic echo of his rusted helm. "Foul usurpers of the road! Your reign of terror ends tonight! The Vanguard comes for your souls, and we shall not leave until this stage is swept clean of your villainy!"

The effect was not quite what Dalton intended, but it was incredibly effective nonetheless. The remaining bandits, groggy from the poison and panicked by the fire, simply froze. They stared at the heavily armored man striding toward them, their axes lowered as they desperately tried to understand the archaic, booming words. 

*What the hell is a 'havoc'?* one bandit’s expression seemed to say.

Cal and Shade took merciless advantage of the confusion. Two more arrows found their marks, and Shade’s daggers claimed another throat while the bandits were still trying to decipher Dalton's monologue.

"Idiots! Don't listen to him!" Gregor's voice boomed over the chaos. The massive Bandit Leader stepped out of the largest tent, his broadsword already in hand. "Kill him!"

The spell was broken. Five bandits shook off their confusion and rushed Dalton, their faces twisted in rage, hacking and slashing with rusty axes and chipped swords. 

Dalton met them with gusto. He raised his militia shield, catching an axe blow that splintered the pine wood, then parried a sword thrust, spinning to avoid a third attacker. He was vastly outnumbered, but the tight formation worked against the bandits, causing them to stumble over each other as they tried to reach him.

He took hits—a shallow cut across his arm, a bruising blow to his chainmail-covered ribs—but he didn't stop moving, and he certainly didn't stop talking. 

"Baseborn curs!" Dalton shouted, driving his sword into the knee of the closest attacker. "Is this the best the shadows can muster? You swing like tired farmers! Have at thee, you unwashed lot!"

He fought like a hurricane of rusted iron, blocking, dodging, and parrying, a faint blue light occasionally washing over him as Deb cast healing spells from the ridge to knit his minor wounds together. 

With a final, sweeping blow that sent two bandits staggering backward, Dalton broke through the frontline. He stepped into the center of the camp, ignoring the chaos raging around him, his eyes fixed entirely on the mountain of a man standing before the command tent.

Dalton raised his sword, pointing the bloodied, rusted tip directly at Gregor's chest.

"Fiend!" Dalton shouted, his voice cutting through the sounds of combat like a clarion call. "I challenge you! A fight to the death!"

The request was so absurd, so entirely out of place in the middle of a muddy, bloody ambush, that the entire camp just... stopped. 

The remaining bandits lowered their weapons, staring at the lone Warrior. Up on the ridge, Cal paused with an arrow half-drawn. Shade froze mid-backstab. Even Ferna let a fireball dissipate in her hands. Everyone simply stopped and stared at the man who had just demanded a formal duel in the middle of a chaotic brawl. 

Gregor looked at the sword pointing at his chest. He looked at the rusted chainmail and the dented helmet. Slowly, a deep, rumbling sound began in the Bandit Leader's chest, echoing out into the night. 

Gregor threw back his head and laughed. It was a cold, cruel sound that promised absolute destruction.

"To the death, it shall be!" Gregor roared, raising his massive broadsword. "Clear the circle! Let the madman die!"
