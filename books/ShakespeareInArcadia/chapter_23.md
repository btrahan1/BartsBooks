# Chapter 23: Fighting With Honor

The muddy center of the bandit camp transformed into a makeshift arena. The surviving bandits, perhaps grateful for a reprieve from the arrows and fireballs, backed away to form a loose ring. On the ridge, the Vanguard of the South Road held their fire, watching in stunned silence as their heavily armored Warrior squared off against the mountain of a man known as Gregor.

"You have spirit, fool," Gregor rumbled, rolling his shoulders. The massive broadsword in his hands looked as light as a willow switch. "But spirit doesn't stop steel."

"We shall see, villain!" Dalton replied, raising his splintering pine shield and resting his rusted arming sword on the rim. "The final act is rarely decided by the size of the prop, but by the conviction of the actor!"

Gregor didn't bother with a witty retort. He lunged. 

The Bandit Leader was fast—terrifyingly fast for a man of his size. The broadsword came down in a whistling arc that held none of the clumsy desperation of his subordinates. 

Dalton raised his shield, bracing for the impact. 

*CRACK!*

The force of the blow drove Dalton to his knees. The iron banding on the militia shield groaned in protest, and a fresh spray of splinters exploded from the pine. Dalton tried to push back and counter, but Gregor was already moving, spinning the heavy blade around and bringing the pommel crashing down onto Dalton's rusted helmet.

Dalton saw stars. His ears rang with the metallic *gong* of the strike, and he scrambled backward in the mud to avoid a horizontal slash that would have opened him from hip to shoulder. 

Gregor laughed, a booming sound of pure confidence. He didn't rush. He stalked forward, testing Dalton's guard with sharp, punishing strikes. Dalton blocked, parried, and desperately tried to find an opening, but the disparity in their skill was painfully obvious. Gregor was a seasoned killer. Dalton was a stagehand who had spent a few hours practicing on giant bugs.

*Clang!* Another heavy blow bypassed the shield, biting deeply into the rings of Dalton's mail hauberk.

**[ DAMAGE RECEIVED: 28 (SLASHING) ]**
**[ HEALTH: 42 / 110 ]**

Up on the ridge, Deb let out a sharp gasp. She stepped forward to the edge of the drop-off, gripping her wooden staff with both hands. The blue crystal at the top flared to life, a pulse of healing magic gathering in the air, ready to wash over the battered Warrior.

Dalton saw the blue light reflecting in the puddles of mud. He knew what it meant. He felt the blood running down his side, and the exhaustion burning in his arms. But as Gregor raised his broadsword for another devastating strike, Dalton did the unthinkable.

He dropped his guard, stopped mid-fight, and threw his left hand up toward the ridge.

"No!" Dalton roared, his voice echoing fiercely over the crackle of the bonfire. "No healing!"

Deb froze, the magical aura sputtering around her staff. "Dalton, you're going to die! Let me cast!"

"I will not!" Dalton shouted back, his chest heaving. He turned his gaze back to the massive Bandit Leader, his eyes burning with an unshakeable, terrifying conviction beneath his dented visor. "This is a duel! A challenge issued and accepted! I will not taint the stage with external machinations! I will finish this knave with honor!"

Gregor paused, his broadsword resting on his shoulder. He looked at Dalton, genuinely bewildered. "Honor? You're bleeding out in the mud, wearing garbage, and you refuse the witch's magic because of *honor*?"

"It is all a man truly owns!" Dalton declared, gripping his rusted sword with both hands and abandoning the ruined shield. 

Gregor scoffed, his eyes hardening into twin chips of black flint. "Then die with it."

The Bandit Leader charged, putting his entire massive weight behind a devastating, two-handed overhead strike designed to cleave Dalton in twain. It was a finishing move, telegraphed by the sheer momentum required to execute it. 

Dalton didn't try to block. He couldn't. Instead, he relied on the one thing he possessed in abundance: theatrical timing. He had watched a thousand stage-fights. He knew the rhythm of a heavy strike.

At the exact second the broadsword reached the apex of its arc, Dalton stepped *inside* Gregor's guard. He slipped past the descending blade by a fraction of an inch, feeling the wind of the steel rush past his cheek.

"HEROIC STRIKE!" Dalton screamed, pouring every remaining ounce of his stamina and strength into his arms. 

He drove the rusted iron sword upward, burying it to the hilt in the gap of Gregor's studded leather armor, right beneath the sternum. 

The white glow of the skill flared brilliantly, illuminating the shocked expression on Gregor's face. The massive Bandit Leader let out a choked, wet gasp. His broadsword slipped from his hands, burying itself point-first into the mud with a dull thud. 

Gregor staggered backward, his hands grasping at the hilt of the sword protruding from his chest. He looked at Dalton, opening his mouth to speak, but only a gurgle escaped. The giant man slowly, inevitably, crashed to his knees, and then pitched forward into the dirt.

Dalton stood alone in the center of the camp. The silence was absolute. He looked down at the fallen leader, then up at his party on the ridge. He offered a slow, shaky nod of triumph.

"The curtain..." Dalton wheezed, his voice barely a whisper. "...falls."

And with that final, dramatic declaration, his eyes rolled back in his head, and Sir Dalton toppled over like a felled oak, hitting the mud face-first.

There was a moment of stunned silence, followed immediately by the sound of someone scrambling down the rocky incline of the ridge. 

Deb rushed into the clearing, her robes swishing through the mud. She dropped to her knees beside Dalton, flipping the unconscious, heavily armored man onto his back. She practically slammed her glowing staff into his chest, flooding his battered body with blue, healing light.

As the color returned to his face and his breathing steadied, Dalton’s eyes fluttered open. He looked up at the Healer, offering a weak, lopsided smile.

Deb let out a long, shuddering breath, her expression a mix of profound relief and absolute exasperation. She looked down at him, her staff still glowing softly.

"Can I heal you now, 'Sir Dalton, The Idiot'?" Deb asked, her voice trembling slightly.

Dalton opened his mouth to reply, but a metallic clatter interrupted him. 

Around the camp, the remaining bandits—having just watched a madman refuse magical healing, slay their invincible leader in single combat, and then pass out in the mud—were dropping their axes and swords. 

The clattering of weapons was followed by the sound of twelve men simultaneously dropping to their knees, their hands raised in the air. 

"I believe," Dalton whispered, closing his eyes again, "that the audience approves."
