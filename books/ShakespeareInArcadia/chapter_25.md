# Chapter 25: Gearing Up

The return of the Vanguard to Oakhaven was the closest thing the village had seen to a proper parade in a decade. 

The six surviving bandits, bound tightly by Cal's hempen ropes, were marched through the muddy gates with their heads hung low. The villagers lined the streets, staring in awed silence at the captives and the four adventurers leading them. 

At the head of the procession marched Dalton. He was battered, his rusted mail was torn, and he smelled of smoke and goblin-stew, but he walked with the posture of a conquering emperor. 

Reeve Franklin was waiting on the steps of his large house. He looked at the prisoners, then at the massive sack of bandit loot that Shade dragged behind him. The Reeve’s calculating eyes widened, rapidly doing the mental math on how much this would increase the town's safety—and his own tax revenues.

"The bounty is yours, Vanguard," Franklin announced, his thin voice carrying over the murmuring crowd. "And as per the King's law, you are entitled to twenty percent of the value of all stolen goods recovered from the camp."

The next few hours were a blur of bureaucratic haggling. Shade handled the negotiations with the local appraiser, employing an intimidating silence that successfully bumped their cut up by an extra handful of coppers. 

When the dust settled and the coin was evenly split among the four party members, Dalton stood outside the Golden Pottle, taking stock of his newfound wealth. He poured the contents of his small pouch into his gauntleted palm. 

Five heavy silver coins, and a modest smattering of copper. 

"Master Sam," Dalton said, the coins clinking together like musical notes. "The rewards of justice are indeed bountiful. Five silver pieces! I am a man of means!"

"You're a man who needs to stop wearing rusty garbage before he gets tetanus," Sam replied, perched on the edge of the inn's water trough. "You have silver now. Actual, spendable silver. It's time to go shopping. And no, you still can't afford a horse. Don't even ask."

Dalton sighed, though he couldn't hide his excitement. "A knight must first secure his armor before he seeks his steed! To the smithy!"

Oakhaven’s armorer and weaponsmith shared a single, soot-stained building near the edge of town. It wasn't the legendary forge of a dwarven master, but it was hot, loud, and smelled of honest work. 

Dalton entered with purpose. He spent the better part of an hour examining the wares, trading his usual poetic bluster for surprisingly practical considerations regarding weight and balance. 

When he finally emerged, he was two silver coins lighter, but the transformation was profound. 

He had traded in the shredded, foul-smelling remains of his rat-fur vest, the dented militia shield, and the chipped, rusted sword he had borrowed from the Reeve. 

In their place, he wore a full suit of proper chainmail. It wasn't enchanted, and it still had a few patches of mild discoloration, but it was clean, oiled, and whole. In his left hand rested a sturdy, iron-rimmed kite shield painted with a simple coat of dark blue paint. At his hip hung a proper arming sword—its iron blade straight, polished, and entirely free of rust.

Dalton stepped out into the afternoon sun. The light caught the oiled rings of his mail and the polished hilt of his new sword. He felt like a new man. He felt like the knight he had always claimed to be.

He took a deep breath, throwing his shoulders back. He raised his chin toward the sky, preparing to embark on a speech of true grandeur. He opened his mouth, ready to let the village know that a true champion of steel had finally arrived.

But the words didn't come. 

Dalton stopped mid-breath. He slowly lowered his chin. He looked down at his reflection in a muddy puddle near the smithy's door. He looked at the clean iron, the sturdy shield, and the reality of the world he was in. He thought about the bandits, the poison, the brutal, unscripted reality of his duel with Gregor.

He closed his mouth, the silence feeling heavier than the new chainmail. 

He turned his head slightly, looking at the invisible imp floating near his shoulder. The theatrical resonance was completely gone from his voice, replaced by a quiet, uncertain vulnerability that Sam had never heard from him before.

"Sam," Dalton asked softly. "Do you think I'm stupid?"

Sam opened his mouth, the words *"Of course you're stupid!"* already sitting on the tip of his tongue. It was his default response. It was the truth, objectively speaking. 

But as the imp looked at the man in the clean iron armor, staring down at a muddy puddle with genuine doubt in his eyes, Sam stopped. He closed his mouth, letting the harsh words die. 

Sam floated down, landing softly on the edge of Dalton's newly painted kite shield. He offered an honest appraisal of the man he had been assigned to. 

"You are reckless," Sam began, his voice surprisingly gentle. "You charge into traps while yelling your own name. You fight with zero tactical awareness. And yes, sometimes, you are incredibly stupid."

Dalton winced slightly, looking away.

"But," Sam continued, "you are also brave. You stood between a Level 7 bandit leader and your party when you didn't have to. You're becoming a genuinely good warrior, a competent party member, and without a doubt, the greatest meat shield this side of the capital."

Dalton looked back at the imp, surprise registering on his face.

Sam sighed, rubbing his tiny temples. "To sum it all up... if nothing else, you've earned the title. You're Sir Dalton, the Honorable."

The effect was instantaneous. The heavy cloud of doubt vanished from Dalton's face, replaced immediately by a blinding, triumphant smile. The posture returned, the shoulders went back, and the chin lifted toward the sky.

"The Honorable!" Dalton boomed, his voice returning to its full, theatrical resonance. "A title bestowed not only by the phantom, but by my most trusted confidant! A glorious day! Come, Master Sam, to the tavern! I must regale the villagers with the tale of the bandit battle in all of its unyielding glory!"

Sam watched Dalton march down the street, already waving his sword and practicing his opening lines. The imp just groaned, pulling a tiny, spectral flask from his vest. 

"I shouldn't have said anything," Sam muttered, flying after him.
