# Chapter 4: Swimming Lessons

The transition from the forge to the lake was a ritual of fire and water. 

Every evening, once the last ember had been banked and Silas had retired to his chair with a pipe, Greg would slip out the back of the shop. He didn't head for the tavern or the communal square where the younger men gathered to boast. He headed for the Silverun Lake—a deep, still body of water that fed the rivers and served as the town’s silent mirror.

In his years of soldering—as he called his time in the Imperial Guard—swimming had been a tactical necessity, usually performed in full gear while trying to keep a crossbow dry. It had never been for peace. It had never been for pleasure. 

Now, stripped to his thin linen drawers, Greg waded into the dark water. The initial shock was always the same—a sharp, icy bite that forced the last of the forge’s heat from his bones. He struck out with long, powerful strokes toward the center of the lake. 

There was a small island there, little more than a cluster of ancient weeping willows and a flat, mossy rock that rose from the water like the back of a leviathan. It was out of sight of the town, shielded by the mist and the distance. 

Greg pulled himself onto the rock, the water shedding from his skin in silver sheets under the rising moon. He stood for a moment, letting the night air dry him, and then he began.

Katas. Forms. 

To a soldier, they were the foundation of violence. To Greg, they were a language he was slowly translating into a prayer. 

He moved with a fluidity that would have baffled the townspeople. His feet found the mossy rock with the delicacy of a cat, his hands carving arcs through the air. These were the forms of the Algorian Dragon Guard—moves designed to snap necks and parry spears. But Greg wasn't thinking of enemies. He was thinking of the way the river flowed around a stone. He was thinking of the way the hammer fell on the anvil.

He began to circulate his Ki. 

This was the only time he allowed it. But he didn't use it to enhance his strength or harden his skin. He used it for balance. He moved the energy through his meridians with a slow, deliberate pace, using it to purify the blockages left by the day's toil. It was a meditative internal bath, a way of "getting in touch" with a body that had been used as a weapon for too long. He wasn't reaching for power; he was reaching for a center.

With every rotation of his core, the golden light of his Ki remained deep beneath the surface, a warm hum that stabilized his spirit. In this isolation, he wasn't Sir Gregory, the hero of Algoria. He wasn't Greg, the apprentice. 

He was just a man, standing on a rock in the middle of a lake, trying to be whole.

***

On the shore of the lake, hidden beneath the shadow of a massive, gnarled oak, Clara sat with a brass viewing lens balanced on her knee. 

If anyone had asked, she would have told them she was studying the stars. The Great Orion was rising in the East, after all, and she had always had a fascination with the celestial maps. She would have pointed to the constellation of the Weaver and explained the importance of the equinox.

She definitely was NOT watching Gregory. 

No sir. That would be improper. It would be scandalous. It would be... fascinating.

She’d been "not spying" on him for a week now. It had started as curiosity—why was the new apprentice sneaking out to the lake every night?—and had quickly evolved into a quiet obsession. Through the lens, she saw a side of him that the forge hid. In the shop, he was a silent, soot-covered wall of muscle. On the island, he was something else entirely. 

He was beautiful. 

Not in a soft way, but in the way a storm or a mountain was. His movements were so precise they seemed impossible for a man of his size. He moved like he was dancing with an invisible partner, his body describing lines of grace that made her breath hitch in her throat. And there were moments, brief and fleeting, where he seemed to glow—not with light, but with a vibrancy that made the space around him feel more alive.

Clara adjusted the lens, her heart pounding against her ribs. She saw him finish his exercise and stand for a long time, looking at the moon. He looked so solitary, so burdened by things she couldn't name. She wanted to walk out there. She wanted to ask him why he was here, why he hid his grace behind the bellows, and why he looked like he was apologizing to the air every time he struck his hammer.

She had been trying to work up the courage for nights. Finally, as she saw him dive back into the water to make the return swim, she stood up. Her hands were shaking as she tucked the lens safely into her leather pack. 

She stepped out from behind the tree and walked toward the small, sandy patch where he always emerged. 

Greg reached the shallows and stood up, the water dripping from his broad shoulders. He saw her immediately. He didn't jump or act startled; he simply stood there, his eyes reflecting the moonlight.

Clara felt her face flush, the heat rising all the way to her ears. She looked at the water, at the island, and then at Greg. 

"I'd love to learn how to swim, someday," she sighed, her voice wistful and perhaps a bit too loud in the stillness of the night.

Greg took a few steps toward the shore, the water splashing softly against his legs. He looked at her, his expression unreadable, though his incredible hearing had likely caught the sound of her lens clicking shut minutes ago.

"The water is cold, Clara," he said softly. 

"I don't mind the cold," she replied, regaining some of her bravado. "I’ve lived in this valley my whole life and I’ve never ventured further than the reeds. It seems... a waste. To have all this beauty and be stuck on the edge of it."

Greg stopped a few feet away from her. The smell of the lake—fresh, mossy, and cold—drifted off him. Up close, without the soot of the forge, he looked younger, but his eyes still held that ancient, weary depth.

"Swimming isn't just about moving through the water," Greg said, his voice level. "It’s about letting the water support you. Most people fight it. They thrash because they’re afraid of the depth. But if you trust it, it will hold you up."

Clara looked at him, her eyes wide. "Is that what you were doing out there? Trusting the water?"

"I was trying," Greg admitted. 

He looked back at the island, then back to Clara. He could see the curiosity and the longing in her face. She wasn't just talking about the lake; she was talking about him. She saw the "not normal" parts of him and instead of being afraid, she was reaching out.

"It’s too dark for a first lesson," Greg said, a hint of a smile finally touching his lips. "But if you’re serious... come down on Sunday. The sun warms the shallows near the old dock. It’s a good place to start."

Clara’s heart did a little flip. "Sunday? You’re sure Silas won't have us making a thousand more horseshoes?"

"Silas needs his rest, too. And I think he’d appreciate you knowing how to get yourself out of the river if you ever fall in while chasing your 'stars'."

Clara froze. "I... I don't know what you mean."

Greg just chuckled—a soft, warm sound that made the night feel a little less lonely. "The Great Orion is in the South this time of year, Clara. Not the East."

He walked past her toward the shop, his silent, powerful stride leaving footprints in the damp sand. 

Clara stood there for a long time, her face burning, watching him disappear into the shadows of the forge. She took a deep breath, the scent of the lake filling her lungs. 

She might be a terrible liar, but she was going to be a swimmer. And maybe, just maybe, she’d find out what Sir Gregory—or Greg, as he insisted—was really doing on that island.

As Greg walked back to his cot, he felt a strange lightness in his chest. He had shared a piece of his new world. For the first time in a decade, he hadn't been watching for an assassin or a scout. He’d just been looking at a girl who wanted to see the middle of the lake.

*Honest work. Honest people.*

He closed his eyes and drifted off to the sound of the distant river, the internal rhythm of his Ki finally, for once, in perfect harmony with the world around him.
