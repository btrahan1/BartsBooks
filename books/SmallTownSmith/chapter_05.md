# Chapter 5: Swimming Party for Three?

Clara had spent the better part of the morning carefully selecting a tunic that was both modest and, in her opinion, slightly more flattering than her usual forge attire. She had brushed her hair until it shone like polished walnut and had even considered—for all of three seconds—stealing a bit of her mother’s old lavender oil. 

She had been looking forward to Sunday with a flutter in her stomach that she refused to acknowledge as anything other than "academic interest" in a new skill. 

Then came Silas. 

"The boy is going with you," Silas had announced, gesturing at a youth who was currently attempting to untangle himself from a fishing net near the back of the shop. 

"Tyler?" Clara had asked, her heart sinking like a lead weight. "Silas, Greg is teaching *me* to swim. It’s not a village outing."

"The boy needs to put some muscle on his scrawny body," Silas replied, ignoring his sister’s pout. "And he needs to stay out of trouble. Since the caravan left, he’s been moping around the square like a kicked dog. Greg’s a worker. Maybe some of that will rub off on Tyler."

Which was how Clara found herself walking down to the old dock with a look on her face that could have soured fresh milk. 

Tyler trailed behind her, a sixteen-year-old whirlwind of gangly limbs and nervous energy. He was tall for his age but thin as a reed, his clothes looking two sizes too big despite being practically new. He was excitable, talking a mile a minute about the 'giant' Greg and how he heard Greg once lifted a whole wagon to fix a wheel (a rumor that was only slightly exaggerated). 

Greg was already at the dock. He was sitting on the edge of the weathered timber, his feet dangling in the water. He was wearing a simple sleeveless tunic, which did nothing to hide the terrifyingly impressive musculature of his shoulders. 

"You're late," Greg said, though his voice held no malice. He stood up, and for a second, even the talkative Tyler went silent.

"This is Tyler," Clara said, waving a hand at the boy with a visible frown. "Silas suggested he join us. Said, and I quote: 'The boy needs to put some muscle on his scrawny body.'"

Greg looked Tyler over. He didn't see a scrawny boy; he saw a conscript. He saw the thousands of sixteen-year-olds he’d seen marched into the meat-grinder of the border wars—boys who had died because their chests weren't deep enough to hold their breath in a trench or because their arms lacked the strength to pull a comrade to safety. 

Greg’s smile was small, but genuine. "Ah, yes. Swimming will do that, and then some."

"You really going to teach us, Mr. Greg?" Tyler asked, his eyes wide. "Are we going to swim to the island? I heard there’s a treasure on the island! Or maybe a ghost!"

"We’re going to stay in the shallows until you stop splashing like a dying trout," Greg said, gesturing for them to enter the water. 

Clara waded in first, the cold water making her GASP and clutch at her tunic. Tyler followed with a clumsy splash, shivering as the Silverun claimed him. 

"Stand together," Greg ordered. His voice had changed slightly—it wasn't the voice of the apprentice. It was the voice of the Commander. It was quiet, firm, and brooked no argument. 

They stood chest-deep in the water, looking at him with a mix of anticipation and trepidation. 

"Swimming is the most honest exercise there is," Greg said, wading deeper until he was level with them. "On land, you can use gravity. You can lean on things. In the water, you have only yourself. Every muscle in your body has to work together, or you sink. If you want strength, Tyler, you have to earn it from the water."

The first hour was less 'swimming' and more 'disciplined drowning.' Greg had them practice treading water—a task that looked easy when he did it, but left Tyler purple-faced and Clara gasping within minutes. 

"Higher knees, Tyler! Use your hips, not just your ankles!" Greg barked, though he stayed close enough to grab the boy if he went under. 

"It... hurts..." Tyler wheezed, his thin arms churning the water into a froth. 

"Good," Greg said. "That hurt is your muscles waking up. In a month, you won't even feel it. In two months, you’ll be able to carry a crate of shoes without staggering. In three months..."

Greg trailed off. He didn't say *'In three months, you might survive a forced march.'*

Clara, despite her initial annoyance, found herself watching Greg with renewed fascination. He was a natural teacher. He was patient with Tyler’s clumsiness, but he didn't coddle him. He pushed them both, demanding focus and form. He spoke of the water as an ally, a "cradle of resistance" that forged a different kind of strength—one that was supple rather than brittle.

As they took a break, sitting on the edge of the dock to let their legs stop shaking, Tyler looked at Greg with pure hero-worship. 

"You're really strong, aren't you, Greg? Like... really strong. Silas says you're the best worker he’s ever had. Were you a smith back where you came from?"

Greg looked out toward the island. The sun was golden now, casting long, peaceful shadows across the lake. 

"I was a man who focused on the wrong things," Greg said quietly. "I spent my life learning how to break things. Now, I’m trying to learn how to sustain them."

Tyler tilted his head, confused. "Sustain? You mean like... feeding the cows?"

Greg chuckled. "Something like that, Tyler."

Clara watched Greg’s profile. She saw the way his jaw tightened when Tyler mentioned his strength. She saw the way he looked at the horizon—not with hope, but with a weary expectation of trouble. 

"Greg," she said softly, catching his eye. "Silas is worried. About the recruiters."

The name hit Greg like a physical blow. He felt the phantom weight of his signet ring on his finger, the ghost-scent of Algorian fire-oil. 

"I know," Greg said. 

"They’ll come here, won't they?" Tyler asked, his excitement suddenly replaced by a cold, sharp fear. "They took the Hatcher boys from the next valley last week. My dad says the army needs 'fresh blood' for the new territories."

Greg looked at Tyler’s scrawny shoulders, at the vulnerability in the boy’s eyes. He felt a surge of something he thought he’d buried—protectiveness. Not the protectiveness of a general for his troops, but the protectiveness of a man for his neighbors. 

"If they come," Greg said, his voice dropping to a low, dangerous frequency that made Clara’s skin prickle, "they’ll find that Twin Rivers isn't as 'unstable' as they think."

"What could we do?" Tyler asked, his voice trembling. "They have knights. They have Ki. We’re just... we’re just us."

Greg placed a heavy hand on Tyler’s shoulder. He didn't use any Ki, but the sheer, solid reality of his presence seemed to steady the boy. 

"A knight is just a man in a fancy suit if he doesn't have a heart," Greg said. "And Ki is just a tool. It’s the hand that holds the tool that matters. You just focus on your swimming, Tyler. Focus on making those shoulders broad. We’ll Worry about the recruiters when they get here."

They practiced for another hour. By the time the sun dipped below the mountains, Tyler was exhausted but grinning, bragging about how he’d 'almost' made it ten strokes without stopping. 

Clara walked back with them, her annoyance at Tyler completely gone. She looked at Greg, who was carrying their wet towels with an effortless ease. 

"You're going to keep training him, aren't you?" she asked.

"As long as he shows up," Greg replied. 

"And me?"

Greg looked at her. The moonlight was catching the damp strands of her hair. 

"You’re a better student than he is, Clara. You have balance. He just has energy. One is for the sprint. The other is for the journey."

As they reached the forge, Silas was waiting for them, a lantern in hand. He looked at Tyler’s wet hair and exhausted grin, then at Greg’s steady gaze. 

"Well?" Silas asked. "Is he a trout yet?"

"He’s a minnow," Greg said. "But minnows grow."

Silas nodded, satisfied. He ushered Tyler off toward his home and looked at Greg. 

"Clara told you?" Silas asked, his voice low.

"The recruiters," Greg confirmed. 

"There was a rider at the Trout tonight. A message from the District Governor. They’ll be here by the end of the week. They’re looking for twenty able-bodied men."

Twenty. 

Twin Rivers barely had fifty households. Twenty men would gut the town’s future. 

Greg looked at his hands—the soot-stained, calloused hands of a blacksmith's apprentice. He felt the golden pool of his Ki swirling in his gut, a sleeping dragon that hadn't been fed in a long time. 

"I’ll be here, Silas," Greg said. 

"I know you will, Greg. I know you will."

As Greg lay on his cot that night, the sound of the river felt louder than usual. It was no longer a lullaby; it was a drumbeat. 

The peace of Twin Rivers was about to be challenged. And for the first time since faking his death, Greg wondered if his hammer would be enough, or if he would have to reach for the dragon once more. 

He closed his eyes, his mind's eye picturing Tyler’s scrawny shoulders. 

*Not this time,* Greg vowed. *Not this town.*
