# Chapter 6: The Price of Peace

"They'll be here tomorrow, Greg."

Silas’s voice was like grinding stone. He was standing by the cooling hearth, the lantern light casting long, trembling shadows across the tools. Outside, the crickets of Twin Rivers were silent, as if even the insects knew the air had changed.

Greg didn't look up from the sharpening stone. "I know. Are the boys rounded up?"

"Yes. Twenty-two of them, including Tyler and the Miller twins. They’re gathered at the communal barn near the East pasture. They think you’re going to teach them how to handle a spear before the recruiters arrive. They think they can 'impress' the army, Greg. They’re excited."

Greg stopped the rhythmic scrape of the stone. He looked at the blade—a simple utility knife, but in his hands, it felt like a part of his soul. "Excitement is a luxury for those who don't know the cost of the coin."

Silas walked over, his massive chest heaving with a slow, heavy breath. "What are you going to do, Greg? You’ve been quiet ever since the rider left. Even Clara is terrified to talk to you."

Greg finally looked at Silas. His eyes weren't the eyes of an apprentice anymore. They were cold, Calculating, and ancient. "You won't like it, Silas. But I am going to do what I have to do. For the town."

"What are you planning?" Silas pressed, his hand gripping Greg’s shoulder with enough force to crush bone. Greg didn't move. He didn't even lean away. 

"They aren't ready," Greg said, his voice a low vibration. "The army doesn't want soldiers. They want bodies to fill gaps. If those boys go, they’ll be dead or broken within a month, and Twin Rivers will be a ghost town by Winter. I'm going to make sure the recruiters see that they are useless."

"And how do you plan on doing that?"

"By evening, I want you to have everyone in town gathering bandages, splints, and as much stinging-nettle paste as you can find. Tell the mothers not to scream when they see the boys. Just tell them it’s the price of peace."

Greg didn't wait for an answer. He grabbed his wool cloak and stepped out into the night.

***

The East barn was a cathedral of nervous energy. Twenty-two teenagers sat on hay bales, clutching pitchforks, rusted woodsman’s axes, and the occasional sharpened stick. Tyler was in the center, his chest puffed out, talking about how Greg would show them the 'secret' moves of the knights.

"You really think he'll show us how to use Ki?" one of the Miller twins asked, his eyes gleaming. 

"Greg doesn't need Ki," Tyler boasted. "He’s just strong!"

When the barn doors creaked open, the chatter died instantly. Greg stood in the moonlight, his presence filling the space with a sudden, suffocating weight. He didn't look like their friend. He looked like a god of war who had lost his patience.

"On your feet," Greg commanded.

They scrambled up, stumbling over their makeshift weapons. 

"We're going into the Blackthorn Woods," Greg said. "We’re going to train. And god help the man who holds back."

The boys cheered, their voices high and bright. They didn't see the sorrow in Greg’s eyes. 

The training didn't turn out how they thought. 

Greg didn't show them how to parry or how to strike. Instead, he drove them into the thickest, most treacherous part of the Blackthorn—a place even the hunters avoided. He made them run through the briars until their clothes were tatters. Then, he had them "spar." 

But it wasn't sparring. Greg moved among them like a shadow, his hands striking with surgical precision. He didn't break bones—not really—but he knew exactly where to hit to cause the most vivid, terrifying-looking bruises. He taught them how to fall, how to make a joint look dislocated without it actually being useless, and how to use the mud and the red-berry juice to mimic the look of infected wounds.

"You're hurting us!" Tyler cried, clutching a shoulder that felt like it had been hit by a falling log. 

"The army will do worse," Greg said, his voice a lash. "The recruiters are looking for health. They are looking for vigor. They want boys who can march twenty miles. If you look like you can’t even stand, they will discard you like trash. Do you want to die for a border you’ve never seen, or do you want to stay here and help Silas?"

The realization hit them like cold water. The "training" wasn't for combat; it was for acting. 

Under the canopy of the Blackthorn, Greg orchestrated a massacre that never happened. He had them crawl through the stinging nettles until their skin rose in angry, weeping welts. He had them bash their shields against their own shins until they limped. By the time the moon was high, the twenty-two "warriors" were a pathetic, groaning mess of mud, blood, and bandages.

"Now," Greg said, kneeling beside Tyler, who was sobbing from the sting of the nettles. "Listen to me. Tomorrow, when the soldiers come, you tell them one story. A scouting party of Kragh-loyalist goblins caught you in the woods. You tried to fight. You were slaughtered. You barely escaped. If even one of you looks 'fit,' they will take you. Do you understand?"

"Yes... Mr. Greg," Tyler whispered, clutching his discolored arm.

"Good. Now, back to the barn. Clara and Silas are waiting."

***

The next morning, the sun rose on a Twin Rivers that looked like a war zone. 

The recruiters arrived at Midday—two Algorian officers in pristine blue-and-gold surcoats, followed by a squad of iron-clad heavy infantry. They rode into the square with the arrogance of conquerors, their silver-rimmed glasses scanning the crowd for the "fresh blood" the Empire demanded.

Captain Kael, the lead recruiter, dismounted with a flourish. He was a man Greg knew—a career officer with a penchant for gambling and a total lack of empathy for his subordinates. 

"Where is the headman?" Kael barked.

Silas stepped forward, looking older than Greg had ever seen him. He was draped in a blackened apron, soot covering his face. "I speak for the town, Captain."

"The census says you have twenty capable males of age. Bring them out. We have orders to mobilize immediately."

Silas nodded slowly. He gestured toward the communal barn. 

The doors opened. 

It was a parade of misery. The twenty-two boys emerged, but they didn't march. They limped. They leaned on each other. Tyler was at the front, his face swollen from the nettles, his arm bound in a splint that Greg had expertly fashioned. The Miller twins were pale, their tunics stained with "blood" and mud, their eyes wide with a very real, lingering terror from the night’s ordeal.

Captain Kael backed away, his nose wrinkling in disgust. "What in the Emperor’s name is this?"

"Goblins, Captain," Silas said, his voice flat. "A scouting party from the Blackthorn. The boys were out training... they wanted to be ready for the army. They were ambushed. We’ve spent the night trying to keep them alive."

Kael walked down the line of "soldiers." He poked at a bandage on one boy’s leg, and the youth let out a high-pitched, genuine wail of pain (Greg had ensured the skin beneath was raw from salt-rubs). 

"Goblins?" Kael spat. "You mean to tell me twenty grown boys couldn't even hold off a scouting party of vermin? Look at them! They’re pathetic! They look like they’ve been through a meat-grinder."

"They fought hard, sir," Silas said, hiding a smirk behind his rough hand. "But they are just village boys."

Kael turned to his subordinate, a look of utter contempt on his face. "We can't take these. Half of them will die before we even reach the crossroads, and the other half will infect the camp. It would cost more in medicine just to keep them upright than they’re worth in steel."

He turned back to Silas, pointing a gloved finger at the groaning group. "You should be ashamed. This is the 'security' of our borders? Pathetic. We’ll take the grain and the horses instead. At least the animals can stand."

"As you wish, Captain," Silas said. 

The recruiters stayed only long enough to seize the surplus grain and three of the town’s older mules. They didn't even look twice at the "broken" boys as they rode out of town, shaking their heads in disgust at the weakness of the frontier. 

As the dust from the Algorian horses settled, the "soldiers" began to straighten up. The groans ceased. 

Greg stepped out from the shadows of the forge. He looked at the boys—his twenty-two "students"—who were now looking at their bandaged limbs with a mix of relief and awe. 

"You did well," Greg said.

Tyler stepped forward, his nettle-rash still burning, but his eyes were clear. "We’re really not going, are we?"

"No, Tyler. You’re staying right here."

The cheers this time were quiet, hushed by the weight of what they had nearly lost. They began to unwind the bandages, helping each other clean the mud and the mock-blood. 

Silas walked over to Greg. He looked at the departing recruiters, then at his apprentice. "You were right. I didn't like it. But they're still here."

"The price of peace is often blood," Greg said. "Last night it was just yours and the boys’. But Silas... the Empire isn't stupid. They’ll be back. And next time, they won't be looking for health. They’ll be looking for answers."

"Then we’ll give them those, too," Silas said. 

Greg looked at his hands. The "Dragon" in his core was still asleep, but it was stirring. He had protected the town with a lie, a trick from his old life. But as he watched Clara helping Tyler clean his wounds, he knew that the day was coming when the lie wouldn't be enough. 

For now, though, the Sound of Steel returned to the forge. 

And for the boys of Twin Rivers, the war remained a story told by others.
