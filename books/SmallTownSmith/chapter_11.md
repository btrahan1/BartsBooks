# Chapter 11: The King's Law

Winter had a way of distilling the essence of a thing. 

Under the heavy, silent pressure of the frost, the "Boys of the Lake" had finally, irrevocably, become men. The transformation was startling. Where once there had been gangly limbs and nervous glances, there was now a quiet, coordinated presence. Their skin was darkened by the forge-smoke and the mountain sun, their hands were calloused into leather, and their eyes held the steady, unblinking focus of predators.

They were no longer a ragtag group of easy pickings. They were a unit. 

The winter had been a long, ringing song of steel. Greg had worked alongside Silas, day and night, refining the iron from the Old Crow Mine. Every one of the twenty-two youths now possessed a kit that would have made a regular imperial conscript weep with envy: a mining pick upgraded with a steel-tempered head, a seven-foot ash spear tipped with a needle-sharp iron point, and the masterpiece of the winter—the iron-ringed shield.

The shields were heavy, made of layered oak harvested from the Blackthorn and rimmed with a thick band of Old Crow iron. In the center was a massive iron boss, hammered into a blunt, defensive dome. 

Greg had grilled them mercilessly. In the sub-zero temperatures of the communal barn, he had pushed them through the "Three-Point Phalanx" and the "Shield-Wall Interlock." He had taught them how to move as a single organism, how to lock shields so that no blade could find a gap, and how to use the weight of the man behind you to push back a charge. 

"Time is short," Greg had told them, his breath a white plume in the freezing air. "And the Empire does not wait for the flowers to bloom before it comes for its due."

***

"A town guard?" Silas asked, his eyebrows nearly disappearing into his hairline. 

They were sitting in the small living quarters of the forge, the map of the District spread out between them. Greg had been writing in a neat, disciplined hand on a piece of official-looking parchment he’d acquired from the town’s silent, reclusive elder.

"Exactly," Greg said, his voice level. "The boys will officially form as the Town Guard of Twin Rivers. We’ll register it with the District Governor’s office. I’ve already prepared the scrolls. All it needs is the signature of the Town Elder and your stamp as the Master Smith."

"Why, Greg? Why go through the bother of the paperwork? We’ve got the spears. We’ve got the shields. If they come, we fight."

"Because of The King’s Law," Greg said, a cold, clinical light in his eyes. 

Silas frowned. "The King’s Law? You mean the old edicts from the Capital?"

"Paragraph Four, Section Twelve," Greg recited, his memory of the Imperial Code as sharp as a sword. "No recruiter or local mobilization officer may recruit from an established, chartered Town Guard. Guards are considered essential local infrastructure for the defense of the King’s peace against internal threats—meaning monsters, bandits, and orcs. They can ask for volunteers from a Guard, but they may not conscript them."

A rare, wide smile appeared on Silas’s face—a grin that made him look like a hungry wolf. "Oh, really! Now that’s interesting! You’re saying that because we call them 'guards' instead of 'sons,' the Empire can't touch them?"

"As long as we can prove they are necessary for the 'security' of the valley," Greg said. "And I think we’ve already laid the groundwork for that. Between the 'Goblin ambush' in the Blackthorn and the 'Bear' in the mine, Twin Rivers has a documented history of being under siege."

***

Two weeks later, the frost finally broke. The Silverun Lake groaned as the ice shattered, and the muddy roads leading into the valley became navigable for the first time in months.

The recruiters arrived with the first thaw. 

Captain Kael was at the lead, his blue-and-gold surcoat slightly tarnished from the winter’s damp, followed by a larger escort than before—thirty heavy infantry and a pair of mounted scouts. Kael was expecting more of the same "pathetic" misery he had seen in the autumn. He was expecting weeping mothers and crippled boys.

Instead, as the imperial party rounded the final bend into the valley, they pulled their horses to a sudden, stumbling halt. 

Twin Rivers didn't look like a village anymore. It looked like a frontier outpost. 

The makers of a wall—a sturdy timber palisade reinforced with stone from the mine—were visible around the main gate. Atop the gatehouse, two men stood with longbows, their eyes fixed on the approaching soldiers. They weren't "archers" in the imperial sense, but Twin Rivers hunters who could hit a rabbit's eye at fifty yards.

But the most impressive sight was in the communal square. 

Silas was "drilling" fifteen of the young men. They weren't wearing armor—that would be too provocative—but they were equipped with their iron-ringed shields and steel-tipped spears. They moved with a terrifying, rhythmic precision. 

*Stomp. Thrust. Reset.*

The sound of fifteen boots hitting the mud in perfect synchronization echoed off the valley walls like a heartbeat. The shields stayed perfectly level, forming a wall of oak and iron that caught the morning light. 

"I thought you told me there were a bunch of scrawny teens here!" Captain Kael whispered harshly to one of his aides, his face pale. 

The aide looked at his scrolls, then at the muscular, disciplined line in the square. "The report said... well, it said they were broken, sir. Goblins, apparently."

Silas called a halt to the training. "Set!" he roared. 

The fifteen men dropped their spears into the "Receive Charge" position and locked their shields. It was a flawless execution. They looked at the imperial soldiers not with fear, but with a cold, professional curiosity. 

"Remember!" Silas bellowed, his voice carrying clearly to the recruiters at the gate. "This town is your home! If the Goblins and Orcs attack tonight, you hold the line! No one breaches! No one retreats!"

Silas then turned, walking slowly and deliberately toward the gate. He wiped his hands on his blackened apron, looking every bit the weary town protector. 

Captain Kael rode forward, his hand hovering near the hilt of his sword. "What is the meaning of this? Why is this town under arms?"

Silas looked up at Kael, his expression one of grim concern. "Ah, what brings you to Twin Rivers, Captain? Have you sent us reinforcements? Our scouts report a massive gathering of Kragh-shadows in the high peaks, and the Green-skin tribes have been raiding the lower pastures since the ice melted. We’ve had to form a Town Guard just to keep the livestock alive."

Kael looked at the "guards" in the square. They were standing at ease now, but their spears were still clutched in hands that knew how to use them.

"Town Guard?" Kael spat. "You don't have a charter for a Town Guard."

Silas reached into his tunic and pulled out a rolled, heavily sealed parchment. "Actually, Captain, the Elder received the confirmation from the District Governor’s office three days ago. The Twin Rivers Guard is officially chartered for the defense of the King’s Border against the 'Barbaric Incursion'."

Kael snatched the scroll, his eyes scanning the elegant, bureaucratic script. His jaw tightened as he reached the section on conscription immunity. 

"This is a trick," Kael hissed, looking toward the forge where a thin column of black smoke rose into the sky. "You can't hide twenty men behind a piece of paper."

"It’s not just paper, Captain," Silas said, gesturing to the men. "It’s the King’s Law. We are his subjects, and we are doing his work—protecting his valley. We would love to send you men, truly, but if we lose even five of these 'guards,' the Goblins will be down the ridge and into the imperial granaries before the moon turns."

Kael looked at the fifteen men. He looked at the wall. He saw the sheer, disciplined strength of a village that had decided it wasn't going to be "taken." He knew that if he tried to force a conscription now, it wouldn't be a recruitment; it would be a battle. And thirty imperial infantry against fifteen trained spearmen behind a gate... those were odds Kael didn't like.

"We’ll see what the Governor says about this," Kael growled, tossing the scroll back to Silas. "There are other ways to pay your 'blood tax,' Smith."

"We look forward to his visit," Silas said, bowing low. "In the meantime, perhaps you’d like to purchase some of our surplus iron? We’ve got a mine running again. The King’s Law says we have to keep the local economy 'industrious,' after all."

Kael didn't answer. He turned his horse and signaled his men to retreat. The imperial party rode out of Twin Rivers much faster than they had ridden in.

Greg watched from the small, high window of the forge. He saw the blue surcoats disappear over the ridge. He saw Tyler and the Miller twins break their formation, their faces splitting into grins as they realized they were still home. 

He stepped away from the window and returned to the anvil. He had won the first battle of the spring. The King’s Law had held. But Greg knew that "law" was only as strong as the steel that backed it. 

The Dragon in his core was quiet, but it was awake. Greg picked up his hammer and struck the iron once, a single, ringing note that announced to the world that Twin Rivers was no longer just a small town. 

It was a fortress. And the Smith was its heart.
