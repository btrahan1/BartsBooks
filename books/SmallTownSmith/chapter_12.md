# Chapter 12: The Goblin Camp

The spring air in Twin Rivers was usually sweet with the scent of blooming apple trees and damp earth, but this morning, it carried the sharp, acrid tang of distant smoke.

Clara hurried toward the forge, her cloak fluttering behind her. She had just come from the *Drunken Trout*, where the atmosphere was thick with more than just ale-fumes. A messenger from the Blackthorn border had arrived at dawn, and his news had set the town on edge.

As she rounded the corner, she saw Silas and Greg—the man she had already started calling her "future husband" in the quiet, guarded reaches of her own mind—standing by the cooling trough. 

"How many did you say?" Clara asked, her voice slightly breathless as she reached them.

Silas looked at her, his expression grim. "About fifty, according to the rider. They’ve been raiding the outlying farms near the Red Creek. Burnt two barns and took a dozen head of cattle. The District Governor has put out a bounty—ten silver crowns for every head brought to the crossroads."

Greg, who had been focused on quenching a red-hot iron rim, slowly looked up. 

A smile spread across his face—not the gentle, tired smile he sometimes shared with Clara, but "that smile." It was the expression of a predator that had finally seen its prey emerge from the brush. It was a cold, sharp, and utterly terrifying look that sent shivers down Clara’s spine, despite her affection for him.

"Perfect!" Greg said. 

Silas blinked. "Perfect? Greg, fifty goblins are more than a nuisance. They’re a slaughtering party. If they turn south, they’ll hit Twin Rivers before the week is out."

"Exactly," Greg said, his voice humming with a dangerous excitement. "Kael and the recruiters just left. They’re looking for any excuse to invalidate our Town Guard charter. If we sit here and wait for the Empire to 'protect' us, we prove we’re just subjects. But if the Twin Rivers Guard marches out and clears a raider camp... we prove we are a necessity. We legitimate the King’s Law with goblin blood."

He looked at the row of iron-ringed shields leaning against the wall. 

"Besides," Greg added, "the boys need to see what happens when the ‘sound of steel’ meets something that fights back. Twenty-two spearmen against fifty goblins in the open... it’s the training ground they need before the Empire sends something more competent than Kael."

***

An hour later, the square of Twin Rivers was a scene of disciplined activity. 

Greg didn't have to shout. He simply had Silas ring the "Anvil Bell"—a heavy, resonant toll that signaled a Grade One mobilization. The twenty-two men of the Guard appeared as if they had been waiting for the signal. They came from the fields, from the wood-shop, and from their homes, already carrying their spears and shields.

They gathered in the square, their new boots crunching on the spring mud. They didn't look like the terrified boys who had hidden in the Blackthorn woods. They looked like a wall of iron and oak. 

"Listen to me!" Greg commanded, stepping onto the stone steps of the communal barn. 

The silence was absolute. Even the birds in the apple trees seemed to stop singing.

"There is a camp of fifty raiders at Red Creek," Greg said. "They are not soldiers. They are vermin. They fight with jagged knives and poison, and they rely on fear. They expect you to run. They expect you to be the same 'pathetic' village the world thinks you are."

He stepped down, walking through their ranks, his eyes meeting theirs. 

"Today, we prove them wrong. We march in three groups. Tyler, you take the left flank. Marcus, you take the right. I will lead the center. We do not break the line. We do not chase glory. You stay behind the shields, you trust the man next to you, and you let the spears do the work."

"Will we be home by dark, Mr. Greg?" one of the younger boys—little Petey, who had just turned fifteen—asked, his voice trembling slightly.

Greg looked at him. He saw the fear, but he also saw the sturdy weight of the iron-ringed shield Petey was holding. 

"If you hold the line, Petey," Greg said softly, "The march to Red Creek was a five-mile trek through the thickening brush of the valley. 

Greg stayed at the head of the center column, his own spear held at the ready. He watched his men—no, his students. They were silent, but he could smell the sharp, metallic tang of their sweat. It wasn't the sweat of work; it was the sweat of anticipation. It was a volatile thing.

They reached the ridge overlooking Red Creek by Midday. Below, the goblin camp was a festering sore of mud and stolen hides. 

"They don't see us," Tyler whispered. His knuckles were white on his spear-shaft, and his breath was coming in short, jagged hitches. 

"They will soon," Greg said. "Remember. The line is your life."

Greg led the descent. But as they hit the level ground, the "perfect" plan began to fray at the edges. A goblin scout, hidden in a hollow log, let out a screech that sounded like tearing metal. 

The camp didn't just panic; it erupted in a desperate, frenzied counter-charge. Fifty goblins didn't run away; they swarmed. 

"SET!" Greg roared.

Most of the boys locked shields, but the suddenness of the charge was overwhelming. Little Petey, on the right flank, fumbled his brace. A wiry goblin with a jagged bone-knife slid under the gap in his shield, slashing at Petey’s shin.

Petey cried out, his shield dipping. "They're under! They're under!"

The right flank began to buckle. The coordination Greg had drilled into them started to dissolve into the raw, lizard-brain instinct of flight. The Miller twins wavered, their spears poking aimlessly at the air as two more goblins threw themselves against their oak barriers.

"Hold the line!" Greg shouted, but his voice was drowned out by the screeching of the raiders.

Suddenly, a voice cut through the chaos—high, cracking with adolescence, but carrying a surprising weight of authority.

"PETEY, CLOSE THE GAP! MARCUS, OVERLAY!" 

It was Tyler. The boy who had once been a "minnow" had stepped out of the center rank. He didn't look at Greg for orders. He saw the disaster forming on the right and moved. He jammed his own shield into the space Petey had left, his spear-point taking a goblin in the chest before the creature could finish Petey off.

"RE-LINK!" Tyler bellowed, his face red with effort. "SQUARE THE LINE! DON'T YOU DARE RUN!"

The boys, hearing Tyler’s familiar voice steadying them, did what Greg’s barked commands couldn't do in the heat of the moment. They remembered their training because one of their own was doing it. Petey, teeth gritted against the pain in his leg, shoved his shield back into place. Marcus and the others tightened their grip. 

The wall of Twin Rivers solidified. 

*Clack-hiss.*

The spears began to find their rhythm. It wasn't the "harvest" Greg had envisioned—it was a messy, desperate struggle. A goblin managed to climb atop the shield-wall, its claws digging into Marcus’s shoulder before Greg could swat it down with the butt of his spear. 

But the line held. Tyler kept the cadence, his voice a constant drumbeat of "THRUST! RESET! STEP!"

Greg watched Tyler with a quiet, surprised pride. He stepped back from the main line, only intervening when a goblin got too close to a wavering flank. He saw Tyler directing, saw him pulling the Miller twins back into formation when they got too greedy for a kill. 

By the time the last goblin had been driven into the creek, the camp was a slaughterhouse. 

The silence that followed was broken by the sound of heavy, panicked breathing and the soft whimpering of the injured. 

Petey was sitting in the mud, his leg bleeding from a shallow but long gash. Marcus was clutching his shoulder, where the goblin’s claws had left four angry, red tracks. Two other boys were nursing bruised ribs and cut forearms. 

"Status," Greg commanded, walking through the ranks.

"We... we won," Tyler said, leaning heavily on his spear. He looked down at his own tunic, which was splattered with ichor. He looked like he was about to be sick, but he kept his head up.

Greg knelt beside Petey. He checked the wound—messy, but the bone was intact. He looked at the boys, many of whom were pale and shaking, looking at their first real blood.

"You took some hits," Greg said, his voice loud enough for all of them to hear. 

Marcus looked at his shoulder, his eyes wide with shock. "I'm going to have a mark. A permanent one."

Greg let out a short, dry chuckle as he began to wrap Petey’s leg with a clean strip of linen. He looked at the injured boys, a twinkle in his eye that cut through the grim aftermath.

"Don't look so miserable, Marcus," Greg said. "Consider it a gift."

"A gift?" Marcus croaked.

"Back in the Capital, they say the girls love a man with a few scars," Greg said, winking at Tyler. "It proves you were standing somewhere important when things went wrong. A smooth-skinned man is a man who stayed in the cellar. You? You’re a man with a story."

Petey let out a weak, shaky laugh. "You... you think Clara will like it?"

"I think Clara will be too busy making sure you don't get an infection," Greg said, standing up. "But the rest of the village? They’ll see the Guard. And they’ll see that you didn't run."

The mood shifted instantly. The pale faces regained a bit of color. The boys looked at their shallow wounds not as injuries, but as trophies. Even Tyler managed a small, exhausted smile. 

"Take the heads," Greg ordered, though this time he looked at Tyler. "You're the Lead, Tyler. You direct the cleanup. We march back in twenty minutes."

Tyler nodded, his chest swelling just a fraction. "Yes, Mr. Greg."

As they began the grim work of the bounty-collect, Greg watched them. They had bled. They had panicked. And then, they had listened to each other. They were no longer just trainees. 

They were a unit that had survived the fire. 

And as Greg looked at Tyler, he realized that he hadn't just forged a Guard. He had forged a successor. 

"Perfect," Greg whispered.

As they began the grim work of the bounty-collection, Greg felt the dark weight in his chest lift slightly. They had survived their first real test. They weren't just "guards" on paper anymore. They were warriors. 

But as he looked north, toward the high peaks where the Empire’s shadows still lingered, Greg knew that the goblins were just the beginning. Next time, the enemy wouldn't be screeching vermin. 

But as he looked at the proud, soot-and-blood-stained faces of his young men, Greg smiled once more. The Shield of Twin Rivers had finally been tested, and it had held.
