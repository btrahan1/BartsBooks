# Chapter 13: The Bounty

The city of Oakhaven was the administrative heart of the district, a place of stone walls, paved streets, and a bureaucracy that moved with the speed of a half-frozen river. It was also the closest seat of the Municipal Court—and the place where bounties were paid in imperial coin.

Silas didn't ride into Oakhaven alone. He brought ten of the Guard with him, including Tyler and the Miller twins. They weren't wearing the blood-stained tunics from Red Creek; they had scrubbed their gear, polished their iron-ringed shields, and marched in a tight, three-man column that turned heads from the moment they passed through the city gates. 

They didn't look like farmers. They looked like professional infantry.

The Municipal Court office was a drafty building of gray granite, filled with the scent of old parchment and the rhythmic scratching of quills. A lone Clerk sat behind a high wooden counter, his spectacles perched on a nose that had clearly seen more ledgers than sunlight. He looked up as the heavy oak doors swung open and Silas led the Guard inside.

*Thud.*

Silas placed a burlap sack on the counter. He didn't say a word. He simply untied the cord and reached inside, pulling out the head of the goblin chieftain. The creature’s amber eyes were dull, its gray skin mummified by the salt Silas had used for the journey.

The Clerk stared. His quill hovered over a ledger, a droplet of ink falling to stain the paper. 

"That’s the goblin chieftain's head," Silas said, his voice a low, gravelly rumble that filled the quiet room. 

The Clerk swallowed hard, his Adam's apple bobbing. "I... see. And the raiding party?"

Silas gestured to the ten guards standing at attention behind him. They didn't lean against the walls. They didn't gossip. They stood with their shields braced and their expressions neutral. On a signal from Tyler, they stepped forward, each placing a smaller sack on the counter.

"Five more heads in each sack," Silas said, nonchalantly. "For a total of fifty-one."

"Fif... fifty..." the Clerk stuttered, his eyes darting from the chieftain’s severed head to the mountain of burlap sacks. "You mean to tell me a village smith and a handful of boys cleared the whole Red Creek nest?"

"Fifty-one," Silas corrected, his eyes narrowing. "I believe that comes to five gold and ten silver? Ten silver per head, according to the Governor’s proclamation."

"Uhm... I... I have to check with the Quartermaster," the Clerk said, scrambling off his stool. "Please... wait here."

Silas didn't mind waiting. He leaned his massive weight against the counter and signaled for the Guard to stand at ease. Tyler and Marcus didn't break their formation, but they allowed themselves a small, private exchange of glances. They were in the heart of the district, and for the first time, they felt like they belonged there.

Ten minutes later, a man dapper in a silk vest and a sour expression emerged from the back offices. This was the Quartermaster, a man whose job was to ensure the Empire paid as little as possible for its peace. He poked at one of the sacks with a silver-tipped cane.

"Common goblins," the Quartermaster mused, his voice thin and patronizing. "Most of these are runts. Not worth the full ten silver. And the chieftain... look at this iron breastplate. It’s imperial scrap. You probably just found a scavenger party."

Silas didn't blink. He reached out and grabbed the Quartermaster’s cane, his hand closing over the silver tip with a strength that made the smaller man flinch.

"Scavengers don't burn barns, and runts don't kill cattle," Silas said, his voice dropping to that dangerous, vibrating frequency Greg had taught him. "Ten silver per head. Fifty-one heads. The proclamation wasn't a suggestion, Quartermaster. It was an offer. We’ve fulfilled our side. Now fill yours."

The Quartermaster looked at Silas. Then he looked at the ten young men standing behind him—twenty hands gripped on spear-shafts, twenty eyes watching him with a cold, unnerving discipline. He realized that this wasn't a group of peasants he could haggle with. These men knew the value of their blood.

"Fine," the Quartermaster hissed, tugging his cane back. "Fifty for the heads, plus the chieftain bonus. Five gold, ten silver."

He went back into the office and returned with a heavy leather purse. Silas took it, the weight of the coin striking a resonant *clink* against the counter. He counted it out—fifty-one silver pieces and five heavy, gold crowns marked with the Emperor’s lion.

Then, Silas did something that made the Clerk and the Quartermaster freeze.

He picked up one of the gold crowns and placed it back on the counter.

"Keep this," Silas said. 

The Quartermaster blinked. "A bribe? Smith, we do not—"

"A pre-payment," Silas interrupted. "Twin Rivers is a loyal town. We know the Empire has been under strain with the wars in Kragh. Consider this a sign of support for the Crown—a payment toward any 'Blood Taxes' or unexpected levies the Governor might see fit to impose on our valley in the coming year. We pay our debts, Quartermaster. Even the ones we haven't incurred yet."

The Quartermaster stared at the gold coin. He knew exactly what Silas was doing. He was buying a "good reputation" for Twin Rivers. A town that proactively paid its taxes in goblin heads *and* gold was a town a Governor would hesitate to harass. It was a strategic bribe, delivered with the mask of patriotism.

"I... I will see that it is noted in the District Ledger," the Quartermaster said, his tone shifting to one of grudging respect. 

"See that it is," Silas said. 

He signaled to the Guard. "We’re done here. Let's go shopping."

***

With four gold and ten silver still in the purse, the group headed for the city’s industrial district. Greg had given Silas a very specific list. It wasn't a list for luxury. It was a list for war.

They went to the leather-worker’s guild first.

"Twenty-three sets of cured bull-hide," Silas told the startled guild-master. "Tanned for hardness, but supple enough for movement. And I need copper rivets. A lot of them."

Next, they went to the textile merchant. 

"Heavy linen," Silas ordered. "And wool batting. We need three layers for every man. The kind of padding an arrow won't bite through."

Finally, they went to the ironmonger. They didn't buy finished weapons; that would draw too much attention. Instead, Silas bought high-quality bellows-spare-parts, a crate of hardened steel pins, and a specific type of thin, flexible iron strapping that could be used for rim-work.

The guards carried the crates and rolls of hide with a quiet pride. They weren't just bringing home stories of the battle; they were bringing home the materials for their transformation. 

As they marched back toward the city gates, laden with their spoils, Tyler walked alongside Silas.

"Greg said the gambesons were the most important," Tyler noted, looking at the mountain of linen. "Why? I thought we wanted breastplates like the knights."

"Armor is about layers, Tyler," Silas said, quoting Greg’s own words. "A breastplate is fancy, but it’s brittle. A good gambeson—the padded linen Greg’s designing—will turn a blade better than cheap iron. And more importantly, you can wear it under your shirt. It makes you a 'smith' to the world, but a 'fortress' to a knife."

They arrived back at Twin Rivers three days later. The whole town was waiting for them at the gate. When the people saw the four gold coins (minus the costs of the materials) and the crates of war-gear, a cheer went up that shook the apple blossoms from the trees.

Greg was waiting at the forge. He didn't join the celebration in the square. He was already prepping the tables for the "Shield-Work."

Silas walked into the shop, dropping the heavy purse of remaining silver and the list of goods on the table. "One gold left with the Quartermaster as a 'sign of loyalty.' The rest... we got exactly what you asked for."

Greg picked up a piece of the new bull-hide. He felt the texture, the density. "Good. The ‘Price of Peace’ just went up, Silas."

"The boys felt it, Greg," Silas said, leaning against the anvil. "In the city... people looked at them. They didn't see scrawny teens. They saw a Guard. They’re ready for the next layer."

"Then we’ll give it to them," Greg said. 

He looked at the remaining 2 gold crowns, 52 silver. This was the fuel for the next six months. He would teach them to sew the gambesons, to rivet the leather, and to reinforce the shields. They would become an army in everything but name, hidden in plain sight as a "Town Guard."

"Spring is fully here," Silas noted. "The Governor will be sending an inspector soon. Not just a recruiter like Kael. A man with a ledger and a sharp eye."

"Let him come," Greg said, his hammer rising for the first strike of the day. "The King’s Law is on our side. And soon, so will be the steel."

As the Sound of Steel returned to the forge, louder and more confident than ever, Greg felt the dragon in his core pulse with a strange, quiet satisfaction. He wasn't conquering an Empire. He was saving a village. 

And for the first time, the "Blood Tax" didn't feel like a debt. It felt like a foundation.
