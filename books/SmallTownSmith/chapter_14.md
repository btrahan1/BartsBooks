# Chapter 14: Learning to Breathe

The morning mist was clinging to the surface of Silverun Lake like a protective velvet layer as the twenty-two men of the Twin Rivers Guard gathered at the old dock. 

They had spent the last week in a frenzy of creation. Under Greg’s meticulous (and often exhausting) direction, they had sewn their three-layer linen gambesons, reinforced their bull-hide bracers, and finished the iron-strapping on their oak shields. They arrived this morning with their new gear slung over their shoulders, their faces flushed with the pride of ownership. They were ready for another drill, another simulated battle, another chance to prove they were the "Shield of the Valley."

Greg was already there. He wasn't wearing his smith's apron. He wasn't even wearing his tunic. He was standing on the mossy rock near the shallows, stripped down to nothing but his linen undergarments and bare feet. 

The boys skidded to a halt, their gear clattering. 

"Strip," Greg ordered. "Down to your undergarments. Bare feet. Leave the gear on the dock."

"Strip?" Marcus Miller asked, shivering as the cold mountain air hit him. "Greg, we just spent a week making the armor. Aren't we going to put it to use?"

"The armor is for your skin," Greg replied, his voice a steady, grounding force. "Today, we work on the man inside the skin. Strip. Now."

One by one, the twenty-two men complied. It was a visual testament to the last six months of "Humble Beginnings." They were no longer the scrawny, soft-bellied boys of the summer. Their bodies were lean, their muscles corded and dense from a winter of mining, swimming, and forging. They stood in the morning light like a group of unfinished statues—strong, but raw.

Greg, however, was in a different category entirely. Without the soot of the forge or the layers of common clothing, his body was a map of a decade of high-level martial cultivation. He didn't have the bulging, useless muscles of a tavern-brawler. His physique was a masterpiece of "Battle Ki" integration—shoulders that looked like they could carry the sky, a chest like an anvil, and legs that seemed rooted into the very earth. And covering it all were the scars: thin white lines from Kragh blades, jagged marks from splintered shields, and a long, faded track along his ribs that spoke of a spear-thrust that should have taken his life.

Up on the ridge, hidden behind the trunk of the Great Oak, Clara was definitely NOT looking with her viewing lens. 

She had told herself she was checking the weather-patterns for the spring planting. Or looking for the first eagle of the season. She had absolutely no interest in seeing twenty-three men standing in the shallows of the lake in their undergarments. 

And yet, her lens was fixed squarely on the rock where Greg stood. Her face was a shade of crimson that rivaled the dawn. She traced the lines of his shoulders through the glass, her heart hammering against her ribs so loudly she was sure he could hear it. He was... magnificent. And terrifying. The scars told a story of a world she couldn't imagine, a world of violence that he had somehow survived to become the quiet smith of her village.

"Today, we learn to breathe," Greg announced, his voice carrying across the water.

"Breathe?" Tyler asked, his teeth beginning to chatter. "Mr. Greg, we've been breathing since we were born. I think we’ve got that one down."

"You’ve been surviving," Greg corrected. "Most people breathe to stay alive. A warrior breathes to stay in control. You breathe from your chest when you’re afraid. You breathe from your throat when you’re angry. But to find your Ki... you must learn to breathe from your core."

He gestured for them to sit, cross-legged, on the damp sand of the shore. 

"Nature is all around us," Greg said, sitting down himself. He didn't seem to feel the cold. "The lake, the mountains, the wind. But the most important piece of nature is the one sitting where you are. Your body is a vessel. Training, gear, and guts will make you true men. But I aim to make you more than that."

The boys sat, looking at him with a mix of awe and skepticism. 

"What’s Ki, really?" Petey asked, his eyes wide. "The stories say it makes you fly. Say it makes you a god."

"The stories are lies told by men who want to be feared," Greg said. "Ki is just energy. It’s the breath of the world, focused through a single point. It doesn't make you a god. It makes you... more of what you already are. If you are a coward, Ki will make you a faster coward. If you are a hero, it will make you a dead hero. But if you are a protector... it will give you the stamina to stand when others fall."

He closed his eyes. "Follow my count. In for four. Hold for four. Out for eight. Focus on the space two inches below your navel. That is your core. Your furnace. Imagine a single ember, cold and gray, waiting for a draft."

The next hour was a lesson in silent torment. 

For the boys, "breathing" proved to be harder than carrying stones across the bottom of the lake. Their minds raced. Their bodies shivered. They wanted to talk, to scratch an itch, to ask when they could put their tunics back on. 

But Greg was an immovable ghost. He sat with his spine perfectly straight, his breath so slow and deep that it barely disturbed the air around him. 

"Don't fight the shiver," Greg’s voice drifted through their concentration. "Let the cold enter you. It is just another sensation. Find the core."

Tyler was the first to feel it. 

He had been struggling to keep his mind from drifting to the cold sand beneath his seat, but as he slowed his breath to the eight-second exhale Greg demanded, he felt a sudden, sharp flicker of heat in his gut. It wasn't the heat of the sun. It was internal. It felt like a tiny, golden spark had just caught on a piece of dry tinder.

Tyler’s eyes snapped open. He looked at Greg, his expression one of pure shock. "Mr. Greg... I... I felt something. It was hot. Right here."

"Close your eyes," Greg commanded, though his voice was softer now. "Don't chase it. If you chase it, it will vanish. Just feed it the breath. Slow. Steady."

One by one, others began to find it. Marcus and his brother. Petey. A sense of "rightness" began to settle over the group. The shivering eased. Not because the air had warmed, but because they were beginning to generate their own internal fire. 

Greg watched them through slitted eyes. He felt the ripples in the air as their dormant cores began to stir. It was a humble beginning—mere sparks compared to the raging sun of an Imperial Master’s Ki—but it was more than they had been yesterday. 

*I shouldn't be doing this,* a voice in Greg’s head whispered. *The Emperor’s Law explicitly forbids the teaching of Ki-circulation to commoners. It’s the one thing they fear—an armed populace that can think and breathe as one.*

But Greg looked at his twenty-two "students," their faces serene in the morning light, and he knew he’d make the same choice a thousand times. He wasn't training them to be an army of conquest. He was training them to be a unit that could stand against the elite "Battle-Ki" users the Empire would inevitably send when the "Town Guard" deception no longer held. 

"A man with a shield and spear is a soldier," Greg said, standing up as the sun finally broke over the ridge. "A man with a shield, a spear, and a core... is a guardian. Today, you found the ember. Tomorrow, we start to build the forge."

Through her lens, Clara saw Greg stand. She saw the way the sunlight caught the white lines of his scars, the way he looked at his men with a terrifying, protective pride. She saw him as something ancient and powerful, a guardian who had finally found something worth guarding. 

She slowly retracted the lens and tucked it into her pack, her hands still trembling. She had come to the lake to "watch a smith." But as she walked back toward the village, she realized that she was watching the birth of something else entirely. 

Greg wasn't just building a Guard. He was building a legend. 

And as the men of Twin Rivers stood up, their bodies warm despite the freezing dew, they looked at their hands as if they were seeing them for the first time. 

The "Real" training had just reached the soul. 

"Mr. Greg?" Tyler asked as they began to gather their gear. "Will we be able to break stones like you did to the bear?"

Greg looked at his apprentice, a small, genuine smile finally reaching his eyes. "Let's focus on standing still for five minutes first, Tyler. The stones can wait."

As they marched back to the village, their new gambesons for once felt like more than just linen and wool. They felt like outer shells for the fire they had just found inside. 

The Price of Peace was indeed going up. And for the first time, the boys were eager to pay it.
