# Chapter 18: It’s not a Farm, it’s Training

Clara was over the moon. 

For the last week, the gossip in Twin Rivers had reached a fever pitch. The "Smith’s Apprentice" and the "Smith’s Sister" were no longer just a possibility; they were an official fact. Clara walked through the village with a predatory grace, her head held high and the steel spear-shaft Greg had given her leaning against her door at night like a silent sentinel. 

Even Greg seemed different. The "not-disciples" noticed it first. He was smiling more—not the grim, predatory smile of the training grounds, but a softer, lighter expression that felt like the sun peeking through a gap in a storm. 

One afternoon, Greg had found Clara at the well and simply asked, "Walk with me?"

They had been walking for nearly an hour, heading north toward a secluded valley hidden behind the ridge of the Old Crow Mine. It was a place Clara knew—or thought she knew—a stretch of flat land that offered a stunning view of the Silverun Lake below. 

As they neared the ridge, Clara heard it. 

*Thump. Thump. Crack.*

It was the sound of construction. Not the rhythmic ringing of the forge, but the heavy, deliberate noise of many men working with timber and stone. 

"What in the world is Silas up to now?" Clara asked, narrowing her eyes at Greg. "I thought we were done with the palisade at the gate."

Greg just smiled. It was a mixture of amusement, a bit of an evil glint, and a healthy dose of guilt. He didn't answer. He just gestured for her to continue up the final slope of the hill. 

When they crested the ridge and looked down into the hidden valley, Clara caught her breath. 

Below them, on a beautiful overlook, she didn't see an obstacle course or a military barracks. She saw the foundations of a substantial, elegant timber-frame house taking shape. Twenty-one men—the "worker bees"—were dressed in their grey training-smocks, but they weren't drilling with spears. They were hauling massive oak beams on their shoulders, moving with the same synchronized, rhythmic trot Greg had taught them in the lake. Others were carefully laying stone for a wide, sturdy hearth, their movements precise and coordinated.

In the center of the site stood Tyler. He was the "Queen Bee," standing atop a partially finished stone wall with a set of blueprints in his hands. He wasn't barking orders; he was directing the flow of labor with a focused intensity.

"Greg," Clara whispered, her eyes wide as she took in the sight. "What... what is this? This isn't the village square. And those aren't public buildings."

Greg tried to keep a straight face, his eyes fixed on the house that was clearly designed for more than just a smith’s apprentice. It had wide windows facing the lake and a footprint that spoke of a home meant for a family. 

"It's not a Farm, it's Training!" Greg said, his voice brimming with a mixture of amusement and guilt.

"Training?" Clara turned to him, her mouth agape. "Greg, they're building a house! I can see where the kitchen is going to be! Since when does a spear-thrust help with stone-masonry?"

Greg chuckled, leaning his weight against a gnarled pine. "It’s the ultimate training, Clara. Heavy lifting for the core. Precision in the joinery for the hands. Coordination in moving the master-beams for the unit. And most importantly... it’s a lesson in what we’re actually protecting."

He looked at Tyler, who had just signaled for the center beam to be raised. The twenty-one men moved as one, their breathing synchronized—the "core-breath" Greg had taught them—as they hoisted the massive weight into place. 

"The boys came to me three days after the bounty was paid," Greg admitted, scratching the back of his neck. "Tyler said the Guard wanted to provide a 'proper residence' for their Teacher and his Lady. I told them it was a distraction. I told them we had a wall to build and an Empire to watch."

"And?" 

"And Tyler told me that if they could build a house that would stand for a hundred years, an Imperial recruiter was nothing by comparison. So, I made a deal. I’d allow it, provided every hour of work was treated with the same discipline as a drill. Hence... training."

Clara looked at the house. She saw the care in the stone-work, the way Petey was hand-sanding a beam for the porch, and the way Marcus was ensuring the hearth was perfectly level. It wasn't just a building; it was a gift. It was twenty-two young men saying thank you to the man who had given them a chance to stay home.

"They're building our home, Greg," she whispered, her eyes glistening. 

"They're building a fortress of a different kind," Greg replied, his voice softening as he looked at her. "A place where the smith can be a man. And where his wife can watch the stars without a viewing lens."

Clara laughed, a bright, clear sound that made several of the men below look up. "You're a menace, Greg. Using your 'not-disciples' as a free labor force for our domestic bliss."

"Think of it as an engineering challenge for them," Greg teased. "By the time they're done with the roof-thatching, their grip-strength will be enough to snap a spear-shaft."

Tyler looked up from the blueprints and saw them on the ridge. He raised a hand in a silent salute, before returning to his "Queen Bee" duties, directing the next team to the stone-quarry. 

"You've given them a world, Greg," Clara said, leaning her head against his shoulder. "And in return, they're giving us a home. I think... I think I prefer this to a farm."

"Just don't tell Silas," Greg whispered. "He still thinks they're building an annex for the forge."

As they walked down the ridge toward the construction site, the men of the Guard cheered—a boisterous, happy sound that had nothing to do with war. They were building something that wouldn't be used for killing. They were building a legacy of peace.

And as Greg looked at the rising timber and the proud, sweating faces of his young men, he realized that he hadn't just forged a Guard. He had forged a family. 

The Shield of Twin Rivers had found its hearth. It wasn't just a wall. It was a way of life.
