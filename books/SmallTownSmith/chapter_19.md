# Chapter 19: The Inspector

The new house on the ridge was not quite finished, but it was already a home. 

The scent of fresh pine resin and linseed oil was the background to their lives now, a sharp, clean smell that matched the clarity Greg felt in his own heart. He and Clara were sitting on the wide porch—Greg hand-sanding a piece of oak for a chair, and Clara mending a set of heavy wool socks—when the sound of hooves drifted up from the valley road. 

It wasn't the rhythmic clatter of a single rider. It was the heavy, disciplined cadence of an imperial escort. 

Greg’s hands slowed. He didn't have to look at the road to know who it was. The "sharp-eyed inspector" Silas had warned them about had finally arrived. 

A carriage, draped in the silver-and-black of the District Governor’s office, pulled into the small clearing in front of the house. It was flanked by ten heavy cavalry, men in polished plate-mail with lances held at a perfectly uniform angle. 

The man who stepped out of the carriage was not Captain Kael. He was slender, with silvering hair and a face that looked like it had been carved from a piece of cold flint. This was Inspector Valerius—a man whose reputation for finding "discrepancies" in local reports was legendary across the Empire. 

Valerius looked at the house, then at Greg, then at Clara. His eyes were like ice-picks, chipping away at the surface of everything they touched.

"I am looking for the man who calls himself Greg," Valerius said. His voice was a thin, whistling thing, like a winter wind through a keyhole.

Greg stood up, his massive frame casting a long shadow across the porch. He didn't bow. He didn't scrape. He just stood there, the sanding-block still in his hand. "You’ve found him."

"And I," Clara said, stepping forward before Greg could even open his mouth, "am Clara. Greg’s future wife, the Master Smith’s sister, and the person who’s about to tell you that you're trespassing on private land."

Valerius blinked. He was accustomed to terror, to stuttering excuses, and to frantic bows. He was not accustomed to a woman in a blue tunic looking at him as if he were a particularly irritating cockroach.

"Private land?" Valerius repeated, a cold sneer touching his lips. "All land is the Emperor’s land, child. I am here to inspect the 'Twin Rivers Guard' and the 'experimental ranch' that has been receiving so much attention in the District Ledgers."

"First of all," Clara said, stepping down from the porch until she was inches away from the Inspector’s chest, "I am not a child. I am a citizen of Twin Rivers who pays her taxes and manages the forge’s ledgers. Second, the 'experimental ranch' is currently undertaking a sensitive breeding cycle for mountain sheep. If your horses disturb the flock, the Governor will be hearing about the 'loss of imperial revenue' from the local Elder—with my signature on the complaint."

Valerius stared at her. He looked at the fire in her eyes, the way she didn't even blink when his guards shifted their lances. She wasn't a "delicate flower." She was a briar-bush. 

"You talk of revenue, woman," Valerius hissed. "I talk of rebellion. I have reports of men under arms. Reports of a 'Guard' that claims immunity from the King's Law."

"We don't claim immunity from the law," Clara retorted, her voice like a whip. "We claim the *protection* of it. Paragraph Four, Section Twelve of the Imperial Charter. Surely an Inspector of your... reputation... is familiar with the charter? Or did you skip the parts about local defense in your hurry to find coin?"

Valerius’s face turned a mottled red. He opened his mouth to deliver a cutting retort, but the words were drowned out by a sound that made his guards’ horses whinny in sudden, primal fear.

*Stomp-Stomp-CLACK.*

The "not-disciples" arrived. 

They didn't come from the road. They emerged from the woods in three columns of seven, with Tyler at the center. They were in full gear—gambesons, bull-hide bracers, and their iron-ringed shields. Their steel-tipped spears were held at a forty-five-degree angle, the sunlight catching the fire-hardened points.

They didn't stop at the edge of the clearing. 

Under Tyler’s silent signal—a quick, sharp movement of his hand—the twenty-one men executed a "Split-Crescent" maneuver. They moved with a synchronization that made the imperial cavalry look like amateurs. One moment they were marching; the next, they were a wall of iron and oak, surrounding the Inspector’s carriage in a perfectly spaced circle. 

"HALT!" Tyler commanded.

The sound of twenty-one boots hitting the earth simultaneously was like a thunderclap. 

The Guard stood perfectly still. They didn't breathe hard. They didn't look at the cavalry. They looked straight ahead, their shields level, their spears motionless. It was a display of discipline that only came from months of "breathing" and "running" and "training."

Valerius looked around his carriage. He saw the "scrawny teens" Kael had reported. But he didn't see weakness. He saw a phalanx that would have made the Imperial Guard pause. He saw men who moved as a single organism. 

"This..." Valerius whispered, his arrogance finally cracking. "This is not a village guard."

"Of course it is," Clara said, her voice dripping with sweetness as she stepped back onto the porch. "We just take our 'security' very seriously in Twin Rivers. Goblins, you know. They’re such a menace."

Valerius looked at Greg. He saw the way Greg stood—the suppressed power in his shoulders, the calm, watchful eyes of a man who had seen a thousand Inspectors come and go. He realized that this wasn't a battle he could win with words, and certainly not with ten cavalry against twenty-one masters of the spear. 

The "King’s Law" was a double-edged sword. If he tried to disband them, he would be leaving an "industrious" town defenseless. If he tried to arrest them, he’d have to fight his way out of the valley. 

"I have seen enough," Valerius said, his voice tight. He turned back toward his carriage. "The Governor will have my report. Twin Rivers is... within its rights. For now."

"Wait!" 

Tyler stepped forward, his spear held in a formal salute—the vertical-hold that signaled a parley. 

The Inspector paused, his foot on the carriage step. 

"I am Tyler, Lead of the Twin River Guard," Tyler said, his voice clear and professional. He didn't look like a boy anymore; he looked like a captain. "I understand the Empire is concerned about the 'instability' of the frontier."

Valerius looked at him. "The frontier is a place of constant threat, Lead. We have more 'menaces' than we have soldiers to handle them."

Tyler smiled—a small, confident upturn of his lips that Greg had often seen on his own face. 

"Then let it be known," Tyler said, "that the Twin River Guard is available for duty. If the Governor knows of any... menaces... that the Imperial Legions are too busy to handle, we would be glad to handle them ourselves. We know these mountains better than any map-maker in the Capital."

Valerius narrowed his eyes. "You would serve the Crown?"

"For an appropriate reward, of course," Tyler added, his tone purely transactional. "A town guard has to pay its way. We protect our home first, but for the right price, we’re happy to ensure the King’s Peace remains across the whole district."

Valerius stared at Tyler. He saw the logic of it. Instead of fighting a "rebellion," the Empire could hire a specialized, local force that required no maintenance and knew the terrain. It turned a threat into an asset.

"I will convey your 'offer' to the Governor," Valerius said. 

He climbed into the carriage, the doors slamming shut with a final, hollow sound. The imperial escort turned and rode out of the clearing, their retreat far more hurried than their arrival. 

As the dust settled, the Guard remained in their formation. Tyler signaled for "At Ease."

Clara exhaled, her shoulders finally dropping. She turned to Greg, who was still holding his piece of oak. 

"Was I too much?" she asked, a mischievous glint back in her eyes. 

"You were perfect," Greg said. He stepped down and pulled her into a brief, crushing hug. "I think the Inspector will be having nightmares about 'mountain sheep' and 'disturbed revenue' for the next decade."

Tyler walked up the porch steps, his expression serious. "Did I overstep, Mr. Greg? With the offer?"

Greg looked at his apprentice. He saw the leader he had forged. He saw a man who had just secured his town’s future by making them indispensable. 

"You did exactly what was needed, Tyler," Greg said. "You just turned us into the most powerful mercenaries in the North. The Empire won't bother us now. They'll be too busy figuring out how much it’ll cost to hire us."

Greg looked toward the "experimental farm" where the beams of his new home were catching the afternoon sun. He felt the golden spark in his core, no longer a hidden secret, but a shared strength. 

He wasn't a Noble. He wasn't a General. 

He was Greg, the Smith of Twin Rivers. And as he looked at Clara and his Guard, he knew that the "Price of Peace" had finally been paid in full. 

Twin Rivers was no longer a small town in the shadow of the Empire. It was the heart of its own destiny. 

And as the Guard began their journey back to the village, their spears catching the light of a new spring, Greg went back to sanding his chair. 

He had a home to finish.
