# Chapter 20: Bandits

The ink on the official "Twin Rivers Mercenary Clause" was barely dry when the first test arrived. 

It wasn't even a week after Inspector Valerius had ridden out of the valley that a lone imperial messenger arrived at the forge. He didn't come with the pomp of an escort; he came with a sealed scroll bearing the Governor’s personal signet and a set of orders that read more like a royal quest than a municipal contract. 

Greg, Silas, and Tyler gathered in the cool shade of the forge’s back room to read the decree. Clara leaned over Greg’s shoulder, her eyes scanning the sharp, bureaucratic script.

"The Inspector certainly didn't waste any time," Silas muttered, wiping his hands on his apron. 

The terms were blunt. The Twin Rivers Town Guard was tasked with the patrol and "pacification" of the high road between Twin Rivers and Oakhaven for a period of one month. It was a stretch of territory famously plagued by the "Black-Dagger" bandits—deserters from the Kragh wars who had turned to preying on the merchant caravans that struggled through the mountain passes. 

"Twenty-five silver for every bandit captured or eliminated," Tyler read aloud, his voice low with a mix of excitement and gravity. "And a ten-percent recovery fee for any loot returned to the municipal town of Oakhaven."

"Twenty-five silver?" Marcus Miller whispered from the doorway, where he had been listening with the rest of the boys. "That’s two and a half times the goblin bounty. For one man?"

"Goblins are vermin," Greg said, his voice flat. "Bandits are intelligent threats. They have steel. They have training. They have desperation. The Governor isn't paying for your labor; he’s paying for the risk that you won't come back."

He looked at Tyler. "This is what Valerius wanted. A test. He wants to see if your 'Split-Crescent' maneuver works when the enemy isn't screeching and charging like an animal. He wants to see if you can handle men who know how to bait a trap."

Tyler squared his shoulders. "We’ve been running the mountain roads for months, Mr. Greg. We know every rock and hollow between here and the city. If there are 'Black-Daggers' on that road, we’ll find them."

"Finding them is the easy part," Greg said. "Surviving the find is where the training actually begins."

***

The patrol began the next day. 

Greg decided to split the Guard into two squads of eleven. He didn't go with them. He had a house to finish and a forge to run, and more importantly, he knew that if he were there, they would always look to him for the final command. This month was for Tyler to prove that the "Queen Bee" could lead in the field. 

Tyler took the first squad. They didn't march down the center of the road like an imperial patrol. They moved through the brush, parallel to the path, using the "shadow-stepping" Greg had taught them during their swimming lessons. They moved in silence, their equipment muffled with wool-padding, their iron-ringed shields strapped tightly to their backs.

On the third day, they reached the "Weeping Pass"—a narrow, high-walled section of the road where the Silverun dropped a thousand feet into a gorge. It was the perfect spot for an ambush. 

Tyler signaled for a halt. He didn't see anything, but he *felt* it. The golden spark in his core was buzzing—a low, discordant vibration that Greg had described as "The Dragon’s Warning."

"Ambush?" Marcus whispered, his hand white on his spear. 

"Look at the birds," Tyler signaled back. 

In the trees above the pass, the scavenger crows were silent. They weren't circling; they were waiting. 

Tyler didn't lead them into the pass. He signaled for the squad to take a "side-scramble"—a vertical ascent up the cliff face that Greg had drilled them on. Using their Ki to find grip on the raw stone, the eleven men climbed until they were overlooking the very ledge where an imperial patrol would have been trapped. 

They saw them. 

Sixteen men were crouched behind the boulders above the road, their blackened daggers and short-swords ready. They had a barrel of pitch-covered rocks rigged to drop on the first caravan that passed. They were laughing quietly, confident in their invisibility. 

"They're waiting for the merchants," Tyler whispered, his eyes narrowing. 

He didn't order a charge. He used the flags he’d developed for the "Farm." A small, blue flag for *Containment*. A red flag for *Collapse*. 

The Guard moved with a terrifying, ghost-like efficiency. They surrounded the bandit ledge in a wide circle. When every man was in position, Tyler raised his spear. 

"TWIN RIVERS! HOLD!" 

The cry echoed off the canyon walls. The bandits scrambled, their laughter turning to shock as they saw eleven armored men appearing from the "unclimbable" cliffs. 

The bandits didn't surrender. They were deserters—men who knew that the gallows-rope was their only alternative. They charged the nearest guard—little Petey—thinking he was the weak link. 

"SHIELD-LOCK!" Tyler bellowed. 

Petey didn't flinch. He dropped his spear into a "Low-Thrust" and slammed his iron-ringed shield into the first bandit’s chest. The impact sounded like a hammer hitting a drum. 

*Clack-Crunch.*

The bandit’s short-sword shattered against the oak-and-iron. Before he could recover, Petey’s spear-point took him in the shoulder. 

"RE-LINK!" 

The eleven men formed the circular "Turtle" around the bandit position. Every time a Black-Dagger tried to break the line, they hit a wall of oak and were met with the lightning-fast thrusts of Greg’s steel-tipped ash. 

It wasn't a slaughter like the goblin camp. It was a surgical, disciplined suppression. 

"Drop your steel!" Tyler commanded, his spear-point inches from the bandit leader’s throat—a man with a scarred face and an imperial legion mark on his forearm. "You’re outnumbered and outclassed. Surrender, and you’ll at least see the Governor’s trial."

The bandit leader looked at Tyler. He looked at the eleven young men who weren't even breathing hard. He saw the "glow" in their eyes—the sign of a cultivator’s focus—and he realized he wasn't fighting peasants. 

He dropped his dagger. 

"Who the hell are you lot?" the bandit spat, his hands going behind his head. 

"The Twin Rivers Guard," Tyler said, his voice steady. "And you’re about to become our first 'recovery fee.' Marcus, Petey—shackle them. We’re marching them straight to Oakhaven. I want these rats in the municipal dungeon, not anywhere near our valley."

***

When Tyler’s squad reached the city of Oakhaven two days later, they were a sight the city wouldn't soon forget. Eleven young men, dusty from the road but moving with a frighteningly calm discipline, ignored the gawking crowds as they marched fourteen bound "Black-Daggers" directly to the municipal court. 

The Clerk almost fell off his stool as the bandit leader—the man with the imperial legion mark—was shoved toward the bars of the holding cell. 

"Fourteen captures," Tyler announced to the stunned Quartermaster, who had emerged from the back office. "And three chests of recovered goods from the Weeping Pass. By my count, that’s three hundred and fifty silver for the men, plus the ten-percent recovery on the silk."

The Quartermaster looked at the silk, then at the bandits, then at Tyler. He realized that Twin Rivers hadn't just 'patrolled' the road—they had decapitated the local bandit network in less than a week. He didn't even try to haggle. He counted out the silver and the gold bonuses into three heavy purses, his hands trembling slightly as he handed them over.

"I’ll see that the Governor knows you've... cleared the pass," the Quartermaster whispered.

"See that you do," Tyler said. "We have a village to get back to. And we don't like having this kind of filth in our mountains."

***

When the news reached Greg at the forge, he was in the middle of fitting the front door to his and Clara's new house. 

Silas came running up the ridge, gasping for breath, clutching a small parchment delivered by a runner Tyler had sent ahead. "They did it, Greg! Tyler’s in Oakhaven! He’s turned in fourteen prisoners! Fourteen! The Governor’s office is in a panic—they’ve never seen a clean-up this fast, and they've already got the 'Daggers' in the city dungeon."

Greg stopped his work, a slow, deep sense of pride filling his chest. He looked at the steel spear-shaft leaning against the porch—the one he’d given Clara. 

"He’s learning," Greg said softly. 

"Learning?" Silas laughed. "Greg, he didn't even bring them back here! He marched them straight to the city to get them off our hands. He said Twin Rivers was for 'honest folk,' not bandit-trash."

Greg smiled. That was a sign of true leadership—not just the ability to fight, but the wisdom to keep the poison away from the well. 

"He’s doing more than learning, Silas. He’s protecting."

"Well, whatever he’s doing, he’s coming back with a purse so heavy he’s had to rent a pack-mule," Silas said, grinning. "The 'Price of Peace' is suddenly making us the wealthiest town in the district."

"It’s not about the money, Silas," Greg said, though he couldn't hide the satisfaction in his voice. "It’s about the reputation. The Empire doesn't mess with things it has to pay for."

Clara stepped out onto the porch, her hair windswept and her eyes bright. She looked toward the road, where the first of Tyler’s squad were finally visible, marching home with their heads held high. They were empty-handed, save for the heavy purses of silver, but their presence was more powerful than a thousand captured daggers.

"They kept it clean, Greg," she noted, leaning her head against his shoulder. "They kept the trouble in the city where it belongs."

"They did," Greg said. 

As Tyler walked up the ridge later that evening, his step was confident and his gaze was steady. He didn't look like he had been in a skirmish; he looked like he had been doing a day's work. He handed the weight of the silver to Silas, but his eyes were on his Teacher. 

"The pass is clear, Mr. Greg," Tyler said. "And the Municipal Jail is full."

Greg stepped down from the porch and met him halfway. 

"Well done, Lead," Greg said, placing a hand on Tyler’s shoulder. "Did they bait the trap?"

"They did, Mr. Greg," Tyler said, a ghost of a smile on his face. "But they found out that a 'mountain sheep' has very sharp teeth."

Greg laughed, the sound echoing across the valley. 

"And Tyler... the next lesson is on night-infiltration. I think the Black-Daggers might have a few more friends hiding in the peaks."

The hunt was far from over. But Twin Rivers was no longer just a village in the woods. It was a sanctuary that knew how to defend its borders—and how to keep its secrets in the dark. 

Greg went back to his door. He had a home to finish. And for the first time, he felt that the peace he had built wasn't just a ruse. 

It was real.
