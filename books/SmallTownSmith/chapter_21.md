# Chapter 21: The Wedding

If Twin Rivers had been a dot on a map before, today it was a star. 

The wedding of Greg the Smith and Clara of the Forge was the single largest event in the town's memory, stretching from the banks of the Silverun to the very gates of the Old Crow Mine. Greg had tried to keep it simple—a quiet vow under the Great Oak and a shared cask of ale—but Clara would have none of it. She had finally "conscripted" her man, and she intended for the entire district to know that the Smith was spoken for.

The village had been transformed. Every house was draped in garlands of primrose and white clover, and the communal square was filled with tables laden with the remaining boar-ham and the first of the spring salmon. 

But the most striking sight was the two surprise guests who pulled into the square just as the sun reached its zenith. 

Inspector Valerius and the District Governor himself—a man named Lord Alaric, whose reputation for pragmatic power-matching preceded him—stepped out of an ornate obsidian carriage. They had been invited secretly by Clara, a move that would have made a professional diplomat proud. She had wanted their marriage not just to be a union, but a statement of legitimacy.

Clara greeted them personally at the gate, her blue tunic replaced by a dress of ivory linen that shimmered with the local "Silver-thread" weave. 

"Lord Alaric, Inspector Valerius," Clara said, dipping into a perfect, practiced curtsy. "Twin Rivers is honored by your presence. Thank you for honoring my husband’s invitation."

Valerius looked at her, his flint-like eyes narrowing slightly. He knew he hadn't been invited by Greg, but he saw the strategic brilliance in Clara’s move. By attending a wedding, the Empire was officially recognizing the "Smith’s Apprentice" as a pillar of the community. 

Security for the event was handled by the Guard—all twenty-two of them. For the ceremony, they had traded their sweat-stained smocks for ceremonial tunics that the entire town had worked on for a month. They were a deep forest-green with silver embroidery, and though the boys were nearly blushing in their finery, they were "all business." They stood at the perimeter of the square, their steel-tipped spears held at a rigid, vertical attention. 

Lord Alaric walked through their ranks, his eyes tracing the line of their shoulders and the unnerving, unified focus in their gaze. He didn't see "guards." He saw an elite unit that was better drilled than his own household guard in Oakhaven.

Interestingly, the guests saw very little of the groom. 

Every time Lord Alaric or Valerius inquired about "the man Greg," the townspeople seemed to have a thousand urgent tasks for him. He was "checking the tap on the royal keg," or "fixing a loose hinge on the Elder’s chair," or "ensuring the children didn't get too close to the river." The entire village seemed to be in a quiet, coordinated conspiracy to keep Greg away from the imperial spotlight. Greg himself seemed content to stay in the background, a ghost in the celebration, his presence felt but never scrutinized.

The ceremony took place as the shadows began to lengthen. Under the Great Oak, Greg and Clara exchanged vows that were short, honest, and as unbreakable as the steel shaft Greg had given her a month prior. When the Elder pronounced them "One Hearth, One Fire," the cheer from the Guard was enough to startle the birds in the next valley.

The reception was a feast of epic proportions. But the highlight occurred when the torches were lit. 

"An honorary drill!" Tyler announced, stepping into the center of the square. 

The Guard moved. 

It wasn't a military march; it was a dance of lethal precision. Under the orange glow of the torches, the twenty-two men moved in a "Vortex-Phalanx," their shields interlocking and separating with a sound like a rhythmic thunderclap. They used their spears not just to thrust, but to pivot and parry in a synchronized display that made Lord Alaric lean forward in his chair.

As the Guard finished their drill and stood at a flawless attention, Alaric signaled for Tyler to approach his table. 

"Lead Tyler," the Governor said, his voice carrying the weight of a man accustomed to making deals. "Your 'Twin Rivers Guard' has certainly made a name for itself on the Oakhaven road. The 'Black-Daggers' are a memory, thanks to you."

"We serve the valley, My Lord," Tyler replied, his ceremonial spear held in a formal salute. 

"The Empire needs more than just valley-defense," Alaric said, his eyes fixed on the green-clad men behind Tyler. "The Black-Daggers were just a splinter. Their main camp is hidden somewhere in the Crag-Shadows. I have a new contract for you. Find the camp. Eliminate the threat. A simple ten-gold-crown fee will be paid upon the removal of their leader."

Tyler didn't blink. He knew the Crag-Shadows. It was a place of raw stone, altitude, and narrow "Kill-Zones." 

"The leader is a man named Thorne," Tyler said, his voice level. "My scouts tell me he's an ex-Sergeant of the Imperial Third. And... he’s a cultivator. A man with a core who knows how to move the fire."

Alaric raised an eyebrow. "You are well-informed, Lead."

"A cultivator requires a different kind of effort, My Lord," Tyler said, his tone turning transactional. "Fifteen gold crowns. Ten for the camp, five for the risk of a Ki-warrior."

The Governor smiled—a slow, appreciative expression. He looked at Valerius, who looked back with a grudging nod of respect. 

"A cultivator leader... and a boy who knows the value of a Ki-warrior's life," Alaric mused. "I think we can manage fifteen gold, Tyler. Provided the 'cleanup' is as efficient as the last one."

"It will be," Tyler promised.

As the Governor and his Inspector finished their drink and prepared to depart, they briefly saw Greg standing at the edge of the square, his arm around his new wife. Greg simply nodded a silent, respectful acknowledgement to the Governor, before turning back toward the shadows of his new home. 

Alaric watched them go, realizing that he had just made a pact with a village that was no longer a subject. It was a partner. 

Later that night, as the torches burned low and the music of the village faded into the quiet hum of the forest, Greg and Clara stood on the porch of their new house on the ridge. 

"They're going after the camp," Greg said softly, looking toward the north where the shadows of the Crag-Shadows were black against the stars. 

"I know," Clara replied, leaning her head against his shoulder. "And Tyler got his fifteen gold. He’s becoming quite the businessman, isn't he?"

"He's becoming a leader," Greg corrected. "But for tonight... I don't want to think about gold or contracts or bandits."

He pulled a small, silver primrose from his tunic—a flower he had kept hidden from the "Surprise Guests." He placed it in Clara’s hair, his hand lingering against her warm skin. 

"I have my home," Greg whispered. "And I have my Lady."

Clara looked up at him, her eyes bright in the moonlight. "And I have my Smith. Though I suspect I’ll be spending half my life making sure the Governor doesn't try to draft the whole village."

"Let him try," Greg said, a small, genuine laugh escaping him. "I think the 'Price of Peace' is finally working in our favor."

As they stepped across the threshold of their new home, the Shield of Twin Rivers was quiet. The Guard was at its post, the village was at its rest, and for the first time in ten years, Sir Gregory—now just Greg—didn't feel like a man who was running away. 

He felt like a man who had finally arrived.
