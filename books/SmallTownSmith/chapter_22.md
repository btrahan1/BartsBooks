# Chapter 22: The First Seed

The ridge overlooking Twin Rivers had once been a place of stone and stubborn gorse, a "desolate patch" that defied the plow for generations. But as the first full month of their marriage unfolded, the land around Greg and Clara’s new home began to undergo a transformation that was more profound than any wall or spear-wall Greg had ever built.

For Greg, the field was not a chore. It was a catharsis. 

He spent his mornings in the soil, his hands—once stained with the blood of thousands and the soot of a thousand fires—now caked in the rich, dark earth of the valley. He had traded his heavy smith’s hammer for a rhythmic, hand-forged hoe, but the way he used it was unlike any farmer the people of Twin Rivers had ever seen. 

Greg wasn't just breaking the ground. He was "tempering" it. 

As he moved through the rows, he allowed his Ki to flow down his arms, through the wooden handle, and into the very roots of the earth. He didn't use the explosive, violent energy of the "Dragon Strike." Instead, he reached for the "Verdant Pulse"—a slow, nurturing vibration that he had only ever used before to stabilize his own body during long, freezing winters in the North. 

He closed his eyes as he worked, his breath synchronized with the heartbeat of the land. He could "feel" where the soil was tired, where the rocks were choking the minerals, and where the water was pooling in stagnant pockets. He would pause, press his palm to the earth, and send a gentle, warming pulse into the ground. 

He was essentially talking to the valley. And the valley was starting to answer.

***

Clara spent her mornings on the porch, ostensibly spinning wool, but her eyes were almost always on Greg. 

She wasn't a cultivator—not yet—but she found herself becoming increasingly in tune with the world around her just by watching him. She noticed the way the birds seemed to gather in the trees near Greg’s fields, their songs more intricate and louder than anywhere else in the valley. She noticed the way the morning dew seemed to linger longer on their soil, as if the land itself were reluctant to let the moisture go. 

"You're not just tilling, are you?" she asked one afternoon, bringing a pitcher of cold cider out to the field. 

Greg stopped, wiping his brow with a forearm. He looked at the row of barley he had just finished. The sprouts were already peaking through the surface—vibrant, emerald shoots that looked twice as healthy as the crops in the communal fields below. 

"I'm listening, Clara," Greg said, his voice holding a peaceful resonance she had never heard during the war-drills. "In the Empire, they taught us that Ki is a weapon. You sharpen it, you aim it, and you use it to break things. But nature doesn't want to be broken. It wants to be fed."

He reached down and picked up a handful of dirt, letting it sift through his fingers. 

"I spent twenty years taking lives," Greg whispered, his eyes fixed on the green sprouts. "I used my soul to fuel a machine of death. Now... for the first time... I’m using it to give back. Every seed I plant feels like a debt being paid."

Clara stepped into the field, her bare feet sinking into the warm, Ki-infused soil. She felt a strange, tingling sensation—not a burn, but a gentle, steady thrumming that seemed to vibrate up from the earth. 

"I can feel it," she whispered, her eyes wide. "It’s like... the ground is humming."

Greg smiled, a genuine, quiet expression of joy. "That’s the life, Clara. It’s always been there. Most people just walk over it. But when you give it a little warmth... it remembers how to grow."

***

As the season progressed, the "Experimental Ranch" became the wonder of Twin Rivers. 

While the other farmers in the valley were struggling with a particularly erratic spring—a late frost followed by a week of heavy, drowning rains—Greg’s ridge remained a verdant island of productivity. His barley stood tall and straight, its stalks thicker than a man’s thumb. His vegetables—tubers, greens, and beans—seemed to grow in days what usually took weeks. 

The Guard noticed it, too. In the evenings, after their drills, they would often walk up the ridge to help "with the harvest," but they mostly came to just be near the field. They felt the "glow" of the place. Tyler, in particular, spent hours watching Greg work, his own golden spark pulsing in sympathy with the Verdant Pulse Greg was weaving.

"It feels different here, Mr. Greg," Tyler noted one evening as they were staking a row of climbing peas. "Down in the village, the air feels... heavy. But up here, I feel like I could run to the peaks and back and never get tired."

"That’s the resonance," Greg explained. "When you nurture the life around you, it nurtures you in return. A soldier is only as strong as his gear. A guardian... a guardian is as strong as the life he protects. Remember that when you're in the Crag-Shadows."

The growing season was more than just a source of food. It was a transformation of Greg’s identity. 

The "Sir Gregory" who had faked his death in the mud of Kragh was finally, truly gone. In his place was a man who found his greatest power not in the snap of a bone or the ring of a spear, but in the silent, miraculous unfurling of a leaf. He was unleashing his inner self, no longer as a dragon of fire, but as a shepherd of the green. 

Clara, watching him, felt her own connection to the valley deepening. She began to recognize the signs of the seasons before they happened. She knew when the rain was coming by the smell of the pine needles. She knew when the frost would strike by the way the wind whispered through the eaves of their house. She wasn't healing people yet, but she was healing herself—letting go of the fear and the uncertainty of the past year and grounding herself in the steady, rhythmic pulse of the life Greg was building.

By the time the first early harvest of greens was ready, the house on the ridge was more than just a residence. It was the heart of Twin Rivers. 

Greg brought the first basket of crisp, Ki-tempered spinach and radishes down to the village square. They were larger, sweeter, and more nutritious than anything the town had ever tasted. 

"A gift from the ridge," Greg told Silas and the Elder as he set the basket on the communal table. 

"Gift?" the Elder asked, biting into a radish that tasted like liquid sunshine. "Greg, this... this could feed the whole village through a drought! How did you do it?"

Greg just looked back toward the ridge, where the green fields were glowing in the late afternoon sun. 

"I just gave the earth something it’s been waiting for," Greg said. "A reason to grow."

As the village celebrated the "Early Harvest," Greg walked back up toward his home. He saw Clara waiting on the porch, her blue tunic a vibrant contrast to the green of the fields. 

He realized then that he hadn't just built a home. He had initiated a cycle. A cycle where strength didn't have to lead to war, and where power didn't have to lead to conquest. 

The "First Seed" had been planted. And as Greg looked at his hands, clean of blood and full of life, he knew that the harvest would be more than just grain. 

It would be peace. 

But as he looked north toward the dark, jagged peaks of the Crag-Shadows, Greg knew that the peace was still a fragile thing. The green shoots of the valley were beautiful, but the frost was still out there, waiting in the shadows. 

For now, though, the sun was warm. The soil was rich. And the Smith was at rest.
