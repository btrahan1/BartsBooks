# Chapter 23: Bandits, Round 1

The Crag-Shadows were aptly named. 

The jagged, slate-grey peaks rose like the teeth of a dragon, casting long, bruised shadows across the narrow passes even at midday. It was a place of thin air, treacherous scree, and a silence that felt heavy with the threat of violence. For a month, the Twin Rivers Guard had been haunting these heights, scouting the territory Lord Alaric had "leased" to them in exchange for fifteen gold crowns and the head of the bandit leader, Thorne.

Tyler led the patrol, his forest-green tunic now dusty and salt-stained from weeks in the wild. Beside him marched the core of the "Unbreakable Guard"—Marcus, Petey, and the Miller twins—but there were new faces now. The reputation of Twin Rivers had spread like wildfire through the district, drawing young men who were tired of being easy pickings for recruiters and raiders alike.

One of those faces belonged to a wiry, narrow-eyed youth they called "Snake." 

Snake hadn't come from a farm or a forge. He had been a low-level thief in Oakhaven, a runner for the very "Black-Daggers" they were now hunting. He had heard the stories of the "Boy-Captain" and the "Invincible Spearmen" and had realized that his current life ended at the gallows. He had journeyed to Twin Rivers, begged for a trial, and had somehow survived the "mountain-running" and the "lake-breathing" that had broken men twice his size.

Snake idolized Tyler with a fervor that was almost embarrassing. He watched the way Tyler moved, the way he signaled with his flags, and the way he circulated his Ki. To Snake, Tyler wasn't just a leader; he was a god of the frontier. 

***

The sound of the attack reached them as they crested a jagged ridge overlooking the North Pass. 

It was the unmistakable scream of a wounded draft-horse and the rhythmic *thrum* of archery. Below, a merchant caravan—four heavy wagons carrying grain and imperial iron—was pinned between two low cliffs. A dozen bandits in blackened leathers were swarming the lead wagon, their jagged blades flashing in the sun.

"Positions!" Tyler signaled.

The Guard didn't hesitate. They didn't charge down the road in a disorganized mess. They split into two "Drop-Squads." Marcus led the center down the slope in a controlled slide, while Tyler led the flank through the boulders to the rear of the bandit line.

The bandits never saw them coming. 

They were busy laughing as they cut the harnesses of the horses when Marcus’s squad hit them like a landslide. *Clack-Crunch.* The iron-ringed shields slammed into the first line of raiders, throwing them back into the dirt. 

"STRIKE!" Marcus roared.

Seven spears shot out in perfect synchronization. Three bandits were down before they could even raise their swords. The others turned to fight, but they were met with a wall of oak and fire-hardened steel that refused to budge.

The bandits’ discipline broke instantly. These were men who relied on the fear of their victims, not the skill of their own arms. Faced with the "Unbreakable Guard," they found themselves outmatched and outplayed. 

Tyler appeared from the rocks behind the bandit rear-guard. He didn't use a shield today. He held his steel-tipped ash spear with both hands, his movements a blur of lethal, Ki-enhanced precision. He moved through the raider ranks like a gale, his point finding gaps in their leather armor with terrifying ease.

Within five minutes, the clearing was a scene of total bandit collapse. Nine raiders lay dead or dying in the mud, and four others were pinned against the wagon-wheels, their weapons discarded.

"Two are breaking for the high cave!" Petey shouted, pointing toward the eastern ridge where two bandits were sprinting toward a narrow, hidden path in the scree. "Tyler! I can take them!"

Tyler looked at the escapees. He could have taken them easily. A single focused "Dragon-Pulse" in his legs would have put him on their backs within seconds. 

Instead, Tyler stumbled. He intentionally "slipped" on a patch of wet moss, his spear-tip digging into the earth to catch his balance. 

"I... I missed the opening!" Tyler called out, his voice a convincing mix of frustration and effort. "They’re too fast! Marcus, Petey—hold the prisoners! Don't chase them into the dark!"

The Guard slowed, looking at their Captain with confusion. Tyler was never clumsy. He never "missed an opening." But his command was firm, and the boys stayed their ground, securing the four captives against the wagons.

Snake, however, was watching Tyler with his narrow, thief-trained eyes. He didn't see a stumble. He saw a calculation. 

Tyler pulled Snake aside as the Guard began the grim work of tending to the wounded merchants. 

"Snake," Tyler whispered, his eyes fixed on the distant, disappearing silhouettes of the two bandits. "You still have your 'city-shoes'?"

Snake reached into his pack and pulled out a pair of soft, dark-leather boots—the Kind that made no sound on stone. He looked at Tyler, his heart pounding. "I do, Lead."

"Those two aren't just running," Tyler said, his voice dropping to a low, intense frequency. "They’re heading for the 'Eagle’s Nest.' I can feel their fear—they’re going back to tell Thorne that the 'Guard' has found them. I want you to follow them. Don't engage. Don't let them see you. I want the exact location of that camp by dawn."

Snake’s eyes widened. This was it—the "Special Mission" he had been dreaming of. "I... I won't let you down, Tyler. I swear on the spark."

"If you get caught, Snake, we can't save you," Tyler warned. "But if you find that camp... you'll be the one who earns us the fifteen gold. You won't be a 'former thief' anymore. You'll be the Eyes of Twin Rivers."

Snake didn't answer with words. He simply nodded, his face hardening into the look of a predator. He changed his boots with a speed that spoke of long nights in the Oakhaven alleys and vanished into the shadows of the cliffs before the rest of the Guard even noticed he was gone.

***

As the evening shadows lengthened, the Guard set up a temporary camp near the salvaged caravan. The merchants were effusive in their praise, offering bags of grain and silver in gratitude, but Tyler was distant. 

He sat by the small, smokeless fire Greg had taught them to build, his eyes fixed on the eastern ridge. 

"You missed that slip, Tyler," Marcus said, sitting down beside him with a bowl of stew. "I saw it. You didn't fall. You let them go."

Tyler looked at his friend. "Thorne is a cultivator, Marcus. He won't be sitting in a cave waiting for us. He’ll have traps, he’ll have scouts, and he’ll have decoys. If we march in blind, we’re just 'extra heads' for his bounty. But if we follow his own men home..."

"You sent the new boy," Marcus realized, looking toward the dark peaks. "You sent Snake."

"Snake is the only one of us who knows how to move in the dark without a spear making noise," Tyler said. "Greg says the 'Price of Peace' is often paid in secrets. Snake is our secret."

Tyler looked back toward the ridge. In the distance, a single owl hooted—the signal Greg had taught them for "All Clear." But Tyler knew that the "All Clear" was just a prelude to the storm. 

Thorne was out there. A man with a core and the training of the Imperial Legion. 

"Round 1 goes to us," Tyler whispered to the darkening air. "But the real fight hasn't even started."

As the Guard settled in for the night, their spears never far from their hands, the "Unbreakable Guard" waited for the return of their eyes. 

And high in the Crag-Shadows, Snake moved through the night like a wisp of smoke, following the trail of fear that led straight to the heart of the bandit king.
