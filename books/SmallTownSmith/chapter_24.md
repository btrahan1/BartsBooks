# Chapter 24: Snake

"I will not mess this up," he whispered to the stone. "I will *not* mess this up."

Snake didn't walk; he slithered. In the high, jagged shadows of the North Pass, he was a wisp of smoke caught in a crevice. He moved through the stiff mountain grass and behind the gnarled pine trunks with a silence that was unnatural even for a hunter. This was the movement of the slums—the quiet, desperate slink of a boy who had learned that a clicking heel or a heavy breath meant a beating, or worse.

He had grown up in the gutters of Oakhaven, a place where hunger was a permanent resident and hope was something people sold for half a loaf of bread. He knew the desperation of the "worthless." He had been a runner, a lookout, a piece of human scrap used by men whose only skill was cruelty. He was a loser. He had felt it deep in his bones, a dark, cold weight that told him he would die in an alley, unremembered and unmourned.

Until Tyler. 

Snake remembered the day he had arrived in Twin Rivers, his ribs showing through his rags and his eyes darting around for something to steal. He had expected to be chased away with stones. Instead, he had been met by a boy not much older than himself, but with eyes that seemed to hold the depth of the Silverun Lake. 

Tyler had looked at him—really looked at him. Every secret Snake held, every petty theft, every moment of cowardice... it had felt bare under those deep, mesmerizing eyes. Snake had wanted to slink away, to run back to the familiar misery of the city. But Tyler hadn't looked at him with pity. He hadn't looked at him with disgust. He had looked at him as if he were a piece of raw iron waiting for a forge. 

Snake wanted whatever those Guards had. He wanted the strength to stand without trembling. He wanted the "warmth" in his chest that seemed to make the boys of Twin Rivers immovable. And in that moment, under Tyler’s search, Snake had decided he would do whatever it took. 

He might be worthless, but Tyler didn't think so. Tyler saw something. And if he had to crawl across the entire Crag-Shadows on his belly to find what Tyler saw, Snake would do it. 

***

The two bandits were loud. 

Panic was a clumsy thing. The escapees were breathing like broken bellows, their boots scuffing the scree and their voices echoing in the narrow draws. They thought they were alone. They thought their "stumble" from the Boy-Captain had been a stroke of luck. 

Snake followed them from fifty yards back, always in the shadows, always downwind. He didn't use the 'Infantry Trot.' He used the 'City Glide'—a stay-low, weight-forward movement that ensured he never displaced a single pebble. 

"Thorne’s gonna kill us," one of the bandits gasped, stopping to lean against a boulder. "He’s gonna take our heads for losing the grain-wagons."

"Not if we tell him about the Green-coats," the other replied, his voice shaking. "He needs to know. They ain't just farmers. They're... they're *something else*."

Snake’s heart hammered against his ribs, but he didn't let the adrenaline reach his hands. He focused on his breath, the way Greg had taught the men—even if he hadn't yet found his own "spark." He imagined his breath as a silken thread, connecting him to the ground. 

The trail led higher, into the "Eagle’s Nest"—a natural fortress of limestone cliffs and ancient, wind-hollowed caves. It was a place that shouldn't have been reachable, but the bandits knew a secret path, a series of hidden hand-holds and a narrow ledge that was invisible from below. 

Snake watched them disappear into a cleft in the rock. He didn't follow immediately. He waited, counting to five hundred, his chest pressed to the cold stone. Then, he began the climb. 

He didn't need Ki to climb. He had spent his childhood scaling the crumbling tenements of Oakhaven to escape the city watch. He found the gaps in the stone, his fingers bleeding but his will as hard as the mountain. He reached the ledge and pulled himself up, peering through a thicket of stunted juniper. 

Below him, in a wide, volcanic bowl hidden from the sky, was the heart of the Black-Daggers. 

It wasn't a camp. It was an armed compound. 

There were forty men, at least. They had rows of stolen tents, a dozen horses, and a stockpile of imperial gear that would have outfitted a company. But the most terrifying thing was the man at the center of the bowl. 

He was sitting on a stone throne, a massive, double-headed axe leaning against his knee. He was bare-chested despite the mountain cold, his skin covered in tattoos that seemed to pulse with a low, dark energy. 

Thorne.

Even from his height, Snake could feel the "weight" of the man. It wasn't the warm, nurturing heat of Greg’s forge. It was a cold, suffocating pressure—the Ki of a man who had used his core to kill until the fire turned to ash. 

The two escapees stumbled into the clearing, collapsing at Thorne’s feet. Snake couldn't hear the words, but he saw Thorne’s reaction. The bandit king didn't rage. He didn't shout. He slowly stood up, the axe in his hand, and looked toward the pass where the Guard was waiting. 

Snake felt a surge of terror. He was one boy. A "worthless thief" in the den of wolves. 

But then he remembered Tyler’s eyes. *I am the Eyes of Twin Rivers,* he told himself, the words a mantra that blocked out the fear. *I am NOT a loser.*

He watched the camp for another hour, memorizing the positions of the lookouts, the location of the horse-lines, and the single narrow entrance to the "Nest." He saw the way the bandits moved—they were disciplined, but they were afraid of Thorne. That was a weakness. 

He began the descent. 

He moved faster now, the moon providing a silver-pale light that marked his way. Every slip, every snap of a branch, felt like a bell-toll, but he didn't stop. He pushed through the exhaustion, his "worthless" legs screaming for rest, his mind a single-minded drive to return to the fire of the Guard. 

When he finally reached the temporary base camp at dawn, he was more shadow than man. He stumbled into the perimeter, and two spears were at his throat before he could even gasp a breath. 

"It's me!" he choked out, collapsing into the mud. "It's Snake!"

Petey and Marcus lowered their spears, their eyes wide as they saw the boy who looked like he had been dragged through a rock-shredder. 

Tyler appeared from the main tent, his face drawn but his eyes instantly lighting up. He knelt beside Snake, ignoring the grime and the blood. 

"You found it?" Tyler asked, his voice a low, intense anchor. 

Snake looked up at him. He didn't see a "loser" in the reflection of Tyler’s eyes. He saw a survivor. 

"The Eagle’s Nest," Snake whispered, his hand shaking as he pointed toward the north. "Forty men. Horses. And Thorne... he’s waiting for you, Tyler. He’s... he’s ready."

Tyler placed a hand on Snake’s head—a gesture of such profound approval that Snake felt like he could have flown to the peaks. 

"Get him water," Tyler commanded the others. "And food. The best we have."

He looked at the Guard, who were gathering around. 

"We have our eyes," Tyler said. "Now, we show them our teeth."

As they began the final mobilization, Snake lay on a wool blanket, his body aching, his hands bandaged. He was still hungry. He was still tired. But the dark, cold weight in his bones was gone. 

He had not messed it up. He was a Guard. 

And as he looked at Tyler’s forest-green tunic, reflecting the first light of the new day, Snake realized that Tyler hadn't just given him a mission. 

He had given him himself.
