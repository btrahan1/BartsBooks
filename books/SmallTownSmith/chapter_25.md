# Chapter 25: Thorne

Thorne was a man who lived by the math. 

Sitting on his throne of jagged basalt, overlooking the volcanic bowl of the Eagle’s Nest, he watched his men sharpen their blades and boast about their exploits. To them, it was a life of freedom. To Thorne, it was a calculated risk with a high yield. 

In the Imperial Third Legion, Thorne had been a sergeant of the line—a role that required a cold, analytical eye for the "soldier’s path." He had seen men die for honor, and he had seen them die for glory. He had watched brothers-in-arms bleed out in the Kragh mud for a "cause" that didn't pay for their funerals. Thorne had done the math early, and the result was always zero. 

Honor didn't buy grain. Glory didn't buy wine. And companionship was a liability when the food ran low. 

So, Thorne had left. He had taken his "severance package" from the Legion’s armory—two heavy-headed battle axes—and walked into the mountains to become a bigger fish in a smaller, bloodier pond. 

Here, in the Crag-Shadows, he was a god. He looked at the women in the camp—some who had come for protection, others who were prisoners of his raids—and he felt the intoxicating "perk" of being the strongest. He didn't have to follow orders. He didn't have to worry about the "District Governor" or the "King’s Law." He was the law. 

He was "average" as a soldier in the Imperial ranks, but on the frontier, average was lethal. He possessed something that most commoners only heard about in fearful whispers: a core. 

Thorne closed his eyes, focusing on the dark, simmering heat in the center of his chest. He had a natural affinity for fire—a jagged, uncontrolled element that matched his temperament. He reached out, his calloused hands closing around the handles of his twin axes. 

*Whoosh.*

A low, orange flame erupted along the edges of the steel. It wasn't the clean, golden light of the Twin Rivers sparks; it was a hungry, smoky fire that smelled of ozone and scorched iron. Just the sight of the "Flame-Brand" axes had sent entire merchant caravans to their knees, begging for their lives. No farmer with a pointed stick could stand against a man who wielded the heart of a furnace.

***

"They’re coming, Boss," the escapee gasped, kneeling at the base of the basalt throne. "The Green-coats. They... they killed a dozen of us at the North Pass. Didn't even break a sweat."

Thorne opened one eye, the orange glow of his Ki reflecting in his pupils. "Goblins killed a dozen of you last summer, Silas. You’re weak. You always have been."

"But these ones... they move different," the bandit insisted, his voice trembling. "They got iron-rimmed shields. And they move together... like a single beast. They didn't even shout. They just... stood there."

Thorne let out a short, guttural laugh. "Farmers always think they can play soldier once they get a few silver coins and a new tunic. I’ve seen ‘phalanxes’ of militiamen broken by a single heavy charge. These 'Twin Rivers' boys probably found a sergeant who taught them how to hold a line for five minutes."

He stood up, his massive frame casting a long, intimidating shadow across the clearing. The twin axes were still burning, the heat shimmering the air around him.

"Kael and Valerius are fools," Thorne spat. "They think they can use a bunch of village kids to clear my mountains. They think a 'Town Guard' is enough to handle a cultivator."

He walked to the edge of the bowl, looking down the narrow, secret path that Snake had traversed just hours before. 

"It’s time a few farm boys saw the difference between a man and a titan," Thorne said, his voice a low, crackling rumble. "If they want to play hunter, I’ll show them what it’s like to be the prey. We don't wait for them. At dawn, we move out. I’m going to melt their shields and roast their 'Teacher' in his own forge."

Thorne truly believed it. In his world, the math was simple. He had the fire. He had the training. He had the experience of twenty years in the Imperial Legions. A group of "boys" from a mining town were nothing more than fodder for his Flame-Brand. 

He couldn't conceive of a "Teacher" who was more than a retired sergeant. He certainly couldn't imagine a man like Gregory—a master who had forgotten more about Ki-warfare than Thorne would ever learn. To Thorne, Twin Rivers was an annoyance to be crushed, a "small pond" that needed its fish taught a lesson.

He felt the heat in his core intensify, a dark, suffocating pressure that matched the volcanic pit he called home. He didn't care about the gold crowns or the Governor’s quest. He cared about the dominance of the fire. 

As his men began to prepare for the night-march, Thorne sat back on his throne, his eyes fixed on the distant ridge where a "verdant spark" was quietly growing. 

"Enjoy your dinner, boys," Thorne whispered, the flames on his axes flaring brighter. "It’ll be the last hot meal you ever eat."

He didn't know that Snake was already miles away, carrying the exact location of his "impenetrable" fortress back to the very boys he dismissed as farmers. He didn't know that the "math" he relied on was fundamentally broken. 

Thorne thought he was the hunter. He thought he was the most dangerous thing in the Crag-Shadows. 

And as the night-wind whipped through the Eagle’s Nest, Thorne laughed, a sound that held no joy—only the cold, hungry anticipation of the fire.
