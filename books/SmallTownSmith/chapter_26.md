# Chapter 26: The Trap

Snake woke before the sun, his body aching from the climb, but his mind sharp with a strange, jittery energy. 

He sat up on his wool roll, the cold mountain air biting at his skin, and did what he had seen Tyler do every morning. He closed his eyes. He focused on the rhythm of his breath—in for four, hold for four, out for eight—and he reached for the space below his navel. 

And there, he saw it. 

It wasn't the steady, golden "spark" that Petey and Marcus bragged about. It wasn't the sun-like radiance that Tyler carried. It was a dark flame. It was smoky, elusive, and hard to "see" with his internal eye, but he could feel it swirling in his core like a trapped shadow. It felt cold where the others felt warm, and it seemed to hunger for the silence of the night. 

Snake didn't know what it was, but he knew it was *his*. He coaxed the smoky heat through his limbs, and for the first time, the "City Glide" felt truly effortless. He wasn't just moving through the shadows; he felt like he was *part* of them.

***

The Guard broke camp as the first finger of grey light touched the peaks. They moved toward the "Eagle’s Nest" with a grim, focused efficiency, Tyler leading the way with Snake just a few paces ahead. 

But as they neared a narrow gorge that led to the volcanic bowl, Snake stopped dead. 

The air had changed. It didn't smell like pine and stone anymore; it smelled like something had been singed. There was a faint, brassy tang on his tongue that made his "Dark Flame" hum with a discordant alarm. 

"Something’s wrong," Snake whispered, materializing beside Tyler like a ghost. "The air is... heavy. Different."

Tyler looked at him, his own golden eyes narrowing as he sensed the dark, jittery energy radiating from his scout. "Go ahead. Be the eyes, Snake. Don't engage."

Snake didn't need to be told twice. He vanished into the shadows of a limestone cliff, his movements a blur of dark-fluidity. He raced ahead, his new senses picking up the "shiver" of footfalls that weren't the Guard’s. 

He reached a high overlook and pulled his breath into his core. 

Below him, moving with a silent, imperial discipline, was a column of forty men. Most were the Black-Daggers he had seen in the Nest, but at the lead was a massive, bare-chested figure with twin axes that seemed to shimmer with an unnatural, smoking heat. 

They weren't defending. They were hunting. 

Snake dashed back to the Guard, his heart hammering in his throat. He reached Tyler just as they were entering a wide, rocky clearing. 

"They're coming!" Snake gasped, his voice a low, urgent hiss. "Thorne... he's not in the Nest. He’s on the road. Forty men, fully armed. They’re less than a mile out."

"Oh, really?" Tyler mused, a slow, dangerous smile spreading across his face. "So the prey wants to hunt? Well, then... it’s time for us to prepare a feast for them!"

Tyler’s eyes scanned the clearing—a natural hollow surrounded by thick juniper-brush and high, jagged boulders. It was a perfect "Kill-Box," but only if the enemy thought it was theirs. 

"Men! Listen to me!" Tyler commanded, his flags ready in his hands. "We change the game. We set a camp. Right here, in the center of the hollow."

"A camp?" Marcus asked, his hand tightening on his spear. "Tyler, they're coming for us! We should be in the cliffs!"

"No," Tyler said, his voice a steady, grounding anchor. "We show them exactly what they expect to see—a group of 'farm boys' who think they've already won. Set up the tents. Build a large fire. I want you laughing, I want you shouting, I want you to look like you're having the time of your lives."

He looked at Petey. "But stay in your armor. Keep your shields beneath your sitting-spots. And NO drinking! We’ll use the cider-kegs as water barrels."

The Guard moved with a speed that spoke of their "Farm" training. In ten minutes, the clearing looked like the most disorganized, arrogant campsite in the Crag-Shadows. 

Tyler then turned back to Snake. He didn't just look at him—he "looked," his golden Ki-sight piercing through the physical layers. He saw the smoky, dark flame swirling in the boy’s chest, a shadow-twin to his own golden radiance. 

Tyler smiled, a look of profound, fraternal pride. 

"After this is over, Snake," Tyler said softly, "you will be called The Dark Flame. You’re not just a thief anymore. You’re the shadow that protects the valley."

Snake felt a surge of heat in his gut—the dark flame flared, not with cold, but with a fierce, loyal intensity. He bowed low, his hood masking his face. 

"Go into the rocks," Tyler ordered. "Wait for my signal. When the 'feast' begins, I want the eyes of their lookouts silenced."

Snake vanished. 

***

The sun was beginning to dip behind the western ridge when the sound of Thorne’s march reached the clearing. 

In the center of the hollow, the "Unbreakable Guard" was putting on a performance that would have made a stage-actor proud. Petey was "recounting" the boar hunt with loud, exaggerated gestures. Marcus and his brother were "wrestling" in the mud, their laughter echoing off the canyon walls. They looked like a group of children playing soldier. 

Hidden in the thick juniper-brush, Tyler sat with his spear across his knees, his eyes fixed on the entrance to the hollow. He could feel the fire coming. 

The air grew hot. A dark, suffocating pressure began to roll into the clearing, like the breath of a dying furnace. 

Thorne stepped into the light of the hollow’s entrance. His twin axes were already burning, the orange flames casting long, flickering shadows across the rocks. He looked at the "camp," the sneer on his face visible even from fifty yards. 

"Look at them," Thorne’s voice carried through the silence, a low, crackling growl. "Playing house in the dark. They didn't even set a picket."

Behind him, forty bandits fanned out, their blades drawn, their eyes hungry for the easy slaughter they had been promised. 

Tyler didn't move. He didn't signal. He waited until the bandit line was fully committed to the clearing, their focus entirely on the "laughing" boys in the center. 

High in the rocks, Snake watched Thorne. He felt the cold "Dark Flame" in his chest vibrating with a strange, predatory hunger. He saw the three bandit scouts Thorne had sent to the flanks—men who were supposed to be the "eyes" of the bandit king. 

Snake didn't use a spear. He used the two thin, blackened daggers Greg had forged for him. 

He moved like a shadow across the stone. 

Within minutes, the bandit "eyes" were silenced, their bodies hidden in the crevices without a single sound reaching the clearing. 

Thorne raised his axes, the fire on the blades leaping higher as he prepared to deliver the "Dragon-Melt" that would end the "Twin Rivers" dream. 

"For the Empire!" Thorne roared, his voice the signal for the charge. 

The bandits broke into a run, a chaotic, shouting wave of steel heading for the "laughing" men at the fireside. 

Tyler’s hand went to his yellow flag. 

"WAIT FOR IT," he whispered to himself, his core-breath reaching its peak. 

The bandits were thirty yards out. Twenty. Ten. 

"NOW!" 

Tyler’s yellow flag snapped through the air. 

At the fireside, the "laughing" boys didn't run. They didn't scream. In a single, unified motion, they dropped their gestures and grabbed the iron-ringed shields from beneath their seats. 

*CLACK-CLINK.*

The "Vortex-Phalanx" snapped into place around the fire. 

Thorne stopped. The surprise on his face was the most beautiful thing Tyler had ever seen. The "farm boys" were gone. In their place was a ring of oak and iron, their spears held level, their eyes glowing with the same golden focus as their Captain. 

From the brush, Tyler’s two hidden squads—the ones who had been "silent" while the others "played"—erupted from the juniper. 

The "prey" had just closed the trap. 

And in the shadows of the rocks, the Dark Flame waited for the fire to flicker.
