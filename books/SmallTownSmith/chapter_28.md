# Chapter 28: Clara, The Druid Priestess

The clearing was quiet now, the orange flames of the bandit pyre casting long, flickering shadows against the limestone walls of the Eagle’s Nest. The "Vortex-Phalanx" had dissolved, replaced by a circle of weary young men sitting on their bedrolls, their heads bowed and their armor loosened. 

But in the center of the camp, something extraordinary was happening. 

Clara was moving between them, a small basin of mountain water and a roll of clean linen in her hands. To an outsider, she looked like a simple village woman tending to her brothers. But to the men of the Guard, she was becoming something else entirely. 

"Are you a Priestess, Miss Clara? Or a Druid?" 

It was Petey who asked the question, his voice small and shaky. He was sitting on a cedar stump, his tunic pulled back to reveal a jagged, angry gash across his shoulder where a bandit’s short-sword had found a gap in his gambeson. 

Clara laughed softly, a sound like a gentle breeze through the barley fields. She didn't look up from her work, her hands moving with a fluid, confident grace that seemed to calm the air around her. 

"No, silly boy," she said, her voice a soothing melody. "I'm still the simple Lady of the Forge—or the Lady of the Farm, I suppose, now that Greg has started his 'Sheep Ranch'—that you've always known, Petey. Now be still. This won't hurt, but it might feel a bit... strange."

Clara worked for the next few hours, a focused intensity in her eyes that reminded Greg of his own when he was folding steel at the anvil. She didn't just wash the wounds. She "saw" them. 

As she touched the edges of Marcus’s sliced ribs or Petey’s torn shoulder, she let the essence of the valley—the same "Verdant Pulse" she had watched Greg use on the ridge—come to her. It wasn't a weapon. It was a bridge. 

She began to "negotiate" with the wounds. In the silent, internal language of her burgeoning power, she reached out to the disrupted flesh, offering it a bit of her own essence. She spoke to the blood and the bone, reminding them of how they were part of the whole, beautiful body they were attached to. She showed the wounds the "Golden Sparks" that lived in the boy’s chests—the fire that Greg had kindled in them—and she coaxed that fire to the surface. 

She wasn't using her own energy to heal; she was using her connection to nature to show the boys’ own bodies how to mend themselves. 

"See the fire, Marcus?" she whispered, her hands glowing with a soft, translucent light. "Let it flow north. The skin wants to be whole. The blood wants to stay home. Just... let it."

The effect was instantaneous. Under her touch, the angry red of the gashes began to fade into a healthy, itching pink. The jagged edges of the flesh seemed to crawl toward each other, knitting together with a speed that defied the laws of common medicine. 

The boys didn't scream. They didn't even flinch. As the "negotiation" took place, a profound, heavy sense of peace settled over them. One by one, their heads would drop, their breathing becoming slow and deep, as they drifted into the deep, restorative sleep of the truly safe. 

Greg watched from the shadows of the juniper brush, his arms crossed. He had seen "Healing Arts" in the Capital—priests who demanded gold and chants to close a wound, often leaving a man crippled or scarred. But he had never seen anyone do what Clara was doing. She wasn't commanding the healing. She was *inviting* it. 

"She's doing what I did with the soil," Greg whispered to himself, a sense of awe warming his chest. "She’s not breaking the injury. She’s feeding the health."

***

As the moon reached its zenith, the clearing of the Eagle’s Nest was no longer a slaughterhouse. It was a sanctuary. 

Twenty-one men slept in a wide circle around the central fire, their wounds already mending under the bandages Clara had applied. The "Price of Victory" was being repaid in the restorative warmth of her essence. 

Clara finally sat down next to Tyler, who was the last to receive her attention. His spear-wound—a deep bruise and a shallow gash from Thorne’s final charge—was almost gone, the skin already smooth and cool. 

"You're tired, Clara," Tyler said, his voice thick with sleep. "You should rest. We’ll take the first watch."

"The watch is set, Tyler," Clara said, leaning her head back against a rock. "Snake is in the cliffs, and Greg is... well, Greg is everywhere. You sleep. You earned it."

As Tyler’s eyes fluttered shut, he reached out and touched the sleeve of her ivory tunic. 

"Druid... Priestess..." he murmured, the words vanishing into the soft crackle of the fire. 

Within minutes, the clearing was silent, save for the collective, healthy rhythm of twenty-two lungs—and the one shadow in the rocks who watched them all. 

Clara sat in the silence, her own "spark" flickering with a tired but satisfied glow. She looked at her hands. They were stained with the mountain mud and the boys' blood, but beneath the grime, she could still see the pale, shimmering remnants of the healing light. 

She wasn't just a "Lady of the Forge" anymore. She was the one who kept the Forge from breaking. 

Greg walked into the circle, his step silent, and sat down beside her. He didn't say a word. He just took her hand in his and pulled it to his lips, his rough, calloused fingers a stark contrast to the softness of her own. 

"You saved them, Clara," Greg whispered. 

"I just reminded them that they weren't broken, Greg," she replied, leaning her head on his shoulder. "The land did the rest. Just like on the ridge."

Greg looked at the sleeping Guard, their faces peaceful for the first time since they had entered the Crag-Shadows. He realized that Twin Rivers had achieved something more than a military victory. They had achieved a wholeness. 

If Greg was the Shield that turned the fire, Clara was the Light that mended the oak. 

"The Guard is whole," Greg said, his voice full of a quiet, profound certainty. "And the fifteen gold is won. But as far as these boys are concerned... you're worth more than the whole Empire’s treasury."

Clara smiled, her eyes closing as the exhaustion finally took her. "Then they better start eating less, Greg. Because a 'Druid Priestess' is expensive to keep in radishes."

Greg laughed, pulling her closer as the dawn began to break over the peaks. 

Act 2 had begun with a harvest of steel, but as the "Druid Priestess" slept in her husband’s arms, the true harvest was revealed. 

Twin Rivers was no longer just a town under arms. It was a place where life could find its way back to the sun.
