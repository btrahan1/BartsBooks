# Chapter 29: Everyone goes to Oakhaven

The road to Oakhaven had never seen a procession quite like the one that descended from the Crag-Shadows that Tuesday. 

It was a grim, dusty symphony of clanking chains and creaking wood. At the lead were eleven members of the Twin Rivers Guard, their forest-green tunics now symbols of a victory that the district’s elite legions had failed to achieve for years. Behind them marched a line of twenty-two bedraggled prisoners—the remnants of the Black-Daggers—followed by two heavy wagons stacked with the bodies of those who had chosen to die by the spear. 

Greg and Clara walked at the center of the column, Greg’s massive frame acting as a silent, immovable anchor for the group, while Clara, in her travel-stained ivory dress, looked less like a village woman and more like the "Druid Priestess" the boys had started to call her. 

"Where's Snake?" Petey asked, his voice low as he scanned the jagged ridgeline to their west. 

Tyler didn't even look up from the road. He simply motioned with a slight tilt of his head toward the deep, shadowed crevices of the western peaks. "The Dark Flame is in that direction, Petey."

"How can you tell?" Petey asked, blinking. "I don't see anything but rock and crow-shadows."

Tyler stopped for a moment, his golden eyes momentarily losing their focus as he reached out with his core. He felt a cold, smoky vibration on the edge of his senses—a shadow that harmonized perfectly with his own golden spark. He shrugged, a small, puzzled smile touching his lips. 

"Not sure," Tyler admitted. "I just know. It’s like... he’s the shadow my light makes."

They didn't talk more after that. The weight of their mission was still heavy on their shoulders, even as the white limestone walls of Oakhaven began to glitter in the distance. 

***

The reaction at the city gates was one of stunned, terrified silence. 

The city watch, men who usually spent their days taxing cabbage-carts and ignoring petty thefts, stood with their mouths open as the "Unbreakable Guard" marched their prisoners into the main square. The news spread like a mountain fire; by the time they reached the Municipal Court, half the city was lining the streets, whispering about the "Boys from the Lake" and the "Smith" who had supposedly taught them to eat fire. 

Tyler led the delegation into the Clerk’s office, the same office where they had once turned in fifty-one goblin heads. This time, however, there was no haggling. 

The Clerk—a man named Hadrin who had aged ten years since their last visit—stared out the window at the wagons of dead bandits. 

"Twenty-two captures," Tyler announced, his voice echoing in the stone-walled room with the authority of a captain. "And the rest of the Nest is in those two wagons. You can keep the wagons themselves; consider them a bonus from the Twin Rivers to help with the... cleanup."

He then gestured to three more wagons that had pulled up behind the column, laden with crates of imperial-stamped iron and stacks of high-grade plate-mail. 

"That’s the loot from the Eagle’s Nest," Tyler continued. "Most of it bears the mark of the Third Legion. Stolen gear from the Kragh border-skirmishes, I suspect. We’re returning it to the Empire as our contribution to the District’s security."

Hadrin the Clerk looked at the iron, then at the armor, then at the fifteen gold crowns Tyler was clearly expecting. He realized that by returning the imperial gear, the Guard was making it impossible for the Governor to claim they were "hoarding military assets." It was a brilliant, strategic "gift" that bought them another year of independence. 

He didn't even check the wagons. He reached into the municipal safe and pulled out three heavy silk pouches, each containing five gold crowns—more wealth than Twin Rivers had seen in a century. 

He handed the pouches to Tyler, his hands trembling. "The Governor... he will be pleased, Lead Tyler. Extremely pleased."

"We’re happy to be of service," Tyler said, his tone purely professional. "But for now, the Crag-Shadows are clear. We'll be returning to our 'Sheep Ranch'."

***

Once the business was concluded and the prisoners were handed over to the city dungeon, the grim atmosphere of the day evaporated like morning mist. 

"Shopping!" Clara announced, her eyes suddenly bright with a mischievous, predatory energy that made Greg’s "Dragon" instincts scream "Danger."

Greg groaned silently, his hand instinctively going to his belt-pouch. He had watched as his squad had faced down an imperial sergeant, fifty goblins, and a rogue cultivator with flaming axes without blinking, but the prospect of following his wife through the Oakhaven market-district filled him with a primitive, soul-crushing dread. 

"Clara, we have a farm to tend," Greg tried, a weak attempt at defense. 

"The barley can grow for one more day without you talking to it, Gregory," Clara retorted, her smile widening as she looked toward the "Silk and Spice" lane. "We need real curtains for the house. And I suspect Petey needs a new tunic that doesn't smell like bandit-fire. And Marcus... well, Marcus needs everything."

The Guard, seeing the "Priestess" in her element, didn't need any further encouragement. They were young men with silver in their pockets for the first time in their lives, and the city was full of wonders they had only ever heard about in Silas’s stories. 

"Go on," Greg sighed, waving them away. "Just... try not to spend it all on sugar-bread and ribbons. We have to buy a new seed-drill tomorrow."

"Ribbons first, Greg!" Petey shouted, already running toward the haberdashery with Marcus in tow.

The rest of the afternoon was a whirlwind of sensory overload. Clara led Greg through the high-end shops, her eye for quality as sharp as any smith’s. She bought bolts of deep-blue velvet for their bedroom, a set of copper-bottomed pans that Greg grudgingly admitted were well-forged, and a small, delicate silver mirror that she held as if it were a holy relic. 

Greg followed behind her, carrying an increasingly heavy load of packages, his face a mask of stoic endurance. But as he watched her laughter, and the way the city-folk looked at her with a mixture of awe and respect, he couldn't find it in himself to truly be annoyed. 

By the time they reached the "Black-Lion Inn" for their evening meal, the Guard was transformed. Gone were the dusty warriors of the morning. In their place were a group of well-dressed, well-fed young men who were currently occupying four tables and regaling the innkeeper’s daughters with a highly sanitized (and possibly exaggerated) version of the "Battle of the Nest."

"They're becoming a legend, Greg," Clara whispered as they sat at a quiet corner table, a glass of expensive southern wine between them. 

Greg looked at his men, then at the pouches of gold on the table. "They're becoming a target, Clara. The Empire doesn't like legends they don't control. But for tonight..." 

He reached out and took her hand, his rough thumb tracing the silver ring he had forged for her. 

"...tonight, we’re just a couple of farmers in the city."

"With a lot of curtains," Clara reminded him, her mischievous smile returning. 

"With a LOT of curtains," Greg agreed, his own smile finally breaking through. 

The legend of Twin Rivers was now a matter of public record. In the taverns and the marketplaces of Oakhaven, the story of the "Unbreakable Guard" and their "Druid Priestess" would be told for years. But as Greg looked at his wife and his boys, he knew that the greatest victory wasn't the gold or the fame. 

It was the fact that they could finally afford to just be happy. 

Even if it meant carrying eighteen bolts of blue velvet back to the ridge, so be it.
