# Chapter 31: The Wall

Two weeks. 

In the time it took for a single merchant caravan to make the round trip from Oakhaven, the face of Twin Rivers had changed forever. It wasn't just the Temple in the center, or the vibrancy of Greg’s ridge-harvest. It was the skin of the town itself. 

The "Peace-Wall" had been completed. 

It wasn't an imperial fortification. It lacked the jagged battlements and the imposing, fifty-foot heights of the Citadel in the Capital. It stood exactly ten feet tall—just high enough to discourage a bandit’s ladder or a goblin’s leap, but low enough to still see the tops of the pine trees from within the square. Its four towers, one at each corner, rose only marginally higher, reaching twelve feet to provide a clear vantage point for the "Dark Flame" and his scouts. 

There were two gates. The main gate faced Oakhaven, built of iron-banded larch that could withstand a battering ram, yet carved with scenes of the Silverun Lake to make it feel welcoming. The second, smaller gate faced the northern forest—a "back door" for the hunters and the miners. 

The wall was not intended as a threat. It was a statement of fact. It said, with a quiet, unshakeable certainty: "We defend our own." 

Greg stood at the Oakhaven gate, his massive hands resting on the raw, cold stone of the foundation. He had been the lead engineer, drawing up the "math" for the basalt joints and the weight-bearing arches. It was his expertise—filtered through the soul of a smith who wanted peace—that had made the structure possible in such a short time. 

But the labor... the labor had been a miracle. 

"Two weeks," Silas said, walking up beside him and wiping his brow. "Greg, if the Governor heard about this, he’d send an entire engineering corps just to find out how we did it. Two weeks for a mile of basalt-and-stone?"

"They had help," Greg said, his eyes scanning the finished wall. "They had the gold from the Nest, the tools from Oakhaven, and the Breath in their lungs. When a man believes in what he’s building, Silas, his hands don't get tired."

It was true. The entire village had become a single organism during the construction. The Guard had spent their days hauling the heavy basalt blocks from the Old Crow Mine, while the women and children had gathered the river-stone for the fill. Under Greg’s direction, they had used the "Collective Spark"—a synchronized pulsing of their Ki—to lift weights that should have required a team of oxen. They had "hammered" the mortar into the joints with their own steady breathing, creating a bond that was more than just lime and sand. 

The wall didn't just look solid; it *felt* solid. It resonated with the same "Verdant Pulse" as Greg’s fields and the "Healing Warmth" of Clara’s temple. 

***

As the sun caught the edge of the western tower, the first merchant caravan since the "Black-Dagger War" arrived from the city. 

The merchants stopped their wagons ten feet from the new gate, their eyes wide. Where there had once been a collection of scattered hovels and dusty paths, there was now a fortified sanctuary. 

"Is this... Twin Rivers?" the lead merchant asked, looking up at the ten-foot wall with a mixture of awe and caution. 

"It is," Petey said, standing at the gate in his green ceremonial tunic, his spear held in a relaxed but professional ready-position. 

The merchant looked at the gatehouse. Carved into the heavy larch beam above the entrance, in characters that were clear and unapologetic, was the town’s new motto: 

**WELCOME TO TWIN RIVERS. BE NICE.**

The merchant chuckled, a nervous, appreciative sound. "Short and sweet. I like it."

"It’s not a suggestion," Snake whispered from the shadows of the gate’s archway, his hooded presence making the merchant’s horse whinny in sudden, primal alarm. 

The guards in Oakhaven had a more vulgar, more direct interpretation of the sign: *"Enter with a smile, or leave with a spear-point."* But regardless of the wording, the message was the same. Twin Rivers was no longer a patch of dirt for the taking. It was a home that knew its own value. 

Clara walked down from the Temple to meet the caravan, her new blue velvet shawl draped over her shoulders. She looked at the wall and felt a sense of profound completedness. If the Temple was the heart and Greg was the mind, the Wall was the skin. It gave the body a boundary. It allowed the peace to stay inside, and the chaos to stay out. 

"The gate is open," Clara told the merchants, her voice warm and inviting. "The market square is ready, and the Priestess’s Hearth is burning. You're welcome to stay, provided you remember the motto."

The merchants didn't need to be told twice. They rolled their wagons through the iron-banded larch, entering a world that felt fundamentally different from the frantic, fearful city they had left. 

***

That evening, as the Guard took their first formal watch on the new 12-foot towers, Greg and Clara sat on the wall’s walkway, looking out over the darkening valley. 

"Ten feet of stone," Greg muttered, his hand tracing a seam in the basalt. "It’s not enough to stop a legion, Clara."

"It doesn't have to stop a legion, Greg," Clara said, leaning against him. "It just has to stop the *thought* of one. It’s a border. It tells the world that the land between these stones is different. That we aren't subjects anymore."

Greg looked toward the Oakhaven gate. He saw the sign flickering in the torchlight: *BE NICE.*

"They're going to come for us eventually," Greg said, his voice a low, prophetic rumble. "The Governor will want the wall. The Legions will want the Guard. And the Priests will want the Temple."

"Then we’ll just have to make sure that whatever they want... it’s not worth the price of taking it," Clara replied. 

She reached out and touched his hand, her "Healing Glow" flickering momentarily as she shared the warmth with him. 

"We built a soul in two weeks, Gregory. I think we can handle a few governors."

As the "Dark Flame" vanished into the forest shadows for the night-scout, and the "Unbreakable Guard" settled into their towers, the "Peace-Wall" of Twin Rivers stood as a silent witness to a miracle. 

The "small town" was now a fortress of the soul. 

And as the last light of the spring day faded from the silver surface of the lake, the message above the gate remained clear to anyone who would listen. 

Twin Rivers was open. The harvest was ready. The Temple was mending. 

Just... be nice.
