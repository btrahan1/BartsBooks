# Chapter 32: Surprise! Wait! What The Hell?

Lord Alaric, Governor of the Oakhaven District, had intended for this visit to be a "lesson in reality." 

He sat tall in his gilded saddle, his thirty heavy cavalry escorts—men of the Imperial Sixth, in polished silver breastplates—fanning out behind his obsidian carriage. He had chosen to make this trip unannounced, a "surprise" inspection meant to remind the people of Twin Rivers that while they might have cleared a few bandits, they still lived under the shadow of the Empire. He wanted to see them scramble. He wanted to see the "Smith" scrape and bow, and the "Priestess" fumble for a welcome. 

He wanted to establish dominance. 

But as the column marched through the high passes of the Crag-Shadows, Alaric discovered that "dominance" was a difficult thing to feel when your men were hyperventilating from fear. 

"My Lord," the Captain of the Escort whispered, his hand white on his sword-hilt as they passed a particularly dense cluster of cedar. "We’re being shadowed. Still."

Alaric looked into the dark woods. He saw nothing. But for the last twenty miles, his scouts had reported the same thing—a "shiver" in the shadows, a sense of eyes watching them from places where no man should be able to stand. Snake and his new "acolytes"—four wiry youths from the Oakhaven slums who had followed the "Dark Flame" back to the valley—had been haunting the Governor’s column since the moment they crossed the district line. 

No one, human or otherwise, would ever reach the Wall of Twin Rivers without the "Dark Patrol" giving the signal. 

***

As the Governor’s column crested the final ridge and the ten-foot basalt wall came into view, Alaric prepared his "Command Presence." He expected the gates to be closed, the guards to be frantic, and the village to be a hive of disorganized panic. 

"Open the gate in the name of—" the Captain began, but the words died in his throat. 

The heavy iron-banded larch gates were already wide open. 

Standing on the battlements, flanking a massive silk banner that proclaimed **"WELCOME GOVERNOR ALARIC"** in shimmering gold letters, were two guards in full ceremonial green. The banner was flying directly above the **"BE NICE"** sign, as if they had been waiting for him to arrive for a week. 

Clara stood at the center of the gateway, her velvet shawl draped over her shoulders, her eyes bright with a hospitable, almost feline amusement. 

"What the hell?" Alaric muttered, his "Command Presence" evaporating as he looked at the banner. 

"Welcome, Lord Alaric!" Clara said, her voice carrying through the spring air with effortless clarity. "We were beginning to think the mountain roads had slowed you down. The scouts said you crossed the North Pass at noon yesterday, so we’ve been keeping the cider chilled."

Alaric blinked. "You... you were expecting me?"

"Twin Rivers always welcomes its friends," Clara replied, stepping aside to reveal a street lined with the Guard. 

They weren't "scrambling." They were standing at a flawless, vertical-spear attention. And as Alaric’s column rode into the square, the level of "out-logisticked" surprise reached its peak. 

"The horses, My Lord," a boy named Petey said, stepping up to take Alaric’s reins. "We’ve just completed a new set of stables behind the Temple. Thirty-four stalls, all with fresh mountain hay and Ki-tempered water. Your men will find the 'Activity Center'—the new barracks building—to be quite comfortable. We’ve already laid out the bedrolls and the stew is on the hearth."

Alaric looked at the Captain of the Escort, who looked equally baffled. They were being treated like honored house-guests, not a military incursion. 

"What the hell?" Alaric said again, this time to the empty air. 

He looked toward the village green, where a group of ten *new* recruits—men who hadn't been in the Oakhaven procession—were currently drilling with Silas. They were moving through the "Vortex-Phalanx" with a raw, focused energy that showed the Guard was growing faster than the Governor’s reports could track. 

"Silas!" Clara called out. "The Governor has arrived! Ensure the men are ready for the feast tonight!"

Silas waved a massive, soot-stained hand, his grin as wide as its always was. "Aye, Milady! The roast is ready, and the Mayor has his speech prepared!"

Alaric dismounted, his silver breastplate feeling heavy and unnecessary in the face of such overwhelming hospitality. He looked around for the man he truly wanted to see—the "Smith" who was the engine of this impossible transformation. 

"Where is Greg?" Alaric asked, his voice regaining some of its authority. 

"Oh, my husband is busy 'elsewhere' today, My Lord," Clara said, her deflection as smooth as the river-stone. "The spring planting doesn't wait for dignitaries, I’m afraid. He sends his deepest regrets and has ensured that the 'Governor’s Suite' at the upgraded Inn has the finest view of the valley."

"Planting?" Alaric repeated, the word sounding foreign in his mouth. "A man who can Forge fifteen gold crowns out of a bandit camp is... planting?"

"A smith’s work is never done, My Lord," Clara said, leading him toward the Inn. "But I think you’ll find the 'Activity Center' to be a marvel of local engineering. We modeled the ventilation on the Imperial Barracks, but added a touch of our own 'Verdant Pulse' to keep the men refreshed."

Alaric walked into the Inn, which had been expanded with a second story of polished larch. His room was indeed a marvel—a wide balcony overlooking the "Peace-Wall," a bed of cedar-down, and a bowl of fresh fruit from Greg’s ridge. 

He sat on the bed, looking out at the ten-foot wall and the "Welcome" banner. He had come to establish dominance. He had come to prove that he was the big fish in this district. 

Instead, he had been met with a logistics operation that would have made the Imperial Quartermaster weep with joy. He had been "welcomed" into a trap of perfect comfort and unshakeable certainty. 

He realized then that Twin Rivers hadn't just built a wall. They had built a reality that simply didn't recognize his authority as anything more than a polite inconvenience. 

"What the hell," Alaric whispered for the third time, reaching for a bunch of grapes that tasted like liquid sunlight. 

He looked down at the street, where his thirty heavy cavalrymen were being led to the "Activity Center" by a group of smiling village girls, their horses already being groomed by the Guard. 

His "Command Presence" was gone. He wasn't a dominator. He was a tourist. 

And as the "Be Nice" sign caught the afternoon light, Lord Alaric, Governor of the Oakhaven District, decided that for tonight... he would indeed be very, very nice. 

Because he was terrified of what would happen if he wasn't.
