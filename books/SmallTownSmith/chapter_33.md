# Chapter 33: The Druid Priestess in Action

The "Activity Center" of Twin Rivers was a marvel of golden larch and exposed cedar beams, designed to be tanto a barracks for the Guard and a communal hall for the village. Tonight, it was a palace of light and laughter. Torch-brackets held flickering pine-knots that smelled of forest honey, and the long tables were laden with three different types of roasted fowl, mountain-herb stew, and loaves of bread so fresh they were still steaming. 

Lord Alaric sat at the head of the center table, an guest of honor in a room full of thirty Imperial Sixth cavalrymen. His men were eating with a gusto that suggested the "restless road" had left them famished. The wine was flowing, the atmosphere was celebratory, and the "surprise" of the day seemed to have settled into a comfortable, almost festive, peace. 

Until the main doors opened. 

The silence that followed was so absolute, it felt as though someone had sucked the air out of the room. A thirty-year-old veteran of the Sixth, in the middle of biting into a drumstick, simply stopped, his eyes widening as he looked toward the entrance. 

Greg walked in. 

He wasn't wearing ceremonial tunics. He was in his smith’s leathers, his massive arms bare and still smudged with coal-dust. He didn't look like a "guest of honor." He looked like a storm that had just decided where to strike. 

He didn't bow to the Governor. He didn't even look at the cavalrymen. He walked straight to the head of the room, his physical presence so overwhelming that Alaric found himself standing up without even realizing why. 

"My wife sends her apologies," Greg said, his voice a low rumble that seemed to vibrate the floorboards. "She won't be able to attend this... hospitality. So she sent me instead. I apologize for my lack of decorum."

Alaric stammered, his polished city-voice failing him for a moment. "Thank... thank you for joining us, Smith Greg. I am... I am sorry that your wife has been detained. Is she unwell?"

Greg stared at him. It wasn't a glare; it was the cold, analytical search of a predator looking at a flawed piece of meat. 

"She is well," Greg said, his words sharp as a winter frost. "She is currently in the stables. I just finished repairing twelve sets of horseshoes—shoes that were cast poorly, neglected, and allowed to bite into the hooves of your mounts. My wife, however... my wife is tending to what she calls 'Mistreatment'."

The room went cold. The cavalrymen, many of whom prided themselves on the "hard riding" that was the hallmark of the Imperial Sixth, suddenly felt the weight of Greg’s gaze. 

"She is not happy," Greg continued, his voice dropping to a dangerous, resonant frequency. "When the Lady of the Farm is not happy with the way life is treated in her valley, the valley has a way of becoming... inhospitable. You might want to consider that as you finish your meals. I hope you find the stew to your liking."

Without another word, Greg turned and left, his heavy boots echoing like drumbeats against the larch-wood until the doors swung shut behind him. 

***

Alaric didn't finish his meal. Neither did the Captain of the Escort. 

Ten minutes later, the Governor and a handful of his officers were standing at the entrance to the new stables behind the Temple. They expected to see a woman shouting at a groom, or perhaps a frantic effort to clean the stalls. 

What they saw instead was a scene that defied every military manual Alaric had ever read. 

The thirty-four horses—beasts of the Imperial Sixth, bred for war and known for their skittish, aggressive temperaments—were standing in a line of absolute, blissful silence. There was no whinnying. No stomping. 

Clara was in the center of the stable-aisle, her hands resting on the neck of Alaric’s own obsidian stallion. She wasn't using a brush. She wasn't using a sedative. Her hands were glowing with that soft, translucent "Verdant Pulse," the same light she had used to mend the boys' wounds in the Crag-Shadows. 

She turned as Alaric approached, and for the first time, the Governor saw the "Priestess" in her true form. 

Her eyes weren't bright with hospitality. They were dark with a quiet, maternal fury. 

"Fifteen of them have thrush in their hooves," Clara said, her voice a whip-crack in the silent stable. "Ten have saddle-sores that have been ignored for a month. And your stallion, My Lord... your stallion has a cracked rib from a fall that your groom decided wasn't 'worth the delay'."

"I... I had no idea," Alaric said, stepping forward. 

"Then you should have," Clara snapped. She didn't curtsy. She didn't bow. She walked toward him, the "Healing Glow" on her hands flickering with a rhythmic, pulsing energy. "These animals brought you through the shadows of the peaks. They carried your men safely while my husband and his boys kept the road clear. And you treated them like... equipment."

She reached out and touched the breastplate of Alaric’s silver armor. He felt a sudden, sharp jolt of heat—not a burn, but a "Correction" that made his very core ache. 

"A town guard protects the people," Clara said, her voice dropping to a whisper. "A Druid Priestess protects the life. If you want to stay in my valley, Lord Alaric, you will learn to respect the life that serves you. Or you will find that the road back to Oakhaven is much, much longer than the road here."

She turned back to the stallion, her hands returning to their mending work. The horse let out a long, shuddering breath, its muscles relaxing under her touch as if it were being born again. 

"Greg has fixed the steel," Clara said without looking back. "I am fixing the spirit. But don't expect a 'Welcome' banner tomorrow morning unless your men spend the night in these stalls, mending what they broke."

Alaric looked at the Captain of the Escort. The Captain looked at the horses, then at the "Priestess" who seemed to be communicating with the very soul of the Imperial Sixth’s cavalry. 

"We'll... we'll begin the mending immediately," the Captain whispered, his pride in his "Sixth" status entirely gone. 

Alaric walked back toward the Temple in the moonlight. He had come to establish dominance. He had come to prove his power. 

Instead, he had been found wanting by a Smith who saw through his decorum and a Priestess who could command the very breath of his mounts. 

He looked toward the ridge, where the dark silhouette of Greg’s house was visible against the stars. He realized then that Twin Rivers wasn't a town he could rule. It was a place he had to earn the right to exist in. 

"The Smith fixed the steel," Alaric repeated to himself, the "Correction" in his chest still pulsing. "And the Priestess is fixing the soul."

He decided then that he would not be sleeping in his luxury suite tonight. He would be in the stables, holding the water-bucket for his horse. 

Because for the first time in his life, Lord Alaric was truly, profoundly, "Not Nice." 

And he didn't want to find out what happened when the Lady of the Farm decided to balance the scale.
