# Chapter 34: Extended stay

The bells of the new Temple didn't ring for the 3:00 AM watch, but the silence of the night was equally heavy. In the stables behind the "Priestess’s Hearth," the only sound was the rhythmic *hiss-scuff* of brushes against obsidian hide and the occasional, weary grunt of a man who had forgotten how much work it took to actually love a horse. 

"Does she have to glare like that?" one of the younger guards—the same "idiot" who had been boasting about his "hard riding" earlier—whispered to his companion. 

He didn't see Clara. He was facing the flank of a grey mare, but he felt the "Verdant Pulse" in the air sharpen into a cold, focused intent. 

"Shut up, you fool!" his sergeant hissed, his own hands white from scrubbing stone-grit out of a fetlock. "Keep brushing! Do you want her to give you a 'Correction' too?"

Clara was standing at the edge of the stable-light, her shawl pulled tight, her eyes not just watching their hands, but watching the spirits of the animals. She didn't say a word, but her presence was a physical weight that kept the thirty "elite" cavalrymen of the Sixth anchored to their tasks. 

Finally, as the first hint of grey dawn touched the high peaks of the Crag-Shadows, Clara exhaled, a long, misty breath that seemed to carry the tension of the night away. 

"That will do, gentlemen," she said, her voice cool and crisp. "The horses have seen enough of your clumsy hands for one night. Go to the Inn. Be back after breakfast."

The whispering guard let out a long, audible sigh of relief. "Finally going home," he muttered, his shoulders slumping. 

Clara stopped, her hand resting on the latch of the stable door. She looked back at him, her smile small and incredibly dangerous. 

"After feeding and grooming your horses, soldier, you will be joining the Twin Rivers Guard for 'Joint Training'. I’m sure you’ll find the 'Lake-Running' to be much more invigorating than a city-scrawl."

With that, she vanished into the morning mists, leaving thirty terrified men staring at the empty doorway. 

***

Lord Alaric stood on the balcony of his "Governor’s Suite," watching the sunrise bleed across the valley. He had intended to be on the road back to Oakhaven by now, his "dominance" established and his point made. 

Instead, he was stiff, his own shoulders aching from the two hours he had spent scrubbing his stallion’s cracked rib-area under Clara’s silent judgment. 

"Sir?" 

Silas appeared at the base of the balcony, his massive arms crossed over his chest. He looked like he hadn't slept either, but he bore the exhaustion with the casual dignity of a man who owned the very ground he stood on. 

"The Lady of the Farm says your horses need rest," Silas explained, his voice a low, echoing rumble. "Three days. If she is satisfied with their health and their 'spirit' by the third sunrise, she’ll allow the gates to be opened for your departure. Provided, of course, the boys don't break you in the meantime."

Alaric gripped the wooden railing. "Three days? Silas, I am the District Governor! I have reports to file! I have—"

"You have thirty horses that can't walk ten miles without throwing a shoe or collapsing from a sore," Silas interrupted, his tone devoid of malice but full of an immovable reality. "If you leave now, you’ll be walking back to Oakhaven. And I suspect the 'Dark Flame' might find that... interesting."

Alaric looked toward the northern cliffs, where the shadows of Snake’s patrol were already moving. He sighed, a sound of total, tired surrender. 

"Three days," Alaric whispered. 

***

The next morning, the "Joint Training" began. 

The Imperial Sixth emerged from the stables, their polished silver breastplates looking a bit duller in the early light, their eyes red-rimmed from the night shift. They expected a light drill, perhaps a bit of spear-work or a "display" for the Governor. 

Instead, they were met by the twenty-two members of the Twin Rivers Guard, dressed in their "Working Gear"—heavy bull-hide gambesons and iron-ringed shields that looked like they had been through a volcanic eruption. 

Tyler stepped forward, his forest-green tunic impeccable, his iron-tipped ash spear held at a perfect vertical. Beside him stood Marcus and Petey, their faces masks of calm, focused discipline. 

"We practice in full gear," Tyler announced, his voice carrying the authority of a veteran commander. "Imperial Sixth, line up behind my two lead squads. We begin with the 'Twice-Lake' run. Gear up. We begin in thirty minutes."

He then turned his gaze toward the porch of the Activity Center, where Lord Alaric was standing, still trying to look like a dignitary despite the straw on his boots. 

"Governor Alaric," Tyler called out, his tone purely professional. "As the ranking military official of the District, you are to join me as co-commander for the day’s activities. You’ll be leading the Third Squad. We wouldn't want you to miss the... nuances... of our tactical doctrine."

Alaric stared at the teenager. He looked at the twenty-two "farm boys" who were already starting their "Breathing Meditations," their golden sparks beginning to hum in a low, unified vibration that shifted the very air in the square. 

He realized that Tyler wasn't "honoring" him with the title. He was "conscripting" him. By making him a "co-commander," Tyler was ensuring that Alaric saw the Guard’s power from the inside out. He wanted the Governor to know exactly what he was up against. 

"Full gear?" the 'idiot' guard whispered, looking at his heavy plate-mail. "The Lake-Run? He’s joking, right?"

"Thirty minutes," Tyler reminded him, his eyes flicking toward the Great Oak. 

Alaric sighed and walked toward his obsidian carriage, reaching for the heavy field-armor he usually only wore for triumphs and parade days. 

"Get your gear, men," the Governor commanded, his voice holding a trace of the "Correction" Clara had given him. "We have a 'nuance' to learn."

As the Twin Rivers Guard and the Imperial Sixth began their march toward the Silverun—the boys moving with a rhythmic, effortless "Shadow-Step" while the cavalrymen clanked and cursed through the mud—Greg watched from the ridge. 

He was holding a seed-drill he had just finished fixing. He looked at his wife, who was standing at the Temple steps, her hands glowing with a soft, restorative light. 

"An 'Extended Stay'," Greg muttered to himself, a small, genuine laugh escaping him. "I think the Governor’s about to find out that the 'Price of Peace' is a lot of running."

"He needs it, Greg," Clara said, walking up the ridge to join him. "His spirit is as cracked as his stallion’s ribs. We can't have a Governor who doesn't know how to breathe."

"I hope he survives the lake," Greg said, leaning his drill against the cedar wall of their home. 

"Oh, he’ll survive," Clara promised. "But he might not be able to walk back to the Inn for a week."

Twin Rivers was longer just about survival. It was about education. And as the "Unbreakable Guard" led the "Elite Legion" into the freezing waters of the Silverun, the "Joint Training" of Twin Rivers began in earnest.
