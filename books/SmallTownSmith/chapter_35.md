# Chapter 35: An Extended, Extended stay

Lord Alaric, Governor of the Oakhaven District, sat in his obsidian carriage on the eve of his theoretical departure, dreaming of the smooth, paved roads of the city. He had spent three days in "Joint Training." He had run into the freezing Silverun until his lungs burned, he had carried an iron-ringed shield until his shoulder was a single, throbbing bruise, and he had slept on a cedar-down bed that—while comfortable—was not his own. 

He was already dreaming of hitting the road as soon as the sun rose on the fourth day. 

"You want *what*?" Alaric exclaimed, his voice cracking as his Lieutenant entered the carriage. 

The Lieutenant, a man who had usually spent his evenings in Oakhaven complaining about the lack of fine wine, stood at a rigid imperial attention. His silver breastplate was polished, but his eyes held a strange, new clarity. 

"The men, My Lord," the Lieutenant reported, his voice steady. "They... they want to stay the week. They’ve held a vote."

"A vote?" Alaric repeated, the word sounding like a heresy. "In the Imperial Sixth? Since when does a cavalry company vote on their departure date?"

"Since Timothy, My Lord," the Lieutenant said. "The... the younger one. The one you called the 'idiot' guard. He spoke for the men. He said, and I quote: 'I have learned more in three days about my horse, my breath, and my own soul than I have in three years of legion-drills.' He says the 'Lake-Running' has cleared his head, and he doesn't want to leave until he can hold the 'Golden Glow' for more than a minute."

Alaric just stared at his Lieutenant. He wanted to rage. He wanted to command them into their saddles. But as he looked at the man, he realized that he, too, was standing straighter. His own breath was deeper. The "Correction" Clara had given him in the stables hadn't just mended his horse’s rib; it had mended something in his own cynical, bureaucratic heart. 

He reached up and brushed off his tunic of invisible dust—a habit of the city he couldn't quite break—and then stood up. 

"Very well," Alaric said, his voice holding a trace of unexpected dignity. "If the Sixth wants to learn, I will not be the one to keep them in the dark. We will stay the week. But we will not be guests. We will earn our keep."

He looked through the window toward the ridge, where the green fields of the mountain-slope were glowing in the twilight. 

"Lieutenant, please put in a formal request," Alaric commanded. "Send word to the... to the Farmer-Smith-Husband of the Terrifying-Priestess-Druid. Tell him that the Governor and his men would like to see his 'Farm' and offer our labor in any way he finds appropriate."

The Lieutenant blinked. "The Farm, My Lord?"

"Yes," Alaric said, the word feeling heavy in his mouth. "The capital-F Farm. I suspect we haven't seen the half of what’s happening in this valley."

***

The request was accepted, but the terms were clear. 

When Clara met them at the gatehouse the next morning, she didn't have her mischievous "Welcome" smile. She was standing at the threshold of the ridge-path, her hands clasped behind her back, her posture that of a guardian who was deciding whether to allow an infection into a healthy body. 

"The Farmer-Smith-Husband says you may enter," Clara said, her voice a low, echoing resonance. "But the Farm is a sanctuary, Lord Alaric. It is not property. It is not an experiment. When you cross this line, you are guests of Greg. Tread lightly. The soil has a long memory."

Alaric nodded, his men falling into a silent, two-by-two line behind him. They had traded their silver lace for working tunics, and their lances for empty baskets. 

As they crested the ridge, the "what the hell" feeling returned, but this time, it was laced with a profound, spiritual awe. 

It was a Farm, but not the way Oakhaven understood the word. It was a masterpiece of "Verdant Pulse" engineering. The rows of barley weren't just straight; they were pulsing with a green vitality that seemed to hum in a low, harmonious frequency. The fruit trees were heavy with blossoms that smelled like liquid summer, and the air itself felt "thick" with a restorative heat that made the men’s training-bruises stop aching the moment they crossed the perimeter. 

Greg was in the center of the field, holding a hoe that looked like it had been forged from a piece of the sun. He wasn't wearing his smith’s leathers. He was in a simple linen shirt, his bare feet sinking into the rich, dark soil. 

He didn't look like a "Dragon" today. He looked like a master of life. 

"You want to help?" Greg asked, his voice carrying across the quiet field without effort. 

"We want to learn," Alaric corrected, stepping into the soil beside Greg. "My men say you have a 'nuance' here that we lack."

Greg looked at the thirty imperial soldiers. He saw their fatigue, but he also saw the "Spark" that Tyler and his boys had kindled in them during the lake-runs. 

"Fine," Greg said. "Pick up the baskets. The early potatoes need to be moved to the root-cellar. But don't just dig. Listen to the plant. If you feel it resist, stop. You don't force the earth. You negotiate with it."

For the next four hours, the Imperial Sixth—the "Elite Legion" of the North—became farmhands. They dug through the soft, warm soil, their hands becoming caked in the same earth as the village boys. They found that "negotiating" with a potato was harder than parrying a spear; it required a level of focus and gentleness that none of them had ever practiced. 

Timothy, the "idiot" guard, was the first to find the rhythm. He was kneeling in a row of greens, his hands glowing with a faint, tentative warmth. 

"I can feel it," Timothy whispered, his eyes wide as he looked at a head of cabbage. "I can feel the... the hunger of the root."

"Feed it your breath, Timothy," Greg’s voice drifted over from the next row. "Don't push. Just give it the warmth."

Alaric, working beside Greg, found himself digging with a rhythmic, quiet intensity. He realized that this wasn't "Joint Training." It was a trial. Greg was showing them that the power they sought—the "Golden Spark" of the Guard—wasn't a military technique. It was a way of being. 

"You're a strange man, Smith Greg," Alaric said, wiping his brow. 

"I'm a farmer," Greg replied. "And a husband. That’s enough for any man."

He looked toward the porch, where Clara was sitting with a basket of strawberries, watching the "Elite Legion" labor in her husband’s fields. 

"She’s not terrifying, Alaric," Greg said softly. "She’s just... she’s the one who reminds us what we're actually protecting. If you learn that, you’ll be a better Governor than any man in the Capital."

As the sun reached its zenith and the "Extended, Extended" stay began in earnest, the Farm on the ridge became the most unique military camp in the Empire. Thirty soldiers, a Governor, and a legendary Smith, all working the soil in a silent, unified "Verdant Pulse."

The "Price of Peace" had evolved again. It was no longer about gold or walls. 

It was about the harvest. 

And as Timothy laughed, holding up a potato the size of a man’s head, Greg knew that the "nuance" had finally taken root. 

Twin Rivers wasn't just a town anymore. It was an idea. 

And as Lord Alaric looked at his dirt-stained hands and felt the "Golden Glow" in his own chest, he realized that he wouldn't just be going home a guest. 

He would be going home a believer.
