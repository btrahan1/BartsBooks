# Chapter 36: The Final Message

The Eighth Day had arrived, and with it, a sunrise that seemed to hold its breath. 

In the square of Twin Rivers, the atmosphere was no longer the festive, chaotic energy of the homecoming parade. It was the heavy, focused stillness of a transformation complete. Lord Alaric and his thirty men of the Imperial Sixth stood at a rigid attention, their horses groomed to a mirror-shine, their armor polished, and their spirits... quiet. 

They weren't standing before a Governor. They weren't standing before a General. They were standing before Greg. 

Greg was dressed in his simple smith’s leathers—clean but worn, without a shield on his back or a spear in his hand. He looked like exactly what he claimed to be: an apprentice smith who had found his hearth. But as he looked at the thirty elite cavalrymen, the men found themselves struggling not to bow. It wasn't the fear of his strength anymore; it was the weight of his presence. 

"Thank you for staying with us this past week," Greg said, his voice quiet but carrying to every corner of the square. "Your labor was... appreciated. And your desire to learn was noted."

He gestured toward the thirty-four horses—beasts that had arrived a week ago as "equipment" and now stood as partners. 

"My wife is well pleased with your mounts," Greg continued, a small, genuine warmth touching his eyes. "They are strong, their spirits are healthy, and they will take you wherever you need to go. Each of you will find a kit on your saddle—a brush of mountain larch, a steel cleaning pick I forged myself, and a handful of apples from the ridge. My wife says that you know what to do with them."

The soldiers looked at the kits, then at each other. They did know what to do with them. They had spent seven nights in the stalls, learning that a horse was a living thing that reflected the soul of its rider. The "idiot" Timothy reached out and patted the neck of his mare, his hand steady and full of the "Verdant Pulse" he had begun to find. 

The Twin Rivers Guard stood in their own formation at the perimeter of the square. There were thirty-two of them now—the twenty-two veterans of the Crag-Shadow War and the ten recruits who had spent the week drilling under Silas’s eye. They stood like a wall of forest-green, their spears held in a vertical salute. The "Dark Patrol"—Snake and his shadows—remained hidden in the cliffs above, but everyone in the square could feel their cold, vigilant presence. 

"I am sure you had certain ideas about Twin Rivers before you arrived," Greg said, beginning to pace back and forth between the Governor and his men. "You came here as a show of strength—a reminder that the Empire has teeth. You leave with something else."

He stopped in front of Alaric, his gaze locking onto the Governor’s eyes. 

"You leave with EARNED strength," Greg said, the words ringing like a hammer on an anvil. "You have felt the lake in your lungs. You have felt the soil in your hands. You have seen the 'Spark' that lives within my men. Do not mistake the source of that power."

Greg continued his pace, his voice dropping to a low, prophetic frequency. 

"We are strong because we protect what is ours," Greg said. "We will never be a militia. we will never be an invading force. We have no interest in Oakhaven’s throne or the Capital’s glory. We are the mountain... and a mountain does not move unless it is broken. We are the stone on which invaders will break themselves."

Alaric listened, the "Correction" in his chest vibrating in sympathy with Greg’s words. He realized that this wasn't just a farewell; it was a treaty. Greg was defining the terms of his existence. He was telling the Empire: *Leave us in peace, and we will be your greatest asset. Try to own us, and you will find only your own destruction.*

"Go back to Oakhaven," Greg said, stopping one final time at the center of the square. "Tell the Governor and the Inspector that Twin Rivers is content. Our harvest is rich, our wall is solid, and our Priestess is mending what was torn. Use the strength you’ve earned to protect your own district. Be the men your horses think you are."

With that, Greg turned his back on the thirty elite cavalrymen and walked away. He didn't look back to see them mount. He didn't look back to see them ride through the Oakhaven Gate. 

***

As the Imperial Sixth clattered through the "Peace-Wall" and onto the North Road, the silence of the valley returned. 

Alaric looked back one final time as the ten-foot basalt wall faded into the distance. High on the cliffs, he saw a single, dark silhouette—a flash of a smoky essence that vanished into the stone. It was a salute from the Dark Flame, a final acknowledgment between warriors. 

Alaric reached into his saddle-kit and pulled out an apple. He didn't eat it. He held it out to his obsidian stallion, who took it with a gentle, trustful nudge. 

"The mountain on which they break themselves," Alaric whispered, looking at his dirt-stained, calloused hands. 

He wasn't the same Governor who had arrived a week ago. He was a man who knew how to breathe. 

Back in Twin Rivers, Greg stood in the door of his smithy. The fire was already roaring, the sound of the bellows a steady, rhythmic heartbeat. 

"They're gone, Greg," Clara said, walking into the forge and leaning her head against his shoulder. "The horses went home happy."

"I hope the men did, too," Greg said, looking at the glowing iron in the hearth. "The Empire is a large machine, Clara. It takes a lot of fuel to keep it moving. But for now... the North is quiet."

He reached for his hammer, the weight of it familiar and grounding. 

"The message was delivered," Greg said. "Now, we have to make sure the harvest is ready for the winter. I suspect we'll have more than thirty guests next time."

"Then we’ll just have to plant more radishes," Clara said, her laugh echoing through the forge. 

The "Price of Peace" had been paid in labor, heart, and the transformation of a Governor. Twin Rivers was no longer just a village; it was a legend in the making. 

And as the Smith’s hammer fell on the iron, the sound was no longer a call to war. 

It was the song of home.
