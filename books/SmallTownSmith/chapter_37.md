# Chapter 37: The Restless Spark

The harvest had been brought in, the snows were just beginning to dust the peaks of the Crag-Shadows, and for all intents and purposes, Twin Rivers was at peace. 

But Greg was not. 

Clara stood on the wide porch of their house on the ridge, her arms crossed against the autumn chill, watching her husband with a mixture of amusement and genuine concern. Greg was currently in the pasture, a place where he spent an alarming amount of time these days. He wasn't harvesting. He wasn't building. He was just... touching things. 

He had started with the soil, which Clara understood. But lately, it had escalated. He had infused the field-stone walls around the perimeter until they hummed with a low, vibrating warmth that kept the snow from settling. He had infused the cedar barn. He had infused the wheat in the silo. Earlier that morning, Clara swore she had seen him standing in the coop, pressing his hands against a very confused flock of chickens, muttering something about "reinforced eggshells."

The breaking point, however, had arrived an hour ago when he brought home her "Autumn Gift."

It was a cast-iron skillet. 

To the untrained eye, it looked like a normal, albeit exceptionally well-crafted, piece of kitchenware. But when Clara had reached out to take it, she had felt the sheer, ridiculous intensity of the object. The iron was so deeply saturated with the "Verdant Pulse" that it felt like it had its own heartbeat. 

"What am I supposed to cook with this!?" Clara had exclaimed, staring at the skillet as if he had just handed her a loaded imperial siege-ballista. She could feel the lingering heat from the forge, not physical fire, but the raw, spiritual intensity Greg had poured into the metal. 

Greg had smiled, a sheepish, almost apologetic expression that looked entirely out of place on a man built like a mountain. 

"Well, pretty much anything you want," Greg had offered, scratching the back of his neck. "It’ll hold heat perfectly. It should never rust. And the food should taste... well, it should taste very vibrant. Just... just don't ever, ever hit me with it, Clara. I might not survive."

She had laughed, setting the terrifyingly powerful skillet down on the porch table. 

Now, standing on the porch, she called out to him. "Greg! Up here!"

Greg patted the flank of a dairy cow—which let out a deeply contented, Ki-infused *moo*—and walked up the slope to the house. He looked exhausted, but not from labor. He looked like a man trying to hold back a river with his bare hands. 

"Greg, seriously, what are you doing?" Clara asked, reaching out to wipe a smudge of dirt from his cheek. "Why are you so restless? You are like an energy source trying to supply the entire town!"

Greg’s shoulders slumped. He looked away, his eyes scanning the horizon where the dark peaks met the grey sky. 

"I... I have to," Greg said, his voice carrying a rare edge of desperation. "I have nowhere to put it, Clara. Ever since the fight with Thorne, ever since I had to reach back into the fire to show Tyler what he was up against... the door won't close. The Ki keeps building."

"What about yourself?" Clara asked gently. "Tyler internalizes his spark. Why can't you?"

"No!" Greg snapped, his voice louder than he intended. He immediately winced, taking her hand. "I’m sorry. But... no. I don't *want* to grow stronger. I don't want to be *that* person again."

He looked down at his massive, calloused hands. 

"I spent twenty years filling my core until I was a walking weapon," Greg whispered, the ghosts of the Imperial Legions haunting his eyes. "I became something that made people stop breathing when I walked into a room. I don't want to have people afraid of me, Clara. I try so hard to keep it hidden, to keep it inside... but it’s too much! If I absorb this energy, I won't just be the Smith. I’ll be the Dragon again."

Clara looked at him. She saw the profound, beautiful tragedy of a man who possessed enough power to rule the world, but who only wanted to be a farmer. He was literally infusing chickens and cookware just to keep himself from becoming a warlord. 

She smiled, a slow, radiant expression that chased the ghosts from the porch. She grabbed his hand, her "Healing Glow" flaring for a brief second to soothe the frantic, restless energy radiating from his skin. 

"Gregory," Clara said, her voice dropping to a low, intimate murmur. "If you really want to 'infuse' something... if you have so much 'essence' that you're turning my kitchenware into legendary weapons... then I think it's time you put all of it to good use."

Greg blinked, his mind trying to catch up with the sudden shift in her tone. "Good use? Like... a new plow?"

"I want a baby," Clara said. 

Silence descended on the ridge. The wind seemed to stop. In the pasture below, even the Ki-infused cow paused its chewing. 

Greg’s mouth fell open, his jaw unhinged in an expression of total, complete shock. The legendary "Shield of the North," the man who had faced down charging cavalry without blinking, stood agape, utterly defenseless. 

"Oh," Greg managed to squeak out, his voice suddenly sounding an octave higher. "...okay."

Clara didn't wait for his brain to fully process the command. With a laugh that rang clear and bright across the autumn air, she pulled him by the hand, leading the most dangerous man in the Empire through the front door of their home. 

The door clicked shut. 

In the pasture, the cow let out another contented *moo*. On the porch table, the legendary cast-iron skillet hummed quietly to itself. The restless spark of Twin Rivers had finally found its true focus. 

Act 3 had officially begun.
