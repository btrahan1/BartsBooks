# Chapter 38: 1 week later

The village of Twin Rivers was accustomed to the rhythmic certainty of its days. The sun rose, the lake shimmered, the forge rang with Silas’s hammer, and Greg the Smith was usually somewhere visible—either tending the forge or working the impossible soil of the ridge. 

But it had been an entire week. 

Seven full days had passed without a single sighting of Greg or Clara. The "Peace-Wall" remained closed to merchants out of a creeping, communal anxiety. Even the Guard had suspended their morning drills, the boys standing around the village square casting nervous glances up toward the "capital-F Farm." The forge was quiet; Silas had spent most of the week pacing the floor, occasionally muttering about whether he should grab a spear and march up the hill to check on his sister.

"You don't think... the Empire sent an assassin, do you?" Petey whispered to Marcus on the seventh morning, his iron-ringed shield held tightly against his chest.

"Assassin?" Marcus scoffed, though his voice lacked conviction. "Greg would have forged the assassin into a plowshare before breakfast. But... something’s wrong. It’s too quiet up there."

Finally, just as Tyler was preparing an official scouting party to breach the ridge perimeter, a movement caught his eye. 

The heavy cedar door of the Farm house opened, and two silhouettes emerged into the crisp autumn air. The collective sigh of relief that swept through the village square could have ruffled the leaves on the Great Oak. 

They were walking down the winding dirt path toward the town, hand in hand. 

But as they drew closer to the village gates, the relief in the crowd rapidly morphed into a baffled, wide-eyed astonishment. The Guard, the miners, and the weavers began to crowd the entrance, their whispers carrying over the cold mountain breeze. 

"Tell me I'm not seeing what I think I see," one of the older miners whispered, rubbing his soot-stained eyes. 

"You mean, the grass that is growing underneath her feet every time she takes a step?" his wife replied, her voice hushed in genuine awe. 

It was true. The autumn frost had already turned the path brown and brittle, but where Clara’s bare feet touched the earth, tiny, vibrant shoots of green clover and sweetgrass sprung up instantly. It was as if the "Verdant Pulse" of the farm had saturated her very bones. 

"Well, yeah, that," the miner admitted, staring at the fresh greenery. "But... I don't think I've ever seen Greg mostly stringy and... a) Tired, or b) Smile. Much less both at the same time."

Greg looked like a man who had been hit by a mountain and had thoroughly enjoyed the experience. The dark, brooding intensity that usually haunted his eyes was entirely gone. His shoulders were relaxed. His massive chest rose and fell with a calm, peaceful rhythm. But what truly unnerved the village was the expression on his face—a broad, exhausted, completely goofy smile that made him look a decade younger. 

Clara, on the other hand, looked like the sun itself. 

She wore a simple blue tunic, but the fabric seemed to catch light that wasn't there. Her hair fell in loose, vibrant waves, and her eyes held a mischievous, radiant energy that made the very air around her feel thick and warm. 

They reached the gates, the villagers parting for them like water before the prow of a ship. 

Clara stopped, turning to Greg and giving his hand a final, affectionate squeeze. 

"Go say hi to Silas, Greg," Clara beamed, her voice ringing with an undeniable, vibrant joy. "I'll be doing some shopping. I think it’s time we started looking for... smaller blankets."

Greg nodded, his smile widening if that were even possible. "Whatever you need, Clara. Take the heavy silver pouch."

As Clara walked away toward the market stalls, her steps leaving a faint trail of spring in the middle of autumn, a hushed murmur rippled through the onlookers. 

"Wait," Petey whispered, his eyes wide. "Is she... glowing?"

"She’s a Druid Priestess," Marcus hissed back, though he looked equally stunned. "I suppose glowing is just... part of the job description?"

Tyler watched her go, his golden Ki-sight picking up something that the naked eye couldn't quite grasp. Clara wasn't just glowing. There was a second, tiny spark resonating within her—a pinpoint of light that held the absolute, terrifying density of a star, wrapped in the gentle, nurturing warmth of the valley. 

Tyler swallowed hard, slowly turning to look at his Teacher. 

Greg had walked over to Silas, resting a heavy hand on the other smith’s shoulder. 

"Greg," Silas said, looking at his brother-in-law’s exhausted, happy face. "You... you look like you just fought Thorne again. But for a week."

"Silas," Greg laughed, a deep, rumbling sound that the village had never officially heard before. "Thorne was a warm-up. I assure you, my friend... I am completely, utterly drained. And I have never felt better in my entire life."

The "Restless Spark" of Twin Rivers had found its home. The walls no longer hummed dangerously. The chickens were safe. And the legendary Smith, the man who could crack mountains with his breath, was going to be a father.
