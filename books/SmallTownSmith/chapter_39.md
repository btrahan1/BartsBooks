# Chapter 39: 1 month later

"She’s definitely pregnant."

"You think?" Marcus whispered back, leaning against the wooden post of the market awning. "Go ahead. Tell her she’s showing. I’ll start digging your grave right now behind the Temple."

It was the town secret that wasn't so secret anymore. It had only been a month since the "Week of Silence," but Clara’s condition was already the primary subject of hushed conversations from the forge to the mill. It wasn't just the visible bump that was forming beneath her tunic—it was the fact that she simply seemed to glow brighter with every passing week. The "Verdant Pulse" she carried was so vibrant that the autumn frost refused to settle within ten feet of her, creating a permanent, walking pocket of springtime wherever she went. 

Across the square, two village women were watching her browse the apple stalls. 

"Well, that's not really fair, now is it?" one of the women muttered, adjusting the woven shawl around her shoulders. "Did I look like that when I was pregnant? Glowing like a noon sun and walking on fresh grass?"

Her husband, a practical miner who had learned the value of brevity, didn't even look up from the leather harness he was mending. "Yes, dear. Of course, dear."

***

Up at the forge, the heat of the fire was a welcome contrast to the November wind. Silas was hammering out a set of iron plowshares, the rhythmic *clang-clang* of his work a steady heartbeat for the village. He paused, wiping the sweat from his forehead with a soot-stained rag, as a massive shadow fell across the entrance. 

Silas stepped out from the main forge area as Greg approached. 

"Ready to get back to work, slacker?" Silas called out, his grin bright against the coal dust on his face. 

Greg just smiled. It was the same calm, peaceful smile he had worn since he and Clara had emerged from the farm, though the profound exhaustion had finally faded. He looked like a man who possessed the strength of an avalanche but was perfectly content to use it to carry groceries. 

"Indeed I am, Silas," Greg said, clapping his brother-in-law on the back with enough force to make Silas’s teeth rattle. "But not with the iron today. I need to craft some woodworking tools, my friend."

Silas raised an eyebrow. "Woodworking tools? Greg, we have the best master-carpenter tools money can buy from Oakhaven. Didn't you pick up a set during the 'Victory Lap'?"

"I did," Greg admitted, leaning against the anvil. "But I need something... different. Something sharper. Something that can cut iron-wood without splintering it. I need to draw up the designs, and I’ll need your help with the heat-treating."

Silas crossed his arms, leaning back. "What did you have in mind, Greg?"

Greg’s expression softened, a look of profound, terrified joy crossing his features. "Something with which I can build a crib, Silas. And not just any crib."

Silas grinned, a slow, understanding expression replacing his confusion. He looked up toward the ridge, where the "Restless Spark" of the Smith had been channeled into a single, miraculous pinpoint of life. 

"I see," Silas chuckled. He paused, picking up a set of heavy tongs and turning them over in his hands. "You know, you can just buy one, right? The Carpenters' Guild in Oakhaven has some fine crafters. They build cribs for the Governor's own family out of polished mahogany."

Greg paused, his golden eyes staring into the coals of the forge. He thought of the sheer, terrifying density of the Ki that Clara was currently carrying. He thought of his own "Dragon" spark and the intense, mending warmth of Clara’s "Druid" nature combining into a single vessel. 

"Pretty sure it wouldn't last a week if my guess is right, Silas," Greg said, his voice deadpan. "If that child accidentally sneezes during its first breath, an Oakhaven mahogany crib is going to turn into splinters and ash. No, it has to be iron-wood. From the deep Crags. And I need to infuse the joints with silver to absorb the excess Ki."

"A reinforced, Ki-dampening crib," Silas mused, shaking his head in amusement. "Only you, Greg. Only you would engineer a baby bed like a fortress."

"It's not to keep things out, Silas," Greg muttered. "It's to keep the house standing when the child decides it wants a midnight feeding."

Silas laughed, a booming sound that echoed off the basalt walls. "Well, let’s get the fire hotter, then. We have a fortress to build."

***

While Greg was plotting the architectural defense of their nursery, Clara was on a mission of her own. 

She walked past the fruit stalls and the bread ovens, nodding politely to the villagers who "discreetly" watched her pass. She headed straight for the small, neat shop of Madam Eloise, the town’s resident seamstress and weaver. 

The bell above the door chimed a clear, high note as Clara entered. Madam Eloise, a woman with sharp eyes and fingers stained with indigo dye, looked up from her loom. 

"Lady Clara," Eloise greeted, rising to offer a small curtsy. "We weren't expecting you today. I hope the velvet curtains are holding up?"

"The curtains are perfect, Eloise," Clara said, her voice warm but holding an undeniable edge of focused determination. "But I’m not here for the house. Are you busy?"

Eloise looked at the pile of mending on her table—mostly torn tunics from the Guard’s training sessions—and pushed it aside. "For the Priestess of Twin Rivers? I am entirely free. What do you need? Silk? Something soft for the... upcoming arrival?"

"No," Clara said flatly. She walked to the large oak table in the center of the room and placed a heavy leather sack on it. *Clink.* The sound of imperial silver echoed in the small shop. 

Eloise’s eyes widened. "My Lady... what is this?"

"I need baby clothes, Eloise," Clara explained, her glowing presence lighting up the shadowed corners of the seamstress’s shop. "But not linen. And certainly not silk."

Clara reached into the bag and pulled out a bolt of fabric she had procured during the chaotic "Shopping Spree" in Oakhaven. It wasn't cloth. It was finely woven, supple Kragh-Spider silk—a material famous on the frontier for being as soft as cotton but as resilient as boiled leather. It was incredibly rare, terrifyingly expensive, and completely inappropriate for an infant. 

"Spider-silk?" Eloise asked, her voice hitching. "Clara, this is armor-weave. The Imperial Scouts use this under their chainmail to stop bodkin arrows!"

"I know," Clara said, her determined look not wavering. "Greg is building a crib out of iron-wood. But a baby can't stay in a crib all the time. If this child inherits even a fraction of Greg’s... clumsiness... a normal linen swaddle is going to tear the first time he stretches his arms."

Eloise stared at the fabric, then at the glowing, radiant woman possessing the most formidable maternal instinct in the District. 

"You want me to make armor-weave swaddling clothes," Eloise repeated, trying to wrap her mind around the request. 

"Swaddling clothes, several tunics, and perhaps a small, reinforced hat," Clara nodded firmly. "And I have some silver thread that Greg tempered in the forge. I want it woven into the seams. It should act as a grounding wire."

"A grounding wire," Eloise said, blinking rapidly. "For a baby."

"He might be a heavy sleeper," Clara smiled, though the determined glint in her golden eyes never faded. 

Eloise finally let out a long, incredulous sigh and picked up her heavy shear-scissors. "Very well, Lady Clara. Armor-weave diapers it is. But I have to warn you, I’ll need to borrow one of your husband’s blacksmith files just to cut the fabric."

"I’ll send Silas down with an anvil," Clara beamed, turning to leave. "Thank you, Eloise! I knew I could count on you!"

As Clara stepped back out into the autumn air, the fresh grass springing up beneath her feet, the town of Twin Rivers continued its quiet, peaceful afternoon. 

But below the surface, the preparations for the "Restless Spark’s" arrival had officially begun. The Smith was forging a fortress-crib, the Seamstress was sewing armor-weave baby clothes, and the Guard was secretly taking bets on whether the child’s first cry would shatter the windows of the Temple. 

Act 3 was well underway, and Twin Rivers was doing what it did best: preparing for the impossible with a perfectly straight face.
