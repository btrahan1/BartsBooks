# Chapter 40: 3 months later

It started with the squirrels. 

At first, the townspeople thought it was just a strange quirk of a particularly mild winter. A few extra red-tails lingering near the Great Oak, a couple of snow-hares braving the edge of the market square. But as Clara entered her fourth month of pregnancy, the "quirk" escalated into a full-blown phenomenon. 

It was the animals. All of them. 

They followed Clara everywhere. When she walked from the Farm down to the Temple to hold her morning "healing hours," she was invariably accompanied by a procession that would have made a traveling circus envious. Flocks of blue-jays and mountain finches fluttered in the eaves of the buildings she entered, their songs a constant, cheerful background noise. Rabbits sat boldly on the edges of the dirt roads, unbothered by the passing carts, their noses twitching as they watched her pass.

It was just a few at first, but now, she was always accompanied by at least a dozen small woodland creatures. They didn't beg for food. They didn't cause trouble. They simply seemed content to bask in the overwhelming, nurturing "Verdant Pulse" that radiated from her frame. 

And those were just the small ones. 

As the snows deepened in the Crag-Shadows, driving the larger predators down toward the valley, the Guard prepared for the usual winter incursions. But the incursions never came. 

Instead, there were the wolves. 

It was a massive pack of Timber-Wolves, beasts the size of small ponies with fur the color of slate and eyes that glowed like cold embers. They didn't attack the livestock. They didn't threaten the villagers. They simply prowled around the edge of the "Peace-Wall," their low, resonant howls blending with the winter wind in the evenings. 

"They’re... guarding her," Petey whispered one night, standing on the western watchtower and shivering beneath his heavy wool cloak. 

He was looking down at the treeline, where the alpha of the pack—a massive, scarred beast with one silver eye—was sitting perfectly still, staring back up at the tower. 

Beside Petey, a shadow detached itself from the stonework. Snake pulled his hood back, his own eyes matching the cold, calculating intelligence of the wolf below. 

"They are," Snake agreed, his "Dark Flame" Ki thrumming in a strange, harmonious frequency with the wild predators. "We’ve made a type of peace with them. They watch the north woods. My boys and I watch the roads. They take the day shift in the deep brush, we take the night."

Petey blinked. "You... you made a treaty with a wolf pack?"

Snake shrugged, though a small smile touched his lips. "The Alpha recognizes the 'Priestess.' He knows she’s carrying a Spark that could level this mountain, and he knows she’s the one keeping the valley warm. We don't speak the same language, Petey, but we both know our jobs."

***

For the locals of Twin Rivers, the wolves became just another eccentric feature of their newly legendary town. The miners would toss a scrap of dried meat over the wall on their way home, and the wolves would accept it with a silent nod of acknowledgment. 

But for the outsiders, it was a terrifying ordeal. 

Caravans from Oakhaven still made the trek, drawn by the lucrative trade of Greg’s "tempered" iron tools and the surplus of the inexplicably thriving winter harvest. But the journey had changed. 

The merchants would approach the basalt gates, only to feel the intense, suffocating weight of being watched. They would look to the treeline and see a dozen pairs of glowing, predatory eyes tracking their every move. They would look to the cliffs and catch glimpses of Snake’s "acolyte" guards, moving through the rocks like phantoms. 

The merchants didn't linger. 

They would roll their wagons into the square, conduct their business with a frantic, sweating efficiency, and then practically sprint back to their wagons. They stayed in town for as short a time as humanly possible, often throwing extra silver onto the counter just to avoid waiting for change, before whipping their horses into a terrified gallop back toward the city. 

"Welcome to Twin Rivers," chuckled Silas one afternoon, watching a silk-merchant throw a crate of spices onto the cobblestones and flee without taking his receipt. "Be nice... or the forest will eat you."

Greg, who was busy carrying three massive, iron-wood beams toward his house for the "indestructible crib" project, just shook his head. 

"I tried to tell Clara to ask the Alpha to remain in the woods during business hours," Greg sighed, shifting the five-hundred-pound load on his shoulder as if it were a stack of kindling. "She said they were just being 'protective uncles'."

"Well, they're good for the local economy," Silas noted, picking up the silver the merchant had left behind. "Panic seems to eliminate haggling."

***

Unsurprisingly, news had traveled fast to Oakhaven, and the rumors were wild. 

In the Black-Lion Inn, heavily fortified by strong ale, the returning merchants spun tales that grew taller with every telling. 

"I swear by the Emperor’s crown," a grain-trader loudly proclaimed to a captive audience, "the Smith’s wife is carrying a demigod! She walks on air, and the very grass blooms in the middle of December!"

"That’s nothing," interrupted a blacksmith from the Guild. "I heard the Twin Rivers Guard consists entirely of werewolves! They don't even use spears anymore. They just turn into beasts and rip bandits apart with their teeth!"

"And the Temple," a third voice chimed in, leaning over his mug conspiratorially. "They say if you go to the Priestess with a severed arm, she’ll just slap a piece of glowing mud on it and a new one grows back in an hour!"

Lord Alaric, who happened to be sitting in a dark booth in the corner of the tavern, listened to the rumors with a quiet, knowing smirk. 

His lieutenant, sitting across from him in civilian clothes, leaned forward. "Should we do something about this, My Lord? The rumors are making the city guard nervous. They think Twin Rivers is building an army of monsters."

Alaric took a slow sip of his wine. He thought of the "idiot" guard Timothy, who was currently back at the barracks, still practicing the "Breath" exercises he had learned in the valley. He thought of the cracked rib of his stallion that had healed flawlessly overnight. 

"Let them talk, Lieutenant," Alaric commanded softly. "The rumors are wild, yes. The exaggeration is ridiculous." 

He paused, looking down at his own calloused hands, remembering the soil of the capital-F Farm. 

"But the terrifying thing," Alaric murmured, "is that the core of the rumors... the magic, the wolves, the unshakeable power of that valley... is mostly true." 

He raised his glass in a silent toast toward the northern mountains. 

"Leave the 'werewolves' and the Priestess alone. Let them guard their demigod in peace. Because heaven help the fool who tries to test if the rumors are real." 

Back in Twin Rivers, the snow finally began to fall in earnest. But inside the house on the ridge, the hearth fire burned bright and warm. Clara sat in a rocking chair, knitting a tiny tunic out of silver-laced armor-weave, while a massive timber-wolf dozed peacefully on the rug at her feet. 

The valley was safe. The harvest was secure. And the 'protective uncles' were on watch. 

The baby was going to be very, very well-guarded.
