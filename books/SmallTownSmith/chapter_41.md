# Chapter 41: The wail that shook the mountains

It had been almost nine months since the initial "infusions." The frost of autumn had given way to the deep, silent freeze of late winter, but the inner warmth of Twin Rivers remained unbroken. 

Clara was definitely showing. In fact, "showing" was a polite word utilized by people who were too terrified to state the obvious. She looked like she was carrying a small boulder under her tunic. The permanent patch of spring grass that followed her footsteps had grown wider, blooming with tiny, out-of-season wildflowers even when the cobblestones were rimed with ice. 

"That baby is going to be twelve pounds, Clara," Silas murmured nervously. 

He was standing near the market well, watching Greg and Clara take a slow, deliberate walk around the village for "supplies." The town had essentially stopped working just to observe them. 

"Are you absolutely sure it's not twins?" Silas pressed, eyeing the sheer mass of her silhouette. 

Clara turned to him. The nurturing "Verdant Pulse" that usually softened her features was currently dialed up to a level that felt heavily, fiercely territorial. 

"Just the one, Silas," Clara said, her voice carrying a resonant, Mother-Earth finality. "Samuel. That's his name. And..." 

She looked him straight in the eye. The golden glow in her irises flared with the protective intensity of a she-bear. 

"...be glad you're my brother."

Silas, a man who regularly shaped red-hot iron and had faced down goblin hordes, actually took a physical step backward, raising his hands in a gesture of total surrender. "Understood. Big boy. Samuel. Got it."

As Clara turned away from her brother to head toward the Oakhaven gate, the crowd that had naturally gravitated toward the Priestess seemed to hold its collective breath. Everyone sensed that the "wait" was over. The air felt heavy, charged with the same pre-storm electricity that usually preceded one of Greg's serious combat drills. 

Clara stopped and addressed the crowd. Her fierce look dissolved back into the radiant, impossibly warm smile that had healed the Guard. 

"Thank you," Clara said, her voice projecting effortlessly across the square. "Thank you, all of you! Your love, your concern, and your hard work have been such a blessing to our valley. Samuel can't wait to meet you all!"

With that, she turned and "glided" away from the town, heading up the path toward the ridge. She moved with an eerie, frictionless grace that made it look as though the earth itself was gently pushing her forward. 

Greg didn't immediately follow. He stopped to face the crowd, looking at the thirty-two members of the Guard, the miners, and the weavers. He looked serious. Dead serious. 

"Samuel will make his presence known tonight," Greg said, his deep voice carrying the weight of a military command. "But please... do not visit the Farm for at least a week. Let the energy settle. It might not be safe."

He paused, glancing up toward the timberline where the Alpha wolf was already pacing nervously. 

"And," Greg added, shifting his weight uncomfortably. "If you hear anything... 'a bit loud' tonight... do not be startled. Stay in your homes. Keep the fires lit."

He then turned and hurried up the path, his massive strides eating up the distance as he rushed to catch his wife. 

***

That evening, the valley seemed to hold its breath. Even the Silverun river felt sluggish, as if the water itself was waiting. 

Inside the house on the ridge, the event was—in the most technical sense of the word—a mostly routine birth. If, of course, birthing a literal demigod could ever be considered "routine." 

There was no midwife. Clara had insisted that only Greg be in the room. He was the only one in the district who possessed enough raw Ki to safely absorb the ambient energy spikes that were radiating from her body. The air in the bedroom was so thick with power that the blue velvet curtains seemed to be breathing on their own, and the iron-wood fortress-crib was actually vibrating. 

As the final, intense surge of the "Verdant Pulse" peaked, Greg delivered his son himself. 

Samuel was ten pounds of flesh. He should have been mostly soft baby fat, but as Greg pulled him into the world, the child felt dense. He didn't feel delicate; he felt like a perfectly carved block of warm, living marble. 

Greg held his son. The room was deathly quiet. 

Samuel opened his eyes. They were a brilliant, piercing gold—the exact shade of the "Restless Spark" that had forged the town’s defenses. He stared his father straight in the eyes, analyzing the massive Smith with a terrifyingly ancient intelligence. 

And then, Samuel opened his mouth and WAILED. 

It wasn't a cry. It was a shockwave. 

The sound hit the walls of the house with the physical force of a battering ram. The heavy cedar beams groaned in protest. Dust drifted down from the rafters. Outside, the very earth of the Farm moaned, a deep, sympathetic vibration that echoed down into the valley, rattling the windows of the Temple and shaking the snow from the Great Oak. 

Up in the Crag-Shadows, the Alpha wolf threw his head back and howled, instantly joined by his entire pack. The wolves didn't stop howling for a full hour, answering the cry of the new apex presence in the valley. 

In the bedroom, Greg braced his legs, using his own core to absorb the brunt of the sonic force. 

Finally, the wail subsided into a series of heavy, powerful breaths. Samuel turned his head, his golden eyes locking onto his mother. 

Greg gently laid the ten-pound demigod onto Clara’s chest. 

Samuel immediately snuggled into the warmth, his dense little hands grabbing the edge of the armor-weave blanket. As he latched onto his mother's breast and began feeding, Clara’s eyes widened to the size of saucers. 

It was the most sensational thing she had ever experienced. It didn't feel like feeding a newborn. The sheer pull, the energetic demand of the child, was like trying to nourish a miniature sun. She felt the "Verdant Pulse" within her rushing to meet the demand, cycling energy at a terrifying, exhilarating rate. 

She felt like she was literally feeding an immortal. 

Clara looked up at Greg, who was leaning against the iron-wood crib, looking paler than he had during his fight with Thorne. 

"Greg," Clara gasped, her face flushing with the intense energetic transfer. "You better start cooking."

Greg blinked, his mind still slightly rattled by the sonic boom. "Cooking?" 

"Yes, cooking!" Clara said, gripping the edge of the bed as Samuel continued to feed with the intensity of a starving dragon. "Everything! I need stew! I need bread! I need all of it! I am going to need it!"

Greg didn't argue. He turned and practically bolted for the kitchen, thanking the heavens that he had infused that cast-iron skillet. 

Gregory had arrived in Twin Rivers. The mountain had shaken. The wolves had sung. And Samuel the "Restless Spark" was finally here—and he was very, very hungry.
