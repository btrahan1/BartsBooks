# Chapter 42: The morning after

The town was restless that night. 

In the village square, the miners and weavers had stayed awake for hours, clustering around the Temple’s hearth as if the fire could protect them from the shockwave that had rolled down from the ridge. Out in the deep woods, the forest itself was restless, the ancient pines shivering under the weight of an unfamiliar, overwhelmingly dense energy. The night itself seemed restless, the stars flickering with an erratic brilliance that defied the winter chill. 

But when the sun finally crested the jagged peaks of the Crag-Shadows, it was really something else. 

The entire valley came alive. 

It was late November—a time when the mountain passes were usually choked with ice and the soil was hardened into a slate-grey freeze. Yet, as the morning light hit the valley floor, the frost didn't just melt; it retreated. Within an hour, it was as fine a Spring Day as any elder in Twin Rivers had seen in decades. The air was thick with the scent of blooming clover, and the Silverun river ran clear and warm. It was surreal.

The arrival of the "Restless Spark" hadn't just woken the valley; it had bullied the seasons into submission. 

Up on the ridge, Greg stepped quietly out of the bedroom, leaving Clara asleep with the tiny, marble-dense form of Samuel still clutched to her chest. He was exhausted—having spent the night cooking massive quantities of stew, baking four loaves of bread, and frying an entire ham in the infused cast-iron skillet just to keep up with his wife's terrifying caloric demands. 

He opened the heavy, iron-wood front door, stepping out onto the porch to take a deep breath of the spring-like air. 

What he saw leaning against the supporting beam was even more surreal than the weather. 

It was a wolf. But calling it a wolf felt like a severe understatement. It was easily the size of an imperial warhorse, its fur a mix of slate-grey and midnight-black, its muscles coiled with a terrifying, primal tension. It had one silver, sightless eye and one eye that burned like a cold ember. 

It was the Alpha. 

The giant wolf looked up as the door opened, its lips curling back to reveal fangs the size of daggers. A low, rumbling growl vibrated in its chest—a sound that usually meant the immediate, violent end for whatever it was looking at. 

Greg didn't reach for a spear. He didn't tense. He simply looked down at the massive predator and smiled. 

It wasn't a gentle "Farmer" smile. It was *the* smile. The same slow, terrifying smile he had given Thorne before shattering the bandit’s fire. It was the smile of the Dragon, letting a sliver of his true, overwhelming "Golden Spark" leak into the morning air. 

The Alpha’s growl died instantly. The giant beast blinked its one good eye, its ears flattening against its skull, and let out a very high, very undignified whimper. 

"Oh, shush," Greg said softly, stepping closer to the massive animal without an ounce of fear. "I'm Greg. And as of this morning, you are now 'Wolfie'."

The giant predator, likely the most feared creature in a hundred miles, let out another soft whine, accepting the humiliating moniker with the grace of a creature that knew exactly when it was outmatched. 

"You are now on duty," Greg continued, crouching down and scratching the terrified Alpha behind its ears. "For the next ten years, at minimum. Do a good job, Wolfie, and I won't have to turn you into a rug for the nursery."

With that, Greg stood back up, adjusting his simple linen shirt, and began his morning routine. He stretched his massive shoulders, looking out over the "capital-F Farm." 

The Alpha wasn't alone. The entire perimeter of the farm was surrounded by wolves. There were dozens of them, pacing the fenceline and watching the surrounding woods with an obsessive, hyper-vigilant intensity. They didn't look like wild animals; they looked like a highly coordinated, heavily armed perimeter guard. 

Greg walked to the edge of the porch, looking out at the pack. He thought of Clara, asleep inside, having completely drained the pantry of its reserves during the night. 

"Clara is hungry!" Greg announced, his voice carrying over the pasture. 

He didn't know if they could understand the words, but they clearly understood the intent. The Alpha let out a sharp, commanding bark. Instantly, a group of five large timber-wolves broke off from the perimeter and vanished into the northern treeline, moving with a fast, lethal purpose. They were going hunting. 

Greg watched them go, folding his arms across his chest. He looked at the blooming wildflowers in the pasture, the impossibly warm sun, and the giant, one-eyed wolf that was currently sitting on his porch like a very intimidating guard dog. 

"I could get used to this," Greg smiled again, turning to head back into the house. 

Inside, Samuel let out a soft, sleepy snort. The floorboards visibly vibrated. 

"Or," Greg amended quietly to himself, looking at the ceiling, "I might not survive the week. Either way, at least I don't have to go hunting."

The "Restless Spark" was awake, and the valley of Twin Rivers would never be the same. The seasons were broken, the wildlife was conscripted, and the Smith was officially a father. 

It was going to be a very interesting Spring.
