# Chapter 43: The Governor

"I'm sorry, come again?"

Lord Alaric, Governor of the Oakhaven District, leaned across his massive oak desk, staring at the mud-splattered scout who had just barged into his private office. The boy was shivering, though whether from the cold or from sheer nervous exhaustion, Alaric couldn't tell. 

"Sp... sprin... Spring, Sir!" the scout stammered, his teeth chattering audibly in the draughty stone hall of the Governor's keep. 

"Yes, I heard that part perfectly," Alaric said, rubbing his temples in a desperate bid to stave off an impending headache. He stood up and pointed out the high, arched window to the city below. The streets of Oakhaven were buried under a foot of hard-packed December snow, and the sky was the color of a bruised plum. "What do you mean, *Spring*?"

The scout swallowed hard, his eyes wide with a frantic, desperate sincerity. "It's Spring in Twin Rivers, sir! Like... like sun, and flowers, and fresh grass, and birds singing! And... well... Spring! The snow line just... stops at their 'Peace-Wall.' The river isn't even iced over!"

Alaric slowly sank back into his high-backed leather chair. 

He had already heard the news of the birth. Or rather, he had felt it. Two nights ago, Alaric had been awake, reviewing taxation ledgers by candlelight, when the heavy oak desk had suddenly vibrated. It had felt like the earth itself had let out a deep, resonant groan. The tremor had rattled the glassware in the cupboards and set the hounds in the city kennels to howling. 

It was impossible that the localized earthquake in Oakhaven was related to the birth of a child in a mountain village thirty miles away. Surely. 

And yet, Alaric found himself staring at the scout, remembering the impossibly dense "Verdant Pulse" of the Smith's farm and the terrifying, healing glare of the Priestess. 

"The Smith had a son," Alaric murmured to himself, tapping his signet ring against the desk. "A son with his fire and her life-force. That explains the tremor. And now... he's apparently bullying the weather into submission."

The scout nodded enthusiastically, grateful his report wasn't being dismissed as frost-madness. "Yes, Sir! The merchants are calling him the 'Mountain-Shaker.' They say the wolves brought the mother an elk for breakfast!"

Alaric rubbed his temples harder. Twin Rivers was becoming a logistical nightmare for his sanity. It was one thing to have a highly trained, elite Guard operating within his borders. It was quite another to have a family that could rewrite the laws of nature on a whim. 

"This is unprecedented," Alaric muttered, standing up and reaching for his heavy fur-lined cloak hanging on the wall. "If they've genuinely altered the seasonal climate, the Imperial Scholars in the Capital are going to hear about it. We should visit, then! We need to assess the situation before this gets out of control."

"NO!" 

The shout was so loud, so devoid of imperial decorum, that Alaric actually dropped his cloak. 

He spun around to see his lieutenant—the hardened veteran of the Imperial Sixth who had spent a week pulling weeds on Greg’s farm—standing in the doorway of the office, his face pale and his eyes wide with genuine panic. 

Alaric stared at him. "Sorry, what did you say, Lieutenant?"

"No visitors for a month, sir! That's what I heard!" the lieutenant blurted out, stepping into the room and practically blocking the door with his body. "The merchants coming back said the Smith gave the order before the birth. He specifically asked that no one visit the Farm because it 'might not be safe.' And then the mountain shook, My Lord! The mountain *shook*!"

"Lieutenant," Alaric said firmly, trying to regain his composure. "I am the Governor. I do not take orders regarding visiting hours from a local blacksmith."

"Sir, with all due respect," the lieutenant pleaded, stepping closer to the desk. "Remember the horse stalls? Remember the 'Joint Training' in the freezing lake? If the man who turned thirty elite cavalrymen into farmhands says that his house is currently 'not safe,' we do not knock on his door!"

Alaric paused, the memory of his cracked ribs and the horrifying realization that he could never defeat the Smith washing over him. He thought of the impossible grass growing in the snow. He thought of the wolves. 

"They say the baby is made of marble, My Lord," the scout added helpfully from the corner. 

"Enough," Alaric sighed, raising a hand to silence them both. He walked back to his desk and slumped into his chair, the fur cloak forgotten on the floor. 

It was a humiliating reality for an imperial official to accept, but Twin Rivers was no longer under his jurisdiction. It existed in a pocket of reality entirely dictated by a man who forged invincible steel and a woman who commanded the seasons. And now, they had a son. 

"Very well," Alaric conceded, his voice heavy with resignation. "We will honor the 'request' of the Smith. No official delegations to Twin Rivers for a month."

The lieutenant sagged against the doorframe, letting out a massive breath of relief. "A wise decision, My Lord. Extremely wise."

"However," Alaric added, pulling a fresh sheet of parchment toward him and dipping his quill in the inkwell. "We must still manage the rumors. Lieutenant, double the watch on the North Road. If any 'zealous' imperial officials or curious scholars from the Capital come through seeking the 'Mountain-Shaker,' turn them back. Tell them the pass is blocked by an avalanche. Tell them there's a plague. Tell them whatever you have to."

"I’ll tell them the wolves will eat their horses," the lieutenant suggested, a grim smile returning to his face. "It has the benefit of being completely true."

"Do it," Alaric nodded, already writing orders to quarantine the rumors. "We protect Twin Rivers from the Capital, and Twin Rivers protects us from the frontier. That is the arrangement."

As the lieutenant and the scout left the office, Alaric looked out the window one last time toward the jagged, snow-capped peaks of the Crag-Shadows. 

"Spring in December," Alaric whispered to the empty room, a small, involuntary smile touching his lips. "I don't know what you’re building up there, Smith. But heaven help the man who tries to knock it down." 

He went back to his ledgers, content to let the Demigod of Twin Rivers enjoy his first month of Spring in peace. After all, a month was a very short time. And Alaric had a feeling that when the gates finally reopened, the world was going to be very, very different.
