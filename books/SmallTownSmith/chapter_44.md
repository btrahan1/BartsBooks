# Chapter 44: Tribute

It was exactly thirty days before the trio finally showed up in town. 

In that single month of isolation on the ridge, Samuel did not grow like a weed. Weeds were fragile. Weeds could be pulled. Samuel grew like a mountain oak taking root in volcanic soil. 

At four weeks old, he was already crawling with terrifying speed, his dense, twenty-five-pound frame leaving shallow divots in the dirt of the pasture. By week five, he was pulling himself up on the heavy cedar furniture. And despite Greg’s best engineering efforts, climbing out of the "fortress-crib"—a structure made of solid iron-wood and Ki-dampening silver—had become an easy, if somewhat destructive, task for the boy. 

It was during one of these escapes that Clara decided it was time to introduce him to the perimeter guard. 

She opened the heavy front door, carrying the impossibly heavy infant on her hip. Samuel was dressed in the silver-laced armor-weave tunic Eloise had crafted, his golden eyes wide and alert. Sitting on the porch, as still as a slate-grey statue, was Wolfie. 

Clara set Samuel down on the wooden planks. 

The Demigod and the Alpha stared at each other. 

It was a long, silent minute or two. Wolfie’s one good eye locked onto Samuel’s radiant golden ones. The wolf didn't growl. Samuel didn't cry. It was an unspoken, primitive negotiation of power and respect between the valley’s two apex presences. 

Finally, Wolfie let out a soft huff of breath. The giant beast lowered his massive head, bending his front legs until his belly touched the porch. 

Samuel didn't hesitate. With a gurgle of pure delight, he scrambled forward and climbed onto the thick fur of the wolf's back, his dense little hands grabbing fistfuls of slate-grey mane. 

"Wait, hold on—" Clara began, reaching out a hand. 

She didn't have a chance to object. Wolfie stood up and bolted. 

They were off, racing down the steps and tearing through the blooming spring fields of the pasture at a terrifying speed. Clara watched in stunned silence as the giant wolf bounded over the stone walls, Samuel clinging to his back and howling in unfiltered, joyous laughter that echoed across the ridge. 

Greg walked out onto the porch, wiping soot from his hands. He watched his one-month-old son riding a monster through a field of out-of-season wildflowers. 

"That's not normal for a one-year-old, Clara," Greg noted, his voice remarkably calm. "Much less a one-month-old. How much does Samuel weigh now?"

"About twenty-five pounds?" Clara sighed, leaning against the doorframe. "I stopped trying to use the grain-scale when he broke the iron hook."

"Right," Greg said. "Well... I suppose it's time we went down to the village. The quarantine is officially over. And I think Silas is going to want to see this."

***

Later that morning, a strange and magnificent procession entered the gates of Twin Rivers. 

The villagers, who had been anxiously waiting for the end of the month-long isolation, froze in their tracks. It wasn't just the sheer size of the baby that stunned them. It was the formation. 

Wolfie and Samuel were up front. The infant was sitting proudly on the back of the Alpha, his armor-weave tunic shining in the sunlight. Behind them walked Clara and Greg, looking like royalty in their simple farming clothes. And surrounding the entire family was an escort of twelve massive timber-wolves, moving in perfect, synchronized step with the "Dark Patrol" hidden in the shadows. 

As the procession moved down the main thoroughfare toward the square, a strange phenomenon occurred. 

The townspeople—the miners, the weavers, the merchants who had braved the journey—all bowed their heads. They didn't do it consciously. No one ordered it. But the sheer, overlapping weight of Greg’s "Dragon" presence, Clara’s "Verdant Pulse," and Samuel’s dense, radiant energy simply demanded an physical acknowledgment of respect. 

They reached the center of the square, right in front of the Priestess’s Hearth. 

Waiting for them, flanked by his own significantly less-impressive imperial guards, was Lord Alaric. The Governor had arrived at the exact moment the quarantine lifted, intending to present a formal gift to the Smith and smooth over any lingering diplomatic friction. 

Beside Alaric sat an incredibly ornate, beautifully crafted stroller. It was made of polished mahogany, trimmed with Oakhaven silver, and lined with imported southern silk. It was a gift fit for a prince. 

Alaric looked at the mahogany stroller. Then he looked at the twenty-five-pound, golden-eyed infant who was currently riding a one-eyed dire-wolf. 

The Governor cleared his throat, adjusting his fur-lined cloak. 

"I guess the stroller we brought won't be necessary?" Alaric asked, his voice entirely deadpan. 

Samuel looked down at the Governor. He reached out a dense little hand, pointing at the shiny silver trim on the stroller. 

*Crack.*

The sheer force of the infant's unfocused Ki snapped the mahogany handle clean in two. 

Alaric didn't flinch. He just nodded slowly, accepting the reality of the situation. "Yes. Definitely not necessary. Though I must say, Smith Greg, your son has an excellent eye for structural weaknesses."

Greg smiled, resting a hand on Wolfie’s flank to settle the beast. "He takes after his father, My Lord. Thank you for the gift. Silas can probably use the silver for the forge."

"Consider it a tribute, then," Alaric said, bowing his own head slightly. "From Oakhaven, to the... to the future of Twin Rivers."

Clara stepped forward, the grass springing up between the cobblestones beneath her feet. She looked at Alaric, then at the villagers who were still standing with bowed heads. 

"He's not just the future, Governor," Clara said, her voice warm and clear. "He's just Samuel. And he's very happy to meet you all."

As if on cue, Samuel let out a happy, booming gurgle that vibrated the stained glass of the Temple windows. 

The quarantine was over. The Demigod was introduced. And as the wolves settled into a protective ring around the square, Twin Rivers accepted its new reality. They weren't just a town anymore. 

They were the cradle of a legend.
