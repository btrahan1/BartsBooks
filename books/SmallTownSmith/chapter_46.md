# Chapter 46: Bureaucratic Armor

The festivities in the village square eventually gave way to the quiet, star-filled evening of the mountain ridge. The twelve timber-wolves had melted back into the treeline to begin their night patrols alongside Snake’s men, leaving the Farm in a state of profound, isolated peace. 

Inside the house, the hearth fire crackled merrily. Samuel, having thoroughly exhausted himself by terrifying the District Governor and breaking a piece of solid mahogany, was finally asleep in his iron-wood crib. 

At the heavy oak dining table sat Greg, Clara, and Lord Alaric. 

The Governor looked out of place in the simple, rustic kitchen, his silver breastplate catching the firelight. He was nursing a mug of Clara’s spiced cider, staring into the liquid as if searching for a diplomatic loophole. 

Finally, Alaric reached into the inner pocket of his tunic and pulled out a heavy roll of imperial parchment. He laid it flat on the table, the crisp, expensive paper a stark contrast to the rough-hewn oak. 

"I didn't just come to bring a stroller," Alaric said, his voice dropping the casual tone of the afternoon and adopting the formal cadence of his office. "I came to deliver this."

Greg looked at the parchment. His eyes narrowed, the relaxed "Farmer" demeanor instantly hardening back into the "Shield of the North." He recognized the seal of the District Governor at the bottom of the page, waiting for a signature. 

"What is it?" Greg asked, his voice low and dangerous. 

"It is a formal declaration," Alaric said, meeting Greg’s gaze without flinching. "I am using my authority as District Governor to officially recognize Twin Rivers as a protectorate domain, and I am granting a title to your son. He is to be recognized in the Imperial ledgers as Lord Samuel of the Silverun."

Silence descended on the kitchen. The only sound was the popping of a pine knot in the hearth. 

Greg stood up, his massive frame suddenly making the kitchen feel incredibly small. 

"No," Greg said, the word ringing with absolute finality. "No titles. No ledgers. I didn't leave the Legions and build a farm just to have my son branded as property of the Empire."

"Greg—" Alaric started. 

"He is going to be a farmer, Alaric," Greg interrupted, taking a step toward the table. "He is going to learn how to plant barley and forge plows. He is not going to be a 'Lord.' He will not owe the Emperor a tithe of blood or service. If you try to leash him to the Capital with a piece of paper, I will forge that signet ring of yours into a horseshoe."

Alaric didn't back away. He held his ground, though his heart hammered against his ribs. He had expected this. He had prepared for this. 

"You think I’m trying to leash him?" Alaric shot back, his own voice rising. "Smith, I am trying to *hide* him!"

Greg stopped, his brow furrowing in confusion. "Hide him by putting his name in an Imperial ledger?"

Clara, who had remained silent, leaned forward, her golden eyes scanning the parchment. She possessed a sharp, analytical mind that had navigated Oakhaven’s legal system before. She looked up at Alaric, the realization dawning on her face. 

"It's armor," Clara whispered. 

"Exactly," Alaric nodded, turning his attention to Clara, relieved that at least one of them understood the game. "Greg, you can break a cavalry charge. You can terrify a wolf pack. But you cannot punch a bureaucracy. If the Capital Scholars or the High Generals hear the rumors—and they will—they can legally march into this valley and seize a 'commoner' child with magical affinities for study or conscription. They do it all the time on the frontier."

Greg’s hands balled into fists at his sides, the air around him beginning to heat up. "Let them try."

"If they try, you will fight them," Alaric countered fiercely. "And you will win. And then the Emperor will send an entire Legion. And then another. You’ll spend the rest of your life killing imperial soldiers to protect your farm. Is that the life you want for him?"

Greg looked toward the iron-wood crib. The thought of bringing endless war to the valley made his chest ache. 

"But," Alaric continued, tapping his finger against the parchment. "Imperial Law is rigid. The Legions can conscript a farm boy without a second thought. But they *cannot* conscript a recognized Noble without a direct Royal Decree from the Emperor himself. It takes years of red tape, petitions, and hearings just to summon a Lord to the Capital."

Alaric picked up his cider, his hand trembling slightly from the adrenaline of arguing with a Dragon. 

"I'm not tying him to the Empire, Greg. I’m building a legal wall around him that’s thicker than the basalt wall around your town. 'Lord Samuel of the Silverun' isn't a leash. It’s bureaucratic armor."

Greg stared at the parchment. He hated the Empire. He hated the games, the titles, and the endless ledgers. But as he looked at Alaric—a man who had caught his son that afternoon, a man who had accepted the title of "Uncle" with genuine warmth—he realized the Governor was telling the truth. Alaric was weaponizing the very system he served to protect the boy. 

"A Royal Decree," Greg murmured, the tension slowly draining from his shoulders. "It would take them five years just to agree on the wording."

"At least," Clara agreed. She reached across the table and took Greg’s massive, calloused hand. "He's right, Greg. We protect him with Ki and iron here. Alaric protects him with ink and wax out there."

Greg let out a long, heavy sigh, collapsing back into his wooden chair. He looked at the Governor, a newfound respect settling in his eyes. 

"Lord Samuel of the Silverun," Greg tested the words on his tongue. He grimaced. "He’s still going to shovel manure when he’s old enough."

"I would expect nothing less of a Twin Rivers noble," Alaric smiled. 

The Governor reached down and pulled the heavy, gold Imperial Signet ring from his finger. He walked over to the hearth, melting the end of a stick of deep red wax, and let it drip onto the bottom of the parchment. 

With a firm, decisive motion, Alaric pressed the ring into the wax, sealing the document. 

"It is done," Alaric announced quietly. "Your son is legally untouchable by anyone below the Emperor himself."

Clara stood up and poured the Governor another mug of cider, her glowing presence filling the kitchen with a profound warmth. 

"Thank you, Unkie Alaric," Clara teased softly. 

Alaric groaned, rubbing his temples, though the smile on his face was undeniable. "Please, Priestess. The 'Unkie' title is far more terrifying to me than the Imperial ones."

The night deepened over the Crag-Shadows. In his crib, the newly minted Lord of the Silverun slept peacefully, unaware that his parents and his "Uncle" had just secured his future with the most powerful weapon in the Empire: a really, really complicated piece of paperwork.
