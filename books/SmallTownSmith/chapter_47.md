# Chapter 47: Happy Birthday, times 11

When Lord Samuel of the Silverun was three months old, something strange happened in Twin Rivers. 

The town was accustomed to the massive timber-wolves patrolling the perimeter and moving through the shadows alongside the Guard. But one evening, ten of the wolves broke their usual patterns. Instead of pacing the "Peace-Wall" or hunting in the deep brush, they walked into the village square and deliberately camped out in front of ten different homes. 

At first, the people were unsure of why. The wolves didn't beg for food. They didn't threaten anyone. They simply sat on the front porches, their glowing eyes fixed on the doors, acting as silent, immovable sentinels. 

Within a month, the mystery was solved. 

Word spread from the Hearth of the Priestess that the ladies of those ten specific homes were all pregnant. The sudden, localized baby boom wasn't entirely unexpected—the "Verdant Pulse" of the valley had a way of encouraging life in all its forms—but the wolves’ prophetic knowledge of the pregnancies sent a new wave of awe through the village. 

By the time Samuel was six months old, his development had accelerated from "terrifying" to "mythological." 

He was walking extremely well, his balance impeccable despite his incredible density. When Clara or Greg brought him into town, he rarely rode in the rebuilt stroller (which was now plated in iron). He mostly rode Wolfie, sitting comfortably on the Alpha’s broad back. Whenever they passed one of the ten guardian wolves, Samuel would command Wolfie to stop. 

The Demigod toddler would slide off, wobble over to the massive, slate-grey sentinels, and pat them on the head. 

"Good dog," Samuel would babble, his voice carrying that heavy, vibrating Ki. The terrifying predators would lean into his small hands, thumping their tails against the porch wood. 

Around nine months old, the pregnancies were far enough along that the expectant mothers began to waddle when they walked to the market. The ten wolves were no longer just camping on the porches; they were actively shadowing the women. If a mother-to-be went to the baker, her wolf walked beside her. If she went to the river to wash clothes, the wolf sat on the bank. 

Samuel, whose vocabulary was expanding as rapidly as his three-foot-tall frame, took a special interest in these patrols. 

"Take good care of my sisters," Samuel would tell the wolves, his golden eyes locking onto theirs with absolute, commanding clarity. He would pet their snouts, sealing the directive with a surge of his own "Restless Spark."

The villagers didn't question how a nine-month-old boy knew the gender of ten unborn children. They had long since learned that questioning Samuel was a fast track to a headache. They simply accepted that the Mountain-Shaker was preparing his court. 

The climax of the phenomenon arrived exactly on the winter solstice—Samuel’s first birthday. 

The "Peace-Wall" was closed to outsiders, and the atmosphere in Twin Rivers was thick with anticipation. Inside the Temple, the Hearth fire was roaring. Ten cots had been set up in a wide semi-circle around the central warmth, and the ten expectant mothers had been moved in the night before. 

Clara, wearing her simple blue tunic that seemed to glow with its own internal sunlight, moved between the beds with the calm, tireless energy of a true Priestess. Greg and Silas were outside, running cauldrons of boiling water from the forge and keeping the nervous fathers from pacing holes in the cobblestones. 

But the true guardian of the Temple stood right at the main double doors. 

Samuel, wearing a new armor-weave tunic and a small, iron-wood belt Greg had forged for him, stood proudly at the entrance. He was three feet tall, built like a miniature gladiator, and possessed a golden stare that kept even the most anxious father from trying to push past him. 

Wolfie sat by his right side, the rest of the pack forming a protective ring around the entire building. 

As the sun reached its zenith, the first cry echoed from within the Temple. 

It wasn't a shockwave like Samuel’s, but it was mighty. It vibrated the polished river-stones of the floor and sent a sudden, warm pulse of Ki radiating out into the square. 

Samuel smiled, his tiny fists resting on his hips. "Happy Birthday, my sister!" 

Over the next four hours, the crowd in the square held a silent, awe-struck vigil. One after another, the mothers delivered. Each birth was accompanied by a powerful, resonant wail that shook the dust from the Temple eaves and made the windows rattle. And with every cry, the timber-wolves outside threw their heads back and howled in pure, unadulterated glee. 

Ten wails. Ten howls. Ten new Sparks born into the protection of the valley. 

Finally, as the winter sun began to dip behind the Crag-Shadows, Clara appeared at the Temple doors. She looked exhausted, but her smile was as bright as the day she had first walked out of the Farm. 

"Ten healthy girls," Clara announced to the cheering square. "The Hearth is full."

Greg walked up the steps, pulling Clara into a gentle, supportive embrace. He looked down at his one-year-old son, who was staring into the Temple with a look of profound satisfaction. 

"Well, Samuel," Greg said, his deep voice rumbling with amusement. "It looks like your birthday party is going to be a bit crowded from now on."

Samuel didn't look up at his father. He kept his golden eyes fixed on the ten cots inside the Temple, sensing the ten new, bright Sparks that were harmonizing perfectly with his own. 

"Valkyries," Samuel said simply, the word dropping into the quiet evening air with the weight of an ancient prophecy. 

He nodded once, a sharp, decisive motion, and then reached up to grab Wolfie’s fur. 

The three-foot-tall Lord of the Silverun had received his birthday present. The empire had its Legions. The Governor had his Sixth. But Samuel, at exactly one year old, had the beginnings of his Clan. 

And as the ten new baby girls settled into a synchronized, peaceful sleep under Clara's watchful eye, Greg realized that the "fortress-crib" he had built was going to need an entire barracks. 

Twin Rivers was no longer just a sanctuary. It was a foundry for a new era.
