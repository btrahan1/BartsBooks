# Chapter 48: Valkyries

"Come again?" asked a bewildered Alaric. 

The Governor of the Oakhaven District sat behind his massive oak desk, staring at his lieutenant. He had just poured himself a very expensive glass of southern wine to celebrate the passing of the winter solstice. He hadn't even taken a sip before the news arrived. 

"Valkyries, sir," the lieutenant repeated, his face completely devoid of expression, the mask of a soldier who had long since accepted that logic died at the borders of the Twin Rivers protectorate. 

Alaric set his glass down very slowly. He rubbed his temples, a habitual gesture whenever the subject of the northern valley arose. 

"Yes, Lieutenant, I heard that part perfectly," Alaric said, his voice straining for patience. "Let's back up. Did you just say that ten girls in Twin Rivers were all born on the exact same night?"

"Yes, sir," the lieutenant confirmed, nodding stiffly. "All on Lord Samuel's first birthday, sir. The solstice. The scouts report that the Priestess delivered them all within a four-hour window."

"Ten girls," Alaric murmured, staring into the flickering light of the fireplace. "Ten girls born on the exact same day to a town of barely four hundred people. The Imperial Statisticians would declare that a mathematical impossibility."

"It gets worse, My Lord."

Alaric looked up, his brow furrowing. "Worse?" 

"The wolves, sir. Apparently, the wolves knew it was going to happen. They spent the last nine months guarding the pregnant women. And when the babies were born, the entire pack stood outside the Temple and howled in unison. The scouts say it sounded like... like they were cheering, sir."

Alaric sighed deeply, pinching the bridge of his nose. He had granted Samuel nobility to protect him from the Capital's scrutiny. He had built "bureaucratic armor" around the boy to hide him in plain sight. But it was becoming increasingly difficult to hide a child who threw localized earthquakes for tantrums and apparently commanded a private army of prophetic wildlife. 

"Fine," Alaric said, waving a hand dismissively. "Wolves cheering. Let’s just accept that as normal and move on. So, where do you get 'Valkyries' from, then? Is that some obscure northern dialect for a group of infants?"

The lieutenant shifted uncomfortably, his armor clinking softly in the quiet office. "No, My Lord. That's what Lord Samuel named them. The scouts overheard it when he stood on the steps of the Temple."

Alaric blinked. "Samuel named them? He’s one year old!"

"Well, he’s one year old, but he’s three feet tall, sir," the lieutenant pointed out helpfully. "And he rides a dire-wolf."

"I am aware of his transportation preferences, Lieutenant!" Alaric snapped, his composure finally slipping. "But naming a group of newborns? What did he say?"

"Just one word, sir," the lieutenant replied. "'Valkyries.' He looked at the Temple, said the word, nodded, and then rode off on his wolf."

Alaric leaned back in his leather chair, staring blankly at the ceiling. 

He knew exactly what a Valkyrie was. It wasn't a common word in the Empire. It was an ancient, pre-imperial term from the old northern myths—legends of female warriors who rode the storms, carried the souls of the brave to the afterlife, and served as the absolute elite guard of the gods. 

"He's building an army," Alaric whispered, the realization hitting him with the force of a physical blow. 

"Sir?"

"He's not just a child, Lieutenant," Alaric said, sitting up abruptly. "And he’s not just a Lord. He’s the Mountain-Shaker. And he just looked at ten newborn girls and declared them his elite, mythological vanguard. He’s building his own personal guard. From scratch."

The lieutenant swallowed hard. "Should we... should we report this to the Capital?"

"Absolutely not!" Alaric roared, slamming his hand down on the desk. He took a deep breath, forcing himself to calm down. "If the Capital hears that a one-year-old Noble is magically drafting an army of storm-maidens guarded by wolves, they won't send Scholars. They’ll send the First Legion. And then the Smith will wipe the First Legion off the face of the map, and we’ll have a civil war in my district."

Alaric stood up and began to pace the floor, his mind racing. He had to quarantine this information. He had to contain the legend of Twin Rivers before it grew too big for the valley to hold. 

"Here is the official report," Alaric commanded, stopping in front of his lieutenant. "Twin Rivers experienced a minor, albeit statistically unusual, birth rate this winter. The Governor extends his congratulations to the families. As for Lord Samuel... he is a precocious child who enjoys playing with dogs. That is all."

"But what about the word, sir? 'Valkyries'?"

"He was babbling," Alaric insisted, pointing a stern finger at the lieutenant. "He is a one-year-old. He sneezed, or he coughed, and the scouts misinterpreted the sound. Is that understood?"

"Yes, My Lord. The boy sneezed."

"Excellent," Alaric said, collapsing back into his chair and finally reaching for his wine glass. "Now, send a rider to Twin Rivers with a crate of the finest silk blankets we have in the storehouse. And perhaps... perhaps ten small, unsharpened wooden training swords."

The lieutenant raised an eyebrow. "Training swords, sir? For newborn girls?"

Alaric took a long sip of his wine, the rich, southern vintage failing to completely warm the sudden chill in his bones. 

"They're Valkyries, Lieutenant," Alaric sighed, staring into the ruby depths of his glass. "Knowing that valley, they’ll probably be walking by spring and demanding armor by the harvest. Best we get on their good side now. After all... I am their Unkie."

The lieutenant saluted smartly and turned to leave, leaving the Governor of Oakhaven alone in his office to ponder the terrifying, exhilarating reality of the North. 

The Empire thought it was the absolute power in the world. But in a small valley thirty miles away, a three-foot-tall boy with golden eyes was already assembling a force that would make the Emperor weep. 

And all it had taken was a single word.
