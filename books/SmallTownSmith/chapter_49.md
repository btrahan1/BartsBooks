# Chapter 49: The Fjellbjorn

It was three months later, just as the last of the mountain snow was finally beginning to yield to the relentless "Verdant Pulse" of the valley, when Lord Alaric made his next official visit to Twin Rivers. 

He rode through the heavy larch gates with his lieutenant and a small escort, expecting to see the usual bustling prosperity of the market square. He did see that, of course. He also saw that the "Unbreakable Guard" had grown to a formidable fifty members, their green tunics and polished iron-wood spears moving through the streets with precise, military efficiency. The number of Snake’s "Dark Flame" scouts remained a mystery—a shadow-unit whose true strength was known only to Tyler and Snake himself. 

But what brought the Governor of Oakhaven to an absolute, dead halt was the scene taking place near the Hearth of the Priestess. 

Alaric pulled back on his stallion’s reins, his jaw falling slack. He didn't speak. He didn't move. For a full ten minutes, the highest-ranking imperial official in the District simply sat in his saddle and stared at the total collapse of biological and developmental reality. 

Samuel, now fifteen months old and built like a miniature boulder, was standing in the center of the square. He was holding a small, unsharpened wooden training sword. 

Arrayed before him, standing in a surprisingly neat line, were ten infant girls. They were three months old. They were standing perfectly upright. And they were each holding the wooden training swords Alaric had sent as a "joke." 

Samuel raised his sword. The ten girls raised theirs in perfect, terrifying synchronization. 

"Step," Samuel commanded, his golden eyes blazing with focus. 

Ten three-month-old infants took a synchronized step forward, their tiny, armor-weave-clad feet hitting the cobblestones in unison. 

"Thrust," Samuel barked. 

Ten wooden swords shot forward, slicing the air with a faint, unified hum of Ki. 

Alaric rubbed his eyes, convinced that he had finally lost his mind to the mountain air. He looked at his lieutenant. The veteran soldier was staring at the "Valkyries" with an expression of profound, existential horror, clearly trying to calculate how long it would take for these infants to conquer the entire Northern District. 

At that exact moment, Samuel sensed the presence of the Governor. 

The fifteen-month-old Demigod lowered his sword. Instantly, the ten Valkyries lowered theirs. Samuel turned to face the riders, the ten girls pivoting flawlessly to match his movement. 

Samuel bowed his head slightly. The Valkyries bowed their heads. 

Then, in a soft, resonant unison that vibrated through the cobblestones and straight up into Alaric’s boots, the eleven children spoke a single word. 

"Uncle."

Alaric swayed in his saddle. The edges of his vision went grey, and for a terrifying second, he genuinely thought he was going to faint right there in the square. He gripped the pommel of his saddle until his knuckles turned white, desperately trying to anchor himself to a reality that made sense. 

"Good... good morning, Samuel," Alaric choked out, his voice sounding thin and weak. "Good morning, girls."

But before Alaric could even begin to process the fact that he was now the honorary uncle to an infant strike-force, the reality of Twin Rivers decided to escalate once again. 

A heavy, rhythmic thudding sound echoed from the direction of the smaller, northern gate—the "back door" that faced the deep woods. 

The villagers in the square paused. The Guard turned their heads. Even the perimeter wolves seemed to tense, though they didn't howl an alarm. 

A group of shadows detached themselves from the timberline and lumbered through the open gate. 

They were bears. 

Seven of them. Seven fully grown, terrifyingly massive Crag-Bears, their thick brown fur matted with mountain frost, their claws the size of iron plowshares. They walked into the village with a slow, deliberate arrogance, completely ignoring the humans, the Guard, and the wolves. 

Each bear lumbered down the street, sniffing the air, until they seemed to find a specific scent. Then, acting with the same bizarre, prophetic certainty the wolves had displayed months earlier, each bear picked a different house at random and plopped down heavily on the front porch. 

They didn't roar. They didn't threaten. They just sat there, acting as massive, furry barricades. 

The square was utterly silent. 

Alaric’s lieutenant looked at the nearest bear, which was currently scratching its ear lazily against the wooden post of a weaver’s shop. 

"Oh, shit," the lieutenant whispered, his voice cracking with the realization of what this meant. 

Alaric looked at the bears. He looked at the Valkyries. He looked at the fifteen-month-old boy with golden eyes who was currently smiling at the new arrivals. 

"Oh, shit indeed," echoed the Governor, his soul wearying at the thought of the imperial paperwork this was going to generate. 

***

And so it was, exactly nine months later, that the Hearth of the Priestess was once again filled with the sounds of labor. 

It was deep winter, exactly a year after the Valkyries were born. The seven expectant mothers had been shadowed by their terrifying, three-ton guardians for the entire pregnancy, the bears acting as immovable sentinels whenever the women left their homes. 

When the first cry echoed from the Temple, it was deeper than the Valkyries' wails. It was a heavy, rumbling sound that shook the snow from the eaves and resonated in the chest cavities of the anxious fathers waiting outside. 

By the end of the night, Clara emerged from the Temple, exhausted but triumphant, her "Verdant Pulse" glowing like a beacon in the winter darkness. 

"Seven boys," Clara announced to the waiting crowd, leaning against Greg for support. "Nine pounds each. They’re perfectly healthy."

Samuel, now two years old and standing at the Temple doors with his wooden sword resting on his shoulder, watched as the seven massive Crag-Bears outside let out a synchronized, earth-shaking roar of approval. 

The Governor, who had smartly decided to attend this solstice celebration in person rather than wait for the terrifying scout reports, stood beside Samuel. Alaric looked at the two-year-old commander. 

"Well, Nephew," Alaric said, taking a long drink from a flask of fortified wine he had brought specifically for this occasion. "You have your Valkyries. What do you call the heavy infantry?"

Samuel didn't look up. He kept his golden eyes fixed on the seven cots inside, sensing the new, dense, heavy Sparks that were joining the harmonic resonance of the valley. 

"Fjellbjorn," Samuel said simply, the ancient northern word for Mountain-Bear rolling off his tongue with heavy, absolute certainty. 

He looked up at the Governor, his golden eyes flashing with the same terrifying, protective fire that lived in his father’s forge. 

"My brothers," Samuel nodded. 

Alaric took another, much larger drink from his flask. 

Twin Rivers didn't just have a Guard anymore. They had the Valkyries for precision, the Fjellbjorn for the vanguard, and a Demigod to lead them all. 

"I need to send a letter to the Capital," Alaric muttered to himself as the celebration erupted around them. "I need to tell the Emperor that the North is secure. And that he should absolutely, under no circumstances, ever come to visit."
