# Chapter 51: Journey

The celebration of the solstice had lasted from the first light of dawn until the deep, starry chill of the mountain night. Twin Rivers had grown over the last six years, its "Peace-Wall" expanded and its fields rich, but the center of the village remained the Hearth of the Priestess. 

Tonight, the Hearth was quiet. The villagers had retired to their homes, leaving the core family standing in the center of the Temple. 

It was Samuel’s seventh birthday. 

"Mother, I have a task for you."

Clara turned from the fire, the "Verdant Pulse" still glowing softly in her eyes. She looked at her son, and for a fleeting moment, a sense of startled awe washed over her. 

At seven years old, Samuel was an impossibility of nature. He stood six feet tall, his physique resembling that of a young, marble-carved Adonis rather than a child. He was clad in his latest armor—a masterpiece of steel, silver, and Kragh-spider weave that Greg had spent the last three months forging. It looked like it was forged for a God, and given the density and sheer power of the boy wearing it, that was mostly true. 

Behind Samuel stood the ten Valkyries. They were six years old, yet they moved with the lithe, silent grace of veteran scouts. They wore perfectly tailored leather armor and carried unstrung bows over their shoulders. 

As Clara looked at them, the ten girls bowed deeply. "Mother," they said in absolute, harmonious unison. 

"I would like for you to take my Sisters to The Grove," Samuel said, his deep, resonant voice entirely devoid of childhood hesitation. 

Clara blinked, her brow furrowing. "The Grove?"

"Yes," Samuel nodded. "They are fast. They are strong. But they need to learn the bow. And not from the Guard, and not from the imperial archers of Oakhaven. They need to learn from your family. From the Druids of Alveron."

Clara’s breath caught in her throat. Alveron. It was a name she hadn't heard in years, a whispered legend of the deep, ancient forests far beyond the district borders. "Samuel... I am a healer. A smith's wife. I don't know the Druids of Alveron."

"You do," Samuel said simply. "Let me show you your lineage."

He stepped forward, his heavy boots making no sound on the river-stone floor. He raised a hand—a hand as large and calloused as his father’s—and gently touched two fingers to Clara’s forehead. 

A surge of pure, golden Ki, wrapped in the familiar warmth of the "Verdant Pulse," rushed into her mind. Clara gasped as centuries of suppressed genetic memory flooded her vision. She saw ancient, massive trees that dwarfed the Great Oak of Twin Rivers. She saw women with eyes like hers, pulling bows made of living wood that shot arrows of pure, green light. She felt the song of Alveron, a melody that had been sleeping in her blood for generations. 

When Samuel pulled his hand away, Clara was weeping softly, a profound, joyful understanding radiating from her core. 

"I... I will take them," Clara whispered, looking at the ten girls who were waiting patiently for their mother’s guidance. 

Samuel nodded respectfully. Then, he turned. 

"Father," Samuel said, his tone shifting from reverence to the hard, unyielding weight of command. "I also have a task for you. One that you won't like."

Greg, who had been leaning against a basalt pillar watching the exchange, stared at his son. His expression was completely unreadable, a mask of sheer stone. 

Behind Samuel, filing into the Temple from the shadows of the porch, came the seven Fjellbjorn. They were five years old, yet they were already the size of full-grown men, their shoulders thick and their eyes burning with a heavy, restless energy. They didn't carry spears. They carried heavy, blunt training axes. 

"You gave up the Twin Axes of the Dragon to find peace," Samuel said, his golden eyes locking onto his father’s. "You built a sanctuary. You taught the Guard to be a shield. But a shield will not be enough for what is coming. Now, I want you to train my Fjellbjorn. In the way of the Dragon."

Greg pushed himself off the pillar. The air in the Temple immediately grew heavy, the temperature rising as Greg’s suppressed Ki flared in warning. 

"No," Greg said, his voice a low, dangerous rumble. "I buried that style. The Dragon’s path is the path of slaughter, Samuel. I will not teach your brothers to be butchers."

"It is time, Father," Samuel insisted, not backing down an inch from the terrifying presence of the most dangerous man in the Empire. He held up one hand, a gesture of absolute, unshakeable calm. "It is not to fight men. It is not to conquer the Capital or break the Legions. It is to fight the darkness that comes. The Ashen Tide."

Greg froze. The name triggered a deep, primal instinct he hadn't felt since he stood on the borders of Kragh. 

"The boys have the strength," Samuel continued softly. "But they need the fire. They need the fury of the Dragon, tempered by the discipline of the Smith. Only you can teach them how to wield the axes without losing their souls."

Greg looked at the seven boys, who were staring at him with a mixture of awe and absolute readiness. He looked at Clara, who gave him a slow, encouraging nod. The sanctuary they had built wasn't an escape; it was a crucible. 

"The Twin Axes," Greg murmured, the ghost of his past finally settling heavily on his shoulders. He looked back at his son. "If I train them... and Clara takes the girls to Alveron... what will you be doing?"

"Yes," Clara asked, stepping up beside Greg. "Where are you going, Samuel?"

Samuel turned toward the open doors of the Temple, looking out into the cold, clear night. High in the Crags, a solitary wolf howled, answering the call of the Mountain-Shaker. 

"It's time to find my dragon egg," Samuel said simply. "I will retrieve it and bring it back here."

Greg’s jaw tightened. A dragon egg wasn't a metaphor. It was the ultimate symbol of imperial power, the source of the deepest, most ancient Ki in the world. And there was only one place such a thing could be kept. 

"Where?" Greg asked, though he already knew the answer. 

Samuel looked back over his shoulder, the golden spark in his eyes flashing with the brilliance of a newly forged blade. 

"From the King," Samuel said. 

With that, the seven-year-old Demigod walked out of the Temple, stepping into the mountain night to begin a journey that would shake the very foundations of the Empire. 

Act 4 had begun.
