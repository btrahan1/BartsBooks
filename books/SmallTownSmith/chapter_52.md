# Chapter 52: Arrival

"My King! He's coming!"

King Valerius III stopped mid-sentence. He was standing at the head of the massive, map-covered table in the Imperial War Room, deep in discussion with his High Council regarding the latest grain shipments from the southern provinces and the troubling scout reports from the Kragh border. 

He didn't yell at the frantic, dust-covered messenger who had just burst through the heavy oak doors, past two stunned Royal Guards. Valerius simply raised a hand, his rings catching the light of the chandeliers, and flicked it toward the exit. 

The chambers were empty in less than a minute. Generals, trade ministers, and high lords scrambled over each other to clear the room, recognizing the sharp, cold edge of panic in the Emperor's eyes that had not been seen since he had read the ancient slate in the archives six years ago. 

When the heavy doors finally clicked shut, leaving only the King, the Chief Archivist Malakor, and the panting messenger in the room, Valerius leaned over the table. 

"By 'He'," the King said, his voice dangerously quiet, "you mean?"

"Yes, My King!" the messenger gasped, dropping to one knee, his lungs heaving. "Samuel! The Lord of the Silverun! The... the Mountain-Shaker!"

Valerius felt a cold knot tighten in his stomach. For six years, the Crown had adhered strictly to the policy of absolute non-interference with Twin Rivers. They had ignored the statistical impossibilities, the rumors of infant armies, and the total lack of imperial taxation. They had left the crucible in the North alone, hoping it would only spill over when the "Ashen Tide" finally broke. 

But the crucible had apparently decided it was done waiting. 

"When?" Valerius demanded, his mind already racing through deployment strategies he hoped he would never have to use. 

"He will be here within the hour, Sire," the messenger reported, his eyes wide with a terror that bordered on madness. "He bypassed the checkpoints. He didn't even use the roads. He just... went straight over the mountain passes. He's alone, riding a wolf the size of an elephant!"

The King scoffed, a short, harsh sound of disbelief. He looked at Malakor. "An elephant. The frontier scouts always exaggerate when they are afraid. I’m sure it is merely a large dire-wolf."

Malakor, who had spent the last six years studying everything he could find on the biological anomalies of Twin Rivers, frowned deeply. "Perhaps, Sire. But given the... dense nature of the Ki in that valley, I wouldn't assume the exaggeration is by much."

Valerius waved a hand, dismissing the argument. A giant wolf was a giant wolf. The more pressing issue was the timeline. He needed to organize a reception—not a military defense, which would likely just provoke the boy, but a diplomatic showing of strength. He needed the Royal Guard in full ceremonial armor. He needed the palace secured. 

"An hour, you say?" Valerius said, turning to look at the massive map of the Empire spread out on the table. He traced the route from the Northern Pass to the Capital gates. "If he is only an hour away, he must have already breached the Outer Wall. How far away is he currently?"

The messenger swallowed hard, looking up at the King with an expression of pure, unadulterated awe. 

"About fifty miles, My King!"

Valerius froze. His finger stopped dead on the map, hovering over a stretch of dense, roadless forest far outside the city limits. 

"Fifty miles," Valerius repeated slowly, the mathematical impossibility of the statement crashing into his strategic mind. "He is fifty miles away... and he will be here in an *hour*?"

"Yes, Sire," the messenger nodded vigorously. "He isn't running, Sire. He's... he's flashing. The scouts on the beacon towers said the wolf moves like a lightning strike. It touches the ground, the earth shatters, and then they are just... somewhere else. He cleared the entire High Ridge in three minutes."

The King of the known world sank into his heavy, velvet-lined chair. 

Fifty miles an hour. Through dense forest. On the back of an impossibly massive wolf. 

"He's not traveling," Malakor whispered, his ancient eyes fixed on the map. "He's hunting. The 'Golden Spark' and the 'Verdant Pulse' combined. He is moving through the energy of the earth itself."

Valerius looked at the door. He realized that calling the Royal Guard to assemble would be entirely pointless. If the boy was moving at that speed, with that much power, the heavy stone walls of the Capital were nothing more than decorative suggestions. 

"Send no troops," Valerius commanded, his voice tight. "Stand down the archers on the walls. Open the main gates of the palace. If we try to close a door on a storm, it will simply blow the house down."

"My King," Malakor said, his voice trembling slightly. "What does he want? Twin Rivers has been silent for six years. Why come now?"

"He is the son of the Dragon," Valerius said, staring at his own reflection in the polished wood of the table. "He doesn't want gold. He doesn't want land. He wants something that belongs to his blood."

Fifty miles away, a shockwave of golden light and displaced earth shattered the silence of the King's Forest. 

Wolfie, now standing nearly ten feet tall at the shoulder, hit the ground with the force of a falling meteor. Before the dust could even settle, the massive beast’s powerful hind legs coiled and snapped, launching them another quarter-mile through the dense timber. 

On his back sat Samuel. 

At seven years old, the Demigod was perfectly calm. His custom-forged armor gleamed in the dappled sunlight, completely unaffected by the extreme, hurricane-force winds generated by their speed. His golden eyes were fixed on the distant, glittering spire of the Imperial Palace. 

He wasn't angry. He wasn't waging war. He was simply running an errand. 

"Faster, Wolfie," Samuel commanded softly, the deep vibration of his voice cutting through the roaring wind. 

The Alpha let out a joyous, earth-shaking howl, digging his claws into the bedrock, and blurred into a streak of slate-grey and gold. 

The Capital was about to have a visitor. And the King was about to find out exactly what happens when you hold onto something that belongs to a Mountain-Shaker.
