# Chapter 53: Grandfather

The heavy, gold-inlaid doors of the Grand Throne Room did not burst open. They were pushed open, slowly and deliberately, with a smooth, terrifyingly immense physical force that snapped the iron locking-bars as if they were dry twigs. 

The Royal Guards, fifty of the Empire's most elite, battle-hardened soldiers lining the chamber, drew their swords in unison. The metallic ring echoed sharply off the vaulted marble ceiling. 

Through the ruined doorway stepped the Alpha. 

The collective breath of the room vanished. The dire-wolf was incomprehensibly massive. It stood ten feet at the shoulder, its slate-grey fur rippling over muscles that looked like coiled steel cables. Its one good eye burned with a cold, primal intelligence as it surveyed the heavily armored men, looking at them less like a threat and more like a collection of noisy, metallic appetizers. It could probably eat an elephant, and the Royal Guards knew it. 

But it was the rider that brought the Emperor of the known world to a dead halt. 

"Hello, Grandfather," the rider said. "It is time that we met."

King Valerius III, the ruler of a continent, just stared. 

The boy was seven years old. He stood at least six feet tall. He was built like a gladiator in his absolute prime, his broad shoulders clad in armor that seemed to hum with an internal, golden light. His voice didn't sound like a child’s; it was a deep, resonant rumble that vibrated the polished stone floor and made the fine crystal in the high chandeliers chime in sympathy. 

Valerius looked into the boy’s eyes. They were the exact, piercing gold of the "Restless Spark." They were the eyes of his son. The son who had been the Crown Prince. The son who had been the "Dragon of War," the greatest weapon the Empire had ever known, before he had thrown his royal inheritance into the dirt and walked away to forge farm tools in the freezing North. 

Samuel didn't draw a weapon. He didn't issue a threat. He simply dismounted. 

The movement was so impossibly smooth, so fast and heavily imbued with Ki, that it looked like teleportation. One second he was sitting on the massive wolf's back, and the next, his heavy, armor-weave boots were planted firmly on the marble floor. 

The Royal Guards tensed, several of them stepping forward to form a defensive wall in front of the dais. 

"Hold your ground!" Valerius snapped, his voice cracking slightly with the overwhelming emotional and physical pressure of the moment. He waved a trembling hand. "Let him pass."

Samuel strode past the lines of elite soldiers. He didn't look at them. He didn't radiate anger or malice. He moved with the calm, absolute certainty of a mountain. 

He stopped a respectable distance from the base of the throne. 

The Mountain-Shaker, the prophesied Founder, the boy who had shattered the physical laws of the Northern District, looked up at the King of the Empire. And then, smoothly and with perfect, unforced respect, Samuel took a knee. 

Valerius felt a lump rise in his throat. He had expected a monster. He had expected a conqueror. He had expected the child of the Priestess to demand his crown. 

Instead, he saw a soldier. A soldier forged by the discipline of the Smith. 

"Rise, Samuel," Valerius whispered, gripping the arms of his throne to steady his shaking hands. "You... you have your father's eyes. And his presence."

Samuel stood up, resting his hand casually on the hilt of his wooden training sword—a jarring detail against the masterpiece of his armor. 

"I have his discipline, Grandfather," Samuel corrected respectfully. "But I need his fire."

Valerius frowned, leaning forward. "His fire? I was told you were coming for your dragon egg. I assumed you meant... well, a literal egg. Or perhaps a royal tithe. What do you mean?"

Samuel’s golden eyes narrowed slightly, not in anger, but in profound, terrifying focus. The air in the throne room began to grow heavy, the same pre-storm electricity that used to follow the Crown Prince into battle. 

"My father buried the Twin Axes of the Dragon when he left this city," Samuel said, his voice echoing in the absolute silence of the chamber. "He buried the style, and he buried the weapons. He threw them into the deepest vault of the Citadel so he would never be tempted to use them again."

Valerius nodded slowly. He remembered the day his son had walked into this very room, dropped the blood-stained, legendary axes on the floor, and renounced his titles to go find peace. 

"He found his peace, Grandfather," Samuel continued. "He built a sanctuary. But a sanctuary is a shield. And a shield will not hold against what is rising in the East."

"The Ashen Tide," Valerius breathed, the words of the ancient prophecy returning to him with chilling clarity. 

Samuel nodded once. "War is coming, Grandfather. A war not of men, but of shadows. The Fjellbjorn are ready to learn, and my father is finally ready to teach them. But he needs the tools of his past to secure our future."

The seven-year-old Demigod looked straight into the eyes of the King, holding out a massive, calloused hand. 

"The vault, Grandfather. My Dragon calls me."
