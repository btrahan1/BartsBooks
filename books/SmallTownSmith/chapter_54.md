# Chapter 54: The Dragon Axes

The deepest vault of the Imperial Citadel was a place where light seemed to die. It was buried far beneath the Grand Archive, protected by heavy iron doors that required the synchronized turning of three separate gears by the Royal Guard. 

When the final gear clicked and the massive door groaned open, King Valerius III stepped into the cold, stale air of the chamber, holding a single, sputtering torch. Behind him walked Samuel, the seven-year-old giant who had just completely rewritten the military reality of the Empire in a single afternoon. 

The vault was filled with the spoils of a thousand years of conquest. Crates of Kragh silver, tapestries woven from southern spider-silk, and racks of legendary, blood-soaked weaponry. 

But Samuel ignored all of it. His golden eyes locked onto a raised obsidian pedestal in the very center of the room. 

Resting on the pedestal were the Twin Axes of the Dragon. 

They were a thing of terrible, breathtaking beauty. A lethal combination of artistry and death. The hafts were forged from a black, unyielding metal that seemed to absorb the torchlight, wrapped in worn, dark leather that still bore the faint indentations of Greg’s grip. The curved, double-bearded blades were massive, etched with swirling patterns that glowed with a faint, residual heat even after years of neglect. 

Valerius stopped a few paces away from the pedestal. He shifted nervously, the heavy velvet of his robe rustling in the quiet vault. He looked at the axes, remembering the sheer devastation his son had wrought with them on the frontier. They were so incredibly heavy that it traditionally took two strong men just to lift the crate they were stored in. 

The King looked sideways at his massive, seven-year-old grandson. *Could he even wield them?* Valerius wondered. *Could a child, even one built like a mountain, handle the physical and spiritual weight of the Dragon's legacy?*

Samuel didn't hesitate. He strode past the King, his armor clinking softly. He reached out with both hands and grasped the black hafts. 

Valerius held his breath. 

With a smooth, effortless motion that defied physics, Samuel lifted the axes from the pedestal. He didn't strain. He didn't grunt. He twirled the massive weapons in his hands, testing their balance. As his golden Ki bled into the dark metal, the swirling patterns on the blades flared to life, burning with a bright, blinding gold that illuminated the entire vault. 

"These will do," Samuel said simply, lowering the axes to his sides. 

Valerius exhaled a long, shaky breath, wiping a bead of sweat from his brow. He felt a profound sense of relief wash over him. The boy had what he came for. The Empire wouldn't have to face the wrath of the Mountain-Shaker today. 

"They suit you, Nephew," the King bobbed his head, offering a nervous, diplomatic smile. "I must admit, I am glad you have found what you were looking for. When the messenger burst into my council chambers and said you were coming for your 'dragon egg,' I was terrified."

Valerius chuckled, a dry, humorless sound. 

"For a moment," the King admitted, "I thought you were actually coming for a dragon."

Samuel stopped admiring the axes. He slowly turned his head, his glowing, golden eyes locking onto the King. The residual heat in the vault suddenly felt much, much warmer. 

Samuel smiled. It wasn't the warm, domestic smile of the Priestess. It was the terrifying, predatory smile of the Smith. 

"I am," Samuel said, his deep voice rumbling through the subterranean chamber. 

Valerius stopped chuckling. The silence in the vault stretched, thick and suffocating. "I... I beg your pardon?"

"I am coming for a dragon, Grandfather," Samuel clarified, taking a step toward the King, the heavy axes resting easily in his massive hands. "But she is not here. I can feel her spark, but it is faint. She is buried deep."

"Samuel, listen to me," Valerius said, his voice dropping to an urgent, panicked whisper. "The dragons have been extinct for a thousand years. They were hunted down before the Empire was even founded. There are no eggs. There are no dragons."

"She is in the Iron Mountain," Samuel stated, completely ignoring the historical facts the Emperor was trying to present. The boy looked past the King, his golden eyes seemingly peering through the solid stone of the Citadel walls, staring far to the West. 

"The... the Iron Mountain?" Valerius stammered. "Samuel, that is the home of the Dwarves. That is beyond the Western Expanse. It is a hostile, sealed kingdom. Even our Legions do not dare march on the Iron Mountain."

Samuel finally looked back at his grandfather, his expression hardening into absolute, unbreakable resolve. 

"I am not a Legion, Grandfather. And I do not march." Samuel secured the heavy axes to the magnetic iron-wood clasps on his back. "I need a map."

Valerius stared at the seven-year-old Demigod. The boy was serious. He was going to ride an elephant-sized dire-wolf across the known world, march into the impenetrable fortress of the Dwarven Kingdom, and somehow retrieve a mythical creature that had been extinct for a millennia. 

And the most terrifying part was that Valerius actually believed he could do it. 

"A map," the King echoed weakly. He nodded slowly, gesturing toward the stairs that led back up to the Grand Archive. "Yes. Of course. Malakor will have the oldest maps of the Western Expanse. We... we will get you a map."

"Thank you, Grandfather," Samuel said politely, walking past the King and heading for the stairs. 

As they left the deepest vault of the Citadel, Valerius realized that the prophecy was already unfolding. The Mountain-Shaker wasn't just preparing a shield for his valley. He was gathering the most dangerous, mythical forces in the world. 

The Ashen Tide was coming. But for the first time in his life, the Emperor of the known world actually felt sorry for the darkness.
