# Chapter 55: Incursion

It was one town. 

It used to be a bustling, heavily fortified trade hub on the very edge of the Borderlands, where the rugged plains of the Kragh nation met the jagged foothills of the deep eastern mountains. It used to be a place of thick stone walls, seasoned Kragh warriors, and the ceaseless hum of commerce. 

And then, in a single night, it wasn't. 

The eastern border of Kragh had been quiet for centuries. The great wars of the past had always been fought to the south, against the Algorian Empire and its Legions. The eastern mountains were simply a geographical barrier, a desolate expanse of grey stone and dead pines that no sane army would ever try to march through. 

But the Ashen Tide didn't march. It surged. 

The first warning wasn't a horn or a scout’s cry. It was a smell. A thick, suffocating stench of sulfur and rotting earth that rolled down from the high passes like a physical fog, choking the torches on the town walls and sending the local livestock into a violent panic. 

Then came the drums. A deep, arrhythmic pounding that didn't sound like military coordination, but rather the heartbeat of something ancient and starving. 

The Horde unit that breached the gates was small—only thirty warriors. But they were not the disorganized, easily broken bandits that occasionally plagued the roads. 

They were led by Greth. 

He was a massive Orc, standing eight feet tall, his skin the color of bruised iron and his eyes burning with an unnatural, sickly green light. He didn't carry a sword or an axe. He wielded a massive pillar of jagged basalt, swung with a terrifying, absolute disregard for his own physical limits. The thirty warriors that followed him shared that same green, corrupted light in their eyes. They felt no pain. They possessed no fear. 

When the Kragh defenders—proud, seasoned veterans of the southern wars—formed a shield wall to repel the breach, Greth didn't stop. He simply smashed his basalt pillar into the center of the formation. The shields shattered. The men broke. 

The slaughter that followed was swift, absolute, and utterly devoid of tactical purpose. The Horde didn't loot the armory. They didn't seize the granaries to supply an advancing army. They simply destroyed. They tore down the stone buildings with their bare hands, set the lumber yards ablaze with strange, green-tinged fire, and butchered anyone who didn't run fast enough. 

By dawn, the town was gone. It had been reduced to a smoking, silent crater of ash and shattered stone. 

***

A week later, the first of the evacuees staggered into the Kragh capital of Vengard. 

They were exhausted, half-starved, and shivering, despite the summer heat. They were brought before the Warlord of Kragh, a hardened man who had spent his life preparing for an invasion from the Algorian Empire. He had expected to hear tales of Imperial Legions crossing the southern river. 

Instead, the scene the evacuees painted was straight out of a nightmare. 

"They didn't want the land," a bleeding, soot-stained merchant wept, clutching the remains of a torn cloak around his shoulders. "They didn't want our gold. The big one... the Orc with the green eyes... he just laughed as the stones burned. They didn't even bleed when our spears hit them. They just kept swinging."

The Warlord listened in grim silence, his council of generals muttering nervously amongst themselves. 

"How many were there?" one of the generals demanded. "A thousand? Two thousand?"

"Thirty," a surviving guardsman whispered from the back of the group, his eyes vacant and staring at nothing. "Just thirty."

A heavy, terrified silence fell over the warlord’s throne room. 

If thirty corrupted warriors could erase a fortified border town in a single night, the mathematical reality of a full-scale invasion was too horrifying to calculate. 

The Warlord stood up from his heavy iron throne, looking out the narrow windows of his citadel toward the East. The horizon was supposed to be clear. But even from hundreds of miles away, he thought he could see a faint, unnatural smudge of grey ash hanging in the sky. 

The ancient legends, the old nursery rhymes meant to scare Kragh children into obedience, were suddenly becoming very, very real. The deep earth had cracked open. 

"Send word to the border forts," the Warlord commanded, his voice tight. "Mobilize every spear, every bow, and every mage we have. Pull the southern garrisons off the Algorian border. I don't care if the Emperor marches on Vengard tomorrow. We have a bigger problem."

He turned to his chief scout. 

"The first town has fallen," the Warlord said, his face pale under his battle scars. "And if there are more of those things coming out of the mountains... time is running extremely short."

The Ashen Tide had officially breached the world of men. And while the Kragh Warlord mobilized his armies, he had no way of knowing that the only force capable of stopping the nightmare was currently divided: a mother in a magical forest, a father in a snowy forge, and a seven-year-old boy riding a dire-wolf toward the impenetrable Iron Mountain.
