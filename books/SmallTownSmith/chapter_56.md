# Chapter 56: The Envoy

The Imperial Throne Room was usually a place of sharp, whispered politics and veiled threats. But when the heavy oak doors swung open to admit the envoy from the Kragh Warlord, the silence in the chamber was absolute. 

The envoy was not a diplomat. He was a seasoned Kragh general, his face scarred and his armor dented. He didn't bow with the customary, exaggerated flourish expected by the Algorian court. He simply walked to the base of the dais, unhooked his heavy broadsword, and laid it flat on the marble floor. 

It was the ultimate sign of submission. And desperation. 

"King Valerius," the Kragh general said, his voice echoing in the cavernous room. "The Borderlands have fallen. The Ashen Tide has breached the mountains. The Warlord sends me not to negotiate borders, but to beg for the survival of men."

Valerius III leaned forward in his throne. He didn't gloat over his ancient enemy's submission. He felt the heavy, terrifying truth of the prophecy settling around his neck. 

"The time for disputes is over," the envoy continued, his voice tight with the memory of the smoking craters in the east. "If we fight each other, the Horde will consume us both. We must unite. But even together, our numbers will not hold them forever. The Orcs do not tire. They do not bleed. What we need, Your Radiance... is time."

Valerius looked down at the Kragh general. "Time for what?"

"Time for the North," the envoy said, his eyes meeting the King's. "Time for the Hope of Twin Rivers to finish their training. We know what is growing in that valley, Your Radiance. We know the Mountain-Shaker is building a Clan. We must hold the line until they are ready to break it."

The Emperor of the known world sat back, feeling a strange, profound sense of relief. For years, he had feared an alliance between Kragh and Twin Rivers against his throne. Instead, the world was looking to his grandson—and the valley his son had built—for salvation. 

"You shall have your time," Valerius declared, standing up from his throne. His voice rang out with the absolute authority of a King going to war not for conquest, but for defense. "Send word to the Warlord. The Legions of Algoria will march east. We will stand shoulder-to-shoulder with the Kragh."

Valerius turned to his own generals, who were watching with wide eyes. 

"Summon the Governor of Oakhaven," Valerius commanded. "And tell him to bring his Imperial Sixth. And more importantly, tell him to bring the instructors from the Twin Rivers Guard."

The generals murmured in confusion. "Sire, the Twin Rivers Guard? They are barely fifty men. Why bring them to a war of tens of thousands?"

"Because," Valerius said, his eyes flashing with the memory of his son's discipline, "the Legions know how to conquer. They know how to charge. But we are fighting an enemy that does not feel pain. A charge will simply feed them. We need to learn how to stand like a wall. We need to learn how to slow an army without bleeding to death in the process. The North knows the way."

Hundreds of miles to the west, far from the politics of the Capital and the ash of the Borderlands, the ancient forest of Alveron was alive with a different kind of energy. 

The Grove was a massive, circular clearing surrounded by towering, silver-barked trees that pulsed with a faint, bioluminescent light. The air smelled of ozone and crushed mint. 

In the center of the clearing stood the ten Valkyries. 

They were only six years old, but their movements were sharp, precise, and completely devoid of childhood clumsiness. They wore light, flexible armor woven from the very vines of the forest, and they held longbows carved from living iron-wood. 

Clara stood at the edge of the clearing, surrounded by three ancient, cloaked women—the Elder Druids of her lineage. The Elders watched the young girls with a mixture of reverence and awe. 

"Draw," Clara commanded, her voice ringing out, clear and calm. 

Ten bowstrings creaked in perfect unison. But there were no physical arrows notched on the strings. 

Instead, as the girls pulled back, the air around them shimmered. The "Verdant Pulse" of the forest answered their call, drawing pure, emerald-green Ki from the earth and condensing it into brilliant, glowing arrows on their bows. 

"They pull from the deep roots," one of the Elder Druids whispered to Clara, her voice trembling. "They do not just use the forest's energy. They command it. We have not seen this since the First Age."

"They have to," Clara replied softly, her golden eyes fixed on her daughters. "The darkness they will face cannot be pierced by iron. It must be pierced by life."

The ten Valkyries held the draw. Their tiny arms didn't shake. Their breathing was perfectly synchronized, a steady, rhythmic intake of Ki that mirrored the exercises Tyler taught the Guard, but refined and elevated to a magical degree. 

At the far end of the clearing, ten heavy stone targets had been set up—boulders the size of wagons. 

"Loose," Clara said gently. 

*Thwack.* 

It sounded like a single bowstring snapping. Ten brilliant, emerald streaks of light tore across the clearing. They didn't just hit the boulders; they passed completely through them, leaving smoking, perfectly round holes in the solid stone before detonating into the treeline with a series of concussive, bright green flashes. 

The sheer force of the volley rustled the leaves of the giant silver trees. 

The Valkyries immediately lowered their bows, returning to a perfectly disciplined resting stance. They didn't cheer. They didn't look for praise. They simply waited for the next command. 

Clara smiled, feeling a surge of profound pride mixed with the heavy responsibility of a mother preparing her children for a nightmare. 

"Good," Clara called out, stepping into the clearing. "Again. But this time, I want you to draw the light tighter. Focus the pulse. Make the stone shatter, not just burn."

The ten girls nodded in unison. "Yes, Mother."

The Hope of Twin Rivers was preparing. In the East, the armies of men were gathering to buy them time. In the Forge, Greg was beginning the brutal process of turning the Fjellbjorn into weapons. And somewhere far to the West, Samuel was hunting for a dragon. 

The world was holding its breath.
