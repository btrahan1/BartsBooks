# Chapter 57: The Fjellbjorn Riders

The heat inside the main forge of Twin Rivers was oppressive, even in the dead of winter. The fires had been burning white-hot for three straight weeks, fueled by the relentless, synchronized labor of the town’s master smiths. 

Silas stepped back from the main anvil, his heavy, soot-stained apron soaked with sweat. His massive arms were shaking with exhaustion as he set his hammer down with a heavy, ringing thud. 

"That's the last of them," Silas breathed, wiping his brow with the back of his hand. 

Resting on a long iron cooling rack were fourteen double-edged War Axes. They were not decorative. They were brutally functional, built from the finest Kragh steel and Algorian silver, tempered with the deep, localized Ki of the valley. Their hafts were thick iron-wood, designed to withstand impacts that would shatter normal steel. 

Greg, who had been working the bellows, stood up and wiped his own soot-stained face. He didn't speak. He walked over to the rack, the heavy heat of the forge seeming to bend around his massive frame. He picked up a pair of the axes, testing their weight. They were perfectly balanced, heavy enough to cleave armor but light enough (for a Demigod’s bloodline, at least) to swing with blinding speed. 

He walked out of the forge, stepping into the crisp, cold air of the training grounds. 

Fifty yards away, the seven Fjellbjorn were running through endurance drills. At five years old, they were the size of full-grown men, their chests thick and their movements radiating a heavy, restless power. They were currently sparring with unsharpened wooden axes, their massive Crag-Bears sitting on the sidelines like furry, multi-ton spectators. 

Greg stopped at the edge of the training circle. The Fjellbjorn immediately sensed the shift in the air. The "Golden Spark" wasn't dormant anymore. It was awake, and it was burning. 

The seven boys lowered their wooden weapons and turned to face their father. 

Greg didn't yell. He didn't issue a traditional military command. He simply bowed his head, a gesture of profound respect to the deadly art he was about to resurrect. 

"Watch," Greg said, his voice a low, echoing rumble. "Learn."

He stepped into the center of the circle. And then, for the first time in over a decade, the Dragon of War woke up. 

Greg exploded into motion. It wasn't the rigid, highly disciplined phalanx fighting he had taught the Guard. It was a fluid, terrifying cascade of violence. He performed a series of forms that had been long buried, but never forgotten. The twin axes became a blur of silver and steel, carving through the freezing air with the sound of a tearing hurricane. He moved with a heavy, brutal grace, every strike flowing seamlessly into the next, generating a localized vortex of golden Ki that kicked up the snow in a blinding spiral. 

When he finally stopped, not a single breath out of place, the seven boys were staring at him with wide, burning eyes. 

They understood. 

Over the next six months, the valley of Twin Rivers became an anvil, and the Fjellbjorn were the steel. They learned, and they learned terrifyingly quickly. 

They didn't just learn to fight from the ground. With Silas’s help, they fashioned heavy, reinforced saddles for their massive Crag-Bears, complete with thick leather holsters for their twin axes. They drilled. They marched. They rode. 

A single Fjellbjorn Rider was a nightmare of heavy cavalry—a mountain of fur, claw, and muscle topped by a dual-wielding giant who fought with the speed of a falling star. 

But the most fearsome sight was when they fought together. 

It was a cold, overcast morning when Tyler, now the grizzled veteran commander of the fifty-man Twin Rivers Guard, lined his men up on the ridge for a joint training exercise. 

The objective was simple: the Guard had to hold their impenetrable shield-wall against a frontal assault. 

The assault force was just seven boys and seven bears. 

When the horn blew, the Fjellbjorn charged. They didn't hit the shield-wall head-on like the mindless Horde. They fought as a dervish. The seven riders moved in perfect, chaotic harmony, forming a spinning, seven-pointed star that moved across the battlefield like a golden comet. They hit the flanks, their bears shattering the heavy shields while the boys rained down a dizzying, relentless flurry of axe strikes. 

The Guard, the most disciplined infantry unit in the known world, actually broke formation in sheer panic. 

It was 7 versus 50. And within three minutes, Tyler was pinned to the ground by a massive paw, laughing helplessly as the blunt edge of a wooden war axe rested lightly against his neck. 

As the seven Crag-Bears roared in victory and the Fjellbjorn boys raised their axes to the sky, the very bedrock of the valley shook. 

Greg stood on the porch of the Farm, watching the dust settle over the ridge. He had done it. He had taught them the fire of the Dragon, and they had not lost their souls. They fought with fury, but they stopped when the battle was won. 

The heavy infantry was ready. Now, they just had to wait for the Mountain-Shaker to return.
