# Chapter 58: The Iron Mountain

The Dwarves of the Iron Mountain were a paranoid, isolated, and highly fortified people. They had survived the rise and fall of human empires, the ancient wars of the elves, and the bitter cold of the Western Expanse by adhering to one simple rule: trust nothing that comes from the surface. 

Their outer defenses were legendary. The slopes of the Iron Mountain were rigged with collapsing stone-traps, invisible tripwires forged from spider-silk, and heavy ballistae hidden behind illusory rock faces. 

But as the deep-scouts of the Stone Guard stood at their subterranean listening posts, they realized their traps were going to be entirely useless. 

They heard him coming long before they saw him. 

It wasn't the sound of an approaching army, nor the stealthy footfalls of an assassin. It was a rhythmic, heavy, terrifying *thud* that vibrated through the solid bedrock of the mountain itself. The deep-scouts pressed their ears to the sounding-pipes, their beards bristling in alarm. 

"Is it a siege engine?" a young scout whispered, adjusting his iron helmet. 

"No," an older, grizzled Dwarf replied, his eyes wide. "Siege engines creak. They roll. This... this is walking. And it's heavy."

Above ground, the Iron Mountain actually groaned as Samuel and Wolfie made their way up the treacherous, winding trail. 

The journey from the Imperial Capital, across the sprawling plains of the Empire and through the frozen badlands of the Western Expanse, had been long. It had taken months. And in that time, the "Verdant Pulse" and the "Golden Spark" within Samuel had continued their impossible, exponential growth. 

He was still only seven years old, but he was no longer just six feet tall. He had grown even further. He sat astride Wolfie—who was now the size of a small siege tower—looking less like a child and more like an ancient, mythological titan returning to the world of men. The Twin Axes of the Dragon were secured to his back, their black metal drinking in the weak winter sunlight. 

Samuel didn't trigger the traps. The sheer, overwhelming density of his Ki simply crushed the mechanisms before they could spring. Stone pressure-plates cracked under Wolfie's paws, and illusory walls shattered like glass as the "Mountain-Shaker" drew near. 

Finally, Wolfie stopped. 

They stood on a wide, flat plateau of grey stone that looked like a dead end. To any human eye, it was just the sheer, impassable cliff face of the mountain. 

But Samuel wasn't looking with human eyes. He was looking with the "Restless Spark." He could see the faint, geometric lines of Ki running through the stone, the massive subterranean hinges, and the dense concentration of terrified Dwarven life-forces gathered on the other side. 

He had found the hidden entrance. 

Samuel dismounted, his heavy boots hitting the plateau with a resounding crack that echoed down the valley. He walked right up to the sheer rock face. 

Behind the stone, hundreds of heavily armed Dwarven defenders tightened their grips on their hammers, waiting for the titan to strike the door. 

Samuel didn't strike the door. He didn't even draw the axes. 

He simply took a deep breath, filling his massive chest with the freezing mountain air, and let out a roar that carried the full, unadulterated weight of his bloodline. 

"I am Samuel!" his voice boomed. 

The sound was a physical force. It hit the hidden stone door with the impact of a battering ram, vibrating the heavy locking mechanisms so violently that the Dwarven engineers on the other side had to cover their ears in agony. 

"Wielder of the Dragon Flame Axes!" Samuel continued, the golden fire in his eyes flaring as he unhooked the massive weapons from his back and brought them down, crossing them in front of his chest. The blades ignited, casting a brilliant, searing light across the desolate plateau. 

He took one final step toward the mountain, his voice dropping into a heavy, absolute command that demanded the attention of the very earth. 

"I am here for my Dragon Egg!" 

Inside the mountain, silence fell over the Stone Guard. The King Under the Mountain, a dwarf whose reign had lasted three centuries, slowly pushed his way to the front of the defensive line. He looked at the massive stone door, which was currently glowing hot from the sheer proximity of the golden Ki radiating outside. 

"A Dragon Egg," the Dwarven King whispered, his eyes wide with a mixture of terror and awe. "By the Ancestors... he knows."

The King turned to his engineers, his face pale. 

"Open the doors," he commanded. "Before he decides to just walk through them."
