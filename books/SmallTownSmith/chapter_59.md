# Chapter 59: The Challenge

The mountain groaned again, this time from the inside. 

With a grinding shriek of stone against stone, the massive, concealed doors of the Iron Mountain slowly parted. Plumes of trapped, subterranean air blasted out onto the plateau, carrying the scent of deep forge-fires and cold iron. 

Samuel stood his ground, the golden fire of his Twin Axes casting long, dancing shadows across the grey rock. Beside him, Wolfie let out a low, rumbling growl that vibrated through the boots of the Dwarven vanguard waiting inside. 

As the dust settled, the Stone Guard parted, creating a wide aisle. Down this aisle walked the King Under the Mountain. 

King Algor was a mountain of a dwarf. Where the average dwarven warrior was closer to four and a half feet tall, and nearly the same in width, Algor was an absolute giant of his race. He stood six feet tall and an astounding five feet wide, a literal cube of hyper-dense muscle, scar tissue, and heavily plated Mithril armor. His beard was the color of crushed iron, braided with rings of solid gold, and in his hands, he held a two-handed war-hammer that looked like it could level a small city block. 

To the average man, Algor was a terror to behold. He was the unstoppable force and the immovable object perfectly combined into one reigning monarch. 

But Samuel was not an average man. 

As Algor stepped out onto the plateau, the two giants—one a three-century-old King, the other a seven-year-old Demigod—locked eyes. 

For most of the heavily armed Stone Guard, the tension was suffocating. They expected a diplomatic demand, a furious exchange of threats, or an immediate clash of armies. 

Instead, an unspoken communication flickered between the two combatants. It was a language older than words, a primitive, undeniable recognition of mutual, terrifying power. Algor looked at the golden fire burning in Samuel’s eyes, recognizing the ancient "Dragon" lineage that his ancestors had fought alongside—and sometimes against—in the First Age. Samuel looked at the sheer, unyielding density of the Dwarven King, recognizing a martial spirit that mirrored his father’s "Shield-Wall" discipline, but magnified a hundredfold. 

Algor didn't ask why the boy was there. He didn't ask how a human child had grown so large. He knew what the boy wanted, and he knew that words would not decide the outcome. In the deep places of the world, possession of the ancient artifacts was only proven by one thing: the strength to hold them. 

Algor slowly raised his massive war-hammer, resting the heavy, rune-covered head against his thick shoulder. He let out a deep, booming laugh that echoed off the cliffs. 

"You have the fire of the Old Ones, boy!" Algor roared, his voice sounding like two boulders grinding together. "But fire alone does not melt the Iron Mountain!"

Samuel smiled. It was a bright, terrifyingly joyous expression. He didn't see an enemy. He saw a test. A crucible to see if the training he had observed from his father, and the Ki he had inherited from his mother, were truly enough to face the Ashen Tide. 

Samuel stepped away from Wolfie, who immediately backed off to give the combatants room. 

The young Lord of the Silverun raised his Twin Axes. As his grip tightened, the golden "Restless Spark" flared with explosive intensity. The dark metal of the blades burst into a roaring, brilliant golden flame that melted the frost off the plateau in an instant. 

"Then let us dance, King Algor!" Samuel bellowed, his voice echoing back at the dwarf with equal, staggering force. "As they did in the legends!"

Algor roared in response, charging forward with a speed that utterly defied his massive, five-foot-wide frame. He brought the war-hammer down in a crushing overhead arc, intending to end the challenge in a single, earth-shattering blow. 

Samuel didn't dodge. He didn't rely on his speed. He brought both of the blazing Dragon Axes up, crossing the hafts to catch the hammer. 

*CLANG!*

The impact was deafening. A shockwave of golden Ki and blue dwarven runes exploded outward, knocking the front row of the Stone Guard off their feet and sending a shower of sparks raining down on the plateau. 

The bedrock beneath Samuel’s boots cracked, spider-webbing outward for twenty yards. 

But Samuel didn't buckle. He held the blow, his muscles straining, his golden eyes locked onto Algor’s astonished gaze through the shower of sparks. 

The dance had begun. And the Iron Mountain was about to find out exactly why they called him the Mountain-Shaker.
