# Chapter 60: Clash of The Titans

Few of the Dwarves alive today had ever actually seen their King fight. 

The Iron Mountain had been strictly isolated for decades, avoiding the petty squabbles of the human empires. To the majority of the Stone Guard, King Algor was a towering figurehead of absolute authority, a monarch who ruled from the safety of the deep halls. 

But the elders remembered. The last time King Algor had swung his hammer in earnest had been over a century ago, deep within the lowest, most dangerous strata of the mountain. A scouting team of Draguar—the twisted, ash-skinned Dark Dwarves of the deep earth—had breached a primary mining chamber. The Draguar were merciless and seemingly immune to pain. Dozens of Iron Mountain defenders had fallen, and the line was about to break. 

And then, Algor had arrived. 

He didn't just fight the Draguar; he erased them. In a terrifying, unstoppable whirlwind of crushed stone and shattered steel, Algor had single-handedly decimated the entire Dark Dwarf scouting party, sending the few survivors fleeing back into the abyss before he ordered the chamber permanently sealed with solid iron. 

The legends of that day had grown with every passing decade. And so, as the Stone Guard watched the seven-year-old Surface Dweller catch Algor’s opening strike, they were shocked, but entirely unconcerned about the final outcome. The boy had managed to block one blow. Obviously, he had no chance of surviving the second. 

Or so they thought. 

Algor pulled his hammer back, expecting the boy’s arms to buckle from the sheer recoil. Instead, Samuel pushed forward. The Twin Axes flared with blinding golden light, and Samuel unleashed a flurry of strikes that forced the five-foot-wide King to actually take a step back. 

The plateau became a crucible of fire and stone. 

Samuel and Algor fought for an hour. It was not a duel of finesse or careful parries. It was a brutal, apocalyptic clash of pure, unadulterated physical power. Algor’s hammer crushed the stone beneath Samuel’s feet, creating craters that the boy used to launch himself into the air. Samuel’s axes carved deep, glowing gouges into the sheer cliff face, raining molten rock down onto the battlefield. 

The sound of their weapons colliding echoed across the Western Expanse like continuous, rolling thunder. The Stone Guard had long since abandoned their defensive formations, scrambling up the rock faces just to avoid being pulverized by the shockwaves. 

But the most terrifying aspect of the duel was not the destruction. It was the noise coming from the combatants. 

They weren't screaming in anger. They were laughing. 

By the end of the hour, both of the giants were grinning like absolute madmen, reveling in the sheer, exhilarating joy of finally finding an opponent who didn't shatter after the first strike. 

Algor swung his hammer in a massive, sweeping arc that tore a boulder cleanly in half. Samuel dropped into a crouch, letting the hammer pass over his head, and brought both axes up in an uppercut that caught the heavy, rune-plated shaft of the hammer perfectly in the center. 

The resulting explosion of blue and gold Ki threw them both backward. 

Algor skidded across the ruined plateau, his heavy boots carving two deep trenches in the solid stone before he finally dug his heels in and stopped. 

The King Under the Mountain let his hammer fall to the ground with a heavy thud. His legendary, impenetrable Mithril armor was in tatters, scored with glowing, golden scorch marks. His braided beard was singed, and his massive chest heaved as his breath came in great, ragged gasps. 

Across the cratered battlefield, Samuel landed gracefully. 

He wasn't gasping. His breathing was steady, a perfect, rhythmic cycle of the "Verdant Pulse" that was already rushing to replenish his stamina. His custom armor was dented, and he was covered in grey stone dust, but he didn't look defeated. He looked like he was just getting warmed up. 

Samuel stood tall, the golden fire fading from the blades of his axes as he rested them against his shoulders. The bright, joyous smile of the Smith split his face. 

"The Legends are true!" Samuel called out, his voice ringing clear and strong across the ruined stone. He offered a deep, respectful bow. "Well met, Algor!"

Algor leaned heavily on the haft of his hammer, staring at the boy. His lungs burned, his muscles ached with a fatigue he hadn't felt in a century, and his favorite armor was utterly ruined. 

The Dwarven King threw his head back and let out a roaring, booming laugh that shook the remaining snow from the peaks. 

"Well met, indeed, Mountain-Shaker!" Algor bellowed back, wiping sweat and soot from his eyes. He looked at the shattered plateau, then at his terrified, awe-struck guards, and finally back at the seven-year-old Demigod. 

"Ancestors preserve us," Algor chuckled, shaking his head. "If you hit that hard with a pair of axes, boy... I am terrified to see what you intend to do with a Dragon."
