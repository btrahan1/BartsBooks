# Chapter 61: Elsie

The deepest chamber of the Iron Mountain did not look like a treasury. It looked like the fiery heart of the world itself. 

King Algor, clad in a fresh tunic after discarding the ruined remnants of his Mithril armor, led Samuel across a narrow bridge of obsidian that spanned a massive, churning lake of magma. The heat was absolute, radiating upward in thick, shimmering waves that would have baked an ordinary man alive in seconds. 

Samuel walked casually, entirely unaffected, his golden eyes scanning the cavern. 

In the center of the magma lake, rising on a solitary spire of black rock, sat a thick container forged from volcanic glass. The glass sparkled, catching the intense, hellish light of the lava below. 

Inside the container, resting on a bed of dark ash, was the Dragon Egg. 

It was the size of a barrel, its surface covered in overlapping, iridescent scales that shifted between deep ruby and molten gold. Even encased in glass, the egg pulsed with a faint, impossibly old Ki—the source of the spark Samuel had felt from thousands of miles away. 

Algor stopped at the edge of the spire, staring through the glass. 

"My ancestors found it when they first hollowed out this mountain," Algor said, his deep voice carrying over the low roar of the magma. "We built the glass to contain its heat, though the egg itself has been dormant since the First Age. What makes you think you can hatch it, boy? It’s been sitting right here since long before I was born."

Samuel stepped up beside the Dwarven King, placing one large, calloused hand flat against the hot glass. 

"Her name is Elsie," Samuel said softly. 

Algor raised an eyebrow. "'Elsie'? That’s a rather domestic name for a flying nightmare."

"My mother likes the name," Samuel replied, a faint, genuine smile touching his lips. He closed his eyes, sending a pulse of golden Ki through the glass, feeling the dormant, ancient spark inside resonate with his own. "And she won't hatch here, King Algor. There is no 'Verdant Pulse' in this stone to wake her. But she will hatch in Twin Rivers. Of that, I am certain."

Algor grunted, crossing his massive arms over his chest. "Even if you do manage to wake the beast, it will be entirely useless for the coming war. Dragons are ancient creatures. It will take decades for her to grow large enough to even carry a rider, let alone fight."

Samuel turned his head, his golden eyes meeting Algor’s. His smile widened, carrying that familiar, terrifying edge of the Mountain-Shaker. 

"I am seven years old, Algor," Samuel pointed out simply. "And I am currently looking down at the tallest dwarf in recorded history. I promise you, growth rates in Twin Rivers do not follow the usual rules. It won't take that long. Trust me."

Algor looked at the six-foot-tall, muscle-bound child in front of him. The dwarf sighed heavily, accepting the undeniable logic of the statement. If the boy could bend biology that drastically for himself, a rapidly-aging dragon was entirely within the realm of possibility. 

The King turned away from the egg, his expression darkening as he stared into the churning magma. 

"I will let you take the egg," Algor said, his voice dropping into a stubborn, royal register. "You earned it on the plateau. But if you think this buys you an army, you are mistaken. You know that we won't join the wars of men, Samuel. We have long put that past us. The Empire is not our concern."

"I know," Samuel replied, his tone perfectly calm. He didn't sound disappointed. He sounded like a general setting the final piece on a tactical board. "I am not asking you to march with the Legions. I invoke The Pact."

Algor froze. He slowly turned his head back to the boy, his jaw clenched. 

"The Pact?" Algor whispered, the word carrying a heavy, terrifying historical weight. 

Samuel stared off into the fiery expanse of the cavern, his golden eyes reflecting the ancient history he was calling upon. "Before the Empire, before the Kragh, there was The Pact. The agreement between the Surface and the Deep. If the Ashen Tide rises, the Dark Dwarves will rise with it."

Algor’s hands balled into tight fists. He knew exactly what the Draguar were capable of. 

"I don't want you to fight the wars of man, Algor," Samuel said, his voice ringing with the absolute authority of his bloodline. "The Legions will hold the plains. The Valkyries will hold the forests. My Fjellbjorn will hold the vanguard. But the Horde is coming from beneath the mountains."

Samuel turned back to the King, reaching out and clapping a heavy hand onto Algor’s thick shoulder. 

"I need you to keep the Draguar from joining the Horde," Samuel commanded. "If they breach the surface and hit the Legions from below, the world falls. You must hold the deep pass. You must hold the Iron Mountain."

Algor looked at the hand on his shoulder, feeling the immense, unyielding pressure of the boy’s will. He looked back at the dragon egg, the symbol of the ancient world waking up to fight the darkness. 

The Dwarven King didn't kneel. He didn't need to. He simply offered a slow, solemn nod. 

"The deep passes will not fall," Algor vowed, his voice rumbling with the combined strength of his entire kingdom. "The Draguar will not see the sun. The Iron Mountain will hold the line."

Samuel nodded respectfully. He turned, smashed his fist through the volcanic glass as easily as breaking a pane of ice, and lifted the massive dragon egg into his arms. 

"Then the anvil is set," Samuel said, turning back toward the long, winding path to the surface. "Now, it's time to light the forge."
