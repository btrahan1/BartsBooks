# Chapter 62: Detour

The journey back from the Iron Mountain was significantly slower than the journey there, but only because Samuel was riding with one hand. 

His left arm was wrapped securely around the massive, heavy bulk of the dragon egg. Elsie was currently swaddled in a thick, woolen blanket to hide the iridescent shifting of her scales, resting against Samuel’s armored chest like an impossibly dense, magical boulder. 

Wolfie loped across the expansive plains of the western Empire, his ten-foot-tall frame eating up the miles with a steady, tireless rhythm. The great beast seemed to understand the fragility of their cargo, keeping his gait smooth and avoiding the shattering leaps he had used to travel to the Capital. 

They were still a week away from the Crag-Shadows when the smell hit them. 

Wolfie stopped abruptly, his massive paws digging into the soft earth of the plains. His ears swiveled forward, and a low, rumbling growl vibrated in his chest. His one good eye locked onto the eastern horizon. 

Samuel felt it a second later. It wasn't just a smell; it was an absence. A localized void in the natural Ki of the world, reeking of sulfur and rotting earth. 

He looked toward the horizon and saw the smoke. It wasn't the clean, white smoke of a forge or the steady grey of a hearth fire. It was thick, oily, and laced with a sickly green light that stained the clouds above it. 

"The Tide," Samuel murmured, his golden eyes narrowing. 

He knew from his Grandfather’s reports that the Imperial Army was currently spread dangerously thin across a five-hundred-mile front. Valerius and the Kragh Warlord were bleeding their forces dry just trying to contain the surging masses of the Horde. In the chaos of the massive logistical deployment, smaller, elite units of corrupted Orcs were slipping through the gaps, raiding deep into the unprotected heartlands to spread terror and destroy supply lines. 

And judging by the specific, terrifying density of the green Ki burning in the distance, Samuel knew exactly who was leading this particular raid. 

It was Greth. The massive, iron-skinned Orc and his elite unit of thirty corrupted warriors had found another target. A small, unfortified farming town nestled in a valley, miles away from the nearest Imperial garrison. 

Samuel looked down at the dragon egg in his left arm. Elsie pulsed warmly against his chest, her ancient spark humming in harmony with his own. He looked back at the smoke. He could feel the panic of the villagers, their bright, fragile sparks being rapidly snuffed out by the encroaching darkness. 

The Demigod didn't hesitate. He shifted his grip, securing the heavy egg tighter against his side, and reached back with his right hand to grasp the black haft of one of the Dragon Axes. 

"An hour or two shouldn't matter," Samuel said softly, the golden fire beginning to flare in his eyes. He leaned forward, pressing his cheek against the Alpha’s thick, slate-grey fur. "What do you say, Wolfie? Feel like a detour?"

Wolfie didn't howl. He simply bared his dagger-like fangs, his muscles coiling tight, and launched himself toward the smoke. 

***

The town of Oakhaven-West was already lost. 

The local militia, comprised mostly of farmers with pitchforks and a few retired guardsmen, had broken in the first three minutes. Greth stood in the center of the town square, his massive basalt pillar resting on his shoulder. His green eyes burned with a sickly, corrupted joy as he watched his thirty warriors tear through the wooden houses, setting the thatch roofs ablaze with magical, green fire. 

The Orcs didn't rush. They moved with the terrifying, inevitable slowness of a nightmare, reveling in the slaughter. 

Greth raised his pillar to smash the door of the local tavern, where dozens of villagers had barricaded themselves inside. 

Before the basalt could strike the wood, the sky above the square seemed to shatter. 

A shadow the size of a siege tower blotted out the midday sun. Wolfie hit the center of the square with a concussive, earth-shaking impact that threw four of the corrupted Orcs completely off their feet. The giant dire-wolf landed directly between Greth and the tavern doors, his fangs snapping an Orc's spear in half before the warrior even realized he was under attack. 

Greth stumbled back, his corrupted mind struggling to process the sudden, overwhelming presence that had just dropped from the sky. 

Sitting atop the massive wolf was a human boy. He was holding a swaddled bundle in his left arm. In his right hand, he held an axe forged of black metal, its double-bearded blade burning with a blinding, roaring golden fire. 

Greth let out a guttural roar, swinging his basalt pillar directly at the boy's head. The strike carried enough force to shatter a castle wall. 

Samuel didn't dodge. Still sitting calmly on Wolfie's back, still holding the dragon egg securely in his left arm, Samuel brought his right hand up in a tight, blindingly fast arc. 

The burning blade of the Dragon Axe met the basalt pillar in mid-air. 

The green, corrupted energy of the Horde clashed instantly against the pure, golden "Restless Spark" of the Smith's bloodline. 

There was no contest. 

The basalt pillar didn't just break; it exploded into fine, grey dust. The golden fire from the axe traveled up the shattered weapon, washing over Greth’s arm and instantly incinerating the corrupted flesh. 

Greth, a monster who had felt no pain for centuries, fell to his knees and let out a shriek of absolute, unfiltered agony. 

The remaining twenty-nine Orcs froze, turning toward the center of the square. 

Samuel looked down at the massive, burning Orc leader, his expression devoid of pity. He raised the blazing Dragon Axe, the golden fire reflecting in his ancient, terrifying eyes. 

"You are trespassing," Samuel said, his voice rumbling over the crackle of the burning buildings. 

The detour had officially begun. And the Horde was about to learn that while the Legions fought with steel, the North fought with Fire.
