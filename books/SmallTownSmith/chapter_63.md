# Chapter 63: Hold this for me, please.

The shriek of the Orc leader echoed through the burning square of Oakhaven-West, a sound so utterly foreign to the corrupted Horde that the remaining twenty-nine warriors completely stopped their slaughter. They stared at Greth, who was writhing in the dirt, clutching the charred, smoking stump of his right arm. 

Then, they stared at the boy sitting on the giant wolf. 

Samuel didn't seem particularly interested in their shock. He slid off Wolfie's back with a smooth, heavy grace. The earth beneath his boots cracked slightly as he absorbed his own momentum. 

He didn't draw his second axe immediately. Instead, he turned his back on the twenty-nine bloodthirsty monsters and walked calmly toward the ruined front of the local tavern. 

The heavy oak doors had been splintered by Greth’s initial assault, leaving a jagged, gaping hole that looked directly into the barricaded taproom. Inside, dozens of terrified villagers—farmers clutching pitchforks, mothers shielding their children, and an old barkeep gripping a rusty short-sword—were huddled in the darkness, expecting death. 

Instead, a seven-year-old giant in glowing armor stepped through the debris. 

Samuel walked right up to the main bar, pushing aside a shattered stool. The old barkeep, his hands shaking so violently he could barely hold his sword, stared up at the boy’s radiant, golden eyes. 

Samuel gently unswaddled the massive, heavy bundle he had been carrying. He placed the dragon egg—still encased in the remnants of its volcanic glass container—carefully onto the scarred, sticky wood of the tavern counter. 

The egg pulsed with a warm, deep ruby light, pushing back the shadows of the taproom. 

Samuel looked at the terrified barkeep and offered a polite, surprisingly gentle smile. 

"Hold this for me, please," Samuel said simply. 

He didn't wait for an answer. He turned on his heel and walked back out through the splintered doorway, stepping into the ash-choked air of the town square. 

The Orcs had recovered from their initial shock. Their sickly green eyes flared with renewed, corrupted malice. They tightened their grips on their rusted swords and jagged spears, forming a crude half-circle around the boy and the wolf, recognizing him as the only true threat in the valley. 

Samuel reached over his shoulder. His right hand still gripped the blazing haft of the first axe. With his left, he unhooked the second. 

He brought the Twin Axes of the Dragon down in front of him. 

*CLANG!*

He smashed the heavy, double-bearded blades together. The impact sent a concussive shockwave of pure, golden Ki rippling across the square. A blinding torrent of fire erupted from the dark metal, swirling around Samuel in a localized vortex that instantly vaporized the falling ash and melted the snow in a thirty-yard radius. 

The sheer, overwhelming heat of the "Restless Spark" pushed the corrupted Orcs back a half-step. 

Samuel rolled his shoulders, the heavy armor shifting comfortably against his massive frame. He settled into the low, brutal stance of the Dragon—the exact same stance Greg had used to decimate the bandit camp years ago. 

He looked at the twenty-nine monsters surrounding him, his golden eyes burning with an ancient, furious joy. 

"Hurry up, then," Samuel rumbled, his voice carrying over the roar of the magical flames. "I'm on a tight schedule!"

The Orcs roared, a chaotic, guttural sound of pure violence, and charged. 

They didn't last three minutes. 

Samuel didn't just fight; he became a tempest. The Twin Axes moved faster than the human eye could track, carving through the corrupted armor and thick, unnatural flesh of the Horde as if they were made of dry parchment. The green, sickly magic of the Orcs sparked and died the moment it touched the golden fire of the blades. 

Wolfie didn't even join the fray. The Alpha simply sat in front of the tavern door, his one good eye tracking the golden blur as Samuel systematically dismantled the elite unit. The giant wolf let out a wide, dog-like yawn, clearly unimpressed by the Horde's capabilities. 

Inside the tavern, the villagers pressed their faces against the splintered wood, watching in absolute, silent awe. They had expected to die today. Instead, they were watching a myth come to life, a singular force of nature that burned away the darkness with every swing of his axes. 

When the last Orc fell, reduced to a pile of smoldering ash, the square fell silent. The only sound was the crackle of the remaining fires, which were quickly dying out as Samuel’s pure Ki cleansed the corruption from the air. 

Samuel stood in the center of the square, perfectly still. He didn't have a scratch on his armor. He wasn't even breathing hard. 

He let the golden fire fade from the axes, hooking them back onto his magnetic clasps with a sharp, metallic click. He turned and walked back to the tavern, stepping carefully over the scorch marks. 

The old barkeep, still staring blankly at the destruction outside, jumped slightly as Samuel approached the counter. 

"Thank you," Samuel said politely, picking up the dragon egg and securing it securely under his left arm. "I apologize for the mess. But I really must be going. My father is expecting me."

He gave the stunned villagers a small wave, walked back out to Wolfie, and effortlessly vaulted onto the Alpha’s massive back. 

"Home, Wolfie," Samuel commanded softly. 

The wolf launched himself into the air, clearing the burning remnants of the town wall in a single bound, and disappeared into the eastern horizon, leaving the saved village of Oakhaven-West with a story that no one in the Empire would ever believe.
