# Chapter 64: Homecoming

The journey back to Twin Rivers took longer than Samuel had originally anticipated. With the detours required to clear out two more corrupted Horde warbands that had slipped past the Imperial lines, it was late spring by the time the familiar, jagged peaks of the Crag-Shadows finally came into view. 

As Wolfie crested the final ridge overlooking the valley, the great beast let out a long, resonant howl that echoed down into the basin. 

It was immediately answered by the deep, booming roars of the seven Fjellbjorn Crag-Bears, and the bright, piercing calls of the entire local wolf pack. 

The valley below didn't just wake up; it erupted. 

The massive basalt gates of the "Peace-Wall" swung open. The miners and weavers poured out of their shops, lining the main thoroughfare. The fifty men of the Twin Rivers Guard, standing in perfect, impenetrable formation on the training grounds, raised their iron-wood spears to the sky and let out a unified, thunderous cheer. 

Samuel had left as a boy on a quest. He was returning as a legend. 

The armor he wore was scored with the green, glowing ash of the Horde, a stark testament to the battles he had fought across the plains. The legendary Twin Axes of the Dragon were secured to his back, their dark metal humming with a suppressed, terrifying power. But it was the massive, iridescent bundle secured in his left arm that drew every eye in the village. 

He rode straight through the cheering crowds, offering polite nods to the villagers, and guided Wolfie directly to the center of the square. 

The Hearth of the Priestess was blazing, the eternal fire casting a warm, golden light across the cobblestones. Standing on the Temple steps were Greg and Clara. 

Greg looked at the dark, double-bearded axes strapped to his son's back. The Smith's face was completely unreadable, but the subtle relaxation of his massive shoulders spoke volumes. The Dragon's weapons were home. 

Clara, however, was not looking at the axes. Her golden eyes were fixed entirely on the large, shifting bundle in Samuel’s arm. 

Samuel dismounted with his usual, impossibly smooth grace. He walked up the Temple steps, his heavy boots clicking softly against the stone, and stopped in front of his mother. 

With a gentleness that completely belied the devastating power he possessed, the seven-year-old giant unswaddled the volcanic glass container. He pulled the massive, iridescent egg from its bed of ash and placed it carefully into Clara’s waiting hands. 

The moment the egg touched the Priestess, the "Verdant Pulse" of the valley surged. 

The grass between the cobblestones instantly shot up three inches. The leaves on the Great Oak shimmered with a brilliant, emerald light. Inside the egg, the faint, dormant spark that Samuel had sensed in the Iron Mountain suddenly flared to life, pulsing in a strong, steady rhythm that matched Clara’s own heartbeat. 

"She's beautiful," Clara whispered, tears welling in her glowing eyes as she felt the immense, ancient life-force stirring within the shell. 

"Her name is Elsie," Samuel said, his deep voice softening. He looked at the egg, then up at his mother. "Take care of her, please. The pulse of the valley is strong, but she needs your warmth to finish growing."

Clara nodded, pulling the heavy egg tightly against her chest. "How long, Samuel?"

Samuel looked out over the square. He saw the ten Valkyries standing in perfect formation near the treeline, their living-wood bows resting lightly in their hands. He saw the seven Fjellbjorn boys sitting atop their massive, armored Crag-Bears, waiting for his command. He saw the fifty men of the Unbreakable Guard, the veteran core of their defense. 

"She should be hatching on my eighth birthday," Samuel announced, his voice carrying effortlessly across the silent, attentive square. 

He turned back to his parents. The quiet, domestic peace of the Farm was officially over. The crucible had finished forging the steel. Now, it was time to test the blade. 

"We have a year," Samuel said, his golden eyes locking onto his father’s. "The Horde is testing the Imperial lines, and the Emperor is buying us time. But the Ashen Tide will eventually break through."

Samuel unhooked the Twin Axes of the Dragon from his back, the heavy metal clanking loudly in the quiet afternoon air. He held them loosely at his sides, the golden "Restless Spark" beginning to flicker to life along the edges of the blades. 

He looked at the Valkyries. He looked at the Fjellbjorn. He looked at the Guard. 

"Rest well tonight," the Mountain-Shaker commanded, his voice rumbling with the terrifying, absolute certainty of a general preparing for the apocalypse. 

"Because tomorrow, the Hope of Twin Rivers begins its real training."
