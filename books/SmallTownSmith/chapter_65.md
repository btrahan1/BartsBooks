# Chapter 65: Kargaroth The Invincible

If Samuel was a mountain—a dense, unbreakable foundation of life and fire—then Kargaroth was an abyss. 

Deep beneath the blackened, jagged peaks of the eastern ranges, in a throne room carved from hollowed-out obsidian and lit only by the sickly green glow of corrupted Ki, sat the undisputed master of the Ashen Tide. 

Kargaroth had waited centuries for this moment. He had watched from the shadows as the human empires fractured and fought over petty borders. He had watched the Dwarves seal themselves away in their Iron Mountain, too paranoid to forge alliances. He had watched the Elves retreat into the deep woods of Alveron, abandoning the world of men to its fate. 

The world was divided, weak, and perfectly ripe for the taking. 

Kargaroth had not always been a creature of the dark. Long ago, before the first Algorian Emperor had even drawn a map, Kargaroth had been an Orc. A warlord of unmatched brutality. But mortality was a weakness he had refused to accept. He had sold his soul to the abyss in exchange for eternity, shedding his flesh to become the Lich King—a towering, skeletal nightmare draped in rusted iron and tattered, burial-shroud robes. 

There was nothing left in the world that could stop him. Or so he had believed until this morning. 

"What do you mean, Greth is dead?" 

Kargaroth’s voice did not echo in the cavernous throne room. It seemed to absorb the sound, rasping through the dark like broken glass grinding against a tombstone. 

Kneeling on the cold obsidian floor before the throne was Viola. She was Kargaroth’s spymaster, a pale, gaunt human woman who had traded her humanity for a sliver of dark magic and the promise of power in the new world. 

Right now, however, she was simply trying to survive the next five minutes. 

Viola swallowed hard, her throat clicking audibly in the suffocating silence of the throne room. The green flames burning in the braziers around the room flickered, reflecting the Lich King’s sudden, cold fury. 

Greth was not just a foot soldier. He had been Kargaroth’s greatest vanguard, a heavily corrupted juggernaut who was supposed to carve a path through the unprotected Imperial heartlands. He was supposed to be unkillable by conventional steel. 

"The... the reports from the surviving deep-scouts are somewhat vague, My Lord," Viola stammered, keeping her eyes firmly fixed on the polished obsidian floor. 

"Vague," Kargaroth repeated, the temperature in the room dropping another ten degrees. A thin layer of frost began to form on Viola’s dark cloak. "Greth possessed the strength of fifty men. He felt no pain. He was accompanied by a full unit of my elite guard. Explain to me how 'vague' reports account for his total annihilation in an unfortified farming village."

Viola’s mind raced. The deep-scouts had actually been very specific. They had watched from the treeline as a seven-year-old boy, riding a giant wolf and carrying a bundle on his arm, had dropped from the sky and vaporized the entire elite unit in under three minutes using a pair of flaming axes. 

But Viola was a survivor. And she knew that if she looked the Lich King in his glowing, hollow eye sockets and told him that his greatest vanguard had been casually butchered by a heavily armed toddler who was running errands, Kargaroth would likely flay the skin from her bones just for insulting his intelligence. 

"It was an ambush, My Lord," Viola lied smoothly, pulling her cloak tighter against the unnatural cold. "The Empire must have anticipated our raid. A massive, hidden force. At least a hundred elite warriors, heavily armed with magical artifacts."

Kargaroth leaned forward on his throne of bones, the green light in his sockets narrowing. "A hundred warriors. The Legions do not move a hundred men without my scouts seeing their supply lines. Who led them?"

Viola swallowed again, carefully weaving a grain of truth into the lie to make it believable. 

"They were led by a massive beast, My Lord. A wolf the size of a siege tower," Viola reported, her voice trembling with manufactured awe. "And... and a knight of some kind. A giant of a man, clad in radiant armor, wielding weapons of pure fire. They overwhelmed Greth with sheer numbers and magical artillery before he could establish a defensive perimeter."

Kargaroth sat back, his skeletal fingers drumming a slow, rhythmic beat on the armrest of his throne. 

A knight of fire and a giant wolf. It sounded like an elite Imperial strike team, perhaps a specialized unit Valerius had kept hidden in reserve. It was annoying, but tactically, it made sense. It was a logical, understandable military loss. 

"The Emperor is more resourceful than I anticipated," Kargaroth rasped, the frost retreating slightly from the floor as his fury shifted back into cold, calculating strategy. "He wastes his hidden elites defending useless farming villages."

"Exactly, My Lord," Viola agreed quickly, feeling a massive wave of relief wash over her. "A desperate move. They cannot sustain such tactics."

"Let them have their minor victory," Kargaroth dismissed, raising a skeletal hand. "Greth was a blunt instrument. We will simply apply more force. Send word to the deep caverns. Wake the Draguar. If the surface dwellers wish to play with fire, we will drown them in ash."

Viola bowed so low her forehead touched the freezing obsidian. "At once, My Lord Kargaroth the Invincible."

She scurried backward out of the throne room, her heart pounding against her ribs. She had survived. 

But as she hurried down the dark, twisting corridors of the Lich King's fortress, a cold dread settled in her stomach. She had bought herself time, yes. But if the "knight" on the wolf was actually real... and if he was actually just a child... 

Viola realized, with a sinking feeling of absolute certainty, that Kargaroth wasn't the invincible one in this war.
