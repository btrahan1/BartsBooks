# Chapter 66: Battle Tactics

The year of "real training" had changed them. They were no longer just children with impossible potential. They were a weapon, honed and sharpened by the brutal discipline of the Dragon and the ancient, magical precision of Alveron. 

Seven Fjellbjorn, ten Valkyries, and a flaming Demigod stood on a high, rocky ridge, looking down into a smoking valley. 

Below them, Fort Devron—a critical Imperial stronghold on the eastern plains—was burning. The Imperial Legions had held the stone walls for three days, but the defenders were on their last legs. It simply wasn't a matter of skill or courage; it was a matter of math. 

The Horde had sent an overwhelming force to break the fort. Hundreds of corrupted Orcs and towering, thick-skinned Trolls battered at the main gates. Swarms of Goblins scaled the walls, while a thousand screeching Kobolds acted as expendable "shock troops," throwing their bodies onto the Imperial spears just to create gaps in the defensive lines. 

The sheer weight of the Ashen Tide was about to crush Fort Devron. 

Samuel stood at the edge of the ridge, his arms crossed over his chest. At eight years old, he had somehow grown even broader. He didn't have his Dragon Axes drawn. He simply stood there, radiating a dense, calm heat, watching the apocalypse below. 

"This will be a good warmup," Samuel smiled, his golden eyes scanning the chaotic battlefield and identifying the command structures of the Horde. 

He turned to look at his siblings. 

The seven Fjellbjorn sat atop their massive Crag-Bears, their iron-wood war axes resting easily in their hands. They were practically vibrating with suppressed energy, their faces completely serious. The fire of the Dragon was burning, but it was perfectly contained. 

Beside them were the ten Valkyries. They had spent the last year forming a bond with the ten timber-wolves that had guarded their mothers' homes since the night they were conceived. Now, the girls sat lightly on the backs of the massive wolves, their living iron-wood bows strung and glowing with the faint, emerald light of the "Verdant Pulse."

"Remember, as we practiced," Samuel said, his deep voice carrying over the distant sounds of slaughter. 

He didn't need to give them a motivational speech. They had been bred for this. They had been trained by the greatest warrior and the most powerful healer in the known world. 

"I want to see how well the volley holds together from wolf-back," Samuel instructed, looking at the Valkyries. The girls nodded in perfect, synchronized silence. 

Samuel then looked at the Fjellbjorn. "And I want to see how well you control the momentum. The Horde has no formation. Do not let them bog you down in the mud. You are the comet. Never stop moving."

The seven boys pounded their axes against their bear-saddles in acknowledgment, the deep thuds echoing like war drums. 

"I will secure the main gate and relieve the Imperial commander," Samuel finished, casually stepping to the very edge of the sheer cliff. "Let me know if you need any help. Otherwise, I will just be watching."

The Demigod didn't climb down the trail. He simply stepped off the cliff, dropping like a golden meteor toward the center of the Horde. 

The Valkyries and the Fjellbjorn didn't hesitate. 

"Light them up, sisters!" the lead Valkyrie called out, her voice clear and sharp. 

The ten timber-wolves launched themselves down the steep, rocky slope with terrifying agility. As they ran, the Valkyries drew their bows. They didn't aim at individual targets. They drew deeply on the life-force of the earth, creating a massive, overlapping volley of brilliant green light. 

They fired. 

A hundred emerald arrows rained down on the Kobold shock troops. The arrows didn't just pierce the creatures; they detonated upon impact, sending concussive shockwaves of pure life-energy rippling through the corrupted ranks. The rapid-fire volley from the backs of the sprinting wolves was so devastating that the entire western flank of the Horde simply evaporated in a cloud of green flashes and ash. 

Before the Trolls and Orcs could even turn to face the magical artillery, the heavy cavalry arrived. 

The seven Fjellbjorn hit the eastern flank like a localized earthquake. 

Just as they had practiced against the Guard, the bear-riders didn't charge in a straight line. They moved as a dervish. The seven boys and their massive mounts formed a spinning, chaotic star of violence that tore through the Horde's lines. The bears shattered the Trolls with pure, concussive force, while the boys unleashed a dizzying flurry of axe strikes, carving through the Orcs with the brutal, fluid grace of the Dragon. 

They never stopped moving. They maintained their momentum perfectly, riding the chaotic wave of their own destruction, leaving a wide, bloody trench of shattered monsters in their wake. 

On the battlements of Fort Devron, the exhausted Imperial commander dropped his blood-stained sword. He stared out at the valley, his mouth hanging open. 

The overwhelming, unstoppable Horde was being systematically dismantled. The sky was filled with emerald light, the ground was shaking from the roar of massive bears, and the eastern flank was being ground into dust by a spinning wheel of silver axes. 

And then, the commander looked down at his own main gate. 

A massive Troll had just shattered the outer iron-wood doors. It roared, raising its club to crush the few remaining defenders. 

Before the club could fall, a massive, golden shockwave hit the ground right in front of the gate. The impact threw the Troll backward, snapping its thick legs like twigs. 

When the dust cleared, the Imperial commander saw an eight-year-old boy standing in the gateway. The boy wasn't even holding a weapon. He was just watching the forest and the plains, occasionally nodding in approval as his siblings systematically annihilated an army of thousands. 

The Hope of Twin Rivers had arrived. And the Ashen Tide had absolutely no idea what had just hit them.
