# Chapter 67: The Draugar and The King

Far beneath the sunlit battlefields of the surface, in the crushing, lightless depths of the world, a different kind of war was being waged. 

It was a war without glory, without cheering crowds, and without end. 

For six grueling months, the Draguar had surged upward from the abyss. The Dark Dwarves were a terrifying corruption of their surface cousins—gaunt, ash-skinned horrors with eyes like dying embers, driven by a singular, mindless hatred for the light. They didn't use formations. They didn't use tactics. They simply poured into the deep passes of the Iron Mountain like a relentless, subterranean flood. 

And for six months, the Stone Guard had held them back. 

The primary choke point was the Grand Chasm, a massive, naturally occurring cavern spanned by a single, wide bridge of reinforced obsidian. Here, the Dwarven defenders had locked their heavy iron shields together, creating an impenetrable, unmoving wall of Mithril and muscle. 

At the very center of that wall stood King Algor. 

He had not left the front lines in half a year. He fought with a second-hand hammer, his legendary weapon having been ruined in his duel with the Mountain-Shaker, but his strength remained absolute. When the Draguar pushed, Algor pushed back, crushing skulls and shattering corrupted armor with every swing. 

The Dwarves fought in shifts, but the fatigue was bone-deep. They fought until their arms went numb, until their lungs burned with the ash of the fallen, until they simply couldn't fight anymore. 

And then, looking at their King standing unyielding in the darkness, they picked up their hammers and fought some more. 

By the blood of their ancestors, and by the ancient strength of The Pact, they held the line. The Draguar did not see the sun. 

***

Hundreds of miles to the east, in his throne room of hollowed obsidian, Kargaroth the Invincible was losing his patience. 

The war on the surface was stalling. The Imperial Legions had adopted strange, highly effective defensive formations that refused to break. Entire warbands of Orcs and Trolls were mysteriously vanishing on the western plains, wiped out in localized storms of golden fire and emerald light that left no survivors. 

But the most infuriating tactical failure was beneath the earth. 

"Report!" Kargaroth rasped, his voice echoing with a dark, magical resonance that cracked the frost on the walls. "Where are my Draguar? They were ordered to flank the Imperial lines from the western caves three months ago!"

Viola, the pale spymaster, threw herself onto the freezing floor, groveling so low she practically pressed her face into the dark stone. She was trembling uncontrollably. 

"My Lord!" Viola cried, her voice cracking with sheer, unfiltered panic. "They... they are still in the mountains, My Lord Kargaroth!"

The green flames in the braziers flared violently, casting monstrous shadows across the throne room. 

"Still in the mountains," Kargaroth repeated, the words dripping with deadly, glacial calm. "An army of fifty thousand unfeeling horrors has been stopped by rocks?"

"Not rocks, My Lord," Viola swallowed hard, knowing she was entirely out of believable lies to spin. She had to deliver the truth, even if it killed her. "The Dwarves. The Iron Mountain has sealed the deep passes. We can't even get a shadow-spy through the Grand Chasm. The Dwarven King himself is holding the bridge. They refuse to break."

Viola squeezed her eyes shut, waiting for the inevitable wave of necrotic energy that would strip the flesh from her bones. She had failed. The Horde was stalled on all fronts. The Emperor’s desperate defenses were holding. 

But the death blow didn't come. 

Instead, there was a terrible, grinding sound of bone on stone. Kargaroth stood up. 

The Lich King rarely rose from his throne. When he did, the sheer, concentrated evil of his presence was so heavy it made the air in the room unbreathable. Viola gasped, clutching her chest as the necrotic aura pressed down on her lungs. 

Kargaroth looked down at his groveling spymaster, the green light in his hollow eye sockets burning with a cold, absolute resolve. The surface dwellers were proving to be annoyingly resilient. The deep defenders were stubbornly honoring ancient pacts. 

It was time to escalate. 

"The Dwarves hide in their mountain," Kargaroth whispered, his voice sounding like a rusted blade dragging across a tomb. "And the Empire cowers behind their new shield-walls. They think they can hold the ground."

The Lich King raised a skeletal hand, pointing toward the heavy, iron-bound doors that led deeper into the abyss. The next words he spoke sent a chill down Viola’s spine that had nothing to do with the freezing temperature of the room. 

"Let them keep the ground," Kargaroth commanded. "It's time to wake the Drakes."
