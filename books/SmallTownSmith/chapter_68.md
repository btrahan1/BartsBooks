# Chapter 68: A New Prophesy

The Grand Archive of the Imperial Capital had been relatively quiet since the discovery of the first slate six years ago. The scholars had spent their days organizing ledgers and tracking the massive logistical efforts of the eastern war. 

But tonight, the heavy oak doors of the Emperor's private chambers were thrown open with a frantic, desperate violence. 

King Valerius III, wearing his midnight velvet robe, stood up from his desk, his hand instinctively dropping to the hilt of his ceremonial sword. 

It was Chief Archivist Malakor. The ancient man was not walking; he was practically sprinting, a terrifying sight for a man of his advanced years. His robes were askew, his breathing was ragged, and his hands were shaking so violently that the heavy, jagged piece of dark stone he carried was rattling against his rings. 

"Malakor?" Valerius demanded, stepping out from behind his desk. "What is the meaning of this?"

"The Room of Prophesy, Sire," Malakor gasped, falling to his knees and offering the dark stone up to the Emperor. "The deep-miners... they breached a sealed catacomb beneath the Old Citadel. They found another one."

Valerius felt the blood drain from his face. The last prophecy had predicted the birth of the Mountain-Shaker and the rise of the Ashen Tide. It had perfectly laid out the map of the current apocalypse. 

The Emperor slowly reached out and took the jagged stone. It was colder than ice, and the Old Tongue runes carved into its surface seemed to pulse with a faint, sickening green light. 

"A new prophesy, My Lord," Malakor whispered, his voice trembling with absolute dread. "It speaks of the immediate future. It speaks of the sky."

Valerius carried the stone to his desk, holding it under the bright light of a heavy silver candelabra. He didn't need Malakor to translate the Old Tongue anymore; he had spent the last six years studying the ancient runes himself, preparing for whatever the abyss threw at him next. 

He read the first line, and his heart skipped a beat. 

*"The Lord of the Abyss rises from his throne of bone. The Drakes of the deep ash are awakening."*

"Drakes," Valerius breathed, looking up at Malakor. "The Lich King has Drakes? They were supposed to have been eradicated in the First Age alongside the dragons."

"Shadow and terror, Sire," Malakor nodded, tears welling in his ancient eyes. "Read the rest."

Valerius looked back down at the glowing green runes, his voice steadying into the cold, hard tone of a wartime monarch. 

*"Ten Drakes of Shadow and Terror will fill the skies, breathing the green fire that melts the stone and breaks the shield. The phalanx of men will burn."*

Valerius gripped the edges of his desk. The new defensive formations—the heavy shield-walls taught to them by the Twin Rivers Guard—were the only thing holding the Horde back. If the Lich King brought heavy, aerial artillery into the fight, the Imperial Legions would be roasted inside their own armor. The line would break in a day. 

He read the final lines of the prophecy, desperate for a countermeasure. 

*"The Dragon of the Demigod and his Storm-Maidens are the only shield. But they cannot hold the sky alone. Unless the Archers of the Ancient Glades are dispatched to pierce the shadows, the World will fill with Darkness."*

Silence fell over the Emperor's chambers. 

"The Dragon of the Demigod," Valerius murmured. He had received the intelligence reports of Samuel's detour to the Iron Mountain, and the subsequent return to Twin Rivers with a massive, swaddled bundle. "So, he actually found an egg. And the Valkyries. The prophecy says they are the only defense against the Drakes."

"But they cannot hold the sky alone, Sire," Malakor reminded him urgently. "Ten Drakes of Shadow. The boy’s dragon is an infant. The Valkyries are ten young girls. We need the Elves. The Archers of the Ancient Glades."

Valerius turned away from the desk, pacing across the thick carpets of his chamber. 

The Elves of Alveron had secluded themselves in the deep forests centuries ago. They did not trade with the Empire. They did not answer royal summons. They considered the wars of men to be fleeting, petty things, unworthy of their immortal attention. 

"Diplomats will fail," Valerius muttered, his mind working through the tactical reality. "An envoy of politicians will be turned away at the forest edge, or simply shot for trespassing."

The Emperor stopped pacing. He looked down at the dark stone, then up at his ceremonial armor resting on its stand in the corner of the room. He was the most powerful man in the world, but his armies were useless against the sky. 

If humanity was going to survive, he needed the Elves. And the Elves only respected one thing: the ancient authority of the Crown, presented without fear. 

"Malakor," Valerius commanded, his voice ringing with absolute, unyielding authority. "Wake the Commander of the Royal Guard."

"Sire?" Malakor blinked, startled. "Are we reinforcing the eastern front?"

"No," Valerius said, walking over to his armor stand and resting his hand on the polished steel breastplate. "The Legions must hold the ground. But I cannot ask my grandson to fight ten Drakes of Shadow alone. If the prophecy demands the Archers of the Ancient Glades, then the Empire will secure them."

The King turned back to his Archivist, his eyes burning with the same fierce, unyielding spark that resided in his son and his grandson. 

"Prepare the Royal Escort, Malakor. And saddle my warhorse. We are headed to The Glades."
