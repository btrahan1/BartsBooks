# Chapter 69: Happy Birthday times 19

The year of combat had taken its toll, not in casualties, but in sheer exhaustion. 

For twelve straight months, the Hope of Twin Rivers had operated as an autonomous, elite strike force. While the Imperial Legions and the Kragh army held the massive defensive lines, Samuel and his siblings had become the Empire's troubleshooters. Whenever a Horde unit broke through, whenever a Troll siege camp threatened a flank, the Valkyries and the Fjellbjorn would drop from the mountains and erase the threat before retreating back to the Crag-Shadows to recover. 

But tonight, they were all home. 

It was the eve of the summer solstice. It was the birth night of the Valkyries, the birth night of the Fjellbjorn, and Samuel’s eighth birthday. 

The Temple of the Priestess was crowded. The massive stone doors had been pushed wide open to accommodate the sheer size of the family. The seven Crag-Bears and the ten giant timber-wolves formed a protective, sleeping ring around the perimeter of the Hearth. 

In the center of the room, sitting cross-legged on the river-stone floor, was a very tired group of warriors. 

The ten Valkyries, now seven years old and already veterans of a hundred skirmishes, leaned against each other, their eyes heavy with fatigue. The seven Fjellbjorn, six years old and bearing the minor cuts and bruises of heavy infantry, were sprawled out on the floor, too tired to even take off their boots. 

And sitting at the head of the circle was Samuel. His custom armor was dented and scarred from a year of continuous war, and his massive shoulders slumped slightly. 

They were battered. They were exhausted. But they were awake. 

Because resting in the very center of the Hearth, directly in the hottest part of the flames, was the volcanic glass container. 

Clara knelt beside the fire, her hands hovering inches from the glass. Her "Verdant Pulse" was pushed to its absolute limit, feeding pure, emerald life-energy directly into the ancient egg. Beside her stood Greg, his hand resting reassuringly on her shoulder, the "Golden Spark" of the Dragon lending his wife the stamina she needed. 

"Any moment now," Clara whispered, her voice tight with exertion. "The pulse is... it's overwhelming."

Samuel leaned forward, his golden eyes fixed on the massive, iridescent egg. He could feel Elsie pressing against the shell, frantic to finally break free of her ancient prison. 

"Come on, Elsie," Samuel rumbled softly. "It’s time."

As if responding to the Demigod’s voice, the egg shifted. 

A sharp, metallic *crack* echoed through the quiet Temple. 

The Valkyries sat up straight. The Fjellbjorn scrambled to their knees. The giant wolves and bears lifted their heavy heads, their animal instincts recognizing the birth of an apex predator. 

*CRACK.*

A jagged fissure appeared on the side of the shell, glowing with a blinding, ruby light. Pure heat poured out of the fissure, instantly melting the remnants of the volcanic glass container into a puddle of slag. 

And then, the egg simply exploded. 

A shockwave of intense heat and ancient Ki blasted through the Temple, throwing sparks into the vaulted ceiling. 

Sitting in the ashes of the Hearth was a dragon. 

She was not a helpless, fragile hatchling. The concentrated life-energy of the valley and the sheer density of Samuel’s Ki had done exactly what the Demigod had promised Algor. Elsie was already the size of a warhorse. Her scales were a breathtaking, shifting mosaic of deep ruby and molten gold, hard as diamonds and radiating a heat that made the air around her shimmer. Her wings, tucked tight against her sleek, muscular back, were leathery and tipped with wicked, curved talons. 

Elsie shook the ash from her scales, her long, serpentine neck snapping up. Her eyes—burning with the same pure, golden light as Samuel’s—locked onto the eight-year-old giant sitting across from her. 

Samuel smiled, a genuine, boyish grin that wiped away the exhaustion of a year of war. He held out his massive hand. 

Elsie didn't chirp. She didn't squeak. She threw her head back, opened her jaws, and let out a roar. 

It was a sound that had not been heard in the world for a thousand years. It was not a roar of anger; it was a pure, unadulterated declaration of existence. The sheer volume and magical density of the sound shattered the remaining crystal in the village, vibrated the bedrock of the valley, and shot straight up into the starry sky, echoing across the continent. 

***

Hundreds of miles away, deep beneath the eastern mountains, the obsidian walls of the Lich King's throne room vibrated. 

Kargaroth, who had been studying a map of the Imperial defensive lines, froze. The heavy, necrotic air in the room suddenly felt thin. The green flames in the braziers flickered and dimmed, cowering against an ancient, overwhelming pressure that rolled over the world. 

He had felt the raw, physical power of the Mountain-Shaker. He had seen the magical artillery of the Valkyries. He had assumed his ten awakened Drakes of Shadow would easily overwhelm the sky and turn the tide of the war. 

But as that roar—that ancient, terrifying sound of pure fire and unbroken lineage—echoed through the abyss, Kargaroth slowly lowered his skeletal hands to the arms of his throne. 

For the first time since he had sold his soul to the darkness, the Invincible Lich King felt a cold, sharp spike of absolute terror. 

The Clan was complete. Ten Valkyries, seven Fjellbjorn, one Demigod, and one Dragon. 

Happy Birthday, times nineteen.
