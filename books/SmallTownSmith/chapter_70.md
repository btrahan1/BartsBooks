# Chapter 70: The Mithril Spear

The roar of the newborn dragon had finally faded, leaving a heavy, expectant silence hanging over the valley of Twin Rivers. The dawn of the summer solstice was breaking, casting long, pale shadows across the village. 

Inside the main forge, however, the fires were already burning hot. 

Clara stood in the doorway, her golden eyes wide with a mixture of disbelief and rising panic. The "Verdant Pulse" around her flared erratically, betraying her deep emotional turmoil. 

"What are you doing?" Clara asked, her voice trembling. 

Greg didn't answer immediately. He stood at the far end of the forge, bathed in the red light of the coals. For the first time since they had fled the capital all those years ago, he was not wearing his heavy leather smithing apron. 

He was assembling his armor. 

It was the heavy, articulated plate of the Crown Prince, the armor of the Dragon of War. It had been buried beneath the floorboards of their original cabin for nearly a decade, carefully oiled and maintained in secret. Greg strapped the heavy steel greaves over his massive shins, his face set in an expression Clara had never seen before. It wasn't the calm, domestic contentment of the Smith. It was the cold, terrifying resolve of a man preparing to march into hell. 

"What must be done," Greg said simply, reaching for his breastplate. 

Clara stepped fully into the forge, closing the distance between them. "NO! Greg! You can't! You swore you would never wear that again. You swore you laid down the Dragon!"

Greg paused, holding the heavy steel breastplate in his massive, calloused hands. He looked down at his wife, the "Golden Spark" in his eyes burning with an intense, agonizing heat. 

"I taught the boys how to ride the bears," Greg argued, his voice a low, desperate rumble. "I taught them the Twin Axes. But they are just six years old, Clara. Samuel is eight. The Lich King has awakened ten Drakes of Shadow. I can feel their unnatural Ki polluting the sky from here."

Greg threw the breastplate onto the anvil with a heavy, metallic clang. 

"I won't let my son face this alone!" Greg yelled, the sound vibrating the heavy iron tools hanging on the walls. "I am the Dragon of War. I have to go with him."

"Father." 

The deep, calm voice cut through the tension in the forge like a perfectly honed blade. 

Greg and Clara turned toward the doorway. 

Samuel stood there, fully armored, the Twin Axes secured to his back. But he wasn't looking at his father with disappointment or anger. He was looking at him with profound, overwhelming respect. 

"Father, that is not your task," Samuel said simply, stepping into the heat of the forge. 

Greg clenched his jaw. "Samuel, you don't understand. The Drakes—"

"I understand exactly what the Drakes are," Samuel interrupted gently. He walked up to his father, placing a hand as large as Greg’s own on the Smith’s shoulder. "But you cannot fight them. If you put that armor on, if you ride out to war... the spell breaks. The sanctuary falls."

Greg frowned, his anger giving way to confusion. "What spell?"

"You," Samuel said, his golden eyes filled with ancient wisdom. "You are the catalyst, Father. You are the light that guides us all. It is only through your actions—the profound, magical choice of laying down your weapons to build instead of destroy—that we even have a chance."

Samuel gestured to the massive, heavily fortified valley outside the forge doors. 

"You built this," Samuel said. "Your peace created the Valkyries. Your discipline created the Fjellbjorn. Your love woke Elsie. If you abandon the Smith to become the Dragon again, the foundation of the Clan crumbles."

Greg looked down at his scarred hands. The hands that had killed thousands, and the hands that had forged the plowshares that fed his family. He knew the boy was right. 

"Then how do I help you?" Greg whispered, the fight draining out of him. "How do I protect my son?"

Samuel smiled, that bright, terrifyingly joyous smile of the Smith. He stepped back and reached into the heavy leather pack slung over his shoulder. 

With a loud, resounding thud, Samuel dropped a heavy, iron-bound crate onto the floor of the forge. 

"Your task," Samuel said, his voice ringing with absolute clarity, "is to forge the weapon that will end this war. You must forge the spear that will slay the phylactery of the Lich King."

Greg stared at the crate. He reached down and popped the heavy iron latches. 

When he lifted the lid, a blinding, pure white light spilled out, illuminating the entire forge. 

Greg gasped. Inside the crate rested three massive, unworked ingots of a metal he had only ever read about in the deepest, most restricted archives of the Imperial Capital. It was lighter than air, harder than diamond, and resonated with a pure, uncorrupted magical frequency. 

"Pure Mithril," Greg breathed, running a trembling hand over the smooth, glowing surface of the ingots. 

"A little present from the Dwarves," Samuel nodded, crossing his arms over his chest. "King Algor says hello, by the way."

Greg looked up at his son, his mind already racing through the metallurgical impossibilities of working with pure Mithril. It required a heat that no normal forge could produce. It required a hammer strike of absolute, flawless precision. 

"Why me?" Greg asked, his voice full of awe. "The Dwarven smiths could have forged this. Silas could help. Why must it be me?"

Samuel shook his head. "Because the Lich King cannot be killed by simple metal, Father. His phylactery is forged from pure, ancient hatred and endless slaughter."

Samuel placed a hand over the glowing Mithril ingots. 

"Only you can forge this, Father. You are the greatest warrior in human history, the man who put aside fighting to protect the world through peace. Only your hands carry the necessary spiritual weight. Only you can forge The Spear of Redemption."

Greg looked at the Mithril. He looked at Clara, who was smiling softly, her eyes shining with pride. And finally, he looked at his son, the Mountain-Shaker who was about to ride a dragon into the apocalypse. 

Greg slowly reached down and unstrapped the heavy steel greaves of his armor. He let them fall to the floor. He picked up his heavy leather smithing apron and tied it securely around his waist. 

"I'm going to need the fires hotter," the Smith said, turning to the bellows. 

"I can help with that," Samuel smiled. 

The forging had begun.
