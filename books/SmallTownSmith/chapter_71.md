# Chapter 71: The Traveller

It took exactly one month, thirty full-grown elks, and an unprecedented amount of dragon fire. 

Elsie, despite only being a month out of the shell, possessed a breath weapon that burned white-hot, far exceeding the capabilities of a standard forge. Greg had spent thirty grueling days standing before the anvil, sweating through his heavy leather apron as the young dragon provided the necessary heat to melt the pure Dwarven Mithril. The thirty elks were simply to keep Elsie fed; manipulating pure magical fire took a tremendous amount of calories. 

When the final hammer blow fell, the sound didn't ring. It hummed. A pure, perfect harmonic frequency that vibrated through the very bedrock of Twin Rivers. 

Greg stepped back from the anvil, wiping his soot-stained face with a trembling hand. 

The Spear of Redemption was finished. 

It was almost painful to look at. The Mithril had been folded and tempered so many times that the metal didn't reflect light—it generated it. The spear glowed with a blinding, pristine white aura, completely devoid of the aggressive golden "Restless Spark" or the chaotic green of the Horde. It was the physical embodiment of absolute, uncorrupted purity. 

Greg carefully picked up the weapon, using heavy, magically treated leather tongs. He turned to Samuel, who was leaning against the wall of the forge, feeding a massive haunch of elk to Elsie. 

"It is done," Greg said, exhaustion evident in his deep voice. He held the glowing spear out toward his son. "Take it."

Samuel stopped scratching the dragon's scaled chin. He turned, looking at the blinding white light of the spear. He didn't reach for it. Instead, the eight-year-old Demigod held up both of his massive, calloused hands and took a step back. 

"It's not for me, Father," Samuel said simply. 

Greg froze, his brow furrowing in deep confusion. "What? What do you mean it's not for you? You're the Mountain-Shaker! You're the one leading the Clan into the abyss! Who else could possibly wield it?"

"Only one person is meant to wield this spear," a quiet, perfectly calm voice echoed through the forge. "But it is far too heavy, and far too bright, to hold right now."

Greg spun around, instinctively shifting his weight into a defensive stance. 

A man had just entered the forge. He had completely bypassed the "Peace-Wall," slipped past the Dark Patrol, and entered the most heavily guarded structure in the valley without making a single sound. 

He wore simple, dusty travel robes with a deep hood that shadowed his face. He leaned heavily on a wooden walking stick, appearing old, frail, and entirely out of place in a room filled with a master smith, a Demigod, and a dragon. 

"Who are you?" Greg demanded, his hand dropping toward a heavy forging hammer on the bench. 

The man lowered his hood. His face was unremarkable, but his eyes held the depth of a bottomless, ancient ocean. 

"I am known as The Traveller," the man said, his voice carrying no threat, only profound gratitude. He walked slowly toward the anvil. "Thank you, Dragon, for forging this instrument. You have done exactly what the world needed you to do. I will take it from here."

Greg hesitated, but Samuel gave him a slow, reassuring nod. 

Greg lowered the heavy tongs. The Traveller reached out with a thin, seemingly frail hand and grasped the haft of the glowing Mithril spear. 

Greg expected the old man’s arm to snap. The spear weighed over two hundred pounds. But The Traveller lifted it with effortless grace, holding the blinding weapon as if it weighed no more than a willow branch. 

As The Traveller held the spear, the blinding white light slowly began to dim. The overpowering aura of purity folded inward, hiding the weapon's true, devastating potential. 

The Traveller turned to Greg. 

"Summon to me The Dark Flame," The Traveller instructed gently. "I must attune this weapon to him."

Greg stared at the old man, completely stunned. "Snake? You want to give the ultimate weapon of redemption to my spymaster?"

"Only he has the skills to get this spear to its destination," The Traveller explained, running his hand along the smooth Mithril haft. "The Lich King's phylactery is not on the battlefield. It is hidden deep within his obsidian fortress, guarded by layers of necrotic magic that Samuel cannot simply smash through. The spear must be carried in the shadows, past the Drakes, past the Draguar, and past the Lich King himself."

The Traveller looked down at the spear, which was now completely dark, looking like an ordinary, unpolished iron rod. 

"It will take me three days to attune the metal to his shadow," The Traveller said. "When I am finished, Snake will be able to carry the light into the absolute darkest corner of the world without being seen."

The Traveller turned and faced Samuel. 

The old man and the young Demigod looked at each other. There was a mutual understanding, an unspoken acknowledgment of the grand, terrifying design of the universe. 

"I believe you are ready, Samuel," The Traveller said, a hint of a proud smile touching his lips. "Take the Vanguard. Take the sky. I will make sure Snake is there when the time is right."

Samuel nodded respectfully. "Thank you, Traveller."

The old man turned and walked out of the forge, leaning on his wooden staff, carrying the destiny of the world in his other hand. 

Greg watched him go, his mind reeling. He looked back at his son. 

"So," Greg said slowly, taking off his leather apron. "Snake gets to assassinate the Lich King. What exactly are you doing?"

"Snake will not be assassinating the Lich King," Samuel corrected, his deep voice carrying a sudden, heavy finality. "I will meet him in the Battle of Peace. But once I defeat him, he will simply escape back to his phylactery. The moment the Lich King falls on the battlefield, The Dark Flame will shatter his phylactery with the tool you have just forged. And then... then, my time here will be at an end."

"What?" 

Clara had just stepped into the forge, holding a basket of fresh bread. The basket slipped from her fingers, clattering against the stone floor. She rushed forward, her golden eyes wide with panic. "What do you mean, Samuel? What do you mean your time will be at an end?"

Samuel smiled, but it wasn't the joyous smile of the Smith. A tired, weary sigh followed. "I was brought here for a purpose, Mother. This world cannot sustain one such as myself."

"What? No!" Clara cried out, rushing to her son and gripping his armored arms. "What are you saying? No!"

"It's time I told you both the story," Samuel said softly, placing his massive hands gently over hers. "The full story."

He looked upward, past the heavy timber roof of the forge, and began to speak of a lost prophecy. 

"The Lich King will only appear at man's weakest point," Samuel explained, his voice rumbling with ancient sorrow. "He is a coward, and would never willingly face mankind at its strength. So, only when the Dragon had laid down his weapons and left the battlefield, would he appear."

Greg froze, the realization hitting him like a physical blow. His decision to leave had triggered the apocalypse. 

"But the prophecy had a counterbalance," Samuel continued, looking at his father. "Only after he had committed himself and his forces, would the Chosen One be born, and with him, his brothers and sisters. But a Demigod, a true Avatar, cannot simply manifest in the mortal realm without shattering the physical laws. We had to grow."

Samuel stepped away from the anvil and walked toward the open forge doors, looking out at the training grounds where the Valkyries and the Fjellbjorn were waiting. 

"And our growth," Samuel said, "would be hidden by the souls of children, until, when the time was right, we would assume our true forms."

Samuel smiled as he stepped outside to his brothers and sisters. The seventeen young warriors turned to face him, their eyes reflecting the same ancient, tired wisdom. 

Samuel turned back to Greg and Clara. The two parents who had loved him, fed him, and forged him. 

"Thank you, Mother. Thank you, Father," Samuel said, his voice ringing across the quiet valley. "And thank you, Twin Rivers, for letting us hide behind the souls of your children... which we now return to you."

Samuel, the ten Valkyries, and the seven Fjellbjorn. 

Suddenly, eighteen brilliant, pure sparks of light separated from their chests. The avatars remained, but the mortal souls they had been sheltering were finally set free. The eighteen sparks rose into the crisp morning air, swirling together in a beautiful, glowing constellation, before speeding off to their respective mothers—to be born again, nine months from today. 

This time, as true children. 

Samuel opened his eyes. The "Restless Spark" was gone, replaced by the terrifying, unfiltered stare of a cosmic Avatar. He turned back to Elsie, who let out a low, rumbling growl, a plume of smoke curling from her nostrils. 

"I have a date with ten Drakes of Shadow," Samuel said, his voice no longer human, but the sound of an approaching storm. He clapped his heavy hand against Elsie's scaled flank. "It's time to fly."
