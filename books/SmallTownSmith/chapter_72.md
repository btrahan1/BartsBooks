# Chapter 72: The March of The Chosen

The displacement of air was massive. 

As Elsie’s leathery, diamond-hard wings snapped downward, a localized hurricane swept through the training grounds, kicking up a blinding cloud of snow and dust. The dragon, who had grown to the size of a small whale in a matter of months thanks to the sheer magical density of the valley, launched herself into the sky. 

Sitting easily between her spiked shoulder blades was Samuel, the Avatar of the Mountain-Shaker. He didn't look back. The Twin Axes of the Dragon were secured across his back, and his unblinking, cosmic stare was locked onto the eastern horizon. 

Below him, the rest of the Chosen began their march. 

The ten Valkyries, their living iron-wood bows slung across their shoulders, rode out on their giant timber-wolves, melting seamlessly into the treeline. The seven Fjellbjorn, hefting their heavy silver-and-steel axes, rode out on their armored Crag-Bears, their heavy footfalls shaking the earth as they headed for the mountain passes. 

The entire town of Twin Rivers watched them go in absolute, reverent silence. 

Standing on the porch of the Temple, Clara placed both of her hands over her belly. Beside her, seventeen other women—the mothers of the Valkyries and the Fjellbjorn—did exactly the same. The tears that streamed down their faces were a complex, overwhelming mixture of profound sorrow for the cosmic warriors riding to their deaths, and a deep, blossoming joy for the tiny, mortal sparks of life that had just returned to them. 

The Avatars were gone. But their children had finally come home. 

Standing in the shadows of the forge doorway, The Traveller leaned heavily on his wooden walking stick. He watched the massive, shimmering form of Elsie disappear into the cloud cover, shaking his head with a long, weary sigh. 

"Always with the dramatics," the old man muttered to himself, brushing a speck of ash from his dusty robes. "The Gods love their flair. Could have just walked, but no, let's create a hurricane."

"If you're going to fight an apocalypse, you might as well make an entrance."

The voice came from the deep shadows directly to The Traveller’s left. It was smooth, quiet, and carried absolutely zero physical presence. 

The Traveller didn't jump. He simply turned his head, offering a small, knowing smile. 

Stepping out from the darkness where literally nothing had been a second ago was Snake. The spymaster of Twin Rivers wore his usual dark, form-fitting leather, a collection of throwing knives strapped across his chest. He stood beside The Traveller, his dark eyes scanning the old man with casual, professional scrutiny. 

"I heard that someone called for me," Snake said. 

He didn't mean that someone had relayed a message. The Dark Flame heard *everything*. He had been deep in the Crag-Shadows coordinating the Dark Patrol when The Traveller had spoken his name in the forge, yet his impossibly honed senses had picked up the vibration all the same. 

"Indeed I did," The Traveller replied, turning to fully face the spymaster. 

The old man reached under his dusty robe and pulled out the weapon. 

It no longer looked like the blinding, pure white spear of Mithril that Greg had forged just hours ago. The Traveller’s touch had folded the aura inward, condensing the terrifying power until the weapon simply looked like a five-foot rod of unpolished, blackened iron. 

"What do you need from me?" Snake asked, his eyes dropping to the dark iron rod. 

"I need you to take a walk," The Traveller said, offering the weapon. "A very long, very dark walk."

Snake reached out and took the spear. The moment his fingers closed around the haft, he felt it. The absolute absence of light. The weapon didn't hum, and it didn't burn. It felt like holding a sliver of a black hole, a localized void that completely absorbed his own stealthy Ki and magnified it perfectly. 

"The Avatars are marching to the Battle of Peace," The Traveller explained, pointing his wooden stick toward the East. "They will draw the attention of the Horde, the Drakes, and the Lich King himself. It will be the loudest, brightest, most destructive conflict this world has ever seen."

Snake nodded slowly, turning the dark spear over in his hands. "The ultimate distraction."

"Precisely," The Traveller smiled. "While Kargaroth is busy fighting the sky, his fortress will be empty. His phylactery will be unguarded. But the magical wards surrounding his throne room are absolute. Samuel could not break them without destroying the continent, and the Valkyries could not pierce them."

The Traveller tapped the dark iron spear in Snake's hands. 

"But this weapon, forged by the Dragon of War and tempered by the peace of the Smith, can slip right through the wards," The Traveller said softly. "And you, Dark Flame, are the only mortal in existence who can walk through an army of a million undead without being seen."

Snake looked up, a rare, genuine smile touching his usually grim features. 

"Assassinating an immortal Lich King in his own throne room while the gods fight an apocalypse outside," Snake murmured, testing the balance of the spear. He spun it once, the movement completely silent. "I suppose that beats tracking bandits in the snow."

"You have three days to reach the obsidian fortress," The Traveller said, his voice taking on a heavy, ancient weight. "When the Lich King falls on the battlefield, his soul will immediately flee back to the phylactery to reconstruct his body. You must shatter the vessel the exact moment the soul enters it. If you strike too early, the vessel is empty. If you strike too late, Kargaroth is reborn."

"I won't miss," Snake stated, his voice completely devoid of arrogance, carrying only the absolute certainty of a master. 

He offered a short, respectful nod to The Traveller, turned on his heel, and stepped back into the shadows. 

He didn't make a sound. He didn't disturb the dust. The Dark Flame simply ceased to be there. 

The Traveller stood alone in the quiet forge, listening to the distant cheers of the Imperial Legions as the Avatars began to arrive on the eastern front. 

"May the Smith guide your hand, Snake," The Traveller whispered to the empty room. "Because if you miss... we are all going to burn."
