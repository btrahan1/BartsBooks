# Chapter 74: I will not fail.

Alone. Dark. Cold. 

The Dark Flame moved through the harsh, freezing terrain of the eastern crags like a shadow detached from its caster. He had run without stopping for thirty-six hours, pushing his body past the limits of mortal endurance. Three days to cross the entire continent and infiltrate the Lich King's obsidian fortress. It was a logistical impossibility. 

But Snake kept going. 

He gripped the dark iron rod—the Spear of Redemption—tightly in his right hand. The weapon was heavy, but its magical properties completely absorbed the sound of his footfalls and the heat of his exertion. He was practically invisible, a ghost slipping past the sprawling, disorganized war camps of the Horde reserves. 

As he crested a snow-covered ridge, his lungs burning and his legs feeling like lead, he saw a figure standing in the distance. 

It was an old man, leaning on a wooden walking stick, waiting casually in the middle of a blizzard. 

Snake slowed his pace, his dark eyes narrowing. He had left The Traveller back in the forge at Twin Rivers less than two days ago. There was no physical way the old man could have beaten him here. 

"You move too slow, Dark Flame!" The Traveller called out over the howling wind, sounding mildly annoyed. "We don't have all week!"

Snake came to a halt a few feet away, his breathing heavy but controlled. Even exhausted, his senses were razor-sharp. 

"You can have fast, or you can have hidden," Snake rasped, his breath pluming in the freezing air. "But you can't have both. Not if you want me to get through the Lich King's outer wards undetected."

"A fair point for a mortal," The Traveller conceded with a small nod. He raised his wooden stick, tapping it lightly against the frozen earth. "But they don't call me The Traveller for nothing. Time is relative, Snake. And right now, we need to bend it. Maybe this will help you speed along."

The old man reached out and pressed two fingers against Snake’s forehead. 

The exhaustion vanished instantly. It wasn't just a burst of energy; Snake felt as though he had suddenly stepped out of the flow of time itself. The snowflakes falling around them seemed to hang suspended in the air. 

"The fortress is fifty miles ahead," The Traveller said, his voice echoing from everywhere at once. "I will not fail you, Snake. Do not fail the boy."

"I will not fail," Snake promised, his voice tight with absolute conviction. 

He leaned forward and ran. The world blurred around him. He wasn't just fast; he was moving through the spaces between seconds, a true shadow racing toward the heart of the abyss. 

***

Meanwhile, on the eastern plains, the Battle of Peace had finally begun. 

The Imperial Legions had formed their massive, interlocking shield-walls, bracing for the overwhelming impact of the Ashen Tide. But before the Horde could reach the human lines, the sky split open. 

The Chosen had arrived. 

They did not fight like children playing at war. The mortal shells they had worn were gone, replaced by the terrifying, unbridled majesty of their true, Avatar forms. 

The seven Fjellbjorn hit the center of the Horde like a meteor strike. Their Crag-Bears were no longer just animals; they were massive, armored juggernauts of divine fury. The boys wielded their axes with a ferocity straight out of legend, carving through hundreds of Orcs and Trolls in a matter of seconds. The "Restless Spark" burned so brightly within them that the sheer heat of their presence turned the mud of the battlefield into baked clay. 

On the flanks, the ten Valkyries rode their giant timber-wolves through the chaotic masses. They didn't even need to draw their bowstrings. They simply pointed their living iron-wood bows, and torrents of pure, detonating emerald light rained down upon the Horde, vaporizing entire battalions of Kobolds and Goblins in blinding flashes of life-energy. 

It was divinity come to life. The Avatars tore into the Dark Tide with an efficiency that left the Imperial commanders speechless. 

But even with the devastating power of the Chosen, the math of the apocalypse was brutally simple. The numbers of the Horde were virtually uncountable. For every Troll the Fjellbjorn shattered, three more climbed over the corpse to take its place. 

And then, the sky began to darken. 

Samuel, riding high above the battlefield on Elsie’s massive back, looked up. The clouds were rotting away, dissolving into sickly green ash. 

Descending through the ruined sky were ten massive, horrifying shapes. The Drakes of Shadow had arrived. They were skeletal nightmares, their wings woven from necrotic smoke, their jaws dripping with a magical green fire that could melt through Imperial steel and Avatar flesh alike. 

Elsie roared, a sound of pure, radiant challenge, her own white-hot fire building in her throat. 

Samuel drew the Twin Axes of the Dragon, the golden fire erupting along the blades. But as he looked at the ten massive Drakes fanning out to blanket the battlefield in shadow-fire, he knew the terrible truth. 

He could kill one. Maybe two. But while he fought, the others would roast his siblings and the Legions below. 

As the first Drake opened its jaws to unleash a torrent of necrotic flame, Samuel gripped Elsie's scales and looked toward the western treeline. 

"Please," the Demigod prayed, his voice lost in the roar of the apocalypse. "Please come."
