# Chapter 76: Attrition

When the eight remaining Drakes of Shadow finally began their coordinated bombing run, the sky didn't just burn. It wept. 

What followed was no longer a one-sided slaughter. It became a brutal, agonizing battle of attrition. The Drakes swooped low in perfect formation, their necrotic green fire washing over the eastern plains in massive, overlapping waves. 

The Avatars fought back with the fury of trapped gods. But even gods can bleed. 

The first to fall was the youngest of the Valkyries. 

She had ridden her timber-wolf too far forward, trying to get a clear shot at the underbelly of a diving Drake. The beast twisted in mid-air, unleashing a concentrated blast of shadow-fire. Her scream was instantly muffled by the roaring green flames. The brilliant emerald spark of her Avatar form flickered violently, then vanished, her cosmic essence violently ejected from the mortal plane. 

The reaction to her death was instantaneous. 

From the western treeline, a trumpet sounded again—not a warning this time, but a command of absolute, ancient wrath. The Archers of the Ancient Glades revealed themselves. Hundreds of Elven arrows, glowing with a cold, starlight magic, filled the sky. They struck the offending Drake with such precision that it looked like a porcupine before it shattered into dust. 

Seven Drakes remaining. 

Seeing the Elves exposed on the edge of the forest, two Drakes immediately banked hard, opening their jaws to roast the archers. 

The Fjellbjorn did not let them. 

Abandoning their assault on the Horde’s vanguard, the heavy cavalry fell back. The massive Crag-Bears formed a living wall between the treeline and the sky. One of the brothers, the largest of the Fjellbjorn, drove his bear directly into the path of the falling green flame. 

He raised his silver axes, crossing them in a desperate block. The heat of the shadow-fire was absolute. The boy and his bear held the line for three agonizing seconds, shielding the Elves behind them, before the sheer necrotic weight of the fire consumed them both. His golden spark flashed once, blindingly bright, and was gone. 

Six Drakes remaining. 

Enraged by the loss of their brother and sister, the remaining Avatars changed their tactics. The nine surviving Valkyries began flowing in tight, impossibly fast circles, creating a continuous, spinning vortex of emerald light that unleashed arrow after arrow into the sky. 

High above the vortex, Elsie was engaged in a brutal, prehistoric duel. The young dragon had singled out the largest of the Shadow Drakes—the leader of the rotting pack. It was a terrifying clash of teeth, fire, and diamond-hard claws. Elsie's white-hot solar fire repeatedly clashed against the Drake's necrotic green breath, the resulting magical explosions shaking the clouds themselves. 

And what of Samuel? 

The Mountain-Shaker ignored the sky. He ignored the burning Horde. He even ignored the agonizing flashes of his siblings being sent back to the cosmos. 

He marched directly through the center of the battlefield, a solitary, golden titan walking through a sea of corruption. The Orcs and Trolls that tried to step in his path simply dissolved into ash before their weapons could even touch him. The pure density of his Ki was so overwhelming that the Ashen Tide literally parted around him. 

He stopped fifty yards from the raised ridge where Kargaroth stood. 

Samuel raised one of his burning Dragon Axes and pointed it directly at the skeletal master of the Horde. 

"Your reign ends here now, Demon!" Samuel’s voice thundered, cutting through the roar of the Drakes and the screams of the dying. "Face me, you coward!"

Kargaroth did not laugh. He looked at the Demigod, feeling the searing heat of the "Restless Spark," and finally understood the terrible trap he had fallen into. The boy was the anvil. The battle was the hammer. 

***

Hundreds of miles away, deep beneath the earth, the Dark Flame stepped out of the shadows. 

Snake had bypassed fifty thousand Draguar. He had slipped through the impenetrable, necrotic wards of the obsidian fortress without triggering a single alarm. The Spear of Redemption, still humming with dark, contained energy in his hand, had perfectly masked his approach. 

He stood in the center of the Lich King's inner chamber. It was a massive, vaulted room lined with ancient, terrible tomes and glowing green braziers. 

Snake's dark eyes scanned the room, his pulse steady, his mind completely focused on the mission The Traveller had given him. He had to shatter the phylactery the exact moment Kargaroth's soul returned to it. 

There was only one problem. 

Snake looked at the empty obsidian pedestal in the center of the room, then checked the dark corners of the vaulted ceiling. 

Where was the phylactery?
