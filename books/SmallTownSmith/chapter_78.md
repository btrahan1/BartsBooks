# Chapter 78: 9 months later

The last of the cries filled the crisp night air, a sharp, beautiful sound of pure, unburdened life. 

It was spring in the valley of Twin Rivers, exactly nine months since the white pillar of light had shattered the eastern sky and erased the Ashen Tide from existence. The "Peace-Wall" was open. The Guard stood down. And the Temple of the Priestess was full. 

Inside the Hearth room, eighteen babies drew their first breaths of air. 

They were not Avatars. They did not possess the terrifying "Restless Spark" or the glowing emerald eyes of the "Verdant Pulse." They were simply eighteen fully formed, beautiful, perfectly normal human babies. 

Seventeen mothers of the village, and Clara, the Druid Priestess herself, lay in the comfortable cots surrounding the eternal fire. They were exhausted, but as they looked down at the tiny, fragile lives in their arms, they smiled. They smiled weakly at the eighteen trained acolytes who had helped deliver them, and they smiled at each other. 

Outside the Temple, the crowd rejoiced. The miners, the weavers, the farmers, and the King’s own envoys raised their mugs to the sky, celebrating the true birth of the children whose souls had saved the world. 

But down by the quiet, rushing waters of the river, away from the cheering crowds, one man was not smiling. 

Tyler, the master tracker and former head of the village militia, was deeply melancholy. 

He knelt on the soft grass beneath a new monument that had been erected near the water’s edge. It was a life-sized statue carved from a single piece of dark, polished obsidian. It depicted Snake, the Dark Flame, holding a simple iron rod, his hood pulled low, stepping silently out of the shadows. 

No one knew where the statue had come from. It had simply been there one morning, standing as a silent guardian over the valley. 

Tyler reached out, his calloused hand tracing the cold stone of the statue's boot. 

"You fool," Tyler whispered, his voice cracking with a grief he had carried for nine long months. Snake had been caught in the explosion that vaporized the Lich King's fortress. He had known it was a suicide mission the moment he took the Spear. "You beautiful, heroic fool. I miss you."

"You know, he was the only unknown in this entire saga."

Tyler flinched, his hand flying to the hunting knife at his belt as he spun around. 

Stepping casually out from behind the obsidian statue was an old man in dusty travel robes, leaning on a wooden walking stick. 

"Who are you?" Tyler demanded, his eyes narrowing. "How did you get past the perimeter?"

"I am The Traveller," the old man said gently, ignoring the drawn knife. He walked up to the statue and placed a wrinkled hand affectionately on the obsidian shoulder. "And I go where I please. I created this marker for him, you know. He deserved to be remembered exactly as he was."

Tyler slowly lowered his knife, sensing no threat from the old man, only a deep, ancient sorrow that matched his own. "What do you mean, he was the only unknown?"

The Traveller turned to look at the tracker. 

"All of them," The Traveller explained, gesturing vaguely toward the cheering village and the distant mountains. "Greg, Clara, the Valkyries, the Fjellbjorn, Samuel. The Lich King, the Dwarves in the deep, the Elves in the sky. All of them. They were all part of the prophecy. They were cosmic gears turning in a preordained machine to balance the scales of the universe."

The old man patted the cold stone of the statue. 

"But Snake... The Dark Flame," The Traveller said softly. "He was nothing special. He wasn't prophesied. He didn't have divine blood or a cosmic spark. He was just a scared kid from the capital who knew how to hide in the dark."

The Traveller looked Tyler directly in the eyes. 

"Then you, Tyler, came along and gave him a purpose. You gave him a family. You gave him a home to protect," The Traveller said, his voice thick with emotion. "He loved you, you know? Like the father he never had."

Tyler couldn't speak. The lump in his throat was absolute. The knife slipped from his fingers, falling silently into the grass. 

"The universe didn't send Snake into that fortress," The Traveller continued, looking back up at the obsidian face of the spymaster. "Samuel's raw power couldn't breach the wards. The Elves couldn't shoot through them. Snake went in there because it was the only way to keep Twin Rivers safe. He wouldn't fail. He *couldn't* fail. Because he would never let you down."

Tears, hot and fast, finally broke through Tyler's hardened exterior, streaming down his weathered face. He fell forward, resting his forehead against the cold base of the statue, weeping for the scared kid who had grown up to save the world. 

The Traveller watched him for a moment, a kind, gentle smile touching his lips. 

"But don't you fret, Tyler," The Traveller said softly, leaning on his staff and looking up at the vast, starry heavens above the valley. "His soul didn't return to a mother's belly, but it didn't burn in the abyss, either. I will find him... out there."

Tyler looked up, wiping his eyes, a tiny spark of hope cutting through his grief. "You will?"

The old man winked. 

"They don't call me The Traveller for nothing," he said. 

And then, with the rustle of a dusty robe and the faint scent of old parchment, he was gone. 

Tyler knelt by the river, alone again. But as he listened to the distant cheers of the village, and the faint, beautiful cries of the newborn children, the valley didn't feel so heavy anymore. 

He placed a hand on the statue one last time. 

"See you around, kid," Tyler whispered. 

He stood up, turned his back on the dark, and walked toward the light of the Temple. There was a lot of life to be lived in Twin Rivers, and Tyler intended to see it all. 

*The End.*
