# Chapter 2: The Spar

The workshop was a place of shadows and embers, but the world outside was a riot of noise and blinding white light. 

Tanner sat in his outdoor pen, a sturdy construction of pine and hemp rope situated under the dappled shade of a massive silver-leaf oak. The air smelled of baked earth, wild sage, and the sharp, ozone-tang of expectant energy. Before him, the "yard"—a flat patch of trampled dirt that served as both a loading dock and a training ground—was occupied by the two men who formed the pillars of his young life.

*Thump.*

Valerius moved with a surprising grace for a man built like a cask of iron. In his hand was a heavy smithing hammer, its head scarred and blackened by years of service. It was the same tool he used at the anvil, but here, in the open air, it carried a different weight. 

Tanner gripped the rails of his pen, his knuckles white. To his eyes, his father wasn't just swinging a piece of metal. Valerius was enveloped in a thick, pulsing mantle of orange light. It was Might, raw and dense. Every time the hammer swung, the light trailed behind it like the wake of a ship, solidifying for a fraction of a second before vibrating into the atmosphere. 

Opposite him, Grandfather Kaelen was a specter of dark wood and shifting shadows. He held a staff that looked as if it had been carved from the very heart of a sunless forest—dark, twisted, and inlaid with runes that bled a faint, sickly green light.

"You're rooted, Valerius," Kaelen said, his voice carrying easily over the sound of their exertion. He didn't move his whole body; he drifted. He pivoted on a single heel, the staff spinning in his hands like a living thing. "Strength is a wall. A wall can be climbed. A wall can be broken."

"A wall also stands its ground, old man," Valerius grunted. He stepped forward, the earth beneath his boots cracking. He brought the hammer down in a short, brutal arc.

The collision wasn't just sound. It was a physical pressure that washed over Tanner, ruffling his hair and making the pen rattle. 

Tanner tilted his head, his brow furrowing. He saw the point of contact. The orange aura of his father’s hammer met the dark green ripples of the staff. But where his father’s light was a cohesive, singing whole, the green light of the staff felt… jagged. It flickered. 

Tanner’s "Sight" focused on the runes of the staff. They were beautiful, ancient, and powerful—deeply magical. But they didn't belong to Kaelen. They were like bright gems glued to a dull stone. There was a microscopic gap between the man’s soul and the staff’s intent. A "Misfit," Tanner thought, though he didn't have the word for it yet.

His father’s hammer, however, was silent. It didn't hum with ancient runes. It had no gems. But the orange Might bled into the iron as if the two were made of the same dream. 

Kaelen parried a blow, the dark staff vibrating with a high-pitched whine that set Tanner’s teeth on edge. The old ranger took a long step back, his eyes flashing. 

"The hammer serves you well, son-in-law. Because you poured your sweat into its forging. But it lacks reach. It lacks the versatility of a true wizard-tool."

"It lacks nothing," Valerius replied, wiping sweat from his eyes. "It does exactly what I ask of it. No more, no less."

*Thump. Crack.*

They moved again, a dance of force and evasion. Tanner was mesmerized. He watched the way the essence swirled around them—the orange and the green clashing, creating sparks of violet energy that died before they hit the ground. He watched the way their muscles bunched, the way their breath came in synchronized puffs.

"Train-ing," Kaelen said, catching his breath as he blocked a sideways sweep. "Training, Valerius! You must learn to feel the edge of the Magic, not just the weight of the Might!"

Tanner’s lips moved. 

"Stick," he whispered.

Kaelen froze. Valerius bit back a laugh, his hammer lowering to the dirt. Both men turned to look at the toddler in the pen.

Tanner didn't notice them looking. He was staring at the staff. In his mind, it wasn't a masterwork artifact. It was a stick. A very long, very dark, very busy stick. 

He looked at his own pen. He reached down to a wobbly, vertical dowel that sat slightly loose in its socket. It was a piece of plain, unfinished pine, meant to provide extra stability to the corner gate. 

He grabbed it with both hands.

Tanner sat back on his haunches, pulling with all his weight. His face turned red. A small, faint pulse of that clear, golden resonance flickered around his fingers. 

*Creeeeeak.*

He shifted his weight, twisting the wood. He felt the grain. He felt the place where the glue had grown brittle and the hole had widened. He didn't just pull; he *understood* the failure of the joint.

*Pop.*

The dowel came free. It was about two feet long, rough at one end where it had snapped, and smooth at the other. It was light. It smelled of tree-blood.

In Tanner’s hands, it wasn't just a piece of debris. He felt the connection. He had worked for it. He had wrestled it from its place. 

He pulled himself up to his feet, holding the rail with one hand and the wood with the other. He looked at Kaelen, his small eyes bright with a sudden, fierce intelligence. 

He held the pine dowel out, mimicking the way Kaelen held the dark staff—one hand high, one hand low.

"Train-ing," Tanner said. His voice was small, but it carried the same weight as his father’s hammer strikes.

Valerius stepped closer to the pen, his expression softening from the warrior's focus to the father's pride. But Kaelen didn't move. He stood perfectly still, his dark staff held loosely at his side.

He saw the golden shimmer. It was faint—barely more than a trick of the sunlight—but it was there. It wasn't the forced Might of the human nor the borrowed Magic of the elf. It was something else. A third path.

"He doesn't want the staff, Valerius," Kaelen said softly, his voice full of wonder. "He wants the work."

Tanner swung his stick. It was a clumsy, top-heavy move that almost knocked him off his feet, but he caught himself. He brought the wood down against the rail of his pen with a satisfying *thwack*.

He looked at the small dent he had made in the rail. He looked at the grain of the stick. He felt the way the vibration traveled up his arms, settling into his bones. 

He wasn't just playing. He was measuring. He was learning how his body moved, how the wood resisted, and how the two could become one.

"Training," he said again, more firmly this time.

Valerius reached over the rail and ruffled Tanner’s hair. "Aye, son. Training. But first, you have to learn to stand without wobbling."

Tanner swatted his father’s hand away with his stick. He didn't want to be coddled. He wanted the heavy air, the scent of sage, and the rhythm of the clash.

Kaelen walked to the pen and set his dark staff on the ground. He knelt until he was eye-level with Tanner.

"A stick of your own making, little sprout?"

Tanner noddded solemnly. He held the pine dowel close to his chest.

"Then guard it well," Kaelen whispered. "For the world will tell you it is just a piece of pine. But to those with eyes to see, it is the beginning of a mountain."

Tanner didn't reply. He was already looking back at the yard, waiting for the spar to begin again. He had his stick. He had his path. 

And for the first time, he was ready to join the dance.
