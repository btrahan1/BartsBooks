# Chapter 3: The Needle

The stick was a constant now. It leaned against the slats of the pen when Tanner slept, and it served as a third leg when he navigated the uneven stones of the workshop floor. It was a rough, pine companion, but it was *his*, and that made it the most important object in the world.

But today, his gaze was pulled away from the stick.

He sat on a small rug of woven wool, positioned near the sun-drenched window of his mother’s studio. The air here was different from the forge. It didn't smell of burning coal and hot iron; it smelled of beeswax, cured leather, and the sweet, sharp scent of lavender used to keep the moths away.

His mother, Elara, sat on a low stool. In her lap was a piece of hide that looked like liquid sun—salamander-scale leather, rare and incredibly difficult to work. 

Tanner shuffled closer, the stick dragging behind him with a rhythmic *scuff-scuff*.

He watched her hands. Specifically, he watched the needle.

It was a beautiful thing. It had been forged by Valerius during the long nights of the mid-winter thaw, a gift for his wife. The body of the needle was a high-carbon steel alloy, but the eye was lined with silver and the tip was capped with a microscopic point of obsidian. 

To Tanner’s Sight, the needle was a marvel of cooperation. Often, when he saw an artisan using a tool, there was a faint shimmer of resistance—the "Misfit" that Kaelen’s staff had displayed. But between Elara and this needle, there was nothing but silence. 

Because his father had made it specifically for his mother—knowing the exact weight of her hand, the speed of her stitch, and the frequency of her magic—the needle had become an extension of her. The orange Might of Valerius’ forge-fire had been laid down so perfectly that Elara’s blue Magic flowed over it like water over smooth river stones. 

They were one. Their work was one. 

Elara looked up and smiled, her pointed ears twitching with amusement. "Still studying, little sprout? You’ve been staring at that stitch for an hour."

Tanner didn't blink. He watched the blue ripples of mana follow the silver eye of the needle. It looked like a tiny, glowing fish swimming through a sea of gold.

Then, he saw the shape of the work.

Until now, he had assumed she was making a glove or a pouch. But as she joined the three sections of salamander-hide, the object began to take on a familiar form. It was small. Too small for a man. Too small even for his mother’s slender hands.

It was a tunic. A tiny, scale-leather tunic, lined with soft rabbit fur.

Tanner’s heart gave a sudden, sharp thump against his ribs. His eyes widened, reflecting the golden shine of the leather. 

"Mine?" he whispered.

The word felt heavy in his mouth. It was a question, but it carried the weight of a realization. 

Elara’s hands paused. She looked at the tiny garment, then at the ragged linen shift Tanner was currently wearing—the simple, machine-woven cloth that he had already begun to resent. 

"It is a gift, Tanner," she said softly. "A gift from your father’s fire and your mother’s hand. For the coming winter."

Tanner reached out a hand, then stopped. His fingers hovered inches away from the warm, magic-infused leather. 

He looked at his stick. 

The stick was his because he had pulled it free. He had fought the wood and the glue. He had earned the right to hold it. 

But this? This was made by others. 

He felt a strange, cold knot of confusion in his chest. According to Grandfather Kaelen, borrowed strength was false strength. If he didn't make it, he didn't own it. If he wore this tunic, would he be wearing "false" dragon-fire? Would the salamander-hide protect him, or would it simply be a lie he draped over his skin?

He looked at his mother. He saw the love in her eyes—a resonance that was deeper and more profound than any mana flow. 

He looked at the needle. He saw the way his father’s labor was supporting his mother’s art. 

This wasn't "borrowed" strength, he realized. This was *given* strength. It was the foundation upon which his own work would one day stand. 

He didn't know how to process the distinction. He was too young for the complex theology of craftsmanship. But he knew the feeling of the work. 

Slowly, Tanner reached forward and let his fingertips brush the sun-warmed scales. 

The resonance hit him immediately. It didn't feel like the cold, parasitic energy of Kaelen’s staff. It felt… welcoming. Because his parents’ essences were so closely tied to his own—because he was literary made of their Might and their Magic—the tunic didn't feel like a foreign object. It felt like an extension of his own family’s circle.

But he wouldn't just take it. 

He pulled his hand back and looked his mother in the eye. 

"Learn," he said.

Elara tilted her head. "Learn? You want to learn the stitch, Tanner?"

He nodded fiercely. He pointed to the needle, then to the scraps of leather on the floor.

If he was to wear it, he would not wear it as a passive recipient. He would study every stitch. He would learn how the silver eye guided the mana and how the obsidian tip found the path through the scales. He would memorize the way the rabbit fur was anchored to the leather. 

He would turn the gift into a lesson. And one day, when he outgrew this tiny tunic, he would be the one holding the needle. He would be the one making the clothes for the man he was to become.

"Show me," Tanner said, his voice gaining the stubborn edge that both Valerius and Kaelen recognized so well.

Elara laughed, a sound like wind-chimes in a summer breeze. She reached down and picked up a large, blunt-tipped bone needle and a scrap of thick ox-hide. 

"Very well, little master. If you will not be dressed without knowing the why of it, then sit. We shall start with the simple cross-stitch."

Tanner sat. He gripped his stick in his left hand, and he reached for the blunt needle with his right. 

Outside, the hammer of the forge rang out—*Clang-Clang-Clang*—providing the rhythm. Inside, the silver needle danced through the gold leather, providing the melody.

And in the center of the workshop, a three-year-old half-elf began to learn the language of the world. He would cherish the gift, not for the protection it offered, but for the knowledge it contained. 

He was Tanner Smith. And he was already starting to realize that to make himself, he would first have to understand everything that had made him.

The stick remained leaning against his leg, a silent witness. The training had moved from the yard to the bench. And the world of Might and Magic was about to discover just how much a small boy could hold in his hands.
