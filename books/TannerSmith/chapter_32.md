# Chapter 32: Communion

The living hollow was quiet, save for the soft, rhythmic drumming of the rain against the bark and the low, guttering flame of the sun-berry lamp in the corner. 

Tanner lay on his unrolled velvet scrap, his head resting on the supple leather of his spare tunic. His body was heavy, still processing the calories of the honey-bread and the restorative heat of the tea, but his mind refused to settle. The "Empty" part of Sylara’s lesson was done; he felt the cold, hollow space in his chest where his Blue sphere usually resided. Now, he needed to perform the "Store."

He closed his eyes and allowed his awareness to move downward.

He didn't know what he was doing, not in the academic sense. Parmera hadn't taught him the formal meditation postures yet, and Sylara had only given him the basic directive to listen. But Tanner had spent four years listening to the ring of an anvil and the resistance of a needle. He knew how to find the "intent" of a material. 

He didn't reach out with his hands. He reached out with his Sight.

At first, there was only the darkness of the root-hollow. Then, as he lowered his internal threshold, the world began to bloom. To call it a tree was a misnomer—Tanner realized this with the sudden, jarring impact of a new understanding. 

Through the Sight, the Great Tree wasn't just a pillar of wood. It was a sun.

It radiated a deep, resonant gold that made the "Golden Resonance" of his own creations look like flickering candle-smoke. But as he allowed his connection to deepen, moving his awareness past the floor and into the thick, living heart of the main trunk, he realized that the sun had roots—and those roots were everywhere.

He saw them now: thousands, millions of golden threads branching out from the Great Tree and diving into the earth. They didn't just stop. They networked. They connected to the smaller trees, to the ferns, to the mosses, to the very grass at the edge of the clearing miles away. The entire forest was a single, silver-gold tapestry of Binds, and the Great Tree was the central loom.

It was a system. A system so vast and so complex that Tanner’s mind struggled to categorize it. It was the ultimate intersection of Might and Magic—a physical structure that spanned an entire valley, held together by a Life-Bind that had been tensioned for eons.

"I see you," Tanner whispered, his internal voice lost in the roaring hum of the forest.

He opened himself up. He stopped trying to be the "Tanner Smith" who made things, and became the "Tanner Smith" who was a part of things. 

He allowed a small trickle of that golden-silver energy to move from the bark of the hollow and into his own skin. It didn't feel like the sharp, electric mana of the forge or the delicate, thread-like mana of the leather-shop. It felt like... coming home. It felt like a warm bath on a winter’s night, or the taste of the first summer peach. 

The trickle circulated through his body, following the pathways of his Blue sphere. It found the cold, hollow well in his chest and began to fill it. It wasn't just energy; it was *rhythm*. It carried the memory of the rain, the strength of the earth, and the patient, slow-moving intent of the forest.

It felt wonderful. It felt amazing. 

After just a short time, the tremors in his hands stopped. the headache behind his eyes dissolved into a soothing, amber warmth. He felt as though his internal "seedling" had just been given a century’s worth of sunlight. His mana-well didn't just fill; it *vibrated*. It felt larger, sturdier, as if the fibers of his core were actually thickening, weaving themselves into a tighter, more resilient lattice.

"Thank you," he whispered to the walls of the hollow.

He felt the Great Tree acknowledge him. It wasn't a voice, but a gentle shifting of the tension in the room—a welcoming pressure that indicated the tree had accepted its anchor.

Tanner sat up, his body feeling light and powerful once more. The exhaustion was gone, replaced by a clarity that was so sharp it was almost painful. He looked at his hands in the dim light of the sun-berry lamp. They looked the same, but they felt different. They felt like they belonged to a larger world.

He went to lie back down, ready to sink into the deep, restorative sleep Sylara had ordered. But as he closed his eyes, his brow furrowed. He felt a small, persistent "hitch" in the silence.

His own philosophy—the "Self-Made Path"—was already awake and analyzing the transaction. 

*Borrowed strength is false strength.*

Grandfather Kaelen’s voice echoed in his mind, not as a reprimand, but as a reminder of the laws of the world. Tanner had just taken. He had filled his cup from an ocean that didn't miss it, but he had still taken. He had accepted a gift he hadn't earned. He had become a consumer in a system where he had vowed to be a maker.

"Well, this just won't do," he muttered, his voice sounding surprisingly certain for a five-year-old in the middle of a forest. 

He looked at the root-walls. He saw the Golden Bind of the tree, so vast and so complete. What could he possibly give back to an entity that was a god in all but name? What could a child with a small hammer and a handful of runes offer to the heart of the world?

He thought about the "Walk of Nature" from earlier that day. He thought about the enclaved fungi that were efficient but lonely. He thought about the ancient tree that was giving its Bind back to the earth. 

"The Tree is an anchor," Tanner thought. "But it's also a burden. It has to hold the whole forest together. Every single leaf, every single insect... it’s all on the Tree."

He realized that the Tree’s Bind was under immense tension—a beautiful, golden tension, but one that was constant and unyielding. It was the tension of the "Protector," the very thing he had declared he wanted to be.

"Reciprocity," he whispered. "If I am to receive, I have to give back."

He couldn't give it mana; it had an ocean. He couldn't give it structure; it was the foundation of the world. But he could give it something else. He could give it *Perspective*. 

Tanner closed his eyes again, but this time he didn't reach out to gather. He reached out to *Show*. 

He took the memories of his father’s forge—the sound of the hammer on the anvil, the specific, intentional geometry of a well-made hinge. He took the memories of his mother’s shop—the way the needle found the flow of the dragon-hide, the precision of a perfect stitch. He took the logic of Mary’s inscriptions—the "If-Then" commands that gave an object a purpose.

He pushed these thoughts into the connection. He offered the Great Tree his understanding of the *Self-Made Path*. He showed it what it looked like to build a world from the ground up, rather than just growing into it. He offered the tree the human perspective of "Making"—the intent to change the world through focused will.

For a long, terrifying moment, the forest went completely silent. The rain seemed to stop mid-air. The hum of the mana-flow vanished. 

Then, the Great Tree answered. 

A wave of warmth, deeper and more profound than the first, washed over Tanner. It wasn't mana this time; it was *curiosity*. The tree had been growing for a thousand years, but it had never been a blacksmith. It had never been a leatherworker. It had never been a five-year-old boy who refused to use a tool he hadn't forged.

Tanner felt the information being absorbed, woven into the massive, silver-gold tapestry of the forest’s memory. In exchange for the trickle of mana he had taken, he had given the Tree a new way to see the Binds it held. He had given the "Protector" of the forest a new set of tools.

His debt was paid. The system was in balance.

Tanner Smith finally uncurled, his body and mind in a state of perfect harmony. He felt the Great Tree’s approval like a warm blanket. He wasn't just an anchor anymore; he was a contributor. 

He fell into a deep, dreamless sleep, the golden pulse of the forest and the small, steady heartbeat of the child beating as one. *Two fast, one slow. Two fast, one slow.*

Tomorrow, he would wake up with a well that was wider, a heart that was deeper, and a world that was just a little bit more his own.
