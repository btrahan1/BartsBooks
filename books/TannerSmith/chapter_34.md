# Chapter 34: Medicine

The root-hollow classroom felt different this morning. 

The air was cooler, carrying the scent of crushed herbs and steeped tea from the vials Parmera had already arranged on the curved wooden tables. Tanner walked in, his staff clicking softly on the floorboards, his pack slung over his shoulder. He felt balanced in a way he hadn't two days prior. His "well," as he had come to call it, felt like a deep, still pond instead of a shallow, flickering cup. It didn't just hold mana; it held the *memory* of the Great Tree’s patient, golden rhythm.

Parmera was standing by the diagram tripod, her back to the door. As Tanner approached, she turned, and for a split second, she hesitated. She took a half-step back, her eyes narrowing as she swept her Sight over the five-year-old child.

He seemed "fuller." It was the only word she could find for it. Two days ago, he had been a sharp, brilliant spark—intense, but fragile. Today, he possessed a gravitational weight. The ambient mana of the room wasn't just swirling around him; it was leaning into him, as if he were a natural anchor for the Great Tree’s energy. The faint, blue-gold glow of his core wasn't just visible to the Sight; it was almost palpable to the skin, a low-voltage hum that vibrating in the space between them.

"Tanner," she said, her voice carrying a trace of the unease she usually reserved for rogue mana-storms. "You've been busy."

Tanner smiled, the expression grounded and calm. "I spent some time with the Tree, Teacher. And I took a walk."

Parmera adjusted the sleeves of her clinical robes, trying to find her professional center. The boy never ceased to amaze her, and—if she were being honest with herself—he slightly frightened her. To grow that much in forty-eight hours suggested a capacity for internalizing system-logic that defied every textbook in the Thornwood archives. Most students spent months learning to "anchor" their core; Tanner seemed to have simply accepted the Great Tree’s resonance as a personal upgrade.

"I see," she said, clearing her throat. "Well. Since you are... sufficiently recovered, we shall move from anatomy to application. Today’s lesson is Medicine."

She gestured to the table, where several jars of different-colored powders and liquids were arranged in a precise, clinical grid. 

"Medicine," she began, her sharp, analytical tone returning to the foreground. "What is it? How do we use it? When do we use it? What does it do, and most importantly, how does it affect healing? These are the things we will learn." 

She hesitated, her gaze drifting to the heartwood apple sitting on the pedestal where they had left it. 

"I was going to say we would cover this over the next few days," she admitted, her voice dropping an octave. "But... well... it's you. So let's just see how much you learn today."

Tanner stepped to the table, his staff leaning against his shoulder. He looked at the jars through his Sight. They weren't just chemicals; they were bottles of intent. He saw the way the light refracted through the glass, not just hitting the surface of the powders, but sinking into the mana-frequencies they emitted.

"Medicine is a bridge," Tanner said, his voice quiet. "Right?"

Parmera paused, a jar of *Silver-Seed Oil* in her hand. "A bridge?"

"In the forge," Tanner explained, "if I have two pieces of metal that won't fuse, I use flux. The flux doesn't stay in the metal, but it allows the heat to reach the center so the molecules can find each other. It breaks the surface tension. Medicine is the flux for the Life-Bind."

Parmera stood perfectly still. She had spent twenty years teaching novice healers that medicine was a "biochemical catalyst for mana-absorption." Tanner had just described it as a blacksmith’s tool, a temporary agent that exists only to facilitate a permanent change.

"That is... a very accurate, if unconventional, way of putting it," she said. She set the jar down and uncorked it. The scent of ozone and mint flooded the small space. "Tell me what you see in the Silver-Seed."

Tanner leaned in. Through the Sight, the oil wasn't a clear liquid. It was a dense, swirling cloud of tiny, silver-white Blue spheres. They were vibrating at a very high frequency—much higher than the Great Tree’s rhythm. It was a frantic, aggressive energy, designed to pierce rather than to hold.

"It's fast," Tanner noted. "It’s all Magic. There's almost no Might in it at all. It’s like... pure instruction without any wood to hold it. If you use too much, it would just wash everything away."

"Silver-Seed Oil is a *catalyst*," Parmera explained. "We use it when the *Magic* sphere is stagnant. If a patient’s internal mana channels are blocked or cold, the oil enters the system and its high-frequency resonance 'breaks' the blockage, allowing the Blue to flow again. But look at the next jar."

She pointed to a thick, dark-green paste. It looked like clay, dense and slightly oily.

"This is *Crushed Iron-Bark*," Parmera said. "What do you see?"

Tanner shifted his focus. The paste was the opposite of the oil. It was a heavy, dense collection of deep-red spheres. They were slow—almost static—and they had a very strong Golden Bind connecting them to each other. It didn't vibrate; it *anchored*. It felt like the weight of a heavy hammer resting on a soft surface.

"It’s a wall," Tanner said. "It’s all Might. It’s dense and stubborn. It doesn't want to move at all."

"Iron-Bark is for structural failure," Parmera said. "When the *Might* is too weak to hold the *Magic*, we apply the paste. It provides a temporary lattice of structural integrity, giving the Golden knot time to rest and reorganize. In your terms, Tanner, the Silver-Seed is the heat of the forge, and the Iron-Bark is the anvil. One softens, the other supports."

Tanner nodded, his mind already weaving these concepts into his Three Spheres model. He reached into his pack and pulled out a small, leather-bound notebook his father had given him before he left Elveron. He began to draw—not recipes, but diagrams of overlapping spheres. He sketched the jagged, high-frequency spikes of the Silver-Seed against the broad, flat waves of the Iron-Bark.

"So," Tanner said, looking up at her, his pencil tapping against the page. "If I want to heal your shoulder, I wouldn't just use one. I would need to use both at the same time. The Silver-Seed to clear the 'friction' I saw, and the Iron-Bark to give the Gold something to anchor to while it finds the new rhythm."

Parmera felt a chill that had nothing to do with the morning air. "You haven't forgotten about the shoulder."

"I haven't," Tanner said. "It’s a broken intent. The bone thinks it’s still falling, and the mana is trying to move around a shadow of the break. You need to remind the bone it's already on the ground, and you need to flux the mana so it stops circling. It’s a cold-shut in the Bind."

He looked at the jars again, his eyes narrowing as he analyzed the interaction between the two substances.

"But the Silver-Seed is too fast," Tanner noticed. "If I put it in your shoulder, it would break the blockage, but it would also rattle the bone. The frequency is too high for the Red to absorb. You’d be in more pain."

Parmera stepped around the table, her interest now entirely overriding her clinical caution. She had never considered the *speed* of the medicine’s resonance as a conflict with the body’s physical mass. "And how would you solve that, Student?"

Tanner picked up his staff. He touched the steel cap to the jar of Silver-Seed Oil. 

"I would use the staff to flux the medicine first," he said. "I would match the Silver-Seed to the Great Tree’s rhythm *before* I give it to you. I would use my own well to anchor the oil, slowing it down just enough to be accepted by the bone, but keeping it fast enough to clear the channels. I'd make the medicine part of the song."

Parmera reached out and took the jar from him, her fingers brushing his. She felt the resonance of his "well"—that deep, stable hum of the forest. She realized that Tanner wasn't just learning how to use medicine; he was learning how to *enchant* it on the fly. He was treating pharmacology as a branch of metallurgy and enchanting.

"I have spent my life following the recipes of the Arch-Druids," Parmera said softly. "But you... you are treating it like a construction project. You are engineering the cure."

"Systems are systems," Tanner said simply. "Whether it's iron, dragon-hide, or bone. The laws don't change just because something is breathing. The Bind is the Bind."

Parmera looked at the diagram on the tripod—the Venn Diagram of the Life-Bind. She realized that Tanner wasn't looking at the drawing anymore. He was looking through it. He was seeing the molecules of the oil and the fibers of the bark as if they were threads in a loom he was currently re-stringing.

"Tomorrow," Parmera said, her voice sounding steadier than she felt. "We will combine the medicines. We will create a *Resonance-Poultice*. And then, Tanner Smith... we will see if your 'Reminding' can truly mend a twenty-year-old shadow."

Tanner bowed. "I'll be ready, Teacher."

As he walked out of the hollow, the staff clicking against the bark, Parmera watched him go. She looked at the jars of oil and paste, and for the first time in two decades, they looked like raw materials to her—stubborn ingredients waiting for a smith’s hand. She picked up the jar of Silver-Seed Oil and looked at her own reflection in the liquid.

"He's not just fixing the shoulder," she whispered to the empty room. "He's fixing the world."

Outside, the sun finally broke through the canopy shadows, turning the Great Tree into a pillar of gold. Tanner Smith walked home, his well full, his mind sharp, and his next project already beginning to take shape in the fire of his intent. He had gone from a student of anatomy to a smith of biology, and the forest, it seemed, was more than happy to provide the material.
