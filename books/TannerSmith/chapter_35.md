# Chapter 35: The Model, The Blueprint, and the Mirror Image

The air in the root-hollow felt heavy, as if the Great Tree itself were holding its breath. 

Tanner stopped in the archway, his staff held loosely in his right hand, his eyes widening as he took in the scene. The classroom was no longer a quiet refuge for three. A crowd had gathered, a dozen or more young druidic students and senior acolytes lining the bark walls, their murmurs creating a low-frequency static that vibrated in the damp air.

Parmera stood in the center of the hollow, her clinical robes slightly askew, a deep frown creasing her brow. She looked more like a cornered animal than an anatomy expert.

"He's just a novice," Parmera snapped, her voice sharp with a defensive edge. "Why are you all here? This is a private lesson."

"We were invited," one of the younger students spoke up, a girl with silver hair and eyes that shimmered with nascent druidic power. 

"By whom?" Sylara asked, her voice calm but carrying the weight of authority. She was standing near Parmera, her hands folded into her sleeves.

Several students looked at each other and then shrugged, a ripple of unease moving through the crowd. "I'm not sure," another spoke up, a boy with the sun-gold tint of the High Elves. "It just seemed... like I should be here. Like the Tree was leaning in this direction."

Tanner strode forward, the clicking of his staff cutting through the murmurs. He didn't look at the crowd; he looked at Parmera. He could see the "friction" in her shoulder through the Sight, a jagged, oily void in her Blue sphere that refused to let the Gold knot settle. 

"It is good that you are all here," Tanner said, his voice surprisingly steady for a five-year-old child. He looked at the silver-haired girl and then at the sun-gold boy. "I may need your help."

Parmera turned to him, her eyes wide. "Help? Tanner, what are you planning?"

"A fair exchange," Tanner said simply. 

He didn't wait for permission. He set his pack on the floor and pulled out the velvet-wrapped heartwood apple. He also pulled out the small jars of Silver-Seed Oil and Iron-Bark Paste. The scent of ozone and mint filled the space, and the crowd went silent, their attention locked onto the polished sphere of heartwood.

"Sit, Teacher," Tanner commanded. 

Parmera hesitated, looked at Sylara, who gave a nearly imperceptible nod, and then sat on the low wooden stool. 

Tanner stepped behind her. He didn't go for the medicine first. He closed his eyes and expanded his well. He felt the connection he had established the night before—the anchor to the Great Tree. He felt the "ocean" sitting behind his "cup."

He began the "Reminding."

He placed his right hand on her left shoulder—the damaged one. He began to push a thread of his own resonance into her system. He tried to show the bone and the mana-channels what they were supposed to look like. He used the "Model" he had studied in the diagram—the perfect spheres of Might and Magic.

But he hit a wall. 

The injury was twenty years old. It wasn't just a break anymore; it was a habit. The bone had grown into the shadow of its own pain, and the mana-channels had literalized the friction until they were like calloused leather. The scale of the restoration was too vast for his internal well, even as large as it had become. The "Misfit Penalty" of her body was resisting his "Blueprint."

Tanner’s brow beaded with sweat. His hand began to tremble. Through the Sight, he saw his own Blue sphere beginning to flicker, the "Emptying" phase arriving far sooner than he had anticipated. 

"It’s too deep," he whispered. "The intent is lost."

He didn't panic. He reached into his pocket and pulled out the heartwood apple. He held it in his left hand, the *Internal Mirror* rune catch the morning light.

He didn't focus on the *damaged* shoulder. He turned his Sight to her *right* shoulder—the healthy one. 

"The Mirror Image," Tanner thought.

He used the apple to "Capture" the resonance of her healthy side. Through the apple’s internal system, he created a perfect, golden Blueprint of her biological symmetry. The Apple vibrated, its golden pulse matching the perfect rhythm of her healthy Bind. 

Then, Tanner moved. 

He didn't just hold the apple; he *superimposed* the image. He used his Sight to project the golden Blueprint of the right shoulder directly over the damaged architecture of the left. It was like a master builder laying a translucent drawing over a collapsed wall to see exactly where the bricks should be.

The room gasped. To those with even a spark of druidic talent, a translucent, golden map appeared around Parmera’s shoulder—a perfect, shimmering ghost of what she was supposed to be.

But the ghost was fading. Tanner’s mana reserves hit the bottom. He felt the cold, hollow void rushing back into his chest, and for a second, the golden Blueprint flickered like a dying candle.

Tanner looked up, his eyes glowing with a fierce, desperate gold. 

"The Well is empty," he shouted to the crowd. "Anchor me! Form a chain! Give me your rhythm!"

The students hesitated for a heartbeat, and then the silver-haired girl stepped forward. She placed her hand on Tanner’s shoulder. Then the sun-gold boy. Then another, and another, until a chain of druidic students was connected to the child in the center.

It wasn't a "Stitch." It was a "Communion." 

Tanner became the conductor. He felt their diverse mana-flows—the chaotic reds, the sharp blues, the soft golds—falling into his rhythm. He didn't take their mana for himself; he passed it through his core, filtered it through his connection to the Great Tree, and then poured it into the heartwood apple.

The apple didn't just vibrate; it *shouted*. 

The golden Blueprint over Parmera’s shoulder went from a ghost to a solid, blinding reality. The Silver-Seed Oil and the Iron-Bark Paste on the table suddenly vaporized, turning into a fine, iridescent mist that was drawn into the golden light.

"Remind her!" Tanner roared, his voice carrying the weight of the entire forest.

Parmera’s shoulder flared with a light so bright that Sylara had to shield her eyes. The sound was like a thousand glass harps being struck at once. 

Through the Sight, the jagged, oily void in the Blue sphere was suddenly fluxed. The Silver-Seed mist broke the twenty-year stagnation, and the Iron-Bark lattice provided the structural anvil. The golden knot of the Life-Bind, faced with its own mirror image, didn't just settle. It *leaped*.

The bone audibly clicked—a clean, resonating sound of things finding their place. The mana-channels straightened, the "friction" dissolving into a smooth, sapphire river. 

The light reached its peak, a golden explosion that bathed the entire root-hollow, turning the bark to bronze and the moss to emerald velvet.

Then, silence.

The golden Blueprint vanished. The mist settled. The chain of students broke, many of them stumbling back, gasping as their own wells hit their limits.

Tanner stood behind Parmera, his left hand still holding the apple, his right hand resting on her shoulder. He was pale, his eyes slowly losing their glow, but he wasn't trembling. He was solid.

Parmera slowly stood up. She didn't move her shoulder at first. She just stood there, her eyes welling with tears. Then, with a tentative, almost fearful motion, she raised her left arm. 

She rotated it. Once. Twice. She performed a full circle—a movement she hadn't been able to complete without a sharp, grinding pain since the day she fell as a girl. 

There was no pain. There was only the feeling of a song that had finally found its bridge.

"It’s gone," she whispered. "The shadow... it’s gone."

The students began to cheer, a low, awestruck sound that quickly grew into a roar. But Sylara was looking at Tanner. She saw the way his well was already beginning to knit back together, wider and deeper than anyone had a right to be. 

She saw the heartwood apple in his hand. It wasn't glowing anymore, but it looked... changed. The wood was darker, almost black, and the *Mirror* rune was now permanently etched in a deep, burning gold.

"He's not just a student," one of the acolytes whispered, staring at the five-year-old child. "He's a Mirror-Smith."

Tanner looked at his grandmother and smiled. It was a tired smile, but it was the smile of someone who had faced the system and had seen it blink.

"Tuition is paid, Teacher," Tanner said softly.

He finally sat down on the floor, the apple cradled in his lap, and for the first time in Thornwood’s history, the Great Tree’s rhythm and the room’s rhythm and the boy’s rhythm were exactly the same.

The shadow was gone. The path was clear. And the world had just seen what happens when the "Self-Made" decide to help the world find its way home.
