# Chapter 36: The Feast

The silence that followed the golden explosion wasn't empty; it was thick with the weight of change. 

Tanner stood in the center of the root-hollow, his small chest heaving, his hands finally releasing the heartwood apple that had anchored the world. His well was beyond empty—it was a parched desert, demanding to be filled. The "Mirror Blueprint" had been more than a tool; it had been a storm, and he had been the lightning rod.

He looked around the room, seeing the dazed expressions on the faces of the students, the wet tracks of tears on Parmera’s cheeks, and the silent, profound pride in Sylara’s eyes. He felt the Great Tree’s pulse—steady, slow, and approving. It was a massive, vibrating frequency that seemed to be patting him on the back.

Then, his stomach gave a growl that was surprisingly loud in the quiet chamber. It was a deep, primal sound, the Red sphere demanding its due after the Blue had spent everything.

Tanner smiled, the intensity of the "Mirror-Smith" replaced by the simple, base needs of a five-year-old boy. He looked at the crowd and said, quite simply, "I'm hungry."

It was the perfect "flux" for the tension in the room. The awe broke into a ripple of laughter, then into a wave of relieved energy. Tanner turned to the chain of students who were still catching their breath, their own mana-pools drained by the communion. He saw the sweat on their brows and the same hollow look in their eyes that he had felt after the fern healing.

He stepped toward the silver-haired girl and the sun-gold boy, bowing deeply—a bow that started from the waist and ended with the quiet, humble acknowledgment of a peer.

"Thank you for your help," Tanner said, his voice echoing in the hollow. "And thank you for lending your strength. I was not strong enough on my own. I think that's what I learned here today. We are all one under the eyes of the Great Tree."

He looked at his calloused hands, the hands of a smith who usually worked in isolation. "A single thread is easy to break. But a woven seam... that’s what holds the dragon-hide together. You were the seams today."

For a moment, the students just stared at him. They had expected a lecture on the "Mirror" runes or a boast about his Golden Resonance. Instead, they had received a lesson on the necessity of each other. 

Then, the sun-gold boy grinned and slammed his fist against his chest in the elven salute. "If we are the thread, Little Smith, then you are the needle! You led the stitch!"

Another student shouted, "And we've worked up a thirst that the Great Well itself couldn't quench!"

The chant began then, low and rhythmic, built on the same frequency as the Great Tree’s own pulse. It started with one voice, then three, then everyone in the room, their voices joining in a vibration that rattled the vials on Parmera’s table.

"Feast! Feast! Feast!"

---

The Feast of the Mending wasn't held in the root-hollow. It was held on the "Crowned Canopy"—the highest habitable tier of Thornwood, a vast, open-air platform formed by the natural flattened growth of seven interlocking branches of the Great Tree. 

As the sun began to dip below the horizon, turning the clouds into sheets of burning copper and bruised violet, the entire village seemed to converge on the Great Tree. Thornwood was a place of slow growth, but when it came to celebration, the druids moved with the efficiency of a summer storm. It was as if the forest itself had decided that a 20-year-old shadow had been lifted, and it was time to celebrate the return of the light.

Long, low tables made of living, woven vines were arranged in a circle around a central hearth of "Ever-Warm Stones"—magically heated porous rocks that glowed with a soft, orange light without the need for smoke. The stones didn't just provide heat; they resonated with a low, comforting frequency that helped stabilize the mana-depleted students.

Tanner sat at the head of the inner circle, flanked by Sylara on one side and a glowing, revitalized Parmera on the other. For the first time in his life, he didn't feel the need to be "working." He didn't have a chisel in his hand or a needle in his fingers. He simply sat, his staff leaning against the table, watching the sensory explosion of a Thornwood celebration.

The food was a testament to the "Might" of the forest.

There were bowls of *Sun-Dipped Walnuts*, honey-roasted and dusted with crushed cinnamon-moss. They were sweet, salty, and carried a faint, restorative warmth that Tanner could feel the moment they touched his tongue. There were platters of *Lichen-Cakes*, dense and savory, served with a spread made from the fermented milk of the *Cloud-Goats*. For the first time, Tanner saw meat—thinly sliced, smoke-cured venison from the forest floor, served on beds of aromatic needles.

"Eat, Tanner," Parmera prompted, piling a mountain of lichen-cakes onto his wooden plate. Her left arm moved with a fluid, effortless grace that caught the orange light of the stones. She was constantly moving it, as if she were still convincing herself the pain was truly gone. "Your Red sphere is screaming. You cannot build a mountain on an empty stomach."

Tanner didn't need a second invitation. He ate with the single-minded focus he usually reserved for his forge. Each bite of the rich, honeyed bread and the salty venison felt like it was physically knitting his core back together. He could feel the "fuel" turning into warmth, moving from his stomach to his fingertips. The Red sphere was refilling, providing the foundation for the Blue to return.

To drink, there was *Aether-Sap*—a golden, fermented nectar pulled from the high-canopy flowers. It went down cool, like spring water, but left a tingling trail of mana-heat in its wake. As Tanner drank, he felt his "well" begin to soften, the dry-earth feeling of his Blue sphere being replaced by a gentle, mist-like moisture. It wasn't the roaring ocean of the communion, but a gentle rain that was bringing the desert back to life.

As the feast progressed, the formality of the village began to dissolve into a warm, chaotic harmony. The silver-haired girl—whose name he learned was Faelen—sat across from him, asking a hundred questions about "flux" and "anvils." The sun-gold boy, Elian, showed him a way to weave mana-circlets using only the ambient light of the hearth-stones, creating tiny coronas of shifting color that floated above the plates.

"You changed us, you know," Faelen said, her eyes bright with the sap-nectar. "When the chain formed... I felt my own Bind, really felt it, for the first time. I saw how it touched yours. I didn't know I could be a part of something that big. It was like seeing the stars from the inside."

Tanner paused, a sun-dipped walnut halfway to his mouth. He looked at Faelen, seeing the way her own Gold knot had been strengthened by the connection. 

"You weren't just a part of it, Faelen," Tanner said. "You were the reason it worked. The Mirror Blueprint was just a drawing until you all gave it the light to see. A blueprint without mana is just a piece of paper. You made it real."

Sylara watched the exchange from her seat, her hand resting on the woven vine of the table. She looked at her grandson—this five-year-old child who was currently holding court among some of the brightest students in the enclave.

"He found the rhythm," she whispered to Parmera.

"No," Parmera corrected, her voice thick with wonder. "He *built* the rhythm. He treated our souls like a stack of iron and he forge-welded us into a single piece. I can still feel the weld, Sylara. It’s the strongest thing I’ve ever felt."

The celebration lasted deep into the night. Musicians appeared from the shadows of the secondary branches, playing harps made of resonant root-wood and flutes carved from hollowed ironwood. The music wasn't just sounds; it was a physical force, the melodies matching the Great Tree’s pulse, causing the leaves above them to vibrate in a synchronized, shivering dance that looked like shifting emerald lace.

Tanner sat back, his stomach full, his mind finally quiet. He looked up at the stars, which were visible through the gaps in the high canopy. They looked like the sparks from a giant anvil, scattered across the dark velvet of the sky. 

He realized that his "Self-Made" philosophy hadn't been broken today; it had been *tempered*. He would still never wear a borrowed shirt or use a looted sword. He would still forge his own tools and carve his own path. That was his Red sphere—his foundation.

But he understood now that "Self-Made" didn't mean "Alone." That was the realization of the Blue and the Gold.

A master smith still needed the miners to find the ore. A master leatherworker still needed the hunter to find the hide. And a Protector... a Protector needed a community to protect. If there was no one else, then the "Protector" was just a man with a heavy shield and nothing to shield.

He looked at the Great Tree, seeing the golden threads of the root-network in his mind. He was a part of the system. He was an anchor. And he was a friend.

As the feast began to wind down and the golden sap-nectar started to lull the village into a happy, exhausted sleep, Tanner leaned his head against the living wood of the terrace railing. He felt the warmth of the hearth-stones, the cool breath of the night air, and the steady, patient heartbeat of the forest.

*Two fast, one slow. Two fast, one slow.*

"Tuition is paid," he whispered to the stars. 

"More than paid," Sylara said, her hand resting on his shoulder. "But tomorrow, Tanner... tomorrow, the Arch-Druids are going to want to talk to the boy who smiths life. And I think your parents are going to want to know why their son is suddenly the talk of Thornwood."

Tanner didn't frown. He simply closed his eyes, his "well" finally feeling peaceful, a deep, sapphire pond reflecting the light of a new world. 

He had survived the mending. He had survived the feast. And now, he was finally ready to dream.
