### **Chapter 3: A Transaction of Shadows**

Two days passed. Two days of sweeping. Two days of meticulously cataloging every jar, root, and leaf in the shop, a task Melissa completed with a terrifying efficiency that left Ren's old organizational system looking like a child's messy scrawl. She was bored. Ren could feel it in her Hue, a low, thrumming impatience beneath the cool grey exterior. She was a finely tuned instrument designed for a symphony of espionage, and he had her playing scales.

It was time.

He waited until the end of the second day, after the last customer had left and the bolt was slid across the door. Melissa was wiping down the counter, her movements economical and precise.

"You move quietly," Ren observed, his voice startling her in the quiet shop. It was the first time she hadn't heard him approach.

She turned, her expression unreadable. "It's a professional requirement."

"It is," Ren agreed. "Your footsteps are silent. Your breathing is controlled. The rustle of your clothes is almost nonexistent." He paused, meeting her analytical gaze. "But you are not silent. To me, you are a shout in a library."

Melissa's eyebrow rose. "Explain."

"Your presence," Ren said. "Your Ki. It's coiled, alert, ready. It has a Resonance. A sharp edge. Anyone with the senses to notice can feel you coming from a room away. You hide your body perfectly, but you do not hide your soul."

This was a language Melissa understood. It wasn't philosophy; it was a professional critique of her craft. A flaw in her technique she hadn't even known existed. "And you believe you can teach me how to hide it?" she asked, her voice laced with a professional's skepticism.

"I can," Ren said simply. "I can teach you the Art of Stillness. How to dampen your own Resonance until you are a blank. A gap in the world. Imagine it. They wouldn't just fail to see you. They would fail to even feel you."

He had her. The desire to perfect her craft, to gain an edge, was a powerful lure. The hunter in her grey eyes gleamed. "A valuable skill," she conceded. "What is your price?"

Now came the delicate part. The transaction. "An exchange of services," Ren said, his tone all business. "A lesson for a lesson."

"You want to learn how to kill a man with a piece of string?" she asked, a hint of dry amusement in her voice.

"No," Ren countered. "I am a healer. I often have to go to dangerous places to treat people who cannot come to me—the Undercity, the smuggler's dens on the wharf. I am not a fighter. I have no desire to be one. But I have a great desire to not be seen at all."

He laid out his half-truth, a perfectly plausible and sympathetic story. "You are a master of the physical art of stealth. How to use shadows, how to judge a footstep on gravel versus stone, how to open a locked door without a sound. You will teach me how to walk through the world without being a target."

He met her gaze, his offer clear. "And I will teach you how to walk through it without being a ripple. A cleansing of your spiritual presence, in exchange for a strengthening of my physical one."

Melissa was silent for a long time, her analytical mind dissecting the proposal. It was a fair trade. An elegant one, even. His request was logical for a man in his position. And his offer... his offer was the key to a level of mastery she had never imagined.

"Agreed," she said finally. "Show me."

Ren smiled. "The lesson is not about movement. It is about its opposite." He gestured to the center of the floor. "Stand. Find your center. Now, be still."

Melissa did as she was told, her body instantly falling into a perfectly balanced, low-profile stance. She was as still as a statue.

"That is the stillness of a predator waiting to strike," Ren said gently. "Your body is still, but your spirit is a coiled spring. I want you to find the stillness of a stone at the bottom of a river. Unthinking. Uncaring. Simply... being." He closed his eyes. "Reach for the quietest place inside you. The place that exists between breaths. And hold onto it."

She tried. She focused, attempting to calm the razor's edge of her own Ki. But it was impossible. Her entire life, her every instinct, had been honed for alertness, for action. To be truly still felt like dying.

"I can't," she gritted out, a note of frustration in her voice. "There is no quiet place."

Ren opened his eyes, his gaze soft and full of a sad understanding. He could feel the reason. Her Ki was not a calm pool. It was a turbulent, churning sea, filled with the dissonant, jagged Echoes of her past contracts. The ghosts of the lives she had taken. They were the source of her skill, but they were also the cage that kept her from true mastery.

"Yes, there is," Ren said softly. "It is just buried very deep." He took a step closer. "Let me show you."

He reached out, not to touch her, but to let a single, pure thread of his own silver Ki connect with her turbulent aura. He didn't force it. He offered it. A single, harmonious, silver note in her chaotic, grey symphony.

"Listen to the silence," he whispered.

And for a single, breathtaking second, she felt it. A moment of perfect, absolute peace. The ghosts went quiet. The coiled spring uncoiled. She was not a predator. She was not a weapon. She was just... Melissa.

The moment passed, and the chaos rushed back in. But it was too late. She had felt it. A new path had been illuminated.

She looked at Ren, her cool grey eyes wide with a startling new emotion: wonder.

The cleansing of her soul, and the secret strengthening of The Ghost, had just begun.

Melissa stood frozen, the phantom sensation of that perfect, fleeting silence still echoing within her. It was a peace she hadn't known was possible, a quiet center in the lifelong storm of her existence. When the chaos of her own Ki rushed back in, it felt different—harsher, more dissonant, an intruder in a space that had, for a moment, been sacred.

"How?" she whispered, the single word a raw admission of her own profound ignorance. "How did you do that?"

"I didn't do anything," Ren replied gently. "I just helped you listen to your own silence. It is always there. The ghosts are just very loud." He gave her a moment, letting her absorb the reality of what she had just experienced. "The Stillness is not a technique. It is a state of being. To achieve it, we cannot just suppress the noise. We must understand it. We must... catalog it."

He gestured for her to sit on one of the low stools. He sat opposite her, mirroring their first session with Kaelen, but the energy was entirely different. This was not a confession to be dredged up; it was a professional debriefing of the soul.

"Now," he began, his voice a low, therapeutic hum. "Think about your last... opportunity." He chose the word carefully, a neutral, professional term. "The last contract you fulfilled before Kaelen brought you to my door."

Melissa’s grey Hue sharpened, the professional assassin returning. "A guild official. He was selling shipping manifests to pirates. It was a simple extraction. Clean." Her description was devoid of emotion, a clinical report.

"I do not need the details of the contract," Ren said, his voice soft but insistent. "I need the details of *you*. Think about the reason you did it. The coin, yes. But what else? The challenge? The satisfaction of removing a corrupt piece from the board?"

He leaned forward slightly, his gaze gentle but unwavering. "Think about how it made you feel. Before, during, and after. The cold focus as you planned. The detached calm as you executed the task. The... emptiness, afterward. No judgments," he added quickly, sensing her defenses rising. "There is no right or wrong feeling. Just think about... your soul. Your core. Your Ki. What was its Resonance in those moments?"

He didn't need to touch her this time. He just watched, his senses open. He was asking her to perform a self-diagnosis, to turn her own keen, analytical mind inward and observe the mechanics of her own spirit.

Melissa closed her eyes, her brow furrowed in concentration. It was a strange, unfamiliar exercise. She was a master of observing others, of reading their habits and weaknesses. She had never thought to apply that same ruthless analysis to herself.

She walked back through the memory. She felt the familiar, cold thrill of the hunt, the intellectual puzzle of bypassing the official’s guards and wards. Her Ki, she realized, had been sharp and focused, a honed blade. During the act itself, it had been a perfect, flat, grey calm—the professional detachment she had cultivated for years.

But after...

After, as she had slipped away into the pre-dawn mist, her payment secured, she remembered the feeling. It wasn't triumph. It wasn't guilt. It was... nothing. A profound, hollow silence. An absence. Her Ki, which had been a sharp, focused tool, had simply... receded, leaving behind a void.

And in that void, the ghosts began to whisper again.

Her eyes snapped open, a look of startling, unwelcome revelation on her face. She had always thought of the hollowness as a necessary byproduct of her work, a sign of her professionalism. She had never considered that the hollowness *was* the wound. The work didn't fill a need; it created a void that she then had to endure until the next contract provided a new, temporary distraction.

"The silence afterward," she said, her voice a low, stunned whisper. "It is... louder than the work itself."

Ren nodded slowly, a deep, sorrowful understanding in his eyes. He had his diagnosis. Her soul wasn't diseased like Kaelen's. It was starving. And her profession, the only thing that gave her purpose, was the very thing that was draining her.

"Yes," he said softly. "It often is."

The revelation left Melissa feeling exposed, as if a meticulously crafted piece of armor had been revealed to be nothing more than paper. She had built her entire identity around her professional detachment, her ability to feel nothing. To have that "strength" reframed as a symptom of a profound, spiritual hunger was disorienting. She braced herself for more questions, for a deeper, more uncomfortable excavation of her past.

Instead, Ren stood up and stretched, the moment of intense therapy abruptly finished.

"Now," he said, his tone shifting back to that of a slightly eccentric shopkeeper, "back to work. I see a speck of dust in the corner over there. A particularly stubborn one."

Melissa stared at him, bewildered by the sudden shift. "We're... we're just stopping?"

"The diagnosis is made," Ren replied matter-of-factly. "Now comes the treatment. And the treatment is the work." He gestured to the broom. "But as you sweep, forget what I said yesterday. Don't breathe in and out, don't contemplate the dust... what nonsense."

He walked over to her, his expression turning serious again, the teacher returning. "This time, I want you to focus on what's inside of you. Your Ki. That churning, chaotic sea. As you make each sweep with the broom, I want you to try to faintly 'brush against' your own Ki with your mind. A light touch, like a feather."

He made a slow, sweeping motion with his hand. "Don't try to control it. Don't try to suppress it. Just... feel it. Acknowledge its presence with each physical motion. Find your rhythm. Find your Ki. Find your CORE," he said, his voice dropping to a near-whisper, "and with each pass of the broom, ask it to be... quieter."

He stepped back, leaving her to process the strange, esoteric instruction. It was a meditative exercise, but a profoundly active one. He wasn't asking her to sit still and find a silence that didn't exist for her. He was giving her a physical, repetitive task, a rhythm to anchor herself to, and asking her to integrate her spiritual practice into it. He was teaching her how to find stillness *in motion*.

Melissa looked from Ren to the broom, then to the offending speck of dust in the corner. It was the most ridiculous, and yet the most profound, training exercise she had ever been given. It was a challenge perfectly tailored to her. A puzzle of the soul disguised as a household chore.

She took a deep breath, the ghosts of her past contracts already beginning to whisper in the hollow place inside her. She picked up the broom.

*Find your rhythm.*

She began to sweep. The bristles scraped against the floorboards. *Scrape.*

She reached inward, tentatively, and 'brushed' against the turbulent grey sea of her own Ki. *Whisper.*

*Find your Ki.*

*Scrape.*
*Whisper.*

*Find your Core.*

*Scrape.*
*Whisper.*
*Quiet.*

She swept, and for the first time in her life, she was not running from the silence. She was trying to create it, one speck of dust, one breath, one heartbeat at a time. And Ren watched, knowing that the strengthening of The Ghost was not to be found in learning to throw a knife, but in learning how to be still.

The days fell into a new, strange rhythm. By day, Melissa was the model assistant—efficient, quiet, her presence in the shop a calming, grey-stone anchor. She swept, she ground herbs, she dealt with the occasional surly customer with a dispassionate stare that could curdle milk. And with every sweep of the broom, with every turn of the pestle, she practiced. She found the rhythm, she found her Ki, and she began, ever so faintly, to find the quiet place. The turbulent sea within her hadn't calmed, but she was learning to chart its currents, to navigate its storms. The ghosts still whispered, but their voices were becoming more distant.

On the third evening, after the shop had closed and the last of the cleansing smoke had cleared the air, Ren called her over. He had lit a single candle and placed it on the floor in the center of the room.

"It is time to extract my own payment," he said, his voice calm and matter-of-fact. "The second half of our lesson."

He sat down on the floor, crossing his legs in a meditative posture. He looked up at her, his expression serene. "Come. Hold my hands."

Melissa snorted, a sharp, cynical sound. She crossed her arms, her professional guard slamming back into place. "Not that kind of girl, healer. I am not won over so easily by a few deep thoughts and some fancy breathing." The words were a jest, but they were also a test, a shield against the unfamiliar vulnerability she had been feeling.

Ren didn't react. He didn't argue or try to persuade her. He just sat on the floor, his legs crossed, his hands held out, palms up, in a simple, unwavering invitation. And he waited.

The silence stretched. Melissa stood, her arms crossed, a bastion of cool, grey skepticism. But the longer he waited, the more his patient, unyielding silence began to feel like a force of nature. It wasn't a demand. It was an offer. An opportunity. He was not asking her to be won over; he was asking her to participate, to be a partner in the transaction they had agreed upon.

With a long, suffering sigh that was mostly for show, she finally relented. "Fine," she grumbled, lowering herself to the floor opposite him, her movements fluid and precise. "But if you start chanting about the gibbous moon again, the price of my silence goes up to two gold crowns."

She reached out and took his hands. His grip was light, his skin cool.

"Now what?" she asked, her tone still laced with sarcasm.

"Now," Ren said, his voice dropping to a low, focused murmur, "you become the teacher. And I become the student." He closed his eyes. "Take me with you. Show me how you walked through the Guild Official's house. Not the memory. The *technique*. Let me feel what you feel. Let your Ki be the guide. Show me how a shadow moves."

Melissa looked at their joined hands, skepticism warring with a nascent, professional curiosity. This was not the lesson she had expected to give. She taught by drill, by repetition, by demonstrating a physical action until it was muscle memory. This... this was something else entirely. She had no idea how to "show" someone a technique with her mind.

"I will merge our Ki," Ren explained, sensing her uncertainty. His voice was a calm, steady guide in the strange new territory. "It will feel... strange at first. A second current in your own river. Do not fight it. Just allow it."

She felt it then. A thin, cool, silver thread weaving into the turbulent grey sea of her own Ki. It was not an intrusion. It was a connection, a bridge.

"Then," he continued, "I will show you an image. A memory. Of me making a poultice. Watch it. Study it. Learn from it. It will be your anchor, a simple task to focus your mind."

An image bloomed in her consciousness, as clear and vivid as a waking dream. She saw through Ren's eyes. She saw his hands—*her* hands—expertly grinding herbs. She felt the texture of the leaves, smelled the clean, earthy scent. The memory was calm, peaceful, and deeply focused.

"While you watch," Ren's voice was a gentle undercurrent in the shared mental space, "you will teach. All I want you to do is picture yourself on your last contract. In the shadows of the Guild Official's house. Still. Unnoticeable. Invisible. Not the memory of the act itself, but the *feeling* of being a shadow. Project that feeling to me through our connection."

He added the final, crucial instruction. "All the while, also focus on your own Ki, your own core, which I can actually begin to see forming within you. And will *it* into the shadows as well."

It was an exercise in profound mental multitasking. She had to hold his memory, project her own feeling, and focus on her own nascent core, all at once. It was the most difficult thing she had ever been asked to do.

She took a breath and began.

She held the image of Ren's hands, the simple, peaceful task a rock in the storm of her mind. Then, she reached for the feeling. The feeling of being a ghost. Of the world sliding past, oblivious. She recalled the sensation of her own Ki, flattened and sharp, a razor's edge of pure, silent focus. She took that feeling and pushed it, gently, across the silver bridge into Ren's mind.

At the same time, she followed Ren's final instruction. She sought the quiet place inside herself, the tiny, flickering spark of stillness she had been cultivating. Her core. It was small, fragile, a mere pinpoint of condensed grey light. She focused on it, and with all her will, she tried to infuse it with the same shadowy quality, to teach it to be silent.

From Ren's perspective, the world transformed.

Through their connection, he began to feel what she felt. The world softened. Colors muted. Sounds became distant. His own physical presence seemed to lessen, to fade. He felt the cold, hard focus of a predator, the absolute certainty of a master of her craft. He learned how she adjusted her breathing to match the ambient sounds of a sleeping house, how she used the natural shadows not just to hide, but as a medium to flow through. It was a masterclass in the physical art of stealth, delivered directly to his soul.

And as he learned from her, he could feel her learning from him. He felt the turbulent sea of her Ki begin to calm, influenced by the anchor of his peaceful memory. He felt her fledgling core, that tiny grey spark, pulse. And with each pulse, as she willed it into the shadows, it grew a fraction more stable, a fraction more... quiet.

They sat there for what could have been minutes or hours, two masters of their respective arts, their hands joined in the candlelight. One learning how to hide his body, the other learning how to quiet her soul. A perfect, silent, and deeply profound transaction.

The connection dissolved, not abruptly, but like a slow, gentle tide receding, leaving them both back on the familiar shores of their own consciousness. The scent of phantom herbs faded from Melissa’s mind. The feeling of being a ghost in a silent house receded from Ren’s.

Their eyes opened at the same instant.

They locked gazes across the short distance, the candle flame dancing in their pupils. The air was charged, humming with the residual energy of their shared experience. They had not just exchanged techniques; they had, for a moment, inhabited each other's reality. Melissa had felt the profound, centered peace that was Ren’s natural state. Ren had felt the cold, thrilling, and deeply lonely focus that was hers. The intimacy of the lesson was more profound than any physical touch, more revealing than any confession.

Melissa stared into Ren’s wide, dark eyes, her own cool grey pools filled with a startling, unreadable intensity. The professional mask was gone. The cynical humor was gone. All that was left was a raw, unfiltered honesty.

"Will you marry me?" she breathed, the words a soft, stunned exhalation.

The shock on Ren’s face was a masterpiece.

His eyes went wide, his jaw dropped, and every bit of his serene, mystical composure evaporated in a flash of pure, unadulterated panic. His silver Ki, usually a calm, steady river, erupted into a chaotic, sputtering geyser. For a single, glorious moment, the all-powerful, soul-reading healer was just a flustered young man who had been presented with a question so far outside his experience that his brain simply ceased to function.

And then the laughter erupted from Melissa.

It wasn't a snort or a chuckle. It was pure, unbridled, musical gold. A peal of joyous, unrestrained laughter that filled the small shop, chasing away the last of the shadows. She threw her head back and laughed until tears streamed down her face, a sound of such pure, cathartic delight that it seemed to physically cleanse the air.

She had seen his flustered, terrified face, and in that moment, she had finally, truly, broken through his wall, just as he had broken through hers. The prank had been returned. The scales were balanced.

"Oh, you should... you should see your face," she finally managed to gasp out, wiping tears from her eyes. "Apothecary, I've faced down charging Razorbacks with more composure than that."

Ren, his heart still trying to hammer its way out of his chest, could only stare. The panic slowly receded, replaced by a dawning, reluctant, and deeply amused understanding. He had been played. Expertly.

A slow grin spread across his face, a silent admission of her complete and utter victory. The tension of the lesson, the seriousness of their work, it all melted away, leaving behind the warm, solid foundation of a true and deeply strange partnership.

He had taught her how to be quiet.

And she had just taught him how to be speechless.

Ren watched her, the last of his own shock giving way to a deep, appreciative amusement. He had seen the cool professional, the bored assistant, the skeptical student, and now, this—a woman undone by a genuine, soul-cleansing laugh. Her cool grey Hue was currently sparkling with flecks of pure, silver-white mirth. It was beautiful.

He let her laughter wash over him, a reward in itself. He had chipped away at the fortress and found not a void, but this. It was more than he could have hoped for.

As her laughter finally subsided into a series of soft, breathless chuckles, she looked at him, her eyes shining, the professional mask completely gone. "I needed that," she admitted, her voice soft and unguarded. "It's been... a long time."

He simply nodded, a slow, understanding smile on his face. And in that quiet moment, as he looked at the open, vulnerable woman sitting before him, the mischievous part of his soul, the part that had prescribed a week of celibacy to a Factor, made a quiet, solemn vow.

*Oh, I'll get her back for that.* The thought was a warm, pleasant coal glowing in his mind. *Not today. Not tomorrow. Today was worth it. Today was a breakthrough for both of us.*

He looked at her, truly seeing the complex, capable, and now wonderfully human ally he had found.

*But revenge is a dish best served cold,* his inner voice mused, a perfect echo of his philosophy. *With a dash of honey.*

He would wait. Weeks, maybe months. He would wait for the perfect moment, the perfect opportunity to return the favor with a prank so subtle, so perfectly tailored to her, that she wouldn't see it coming until it was far, far too late. The thought was a delicious, comforting promise to himself.

He pushed himself to his feet and offered her a hand. "Alright, teacher," he said, his voice full of a new, easy warmth. "Lesson's over for tonight."

Melissa took his hand, her grip firm, and let him pull her to her feet. Their eyes met, and a new understanding passed between them. They were no longer just a healer and an assistant. They were partners. Co-conspirators. And the game had just begun.

"Let's clean up," Ren said, breaking the gaze. "We have a long day tomorrow. And who knows what kind of patient will walk through that door."

The next morning began like any other. Sunlight streamed through the shop's single window, illuminating dancing dust motes. Melissa was quietly restocking jars of dried feverfew, her movements now imbued with a new, focused grace. Ren was grinding herbs, the familiar rhythm a calming start to the day.

The bell over the door chimed, a sharp, clear announcement.

And as it did, Melissa simply... vanished.

One moment she was there, a solid, physical presence by the shelves. The next, she was gone. Ren blinked, his senses reaching out. She hadn't left the shop. He could still feel the quiet, steady hum of her Ki, but it was... muted. Shrouded. He looked closer, and finally, he saw her. She was standing in the exact same spot, perfectly still, but the light seemed to bend around her, people's eyes would just slide right past the space she occupied. She hadn't become invisible; she had, for a fleeting, masterful second, become *unimportant*. She was practicing. And she was a terrifyingly fast learner.

*I gotta get me some of that,* Ren thought with a surge of pure, professional admiration. He wasn't even close to that level of control yet.

His attention was pulled back to the customer. The man who entered wasn't what he expected. He was a well-dressed man of middle years, a clerk or a minor bureaucrat by the look of his ink-stained fingers and slightly stooped shoulders. Physically, he seemed perfectly fine. Ren's senses, which would normally detect the dissonant thrum of a sickness or an injury, found nothing. The man's body was a clear, harmonious chord.

But his soul was a different story.

The man's Hue was flickering wildly, like a candle flame in a hurricane. It shifted from a muddy, fearful brown to a hot, angry red, then to a watery, guilt-ridden blue, all in the space of a few seconds. It was a chaotic, unstable storm of pure, raw emotion. Inner turmoil.

This should be interesting.

"Master Apothecary?" the man asked, his voice tight and strained. He clutched a leather satchel to his chest as if it contained a venomous snake.

"I am," Ren said, his voice calm and welcoming. "How can I help you? You do not appear to be ill."

"I am not," the man confirmed, wringing his hands. "Not in body, at least. But I... I have a problem. A matter of... conscience." He glanced around the empty shop, not seeing the perfectly still woman by the shelves. "I was told you were a discreet man. A man who understands... unusual payments."

"I listen to all troubles," Ren said, gesturing to the stool. "And my prices are always fair. Please. Sit. Tell me what burdens you."

The man sank onto the stool, placing the leather satchel on the floor beside him with a heavy, significant thud. He leaned forward, his voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper.

"I am a scrivener for the Imperial Tax Office," he began. "And for the past year, I have been... complicit. My superior, Chief Auditor Thrax... he is a monster. He uses his position to bleed the merchants of the Dock Ward dry with fraudulent tariffs and invented fines. He is a cancer, growing fat on the lifeblood of this city."

The man's Hue flared with a hot, righteous anger. "I have the proof," he whispered, tapping the satchel with his foot. "Ledgers. Doctored manifests. All of it. Enough to see him imprisoned for a lifetime."

"Then you should take this to the City Watch," Ren said simply. "Or to the Magistrate."

The man let out a short, bitter, humorless laugh. "And be found dead in a canal the next morning? Thrax's roots run deep. He has friends in the Watch, cousins in the Magistrate's court. No one will touch him. To officially accuse him is suicide." His flickering Hue shifted to a fearful, muddy brown. "But to do nothing... to watch him ruin good people day after day... my conscience is screaming. I cannot sleep. I cannot eat. I am being torn apart from the inside."

He looked at Ren, his eyes filled with a desperate, pleading hope. "I don't need a cure for my body. I need a cure for my city. I cannot deliver the blow myself. But I can provide the weapon." He slid the satchel forward with his foot. "Take it. Use it. Expose him. You are the only one I can trust."

He was not a patient seeking healing. He was a petitioner, seeking a vigilante. He had come to the Apothecary, but he was asking for The Ghost.

Ren listened, his expression calm and neutral, but inside, a new and powerful dynamic was clicking into place. This was not a problem for a healer, nor was it a quest for an adventurer. This was a case. A perfect, clear-cut case for The Ghost. And for the first time, The Ghost would not be working alone.

The scrivener, whose name was Loric, finished his plea, his entire being a chaotic storm of fear and hope. He had laid his life, and the weapon that could end it, at Ren's feet.

Ren stood in silence for a long moment, appearing to weigh the man's story. In truth, he was in a silent, lightning-fast communion with his new partner. He didn't look at her, didn't make a single gesture, but he projected a simple, clear question across their nascent Ki-bond: *Your assessment?*

Melissa's reply was not in words, but in a pure, cold stream of analytical thought, a feeling of professional confirmation. *The man is terrified. His story is true—the Tone is clear. The target is high-risk, politically insulated. A direct approach is suicide, just as he says. The ledger is the only viable weapon. This is our kind of work.*

Ren's decision was made. But they needed a pretext. They needed to protect their source. Loric couldn't just hand over the satchel and walk away. If Thrax was as paranoid as Loric claimed, his scribes would be watched

"On second thought," Ren said, his voice shifting, taking on the grave, serious tone of a physician delivering a difficult diagnosis. He leaned forward and peered intently at Loric, as if noticing a subtle symptom for the first time. "You look quite ill."

Loric blinked, confused. "But... I told you, I'm not—"

"The inner turmoil has manifested in your physical self," Ren cut him off, his authority absolute. He placed the back of his hand on Loric's forehead. "You have a deep fever of the spirit. It's highly contagious. Left untreated, it could lead to... complications."

He straightened up, a plan forming with lightning speed. The best way to protect Loric wasn't to hide him. It was to make him the perfect, unimpeachable victim.

"You are ill," Ren stated, his voice a firm command. "Very ill. Here is my prescription."

He looked Loric directly in the eye, his gaze intense, willing the man to understand the hidden instructions. "First, you are to go back to your office. You will place that satchel and everything inside it back EXACTLY where you found it. No one can know it was ever gone. This is the most important step. Do you understand?"

Loric's eyes widened as he grasped the logic. He nodded shakily.

"Good," Ren continued. "Then, you will go home. You will tell your wife you feel a chill. You will rest. For three days." He turned to his shelves and began grabbing herbs with practiced speed, not for a cure, but for a potent, fast-acting purgative. "I will give you a poultice. It is to be applied to your chest. It will take effect in about two hours."

He began grinding the herbs, the pestle a sharp, urgent rhythm. He looked back at Loric, a grim, serious expression on his face. "After two hours," he said, his voice dropping, "you REALLY will be sick. A violent fever, sweating, nausea... the works. It will look dreadful, but it is harmless, a cleansing fire. It will be so convincing, your superiors will beg you to go home."

He wrapped the pungent, damp mixture in a cloth and handed it to Loric. "Remember my instructions. Once you are home, you do not leave. Don't even leave your house for any reason. For three days. After three days, the sickness will pass as quickly as it came, and you will be cured. In every sense of the word."

The plan was brilliant in its simplicity. Loric would not be a suspicious missing person; he would be a verifiable sick man, confined to his home with an alibi no one could question. During that time, The Ghost and his new Shadow would have a three-day window to act.
