# Chapter 6: A New Set of Variables

### []{#anchor}Chapter 6: A New Set of Variables

Nate took a bite of the waffle. It was perfect. Not gourmet, not fancy,
just a simple, honest-to-god waffle. He chased it with a sip of the
dark, slightly burnt diner coffee. The combination cut through the
lingering adrenaline from the night's events, grounding him in the here
and now.

"Tell me something, Margaret."

The diner had quieted down. The last of the late-night crowd had
shuffled out, leaving only Nate, a trucker nursing a thermos in a far
booth, and Margaret, who was wiping down the counter with a practiced,
circular motion.

She looked over at him, one eyebrow arched. "If you could get out of
this place, and do anything you wanted to do, anything at all. What
would you do?"

Margaret stopped wiping, planted the damp rag on the counter, and put a
hand on her hip. She didn't miss a beat. "You propositioning me, sweet
cheeks?" She eyed him up and down, a theatrical, assessing gaze. "I
guess I could do worse!" She let out a short, heartfelt laugh that
seemed to echo in the quiet room.

Nate just smiled, a genuine warmth spreading across his face. "I
wouldn't dream of it, Margaret. What would your husband say?"

*(Internal Calculation: Worn gold band on her left ring finger, slight
indentation beneath it. Faded photo of a man with a mustache tucked into
the frame of the register. Mentioned a* '*bowling night*' *she had to
miss. Probability of being married: 96.4%.)*

"He'd twist you into a pretzel!" She laughed again, the sound raspy and
real. Then, the joke faded, and she got a faraway look, her eyes focused
on something beyond the diner's window. The tough Vegas veneer dissolved
for just a second.

"To be honest," she said, her voice softening. "I think I'd move to a
small town and open a bookstore. A used bookstore. Someplace quiet,
where it smells like old paper. I mean, who doesn't love books?"

She lingered in the thought for a moment before shaking her head, as if
waking from a dream. The moment was over. She sauntered away with a
swing of her hips, looked back over her shoulder, and gave him a playful
wink. "You enjoy that breakfast, sugar. Holler if you need anything."

Nate watched her go, the scent of her simple, honest dream hanging in
the air.

Hmmm... a bookstore.

It was an absurd idea. A business with notoriously thin margins,
dependent on foot traffic and community. A quiet, analog life built on
dusty, unpredictable inventory. It was the antithesis of everything he
was: a high-stakes, data-driven system designed for maximum efficiency.

It was perfect.

Nate was back on his phone. His fingers, which had spent years tracking
cards, memorizing discard trays, and calculating odds down to the fourth
decimal place, now moved across the screen with a new purpose. He
ignored his stock portfolio. He closed the news feeds. He opened a
browser.

His search history began to fill with a new set of variables.

*Small town bookstores for sale* *Best states to open independent
bookstore* *Profitability analysis used book market* *Demographics for
successful retail bookstores*

He was no longer looking for an edge against the house. He was looking
for an exit from the game entirely. And in the quiet hum of a Vegas
diner at 5 a.m., surrounded by the remnants of his old life, Nathaniel
Weber began calculating the odds on a new one.
