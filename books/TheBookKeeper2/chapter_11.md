# Chapter 11: A New Chapter

### []{#anchor}Chapter 11: A New Chapter

For a man who was considered "dangerous" in the high-stakes world of Las
Vegas---a man whose mind could track six decks of cards, calculate
shifting probabilities in milliseconds, and read the lie in a man's
pulse---Nate was completely and utterly lost within the first five
minutes of trying to understand "The System."

Lizzie moved through the stacks with the effortless grace of a creature
in its natural habitat. "So, the main history section is here," she
explained, gesturing to a massive wall of books, "but the military
history is over by the window, because the old veterans like to sit in
the sun while they browse. The biographies are shelved alphabetically,
unless the person was a musician, in which case they're with the music
books. Unless they were a *classical* musician, then they're in the
classical section, which is near philosophy, because Alistair always
said Bach and Plato were having the same conversation."

She continued on, her explanations a whirlwind of intuitive leaps and
anecdotal logic. Nate's mind, an instrument honed to find the signal in
the noise, was being presented with a symphony where every instrument
was playing a different song in a different key. He could feel a
figurative blue screen of death error message flashing behind his eyes.

He held up a hand, a gesture of unconditional surrender. "Okay," he
said, a slow smile spreading across his face. "I surrender."

Lizzie stopped, looking at him with concern. "Is it too much?"

"It's a masterpiece," Nate replied, and he meant it. "It's a system so
complex and personal that only one person in the world can run it. And
that's the problem." He let out a short, genuine laugh. "I have no idea
what I'm doing. But you do."

He saw the tension in her shoulders ease. He wasn't criticizing; he was
admitting his own inadequacy.

"So, here's what we're going to do," he said, the strategic, analytical
part of his brain clicking back online, now focused on a new problem.
"You are going to run the store for the rest of the day, exactly as you
always have. I'm going to go to that electronics store I saw down the
street and purchase three laptops, a point-of-sale system, some
accounting software, QuickBooks, Microsoft Office 365, and an inventory
management system."

He began to pace a short, clear path near the front counter, the motion
of a mind at work.

"Phase two: We will spend the next week conducting a knowledge transfer,
in both directions. You will teach me the soul of this place---the
customers, the books, the logic of 'what feels right.' I will teach you
the technology that will handle the tedious parts, so you have more time
for the important ones. We'll bring this store into the 21st century,
from a digital perspective."

Lizzie watched him, her mouth slightly agape, as if he had just laid out
a detailed blueprint for building a starship.

"Phase three," he continued, "we will, together, come up with a way to
tame this organized chaos---to get it under control so we know what we
have and where it is---all while retaining the small-town charm that
makes this place worth visiting in the first place. Your system has a
heart; my systems will build the skeleton to support it."

He stopped pacing and met her eyes, his expression serious but
inclusive.

"Then, and only then, once our foundation is rock-solid, will we work on
increasing foot traffic. Marketing is the last step, not the first." He
took a breath. "How does that sound for a plan?"

She stared at him for a long moment, processing the sheer velocity and
scope of his proposal. It was a hurricane of logic and efficiency aimed
directly at her charming, dusty, analog world. But it wasn't a hostile
takeover. It was an offer of partnership. He hadn't said "I will do
this"; he had said "we will do this."

A slow smile spread across Lizzie's face, mirroring his own.

"A new chapter," she said, the words fitting the moment perfectly.
"Okay, Nate. I think I like that plan very much."
