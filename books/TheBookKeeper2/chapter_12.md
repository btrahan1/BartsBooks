# Chapter 12: The Locked Room

### []{#anchor}Chapter 12: The Locked Room

The next morning, Nate arrived feeling a surge of purpose he hadn't
experienced since he first identified a biased roulette wheel at the
Bellagio. He carried in the last of the boxes, stacking them near the
front counter. The sleek, branded cardboard of Dell, Square, and Netgear
formed a modern, geometric fortress in the heart of the dusty, literary
labyrinth.

Lizzie was already there, a mug of coffee in hand, looking at the pile
with a mixture of awe and trepidation. "Wow," she said. "Christmas came
early."

"Phase one is complete," Nate announced, rubbing his hands together.
"Now, phase two. Knowledge transfer. We need a workspace. A command
center."

He looked around. And for the first time, the charming clutter of the
store ceased to be charming. It became an obstacle. Every flat surface
was occupied. The counter was covered in new arrivals and special
orders. The single small table was buried under a pile of art history
tomes. Even the chairs had books stacked on them.

The frustration began as a low hum of static in his brain. He had the
components for a perfect system, but nowhere to assemble them. He picked
up a box containing the main Point-of-Sale terminal. It was heavy. He
turned in a full circle, looking for a place, any place, to set it down
and unbox it. There was none.

The static in his head grew louder. This wasn't a complex problem of
probability. This was a simple, infuriating problem of logistics. It was
a Tetris game where all the blocks were the wrong shape.

"Arg!" The sound burst out of him, louder than he intended. Lizzie
jumped slightly. "Where do I put all of this stuff! We need more space!"

He set the box down with a thud, the placid control he prided himself on
completely gone. He ran a hand through his hair, the sheer, illogical
chaos of the physical world grating on his nerves. In Vegas, space was a
calculated asset, every square foot designed for maximum traffic flow
and revenue. This place was designed by a squirrel. A well-read,
sentimental squirrel.

He saw the look of concern on Lizzie's face and immediately reined
himself in. He took a deep, centering breath, forcing the tension from
his shoulders. *This isn't a losing table,* he told himself. *It's a new
puzzle. Solve the puzzle.*

He relaxed and a smile replaced the frustrated scowl. "Okay," he said,
his voice returning to its normal, calm register. "My apologies. System
error." He looked at her, seeing her not just as an employee, but as his
partner in this strange venture. "Lizzie... how do you feel about
expanding? Maybe, add a space for an office?"

Lizzie blinked, then gestured vaguely at the book-lined walls that
pressed in on them from all sides. "Expand where? Alistair owned the
building, but the maritime store isn't moving, and I don't think
Mrs. Higgins is giving up her bakery."

Nate's eyes scanned the layout, his mind shifting from frustrated user
to strategic analyst. He pointed toward the back wall, half-hidden
behind a towering, rotating rack of paperback mysteries. "What's back
there? Behind that shelf?"

"Oh," Lizzie said, her expression changing. "That's just the old
stockroom door."

"Is it a big room?"

"It's huge," she said quietly. "It runs the whole width of the store. It
even has a window that looks out on the alley."

Nate's eyes lit up. "That's it! That's our command center. It's
perfect." He started towards it, ready to move the shelf.

"We can't," Lizzie said, stopping him.

"Why not? Is it full of books?"

"No," she said, looking at the door with a strange reverence. "It's
empty. That was Alistair's... well, it was his wife's office, years ago.
Eleanor. She passed away almost ten years back. It was her favorite
place in the world. The day after the funeral, he locked the door, and
no one's been inside since."

The information landed, changing the entire equation. This wasn't just a
stockroom. It was a shrine.

"He never gave you a key?" Nate asked, his voice softer.

"He kept it on his personal keyring," Lizzie said. "He called it
'Eleanor's Room.' It just... wasn't part of the store anymore."

Nate walked back to the front counter. He picked up the single, ornate
iron key Alistair had given him. It was the key to the front door. He
looked at it closely, then walked back to the stockroom door. Below the
modern handle was an old, iron keyhole. He slid the key in.

It turned with a well-oiled, satisfying *click*.
