# Chapter 13: Eleanor's Room

### []{#anchor}Chapter 13: Eleanor's Room

The lock, silent for a decade, turned with a decisive click that seemed
to echo in the quiet store. Nate pushed the door inward. It swung open
on silent, well-oiled hinges, revealing not a dusty, forgotten
stockroom, but a space preserved in time.

"Oh, wow," Lizzie whispered from behind him.

It definitely wasn't a stockroom. It was a memory.

The room was indeed large, running the width of the building. A large
window on the back wall let in the soft, grey Oregon light, illuminating
dust motes dancing in the air. But the room wasn't empty. It was an
office, a study, a sanctuary, left exactly as its occupant had last seen
it.

A beautiful, dark wood desk sat facing the window. On it, a delicate
teacup rested in its saucer, a fountain pen was laid neatly across an
open but empty journal, and a pair of reading glasses sat folded, as if
waiting for their owner to return. The chair was pushed in, and a soft,
cashmere shawl was draped over the back.

Bookshelves lined one wall, but these were different from the chaotic
stacks outside. These were curated, organized, and filled with
well-loved hardcovers---Virginia Woolf, Jane Austen, the Brontë sisters.
A comfortable armchair sat in the corner with a small table beside it.
It was a space of profound peace, a quiet harbor in the middle of the
store's stormy sea of books.

Nate and Lizzie stood on the threshold, feeling like intruders in a
sacred space. He could see it now. Alistair hadn't just locked a door;
he had sealed a part of his life, preserving the memory of his wife,
Eleanor, in this amber of dust and silence.

Nate's analytical mind, which had moments before been buzzing with plans
for network cables and server racks, went completely quiet. He saw the
room not as a solution to his logistical problem, but as the heart of
Alistair's entire world.

This changed everything.

He gently pushed the door closed, the latch clicking softly back into
place. He turned to Lizzie, whose eyes were misty.

"We can't use this room," he said, his voice low and certain.

"But... the space," she said, her practical side warring with her
sentiment. "We need it."

"I know," Nate agreed. "But not like this."

He felt a deep sense of compassion for the old man who had sold him a
business but kept its most precious room a secret. Alistair hadn't been
deceptive; he'd probably just been unable to speak of it, to put this
part of his life onto a contract. It was too personal, too painful.

Nate knew what he had to do. He pulled out his phone, not to look up
data, but to find a contact.

"You said you have his number," Nate said to Lizzie. "I need to go talk
to him."

"About the room?" she asked.

"No," Nate replied, locking the front door and turning the 'Open' sign
to 'Closed'. "I'm going to ask his permission to give his wife's room
back to him."

It was the most illogical business decision he had ever made. He was
about to offer to reduce the usable square footage of his own property,
to give back an asset he legally owned.

But it was the only move on the board that felt right. It was
technically Nate's building now, but this room would always be
Eleanor's. He had to be compassionate. He had to honor the system that
existed long before he arrived.
