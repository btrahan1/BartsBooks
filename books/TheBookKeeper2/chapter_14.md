# Chapter 14: The Transfer of Trust

### []{#anchor}Chapter 14: The Transfer of Trust

The address Lizzie gave him was for a small, well-kept cottage a few
blocks from the ocean, with a garden full of late-blooming roses that
fought valiantly against the coastal mist. When Nate knocked, Alistair
answered the door himself, a look of mild surprise on his face that
quickly shifted to concern.

"Nate! Is everything all right at the shop?"

"Everything is fine, Alistair," Nate said. "I apologize for showing up
unannounced, but I need to talk to you about something. Something I
found."

Nate didn't have to say another word. He saw the flicker of
understanding---and a deep, old sadness---in Alistair's eyes. The old
man knew exactly what he was talking about.

He led Nate back to The Printed Page. The journey was silent, the
unspoken subject of their trip hanging between them. When they arrived,
Nate unlocked the door and led Alistair through the familiar canyons of
books to the back of the store, to the door that had been a silent
monument for a decade.

Nate pushed it open and stepped aside, letting Alistair enter the room
first.

The old man walked into the center of the room and simply stood there,
turning in a slow circle, taking it all in. He ran a hand along the back
of the cashmere shawl on the chair. He picked up the folded reading
glasses from the desk, his thumb stroking the worn plastic of the
frames. He was a man visiting a ghost.

Nate remained by the doorway, a silent observer. "Lizzie told me this
was your wife's room," he said softly. "When I came to see you, it was
to ask for your permission to empty it. To give you back her things. The
room is yours, Alistair. I have no claim to it."

Alistair just stared at Nate. His gaze was intense, analytical, as if he
were seeing Nate for the first time. He didn't say anything for a long
moment. Then he slowly walked over and sat down at his wife's old desk,
in her chair. He rested his hands on the polished wood, and then stared
at Nate again.

"I misjudged you, Nate," he said, his voice thick with emotion.

Nate remained silent, letting him speak.

"I knew you would eventually find your way in here," Alistair continued,
his gaze drifting around the room. "The key fits, after all. I expected,
at best, a delivery. A few boxes showing up on my doorstep with a note
attached. 'Thought you might want these.' That's what I expected, Nate.
That's the way of the world now, isn't it? Efficient. Impersonal."

He paused, and to Nate's astonishment, he reached up and wiped a single,
slow-moving tear from his cheek. "That's what I expected."

He took a shaky breath, composing himself. "But listen, Nate. This
room... it's not doing me, or you, any good. It's a museum. A lovely,
sad, dusty museum. And my Eleanor... my wife hated museums. She said
they were where beautiful things went to stop living."

His eyes found Nate's again, and they were clear now, filled with a new
resolve.

"What my wife would want is for this place to survive. For *her* place
to thrive. And to be honest, I was doing a piss-poor job of that. I was
letting it all slowly fade away with me." He looked around the room, not
with sadness, but with a kind of pained self-awareness. "That's probably
why I haven't been in here in years. It was easier to lock the door than
to admit I was failing her memory."

He pushed himself up from the desk, his back a little straighter than it
was a moment before.

"So, Nate," he said, his voice firm. "If you would just give me an hour
or two... alone... to say a proper goodbye, we can start packing up in
here." He offered a small, watery smile. "Does that sound reasonable?"

"It sounds more than reasonable, Alistair," Nate replied, his own voice
softer than he'd ever heard it.

He had come here to solve a problem of space. He had ended up closing a
transaction that had nothing to do with money and everything to do with
trust. The old man wasn't just giving him a room. He was handing over
the heart of the store.
