# Chapter 15: The Memento

### []{#anchor}Chapter 15: The Memento

Nate stood at the threshold, ready to give the old man his privacy. He
was about to pull the door closed when a thought, uncalculated and
purely instinctive, stopped him.

"Just one thing, Alistair..."

The old man turned, his hand already reaching for a framed photograph on
the bookshelf. "Yes?"

Nate hesitated for a fraction of a second, feeling a strange sense of
stepping onto sacred ground. "Would you mind leaving one memento here? I
actually like this desk. A lot. It's... solid. It has character." He
looked at the beautiful, dark wood desk, a piece of furniture that felt
like it had grown from the floorboards of the store itself. "Do you
think she would mind if we left it in here?"

He took another small step, emboldened by the look of surprise on
Alistair's face. "And if I may be so bold," Nate added, his gaze falling
upon a small, elegant brass plaque on the edge of the desk, its letters
worn smooth with time. "Her nameplate?"

The plaque simply read: *Eleanor Abernathy*.

Alistair stared at the desk, and then at the nameplate, as if seeing
them through Nate's eyes for the first time. He hadn't just asked to
keep furniture; he had asked to keep her *name*. He was offering to make
Eleanor a permanent part of the store's future, not just its past. He
wasn't just clearing a room; he was building a new office *around* her
memory.

The old man's shoulders, which had been stooped with the weight of his
grief, seemed to straighten entirely. A slow, genuine smile spread
across his face, wiping away the last of the tears. It was a smile of
relief, of gratitude, of profound understanding.

"My boy," Alistair said, his voice filled with a warmth that seemed to
fill the entire room. "She would have loved that. She absolutely would
have loved that."

He walked over and placed a hand on Nate's shoulder. It was a gesture
not just of friendship, but of blessing.

"The desk stays," Alistair declared. "And the nameplate. Let this always
be Eleanor's room, no matter what work you do in it."

Nate nodded, a silent agreement passing between them. He felt a sense of
rightness, a click of puzzle pieces falling into place that was more
satisfying than hitting a 21 on a double-down bet.

"Thank you, Alistair," he said simply.

"No, Nate," the old man replied, squeezing his shoulder before letting
go. "Thank you."

Nate stepped back out into the main part of the store and gently pulled
the door shut, leaving Alistair alone with his memories for the last
time. He walked back to the front counter, where Lizzie was pretending
to organize a shelf but had clearly been watching the entire exchange.

She looked at him, her eyes wide with a respect that went beyond that of
an employee for a boss. "That," she said quietly, "was the kindest thing
I have ever seen anyone do."

Nate just shrugged, feeling an unfamiliar heat rise in his cheeks. "It
was the correct play," he said, defaulting to the language he knew best.

But for the first time, he knew the 'correct play' had nothing to do
with the odds, and everything to do with the heart.
