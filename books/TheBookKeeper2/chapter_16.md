# Chapter 16: Command and Control

### []{#anchor}Chapter 16: Command and Control

The next day, the room was different.

After Alistair had left, his face etched with a peaceful sadness, he and
Lizzie had entered the room together. The desk and nameplate remained,
but the rest of Eleanor's personal effects were gone. The space no
longer felt like a shrine. It felt empty, but in a way that was full of
possibilities. It was a blank page.

They spent the entire afternoon cleaning. Dust cloths and wood polish
replaced probability charts and risk analysis as Nate's primary tools.
They worked in a comfortable silence, punctuated by Lizzie's stories
about Alistair and Eleanor and the history of the store. Nate listened,
absorbing the data---the qualitative, human data that you couldn't put
on a spreadsheet. By the time they finished, the room gleamed, the scent
of lemon oil mingling with the old-book smell of the shop.

Now, Nate was in his element.

The boxes he had bought were open, their contents spread across the
floor in neat, organized arrays. This was a system he understood. Wires,
motherboards, RAM sticks, power supplies---it was a world of pure,
predictable logic.

He started with the networking. He ran CAT6 ethernet cables along the
baseboards with the precision of a surgeon, hiding them from view. He
set up a secure Wi-Fi mesh system that would cover every nook and cranny
of the old building. He assembled a small, powerful server, loading it
with redundant hard drives for data security.

Lizzie watched for a while, her expression one of utter bewilderment. "I
thought we were just getting a new cash register," she said, looking at
the complex web of wires and components taking shape.

"The register is the frontend," Nate explained, not looking up from the
delicate task of plugging in a network switch. "This is the backend.
This is the brain. It will run the inventory, the sales data, the
accounting, the website... everything."

Lizzie just shook her head and smiled. "Okay, Mr. Robot. I'm going to go
sell some actual books."

The day flew by in a blur of configuration files and software
installations. Customers came and went, the bell over the door dinging
its cheerful tune, but Nate was in the zone. This was his version of a
deep-count blackjack shoe---a complex, multi-variable system that he was
molding into a perfect, efficient machine.

At six o'clock, Lizzie poked her head into the new office. "Quitting
time," she announced. "Are you coming?"

Nate was on the floor, surrounded by a snake's nest of power cords and
ethernet cables, meticulously bundling them with zip ties. He looked up,
a smudge of dust on his nose. "I'm almost done with the main server
rack," he said, his voice slightly muffled. "You go ahead. I'll lock
up."

"Alright," she said. "Don't forget to eat something that isn't made of
silicon."

"No promises," he mumbled, already turning his attention back to the
task.

The bell dinged her departure, and the store fell silent except for the
quiet hum of cooling fans and the click-clack of his keyboard. Hours
passed. The soft grey light from the window faded to black. Nate worked
on, fueled by coffee from a thermos and the deep, satisfying pleasure of
building something from scratch.

Finally, he pushed himself up and surveyed his work.

Against the far wall stood a small, professional server rack, its blue
and green indicator lights blinking in a steady, hypnotic rhythm. Three
new laptops were set up on Eleanor's desk, their screens glowing. The
Point-of-Sale system was assembled and ready for installation at the
front counter. The entire network was firewalled, encrypted, and backed
up to a cloud server.

He leaned against the doorframe, a wave of exhaustion and satisfaction
washing over him. He looked at the powerful, state-of-the-art nerve
center he had just built.

"This may have been overkill," he thought, a wry smile touching his
lips. He'd designed a system with more processing power and data
security than most small businesses would ever need.

"I could run a small country from in here."

And in a way, he thought, that was exactly what he was doing. This was
his country now. Population: two. And he had just given it the most
secure border in the world.
