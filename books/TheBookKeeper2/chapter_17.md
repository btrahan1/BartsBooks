# Chapter 17: The Great Un-Shelving

### []{#anchor}Chapter 17: The Great Un-Shelving

The Command Center was operational. The network was humming. For Nate,
the easy part was over. Now came the real challenge: the inventory. The
tens of thousands of chaotic, unpredictable, analog variables that lined
the walls.

He walked out of the office, holding a sleek, black barcode scanner like
a talisman, his eyes gleaming with the manic energy of a general
unveiling his battle plan.

"Now! The books!" he declared, his voice full of a triumphant energy
Lizzie hadn't heard before. "We need to catalog every single book, and I
have an idea!"

Lizzie, who was precariously perched on a rolling ladder trying to
reshelve a heavy art book, looked down at him in disbelief. The book
paused halfway to the shelf. "Every. Single. Book?" she repeated,
emphasizing each word as if it were a foreign concept. "Nate, there
are... I don't know... thirty, forty thousand books in here."

"How are we going to do that?" she asked, her voice a mixture of genuine
curiosity and profound weariness at the mere thought of it.

Nate smiled, the confident, assured smile of a man holding a royal
flush. He held up the barcode reader. "With this. Every book published
in the last forty years, with the exception of some of the antique ones,
has an ISBN."

Lizzie tilted her head, confused. "An IBM what?"

"No!" Nate chuckled. "An I-S-B-N. International Standard Book Number.
It's like a social security number for books. It identifies the title,
the author, the publisher, the edition... probably a bunch of other
stuff. The scanner reads the code, and the software I bought will
auto-populate almost all the data for us. It's brilliant."

He started pacing again, the plan solidifying as he spoke. "Here's how
it works. Phase one: The Great Un-Shelving. We take down each and every
book, section by section. We'll put them in boxes and stack them
temporarily in Eleanor's Office. As we box them, we'll dust each one.
See?"

Lizzie didn't miss it. She didn't miss how naturally, how respectfully,
he referred to the office as 'Eleanor's'. It wasn't a performance for
Alistair; it was just what he called it now. A small warmth spread
through her chest.

"Phase two," Nate continued, oblivious to her internal reaction. "The
Great Cleaning. With the shelves empty, we clean this place from top to
bottom. Every shelf, every corner, every floorboard. We start with a
clean slate."

"And phase three: The Re-Shelving. One by one, we'll scan each book,
give it a category, a price if it doesn't have one, and put it back into
place in a system that someone as book-ignorant as me can understand!"

Lizzie stared at him from her perch on the ladder, her mind reeling. She
pictured the weeks of work. The dust. The sheer, back-breaking labor of
moving forty thousand books. Twice. It was a Herculean task, a project
of monumental, borderline insane, proportions.

And it was also the most brilliant, logical idea she had ever heard. It
would fix everything.

She slowly descended the ladder, her expression shifting from disbelief
to shrewd calculation. She crossed her arms, looking him squarely in the
eye.

"Double pay for overtime," Lizzie asked, her voice perfectly even.

Nate stopped dead, his grand strategic monologue hitting a brick wall.
His brain, which had been operating on the level of system architecture
and logistical flow, was suddenly forced to confront a variable he had
completely overlooked: human resources. He looked at her, truly looked
at her---at the determination in her eyes, the set of her jaw. She
wasn't an employee anymore. She was a partner demanding her stake in the
venture.

The shock on his face lasted for a full two seconds before it dissolved
into a wide, appreciative grin. He had just been outplayed, and he loved
it.

"Absolutely!" he said, with a laugh. "And a pizza budget. A very, very
large pizza budget."
