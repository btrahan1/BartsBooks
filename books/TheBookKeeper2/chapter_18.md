# Chapter 18: The General Contractor

### []{#anchor}Chapter 18: The General Contractor

Lizzie's grin widened, the negotiation won. She was about to agree when
Nate held up a finger, a new thought sparking in his eyes.

"And..." he said, a new dimension of the plan clicking into place. "Do
you have any younger siblings? Or know of some kids that could use some
spending money?"

Lizzie's eyebrows shot up, surprised by the question. "I have a
seventeen-year-old brother, Josh," she said. "And yeah, half the high
school football team is always looking for work."

Nate smiled, the picture now complete in his mind. "Good. I need your
brains, Lizzie, not your back. Your value is in knowing the books,
knowing the customers, and making sure my logical system doesn't
accidentally sterilize the soul of this place. The heavy lifting? That's
just labor. It's a problem that can be solved with money and manpower."

He leaned against the counter, shifting from 'project manager' to
'general contractor'.

"So, here's your new job, effective immediately. You're the foreman. I
want you to hire a team. Three or four strong kids. You'll supervise
them. You'll direct them which sections to box up, how to label them,
and how to do it without damaging a single book. I'll handle the
payroll. You handle the people." He gave her a pointed look. "I need you
to take care of that."

The unspoken message was clear: *I am good with systems, but I am not
good with people. You are.*

It was a stunning admission of his own limitations and a profound vote
of confidence in her abilities. He wasn't just giving her a raise; he
was giving her a promotion. He was making her an essential,
irreplaceable part of the command structure.

Lizzie was floored. In a single day, she had gone from a quiet bookstore
clerk to a project foreman with a budget and a hiring mandate. Nate
wasn't just bringing the store into the 21st century; he was
fundamentally changing her role in it, seeing a potential in her that
she hadn't fully recognized in herself.

"Okay," she said, a new sense of authority settling into her voice.
"Okay, I can do that. Josh and his friends can start tomorrow after
school. But they're seventeen. They're going to eat all of that pizza
budget in the first two days."

"Then we'll double the pizza budget," Nate said without hesitation. He
turned and headed back toward Eleanor's office, his mind already moving
on to the next problem. "I'll go print out some employment forms!"

Lizzie stood there for a moment, a whirlwind of thoughts in her mind.
Forty thousand books. A team of teenage boys. A massive, insane,
brilliant project. And a new boss who was unlike anyone she had ever
met.

She pulled out her phone and started typing a text to her brother.

Hey, you and the guys busy for the next few weeks? I've got a job for
you. And pizza.

The Great Un-Shelving had officially begun.
