# Chapter 23: The Bridge

### []{#anchor}Chapter 23: The Bridge

The line for "The Librarian" was now three people deep. An elderly woman
was carefully typing in a request for "a cozy mystery, but not too gory,
set in England," while two teenagers behind her bounced on their heels,
waiting for their turn.

Lizzie came to stand beside Nate again, her arms crossed as she watched
the scene unfold, a look of sheer wonder on her face.

"We're going to need more iPads," she whispered to him, her voice a mix
of awe and amusement.

Nate smiled, his eyes still on the customers interacting with his
creation. "I have two more in the office, just in case."

"I've never seen anything like it," Lizzie continued, shaking her head
in disbelief. "They absolutely LOVE 'The Librarian.' Old Mrs. Gable just
found a book she's been trying to remember the name of for ten years.
That kid with the skateboard is in the fantasy aisle, completely
engrossed. I heard him tell his friend it was like a 'cheat code for
books'."

She turned to him, her expression serious and profoundly impressed. "You
haven't just created a cool gadget, Nate. You've created a bridge. A
bridge between the young and the old, between the tech-savvy kids who
live on their phones and the old fisherman I was just talking to. He's
terrified of computers, but he just asked me if I could help him use
'the book machine.' This is absolutely remarkable."

Just then, a voice piped up from the kids in line. "John, hurry up, I
want to try it!"

Lizzie let out a small, happy laugh. "You've made the bookstore a
destination again," she said. "Not just for the people who already love
books, but for people who didn't even know they were looking for one."

Nate watched as the elderly woman beamed at the iPad's suggestion, wrote
down the aisle number on a slip of paper, and headed off with a new
sense of purpose. He felt a different kind of buzz than he ever had at a
casino. It wasn't the sharp, electric thrill of a win, but a deep,
resonant hum of satisfaction.

In Vegas, his skill was a weapon used to extract value from a closed
system. Here, his skill was a tool used to create value in an open one.
He hadn't just built a better system for a bookstore. He had built a
better way for people to connect with stories.

And that, he realized, was a jackpot bigger than anything he could have
ever imagined.
