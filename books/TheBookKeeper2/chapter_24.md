# Chapter 24: The New Baseline

### []{#anchor}Chapter 24: The New Baseline

The morning after the grand re-opening, the store was quiet. A "Closed
for Recovery" sign hung on the front door. The scent of coffee and the
low hum of the server in Eleanor's office had replaced the buzz of the
crowd.

Lizzie walked in, looking exhausted but happy, carrying a box of donuts.
"Morning," she yawned. "I think my feet are permanently shaped like the
store floor now."

"Morning," Nate replied from the office. He was already at the desk,
Eleanor's desk, staring intently at one of the monitors, a half-empty
coffee mug beside him. The thrill of victory, for him, was always in the
post-game analysis.

"Don't tell me you're working already," Lizzie said, setting the box
down. "We just survived a literary hurricane yesterday."

"I'm not working," Nate said, a strange tone in his voice. "I'm looking
at the numbers." He swiveled in his chair and gestured for her to come
over. "You need to see this."

Lizzie peered over his shoulder at the screen. It was a clean,
professional-looking sales report from the new POS software. It was
filled with graphs, charts, and columns of figures. At the very top, in
a large, bold font, was the final tally for the previous day's sales.

Lizzie squinted, then her eyes went wide. She leaned closer, her nose
almost touching the screen, as if the proximity would change the number.

"Are these numbers right, Nate?" she whispered, her voice full of
disbelief. "\$7,352 in a single day? That can't be right!"

"It's right," Nate said calmly. "Down to the penny."

"No, it can't be," she insisted, shaking her head. "Alistair's best
Christmas Eve, the one he talked about for years, was maybe two thousand
dollars. On a normal Saturday, we were lucky to clear five hundred. We
did almost four times his best day ever... in about five hours."

"It's not just the total," Nate said, his excitement finally showing as
he started tapping the screen, pointing out different metrics. "Look at
the breakdown. The average transaction value is up 40%. Why? Because
people aren't just buying one book they came in for. 'The Librarian' is
upselling them. It's showing them two or three books they didn't even
know they wanted."

He switched to another screen. "And look at this. Unique transactions:
284. Our old average was around 30. We didn't just sell more books to
the same people. We brought in a whole new demographic. I
cross-referenced the credit card data with our old sales logs. Over 70%
of yesterday's sales were to new customers."

Lizzie sank into the spare chair, staring at the numbers glowing on the
screen like a jackpot she couldn't comprehend. She had felt the success
of the day before---the energy, the happiness. But this was different.
This was the cold, hard proof.

"The sign... the party... The Librarian..." she murmured, connecting the
dots. "It all... worked."

"The system worked," Nate corrected gently. He looked from the screen to
her, his expression serious. "This isn't a fluke, Lizzie. This isn't a
one-time party spike. We introduced a new set of variables, and this is
the result."

He leaned back in his chair, a profound sense of satisfaction settling
over him. It was the same feeling he'd had when he first proved he could
control the dice, but magnified a thousand times.

"This," he said, tapping the glowing number on the screen, "is our new
baseline."
