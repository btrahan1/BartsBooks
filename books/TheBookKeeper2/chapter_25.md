# Chapter 25: A Fair Cut

### []{#anchor}Chapter 25: A Fair Cut

Lizzie was still trying to process the sheer scale of their success, her
mind reeling from the impossible number on the screen. It felt like a
dream, a lottery win. The idea that this could be a "new baseline" was
too much to comprehend.

"And this, Lizzie, is for you."

Nate swiveled in his chair, picked up a business envelope from the
corner of the desk, and handed it to her.

She looked at it, then back at him, raising her eyebrows in a silent
question. "What's this? My double overtime pay?" she joked, the numbers
still making her feel giddy.

"Something like that," Nate said with a small smile. "Open it."

She slid her finger under the flap and pulled out a neatly folded check.
As she unfolded it, her eyes scanned the numbers written in Nate's
precise, clean handwriting. Her jaw dropped.

"\$1,000!" she squealed, the word a burst of pure, unadulterated shock.
Her head snapped up to look at him, her eyes wide as saucers. "Nate! I
can't possibly take this!"

"Yes, you can," he said, his voice calm and firm, cutting off any
further protest. "It's not a gift, Lizzie. It's a bonus. It's your cut."

"My... cut?" The word was foreign to her. In her years of working for
Alistair, she got a fair wage and a Christmas card. The concept of a
"cut" was from another world.

"Look," Nate said, leaning forward and resting his elbows on the desk,
his expression serious. "In my old world, when a team has a successful
run, the person running the game makes sure the crew gets a piece of the
action. It's smart business. You keep your best people happy."

He paused, and his tone softened, losing the detached, analytical edge.
"But it's more than that. This wouldn't have happened without you. I can
build a system, but you're the one who knows the inventory, who knows
the people, who kept the heart of this place beating. You were the
foreman, the project manager, and the lead brand ambassador yesterday.
That bonus," he gestured to the check in her hand, "is a low-ball
estimate of the value you brought to the table. It's the correct play."

Lizzie stared at the check, then back at Nate. He wasn't just being
generous. He was acknowledging her contribution in the only language he
truly understood: numbers. He had quantified her value and was paying it
out, no different than a winning hand. It was the most strangely logical
and deeply touching gesture she had ever experienced.

Tears welled in her eyes, blurring the ink on the check. "Thank you,
Nate," she whispered, her voice thick with emotion.

"You earned it," he said simply. He then turned back to his monitor,
giving her a moment to compose herself. "Now," he said, his voice
returning to a more business-like tone. "Let's figure out what we're
going to do with the other six thousand dollars."
