# Chapter 28: Epilogue: The Final Calculation

### []{#anchor}Epilogue: The Final Calculation

Five months later, the bell on the door of *Eleanor's: The Printed Page*
chimed its final note of the day. Lizzie flipped the sign to 'Closed'
and leaned against the door, a contented smile on her face. The store
was a warm, humming hive of quiet success. She was in the middle of a
lively discussion with two of her regulars about the merits of a new
mystery novel.

The bookstore, despite all odds, was an amazing success. After the
festival, a steady stream of new customers became a loyal flood. The
Librarians---now a set of three iPads strategically placed throughout
the store---were a local attraction. They were pulling in a consistent
monthly profit of around \$7,000. Lizzie had done the math in her head a
dozen times, still not quite believing it. That times twelve was
eighty-four grand a year. Not bad for a shop that was on the verge of
bankruptcy just six months ago.

After her patrons had left, she walked back toward the office. The
lights were on, as they always were. Nate was sitting at Eleanor's desk,
not working, just looking at a large manila envelope that sat in the
center of the polished wood.

"Whacha got there, Nate?" she asked cheerfully, beginning her closing
routine of tidying the counter.

His face was calm, but his eyes held an unfamiliar intensity. "Lizzie,
sit down."

His tone stopped her cold. It wasn't angry, but it was serious. Final.
She looked worried, her mind immediately jumping to the worst possible
conclusions. "Everything okay?"

He didn't answer. He simply slid the thick envelope across the desk
toward her.

Her hands trembled slightly as she picked it up. It wasn't a check. It
was a heavy stack of documents, bound by a law firm. The top page was a
contract. Her eyes scanned the dense legal text, but a few words jumped
out in stark, unbelievable clarity: "*...hereby transfers full ownership
of the business known as* '*Eleanor's: The Printed Page*'*, and the
property at 114 Main Street...*"

"What?" The word was a choked whisper. She looked up at him, her face a
mask of confusion. "WHAT?"

He remained calm, his gaze steady. "Lizzie, I've already deducted every
single expense from the day I arrived. The electronics, the sign, the
festival sponsorship, the payroll, the initial purchase price---every
single penny I put into this store. Not a cent more."

He gestured to the laptop. "Everything else is profit, and it's in the
store's bank account. As of my signature on that document, all of this
is yours."

Lizzie was speechless for what felt like a full three minutes. The only
sound in the room was the quiet hum of the server. Her mind was a
complete blank, a system crash more profound than any Nate had ever
experienced. She couldn't form a thought, let alone a sentence. Finally,
her brain rebooted, and she found her voice.

"But... but... WHY?"

Nate leaned back in his chair, a slow, thoughtful smile touching his
lips. It was the question he had been waiting for. The final variable.

"In Vegas," he began, his voice quiet and reflective, "the money was
never the point. It was just how you kept score. The point was the game.
The challenge. Finding the flaw in the system, solving the puzzle, and
beating the house. Once I'd solved a game, I moved on."

He looked around the beautiful, thriving bookstore. "This place... this
was the most complex, wonderful game I've ever played. The puzzle wasn't
just about numbers; it was about people, and history, and heart. The
question was, 'Could we take this beautiful, broken thing and make it
not just survive, but thrive?'"

He met her eyes, his own shining with a quiet, profound pride. "We won,
Lizzie. The system is built. The engine is running perfectly. It doesn't
need its architect anymore. It needs its keeper."

He stood up, signaling the end of his tenure at the desk. "I'm not a
shopkeeper. I'm a systems architect. You... you're the soul of this
place. This was always meant to be yours. I just built the foundation
for you to build on."

Lizzie could only stare, the contract feeling impossibly heavy in her
hands. "So... what are you going to do?"

Nate walked to the window, looking out at the quiet Main Street of the
town he now called home. "I hear the old fishing co-op is having trouble
with their distribution logistics," he said, a familiar, analytical
glint in his eye. "And the community theater can't figure out how to
sell tickets online."

He turned back to her, a full, genuine grin on his face.

"I think I'm going to find a new game."
