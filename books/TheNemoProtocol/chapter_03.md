# Chapter 3: The Hexagon

### []{#anchor}Chapter Three: The Hexagon

The connection was secure---quantum-encrypted, bounced off three defunct
weather satellites, and routed through a hardened server farm beneath
the Swiss Alps. It was the most secure conversation in human history,
and it sounded like a bar fight.

"It is a Russian airframe!" The British Prime Minister shouted, his
voice cracking with a distinct lack of stiff upper lip. "We have the
radar signatures from the RAF station in Cyprus. The telemetry matches
the Zircon profile perfectly!"

"Do not lecture me on my own hardware!" The President of Russia roared
back. The video feed from his bunker in the Urals was grainy, but the
vein pulsing in his forehead was high-definition. "I have no submarine
in that sector! My fleet is accounted for. This is a false flag! A
provocation by NATO to justify a strike!"

"A false flag?" The President of the United States leaned into his
camera. He was in the Situation Room, jacket off, tie loosened. He
looked ten years older than he had at breakfast. "You think I dropped a
hypersonic missile on my own city? On *Los Angeles*? Are you out of your
mind?"

"Please," the President of China cut in. His voice was calm, icy, and
terrifyingly pragmatic. "The missile did not detonate. If the Americans
wished to stage an attack, they would have ensured an explosion to
maximize the political capital. This... this is something else."

"It is a threat to us all," the Prime Minister of Israel said sharply.
"If a submarine can bypass the Pacific sonar net and strike the US
mainland, it can strike Tel Aviv. It can strike Beijing. It can strike
Moscow."

"It *is* a Russian sub!" The President of Ukraine slammed his hand on
his desk. He was in fatigues, dark circles under his eyes. "We have been
telling you for months that their command structure is fracturing! This
is a rogue element. This is what happens when you let a mafia state run
a navy!"

"Watch your tongue," the Russian President hissed. "Or I will---"

"Silence!"

The voice came from the American President. It was loud enough to clip
the audio feed.

"We have boots on the ground at the crash site," the American said,
looking at a tablet handed to him by a trembling aide. "My EOD teams are
scanning the projectile. They're getting readings."

The call went dead silent. Six of the most powerful people on Earth held
their breath.

"Is it nuclear?" the British PM whispered.

"No," the American said, scrolling. "Radiation levels are background
only. It's kinetic. Tungsten and concrete." He paused, his brow
furrowing. "But there's something else. The casing... it's vibrating."

"Vibrating?" The Chinese President asked. "A timer?"

"No," the American looked up, his face pale. "It's a transmitter. It's
broadcasting. Short-range, low frequency. It's not sending data to a
satellite. It's sending a signal to the local area."

"What kind of signal?" the Russian demanded.

"It's hijacking the local bands," the American said, reading the report.
"It just overpowered the emergency broadcast system in LA. It's hitting
every cell phone, every radio, every television within fifty miles of
the impact site."

"What is it saying?" The Israeli PM asked.

The American President tapped the tablet. "It's an audio file. Playing
now."

He patched the audio into the conference call.

For a moment, there was only static. Then, a voice. It was calm,
accented, but unmistakably articulate. It sounded like a professor
giving a lecture to a disappointing class.

"*This is Captain Nemo.*"

The Russian President went rigid. He knew the name. Or rather, he knew
the literary reference, and he knew only one of his commanders had the
arrogance to use it. *Korolenko.*

"*You are confusing luck with mercy,*" the voice continued. "*You are
holding meetings. You are shouting. You are blaming each other. You are
calculating the appropriate response.*"

A pause.

"*Stop calculating. The math has changed. Today, I placed a stone in
your garden to show you that your fences are imaginary. I have nineteen
more stones. And I am not interested in your flags or your borders.*"

The voice grew harder, colder.

"*You treat war like a game of chess. You sacrifice pawns. You trade
territory. You monetize the blood. I am flipping the board. From this
moment on, there are no superpowers. There are only targets.*"

"*I require no ransom. I require no territory. I require your attention.
I will be watching. And if I see you playing your games... if I see a
tank cross a border, or a drone drop a payload, or a politician sign a
check for weapons... the next stone will not be made of concrete.*"

The audio cut out.

The silence in the conference call was heavier than the ocean.

"He... he is dictating terms to the world," the British PM stammered.
"He is declaring a global ceasefire? Under threat of annihilation?"

"He is an anarchist," the Chinese President said quietly. "He is trying
to freeze the geopolitical order."

"He is a traitor," the Russian President snarled. "Give me his
coordinates. I will hunt him down."

"You don't have his coordinates," the American said, staring at the
blank screen where the audio file had played. "And neither do I. He just
proved he can hit us. And if we hunt him... if we corner him..."

"He launches," the Israeli finished.

"We are hostages," the Ukrainian President said, a strange mix of horror
and awe in his voice. "All of us. He has taken the entire planet
hostage."

The American President looked at the camera, meeting the eyes of his
enemies and allies.

"So," he said. "What do we do now?"
