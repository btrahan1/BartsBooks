# Chapter 4: The Art of the Deal

### []{#anchor}Chapter Four: The Art of the Deal

The US President leaned forward, his elbows resting heavily on the
mahogany table of the Situation Room. He stared into the camera lens,
projecting his gaze across thousands of miles of fiber optics, straight
into the bunkers of Kyiv and the Urals.

"I strongly, very strongly suggest," the President began, his voice
dropping to a register that rumbled through the speakers, "that you two
declare a ceasefire. Right here, right now."

On the screen, the Russian President bristled, his mouth opening to
retort, but the American cut him off with a raised finger.

"And you have a joint conference tonight! Within the hour!"

"This is impossible," the Russian President spat. "You are asking me to
bow to a pirate? To show weakness before my people because of a magic
trick in California? If I stop the offensive now, it looks like
capitulation."

"And if I stop defending," the Ukrainian President countered, his voice
tight with exhaustion, "my people will hang me. We have momentum in the
south. We cannot just stop."

"You're not listening," the US President said, shaking his head. He
looked at them with a pitying expression, like a landlord explaining an
eviction notice. "You think he's done? You think he fired his one shot?"

He tapped the screen where the map of the Pacific was displayed.

"He hit Los Angeles to get our attention. Mission accomplished. But look
at the timestamp. That missile traveled four minutes. The sub was close.
Too close. Which means he's moving fast."

The American President leaned back, his eyes narrowing.

"You can spin it any way you want to. Call it a humanitarian pause. Call
it a holiday truce. Make up a holiday if you have to. But unless I miss
my guess, that submarine isn't anywhere near Los Angeles anymore, if you
get my meaning."

The silence on the line stretched thin. The implication hung in the air
like smoke. If *K-329 Belgorod* could slip the Pacific sonar net, it
could certainly navigate the Atlantic. Or the Mediterranean. Or the
Baltic.

"He said he was watching," the US President continued, pointing a finger
at the Russian feed. "He said if he sees a tank cross a border... he
launches. You think he's bluffing? Because I've got a ten-ton dart
sitting in the middle of Wilshire Boulevard that says he isn't."

The British Prime Minister cleared his throat nervously. "If he targets
Moscow... or Kyiv... with a kinetic weapon..."

"It won't be kinetic next time," the Israeli Prime Minister said darkly.
"He said the next stone would *not* be concrete."

The American President nodded. "Exactly. He gave us the warning shot.
Next one is the real deal. And I suggest you hold to that ceasefire, or
you both will regret it. Bigly."

The Russian President looked away from the camera. His eyes darted to
someone off-screen---presumably a general or an advisor. There was a
whispered exchange in Russian, too fast for the translation software to
catch perfectly, but the tone was urgent. Fearful.

The Ukrainian President rubbed his temples. He looked at the American,
then at the Russian. The two men who had been feeding the meat grinder
for two years stared at each other through the pixels.

"A temporary cessation of hostilities," the Russian President said
finally, the words tasting like ash in his mouth. "To... assess the
global security threat."

"Joint statement," the US President pressed. "Split screen. You both
announce it. The world needs to see you two agreeing on something, or
the markets are going to open at zero tomorrow."

"Agreed," the Ukrainian President whispered. "But only if he pulls back
his armor from the eastern line."

"He will," the US President said, staring at the Russian. "Won't he?"

The Russian President gritted his teeth. "We will... consolidate our
positions. For the safety of the troops."

"Good," the American said. He loosened his tie further, exhaling a
breath he felt he'd been holding for an hour. "Get your speechwriters on
it. You have forty-five minutes."

He reached for the button to cut the feed, but paused.

"And gentlemen? Pray he's watching CNN. Because if he doesn't like your
tone... I don't think there's a bunker deep enough for what comes next."

The screens went black, one by one, leaving the US President alone in
the dim light of the Situation Room.

He looked at his Chief of Staff.

"Get me the Fed Chairman," the President said, wiping sweat from his
upper lip. "And tell the Navy to stop looking for that sub off
California. He's not there."

"Where do you think he is, sir?" the Chief of Staff asked.

The President looked at the global map on the wall. The oceans were
vast, blue, and terrifyingly empty.

"If I were him?" the President muttered. "I'd be heading to where the
money is."
