# Chapter 5: Ghosts in the Water

### []{#anchor}Chapter Five: Ghosts in the Water

"Sir! I have Admiral Stevens on the red line." The Chief of Staff held
the receiver out like it was a live grenade.

The President snatched it. He didn't sit down. "This is The President.
Tell me."

"Sir," Admiral Stevens' voice was tinny, filtered through layers of
secure encryption, but the tremor was audible. "We caught a blip. SOSUS
array off the coast of Iceland just registered a transient acoustic
anomaly. It was brief, but we got a direction."

"Where?"

"The speed doesn't make sense, sir," the Admiral said, the disbelief
clear in his voice. "Based on the time of the LA impact... he shouldn't
be there. The math says it's impossible. He's moving faster than any
nuclear vessel on record. Maybe fifty knots. Maybe more. But the vector
is confirmed. He was heading to the North Sea."

The President gripped the edge of the desk, his knuckles turning white.
The North Sea. The backyard of Europe. The throat of NATO. And the
doorstep of the Russian Northern Fleet.

"Can they hit Ukraine or Russia from there?" the President asked, his
eyes darting to the digital map on the wall where the ceasefire was
currently holding by a thread.

"Sir," the Admiral paused, the silence heavy. "With the Zircon system?
From the North Sea, they can hit London in three minutes. Berlin in
four. Moscow in six. They can hit almost anywhere from there."

"Do we have a visual? A hunter-killer in the area?"

"No, sir. He popped up just long enough to ping the sonar---like he was
waving at us---and then vanished into the thermal background. He's
ghosting us, Mr. President. He's playing tag."

The President slowly lowered the phone to his chest.

"He's not running," the President whispered to the room, the realization
turning his blood to ice. "He's positioning."

He looked at the Chief of Staff.

"Call the British Prime Minister back. Tell him to get his cabinet
underground. Tell him it's a drill if you have to, but get them out of
Westminster."

"Sir? You think he's going to strike London?"

"I don't know what he's going to do," the President said, staring at the
blue expanse of the Atlantic map. "But if he's in the North Sea, he's
got a gun to the head of the entire European economy. The oil rigs. The
wind farms. The shipping lanes."

The President walked back to the main screen.

"And if those Russian tanks move an inch," he muttered, "Nemo doesn't
have to launch a missile. He just has to sink a few oil tankers and
Europe freezes to death."

He picked up the phone again.

"Admiral?"

"Yes, Mr. President?"

"Do not engage. I repeat, do not engage. If you find him, you track him.
If you shoot at him, and you miss... we lose a city."

"Understood, sir. Standing by."

The President hung up. He looked at the countdown clock for the joint
press conference between Russia and Ukraine. Thirty minutes.

"Let's hope they stick to the script," he said. "Because the director is
watching from the front row."
