# Chapter 6: The Taboo Breaker

### []{#anchor}Chapter Six: The Taboo Breaker

The *Belgorod* hung suspended in the black water of the Norwegian Sea,
three hundred meters down. The hull groaned softly under the pressure, a
living thing hiding in the dark.

Inside the Command Information Center, the air was recycled and cold.
The screens displayed the global news feeds---the "Historic Ceasefire"
banners running across the bottom of CNN, BBC, and Al Jazeera. The
Russian and Ukrainian Presidents were shaking hands on split screens,
looking grim, forced, and entirely insincere.

Captain Nemo watched the feed with a look of profound disappointment. He
turned to the tactical display, which showed satellite imagery of the
front lines. The tanks weren't retreating; they were idling. The
artillery was repositioning, not packing up.

"Prepare Tube 1," Nemo said quietly.

Antonov, standing beside him, stiffened. He looked at the weapon
designation on the screen. It wasn't a kinetic slug this time. It wasn't
concrete.

"Sir," Antonov said, his voice low so the nearby sonar operators
wouldn't hear. "You know I back you 100%. But, I just have to ask, so I
can follow your wisdom."

Nemo turned to look at his old friend. "Ask."

"Why?" Antonov gestured to the news feed. "They are doing what you
asked. They are talking. The shooting has stopped."

"Because they don't believe me," Nemo said, turning back to the map. He
tapped the cluster of thermal signatures near the border. "Look at them.
They are pausing to catch their breath. They think I am a political
variable. They think I am bluffing."

"But a nuclear tip..." Antonov whispered, the words heavy. "Even a
tactical one. Once we cross that line, Valery... there is no going back.
We become monsters."

"We are already monsters to them, Antonov. Now we must become their
nightmares." Nemo walked over to the Weapons Control key slot. "They
declared a ceasefire, but it won't last. Not unless they understand the
alternative is extinction. They need to see the mushroom cloud to
remember why they fear it."

He pulled the red key from his tunic.

"It is a necessary evil," Nemo said, his eyes hard as flint. "We are not
targeting a city. We are targeting their arrogance."

He punched in a new set of coordinates. Latitude 74 North. Longitude 56
East.

"Novaya Zemlya," Antonov recognized the coordinates. The old Soviet test
site. "The Polygon."

"An uninhabited mountaintop, covered in snow," Nemo confirmed. "No
casualties. No fallout on populated areas. But visible to the
satellites. Visible to the Northern Fleet."

He looked at the Weapon Officer.

"Set the payload to the same as what was dropped on Hiroshima."

The room went deathly silent. Fifteen kilotons. Small by modern
thermonuclear standards, but enough to vaporize rock, turn snow into
steam, and send a shockwave around the world. It was the specific yield
that had ended the last World War. Nemo intended it to end this one.

"Sir," Volkov said from the weapons console, his hand trembling
slightly. "Target locked. Flight path calculated. Detonation altitude
set for airburst to maximize visual signature."

Nemo inserted his key. Antonov, after a long, agonizing second, inserted
his.

"They have forgotten the heat," Nemo murmured. "They have forgotten the
blinding light."

"Tube 1 flooded," Volkov reported. "Equalizing pressure."

Nemo looked at the timer.

"Launch."

The submarine lurched violently as the heavy missile was ejected. The
compressed air roared, followed seconds later by the ignition of the
booster engine underwater. The sound was a thunderclap that washed over
the hull.

"Bird is away," Volkov said. "Breaking surface... now."

Nemo stared at the sonar screen, watching the line trace upward,
breaking the seal of the ocean and climbing into the grey sky of the
Arctic.

"It will take six minutes," Nemo said.

He clasped his hands behind his back and looked at the faces of his
crew. They were terrified. Good. They should be.

"And God have mercy on their souls," Nemo whispered, "because I will
not."
