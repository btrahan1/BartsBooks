# Chapter 7: The Second Sun

### []{#anchor}Chapter Seven: The Second Sun

The atmosphere over the Arctic Circle was torn apart.

It wasn't a battle; it was a desperate, flailing attempt to stop the
inevitable. The moment the Zircon breached the surface, the early
warning radars of three continents screamed in unison.

"Vampire! Vampire! Inbound high hypersonic!"

In the control towers of Murmansk, coffee cups shattered on the floor.
In the cockpits of Ukrainian MiG-29s patrolling the northern border,
pilots slammed their throttles forward, engaging afterburners until the
engines threatened to melt. They were hundreds of miles away, but panic
shrinks geography. Everyone thought they were the target.

Russian Su-57s screamed out of the cloud deck, their pilots fighting
G-forces that crushed the air from their lungs. They locked their
radars. They fired long-range air-to-air interceptors.

The missiles streaked out, leaving trails of white smoke against the
grey sky, hunting the dark needle moving at Mach 9.

But the Zircon was faster than thought. It was a blur, a glitch in
reality. The interceptors lagged behind, chasing a ghost that was
already miles ahead.

"Engage! Engage with everything!" the Russian Commander of the Northern
Fleet screamed into his headset.

From the ground, the *Peresvet* laser batteries swiveled, their lenses
focusing beams of concentrated energy designed to blind sensors and melt
casings. They painted the sky with invisible heat.

It didn't matter.

The Zircon was coated in plasma, a superheated sheath of ionized air
created by its own speed. The lasers scattered against the plasma shield
like flashlights in fog. The interceptor missiles ran out of fuel and
fell uselessly into the sea.

There wasn't enough time to pray, let alone aim.

Six minutes is an eternity to wait for death, but a microsecond when you
are trying to stop it.

The missile began its terminal dive. It didn't aim for a city. It didn't
aim for a base. It aimed for the snow-capped peak of the Severny Island
test site.

Victoria Winters was on air again, her feed hijacked. This time, the
image wasn't coming from her cameraman. It was a satellite feed,
high-resolution, broadcast directly to every screen on Earth by Nemo's
encryption keys.

The world watched in real-time.

They saw the white expanse of the Arctic. They saw the jagged black rock
of the mountain.

And then, they saw the sun rise for the second time that day.

It started as a pinpoint of light, brighter than anything the human eye
was meant to endure. In a fraction of a second, it expanded, an angry,
expanding sphere of pure energy. The snow didn't just melt; it
sublimated instantly into steam. The rock turned to glass.

The shockwave was visible from space---a perfect ring of compressed air
expanding outward, flattening the ancient glaciers, pulverizing the
granite cliffs.

Then came the shape that every human being had been taught to fear since
1945.

The mushroom cloud.

It rose majestically, terrifyingly, punching through the cloud layer and
climbing into the stratosphere. It was a pillar of grey ash and red
fire, roiling and churning, a monument to the end of the world.

The broadcast held the shot. There was no commentary. No pundits
analyzing the yield. Just the silent, high-definition footage of the
nuclear cloud unfolding over the frozen wasteland.

In the Situation Room, the American President sank into his chair, his
face ashen.

In the Kremlin, the Russian President dropped his phone, his hands
shaking uncontrollably.

In Kyiv, the soldiers in the trenches stopped firing their rifles and
looked North, feeling a primal shudder run through the earth itself.

Nemo had done the unthinkable. He had broken the seal. He had reminded a
generation raised on video games and CGI that the fire was real.

The cloud continued to rise, casting a long, dark shadow over the ice.
It was horrific. It was beautiful. It was the end of the old world.
