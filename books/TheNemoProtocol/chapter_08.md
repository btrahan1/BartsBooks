# Chapter 8: The Table of Silence

Chapter Eight: The Table of Silence

The air in the Kremlin's St. George's Hall usually smelled of floor wax
and history. Today, it smelled of stale sweat and fear.

For the first time since the Second World War, the leaders of the East
and West sat at the same physical table. There were no video screens. No
encrypted lines. Just six human beings sitting around a hastily arranged
circular table, stripped of their aides, their generals, and their
grandeur.

The Hexagon.

The President of the United States sat directly across from the
President of Russia. To his right, the President of Ukraine. To his
left, the President of China. The leaders of Israel and Great Britain
filled the gaps.

There was no shouting. The mushroom cloud over Novaya Zemlya had sucked
the oxygen out of their lungs.

The document in the center of the table was thin. Just three pages. It
didn't have complex clauses or trade agreements. It was a surrender
document---not to a country, but to a concept.

"We are agreed," the Chinese President said softly. "Immediate cessation
of all offensive operations. A return to pre-2014 borders. A complete
demilitarization of the contact line."

"It will look like weakness," the Russian President murmured, staring at
his hands. He looked deflated, a man whose legacy had just been
rewritten by a submarine captain.

"It looks like survival," the Ukrainian President said. He picked up the
pen. He didn't look triumphant. He just looked tired. "We stop the
bleeding. Today."

The American President nodded. "We call it the 'Treaty of Moscow.' But
the history books..." He paused, looking around the room. "They're going
to call this 'The Useless War of Greed.' And they'll be right."

He signed his name. Then he slid the paper to the Russian.

"Sign it," the American said. "Before he decides to vote again."

The Russian President hesitated, the pen hovering over the paper. He
knew that somewhere, deep in the Atlantic or the Arctic, Nemo was
listening. He signed.

It was over. The guns fell silent across Eastern Europe. The drones
landed. The tanks turned their engines off. The world exhaled.
