# Chapter 10: The Bedtime Story

### []{#anchor}Chapter Ten: The Bedtime Story

The scotch in the President's glass was twenty-five years old, smooth,
and tasted like victory. He was sitting in his private cabin aboard Air
Force One, thirty thousand feet somewhere over the Atlantic. The engines
hummed a lullaby. The Treaty of Moscow was signed. The world was safe.

The door to the cabin burst open. It wasn't a knock; it was a breach.

"Mr. President." It was the Communications Director. Her face was
drained of blood, her tablet clutched to her chest like a shield.

"Not now, Sarah," the President sighed, swirling the amber liquid. "We
just saved the world. Let me have five minutes."

"Mr. President, please turn on the TV."

The President frowned. "I don't want to see the pundits analyzing my
signature. I know what I did."

"Sir," she stepped forward, reaching for the remote herself, a breach of
protocol so severe he sat up straight. "Turn it on."

"Which channel?"

"All of them, sir."

The President grabbed the remote and clicked the wall-mounted screen to
life.

It wasn't CNN. It wasn't Fox. It wasn't the BBC.

It was a static image. A simple, terrifying graphic: A sonar sweep line,
rotating endlessly in a circle of dark green.

And then, the voice. It wasn't angry this time. It was conversational.
Intimate. Like a man talking to himself in the dark.

"*I am sitting here on a sub, at an undisclosed depth. It's cold down
here.*"

The President froze. The scotch glass tilted in his hand.

"*I am looking at the charts,*" Nemo's voice continued, drifting through
the high-fidelity speakers of the flying fortress. "*And I am thinking
to myself... what should I destroy?*"

The President set the glass down. "Get the Pentagon on the line. Now."

"*I see, in my mind, a World Power,*" Nemo mused. "*One of the largest.
A dragon that has grown fat on the trade of the world. And I see a tiny
island. A jewel they covet. I see an opportunity to end a war before it
starts.*"

The map on the screen changed. It zoomed in on the South China Sea. Two
targets lit up in red. One was the island of Taiwan. The other was the
sprawling port city of Shanghai.

"*The logic is cruel, but simple,*" Nemo said softly. "*With one
missile, I can end this. I can wipe out the tiny country. If the jewel
is dust, the dragon has nothing to fight for. The catalyst for World War
Three is removed.*"

The President watched the red reticle hover over Taipei. "He wouldn't,"
he whispered. "He's... he's a peacekeeper. He wouldn't kill millions of
innocents."

"*Or,*" Nemo's voice shifted, hardening. "*I can destroy one of the
largest shipping centers in the world. I can turn the port of Shanghai
into a radioactive crater. The global economy collapses. The dragon
breaks its spine. And the war is impossible because no one can afford
the fuel.*"

The reticle slid over to the mainland. The busiest port on Earth.

"*Two choices,*" Nemo said. "*The Victim or the Aggressor. The bone or
the dog. Which removal ensures silence?*"

The audio hissed with the sound of a submarine hull creaking under
pressure.

"*I haven't decided yet,*" Nemo yawned. It was a terrifying, casual
sound. "*The tubes are flooded. The coordinates are set for both. I
think I will sleep on it tonight.*"

The screen went black.

"Sarah," the President said, his voice trembling. "What time is it in
Beijing?"

"It's... it's 4:00 AM, sir."

"Wake him up," the President ordered, standing up so fast his chair
knocked over. "Wake the Chinese President up. Tell him he has until Nemo
wakes up from his nap to figure out a solution for Taiwan, or we're all
going to lose something we can't replace."

He looked at the black screen.

"And pray that Nemo is a heavy sleeper."
