# Chapter 11: The Switzerland Protocol

### []{#anchor}Chapter Eleven: The Switzerland Protocol

The screen was split seven ways now. The Hexagon had been forced to
expand.

The President of Taiwan sat in her office in Taipei, the flag of her
nation visible behind her. She looked calm, but her hands were gripping
the edge of her desk so hard her knuckles were white.

"Independence?" The President of China slammed his fist onto his desk,
the audio peaking with the distortion of his rage. "What an insult! We
can't! You *ARE* China!"

"We are a sovereign people with a gun to our heads!" the Taiwanese
President retorted, her voice shaking but defiant.

"And who put it there?" The Chinese President pointed a trembling finger
at the camera. "You courting the West! You inviting the American
destroyers into our backyard! This Nemo is a symptom of *your*
rebellion!"

"In a few hours, we could be a huge hole in the ground!" The British
Prime Minister shouted, cutting through the translation delay.
"Gentlemen, Madam---Shanghai handles twenty percent of China's export
traffic. If he hits the port, the global supply chain snaps. It's the
Stone Age by Tuesday!"

"Then let him hit the island!" a General behind the Chinese President
whispered audibly. The Chinese President spun around and silenced him
with a glare, but the damage was done.

The Taiwanese President's eyes narrowed. "I heard that."

"We are not sacrificing Taiwan," the US President said, rubbing his
eyes. He looked like he hadn't slept in a week. "But Mr. Chairman, you
have to give him something. Nemo wants the tension gone. If you declare
Taiwan fully independent, the cause for war vanishes. Nemo stands down."

"Never," the Chinese President hissed. "I will not go down in history as
the man who fractured the Middle Kingdom because of a pirate."

"And I don't want it given to me!"

The room went silent. All eyes turned to the Taiwanese President.

"We want to challenge for independence! We want to *EARN* it!" she said,
her voice filled with a fierce, desperate pride. "We want recognition
because we are a democracy, not because we are a pity prize awarded to
stop a madman!"

She leaned into the camera.

"We don't *WANT* it like this! If we accept independence now, forever we
are the nation born of fear. We will be illegitimate."

"Madam President," the US President pleaded. "We are talking about
nuclear fire. Or worse. We are talking about extinction."

"But that maniac doesn't care!" the US President continued, his voice
rising to a shout. "He doesn't care about your historical legitimacy! He
doesn't care about the One China Policy! He is doing math! *One minus
one equals zero war.* If we don't do something, and do it now, it will
be moot!"

"He is sleeping," the Israeli Prime Minister said quietly. "We have,
perhaps, four hours."

"There is a third option," the Taiwanese President said.

The Chinese President looked at her with suspicion. "What option?"

"Neutrality," she said. "Permanent, armed neutrality. Like Switzerland."

The US President sat up. "Go on."

"We dissolve our military treaties with the United States," she said,
looking directly at the American. "No more US bases. No more freedom of
navigation patrols in the Strait. We stand alone."

She turned to the Chinese President.

"And you sign a binding UN charter recognizing Taiwan as a Permanent
Neutral Zone. You cannot invade. We cannot attack. We become a buffer
state. The flashpoint is removed."

"You would kick out the Americans?" The Chinese President asked,
skeptical.

"If it saves my people from being vaporized? Yes." She looked at the
American President. "Will you agree? To leave us alone?"

The American President hesitated. It was a massive geopolitical loss. It
meant ceding influence in the Pacific. But then he remembered the image
of the mushroom cloud over the Arctic. He remembered the scotch glass
trembling in his hand.

"If it stops Nemo," the American said softly. "We walk away."

The Chinese President sat back. A neutral Taiwan. No American warships
on his doorstep. It wasn't reunification, but it wasn't a hostile
fortress anymore either. It was... acceptable.

"A Demilitarized Neutral Zone," the Chinese President mused. "We
could... frame this as a gesture of benevolence. A protective status."

"Spin it however you want," the US President said, checking his watch.
"Just draft it. Fast."

"Wait," the Russian President spoke up for the first time. "How do we
know Nemo will accept this? It is not one of his two choices."

"Nemo is a pragmatist," the US President said. "He wants to end the war
before it starts. If there is no board, there is no game."

"Get the lawyers," the Chinese President ordered his aides. "And wake up
the Secretary General of the UN."

"Four hours," the Taiwanese President whispered, looking out her window
at the skyline of Taipei, wondering if it would still be there when the
sun came up. "Let's hope he sleeps in."
