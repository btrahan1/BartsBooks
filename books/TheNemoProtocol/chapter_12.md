# Chapter 12: Level Three

### []{#anchor}Chapter Twelve: Level Three

The ink on the "Taipei Protocols" wasn't even dry. The President of the
United States had just loosened his tie for the second time that night,
allowing himself a momentary fantasy of a nap. The Chinese President was
still on the feed, looking grim but satisfied that he had secured a
buffer zone without firing a shot.

Then, the red light on the secure terminal blinked.

"Another transmission, sir!" the Aide called out, her voice cracking.
She didn't even wait for permission. She patched it through.

The President groaned, a sound that came from the marrow of his bones.
"Let's see it."

The screen shifted. The familiar, hypnotic green sweep of the sonar
returned. The audio crackled with the static of the deep ocean.

"*Well, that was unexpected!*" Nemo's voice chirped. He sounded
genuinely surprised, perhaps even impressed. "*Switzerland in the
Pacific. Creative. Very creative.*"

A pause, filled with the hum of the sub's reactor.

"*Not optimal, but acceptable. The Dragon sleeps, and the Island remains
floating. I will holster my weapon... for that sector.*"

The collective sigh of relief in the digital conference room was
short-lived.

"*Now then,*" Nemo's tone dropped, losing its amusement. It became
heavy, like lead. "*I don't think this one will be so easy for you.*"

The map on the screen panned North. Past Shanghai. Past the Yellow Sea.
It settled on a peninsula divided by a razor-wire scar.

"*There is a little fat man with a big ego,*" Nemo said.

The President of South Korea, who had been listening in on a silent
channel, gasped.

"*I hear he is hard to negotiate with,*" Nemo continued. "*He does not
care about economics. He does not care about trade routes. He only cares
about his statue standing tall.*"

The reticle zeroed in on Pyongyang.

"*I am currently calculating the size of the warhead that it will
require to unify Korea.*"

"Jesus Christ," the US President whispered. "He's going to erase the
North."

"*Well, unity is a strong word,*" Nemo mused, sounding like a contractor
estimating a demolition job. "*But, what can you do. If the North is a
sheet of glass, there is no more border. This will probably take a
couple of days. I have to navigate around some... sensitive fishing
fleets.*"

The threat hung in the air. A couple of days. A grace period. Or a
torture session.

"*I wonder how you will handle this one,*" Nemo said. "*You cannot
reason with him. You cannot buy him. And he has nukes of his own. If I
strike him, he might strike Seoul before he dies. It is a messy
puzzle.*"

The sonar pinged.

"*But that little man will be gone, one way or another. I will be in
firing position in forty-eight hours. Start talking.*"

The transmission cut.

The US President looked at the Chinese President. "He's your client
state. You control his food. You control his oil."

"I control nothing!" The Chinese President snapped, looking genuinely
terrified. "He is a mad dog! If I cut his oil, he attacks *me*! If I
tell him to step down, he launches everything he has!"

"He has to go," the South Korean President said, her voice trembling.
"If Nemo launches a strategic weapon at Pyongyang... the fallout alone
will kill millions of my people. The retaliation will kill the rest."

"Nemo knows that," the US President realized. "That's why he gave us
forty-eight hours. He's challenging us."

"Challenging us to what?" the British PM asked.

" To do the one thing the world has been too afraid to do for seventy
years," the American replied. "He wants regime change. And he wants us
to do it so he doesn't have to."

He looked at the Chinese President.

"We need a coup," the American said. "And we need it by Friday."
