# Chapter 14: The Bailout of the People

### []{#anchor}Chapter Fourteen: The Bailout of the People

The Situation Room was quiet, but it was a nervous quiet. The kind of
silence that precedes a hurricane. The President paced the carpet,
wearing a track into the presidential seal woven into the rug.

"Any word?" he barked, not looking up.

"None," the Admiral replied, staring at the empty Atlantic chart. The
*Belgorod* had vanished again.

"How long has it been?"

"48 hours, sir. Since the message about the 'Bankers'."

"What are they doing?" The President stopped pacing. "Is he targeting
the New York Stock Exchange? The Federal Reserve? Is he going to put a
torpedo into the SWIFT servers?"

"I think he's waiting, sir," the Chief of Staff said.

"Waiting for what?"

At that moment, a special phone rang. It wasn't the Red Line to Moscow.
It wasn't the secure line to the Pentagon. It was a slim, matte-black
smartphone that sat in a dedicated cradle. Only five people in the world
had that number.

The President stared at it. He picked it up and hit speaker.

"E... how's it going?" the President asked, trying to keep his voice
steady. "I'm kind of busy right now."

On the other end, the voice was casual, slightly robotic, but
unmistakably him. The eccentric billionaire. The rocket man. The owner
of the digital town square.

"*Doing what? Nothing?*" E asked. "*Just pacing around wondering where
the submarine is?*"

"Do you know something?" the President demanded.

Another phone rang. A different cradle. This one was sleek, white. The
tech mogul from Seattle. The one who owned the cloud.

The President blinked. He picked that one up too. "J?"

"*Yeah, it's me,*" the voice said, calm and analytical. "*You might want
to turn on the news.*"

"I have the news on!" the President snapped. "Nothing is happening!"

"*Not CNN,*" J said. "*Local news. California feed.*"

The Aide switched the main screen.

It was Victoria Winters. She looked different now. Harder. She wasn't
covering cat shows anymore. She was standing in front of the Federal
Reserve Bank in San Francisco.

"In an unprecedented move," she said, reading from a teleprompter that
seemed to be updating in real-time, her eyes wide. "An... extremely
large transfer of funds has just occurred. The Treasury Department is
reporting a massive influx of capital into the Department of Education's
servicing accounts."

She paused, touching her earpiece.

"We are getting confirmation now. Federal Student debt... all 1.7
trillion dollars of it... has been... paid off."

"What?" exclaimed the President.

He looked at the two phones on the table.

"What did you do?"

"*Me and the boys got together,*" E said, his voice crackling with
amusement. "*We had a little group chat. B was there. Z too. We thought
it might help.*"

"*We liquidated,*" J added calmly. "*Offshore accounts. Shell companies.
The rainy day funds. We realized that if Nemo decides to target the*
'*Bankers,*' *he isn't going to blow up a building. He's going to blow
up the inequality that fuels the anger.*"

"*It's an insurance policy, Mr. President,*" E said. "*If the people are
happy, Nemo has no mandate. If the debt is gone, the economy reboots
from the bottom up. We figured it was cheaper to pay off the country
than to lose everything when a hypersonic missile hits the Hamptons.*"

The President stared at the screen. The ticker tape at the bottom was
going berserk.

**BREAKING: NATIONAL DEBT CEILING LOWERED.** **BREAKING: MEDICAL DEBT
PURCHASED AND FORGIVEN BY ANONYMOUS DONOR.** **BREAKING: MARKETS
RALLYING.**

"You... you just bailed out the American people," the President
stammered. "To save your own skins?"

"*Enlightened self-interest,*" J corrected. "*We prefer that term.*"

"*Besides,*" E laughed. "*I checked the sonar. He's parked off the coast
of Florida now. Right near Mar-a-Lago and then heading toward the Cayman
Islands. We figured he was looking for the tax havens. So... we emptied
them first.*"

The President slumped into his chair. He started to laugh. It was a
hysterical, disbelief-fueled laugh.

"Nemo isn't even firing shots anymore," the President wheezed. "He's
just... pointing. And you sharks are eating each other to get out of the
way."

"*It worked, didn't it?*" E asked.

The Admiral looked up from his console. "Sir! New message from the sub."

"Read it," the President wiped a tear from his eye.

TRANSACTION CONFIRMED. ACCOUNTS BALANCED.

THE OLIGARCHS HAVE BLED. THE PEOPLE CAN BREATHE.

INTERESTING.

I AM BORED WITH MONEY. IT IS TOO EASY TO MOVE.

SETTING COURSE FOR THE AMAZON.

THE LUNGS OF THE WORLD ARE COUGHING.

The President looked at the screen. "The Amazon?"

He picked up the red phone to Brazil.

"Get the President of Brazil on the line," he said, his energy
returning. "And tell him to stop the chainsaws. Right now. Or Nemo is
going to turn his logging equipment into scrap metal."

He looked at the two billionaires on the speakerphone.

"Thanks, boys. You just bought us another day. Now, if you'll excuse me,
I have to go save a rainforest."

He hung up.

"He's running down the checklist," the Chief of Staff murmured. "War.
Dictators. Debt. Environment. He's fixing the world at gunpoint."

"And the terrifying part," the President said, looking at the map where
the green dot of the *Belgorod* was moving south. "Is that it's
working."
