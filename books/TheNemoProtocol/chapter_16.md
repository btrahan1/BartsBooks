# Chapter 16: The Ghost in the Fjord

### []{#anchor}Chapter Sixteen: The Ghost in the Fjord

The destruction of La Orchila was a masterclass in physics. The tungsten
rod struck the island at Mach 10. There was no fireball, only a
localized earthquake and a displacement of earth so violent that the
island simply ceased to be a landmass and became a shoal. It was clean,
terrifying, and silent.

The cleanup of the Amazon, however, was loud.

For six weeks, the jungle roared. It wasn't the sound of wildlife, but
the sound of the Hexagon Army. Joint task forces from the US, Brazil,
Colombia, and Britain swept through the canopy like a scythe. Illegal
airstrips were cratered. Gold dredges were scuttled. Narco-labs were
dismantled. It was the largest police action in human history.

The world held its breath, waiting for the next ultimatum. But the
screens stayed dark. The sonar sweeps stopped interrupting "The
Bachelor."

The world began to relax. Just a fraction.

Four thousand miles away, in the deep, freezing twilight of a fjord near
Tromsø, Norway, the water broke.

It was 3:00 AM. The sun barely dipped below the horizon in the summer,
casting a perpetual, eerie grey light over the black water. The
*Belgorod* rose slowly, shedding water like a surfacing leviathan.

There were no sirens. No scrambling of jets.

A solitary fishing trawler, painted a peeling blue, chugged out from a
private dock. It pulled alongside the massive submarine, looking like a
toy boat next to a skyscraper.

On the deck of the sub, a hatch opened. Captain Nemo emerged, breathing
in the sharp, salty air. He didn't look like a conqueror. He looked like
a man who needed a shave and a cigarette.

An old Norwegian man in a thick wool sweater stood on the deck of the
trawler. He didn't wave. He simply nodded. The Norwegians were strange
like that; they respected the sea, and they respected silence. They
viewed Nemo less as a terrorist and more as a violent storm---something
you weather, not something you argue with.

A crane on the trawler swung over. Pallets were lowered. Fresh
vegetables. Reindeer meat. Coffee. Medical supplies. Hundreds of kilos
of supplies, transferred in total silence.

"The payment is in the account," Nemo said, his voice carrying over the
water.

"We do not want your money," the Norwegian man said. His English was
perfect, clipped. "We want the list."

Nemo reached into his jacket and pulled out a ruggedized hard drive. He
tossed it down to the trawler. The old man caught it with one hand.

"The names," Nemo said. "Every handler. Every safe house. Every bank
account associated with the trafficking rings in Eastern Europe. I
pulled it from the servers in Moscow and Langley before I went dark."

The Norwegian nodded. He didn't ask how Nemo got it. He tucked the drive
into his pocket.

"And the intelligence on the North Sea infrastructure?" the Norwegian
asked.

"You have holes in your pipeline security," Nemo replied. "I patched
them for you. Digitally. But physically? You are vulnerable."

"We will fix it."

"See that you do. I would hate to have to make a demonstration of how
fragile your economy is."

The crane retracted. The trawler pulled away.

"Two weeks," the Norwegian called out over the widening gap.

"What?"

"We will report this sighting in two weeks," the old man said, lighting
a pipe. "Paperwork error. Bureaucracy. We are a slow people."

Nemo smirked. "Thank you."

"God speed, Captain," the Norwegian said, turning his back. "Or whatever
it is you are."

The *Belgorod* vented its ballast tanks. With a hiss of escaping air, it
slipped beneath the surface, leaving only ripples in the mirror-smooth
fjord.

For two weeks, the world heard nothing. The intelligence agencies
panicked. The pundits speculated that Nemo had died, or the sub had
malfunctioned.

Then, the report leaked from Oslo. A sighting. A resupply. A ghost in
the North.

But by then, the *Belgorod* was thousands of miles away, deep in the
Atlantic trench, processing the new data. The world had bought itself
six weeks of peace by cleaning a forest.

But Nemo was done with trees. He was looking at the hard drive he had
*kept*---the one he hadn't given to the Norwegian.

"Antonov," Nemo said, staring at the screen in the dim light of the CIC.
"Set a course for the Mediterranean."

"The Mediterranean, sir? It is a bathtub. Nowhere to hide."

"We aren't hiding," Nemo said. "We are going hunting. The Amazon was
nature. Now, we deal with human nature."
