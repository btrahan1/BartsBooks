# Chapter 17: The Arnaud Option

### []{#anchor}Chapter Seven: The Arnaud Option

The broadcast didn't interrupt a news feed this time. It interrupted the
call to prayer in Mecca. It hijacked the digital billboards in Tel Aviv.
It overrode the state television in Tehran.

The screen showed the Mediterranean Sea, blue and glittering. Then, the
camera panned to a map of the Levant.

"*How do you fix a holy war?*" Nemo asked. His voice was weary,
stripping away the theatrics of the previous weeks. He sounded like a
man exhausted by the stupidity of his own species.

"*If it was easy, it wouldn't have been going on for a thousand years.*"

In the bunkers of the Hexagon, the leaders watched in silence. This was
the one they had dreaded. The one they had whispered about.

"*To be honest, I don't have an answer here,*" Nemo admitted. He swirled
his tea, the steam rising in the cold air of the sub. "*I can fix an
economy. I can remove a dictator. I can clean a forest. Those are
logistical problems. This... this is a pathology.*"

The map zoomed in on Jerusalem. Then Gaza. Then the West Bank. Then
Southern Lebanon. Then the nuclear facilities in the mountains of Iran.

"*A large number of these people want to see it happen,*" Nemo said, his
voice dripping with disgust. "*It's probably in their prophecies or some
stupid shit like that. They want the fire. They want the Mahdi. They
want the Messiah. They want the Rapture.*"

He leaned forward, his face filling the screen. The scar of a life at
sea was visible on his cheek.

"*Be careful what you wish for.*"

The image cut to the missile tubes of the *Belgorod*. Not just one. All
of them.

"*I sure hope someone has an answer. This one might take more than one
missile, and they will be massive. I am not talking about tactical
strikes. I am talking about scouring the board.*"

He paused, letting the silence stretch until it was physically painful.

"*How does the saying go? From the Siege of Béziers, 1209?*"

Nemo smiled, and it was the most terrifying expression the world had
seen yet.

"*Kill them all, and let God sort it out?*"

The screen went black.

"He's quoting the Albigensian Crusade," the British Prime Minister
whispered, horrified. "He's talking about total indiscriminate
slaughter. He's saying if we can't share the land, no one gets to live
on it."

"He means it," the Israeli Prime Minister said, his face pale. "He is
parked in the Mediterranean. He can hit the Dimona reactor. He can hit
the Dome of the Rock. He can hit Qom. He can turn the Holy Land into a
radioactive parking lot."

"We need a ceasefire," the US President said, pacing the room. "A
permanent one."

"We have tried for decades!" The Israeli PM snapped. "The Iranians want
us dead. Hamas wants us dead. We cannot negotiate with people who want
to be martyrs!"

"Then we change the definition of martyrdom," the US President said. He
turned to his aides. "Get the phone."

"Who are we calling? Tehran?"

"No," the President said. "Tehran is political. This isn't political
anymore. Nemo said it himself. It's prophecy. It's religion."

He looked at the Hexagon leaders.

"Get the Pope. Get the Grand Imam of al-Azhar. Get the Chief Rabbis. Get
the Ayatollah. Get the Patriarch of Constantinople."

"Sir, you want to convene a religious summit?"

"I want to lock them in a room," the President growled. "In Rome. Or
Geneva. I don't care. And I want them to issue a joint Fatwa, a Papal
Bull, and a Halakhic ruling that says *God does not want this war*."

"They will never agree," the Saudi King, who had joined the call, said
skeptically.

"Then show them the footage of the Arctic," the President said. "Show
them what Nemo did to the mountain. And tell them that if they don't
find a way to share Jerusalem by Sunday, Nemo is going to send all their
followers to meet the Almighty ahead of schedule."

"And the politicians?" the Chinese President asked. "What do we do?"

"We enforce it," the American replied. "The Hexagon Army. We turn
Jerusalem into an International Zone. The Vatican of the Middle East. No
armies. Only police. And we guarantee the borders."

"It is impossible," the Israeli PM whispered.

"The alternative," the American said, pointing to the black screen, "is
that God sorts it out. And I don't think any of us are going to like
where we end up in that line."

"Get the clerics," the American ordered. "Tell them judgment day is
here. And his name is Nemo."
