# Chapter 18: The Black Blood

### []{#anchor}Chapter Eighteen: The Black Blood

The tea in Nemo's cup had gone cold. The sonar ping was a rhythmic
heartbeat in the silence of the Captain's quarters.

"It will never work," Nemo sighed, staring at the ceiling.

Antonov sat across from him, polishing his glasses with a rag. "The
Religious Summit? The Americans are rounding up the clerics as we speak.
The Pope is already on a plane to Geneva."

"It is theater," Nemo said, his voice heavy. "They will sign a paper.
They will shake hands. And the moment the cameras are off, a boy in
Hebron will throw a stone, or a guard in Tehran will fire a rifle, and
the paper will be used to light the fire."

He rubbed his face. He looked aged. The adrenaline of the last few
months was fading, replaced by the crushing weight of being the world's
jailer.

"I didn't think we'd get this far, to be honest, Comrade," Nemo
whispered.

"We have done the impossible," Antonov said softly. "Europe is quiet.
The Amazon is silent. Korea is free. The American students are
debt-free."

"We've done some good, yes?" Nemo asked, looking for validation in his
old friend's eyes.

"We have saved millions," Antonov confirmed.

"Do we count our blessings and call it a day?" Nemo asked. "We could
disappear. Sink the *Belgorod* in the Mariana Trench, take the escape
pod. Live out our days on a beach in Patagonia with new names. Let them
have their Holy War. Let them slaughter each other over lines in the
sand."

Antonov stopped polishing. He put his glasses back on. "Is that what you
want? To watch the world burn from a beach chair?"

Nemo stood up and walked to the wall map. He traced the coastline of the
Persian Gulf. The Strait of Hormuz. The pipelines crisscrossing the
desert like veins.

"Or do we really want to make a difference?" Nemo murmured.

He turned back to Antonov.

"Why do they fight, Antonov? Truly?"

"God," Antonov said. "Land."

"No," Nemo shook his head. "That is the excuse. They fight because they
can afford to. They fight because the superpowers pump billions of
dollars of weapons into the region to secure the flow of oil. The West
looks away from the atrocities because they need the fuel. The East
sells the missiles because they need the cash."

Nemo tapped the map hard.

"The Holy Land is floating on a sea of black blood. As long as that
blood flows, the war will never end. It is the engine of their hate."

He walked over to the safe in the corner of the room. He spun the dial.

"I threatened to kill them all," Nemo said. "But that would make me the
devil they claim I am. I don't want to kill the people, Antonov. I want
to kill the *relevance*."

He pulled out a binder. It was marked in red Cyrillic: **PROTOCOL:
PROMETHEUS.**

"If the oil burns," Nemo said quietly, "the money stops. If the money
stops, the weapons stop. If the oil is gone, the Americans leave. The
Chinese leave. The Russians leave. The world is forced to switch to
fusion, to solar, to wind. We solve the climate crisis and the funding
for the terror in one stroke."

"Valery," Antonov stood up, his voice trembling. "You are talking about
destroying the energy infrastructure of the entire Middle East. The
economic collapse... it will be catastrophic. People will starve."

"Short term? Yes. It will be chaos," Nemo admitted. "But long term? They
will have to rebuild. They will have to focus on food, on water, on
survival. They won't have time to debate whose prophet is the true one
when they are trying to keep the lights on."

He tossed the binder onto the table.

"It will take more than one missile," Nemo said, echoing his broadcast.
"We target the refineries. The terminals. The pipelines. Abqaiq. Kharg
Island. Basra. We burn it all."

"The world will hunt us to the ends of the earth for this," Antonov
whispered. "They tolerated the other things. But this... this breaks the
machine."

Nemo sighed, a long, rattling exhale that seemed to empty his lungs.

"Then let them hunt," Nemo said. "Prepare the tubes. All of them."

He looked at the cold tea.

"We are going to turn the lights out, so they can finally see the
stars."
