# Chapter 19: The Rainmaker

### []{#anchor}Chapter Nineteen: The Rainmaker

Nemo stared at Antonov. The Prometheus binder lay on the table between
them, a blueprint for global economic suicide.

"Wait, I have an alternative thought, if I may, Captain," Antonov said,
his hand resting on the map of the Persian Gulf.

"Go on." Nemo leaned back, crossing his arms. He trusted Antonov. The
man was less of a philosopher, but more of a surgeon.

Antonov swiped the digital display, moving the map away from the oil
fields. He zoomed in on the eastern Mediterranean. He brought up a
high-resolution map of the Gaza Strip.

"This," Antonov pointed to the narrow, densely populated rectangle of
land. "This is the primary source of all conflict. A thin piece of land
that they have been fighting over for centuries. It is the catalyst.
Every rocket fired from here starts a war. Every bomb dropped here
starts a riot in London or Paris."

"And?" Nemo asked, raising an eyebrow. "You want to occupy it?"

"And, what if we erase it?" Antonov said calmly.

Nemo froze. "Erase it? You mean genocide? I just told you, I am not a
butcher."

"No," Antonov shook his head. "I mean erase the *land*. Literally. We
turn the Gaza Strip into the Gaza Bay."

Nemo blinked. "But the people?"

"We give them time to evacuate," Antonov said, his voice gaining speed.
"We issue a 72-hour warning. We tell the world: *Gaza is sinking.* Let's
see which nations step up. The Egyptians open the border. The Israelis
open the border. The Hexagon Army manages the logistics. The US Navy
brings the transport ships."

Antonov looked at Nemo with a hard, pragmatic stare.

"Most of the zealots will stay, which is fine. They will want to defend
the dirt to the end. But the families? The children? The ones who just
want to live? If the Hexagon Army bands together, and gets those that
want to leave out? We save the innocent."

"And then?" Nemo asked.

"And then," Antonov mimed a downward motion with his hand. "We target
the geological shelf. A massive, concentrated kinetic strike along the
fault line coupled with a series of heavy torpedo detonations to breach
the aquifer and destabilize the bedrock. We don't burn the land. We
drown it. We turn the contested territory into the Mediterranean Sea."

Nemo stared at the map. "If there is no land, there is no occupation. If
there is no land, there are no tunnels. There is just water."

"Hmmm... wow," Nemo whispered, rubbing his chin. "That... might work. It
removes the board entirely."

He paused, his mind racing through the physics.

"But there is one small problem... the fallout," Nemo said. "If we use
the heavy conventional yields needed to crack the shelf, the dust cloud
will be massive. It will choke Tel Aviv and Cairo. And if we have to use
a low-yield tactical nuke to trigger the fault... the radiation..."

His comrade smiled. It was a mischievous, almost boyish grin that looked
out of place on the scarred submariner.

"Not if it's raining," Antonov grinned.

Nemo looked at him, confused. "Raining?"

"We are a nuclear submarine, Valery," Antonov said, walking to the
environmental controls. "We have excess thermal output. We have the
ability to launch seeding drones. We can superheat the surface water."

He tapped the screen.

"We create a storm. A localized, massive depression. We generate a
deluge. Rain so heavy it scrubs the air clean instantly. Rain so heavy
it helps wash the land into the sea."

Nemo looked at the simulation Antonov was running. A hurricane-force
storm centered over the coast.

"The Rainmaker," Nemo murmured.

"The world will see it as an act of God," Antonov said. "Or an act of
nature. We wash away the sins of the past. Clean slate."

Nemo stood up. He picked up the Prometheus binder and threw it into the
waste bin.

"Get the broadcasting equipment," Nemo ordered. "And tell the Weapons
Officer to load the heavy torpedos. We are going to redraw the map."

He looked at the tiny strip of land on the screen.

"Seventy-two hours," Nemo said. "Let's see if the world actually cares
about the people, or if they just care about the fight."
