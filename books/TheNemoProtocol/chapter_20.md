# Chapter 20: The Parting of the Fences

### []{#anchor}Chapter Twenty: The Parting of the Fences

"Impossible!" The French President shouted, his voice echoing in the
digital chamber. "You cannot simply... *sink* a territory! It is
geology, not Lego!"

"It's impossible, right?" The British Prime Minister looked frantically
at his scientific advisors off-screen. Their faces were pale, shaking
their heads---not in denial, but in terror. They were confirming the
fault lines. They were confirming the physics.

"He cracked a mountain in the Arctic," the US President reminded them,
his voice grim. "He erased an island in the Caribbean. If Nemo says the
land is going under, the land is going under."

The screen flickered. The billionaires were patched in. They were the
unofficial logistics arm of the new world order.

E was on the line, calling from the floor of a Gigafactory.

"*1.5 million people,*" E said, looking at a holographic projection of
population density. "*Challenging, but by no means impossible. It's a
math problem. Flow rates. Tonnage. If we use the US Navy landing craft,
the commercial cruise liners in the Mediterranean, and run convoys of
buses 24/7...*"

He tapped a few keys.

"*We can move 25,000 people an hour. In 72 hours? We can clear the zone.
But only if there are no checkpoints. No vetting. No stopping.*"

"What do you think, J?" the US President asked.

J sat in a minimalist office, surrounded by screens showing global
shipping lanes.

"*If anyone knows logistics, it's me,*" he grinned self-deprecatingly,
though his eyes were deadly serious. "*I can have the supply chain for
food, water, and temporary housing set up in the Sinai and the Negev
within 12 hours. We can build a city for them before the first bus
arrives. We have the tents. We have the water purification.*"

J leaned forward.

"*But in the end, it's up to him.*"

On the screen, J pointed a finger directly at the tile occupied by the
Prime Minister of Israel.

The room went silent. The Israeli PM sat stone-faced. For decades, his
career had been built on security, on borders, on the unyielding defense
of the line. Now, he was being asked to erase the line to save the
people behind it.

"You are asking me to open the gates," the PM said, his voice raspy. "To
let a million potential hostiles pour into my country? To abandon the
land we fought for?"

"The land is doomed, Mr. Prime Minister," the US President said softly.
"Nemo has marked it. In three days, it will be the sea floor. The only
question is whether it becomes a graveyard or a memory."

"The Egyptians have agreed to open the Rafah crossing," the Egyptian
President interjected, looking solemn. "We will take half. If you take
half."

The Israeli PM hesitated. He looked at the generals behind him. He saw
the fear in their eyes. Not of the enemy, but of the verdict of history.

J spoke again, his voice cutting through the political calculus.

"*Mr. Prime Minister. You study the Torah. You know the stories.*"

J looked him in the eye.

"*Do you want to be the Sword of God, or the Shepherd? Because right
now, the flood is coming. You can lock the gate and drown them, and
history will call you a monster. Or you can open the gate, lead them to
high ground, and history will call you a savior.*"

The PM looked down at his hands. He thought of the rockets. He thought
of the airstrikes. He thought of the endless, grinding cycle of
funerals.

"If the land is gone," the PM whispered, "what are we fighting over?"

"Nothing," the US President said. "The war ends because the battlefield
is gone."

The PM took a deep breath. He picked up his red phone.

"Commander," he said to his Chief of Staff. "Issue the order. Operation
Exodus."

He looked up at the camera, tears standing in his eyes.

"Tear down the fences. All of them. Let them through."
