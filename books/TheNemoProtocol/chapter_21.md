# Chapter 21: The Great Migration

Chapter Twenty-One: The Great Migration

It began an hour later. And it began with rain.

Nemo's weather modification drones had done their work. The sky over the
Levant turned a bruised, heavy purple. The clouds opened up, unleashing
a torrential downpour not seen in a century. It was warm, heavy
rain---rain that soaked the dust, rain that hid the tears.

On the ground, the unimaginable happened.

The massive steel fences that had divided the world for forty years were
bulldozed flat. Not by tanks attacking, but by engineers clearing a
path.

The Hexagon Army arrived. American Marines, Chinese PLA peacekeepers,
British soldiers, and Egyptian guards stood shoulder to shoulder. They
weren't carrying rifles. They were carrying water. They were directing
traffic.

The buses came in a seemingly endless line. The cruise ships docked off
the coast, ferrying thousands at a time to Cyprus and Greece.

The Zealots stayed. As Antonov had predicted, the hardliners---the men
with the rockets and the men who worshipped death---refused to leave.
They retreated into their tunnels, vowing to fight the ocean itself.

But the families left. The children left. The old men left.

Under the cover of the relentless rain, a river of humanity flowed out
of the kill zone. There were no checkpoints. No interrogations. Just a
desperate race against the clock.

Victoria Winters stood on a hill overlooking the border, soaking wet,
her camera capturing the biblical scene.

"They are walking out," she reported, her voice breaking. "For the first
time in history, the border is gone. And they are just... walking."

High above, the *Belgorod* surfaced briefly, just a dark shadow in the
storm-tossed sea.

"Evacuation is at 94%," Antonov reported. "The remaining 6% are
confirmed combatants or those refusing to leave."

Nemo watched the thermal feed. The heat signatures of the civilians were
moving north and south, away from the red zone.

"The rain is holding the dust down," Nemo observed. "The fallout will be
minimal."

"Time?" Antonov asked.

Nemo looked at the countdown.

"One hour. Then we rewrite the map."
