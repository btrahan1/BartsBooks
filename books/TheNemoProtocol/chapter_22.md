# Chapter 22: The Sound of Water

### []{#anchor}Chapter Twenty-Two: The Sound of Water

The event itself was not an explosion. It was a collapse.

At exactly 12:00 PM on the third day, the *Belgorod* fired. It wasn't a
missile that soared into the sky, but a series of super-cavitating
torpedoes that dove straight down, drilling deep into the tectonic fault
line beneath the continental shelf.

The world watched on shaky camera feeds from miles away.

First, the sea receded. The beaches were sucked dry as the ocean took a
massive, collective breath.

Then, the earth groaned. It was a sound so low it wasn't heard as much
as felt---a vibration that rattled the teeth of people in Cairo and
Beirut.

The coastal shelf fractured. With a terrifying, slow-motion majesty, the
land simply... gave up. The bedrock, destabilized and pulverized, slid
downwards. The tunnels collapsed instantly. The bunkers were crushed.
The empty buildings crumbled into dust.

And then, the Mediterranean rushed in.

It didn't crash like a tsunami. It poured. It filled the void. The rain,
still falling in sheets, mixed with the rising sea, creating a swirling
vortex of grey water and brown earth.

Within thirty minutes, the geography of the Middle East had changed.
Where there had been a strip of contested land, a source of agony for
millions, there was now a bay. The water churned, angry and turbulent,
swallowing the history, the hatred, and the borders.

It was shocking. It was incomprehensible. The human mind struggled to
process that a place could just *stop existing*.

For days, the water roiled. The Hexagon Army stood on the new
shorelines, watching the sea settle. The rain eventually stopped, the
clouds breaking to reveal a harsh, cleansing sun.

The world breathed a sigh of relief. It could have been Armageddon. It
could have been the start of the Third World War.

In the end, it was just... erasure.

There was no victory parade. There were no flags planted. Just the sound
of waves lapping against a new shore.

The zealots were gone, buried beneath a thousand feet of salt water. The
innocent were scattered, living in tent cities that were rapidly
becoming permanent towns, fed and watered by a world that had finally
woken up.

The world was by no means at peace. There were still arguments. There
were still grievances. The refugees needed homes. The politicians needed
to draw new maps.

But the catalyst was gone. You cannot fight for land that isn't there.
You cannot dig a tunnel through the ocean.

It was a start.
