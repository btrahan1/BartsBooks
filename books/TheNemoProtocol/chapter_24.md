# Chapter 24: The Golden Cage

### []{#anchor}Chapter Twenty-Four: The Golden Cage

The coordinates for Point Nemo lead to the loneliest place on Earth. It
is a spot in the Southern Pacific Ocean so far from land that the
nearest humans are usually the astronauts on the International Space
Station.

Nemo expected icebergs. He expected silence. He expected the void.

What he found was a construction site.

The *Belgorod*'s sonar picked it up first---not the organic sounds of
whales or the crack of ice, but the industrial thrum of high-voltage
generators and the churning of screws.

"Surface," Nemo ordered, his voice tight. "Periscope depth."

He looked through the lens and felt his breath hitch.

Floating on the heavy swells were three massive ships. They were
unmarked, painted a matte, radar-absorbing grey. They looked like slabs
of future-tech dropped into the prehistoric ocean. Between them,
anchored to a shallow seamount that shouldn't have been habitable, was a
platform.

And on the platform, machines were moving.

They were colossal 3D printers, gantry cranes moving on automated rails,
squirting layers of quick-dry concrete and polymer. They were building
structures. Geodesic domes. Barracks. Hydroponic towers.

It wasn't a military base. It looked like a colony on Mars.

"Sir," Antonov whispered, looking at the feed. "It is a town. A printed
town."

"Surface," Nemo commanded. "Fully. Man the bridge."

The *Belgorod* broke the water, the foam cascading off its black flanks.
The moment the hatch cracked open, the biting Antarctic wind rushed in,
but it was mixed with the smell of ozone and curing cement.

Nemo climbed out.

A sleek, electric tender boat was already detaching from the largest of
the grey ships. It sped across the choppy water, silent and stable. It
pulled right up to the submarine's hull.

A man stood on the bow. He was wearing a thick orange parka, jeans, and
boots that cost more than a mid-sized car. He didn't look like a head of
state. He looked like he was checking on a construction project.

It was E.

He hopped from the tender onto the slippery casing of the submarine with
a practiced ease, ignoring the armed sailors who had emerged behind
Nemo.

He tapped on the open steel hatch with a gloved knuckle. *Clang, clang.*

"I wondered when you'd get here," E shouted over the wind, flashing a
grin that was half-charming, half-terrifying. "I've been tracking your
thermal wake since you left the Med. You really need to upgrade your
reactor shielding, by the way. It's leaking neutrinos like a sieve."

Nemo stared at him. "You knew?"

"We all knew," E said, gesturing to the ships. "J is on the bridge of
the flagship. He's optimizing the supply chain for the hydroponics. We
figured you'd head for the point of inaccessibility. It's the poetic
choice."

He pointed to the rising structures on the platform.

"I took the liberty of building a place for you to stay. It's got
geothermal power, Starlink V2---uncensorable, obviously---and enough
food synthesizers to feed your crew for a century. It's a nice town.
Little chilly, but great views."

Nemo was speechless. He looked at the town. It was a sanctuary. A
retirement home for monsters.

"Why?" Nemo asked.

"Because you can't go back," E said simply. "You're the most wanted men
on Earth. If you step foot on soil, you'll be assassinated or put in a
black site. But here? In international waters, protected by my private
security fleet?"

E shrugged. "Here, you're just eccentric retirees."

Nemo felt a wave of exhaustion wash over him. A home. A real home, where
his men didn't have to sleep next to a torpedo.

"Thank you," Nemo said, his voice raspy.

"Don't thank me yet," E said. The smile didn't fade, but his eyes went
cold. Hard. The eyes of a man who moved markets with a tweet.

"There is a rental fee."

E patted the hull of the *Belgorod*.

"We're going to need you to turn over the submarine, I hope you
understand."

Nemo stiffened. Antonov stepped forward, his hand moving toward his
sidearm.

"The submarine is our guarantee," Nemo said slowly. "It is the reason
the world listens."

"The world listened because they were afraid," E corrected. "But you
fixed it, Nemo. The war is over. The debt is paid. The environment is
healing. The job is done."

E stepped closer, lowering his voice.

"But a weapon like this? A weapon that can rewrite geography? That can't
belong to one man. Not anymore. You're tired, Captain. I can see it. You
want to sleep? You want your men to see their families again? We can fly
them in. We can make this a paradise."

E held out his hand.

"But the gun stays with me. I'm the only one crazy enough to dismantle
it without blowing us all up."

Nemo looked at the town---the warm lights flickering on in the domes.
Then he looked at the cold, black metal of his ship. The ship that had
been his purgatory and his pulpit.

He looked at Antonov. His old friend looked at the town, then back at
Nemo, and gave a barely perceptible nod.

We are done, Valery.

Nemo turned back to E. He reached into his pocket and pulled out the red
launch key. The key that had held the world hostage.

"If you launch it," Nemo warned, "I will burn that town down around your
ears."

E took the key. He tossed it in the air and caught it.

"I'm a builder, Captain. Not a destroyer. I'm going to turn this reactor
into a power source for the colony. The missiles? We'll probably mine
them for the tungsten."

E gestured to the tender boat.

"Welcome to New Atlantis, gentlemen. Dinner is in an hour. We're having
steak. Real steak. Printed it myself."

Nemo took one last look at the open ocean, the grey sky, and the endless
horizon. The era of the sea monster was over.

"After you," Nemo said.

And for the first time in years, the Captain stepped off the ship, and
walked toward the light.
