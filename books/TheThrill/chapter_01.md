# Chapter 2: The Morning Run

## 6:00 AM - Central Park

The sun was just starting to bleed over the skyline, painting the grey city in temporary shades of pink and orange. It was the only part of the day Beth Sanders actually liked.

She was running. Not jogging. Running.

Her pace was a steady 6:00 minute mile. Her breathing was rhythmic, a metronome that counted down the seconds until her real life began.

*Inhale for two. Exhale for two.*

The park was mostly empty. A few other dedicated sadists were out, pounding the pavement before the city woke up and started screaming.

Beth scanned the shadows under the trees. She looked at the dark underpasses.

*Come on,* she thought. *Just one.*

She almost wished someone would try to mug her. Just a little bit. A guy with a knife? A desperate junkie? That would be something different. That would be a variable in the equation. She played out the scenario in her head as she ran.

*Attacker steps out. Left hook. Disarm. Knee to the solar plexus. Sprint.*

It was a pleasant fantasy. Better than thinking about the 9:00 AM budget reconciliation meeting with Gary.

She rounded a curve near the reservoir. Up ahead, a guy in expensive compression gear was stopped, stretching against a bench. He was flexing his calves, checking his reflection in a puddle.

Beth breezed past him. She didn't look at him. She was a ghost in motion.

Three minutes later, she completed the loop. The guy was running now, chugging along at a respectable pace.

Beth came up behind him. He heard her footsteps—light, rapid efficient strikes—and tried to speed up. The male ego is a fragile thing, especially at 6 AM.

It didn't matter. She passed him again. Lapped him.

He wheezed something that might have been "Good morning" or "Slow down," but Beth was already gone.

She hit the straightaway on the West Side. There was no one around. The path was clear for a quarter mile.

Beth checked her watch. Heart rate 145.

*Boring.*

She unlocked her stride. She opened the throttle. She went from a run to a sprint, her legs eating up the distance, her lungs burning with the good, clean fire of exertion. For ten seconds, she wasn't Beth the Compliance Officer. She was just speed.

She slowed down as she reached the park exit. The high died instantly, replaced by the heavy, suffocating reality of the coming day.

She walked the last block to her apartment, cooling down. This was the start of her day. The peak.

And it went all downhill from here.
