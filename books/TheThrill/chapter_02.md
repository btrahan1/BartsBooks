# Chapter 2: The Mask

The office of *Interstate Logistics* was kept at a constant, sensible 72 degrees. The carpet was a sensible beige. The people were sensible, beige people.

Beth sat at her desk, which was located in the exact center of the "Compliance Pod." It was a tactical position she had chosen carefully—hidden in plain sight, far enough from the manager's office to avoid casual oversight, but close enough to the breakroom to monitor foot traffic.

She was currently auditing the *Q3 Heavy Machinery Safety Logs*.

It was mind-numbing. It was soul-crushing. It was perfect camouflage.

To her left, Gary and Mike were discussing fantasy football. To her right, Susan was complaining about her cat's dietary restrictions. The noise was a constant drone, like a beehive on sedatives.

Beth tuned it out. She had developed the ability to operate on two frequencies. Frequency A was the work: checking boxes, flagging minor infractions, ensuring the company didn't get sued. Frequency B was the simulation: *If shooter enters from North Exit, slide under desk, kick chair at gunman, roll to South Exit.*

She tapped her keycard on the desk rhythmically. *Tap. Tap. Tap.*

It was 4:55 PM. The migration was beginning.

Chairs squeaked. Computers chimed off. The collective sigh of the American workforce preparing to numb itself with ethanol.

"Hey BB!"

Mike leaned over her cubicle wall. He was wearing a polo shirt that was one size too tight, revealing a softness that betrayed a life of sitting.

"We're heading over to *McFadden's* for cheap margs. You wanna come?"

BB.

*Boring Beth.*

They thought she didn't know. They thought it was affectionate. It wasn't. It was their way of categorizing her. To them, she was the human equivalent of unbuttered toast. Reliable. Necessary. But utterly flavorless.

Beth looked up. She adjusted her glasses (which had zero prescription). She gave them the smile she practiced in the mirror—the shy, slightly awkward smile of a woman whose biggest thrill was a BOGO sale at The Container Store.

"Oh, thanks Mike," she said, her voice soft. "But I can't tonight. Yoga."

Mike chuckled. "Yoga again? You're obsessed, girl."

Gary walked by, loosening his tie. "Yeah, maybe y'all should join me sometime," he joked, winking at the group.

The group laughed. The idea of Boring Beth doing anything physical was inherently funny to them. They imagined her doing gentle stretches on a purple mat, listening to whale sounds.

"Yeah, maybe when I turn 60," Susan cracked.

They laughed again as they headed for the elevator.

"Have fun!" Beth called out.

She waited until the elevator doors dinged and closed. The office was silent.

The smile vanished. Her face went slack, cold, and predatory.

She looked at the clock. 5:00 PM.

It wasn't "Yoga." technically. It was *Advanced Vinyasa III* at the underground studio in Tribeca. The instructor was a former Spetsnaz operative named Dmitri who made grown men cry during the warmup.

It was the most advanced class in the city.

And for Beth, it was just the evening warmup.
