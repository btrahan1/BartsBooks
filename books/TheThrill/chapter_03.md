# Chapter 3: The Pretraining

The studio had no sign. It was a concrete basement in Tribeca that smelled of incense and old sweat.

There were only six mats.

Dmitri walked among them like a prison warden. He was a slab of muscle carved from Siberian granite, and he didn't believe in "namaste." He believed in suffering.

"Hold Crow Pose," Dmitri barked. "One minute. Do not shake. Shaking is weakness leaving the body. Keep the weakness inside. Let it burn you."

Beth—known here as Marcy—shifted her weight onto her palms. Her knees tucked into her armpits. Her feet lifted off the mat.

To the untrained eye, it was yoga. To Beth, it was structural engineering.

*Center of gravity: low. Core engagement: 80%. Breath: rhythmic.*

The guy next to her, a hedge fund manager named Todd who paid $500 an hour for this torture, was vibrating like a washing machine. His face was purple. A drop of sweat hit his mat with a splat.

Beth was still. Perfectly, terrifyingly still.

She felt the stretch in her triceps. She felt the load on her wrists. It wasn't difficult. It was just... maintenance. Like taking a car out for a highway run to keep the engine clean.

"Good, Todd," Dmitri lied. "Marcy... lower. More compression."

Beth lowered her center of gravity another inch. She extended one leg back, turning the Crow into a One-Legged Crane. It was a move that required ridiculous core strength and balance.

Dmitri stopped pacing. He looked at her. He knew "Marcy" was holding back. He had seen her do handstand pushups when she thought he wasn't looking.

"Show off," Todd wheezed, collapsing onto his face.

Beth flowed back into a seated position as the timer beeped. She wasn't even breathing hard. Her heart rate had barely touched 100.

"Class dismissed," Dmitri grunted. "Hydrate or die."

Beth gathered her mat. She wiped a non-existent bead of sweat from her forehead—a bit of theater for Todd's benefit.

"Tough one today, Dmitri," she said, keeping her voice light.

Dmitri just grunted. He watched her walk to the changing room. He knew lions when he saw them, even if they pretended to be house cats.

Five minutes later, Beth walked out of the studio back onto the street. The sun had set. The city was dark.

She checked her watch. 7:15 PM.

The warm-up was complete. Her muscles were loose. Her joints were lubricated. Her mind was clear of the "Compliance Pod" static.

She looked up at the fire escape of the building across the alley. It was rusted, high, and theoretically inaccessible from the ground.

Beth smiled. The mask of "BB" dissolved completely.

"And now," she whispered to the shadows. "Some Parkour."
