# Chapter 4: The Research

The thing about New York City was that there were freaks everywhere.

You could wear a tutu and combat boots on the subway, and people wouldn't look up from their phones. You could scream at a pigeon, and they would just walk around you. As long as you weren't actively knifing or shooting someone, you were background noise.

Beth counted on this apathy. It was her cloak.

She moved across the rooftops of SoHo like a shadow with a pulse.

Parkour? Tons of people did it. You could find a hundred YouTube channels of kids jumping off picnic tables in the suburbs. But few did it the way Beth did.

For her, it wasn't about the views. It was about the variables.

*Gap: 8 feet. Drop: 12 feet. Surface: Wet brick. Grip: Low.*

She sprinted toward the edge of an old textile factory. She didn't slow down. To slow down was to doubt, and doubt was gravity's best friend.

She launched.

For a split second, she was flying. The air whipped past her face, cold and biting. The street below was a canyon of yellow cabs and tourists, completely unaware that a woman was soaring forty feet above their heads.

*Impact.*

She landed on the adjacent fire escape railing—a target two inches wide. She absorbed the shock with her legs, collapsing into a crouch, then instantly exploding upward to grab the ladder above.

It was different every night. A new route. A new puzzle.

And if it wasn't dangerous, it wasn't worth doing.

Safety was for Gary. Safety was for people who checked the font size on compliance reports. Beth needed the edge. She needed to feel the proximity of death to remember that she was alive.

She ran along the parapet of a brownstone, vaulting over a chimney stack. She planted her hand for a side-flip, her body rotating in a tight, controlled arc.

Mid-flip, the world inverted. Sky, street, brick, sky.

Her eye caught a glint of metal on the street level below.

She landed the flip and rolled, coming up smoothly against a water tower. She peered over the edge.

It was a Jewelry Store. Not a chain store, but something older. The storefront was a fortress. The steel gate was down—heavy gauge mesh. The windows behind it were barred with iron.

Most people would see a secure building.

Beth saw a challenge.

*Gate: Rolling steel. Mechanism likely internal. Weak point: Guide rails.*
*Windows: Iron. Spacing: 4 inches. Too tight for entry.*
*Roof access: HVAC unit?*

"Hmmm..." she thought, her pulse thumping a happy rhythm against her neck.

She wiped a smudge of soot from her cheek. The boredom of the Compliance Pod felt a million miles away.

"I think I'm going to do some research later tonight!"
