# Chapter 6: The Stakeout

Patience was a muscle. Most people let it atrophy. They fidgeted in lines, checked their phones during lulls in conversation, honked at red lights.

Beth Sanders sat in a corner booth of the *Midnight Espresso* diner across the street from *Mendel & Sons*, nursing a coffee that had gone cold forty minutes ago. She was a statue.

To the waitress, she was just another insomniac working on a laptop. But the laptop screen was dim, and Beth wasn’t looking at it. Her reflection in the dark glass allowed her to watch the street behind her without turning her head.

10:45 PM. The cleaning crew left. Two women, one heavy-set man. They locked the front gate.

11:00 PM. The private patrol car rolled by. *SecurCorp*. They didn't stop, just shined a spotlight on the front door and kept moving. Predictable. Lazy.

11:15 PM. The lights in the second-floor apartment above the shop went out. Old man Mendel. He was a creature of habit.

Beth took a small sip of the acrid coffee. She wasn't just watching; she was feeling the rhythm of the block. The traffic light timing (45 seconds green, 30 red). The gait of the pedestrians (brisk, heads down—nobody looking up). The shadows stretched and contracted with the passing cars.

She ran the simulation in her mind again.

*Entry: Ventilation shaft, rear alley. Cover: HVAC noise creates auditory masking. Obstacle 1: Pressure sensor on the duct floor. Solution: Bridging struts. Obstacle 2: Camera in the hallway. Solution: The looped feed.*

She knew the building better than the people who lived in it. She knew the tensile strength of the iron bars and the model number of the motion detectors.

She checked her watch. 11:30 PM.

The street settled into a low hum. The variable density was dropping continuously.

It was time.

She closed her laptop, left a five-dollar tip on the table, and walked out into the cool night air. She didn't look at the jewelry store. She didn't need to. It was already etched into her cortex.

***

Back in her apartment, the ritual began.

This was the transformation. The shedding of the skin.

She stripped off the beige cardigan, the sensible slacks, the comfortable flats—the uniform of the invisible woman. She folded them neatly and placed them on the chair. Goodbye, Elizabeth.

She opened the bottom drawer of her wardrobe. It had a false bottom, revealing a hardened case.

She clicked the latches open.

First, the base layer. A matte black bodysuit, thermal-regulating and abrasion-resistant. It fit like a second skin, tight enough to prevent snagging but flexible enough for full range of motion. No loose fabric. No sound.

Next, the harness. Lightweight climbing webbing, modified to be low-profile. She tightened the buckles at her thighs and waist. *Click. Click.* The sound was satisfyingly final.

She pulled on the boots. Split-toe *Tabi* style for grip, with soles made of silent, high-friction rubber. She laces them tight, feeling the support around her ankles.

Then, the tools.

The lockpick set went into a pouch on her left forearm. Tension wrenches, rakes, diamonds. The "Digital Key"—the Raspberry Pi responding unit she’d coded—slid into a shielded pocket at the small of her back.

The glass cutter. The suction cups. The slim-jim.

Each item had a place. Muscle memory would find them in the dark.

She stood before the full-length mirror.

The woman looking back wasn't the Compliance Auditor. That woman was small, quiet, apologetic.

This woman was dangerous.

The black fabric absorbed the light. Her silhouette was sharp, angular. She looked like a shadow that had decided to stand up and walk.

She picked up the final piece: a thin balaclava and a pair of customized goggles with heads-up display integration.

She pulled the mask over her head.

The world narrowed. Her peripheral vision was cut off, forcing focus. Her breathing sounded louder in her ears. *In. Out. In. Out.*

She checked the time on her wrist unit. 01:00 AM.

The city was asleep. But the game was just beginning.

Beth walked to her window and slid it open. The fire escape beckoned, a skeletal iron finger pointing up into the night.

She didn't look back at the apartment. There was nothing there for her right now.

She stepped out onto the metal grate, the cold wind greeting her like an old friend.

"Let's go to work," she whispered.

She vaulted over the railing and disappeared into the dark.
