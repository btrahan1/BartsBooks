# Chapter 10: The Poke

The next eight hours were a study in gray.

Beth sat in Pod 4B, staring at a spreadsheet that detailed the depreciation schedules of forklift tires. Usually, this level of monotony was painful. Today, it was a disguise.

"Hey, Beth? Did you see the email about the refrigerator policy?"

Gary, from Accounting. He was wearing a beige polo shirt that matched his beige personality.

Beth looked up, adjusting her glasses. She summoned her most vacuous smile. "Oh, was that the one about labeling the yogurt? I must have missed it. I've been so focused on these tire audits."

Gary nodded solemnly. "It's important, Beth. Chaos in the breakroom leads to chaos in the ledger."

"You're so right, Gary."

She went back to typing. To Gary, she was a diligent drone. In her head, she was replaying the moment she cut the glass at Mendel’s. The sound it made. *Scritch. Pop.*

The duality was delicious.

***

By 7:00 PM, the mask was off.

Beth was on the roof of her own building. The sun had set, painting the sky in bruises of purple and orange.

She moved through a Vinyasa flow, her breathing steady and deep. Downward Dog. Plank. Cobra. Her muscles were tight from the adrenaline dump of the previous night, and the stretching felt like snapping rubber bands back into place.

Transition.

From Cobra, she didn't just stand up. She exploded up, tucking her knees and landing in a precision crouch on the AC unit six feet away.

Static to kinetic.

She ran the length of the roof, vaulted the parapet, caught herself on the fire escape of the adjacent building, and swung down.

Parkour wasn't just exercise. It was calibration. It was reminding her body that it was an instrument, not a vessel for sitting in chairs.

She did this for an hour, tracing a loop through the neighborhood—never touching the sidewalk, never being seen. Just a ghost in the machine.

***

Back inside. 9:00 PM.

Laptop open. Linux kernel loaded. Tor browser active.

Beth dove into the subterranean ocean of the internet. She navigated to the message boards where the "real" people talked. Not the script kiddies or the politicians, but the operators.

She scanned the local New York boards. Usually, it was just drug pricing and counterfeit handbags.

Tonight, it was buzzing.

*> User: RedBear*
*> Message: Looking for info on a break-in at W. Broadway. Clean entry. Tech involved. Paying heavy for a name.*

*> User: Slick_Nick*
*> Reply: I heard Volkov is tearing the walls down. Says someone insulted the family.*

*> User: RedBear*
*> Reply: Not an insult. A declaration. If you know who sold the tech, speak up. Or we start breaking fingers.*

Beth leaned back in her chair, the blue light washing over her face.

"Volkov," she whispered.

She knew the name. Viktor Volkov. rumored head of the 'Odessa Syndicate.' They ran the ports. They ran the unions. They killed people who looked at them wrong.

And she had just walked into his piggy bank and turned it into a church donation.

A normal person would be terrified. A normal person would be booking a flight to Nebraska right now.

Beth felt a smile spread across her face. It started small and grew until she was grinning like a shark.

"Perfect!"

She laughed, a sharp, delighted sound in the quiet apartment.

"They are pissed now? Hah!"

She cracked her knuckles and leaned forward. The spreadsheet of forklift tires was a universe away.

"Let's take a deeper look into these so-called Mobsters," she murmured.

Her fingers began to fly.

*Query: Viktor Volkov. Associates. Holdings. Shell companies. Weaknesses.*

She wasn't running away. She was digging in.

***

But playing required a player. A new persona. "BB" was a shadow. "BB" was a black box. If she was going to poke the bear, she needed a shape. She needed a voice.

She needed to not just be a ghost, but a haunt.

She opened a terminal window to generate a new key pair. A fresh identity, untraceable to "BB" or to Elizabeth Sanders.

"Hmm... I need a name," she muttered.

She looked at the screen, at the code she had written to mimic the alarm system. She had recorded their own signal and played it back to them. Using their own voice to fool them.

"Mocking Bird," she whispered.

It was perfect. A creature that didn't have its own song, but stole everyone else's. And it sang loud.

She typed it in. *User: Mocking_Bird*.

Her finger hovered over the Enter key.

This was dangerous. This was objectively, distinctively stupid. Poking Viktor Volkov was one thing; sending him an engraved invitation was another. It was kicking a hornet's nest while covered in honey.

Her heart rate spiked. *Thump. Thump. Thump.*

"This is so not a good idea," she said aloud.

She could close the laptop. She could go to bed. She could go back to forklift tire depreciation schedules tomorrow and be safe.

She grinned.

"Do it."

She hit Enter. The identity was live.

She navigated back to the thread where *RedBear* was threatening to break fingers. She opened a private message channel.

She typed quickly, before common sense could intervene.

*> To: RedBear*
*> From: Mocking_Bird*
*> Message: Tell Viktor that the Mocking Bird has just begun to sing!*

She hit Send.

The distinct *woosh* of the encrypted packet leaving her system sounded like the starting gun of a race.

Beth leaned back, lacing her fingers behind her head. The thrill wasn't just a buzz anymore. It was a roar.
