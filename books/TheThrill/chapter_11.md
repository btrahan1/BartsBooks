# Chapter 11: The Survey

Lunch break. 12:45 PM.

Beth walked down 11th Avenue, a half-eaten turkey sandwich in one hand and her phone in the other. To the casual observer, she was just another office worker getting some air, perhaps looking for a place to buy a smoothie.

She stopped in front of a massive glass facade. *Volkov Prestige Imports.*

It was a temple of excess. The showroom floor was polished concrete, gleaming under museum-quality lighting. Inside, the cars were arranged like sculptures.

A Rosso Corsa Ferrari 488 Pista.
A matte gray Lamborghini Aventador.
A vintage Porsche 911 Turbo, the "Widowmaker," in pristine black.

Beth took a bite of her sandwich, chewing slowly.

She wasn't looking at the curves of the fenders. She was looking at the reflections in the paint.

*Camera 1: High corner, wide angle. Blind spot: directly underneath.*
*Camera 2: Rotating dome, scanning the entrance. 15-second sweep.*
*Motion sensors: Infrared beams across the display floor. Knee height.*

She tapped her phone screen, pretending to text. In reality, she was logging the Wi-Fi networks.

*SSID: VPI_Guest.* Open.
*SSID: VPI_Secure.* WPA3 encryption.

"Strong," she murmured. "But not invincible."

A salesman in a suit that cost more than her car walked past the glass, adjusting his tie. He glanced at her—a woman in a cardigan eating a sandwich—and dismissed her instantly. He turned his back.

Mistake.

Beth’s eyes drifted to the back of the showroom. There, behind a velvet rope, sat something truly special.

It was an Aston Martin DB5. Silver birch. The Bond car. It was classic, elegant, and undoubtedly the pride and joy of the collection.

It was also Volkov's personal trophy. Her research last night had confirmed it. He didn't sell the classics; he hoarded them.

Beth felt that familiar spark, the electric hum that started at the base of her spine.

Stealing a diamond was one thing. A diamond fits in a pocket.

Stealing a car? That was logistics. That was theater. That was driving a two-ton middle finger right out the front door.

She watched the way the light played off the chrome bumper.

"Hello, my pretties," she whispered, her voice low and sweet.

She finished her sandwich, crumpled the wrapper, and tossed it into a nearby bin.

She had the layout. She had the camera angles. She had the target.

Tonight, the Mocking Bird wouldn't just sing. She was going to fly.
