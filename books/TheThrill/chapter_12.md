# Chapter 12: The Delivery

The police scanner app on her phone was usually just background noise, a rhythmic drone of domestic disputes and traffic stops.

But tonight, a name popped out of the static.

*"...dispatch, tell Detective Thorne that the Mendel report is filed..."*

Beth froze, her fingers hovering over her keyboard.

*Thorne.*

She typed the name into the NYPD public directory. **Detective Jack Thorne. Property Crimes.**

A few clicks later, she was looking at a low-res photo from a departmental newsletter. He looked tired. He looked messy. He looked smart.

"So, you're the one looking for the angel," she mused.

Most cops would have closed the Mendel case the moment the owners said nothing was taken. But Thorne was still filing reports. He was digging.

A slow smile spread across her face. This complicated things. This made it richer.

If she poked Volkov, he would just send thugs. But if she poked Thorne... he would bring the law.

And if she could tie them together?

She looked at the blueprints for *Volkov Prestige Imports*. She looked at the photo of the silver Aston Martin DB5.

"Parking that car in front of his office and disappearing would be a stupid gamble," she whispered.

It was reckless. It was arrogant. It was guaranteed to make her the most wanted woman in the city by both the Mob and the NYPD.

"Perfect!"

***

Three hours later.

The glass of the showroom door didn't shatter; it just ceased to be an obstacle. Beth had used a localized frequency emitter to disrupt the magnetic locks for exactly four seconds—long enough to slide inside.

The air smelled of leather and wax.

She approached the DB5. It was beautiful. A piece of art.

Volkov probably kept the keys in a safe, but these old cars didn't need keys if you knew how to whisper to the ignition.

She slid into the driver's seat. It felt like sitting in a history book.

She hotwired it in thirty seconds. The engine roared to life—a guttural, throaty growl that echoed off the concrete walls.

The alarm started screaming instantly.

"Music," Beth said.

She shifted into gear. She didn't bother with the door this time. She drove the two-million-dollar car right through the plate glass window.

*CRASH.*

Shards of glass rained down like diamonds. The Aston Martin hit the street, tires squealing, and rocketed down 11th Avenue.

***

The drive was blurred neon. Beth felt the vibration of the engine in her chest. She was exposed. She was driving a stolen beacon of wealth through the heart of the city.

Every cop car she passed was a heart attack. Every red light was a dare.

She pulled up to the 19th Precinct. It was 3:00 AM. The street was quiet, save for a few patrol cars.

She parked the Aston Martin directly in the "Reserved for Police Vehicles" zone, right in front of the main entrance.

She left the engine running.

She popped the trunk. Inside, she had left a care package: the hard drives from the dealership's security system, showing exactly who owned the "legitimate" business, and a single, perfect white feather.

A mocking bird's calling card.

She stepped out of the car, pulling her hood up. She walked away, not running, just a pedestrian in the night.

She heard the door of the precinct open behind her.

"Hey! You can't park that there!"

She didn't turn around. She just raised a hand in a lazy wave and rounded the corner, vanishing into the shadows.

Her heart was beating so hard she thought it might break her ribs.

It was the stupidest thing she had ever done.

Note to self: Do it again.
