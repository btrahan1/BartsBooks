# Chapter 13: The Aftershocks

The 19th Precinct was usually a place of controlled chaos. Drunks singing in the holding cell, detectives arguing over coffee, phones ringing.

This morning, it was just chaos.

Detective Jack Thorne walked up the front steps and stopped. He couldn't get in. The entrance was blocked by a circle of patrol officers, two bomb squad techs, and the Captain, who looked like he was about to have an aneurysm.

And in the center of it all, gleaming in the morning sun like a diamond in a coal chute, was a silver Aston Martin DB5.

"Thorne!" CaptainMiller yelled, spotting him. "Is this your idea of a joke?"

Jack squeezed through the line. "Captain? I drive a ten-year-old Honda."

"It's parked in your spot. And it's running."

Jack walked up to the car. It was beautiful. It was a masterpiece of engineering. And it was clearly stolen.

He leaned in through the driver's side window. The leather seats were pristine. On the passenger seat, there was a stack of black external hard drives.

And resting on top of them, white and delicate, was a single feather.

Jack picked up the feather. A mocking bird.

"Bomb squad says it's clean," Miller barked. "Keys are in the ignition."

Jack looked at the hard drives. "I don't think it's a bomb, Captain. I think it's a tip."

He looked around. The street was filled with gawkers taking photos. The press would be here in ten minutes.

"Get this car into the impound garage inside," Jack ordered. "Now. Before anyone sees the plates."

He recognized the car. He had seen it in a magazine about "The King of Brighton Beach."

"Volkov," Jack whispered.

The thief hadn't just stolen a car. They had started a war.

***

Viktor Volkov did not scream. Screaming was for people who had no power.

He stood in his kitchen, wearing a silk robe, staring at the phone in his hand.

"Repeat that," he said softly.

"The police have the car, sir," the voice on the other end said. "It's at the 19th Precinct. It was... dropped off."

"Dropped off."

"Yes, sir. And reports say there were... items inside. Drives."

Viktor ended the call. He placed the phone gently on the marble counter.

Then he picked up the heavy crystal vase of flowers next to it and threw it into the wall with such force that it pulverized the plaster.

The crash was satisfying, but brief.

The Mocking Bird.

He had underestimated the threat. He thought it was a rival gang. He thought it was a business dispute.

It wasn't. It was someone playing a game.

"You want to sing?" Viktor hissed, his eyes cold and dead. "I will cut out your tongue."

***

Beth stood in the center of her living room.

She hadn't slept. She didn't need to.

The morning light was streaming through the windows, illuminating the dust motes dancing in the air.

Usually, the apartment felt like a cage. It was a box where she stored herself between shifts at the office.

Today, it felt like a command center.

She walked to the window and looked out at the city. Somewhere out there, Jack Thorne was analyzing the hard drives. Somewhere out there, Viktor Volkov was planning her murder.

She should be terrified. She should be trembling.

Instead, she started to laugh.

It bubbled up from her stomach, a pure, uninhibited sound that filled the room. She spun around, her arms wide, embracing the empty space.

Her heart was pounding. Her senses were dialed up to eleven. The smell of the coffee, the rough texture of the rug under her bare feet, the sound of the traffic outside—it was all electric.

She realized then that for thirty years, she hadn't been living. She had been existing. She had been a ghost haunting her own life.

She grinned, a wild, dangerous expression that Boring Beth would never recognize in a mirror.

"So THIS is what it feels like to be alive!" she shouted to the walls.

And the best part?

She was just getting started.
