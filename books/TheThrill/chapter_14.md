# Chapter 14: The Encounter

If "Boring Beth" was beige, and "The Mocking Bird" was black, this new woman was red.

Beth stood in front of her mirror, applying a layer of crimson lipstick. She had swapped her contacts for a pair of violet-tinted ones. She wore a wig—a sharp, asymmetrical bob in platinum blonde.

She wasn't the invisible office worker. She wasn't the tactical ghost.

She was "Veronica."

Veronica was an art dealer. Or maybe a freelance photojournalist. Someone who lived out of a suitcase and saw the world in high definition. Veronica didn't apologize. Veronica took up space.

Beth checked the time. 10:30 PM.

Her intel said Jack Thorne ended his shifts at *O’Shaughnessy’s*, a dive bar three blocks from the precinct that smelled of sawdust and regret.

"Time for a drink," she said, her voice dropping an octave, adding a hint of a smoky rasp.

***

The bar was dark, the air thick with the low hum of conversation and the clack of pool balls.

Beth spotted him immediately.

Jack Thorne was sitting at the far end of the bar, hunched over a bottle of beer. He looked like he had been awake for three days. His tie was loosened, his sleeves rolled up. He was staring at the condensation on the glass as if it contained the secrets of the universe.

He looked rugged. Damaged. Interesting.

Beth felt that familiar spike of adrenaline. This was dangerous. This was walking into the lion's den and asking to pet the cat.

She walked over and slid onto the stool next to him. Not too close, but close enough to invade his personal space just a fraction.

"You look like you’re trying to telepathically explode that beer," she said.

Jack blinked, pulling himself out of his thoughts. He turned to look at her. His eyes were tired, dark, and intelligent. He scanned her—the hair, the lips, the leather jacket.

"It's been a long day," he grunted. "Just trying to remember what silence sounds like."

"I'm Veronica," she said, extending a hand.

He hesitated, then took it. His grip was firm, his skin rough. "Jack."

"Well, Jack. You look like a man who has problems." She signaled the bartender. "Whiskey. Neat."

Jack smirked, a crooked, weary thing. "Problems is one word for it. Disasters is another."

"woman trouble?" Beth asked, taking a sip of the whiskey the bartender slid toward her. The burn was pleasant.

"In a way," Jack muttered. He took a pull of his beer. "I met someone recently. Someone... complicated."

Beth’s heart hammered against her ribs. *He's talking about me.*

"Complicated is good," she said, leaning in, resting her chin on her hand. "Simple is boring. Simple is for people who like spreadsheets."

Jack laughed, a short, sharp bark. "You might be right. But this one... she's dangerous. She's playing a game, and I'm not sure if I'm the player or the pawn."

" maybe you're the prize," Beth suggested.

Jack looked at her then, really looked at her. For a second, Beth thought he saw through the wig, the contacts, the attitude. She held his gaze, willing her pulse to stay steady.

"Maybe," he said softly. "Or maybe I'm just the next target."

He looked back at his beer. "She left me a gift today. A very expensive, very illegal gift."

"Sounds like she likes you."

"Sounds like she's crazy."

"The best ones usually are," Beth said. She finished her drink in one smooth motion and slammed the glass down.

"You keep chasing her, Jack," she said, standing up. "She sounds like she's worth the trouble."

Jack looked up, surprised by the sudden departure. "Leaving already?"

"I have a plane to catch," Veronica lied smoothly. "Paris in the morning."

She leaned in close, whispering near his ear. She smelled of expensive perfume and ozone.

"Don't let the bad guys win, Detective."

She pulled back, flashed him a dazzling smile, and walked away. She felt his eyes on her back every step of the way to the door.

She stepped out into the cool night air and inhaled deeply.

She had looked him in the eye. She had touched his hand. She had toasted to her own crime spree.

"You have no idea, Jack," she whispered to the closed door. "You have absolutely no idea."
