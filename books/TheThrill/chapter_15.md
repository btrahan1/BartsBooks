# Chapter 15: The Obsession

Jack Thorne’s apartment was silent, save for the hum of the refrigerator and the distant siren wail of the city.

He sat on the edge of his bed, the unfinished case file on his lap, but he wasn't reading it.

He was seeing violet eyes.

"Veronica."

He said the name out loud, testing the weight of it. It felt fake. It felt like a stage name.

He stood up and paced the small room, scrubbing a hand over his tired face.

*She walked into a cop bar. She sat next to the lead detective on the most high-profile case in the city. She made a joke about 'woman trouble'.*

It was impossible.

Criminals didn't do that. Criminals hid in shadows. They didn't wear red leather jackets and smell like expensive ozone.

"No thief is that arrogant," Jack muttered.

But then he remembered the car.

The Aston Martin parked in his reserved spot. The feather on the seat.

*Yes. This thief is exactly that arrogant.*

He replayed the conversation for the twentieth time.

*"You look like a man who has problems."*
*"Don't let the bad guys win, Detective."*

She knew who he was. Of course, he was a cop in a cop bar—that wasn't hard to deduce. But the way she looked at him... like she was waiting for him to get the joke.

He walked to the window.

If "Veronica" was the Mocking Bird, then she was insane. She was playing Russian Roulette with a fully loaded gun.

But if she *was* the Mocking Bird... she was also the most fascinating creature he had ever encountered.

He checked his watch. 2:00 AM.

He knew he should be sleeping. He had a briefing with the Captain in six hours about the Volkov fallout.

Address the logic.

*Fact 1: The thief is a ghost. High tech, low profile.*
*Fact 2: Veronica was loud. High visibility, high engagement.*

They were opposites.

"Except," Jack thought, "maybe the ghost wants to be seen."

He lay back on the bed, staring at the cracked plaster of the ceiling.

He couldn't prove it. He had no prints (she wore gloves? No, she didn't. He had felt her skin. Fuck, he missed a chance to get prints from the glass). He had no real name. Just a destination: Paris. Which was definitely a lie.

He closed his eyes.

The smell of her perfume seemed to linger in the room, cutting through the scent of old paper and coffee.

"I'm going to find you, Veronica," he whispered into the dark. "And when I do, I'm going to ask for a refund on that drink."

Jack Thorne drifted into a restless sleep, chasing a woman who was already gone.
