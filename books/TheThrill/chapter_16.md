# Chapter 16: The Pivot

Beth peeled the platinum wig off her head and tossed it onto the sofa. The violet contact lenses followed, floating in their case like tiny, alien irises.

She stared at herself in the bathroom mirror. Her own eyes—brown, intelligent, currently manic—stared back.

"Well, that was stupid," she said.

She washed the crimson lipstick off, watching the red swirl down the drain like blood.

"Objectively, tactically, suicidally stupid."

A slow smile crept onto her face, widening until she looked like the Cheshire Cat.

"But god, it was fun."

She felt a vibration in her chest that had nothing to do with her heart rate. It was the hum of the game. She had touched the flame and hadn't been burned.

She walked back into the living room, pouring herself a glass of water. Her mind was racing.

Thorne was smart. She had seen it in his eyes. He was putting the pieces together. And Volkov? Volkov was undoubtedly tearing the city apart looking for anyone who had bought high-end hacking tech in the last six months.

If she hit Volkov again, she was walking into a trap. If she went quiet, she was boring.

"Let's not make this too easy," she murmured, sitting down at her desk. "Let's mix it up a bit."

She woke her computer. The screens glowed to life, a comforting wall of data.

"The Mob was so 'yesterday'," she said, dismissing the folder labeled *ODESSA_SYNDICATE*. "Everyone expects the thief to rob the robbers. It's poetic, but it's predictable."

She cracked her knuckles.

"How about greedy politicians?"

She navigated to a different corner of the Dark Web. Not the marketplaces, but the leak sites. The places where disgruntled aides and hacked servers dumped their dirty laundry.

*Query: NYC Council. Kickbacks. Zoning Commission.*

The results scrolled past. Most of it was boring corruption—nepotism, padded expense accounts.

Then she found it.

*Councilman Robert Sterling.* The man in charge of the Manhattan Redevelopment Fund.

Detailed ledgers. offshore accounts in the Caymans. Photos of meetings with developers in places that definitely weren't City Hall.

He wasn't just skimming off the top; he was stealing the whole bucket. And he was keeping the evidence in a private server in his penthouse on Park Avenue. A server that was supposedly "air-gapped" and secure.

Beth laughed. "Air-gapped just means I haven't flown there yet."

This was different. This wasn't about physical danger like the Mob. This was about intellectual dominance. This was about exposing a hypocrite.

"Oh yes," she whispered, her fingers dancing over the keys. "Come to me, Dark Web."

She pulled up the schematics for Sterling's building. Penthouse security. Biometric lifts.

It was a fortress of glass and steel.

"Perfect."

She leaned back, the blue light reflecting in her eyes. The game wasn't just about stealing anymore. It was about singing a song that the whole city would hear.

"The Mocking Bird has come to play!"
