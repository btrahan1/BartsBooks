# Chapter 17: The Sterling Plan

The blueprints for the penthouse at 740 Park Avenue were impressive. Private elevator, heated floors, a panic room.

But Beth wasn't looking at the real estate porn. She was looking at the contractor invoices she had scraped from a hacked server at *Metropolitan Secure Storage*.

"Well, would you look at that," she whispered.

Three months ago—right around the time the Zoning Commission approved the controversial riverfront casino project—Councilman Sterling had ordered a *Titanium-Vanguard Series 4* wall safe installed in his master study.

"A Vanguard 4," Beth mused. "That's overkill for tax returns, Robert. That's for diamonds, gold bars, or unredacted ledgers."

She zoomed in on the specs. It was biometric and digital, with a mechanical failsafe. Tricky. Fun.

She swiveled her chair to the other monitor, where a list of local non-profits was open.

"If the rumors are true, that safe is going to be heavy," she said. "And the *City Animal Rescue* needs a new HVAC system. The *Harlem Arts Initiative* just lost their grant funding. And the *Lower East Side Food Pantry* is always running low."

She typed the names into a text file labeled *DISTRIBUTION.txt*.

"So many charities, so little time," she smiled. "Consider it a redistribution of wealth from the corrupt to the cute and fuzzy."

She turned back to the safe schematics. The money was for the world. But she needed something for herself. A memento.

She scrolled through the insurance photos of the apartment. Sterling had a taste for expensive, tacky art. But on his desk, there was a paperweight. A solid crystal globe with a gold base. It was heavy, useless, and ostentatious.

"I'll take the world," she decided. "Literally."

Then she paused. Her eyes drifted to the folder on her desktop labeled *THORNE*.

It hadn't been updated since her meeting with him. Since she looked into his tired eyes and lied to his face.

"We absolutely have to give a gift to Detective Thorne, don't we?" she murmured.

He was chasing ghosts. He needed something solid.

If she cracked that safe, she wouldn't just find cash. She would find the dirt. The proof of the bribes. The log of the offshore accounts.

The police couldn't get a warrant for the safe without probable cause. But if the safe just happened to be *opened* by a third party, and the contents just happened to be left on the Councilman's desk...

"Plain view doctrine," she said, remembering her legal research. " admissible evidence."

She grinned.

She would steal the cash for the orphans. She would steal the globe for her shelf.

And she would steal the Councilman's freedom for Jack.

"Happy hunting, Detective," she whispered, and began to code the bypass algorithm.
