# Chapter 18: The Rival

The wind at sixty stories up was a living thing. It tore at Beth’s suit, trying to peel her off the side of the building.

She engaged the suction grips on her gloves. *Thwump. Thwump.*

She was a spider on a wall of glass. Below her, the city was a grid of light. Above her, the penthouse terrace of Councilman Sterling.

She reached the ledge and pulled herself over. The terrace was silent.

The glass doors were bulletproof, but the electronic lock was standard issue. Beth bypassed it in ten seconds.

Inside, the apartment smelled of lemon polish and greed.

She moved directly to the study. The safe was behind a faux-Rembrandt.

"Hello, Vanguard 4," she whispered.

She connected her decoder to the biometric port. The screen flashed numbers, cycling through permutations.

*Beep. Green light.*

The heavy door swung open.

It was packed. Stacks of unmarked bills. A ledger bound in red leather. A hard drive labeled *Riverfront Project*.

Beth worked fast. The cash went into her pack. The hard drive and ledger went into a separate, padded envelope labeled **ATTN: DET. THORNE.**

She placed the envelope on the mahogany desk, right next to the Councilman’s unfinished scotch.

Then she grabbed the crystal globe.

"Yoink."

She was out the way she came, descending into the night before the first alarm cycle could complete.

***

2:00 AM. Queens.

The *Safe Haven Animal Shelter* was a crumbling brick building on a quiet street.

Beth dropped a heavy bundle of cash through the mail slot. That was the third drop of the night. The artists in Harlem and the food pantry in the Lower East Side had already received their dividends.

She felt light. She felt invincible.

She turned to leave, heading for the alleyway shortcut she had scouted earlier.

She stopped.

The alley was dark, but not empty.

A figure stood in the shadows. Lean. Still. He wasn't wearing a mask, but his face was obscured by a hood. He wore a tactical suit, similar to hers, but gray.

"You have been busy," a voice said. It was calm, male, and lacked any trace of fear.

Beth tensed. "Do I know you?"

"You are costing the wrong people a lot of money," the man said. He stepped forward. "You are bad for business."

He moved.

He didn't charge. He flowed. One second he was ten feet away, the next he was in her personal space, a fist aiming for her throat.

Beth dodged, barely. The wind of the punch ruffled her balaclava.

She swept his leg, but he hopped over the strike and countered with a spinning kick that caught her shoulder.

*Pain.*

He was fast. As fast as her. Maybe faster.

"Who are you?" she grunted, rolling away and coming up in a defensive crouch.

"You can call me the Eraser," he said. "And you are a smudge."

He pulled a knife. A karambit. Wicked, curved steel.

This wasn't a game anymore. This was a hit.

Beth didn't fight. She ran.

She sprinted at the wall, running three steps up the bricks and backflipping over his head.

She landed and took off down the alley.

"Running just delays the inevitable!" he shouted.

She heard his footsteps behind her. Light. Fast.

She vaulted a dumpster, caught a fire escape ladder, and scrambled up. As she reached the roof, she risked a glance down.

He was right behind her, scaling the ladder with terrifying speed.

She sprinted across the roof. *Gap: 12 feet.*

She launched.

For a second, she was flying. The landing was hard—a roll onto gravel—but she didn't stop. She dove into the shadows of a water tower, doubling back, using the darkness as a weapon.

She waited.

Silence.

He had lost her trace. Or he was waiting.

She didn't stay to find out. She ghosted away, taking a route so convoluted even she had to think about it.

***

3:30 AM.

Beth crashed onto her apartment floor. She stripped off the suit. Her shoulder was throbbing where he had kicked her.

She touched the bruise. It was tender. Real.

She looked at the crystal globe on her table.

She had stolen from a Councilman. She had tipped off a Detective. And she had just survived a fight with a professional assassin.

Her hands were shaking. Not from fear. From the pure, unadulterated voltage of it.

"A rival," she whispered, a grin splitting her face.

"What a rush!"
