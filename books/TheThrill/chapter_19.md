# Chapter 19: The Fallout

Councilman Sterling was screaming. He was in handcuffs, being led out of his own penthouse by two uniformed officers, shouting about attorneys and framing and knowing the mayor.

Detective Jack Thorne ignored him. He was standing in the study, looking at the mahogany desk.

The safe was open. It was a *Titanium-Vanguard 4*, and it had been cracked without a scratch.

And there, sitting in the center of the desk like a centerpiece, was a padded envelope.

**ATTN: DET. THORNE.**

Jack picked it up. Inside was a ledger and a hard drive. He flipped open the ledger. It was a roadmap of corruption. Bribes, kickbacks, offshore accounts. It was enough to put Sterling away for twenty years.

"Dammit," Jack muttered.

He ran a hand through his hair. He was supposed to be the law. He was supposed to catch criminals, not accept gift baskets from them.

But as he watched the blustering politician get shoved into the elevator, a slow, grudging smile fought its way onto his face.

She had done his job for him. Again. And she had done it with style.

"I hate that I like you," he whispered to the empty room. "But god help me, I do."

***

The mood in Viktor Volkov's office was less festive.

The Eraser stood before the desk. He was still wearing his tactical suit, though the hood was down. He had a bloody lip, and his pride was visibly bruised.

"You told me she was an amateur," the assassin said, his voice flat. "You told me she was a bored girl playing dress-up."

Viktor Volkov stared at him. The Mob boss’s face was a mask of cold fury.

"I told you to solve a problem," Viktor said softly. "I did not tell you to get kicked in the face in an alleyway."

"She is fast. She is trained. This is not a hobby for her."

Viktor stood up and walked around the desk. He stopped inches from the assassin's face.

"I pay you absolute top dollar," Viktor whispered. "I pay you because you are supposed to be a ghost. A legend. I thought you were the best."

The Eraser didn't flinch. "I am the best. And she got lucky. Once."

"Luck runs out," Viktor said, turning back to the window. "Ensure that hers runs out tonight. Or I will find someone who can."

***

Beth Sanders was currently very lucky, and very comfortable.

She was lying on her rug, wearing oversized sweatpants and a tank top. An ice pack was strapped to her shoulder where the Eraser had connected, but the pain was manageable. It was a reminder.

She held a glass of Pinot Noir in her good hand.

On the TV, the late news was showing footage of Councilman Sterling's perp walk. They were calling it the "Penthouse Purge." Anonymous sources cited a "whistleblower" who had provided undeniable evidence.

Beth raised her glass to the screen.

"You're welcome, New York," she murmured.

She let her head fall back against the cushion. What a week.

Monday: Audited forklift tires.
Tuesday: Robbed a Mob front.
Wednesday: Poked the bear.
Thursday: Stole a Bond car.
Friday: Drank with the Detective.
Saturday: Took down a politician and fought a ninja.

She chuckled, the sound vibrating in her chest.

Most people lived their whole lives without a single moment of true, electric uncertainty. Beth had lived ten lifetimes in six days.

She took a sip of wine. Her shoulder throbbed, her muscles ached, and she had never felt so peaceful.

"Same time next week?" she asked the empty room.

She closed her eyes, smiling. Oh, definitely.
