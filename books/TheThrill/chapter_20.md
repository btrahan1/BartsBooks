# Chapter 20: The Warning

The conference room in the basement of the 19th Precinct was soundproofed, stripped of electronics, and smelled of stale coffee. It was where the conversations happened that weren't supposed to be on the record.

Jack Thorne sat opposite Special Agent Miller from the FBI’s Organized Crime Task Force.

Miller slid a manila folder across the table. It was thin. Too thin.

"We picked up chatter on the wire yesterday," Miller said. "Volkov made a call. A number that hasn't been active in five years."

Jack opened the folder. There was a single grainy surveillance photo. It showed a man in a gray coat walking through an airport terminal in Berlin. His face was blurred by motion, or maybe just by bad luck.

"Who is he?" Jack asked.

"We call him The Eraser," Miller said. "Because that’s what he does. He doesn't just kill targets, Jack. He removes them. No body, no evidence, no trace."

Jack felt a cold knot form in his stomach. He had heard the stories. The Eraser was a ghost story cops told each other to scare the rookies.

"He's in New York?" Jack asked.

"He landed at Teterboro on a private charter this morning. Went straight to a safe house in Brighton Beach."

Miller leaned forward. "Volkov isn't playing around anymore. He’s brought in a nuclear weapon to kill a fly."

Jack closed the folder.

A fly. That’s what they thought she was. A nuisance. A thief.

But Jack knew better. She wasn't a fly. She was a Mocking Bird. She was brilliant, arrogant, and skilled.

But she wasn't a killer.

He thought of the woman in the red jacket at the bar. The way she laughed. The way she toasted to the "bad guys."

She thought she was playing a game. She thought she was poking a bear and running away.

She didn't know the bear had called in a hunter who didn't miss.

Jack stood up, his chair scraping loudly against the concrete floor. He grabbed the folder.

"Where are you going?" Miller asked.

"I need to find her," Jack said, his voice tight.

"Jack, she's a criminal. If Volkov takes her out, it solves a problem for both of us."

Jack turned at the door. "No. It doesn't."

He walked out into the hallway, the fluorescent lights buzzing overhead.

He thought of the feather on the seat of the Aston Martin. He thought of the bruised pride in her voice when she talked about "the bad guys."

She was good. God, she was good.

But The Eraser was better.

"Dammit!" Jack punched the wall, hard enough to bruise his knuckles. "She's going to get herself killed!"

He had to find her. Not to arrest her. Not anymore.

He had to find her before the gray man turned her into a memory.
