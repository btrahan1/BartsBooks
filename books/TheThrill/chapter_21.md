# Chapter 21: The Boy

The ACE bandage was tight around her chest, constricting her breathing. It was uncomfortable, but it was necessary.

Beth looked in the mirror.

She had darkened her eyebrows with greasepaint, thickening them. She had contoured her jawline to look sharper, more angular. A beanie was pulled low over her forehead, hiding her hair completely.

She wore a baggy gray hoodie, distressed jeans, and heavy work boots.

She didn't walk like Beth. She didn't walk like Veronica. She walked like a coil of nervous energy. Shoulders hunched, hands in pockets, chin down.

"I'm Kelly," she said. Her voice was a pitch lower, rough and unpolished.

She grinned. It was a boy's grin. Cocky and stupid.

"Let's go bother the Detective."

***

O'Shaughnessy's was emptier tonight. A Tuesday. The desperation was quieter.

Jack Thorne was in his usual spot. He wasn't drinking beer tonight. He was drinking whiskey. And he wasn't staring into space; he was staring at his phone, his thumb hovering over a contact list.

He looked haunted.

Beth slid onto the stool next to him. She didn't sit gracefully. She slumped.

"Buy me a drink, Detective?" she asked, pitching her voice into that cracked, youthful register.

Jack didn't look up immediately. "I'm off duty, kid. And you look underage."

"I'm twenty-one in dog years," Kelly said, drumming fingers on the bar. "Rough day?"

Jack turned then. He looked at the boy in the hoodie.

Beth held her breath. The disguise was good. But eyes were the tell.

Jack’s gaze sharpened. He scanned the face—the fake stubble, the beanie, the too-bright eyes.

He didn't see a boy. He saw the energy. He saw the same reckless spark that had been in the woman in red.

He didn't arrest her. He didn't reach for his badge.

He reached out and grabbed "Kelly's" wrist. His grip was hard, bordering on painful.

"Listen to me," Jack hissed, bringing his face close.

The smell of whiskey was strong, but underneath it was the metallic scent of fear.

"You think this is funny," he said low and dangerous. "You think swapping faces is a game."

"I don't know what you're talking about, old man," Kelly stammered, trying to pull away.

Jack held on. "You tell your friend. The one who likes feathers. You tell her the sky is falling."

He leaned in closer.

"The Eraser is in town. Do you know what that means?"

Beth felt a chill that had nothing to do with the air conditioning. She stopped struggling.

"It means school's out," Jack whispered. "It means playtime is over. If she shows her face again, she won't end up in a cell. She'll end up in a drain."

He released her wrist.

"Go home, kid," Jack said, turning back to his drink. "And tell her to run."

Beth sat there for a second, rubbing her wrist. The cocky grin was gone.

"I'll... I'll tell her," she whispered.

She stood up and walked out of the bar. She didn't swagger. She didn't look back.

She walked out into the night, and for the first time in weeks, she didn't feel the thrill.

She felt the cold.
