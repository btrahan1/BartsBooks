# Chapter 22: The Bait

Fear was a cold thing. It slowed the blood. It made fingers clumsy.

Beth sat on a park bench in Washington Square Park, watching the pigeons fight over a crust of bread. She was shivering, and it wasn't because of the weather.

*Playtime is over.*

Jack’s words echoed in her head. The Eraser. A man who didn't leave corpses, just absences.

She could run. She had enough cash stash to get to Mexico. She could disappear.

But running meant looking over her shoulder forever. Running meant admitting that the game was over and she had lost.

"No," she whispered.

She stood up. The shivering stopped.

She walked to a bodega on 4th Street. She bought a prepaid burner phone and a pack of gum with cash.

She walked three blocks south, ensuring she wasn't followed. She ducked into an alleyway and powered on the phone.

She knew the number. She had memorized it from the police directory weeks ago.

She dialed.

*Ring. Ring.*

"Thorne," a voice answered. It sounded like gravel and exhaustion.

"Tell the detective it's Kelly," Beth said, pitching her voice low, mimicking the boy from the bar.

There was a pause. A sharp intake of breath.

"Where are you?" Thorne demanded. The exhaustion was gone, replaced by instant, high-voltage alertness. "Kid, you need to come in. It's not safe."

"I know," Beth said. "That's why I'm ending it."

"What do you mean?"

"The Eraser wants a target. I'm going to give him one."

"Don't do this," Thorne snapped. "He will kill you. Do you understand? He is not a street thug. He is a machine."

"Machines can be broken," Beth said, her voice steady. "But I need a hammer."

"I'm not your hammer, I'm a police officer. Turn yourself in. I can protect you in custody."

"He'd get to me in a cell. You know he would."

Silence on the line. Thorne knew it. Volkov's reach was long.

"Tomorrow night," Beth said. "2:00 A.M."

"Where?"

"The Brooklyn Navy Yard. Building 77. The roof."

"That's a kill box," Thorne said. "There's no cover."

"Exactly. He won't be able to resist."

Beth took a breath. This was it. The chips were all in.

"I'll draw out The Eraser," she said. "I'll make him show himself. You take him down."

"If you die..." Thorne started, his voice cracking slightly.

"Then you'll have a very easy solve for your paperwork," Beth said.

"Don't you hang up on me. Don't you dare—"

"See you at the party, Jack."

*Click.*

Beth dropped the phone into a trash can.

She looked up at the sky. It was gray and heavy.

"Okay," she whispered. "Let's see who erases who."
