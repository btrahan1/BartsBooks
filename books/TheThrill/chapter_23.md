# Chapter 23: The Trap

Building 77 of the Brooklyn Navy Yard was a monolith of concrete and steel, jutting out into the East River like a tombstone. It was a Volkov holding—officially a logistics hub, unofficially a place where shipping containers went to disappear.

It was 1:15 AM.

Beth was forty-five minutes early.

She moved through the shadows of the rooftop utility shed, her movements precise and fluid. She wasn't wearing the black bodysuit tonight. She was wearing armored motorcycle leathers—kevlar-reinforced, slash-resistant.

She wasn't here to steal. She was here to hunt.

She knelt by the HVAC cluster near the roof access door. She pulled a handful of magnesium flares and a tripwire spool from her pack.

She rigged the wire across the doorway, shin-height. Low enough to be missed in the dark, high enough to stumble a professional.

She connected the flares to a friction igniter.

"Surprise," she whispered.

She moved to the center of the roof. It was an open expanse of gravel and tar, illuminated only by the distant glow of the Manhattan skyline across the river.

She checked her knives. Two ceramic blades, strapped to her thighs.

She had spent the last twenty-four hours training. Not parkour this time. Combat. She had visualized every angle of a knife fight. She had replayed the alley encounter a thousand times in her mind.

He was fast. He was strong.

But he was arrogant. He called himself "The Eraser." He prided himself on silence.

"He won't use a gun," Beth murmured to herself. "Guns are loud. Guns leave ballistics. Ghosts don't use guns."

He would want to cut her. He would want to make it personal.

That was his mistake.

She walked to the edge of the roof, looking down at the silent shipyard.

She took out her phone. She opened the app that controlled the cloned RFID signal she had captured from the Volkov dealership.

She pointed it at the sensor array on the door.

*Transmit.*

The silent alarm tripped. Somewhere in a security office in Brighton Beach, a red light was blinking. Somewhere in a black car moving through Brooklyn, a notification was pinging.

The dinner bell had been rung.

Beth put the phone away. She walked to the center of the rooftop "kill box" she had created.

She widened her stance, focusing on her breathing. *Inhale. Exhale.*

The wind whipped her hair across her face. She didn't brush it away.

She was terrified. Her heart was hammering against her ribs like a trapped bird. But beneath the fear, there was something else. A cold, hard resolve.

If Detective Thorne showed up, he would find a criminal to arrest.
If the Eraser showed up, he would find a victim who fought back.

"Come on," she whispered to the dark. "Come and get me."

She waited. And the night held its breath.
