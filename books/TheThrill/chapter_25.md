# Chapter 25: The Dance

The Eraser didn't lunge. He flowed. He covered the ten feet between them in a blur of gray motion, the karambit slashing horizontally for her stomach.

Beth didn't block. You didn't block a knife; you got cut.

She dropped.

It was a yoga squat, instantaneous and deep, her hamstrings touching her calves. The blade hissed through the air where her ribs had been a microsecond before.

From the squat, she exploded upward, driving her shoulder into his solar plexus.

He grunted, stumbling back half a step. He was heavy, solid as a statue.

Beth didn't wait. She spun away, vaulting over a rusted conduit pipe.

"You're fast," the Eraser said. He sounded bored.

He pursued. He didn't climb the conduit; he sliced through the air above it, anticipating her landing.

But Beth didn't land. She planted a foot on the far wall of the utility shed and pushed off, twisting in mid-air—a parkour wall-run converted into an aerial dodge.

She landed behind him.

She slashed with her ceramic knife, scoring a hit on his tactical vest. It didn't penetrate the armor, but it sent a message.

*I am here.*

The Eraser turned, his eyes narrowing. He realized then what she was doing. She wasn't fighting him. She was leading him.

She knew every rivet on the roof. She knew which gravel patches were loose. She knew the wind shear around the vents.

She moved like smoke. She was a mixture of violence and grace—advanced yoga flexibility allowing her to contort out of holds, MMA discipline driving her strikes, and parkour giving her the Z-axis.

She didn't just fight. She danced.

***

Detective Jack Thorne kicked open the roof access door, his Glock 19 raised and ready.

"Police! Drop it!"

He expected to see a slaughter. He expected to see the kid—Kelly—bleeding out on the tar.

What he saw froze him in the doorway.

Two figures were weaving through the moonlight in a deadly, kinetic ballet. Sparks flew as steel met ceramic. They moved with a speed that registered as a blur.

The Eraser he recognized—efficiency, brutality, power.

But the other...

She was in motorcycle leathers, a balaclava hiding her face. But the way she moved... it was mesmerizing. She vaulted a four-foot vent stack with one hand, spinning in the air to deliver a heel kick to the assassin's head.

It was impossible. It was beautiful.

"Shoot him!" the woman screamed, her voice muffled but distinct.

Jack tried to aim.

He tracked the gray figure of the Eraser.

*Target acquired.*

*Target obscured.*

They were too close. They were grappling now, the Eraser holding her wrist, the knife inches from her throat. She twisted her body in an impossible arc, using the leverage to throw him over her hip.

They crashed into a pile of construction debris.

"Clear shot... negative," Jack hissed, lowering the weapon slightly. "Goddammit, separate!"

He couldn't fire. If he missed by an inch, he killed the only witness. He killed the Mocking Bird.

He watched as she scrambled up, blood dripping from a cut on her arm. She didn't look at Jack. She looked at the killer getting to his feet.

She raised her knife again.

She wasn't running. She was grinning.

Jack realized then that he wasn't just watching a crime scene. He was watching a master at work.
