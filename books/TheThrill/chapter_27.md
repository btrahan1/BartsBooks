# Chapter 27: The Message

The iodine stung, but it was a good sting. It was the feeling of survival.

Beth sat at her kitchen island, applying a butterfly bandage to the cut on her arm. It wasn't deep—just a graze from the Eraser’s second karambit. A souvenir.

The NYPD scanner was quiet now. Thorne had called it in as a "high-priority apprehension." No names on the air. Just "Subject Secured."

Beth poured herself a glass of water. Her hands were steady.

She should go to sleep. She had work in the morning. Compliance never sleeps, and spreadsheets don't audit themselves.

But the game wasn't quite over.

She walked to her computer and woke the screens. The blue light washed over her, familiar and safe.

She navigated through the layers of the Dark Web, bypassing the honey pots and the dead ends, until she reached the encrypted message board known as *The Exchange*. It was where the Odessa Syndicate did its recruiting. It was where rumors went to become currency.

She found the thread where the bounties were discussed. There was no explicit mention of her, just a vague "Pest Control" contract that had gone active two days ago.

Beth smiled.

She didn't use a proxy this time. She used the signature she had built. The digital mock-up of a bird with a diamond in its beak.

She typed a single line.

**FROM: The Mocking Bird**
**TO: Management**
**SUBJECT: Staffing Update**

*The Eraser sends his regards. He's officially retired. Please update your records.*

*P.S. Next time, send someone who can dance.*

She hit send.

The message posted instantly, bold and bright against the black background.

Beth watched it for a second, imagining the phone ringing in a penthouse in Brighton Beach. Imagining Viktor Volkov throwing a glass of expensive vodka against a wall.

"Checkmate," she whispered.

She turned off the monitors. The room plunged into darkness.

She walked to her bedroom, the ghost of the fight still playing in her muscles. She was bruised, she was tired, and she had a target on her back the size of Manhattan.

She had never felt better.
