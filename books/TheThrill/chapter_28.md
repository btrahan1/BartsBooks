# Chapter 28: The Call

The flashbulbs were blinding. They popped in rapid succession, turning the precinct steps into a strobe-lit stage.

"Detective Thorne! Is it true the suspect is linked to international assassinations?"
"Detective, how did you track him down?"

Jack Thorne stood behind the podium, the Chief of Police beaming next to him.

"The apprehension was the result of... extensive intelligence work," Jack said. His voice was steady, but his stomach turned.

"Extensive intelligence work" was apparently code for "a woman in a leather suit kicked him in the head and left him for me."

He was a fraud. He was being hailed as a hero for being the janitor who cleaned up the beautiful mess she had made.

He forced a smile for the cameras.

*I didn't catch him,* he thought. *She gave him to me. Like a cat leaving a dead bird on the doormat.*

And somewhere out there, she was watching this. Probably laughing.

***

Viktor Volkov was not laughing.

He was standing in the ruins of his office. The 80-inch monitor was shattered, a spiderweb of cracks obscuring the mocking message from "The Mocking Bird."

A crystal decanter of scotch lay in shards on the floor. A Ming vase was reduced to dust.

"Retired," Viktor hissed, his chest heaving. "She says he is *retired*."

His men stood by the door, terrified to speak. They had never seen the Boss like this. Viktor was ice. Viktor was control.

This... this was an explosion.

"Get out!" Viktor roared.

The men scrambled, closing the heavy oak doors behind them.

Viktor leaned against his desk, gripping the edge until his knuckles turned white. She had humiliated him. She had stolen his money, his car, and now his reputation.

And she was laughing at him.

*Buzz.*

The sound cut through the silence.

It came from the bottom drawer of his desk. From the safe.

Viktor froze.

He opened the drawer and pulled out a burner phone. It was a satellite encryption model, used only for emergency contacts with the High Table in Moscow. No one had this number. No one.

He stared at the screen. *UNKNOWN CALLER.*

He flipped it open.

"Who is this?" he demanded, his voice trembling with rage.

There was silence on the line for a moment. Then, a voice. It wasn't distorted. It was clear, calm, and maddeningly amused. It was the voice of a woman who had just won everything.

"I hear you've been looking for me, Viktor," she said.

Viktor’s blood ran cold.

"And since your staff seems to be... retiring," the voice continued, "I thought we should chat."

Viktor sank into his chair, the phone pressed to his ear.

The game wasn't over. It had just begun.
