# Chapter 30: The Promise

Jack Thorne was staring at his reflection in the mirror behind the bar. He looked tired. He looked like a man who had won a war he didn't understand.

The bar was quiet. The regulars were occupied with their own miseries.

"Can I buy you a drink, Detective?"

The voice was smoky, familiar, and impossible.

Jack spun around on his stool.

She was there. The platinum bob. The violet eyes. The red leather jacket.

Veronica.

Jack’s hand twitched toward his hip, but he stopped himself. He wasn't on duty. And honestly, he wasn't sure he wanted to be.

"You have a lot of nerve coming here," he said, but there was no heat in it.

"I have a lot of nerve doing most things," she replied, sliding onto the stool next to him. She signaled the bartender. "Two whiskeys. The good stuff."

She turned to face him. Her eyes were sparkling.

"Consider this my retirement party," she said, raising her glass as the bartender set it down.

Jack raised an eyebrow. "Retirement? From what? Being a pain in my ass?"

"From the game," she said. "I've had my fun. I did a little bit of good. Did a little bit of bad, too."

She took a sip, the red lipstick leaving a perfect mark on the rim.

"I'm a bad girl, Jack."

"You're a criminal," Jack corrected. "You broke into a penthouse. You unleashed a mob war. You trapped a hitman on a roof."

" Details," she waved a hand dismissively. "The point is, the curtain is coming down. The Mocking Bird has sung her song."

Jack looked at her. He studied the sharp angle of her jaw, the way she held herself. He was trying to memorize her.

"So that's it?" he asked. "You just... walk away?"

"That's the beauty of it," she said.

She stood up. She placed a hand on his arm. Her skin was warm.

"The next time you see me, it won't be like this," she said softly. "No wigs. No contacts. No leather."

"Then how will I know it's you?"

She smiled, and it wasn't the sharp, dangerous smile of the thief. It was something softer. Something real.

"You might not," she admitted. "But if a quiet young lady asks you to buy a drink... you should probably say yes. She's lonely."

She leaned in and kissed him on the cheek. It was chaste, fleeting, and smelled of ozone.

"Goodbye, Detective," she whispered.

She turned and walked out of the bar, the red jacket disappearing into the night.

Jack sat there for a long time. He touched his cheek where she had kissed him.

He looked at the empty stool next to him.

"I'll say yes," he said to the silence.

He finished his whiskey, signaled for the check, and smiled.
