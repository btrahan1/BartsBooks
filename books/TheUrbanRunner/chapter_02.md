# Chapter 2: Camouflage and AI

At school the next day, Tyler was scribbling down notes. He didn't
really need to, but he had to look busy. Boredom was a kind of
camouflage. Mr. Harrison was droning on about the Louisiana Purchase,
his voice a monotonous hum that Tyler had learned to filter out like
background static.

Tyler was a master of his own time; he had to be. Between his shifts at
Saito's, looking after Maya, and carving out a few precious hours of
sleep, there was no room for waste. That included the eight hours a day
he was legally required to be here. So, over the summer, he'd already
conquered his senior year curriculum.

Books were for the weak. Or, more accurately, for people with time to
burn. He had AI.

His notebook wasn't filled with dates and historical figures. It was
filled with lines of Python script. He was designing a more efficient
routing algorithm for his deliveries, one that could factor in real-time
traffic data from city webcams. A little side project.

He'd taught himself Python and C# over the last two years, using an
open-source AI as his tutor, partner, and intellectual sparring partner.
People thought of AI as a search engine on steroids; Tyler used it like
a scalpel. He could feed the program a query---"Summarize Chapter 4 of
'A People's History of the United States,' focusing on the economic
motivations behind territorial expansion"---and it would spit back the
core data in seconds. Charts, graphs, bullet points. The works. It
stripped out the narrative, the prose, the author's meandering opinions,
and left only the skeleton of pure information.

*Just the Fax, ma'am,* he thought, a smirk playing on his lips as he
sketched out a function in his notebook. It was his guiding principle
for everything. Life was too short for filler.

It was why Coach Renner's speeches had driven him insane. Fifteen
minutes of puffed-up prose about "leaving it all on the field" and "the
heart of a champion" could be distilled into a single, efficient
sentence: "Try hard to win." Renner was the human equivalent of a
five-page book chapter that only contained one actual fact.

The bell shrieked, releasing him. Tyler packed his notebook, weaving his
way into the chaotic river of students flooding the hallway. He was
halfway to his locker when a familiar voice cut through the noise.

"Tyler. Hey."

It was Marco. He was standing by the water fountain, his Stallions gym
bag slung over a shoulder that already seemed broader than it had a week
ago. He looked like he hadn't slept.

"Marco," Tyler said, nodding. "What's up?"

"Practice was brutal this morning," Marco said, rubbing the back of his
neck. "Renner's riding us hard. Says we've got to compensate for... you
know."

The unspoken words hung in the air between them: *for losing you*.

"Yeah, well, he loves his two-a-days," Tyler said, keeping his tone
light.

Marco's face hardened. "He asked about you. Asked if you'd come to your
senses yet."

"And what'd you say?"

"What do you think I said? I told him you were being stubborn." Marco
took a step closer, lowering his voice. "Ty, this is crazy. You're
throwing away a scholarship. You know half a dozen scouts were coming to
our first game just to see you play. You're the best player this school
has seen in a decade, and you're quitting to deliver... noodles."

The word came out like an insult. Tyler felt a familiar coil of
frustration tighten in his gut. Marco didn't get it. He couldn't. His
parents paid for his car, his phone, his expensive cleats. Marco's only
job was to play soccer.

"It wasn't a choice, man. You know my situation."

"There are always choices!" Marco's voice was rising, drawing a few
glances. "Talk to Renner. Explain things. He'll work with you if you
just show him you're committed! If you just buy in!"

*Buy in.* It was a Renner-ism. A piece of prose. Tyler shook his head.
"His version of 'working with me' is me quitting my job and letting my
mom figure out the rent. That's not a choice. That's a fantasy."

Marco stared at him, his expression a mix of anger and bewilderment. He
looked at Tyler's plain t-shirt and worn jeans, then down at his own
team-issued gear. They were standing five feet apart, but they were in
different worlds.

"I just don't get you, man," Marco said, his voice flat with defeat.
"You have this incredible gift, and you just... don't care."

He turned and pushed his way into the crowd, leaving Tyler standing
alone in the hallway. The words stung, but Marco was wrong. Tyler did
care. He just cared about a different set of facts. The fact that his
mom was exhausted. The fact that Maya needed new shoes. The fact that
the rent was due on the first.

Those were the facts. Everything else was just prose.
