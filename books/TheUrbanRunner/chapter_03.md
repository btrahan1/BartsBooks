# Chapter 3: The Lion's Den


The squeak of sneakers on polished hardwood, the faint smell of sweat
and floor cleaner---this was the one class Tyler couldn't optimize his
way out of. Last period of the day: Physical Education. Led, of course,
by Coach Renner.

To be truthful, Tyler had no beef with him. He saw Renner for exactly
what he was: a man who had one thing in his life, soccer, and who had
built his entire world around it. He was a master of that one thing, and
he demanded the same singular focus from everyone around him. Tyler
could respect the dedication, even if he couldn't share it. The coach
wanted a level of commitment Tyler simply couldn't give. It wasn't
personal; it was just a fact. A data point.

He also wasn't so sure about this "gift" thing Marco kept talking about.
He'd been playing soccer since he was five, chasing a ball around a
dusty park with the other neighborhood kids. He was fast because he ran
everywhere. He had endurance because he worked. He could see the field
well because his brain was good at finding the most efficient path from
Point A to Point B, whether that point was a customer's front door or an
open teammate. He wasn't Pelé. He was just Tyler.

"Alright, listen up!" Renner's voice boomed across the gymnasium,
bouncing off the bleachers. He stood, arms crossed, exuding an aura of
coiled intensity that never seemed to switch off. "Today, we're doing
the Pacer Test. We're going to find out who spent their summer on the
couch and who put the work in. This isn't about talent. This is about
heart. It's about what you do when your lungs are burning and your legs
are screaming. It's about pushing past your limits!"

Tyler tuned out the rest of the prose. He knew the test. A series of
20-meter sprints, paced by increasingly frequent beeps. Simple.
Efficient. He could appreciate that.

The class lined up along the baseline. Tyler saw the entire starting
lineup of the Stallions spread out among the other students. Marco was
two people down from him, bouncing on the balls of his feet, eyes locked
on Renner, already trying to project "heart."

The first beep sounded, low and forgiving. The group jogged across the
gym. Easy. Tyler settled into a rhythm, keeping his breathing
controlled, his movements minimal. He wasn't here to win; he was here to
get an A and go home. According to the grading rubric, that meant
reaching level 12. A simple objective.

Level after level, the beeps grew more insistent. The casual chatter
died down, replaced by the sound of panting and the rhythmic slap of
dozens of sneakers. Students began to drop out, missing a line, gasping
for air as they walked to the sidelines.

By level 9, only a handful remained, mostly track kids and the soccer
players. Marco was still in, his face flushed, his jersey already dark
with sweat. He was running with a kind of violent determination, pushing
off hard on every turn. Wasted energy.

Tyler, by contrast, felt...fine. The pace was brisk, but it was nothing
compared to a Friday dinner rush with five orders on his back. His heart
rate was elevated, but steady. His lungs were working, but they weren't
burning. This was just another delivery, with no backpack.

Renner was pacing the sideline, his eyes scanning the remaining runners.
He wasn't yelling. He was observing, calculating. His gaze swept over
Marco with a critical eye, lingered on the track team's star sprinter
for a moment, and then settled on Tyler. It stayed there.

Level 11. Level 12. Tyler crossed the line just ahead of the beep.
Mission accomplished. He could stop now and have his A.

But Renner was still watching him. And Marco, beside him, was starting
to fall apart. His form was sloppy, his breathing a ragged saw. He was
running on pure pride.

The beep for Level 13 sounded. Tyler ran. He didn't know why. Maybe it
was the coach's stare. Maybe it was seeing the desperation on his
friend's face.

Level 14. A few more dropped out. It was just Tyler, Marco, and a wiry
cross-country runner named Dave.

Level 15. Dave missed the line by a full step and peeled off, collapsing
against the wall. Now it was two.

Marco made the turn, a pained grunt escaping his lips. He shot a glance
at Tyler, his eyes wide with a mixture of fury and disbelief. Tyler met
his gaze, his own expression neutral, his breathing still under control.
He wasn't even sweating heavily.

The beep for Level 16. They ran. Marco's legs were wobbling. He was
going to hit a wall, and soon. Tyler could see it. He could probably run
another ten levels, but what would be the point? This wasn't his
competition.

He crossed the line, and then, instead of turning for the next sprint,
he just stopped. He stood perfectly still in the center of the gym,
hands on his hips, taking one deep, cleansing breath.

The next beep sounded. Marco, alone, pushed himself across the line one
last time before stumbling to a halt, hands on his knees, gasping.

The gym was quiet except for Marco's ragged breaths. Everyone was
staring at Tyler. He had just stopped. He hadn't failed; he'd just quit,
effortlessly, having outlasted everyone but one.

Renner walked to the center of the court, his footsteps echoing. He
stopped in front of Tyler. He wasn't angry. He looked like a man who had
just seen a priceless piece of art used as a placemat.

"You weren't even breathing hard, son," Renner said, his voice a low,
dangerous rumble.

Tyler shrugged, the picture of nonchalance. "Just trying to get a good
grade, Coach."

Renner stared at him for a long moment, the muscle in his jaw twitching.
It was the absolute truth, and that's what made it so infuriating. For
Renner, this was a test of character, of will, of the soul. For Tyler,
it was a box to be checked on his way to work.

Without another word, Renner turned and blew his whistle, a piercing
shriek that signaled the end of class. "Hit the showers!" he barked.

Tyler grabbed his shirt and walked toward the locker room, leaving the
coach standing alone in the middle of the gym. He'd gotten his A. Now he
had to get to Saito's. The dinner rush would be starting soon.
