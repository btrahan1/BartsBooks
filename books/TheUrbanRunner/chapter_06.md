# Chapter 6: The View from the Tree

From a secluded corner of the park, partially hidden by the thick trunk
of an old maple tree, another set of eyes watched the game. It was
Renner. He stood with his arms crossed, his face an unreadable mask of
stone. He wasn't watching the game so much as he was dissecting it, his
gaze locked on Tyler.

He just didn't understand. The effortless speed, the vision, the calm
precision---it was all there, raw and untamed. It was a million-dollar
arm on a sandlot pitcher. A thoroughbred pulling a plow. Why was he
wasting it *here*, of all places? On a bumpy field with no stakes,
surrounded by mediocrity? The waste of it all was a physical offense, a
deep insult to the sport that was Renner's entire religion.

Marco stood a few feet behind him, a silent shadow in his gray Stallions
hoodie. He'd been the one to tell Renner about the game, partly out of a
lingering sense of loyalty to his coach, partly out of a need to make
Renner *see*. He answered the unspoken question hanging heavy in the
air.

"Single mom, little sister. I didn't get it at first, Coach. But he's
all they've got."

Renner didn't turn, didn't acknowledge he'd heard. Marco kept talking,
the words spilling out, a confession he didn't know he needed to make.

"I've got two parents. They fight a lot, but..." he trailed off,
shrugging. "Rent isn't an issue. We have a mortgage. We have a place to
live. Both parents work. I'm the youngest." Each sentence was a quiet
admission of a privilege he'd never really considered before. He was
just a kid whose only job was to be a kid. Tyler hadn't had that luxury.

Renner just grunted, a low, frustrated sound in the back of his throat.
He watched Tyler leave the field, not with the swagger of a star, but
with the quiet relief of a worker clocking out. He saw him sit down with
his family, saw the little girl throw her arms around him. It was a
scene of infuriating, impenetrable peace. Renner's world was one of
structured conflict, of earned glory, of pain forged into victory.
This... this was something else. Something he couldn't control, couldn't
break down, couldn't coach.

"I wish we could pay him to play, but we can't," he sighed, the words
tasting like ash in his mouth. It was the first time Marco had ever
heard a note of true defeat in his coach's voice. It was an admission
that the rules of his world didn't apply here. There was a currency more
valuable than a scholarship, and Tyler was earning it.

Renner's posture straightened, the brief moment of vulnerability
vanishing as quickly as it had appeared. The coach, the general,
returned. He turned his gaze from Tyler to the field, where the
Wanderers were passing the ball with newfound confidence. He saw them
not as a collection of misfits, but as an army being quietly assembled
by a rogue general.

"So that makes him the enemy, Marco."

The words were cold, pragmatic. A fact.

Marco's head snapped up. "Coach?"

"We will face them in the city tournament, you know?" Renner said, his
eyes narrowing, already running plays and strategies in his mind.
"They'll be a low seed. A Cinderella story. And he'll be at the center
of it."

He finally turned to look at Marco, his expression devoid of any warmth.

"He made his choice. He chose them over us. Over you. When we meet them,
you will have a job to do. You will mark him. You will shut him down.
You will show him what 'Total Commitment' really means."

Marco stared at his coach, then back across the park at Tyler, who was
now engaged in a thumb war with his little sister. He was the enemy. It
was an order. And for the first time, Marco wasn't sure it was an order
he could follow.
