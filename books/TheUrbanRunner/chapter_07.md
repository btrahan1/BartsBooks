# Chapter 7: Play Your Game

The five minutes between third and fourth period were a maelstrom of
slamming lockers and shouted conversations. Tyler moved through it with
his usual economy of motion, an island of calm in the chaos.

"So," a voice said at his elbow.

Tyler turned. It was Marco, his gym bag slung over his shoulder, his
expression grim. He looked like he was heading to a funeral, not to
geometry class.

"You ready for the tournament this weekend?" Marco asked, his voice low.

Tyler looked over at his friend, a genuine, easy smile on his face. He'd
just finished mapping a new, more efficient route to the old cannery
district in his head and was feeling pretty good about it. "Oh, sure. It
should be fun!"

Marco sighed, a heavy, world-weary sound that was far too old for a
seventeen-year-old. "For you, maybe. You have no stake in it. If you
lose, you lose. You go back to delivering noodles and it's just another
Saturday. But for us?" Marco gestured vaguely around the crowded school
hallway, a sweep of his hand that took in the trophy cases, the banners
hanging in the distant gym, the entire culture of expectation. "Defeat
would be a disaster. Renner would have us running suicide sprints until
Christmas."

The word 'disaster' hung in the air, absurd and yet deadly serious.
Tyler's smile faded. He looked his friend in the eye, really looked,
past the tired bravado. He saw the genuine frustration, the dark circles
of stress under his eyes, the weight of a hundred pre-game speeches
pressing down on him. This wasn't the game talking; this was his friend.

Tyler shifted his stance, turning to face him fully. The chaotic hallway
seemed to fade into the background. He put a hand on his lifelong
friend's shoulder, his expression turning serious.

"Say the word, Marco, and I won't play."

Marco stared at him, his mouth slightly agape. He clearly expected an
argument, or a joke, or a dismissal. He hadn't expected this.

"What?"

"I'm serious," Tyler said, his voice quiet but firm, a pocket of
sincerity in the noisy hall. "This is just for fun for me. For you,
it's... not. If me being there is going to make your life hell, I'll sit
it out. I'll take a delivery shift. Mr. Saito would love the help."

For a split second, a wave of pure relief washed over Marco's face. The
escape hatch had been offered. The pressure could just... vanish. He
could tell Renner that Tyler had backed out. The Stallions would win,
he'd be the hero, and everything would go back to normal.

But the relief was immediately followed by a hot flush of shame. He
looked at Tyler's hand on his shoulder, at the honest concern in his
eyes. Tyler was offering to sacrifice his own enjoyment, his own game,
to save him from a little pressure. He was offering him a win he
wouldn't have to earn. And what kind of victory was that? It would be a
confirmation of everything he secretly feared---that he couldn't win on
his own terms.

Marco finally shook his head, pulling back from Tyler's grip as if it
burned.

"No," he said, his voice raspy. "Don't... don't do that." He looked
away, down the hall, anywhere but at Tyler. "What, you think I want to
win because you forfeited? Because you felt sorry for me?"

He squared his shoulders, a flicker of the old competitive fire
returning to his eyes, fueled now by a complicated mix of pride and
resentment.

"Just... play," he said, the words coming out clipped and sharp. "Play
your game. I'll play mine."

He gave Tyler one last, conflicted look before turning and disappearing
into the river of students.

"We'll see what happens on the field."
