# Chapter 8: The War Room


The film room was dark and cool, the only light coming from the
projector painting a large, complex diagram onto the screen. It was the
bracket for the All-City Championship Tournament. A web of 16 names,
connected by lines that represented a weekend of brutal,
single-elimination soccer. Win and advance. Lose and go home.

16 teams. 16, then 8, then 4, then 2. Four games and it would all be
over.

Renner stood beside the screen, a red laser pointer in his hand. His
team, the #1 seeded Northgate Stallions, sat in rows before him, silent
and attentive. They were looking at their side of the bracket, sizing up
their first-round opponent, a middling team from Central High they had
beaten 5-0 in the pre-season.

Renner didn't even glance at the top half of the page. His laser dot
hovered, circled, and finally settled on the very last line of the
bracket: #16 Portside Wanderers.

"So," Renner said, his voice calm and certain, cutting through the
silence. "It will be in the finals."

He clicked the laser pointer off. The room remained dark.

"On Sunday."

A few players shifted in their seats. The final was a lifetime away.
They had two games to win on Saturday just to get there. Rogers, a
dependable senior defender and team captain, raised his hand slightly.

"Excuse me, Coach?" Rogers asked, his voice laced with confusion. "Our
first match is against Central. Then probably Westview. Are we not
focusing on Saturday?"

Renner turned slowly, letting the question hang in the air. He looked at
Rogers, then let his gaze sweep over every player in the room, making
them feel the weight of his focus.

"Saturday is a formality," Renner stated, as if discussing the weather.
"Central is disorganized. Westview has one good striker and no defense.
We will win those games. They are a means to an end."

He clicked the laser pointer back on, the red dot reappearing like a
drop of blood on the #16 seed.

"I am talking about the objective. The reason we are here." He traced
the Wanderers' potential path through the tournament. "Their side of the
bracket is weak. Full of undisciplined, arrogant teams. And they have a
variable none of them can account for."

He paused, letting the silence stretch. Then he looked directly at
Marco.

"Tyler. We will face him on Sunday."

The name landed in the room like a grenade. Whispers erupted, quickly
silenced by a sharp glare from Renner. This wasn't a rivalry. This
wasn't a grudge. This was strategy.

"He will beat their first-round opponent because they're slow," Renner
continued, his voice a clinical monotone, as if reading an autopsy
report. "He will beat their second-round opponent because they're cocky
and won't see his playmaking until it's too late. And he will beat
whoever they face in the semi-finals because he will make that team of
nobodies believe they can win. He is the most dangerous kind of player:
a force of chaos that creates its own order."

The laser dot returned to the final, empty slot on the bracket, right
next to the pre-printed name of the Northgate Stallions.

"He is the enemy. And he is the only opponent on this entire sheet of
paper that matters. The rest is just noise."

Renner lowered the laser pointer. "Marco. You'll be in here with me
after practice. We're not watching film on Central. We're going to watch
the tape from his game last weekend. We are going to find a weakness. We
are going to design a cage. And on Sunday, you are going to lock him in
it."

The team stared at the bracket on the screen. It was no longer just a
schedule. It was a prophecy. A collision course, pre-ordained by their
coach. And all roads, it seemed, led to Tyler.
