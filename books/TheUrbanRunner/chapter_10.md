# Chapter 10: Upgrading the Team

Tyler's team won easily. The final score was 5-1. The game, played on a
scruffy grass field that smelled of fresh-cut sod and earth, was a
mirror image of the Stallions' match---not in its clinical precision,
but in its absolute dominance. The only answer Eastside United had for
Tyler was when he didn't take the field in the second half, with the
Wanderers already up 4-0.

Even without Tyler, the team played well. Inspired. Sal stood on the
sidelines, beaming, letting his players create and solve problems on
their own.

Highlight Reel: The First Half

- **Minute 6:** The first goal was a case study in Tyler's influence
  without ever touching the ball. He received a pass near midfield and
  immediately drew three orange jerseys to him like moths to a flame.
  Knowing he was a dead end, he simply shielded the ball, held them off
  for a crucial two seconds, and laid it back to his own defender. But
  in those two seconds, the entire Eastside defense had shifted to his
  side, leaving a gaping hole on the opposite flank. The Wanderers'
  left-back saw it, launched a long, hopeful cross-field pass, and
  Hernandez---the small, speedy winger---ran onto it and coolly slotted
  it past the keeper. Tyler was 50 yards away when the goal was scored,
  but it was entirely his creation.
- **Minute 18:** The second was pure chemistry. Tyler picked up a loose
  ball and drove at the heart of the defense. The bookish but
  surprisingly quick center-mid, a kid named Sam, made an overlapping
  run. Tyler played a perfect one-two pass off Sam's foot, receiving the
  ball back without breaking stride, and found himself one-on-one with
  the keeper. He faked a shot, sent the keeper sprawling, and walked the
  ball into the net. 2-0.
- **Minute 29:** Tyler again, this time with vision. He collected the
  ball on the wing, head up, and saw his team's lumbering center-back, a
  kid named Big Mike, chugging up the field in support. No one ever
  guarded Big Mike past the halfway line. Tyler paused, put his foot on
  the ball, and waited, drawing the defense toward him. Then he slid a
  pass backward, against the run of play, into the path of the big
  defender. Mike, shocked to even have the ball, wound up and blasted a
  shot from 30 yards out. It wasn't pretty, but it was powerful, and it
  deflected off a defender's leg and looped into the net. 3-0. The
  Wanderers erupted in celebration, mobbing the stunned defender.
- **Minute 38:** A final, surgical strike before halftime. A corner
  kick. Instead of lofting it into the box, Tyler drilled a low, hard
  pass to the top of the penalty area where a teammate laid it off for
  him. He took one touch and curled a beautiful shot into the far top
  corner. 4-0.

At halftime, Sal clapped him on the shoulder. "You've done enough damage
for one day, kid. Let the others have some fun." Tyler just grinned,
grabbed a water bottle, and became a spectator.

High in the cheap metal bleachers on the far side of the field, Renner
lowered his binoculars. He had seen enough. He stayed, however, forcing
himself to watch the second half, to gather more data. He expected the
Wanderers to fall apart without their talisman, to revert to the
disorganized kickball he'd seen a month ago.

They didn't.

They gave up a goal on a defensive miscommunication, a sloppy mistake.
But then they settled. They kept passing the ball, moving into the open
spaces. Hernandez, playing with a newfound swagger, scored their fifth
goal on a brilliant individual effort. They were still messy, still
unpolished, but they were playing with an idea. A system.

Renner snapped his scouting notebook shut. He had come here to analyze a
single player, to find a weakness in a prodigy. But his conclusion was
far more troubling.

It wasn't all Tyler.

Tyler made them better, no doubt. He was the catalyst, the spark. But
the change was deeper than that. The other players weren't just standing
around watching him anymore. They were anticipating his passes. They
were making intelligent runs into space he created. They were
communicating. He had taken a team of random parts and, in the space of
a few weeks, taught them how to play a cohesive, intelligent game. He
hadn't just joined the team; he had fundamentally upgraded it.

Renner looked out at the field where the Wanderers were celebrating
their win, a happy, chaotic mob of blue jerseys. He had seen them play
before. They were a joke. Now, they were confident. They were organized.
They believed.

He thought of his own team. The Stallions were a weapon he had forged
through heat and pressure and repetition. They would execute his plan to
perfection. But the Wanderers... they were learning to think for
themselves.

Renner stood up, a cold knot of frustration tightening in his stomach.
This was a far more complicated problem than he had anticipated. You can
cage a single player. But how do you cage an idea?

He turned and walked down the bleachers, his mind already racing,
calculating, re-assessing the threat.

Damnit, Tyler.
