# Chapter 11: The Sacrifice

The semi-final match kicked off under a sky the color of slate. The air
was thick with the promise of rain, and a nervous energy crackled
through the larger crowd that had gathered. The Stallions, having won
their second game with an efficient, almost boring 4-0 victory, were all
there. They lined the top row of the bleachers, a silent jury in
matching team warm-ups.

Tyler wasn't on the field. He was on the bench, stretching his legs, a
reserve pinnie hanging loosely over his jersey.

Up in the stands, Renner lowered his binoculars, a flicker of something
new in his expression: respect. He'd expected Sal to be a simple,
good-natured amateur. But this was a clever move. By benching Tyler, Sal
had forced their opponent, the well-coached Bayview Mariners, to play an
honest game. They couldn't just pack their defense and plan to foul
Tyler for 90 minutes. They had to come out and play.

"That Coach is no slouch either," Renner thought, a low rumble in his
chest.

The first half was a tense, grinding affair. Bayview was disciplined,
and without Tyler to break the lines, the Wanderers looked competent but
toothless. They moved the ball well but couldn't deliver the final,
decisive pass. Bayview scored first on a well-executed counterattack.
The Wanderers, showing a grit they wouldn't have possessed a month ago,
fought back, and Hernandez managed to scramble a goal in just before the
whistle.

It was tied 1-1 at halftime. The game was perfectly balanced on a
knife's edge.

As the teams walked back onto the field, a change was made. Tyler pulled
off his pinnie and jogged to the center circle. A ripple of anticipation
went through the crowd. The Bayview coach began yelling frantically from
the sideline, trying to reorganize his defense. It was too late.

The whistle blew to start the second half. The Wanderers' striker tapped
the ball to Tyler. He took one touch, saw a lane that existed for less
than a heartbeat, and accelerated. He blew past two midfielders who were
still trying to find their marks. He was a blur, and before the Bayview
defense had even settled into their shape, he had cut through them like
a hot knife through butter. He drew the last defender, slid a pass to an
open Hernandez, and then continued his run, getting the return pass and
burying the ball in the back of the net.

Forty-seven seconds. The Wanderers were up 2-1.

The game changed instantly. Bayview abandoned their disciplined
formation and threw everything they had at stopping Tyler. The match
became a brutal, physical slog. Tyler was tripped, shoved, and shadowed
by two players at all times. But the damage was done. They had to chase
the game, and the Wanderers, smelling blood, held firm.

With fifteen minutes left on the clock, a long clearance sent Tyler on a
breakaway. He had a step on the last defender, a big, powerful kid who
knew he was beaten. As Tyler prepared to shoot, the defender launched
into a desperate, scything slide tackle that came nowhere near the ball.

The sickening thwack of boot against shin guard echoed across the field,
followed immediately by the shrill blast of the referee's whistle.
Penalty.

Tyler stayed down for a moment, a searing pain shooting up his leg. He
rolled over, his face tight. He got up slowly, waving off the trainer,
and placed the ball on the penalty spot himself. He limped a few steps
back, took a deep breath, and sent the keeper the wrong way with a
calmly placed shot into the bottom corner. 3-1.

He turned to jog back to midfield, but his jog was a pronounced limp.
Sal had seen enough. The substitution board went up. Tyler's day was
done.

He limped off the field to a standing ovation from the Wanderers' fans.
He sat on the bench, unlacing his cleats while his team locked down on
defense for the final, agonizing minutes. They held on, winning 3-1.
They were in the final.

"You okay, Tyler?" asked Sal, kneeling beside him, his face etched with
concern.

Tyler rubbed his already-bruising shin. A grimace flashed across his
face before he suppressed it.

"All good, Coach," he said, forcing a grin. "I'll rub some dirt on it
and work out the kinks during deliveries tonight."
