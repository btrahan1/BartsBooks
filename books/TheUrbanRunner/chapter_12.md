# Chapter 12: Running on 80 percent


The warm, steamy air of Saito's Noodle House usually felt invigorating,
a signal to his body that it was time to fly. Tonight, it felt heavy,
and the dull, persistent throb in his shin was a metronome counting out
his every limitation. He sat in one of the sticky vinyl booths,
carefully lacing his sneakers, avoiding putting too much pressure on his
left leg.

The usual explosive speed was lacking that night. The ten-block warm-up
to Saito's wasn't a warm-up; it was a painful negotiation with his own
body. For once, Tyler jogged. Each footfall sent a jarring echo up his
shinbone. The shortcuts involving vaults or sharp cuts were temporarily
closed for business. He stuck to the sidewalks, a mortal among mortals.

Mr. Saito, perceptive as ever, noticed immediately. On Tyler's return
from his first delivery---a run that felt like it took an eternity but
was only a minute slower than his average---the old man was waiting with
a fresh order and a critical eye.

"You okay, Tyler-san?" he asked, his hands never stopping as he packed
an order of Tonkotsu. "You're moving in slow motion tonight." He said it
with a grin, but his eyes were serious.

Slow motion for Tyler was still faster than the bike delivery guy from
the pizza place next door, but the observation landed squarely.

"Long day today, sir," Tyler managed, trying to keep the wince off his
face as he swung the pack onto his shoulders. "Big game."

Mr. Saito just nodded, a silent understanding passing between them. He
didn't push.

Tyler didn't push it either. He knew his own body better than anyone. He
knew the difference between pain you could run through and the kind that
would break you if you tried. This was the latter. He throttled back his
pace, turning a series of sprints into a long, grueling marathon. He
focused on efficiency, on taking the straightest, flattest routes. He
used his brain more than his legs, anticipating traffic lights and
pedestrian flows to maintain what momentum he could.

The tips were still good---he was a legend, after all---but the joy was
gone. The work was just work tonight. A means to an end.

When he finally clocked out, handing a slightly smaller-than-usual stack
of cash to Mr. Saito, the relief was immense. The walk home was slow and
deliberate. He could feel the swelling in his shin, a hot, tight
pressure against his sock.

He let himself into the quiet apartment. His mom and Maya were asleep.
In the bathroom, he ran a tub of water as hot as he could stand, pouring
in a half-full bag of Epsom salts he'd bought for his mom's aching feet.
He sank into the steaming water, the immediate shock of the heat giving
way to a slow, unwinding release in his muscles. He closed his eyes,
leaning his head back against the cool porcelain.

He ran the numbers in his head. The data was clear. He wouldn't be 100%
tomorrow. Not even close. With a full night's rest, some ice, and a
heavy dose of stubbornness, he could maybe get to 80%. Maybe.

Against any other team, 80% of Tyler was more than enough. But tomorrow
wasn't any other team. It was the Stallions. It was a machine. It was
Marco.

He hoped that would be good enough.
