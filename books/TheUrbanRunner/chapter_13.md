# Chapter 13: Win for Each Other

The locker room at Northwood Park---which was really just a glorified
concrete shed with a few splintered benches---was buzzing with a
nervous, electric energy. This was it. The semi-finals. Win this game,
and they would face the Stallions.

Tyler was already dressed, slowly trying to stretch out a calf that felt
as tight as a steel cable. He was forcing a smile, making jokes with
Hernandez, but every player in the room could see the slight hitch in
his movements, the careful way he put weight on his left leg.

"Okay, listen up guys, and listen up good!" Coach Sal's voice cut
through the chatter. He stood in the middle of the small room, his usual
easygoing demeanor replaced by a look of intense seriousness. The team
immediately fell silent.

"If you want to get to the finals," Sal said, his eyes scanning every
face in the room, "you will have to do it without Tyler."

The statement dropped like a bomb. A wave of shock and disbelief washed
over the players.

"What?"

"Coach, no way!"

The murmurs and complaints abounded, a chorus of protest. Sal held up a
hand, and the room quieted again.

"Listen!" he commanded, his voice firm. "The team we are facing, the
Northside Brawlers? The name fits. That's just what they are. They lead
the league in yellow cards. They're big, they're physical, and they
don't care about the ball if they can take the player. They saw what
happened to Tyler yesterday. They know he's our best player, and they
know he's hurt."

He paused, letting the reality of the situation sink in.

"But you guys," he continued, his voice softening, his gaze sweeping
over Hernandez, and Sam, and Big Mike. "You guys have real talent. Real
teamwork. You've been proving it for weeks. So listen to me!"

He pointed a thumb at Tyler, who was watching him with a surprised but
grateful expression.

"If we put Tyler out there, they will probably use two red cards just to
take him out for good. It's a trade they'll make every single time. Look
at him!"

Every eye in the room turned to Tyler.

"He's smiling, but he's faking it! He's at eighty percent on a good day,
and they will turn that into zero percent before the first half is
over."

Sal clapped his hands together, a sharp, decisive sound.

"The final is at 7:00 tonight. Under the big lights at the city stadium.
Let's give Tyler that time to recuperate. Let's get him to that game.
And if we lose this one trying, so be it."

He looked around the room, making eye contact with his players, his
expression open and honest.

"This isn't life or death. This is supposed to be fun. This whole
season, you guys have learned to play for each other. Now, I'm asking
you to win for each other."

He cracked a smile, the old Sal returning.

"So go out there, play your game, and have some fun!"

There was a moment of stunned silence, and then Hernandez stepped
forward. He walked over to Tyler and clapped him on the shoulder. "You
heard the man. Save it for the final."

Sam nodded. "We got this, Ty."

One by one, the rest of the team followed, a cascade of nods and pats on
the back. The initial shock had transformed into a steely, collective
resolve. This wasn't about their star player anymore. This was about
them.

Tyler watched them, a lump forming in his throat. For the first time all
season, he wasn't the one carrying the team. They were carrying him. And
as they filed out of the locker room, a determined, cohesive unit, he
leaned his head back against the cool concrete wall, closed his eyes,
and hoped it would be enough.
