# Chapter 14: The Shootout

Renner was watching. Openly now. As were the rest of the Stallions. They
had taken over the top three rows of the main bleachers, a silent,
imposing block of royal blue. This was the semi-final. They would play
the winner.

When the Wanderers took the field and Tyler wasn't among them, a low
murmur went through the Stallions. Renner simply nodded to himself, as
if he'd expected it.

"He's hurt," he said to Rogers, his captain, his voice a low, analytical
hum. "The coach won't start him, but he'll have to put him in later if
they want to win. They can't hold out."

He turned his head slightly, raising his voice so the whole team could
hear. "A bold move, but not something I would do. A game of this
magnitude? You play your best players. You hear that, guys? You have to
play through the pain! Pain is temporary, victory is forever!"

Then they all settled in to watch the Brawlers, a team of big, bruising
players, try to dismantle the Wanderers. The game was ugly. It was a war
of attrition, full of shoulder charges, clipped heels, and constant,
grinding pressure. But the Wanderers didn't break. They played a smart,
quick-passing game, refusing to get drawn into a physical fight. They
absorbed the pressure, bent, but did not snap.

And then, against all odds, they scored. A brilliant counter-attack,
with Hernandez using his low center of gravity to weave through two
clumsy challenges before slotting the ball home.

To everyone's surprise, the Wanderers were up 1-0 at halftime.

"See?" Marco muttered to the player next to him. "They're good. It's not
just him."

Renner heard him but said nothing. His eyes were fixed on the Wanderers'
bench. Surely, they would put Tyler in now to protect the lead, to use
his control and vision to ice the game.

But as the clock on the stadium scoreboard wound down---20 minutes, 10,
then 5---Tyler stayed seated. He was active, but not in the way anyone
expected. He was on the ground, methodically using a foam roller on his
calf. He was re-taping his shin guard over a growing ice pack. He was
also his team's loudest cheerleader. He was smiling, his voice hoarse
from shouting encouragement.

"Woohoo! Go guys! Hold the line! You got this!"

It looked like it was going to be enough. They weathered the storm. The
Brawlers were getting desperate, launching long, hopeful balls into the
box. Big Mike and the Wanderers' defense headed everything away. The
referee signaled for three minutes of stoppage time. The Wanderers just
needed to hold on.

Then, disaster.

In the final seconds of stoppage time, the Brawlers won a throw-in deep
in the Wanderers' territory. Their biggest player launched the ball into
the penalty box like a catapult. It was chaos. A Brawler headed it, but
it went sideways. Another swung a foot at it and missed. The ball
bounced off a Wanderer's knee, then off a Brawler's chest. It was a
pinball machine of desperation. Finally, it squirted free, and a Brawler
defender who had charged forward swung his leg at it. The shot was weak,
a mis-hit scuff, but it deflected cruelly off Big Mike's outstretched
leg, changed direction completely, and trickled, in agonizing slow
motion, just inside the post.

The Wanderers' goalie, who had dived the other way, could only watch.

The Brawlers erupted. The Wanderers collapsed, hands on their heads, the
air sucked from their lungs. The referee blew the final whistle a moment
later. It would come down to penalty kicks.

From the stands, Renner watched the scene unfold, a grim, calculating
look on his face. Tyler was still on the bench, his face a mask of shock
and disbelief. He hadn't played a second. His team had fought for 90
minutes, only to have it ripped away at the very end.

"Now we'll see," Renner said to no one in particular. "Now we see if
their 'fun' can hold up under real pressure."
