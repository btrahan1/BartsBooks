# Chapter 15: What Would Tyler Do

Up in the stands, Marco stepped closer to the coach, his eyes glued to
the unfolding drama on the field.

"Coach," he said, his voice a low murmur. "Tyler wasn't listed as
injured. He just didn't play. He can take one of the kicks."

Renner grunted, not taking his eyes off the field. "We'll see."

The teams gathered at the halfway line, arms linked. The Wanderers'
goalie trotted to his line, clapping his hands, a lone figure against
the gaping maw of the goal. The Brawlers' first kicker began the long,
lonely walk from the center circle.

The shootout was a showcase of pure nerve.

- **Brawlers, Kick 1:** Their captain blasts it home. **(Brawlers 1-0)**
- **Wanderers, Kick 1:** Hernandez, cool as ever, sends the keeper the
  wrong way. **(Tied 1-1)**
- **Brawlers, Kick 2:** A powerful shot is brilliantly saved by the
  Wanderers' keeper, who guesses right. The Wanderers' fans erupt.
  **(Tied 1-1)**
- **Wanderers, Kick 2:** Big Mike, tasked with the second kick, looks
  terrified. He hammers it, but it's straight at the keeper who parries
  it away. **(Tied 1-1)**
- **Brawlers, Kick 3:** Their player skies it, sending the ball sailing
  over the crossbar. The pressure is getting to them. **(Tied 1-1)**
- **Wanderers, Kick 3:** Sam, the bookish midfielder, places his shot
  with precision. **(Wanderers 2-1)**
- **Brawlers, Kick 4:** They score, a clean shot into the side netting.
  **(Tied 2-2)**
- **Wanderers, Kick 4:** Their winger tries to be too clever, and his
  chip is easily caught. **(Tied 2-2)**
- **Brawlers, Kick 5:** Their final kicker scores, putting all the
  pressure on the Wanderers. **(Brawlers 3-2)**

Now it was the Wanderers' final kick. Miss, and they go home. Score, and
they go to sudden death. The player Sal had designated, a steady
defender, walked to the spot and calmly tied the score. **(Tied 3-3)**

Sudden death. The next team to miss while the other scores, loses. The
Brawlers' sixth player steps up. He looks exhausted. He shoots, and the
Wanderers' goalie makes a diving, fingertip save, pushing the ball onto
the post.

The stadium exploded. The Wanderers had a kick to win it all.

Automatically, every head on the Wanderers' bench and in the stands
swiveled to look at Tyler. This was the moment. The hero moment. The
star player, even on one good leg, steps up to win the game. It was a
scene from a movie.

Tyler smiled, a calm, easy smile that didn't seem to fit the suffocating
tension of the moment. He got to his feet, and for a second, everyone
thought he was making the walk. Instead, he walked past Hernandez, past
Sam, and over to a scrawny, terrified-looking kid at the end of the
bench named Franklin, who had played maybe five minutes the entire
tournament.

Tyler put a hand on his shoulder. "Franklin, it's on you, buddy!" he
said, his voice loud enough for the players around them to hear. "I've
seen you take these in practice. You're a natural!"

Tyler had seen no such thing. Franklin's practice shots usually ended up
in the parking lot. But Franklin didn't know that. His eyes went wide, a
deer in the headlights. He looked from Tyler to Coach Sal.

Sal had a great big grin on his face. He knew exactly what Tyler was
doing.

"You heard him, Franklin!" the coach boomed, his voice full of
impossible confidence. "Time to be a hero!"

Up in the stands, Renner couldn't believe it. He physically leaned
forward, squinting, as if his eyes were deceiving him. The Stallions
were murmuring in confusion.

"What is he doing?" Marco whispered, half in awe, half in horror.

Renner was speechless. His entire philosophy was built on the principle
that your best player takes the final shot. You live or die by your
strength. To delegate in a moment of ultimate pressure was not just
cowardly; it was tactical insanity. Tyler was not taking the kick!

Franklin, meanwhile, looked as though he might either faint or throw up.
But then something shifted. He looked at Tyler's unwavering smile, at
his coach's beaming face. They believed in him. Or, at least, they were
acting like they did. He took a shaky breath, nodded once, and began the
long walk to the penalty spot, his legs feeling like they were made of
lead. He was the most unlikely hero, chosen for a moment no one, least
of all him, ever thought would come.
