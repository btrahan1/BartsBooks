# Chapter 16: A Borrowed Clarity

The world shrank to the penalty spot, the goal, and the long, lonely 12
yards of grass separating them. Franklin felt the weight of a hundred
pairs of eyes on his back. His heart was a frantic drum against his
ribs. He was the kid who fetched the stray balls in practice, the one
who made sure the water bottles were full. Now, the entire season rested
on his foot.

He placed the ball down, his hands trembling slightly. He looked up at
the goalie, a big, confident kid who looked like he hadn't missed a meal
in his life. Franklin felt his throat go dry. He was going to miss. He
was going to let everyone down.

Then, he thought of the person who had sent him here. The one who had
put this impossible weight on his shoulders with an impossibly confident
smile. He glanced back toward the halfway line and saw Tyler, still on
his feet, nodding at him. Not a nervous nod. A certain one.

*"WWTD?"* The thought popped into his head, clear as a bell. What would
Tyler do?

The panic began to subside, replaced by a strange, borrowed clarity.
Tyler wouldn't be scared. Tyler would see this as just another problem
to solve, another route to map. *What would Tyler do?*

"Tyler would put it in the back of the net, of course." The answer was
simple. The outcome was pre-determined. Franklin just had to execute the
code.

A new energy surged through him. He wasn't Franklin anymore. He was
playing a part. He was playing Tyler.

He looked back at his team, pointed a single, definitive finger at
Tyler, and gave a sharp, confident salute. A murmur went through the
crowd and the players on the field.

Then he turned his attention back to the goal. He locked eyes with the
goalie. He pointed a finger at the keeper, then jabbed it decisively
toward the top right corner of the net, daring him, *announcing* his
intention. A gasp went through the stands. Up on the bleachers, Renner
actually stood up, his mouth agape at the sheer, unmitigated audacity.

The Brawlers' goalie smirked. A mind game. He wasn't going to fall for
it. Or was it a double bluff?

Franklin took three steps back. He took a breath. He ran forward. His
plant foot was firm. He swung his kicking leg...

And pushed the ball.

It wasn't a shot. It was a pass, firm and true, straight down the
absolute middle of the goal.

The goalie, convinced by Franklin's theatrical point, had already
launched himself into a full-stretch, acrobatic dive toward the top
right corner. He was a magnificent blur of motion, a soaring testament
to athletic prowess. He made a diving save at a ball that wasn't even
there.

Franklin's gentle push rolled over the penalty spot and nestled into the
back of the empty net.

For a full second, there was absolute silence. The kind of silence that
happens when a hundred people are trying to process something their
brains refuse to accept.

Then, pandemonium.

The Wanderers bench exploded. The players on the field sprinted toward
Franklin, who stood frozen on the penalty spot, a look of pure,
unadulterated shock on his face, as if he couldn't believe it had
worked. They mobbed him, lifting him onto their shoulders. He was the
hero.

Tyler was the first one there, laughing, screaming with joy, patting
Franklin on the back. He wasn't the star; he was just another fan
celebrating the unlikeliest of heroes.

Up in the stands, the Stallions were silent. Marco was staring, a slow,
disbelieving smile spreading across his face.

Renner just sank back into his seat. He was speechless. He had built a
life on percentages, on discipline, on the quantifiable superiority of
talent. He had just witnessed a victory achieved through sheer,
unadulterated belief. A game won by the weakest player on the field
because the best player had convinced him he could do it. It wasn't
soccer. It was some kind of magic trick.

And he had to face it, under the lights, in the final.
