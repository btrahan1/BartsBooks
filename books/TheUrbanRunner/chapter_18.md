# Chapter 18: The Yellow Beacon

And then they took the field.

The roar of the crowd was a physical thing, a wave of sound that washed
over the players as they walked into the brilliant glare of the stadium
lights. The pre-game formalities began. The Stallions lined up with
militaristic precision. The Wanderers lined up with the loose-limbed
confidence they'd earned. The referee called the captains to the center
circle for the coin toss.

Rogers, the Stallions' captain, jogged out. And from the Wanderers'
side, jogging with a slight, almost imperceptible limp, came Tyler.

And he was wearing... a goalie's jersey?

It was a long-sleeved, padded shirt in a garish yellow, two sizes too
big for him, the sleeves hanging loose over his wrists. On his hands
were a pair of scuffed, oversized goalie gloves.

The entire Stallions team, standing in their perfect line, did a
collective double-take. Up and down the sideline, a wave of confusion
rippled through the Northgate parents.

Marco, standing at the ready in midfield, stared, his brain refusing to
process the image. His one job, his single, consuming focus for the last
24 hours, was to shadow #10 in blue. Who the hell was he supposed to
mark now?

"What the hell?" Marco blurted out to the player next to him, his voice
a choked whisper.

At the center circle, Tyler just smiled at Rogers' dumbfounded
expression. He called "heads," won the toss, and shook the referee's
hand. As he jogged back to his side, he passed Marco, who was still
rooted to the spot, looking utterly lost.

"Goalie got hurt during that shootout celebration," Tyler said, his
voice casual, as if explaining why he'd changed his shoes. "Pulled a
muscle in his back lifting Franklin onto his shoulders."

Tyler shrugged, the baggy yellow sleeves flapping. "We don't have a
backup goalie, so I volunteered."

He gave Marco a quick wink and continued his jog back to the goal,
leaving his friend standing in midfield like a statue. A soldier whose
enemy had just vanished in a puff of smoke.

On the sideline, Renner looked like he was about to have an aneurysm.
The clipboard in his hand was clenched so tightly his knuckles were
white. The meticulous game plan, the hours of film study, the specific
instructions for Marco---all of it was instantly, comically useless. It
was a perfectly designed cage for a lion, and a hummingbird had shown up
instead.

"WHAT IS HE DOING?!" Renner roared, his voice cracking. He spun on his
assistant coach. "Is this legal? Can he do that?"

The assistant just shrugged helplessly. "He's on the roster, Coach."

Renner turned back to the field. His entire defensive strategy had just
been incinerated. The game hadn't even started, and he had already been
completely and thoroughly outmaneuvered. This wasn't a strategy. This
was a farce. This was the ultimate act of disrespect. Tyler wasn't just
playing a different position; he was playing a different game entirely.

The referee blew his whistle to start the match. The Stallions' striker
kicked the ball to a midfielder. Every Stallions player looked to their
coach, their eyes wide with confusion. What was the plan now?

Marco, his mind still reeling, drifted aimlessly in the center of the
field, instinctively looking for Tyler, who was now 50 yards away,
standing casually in the six-yard box, stretching his hamstrings. He was
a bright yellow beacon of pure chaos.

"MARCO!" Renner screamed, his voice hoarse with fury. "PLAY YOUR
POSITION! FORGET HIM! JUST PLAY!"

But it was too late. The damage was done. The Stallions weren't just
playing against the Wanderers anymore. They were playing against an
unsolvable riddle. And Marco was in the middle of it all, a ghost
assigned to mark an empty space.
