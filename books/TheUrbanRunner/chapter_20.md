# Chapter 20: The Duel


The second half began just as the first had ended: with a relentless
siege on the Wanderers' goal. But the Stallions' frustration was
beginning to show. Their shots became more rushed, their passes a little
less crisp. They were forcing it, trying to blast their way through a
problem that required a scalpel.

Then, against all odds, the Wanderers struck first.

The Stallions had pushed everyone forward for a corner kick---everyone
except their last defender and their goalie. The ball was whipped into
the box. It was a perfect delivery, and a Stallions' header was
rocketing towards the bottom corner.

Tyler moved. It was a pure, explosive reaction. He launched himself
sideways, a full-stretch dive, his body parallel to the ground. His
oversized gloves met the ball with a loud thwack, smothering its
momentum just inches from the goal line. But he didn't stop there. He
flowed into a roll, cushioning his landing and bringing the ball
securely into his chest. Then, from his knees, using the forward
momentum of his own roll, he rocketed a throw down the field.

It was a perfect, arcing spiral, a quarterback's pass that traveled 60
yards in the air. It flew over the heads of the retreating Stallions and
landed perfectly in the stride of Hernandez, who had been waiting at the
halfway line. The last Stallions defender, caught completely
flat-footed, was out of the play. Within two seconds, Hernandez and
another Wanderer were bearing down on the lonely Stallions keeper. A
simple pass, an easy tap-in. 2-on-1 against the goalie.

Goal.

The crowd erupted. The smaller section of Wanderers fans went into a
frenzy. The much larger Stallions contingent fell into a stunned,
horrified silence. The scoreboard now read Wanderers 1, Stallions 0.

On the field, Marco grimaced, the shock giving way to a cold, hard
anger---mostly at himself. He clapped his hands, a sharp, commanding
sound. "Bring it in, guys. Huddle up. Now!"

The Stallions gathered around him, their heads down, their shoulders
slumped.

"Look!" Marco's voice was a low, intense growl. "We are playing like
morons. We're panicking. We're taking stupid shots. We're letting them
get in our heads. Back to basics. Pass. Move. Control the ball. We are
better than this. Forget about him," he jerked his head toward Tyler,
"and play our game."

It was the speech of a captain. It was the speech Renner should have
given, but Marco gave it first. The panic in his teammates' eyes was
replaced by a flicker of their old, disciplined focus.

Finally, they fell back into their rhythm. They stopped trying to force
hero shots from long range. They started playing like the star team they
were, building their attack with patient, intricate passing. The game's
momentum shifted palpably.

The breakthrough came in the 75th minute. It started with a crisp
interception at midfield. A quick pass to a winger, a smart diagonal
run, and then a perfectly weighted ball laid back to the top of the
penalty box, right into the path of a charging Marco.

Marco didn't hesitate. He met the ball with the side of his foot, a
controlled, powerful strike that curled with wicked precision. It was
aimed for the one place no keeper, psychic or otherwise, could reach:
the top right corner, the "upper 90."

Tyler read the play. He moved, he leaped, he stretched every inch of his
body, his fingers straining. He felt the whisper of the ball as it
passed just beyond his outstretched fingertips.

He landed in a heap as the back of the net bulged. 1-1.

He didn't slam the ground in frustration. He didn't yell at his
defenders. He just grinned. A genuine, appreciative grin. He got to his
feet, grabbed the ball from the back of the net, and rolled it out
toward a celebrating Marco.

Across the field, their eyes met. Tyler cupped a hand to his mouth.

"Nice shot, buddy!" he yelled, his voice carrying across the field.
"Nice shot!"

Marco's celebration faltered. He gave Tyler a quick, almost
imperceptible nod. The duel was on. This wasn't about the Stallions
versus the Wanderers anymore. It was about two friends, at the top of
their game, leaving everything on the field.
