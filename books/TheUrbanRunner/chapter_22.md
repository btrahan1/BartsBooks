# Chapter 22: Epilogue: LA Noodles

The stadium was slowly emptying, the roar of the crowd fading to a low,
ambient hum. The bright lights that had made the world feel so epic now
just felt like... lights. Tyler was helping Sal and a few other players
gather up the team's water bottles and pinnies, the quiet, unglamorous
work that comes after both victory and defeat.

Marco walked over, a championship medal now hanging around his neck, his
Stallions warm-up jacket zipped up. The joy of the win had settled into
a kind of deep, weary satisfaction.

"Hey," Marco said, picking up a stray ball. "My parents are grabbing
pizza. You should come. Bring your mom and Maya."

"Thanks, man, but I should probably..." Tyler started, gesturing to the
equipment.

"Don't worry about that," Sal interjected, taking the bag from Tyler's
hands. "Go. Go be a kid. You've earned it."

Before Tyler could answer, a man in a crisp polo shirt with a university
logo embroidered on the chest approached them, a warm, professional
smile on his face. He looked to be in his late 40s, with the easy
confidence of someone used to being in charge.

"Just the two I wanted to see," the man said, his voice smooth and
friendly.

Marco and Tyler exchanged a confused glance.

"Marco and Tyler, right?"

"Uhm," Marco spoke first, ever the more formal of the two. "Yes, sir?"

The man stuck out his hand, shaking Marco's firmly, then Tyler's. "Coach
Rhiner. USC. What a magnificent game! Truly awesome."

The name landed with the weight of a dropped anvil. USC. Not some small
D-II school. A powerhouse. Both boys' eyes went wide.

Coach Rhiner smiled, sensing their shock. "And you two are friends? I
saw the hug. That's just amazing, absolutely incredible. The
sportsmanship, the rivalry... that's what this is all about."

He turned his focus to Marco. "Marco, we've had you on our radar for a
while. You're a phenomenal midfielder. You have the vision, the engine,
and tonight, you showed the leadership of a pro. We'd like to talk to
you about a scholarship."

Marco looked like he'd been struck by lightning. He just stammered,
"Th-thank you, sir. Wow."

Then, Rhiner turned to Tyler, his expression shifting from impressed to
something closer to fascinated.

"And you," he said, a genuine, curious grin on his face. "I came here to
see a forward with incredible speed. Instead, I saw a goalkeeper who
played with the mind of a field general. I have never, in twenty-five
years of coaching, seen anything like it. The athleticism is obvious,
but the intelligence, the sheer audacity... that's un-coachable."

Tyler just shrugged, a little embarrassed. "Our goalie was hurt, sir. I
just tried to help out."

"You did more than help out, son," Rhiner said with a chuckle. "You put
on a masterclass. Look, I don't know what your plans are, and I can't
make any official offers right now, but USC has one of the best computer
science and engineering programs in the country. A kid with a brain that
works like yours... you'd fit right in. We have academic scholarships.
And walk-on spots on the team are always available for... unique
talents."

He handed them both a business card. "Talk to your parents. Talk to your
coaches. Give me a call. It was a privilege to watch you two play
tonight."

With another warm smile, Coach Rhiner turned and walked away, leaving
the two boys standing in the middle of the nearly empty field, under the
slowly dimming lights.

They stood in silence for a long moment, each holding the small, crisp
business card that felt impossibly heavy.

Marco finally broke the silence, a low whistle escaping his lips.
"USC..."

Tyler looked from the card in his hand to his friend. He thought of his
emergency fund, of his mom's paralegal textbooks, of the constant,
grinding race to make rent. He thought of his dreams, the hazy ones that
were suddenly snapping into sharp, brilliant focus.

He looked at Marco and a huge, genuine smile spread across his face.

"Think they have a good noodle place in L.A.?"
