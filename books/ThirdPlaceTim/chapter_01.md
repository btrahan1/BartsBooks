# Chapter 1: The King\'s Road

The dust of the King's Road tasted like every other road Tim had ever
been on: gritty, dry, and full of broken promises. He kicked at a loose
stone, watching it skitter into the tall grass. Each step was a tiny
victory against the boy he used to be. The boy who would have been
hiding in that same tall grass, waiting for a merchant cart to pass, his
fingers light and ready.

The familiar, greasy itch started in his palms. It was a phantom
feeling, a memory of coin purses, warm loaves of bread, and the cold,
smooth skin of a stolen apple. An old instinct, honed by a decade of
hunger, made his eyes scan the road ahead. A lone wagon, pulled by a
sway-backed mule, trundled towards him. The driver was half-asleep, his
chin bobbing on his chest. A canvas-wrapped crate was poorly tied in the
back. Easy. So easy.

Tim's breath caught. His fingers twitched. He could be up on that wagon
in a heartbeat, a knife to the rope, the crate in the ditch, and himself
vanished into the woods before the driver even knew what the bump was.
Steal or starve. The law of the gutter was burned into his bones.

But there was a new law now. His own.

He clenched his hands into tight fists, knuckles white. *Breathe in
strength. Breathe out the itch.* It was the first lesson Old Man Kai had
taught him, back in the city slums, after catching Tim trying to lift
his meager coin pouch. Instead of breaking his fingers, the old man had
offered him a bowl of rice and a new way to use his hands.

Tim closed his eyes and focused. He reached for the center of himself,
for the small, warm pool of energy he was just learning to command. His
Ki. It wasn\'t a mighty river like Kai\'s, or a crackling fire like the
dojo masters he'd spied on. Tim\'s Ki was a slow, warm trickle, like
honey. He drew it up from his gut, nudged it down his arms, and let it
pool in his aching palms. The warmth spread, a low hum that vibrated
through his bones, and the greasy itch of the thief began to fade. The
warmth was real. It was his. He had earned it through endless hours of
practice, of holding painful stances until his legs shook, of punching a
splintery post until his knuckles bled.

He opened his eyes. The wagon was passing him now, the driver still
oblivious. Tim's gaze didn't linger on the crate. He looked at the
horizon, where the afternoon sun was beginning to dip towards the low,
gray smudges of the Dragon\'s Tooth mountains. Beyond them, nestled in
the valley, was Devos.

Devos. A proper town with stone walls and a real tournament. His first.

He wasn't aiming for the grand prize. The winner's purse---a hundred
gold crowns and a ceremonial sword---was for the sons of knights and
guild masters, boys who had been training with private tutors since they
could walk. They fought for glory, for their family's honor.

Tim fought for dinner.

Second place was for the ambitious, the ones who tasted glory and wanted
more. They fought with a desperate fire Tim didn\'t possess. He wasn\'t
looking to make a name for himself. A name just got you noticed, and
getting noticed was usually followed by getting trouble.

No, his eyes were on the forgotten prize. The one no one boasted about.
Third place. In Devos, third place paid ten silver crowns.

Ten silver crowns.

To Tim, it was a king's ransom. It was a month of hot meals. It was a
new pair of boots without holes in the soles. It was a dry room at an
inn instead of a leaky spot in a stable. Ten silver crowns was the
difference between a choice and a necessity. It was the price of being
honest.

He adjusted the thin, lumpy pack on his shoulders. Inside was a spare
tunic, a waterskin, and the heel of a loaf of bread wrapped in cloth. It
was all he owned in the world. He fished out the bread, tore off a small
piece, and chewed it slowly, making it last. It tasted of the road, just
like everything else.

The stone walls of Devos were now visible, a dark line against the
greening valley. The tournament started tomorrow at dawn. A flutter of
fear and excitement danced in his stomach. He could do this. He wasn\'t
the strongest. He wasn\'t the fastest. He wasn't the most skilled. But
the streets had taught him to be clever, to endure, and to know exactly
which fights to pick.

And the fight for third place? That was a fight a survivor like him
could win. He just had to be good enough to beat a few people, but not
so good that he accidentally won second. It was a fine line to walk.

Tim picked up his pace, a new energy in his step. The sun was setting,
painting the sky in strokes of orange and purple. He wasn't a thief
slinking in the shadows anymore. He was a competitor, walking right
through the main gate.

He allowed himself a small, rare smile. They could call him what they
wanted. Street rat. Guttersnipe. But by the end of tomorrow, he hoped
they'd have a new name for him.

Third Place Tim.
