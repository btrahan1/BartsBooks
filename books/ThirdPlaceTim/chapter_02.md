# Chapter 2: The Gates of Devos

He thought to himself and smiled. No, he wasn\'t the strongest, not by
far, but the fastest? That was debatable.

He was fast. Very fast. But not in a long race. A meaty-legged town
guard could probably run him into the ground over a mile. But in the
space of ten feet? In that electric moment between a lunge and a
retreat? Tim was a phantom. His fingers were long, once delicate enough
to navigate the hidden clasps of a rich man\'s pouch in a jostling
crowd. Now they were long and strong, calloused from his new work,
deceptively so. His feet? Not fast, but very, very quick.

It was a distinction Old Man Kai had drilled into him during their brief
time together. \"A horse is fast,\" the old man had croaked, tapping
Tim\'s chest with a gnarled finger. \"It covers much ground. You, boy,
are quick. You cover no ground at all, yet you are suddenly not where
you were. There is power in that. The power of surprise.\"

Tim slipped his worn pack from his shoulders and set it on the ground.
From a set of leather loops on the side, he drew his only real
possession. It was the staff his master had gifted him, a parting
gesture of hope. It was short, barely reaching his hip---a stout length
of dark, polished ironwood that anyone else would mistake for a walking
stick. Kai had called it his \"Argument Settler.\"

He gripped it, the familiar weight a comfort in his palm. He took a low
stance there on the dusty road, the lights of Devos beginning to twinkle
in the deepening twilight, and he moved. It wasn\'t a showy display. It
was a series of tight, economical motions. The ironwood stick became a
blur, a shield, a viper. A flick of the wrist sent the tip whistling
past his ear to block an imaginary blow. A pivot on the ball of his foot
brought the staff around in a low, sweeping arc meant to shatter an
ankle. It flowed from his right hand to his left, spun behind his back,
and reappeared with a sharp *thwack* as he jabbed the air in front of
him. He was getting very, very good with it.

He slid the ironwood staff back into its loops and shouldered his pack.
He was no longer just a collection of quick reflexes born of fear. He
was a practitioner. A novice, yes. Untested. But he was something more
than just a survivor.

The gate of Devos loomed, a maw of stone and iron lit by smoky
torchlight. \"Gate tax,\" a guard grunted, not even looking at him, his
hand outstretched. Tim had three copper pennies to his name. The gate
tax was one. He placed the coin in the guard\'s gauntleted palm. The act
felt monumental. He was paying his way.

Inside, Devos was a riot of sound and smell. The clang of a
blacksmith\'s hammer, the shouts of vendors, the smell of roasting meat,
fresh bread, and the less pleasant scent of too many people crammed into
one space. Tim's instincts screamed at him---a thousand pockets to pick,
a thousand opportunities. He pushed it all down, focusing on the hum of
his own Ki, a quiet anchor in the chaotic storm.

He found the tournament grounds on the edge of town. A large board was
posted near the entrance, listing the events. At the very bottom, in
plain, unadorned letters, was: *The Staff*. Next to it, an arrow pointed
toward a smaller, less crowded tent. An old man with a quill and a pot
of ink sat at a rickety table, collecting the five-copper entry fee. Tim
paid it, leaving him with a single, lonely penny.

\"Name?\" the old man rasped.

\"Tim.\"

The man looked up, his eyes rheumy. \"Tim of where?\"

\"Just Tim.\"

The man grunted and scratched the name onto a long sheet of parchment.
\"Brackets will be drawn at sunrise. Don\'t be late.\"

Tim nodded his thanks and stepped away, his heart thumping. It was real.
He was in. He glanced at the list of names already on the sheet. Most
were like his: Brom, Joric, Fen. Simple names. Then, near the bottom, he
saw one that made his stomach tighten. *Lady Elara of House Vance.* The
name itself sounded expensive. He offered a silent prayer that he
wouldn\'t draw her in the first round.

He found a spot behind a hay wagon, out of the main thoroughfare, and
settled down for the night. Using his pack as a pillow, he drew his
short ironwood staff from its loops and laid it beside him. He wasn\'t a
thief in a strange city. He was a competitor. He had a weapon, a name on
a list, and a goal. And for tonight, that was more than enough.
