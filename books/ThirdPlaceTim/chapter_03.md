# Chapter 3: The First Match

The pre-dawn air was cold and damp, carrying the smells of wet
cobblestone and woodsmoke. Tim was up before most of the city, making
his way back to the tournament grounds. The large bracket board was just
as he\'d left it, illuminated by a single, sputtering lantern. Fifteen
entrants. An odd number meant a bye.

He scanned the top half, his eyes finding the name that had worried him:
*Lady Elara of House Vance*. And next to it, the beautiful, blessed
word: *BYE*. He breathed an even bigger sigh of relief. She was in the
top bracket and already through to the quarter-finals. His own finger
traced the path from his name. *Tim*. His first opponent was a man named
Goric. If he won that, he would face the winner of a match between a
Joric and a Fen. That would put him in the semi-finals, against Lady
Elara. Perfect. It was a survivor's plan.

He smiled, a rare, genuine expression, and slipped around the corner of
a large tent into a back alley. He set his pack down, took his ironwood
staff in hand, and began his Kata. He drew a long, slow breath, pulling
his Ki into motion. It was a deliberate current, flowing up his spine,
across his shoulders, and down his arms.

The rhythm shifted. The slow, graceful movements broke apart into a
flurry of sharp, staccato bursts. The staff was no longer a simple
stick; it was a confusing blur of motion. It jabbed forward to strike a
nerve cluster, to make a hand go numb. It swept low to catch an ankle
and destroy a stance. He wasn't fighting like a tournament martial
artist. He was fighting like a cornered rat who had been given a sharp
stick. And he was very, very good at it. He finished, breathing easily,
his body humming with focused energy. It was time to go win a couple of
fights.

The \"arena\" for The Staff was a roped-off square of packed earth
behind the tanner\'s sheds. A grizzled man with a belly that strained
the laces of his jerkin stepped into the circle. \"First match! Goric of
the Westfarms versus\... Just Tim!\"

Tim stepped forward, his heart a frantic drum. His opponent was a wall
of a man, with arms as thick as small logs. He carried a staff of raw,
heavy oak and gave Tim a dismissive smirk. They met in the center,
tapped their staffs on the ground in salute, and the judge grunted,
\"Begin!\"

Goric, as predicted, was not a man for subtlety. He let out a bellow and
charged, swinging his heavy staff in a wide, horizontal arc aimed at
Tim's ribs. It was a haymaker of a swing, meant to end the fight in a
single blow.

Tim wasn\'t there. He took one sharp, explosive step backward, and the
huge staff whistled through the air where he had been a heartbeat
before, the force of it making Goric stumble. Before Goric could recover
his balance, Tim flowed forward. *Thwack!* The hard tip of his ironwood
staff connected squarely with Goric's right kneecap. It wasn't a
crippling blow, but it was sharp and shocking.

Goric roared in anger and brought his staff around in a clumsy
backswing. Tim parried, deflecting the oak staff so it slid harmlessly
past. He danced inside Goric's reach. *Tap.* A sharp jab to the thigh.
*Tap.* A quick rap across the knuckles.

The smirk was gone from Goric's face. He abandoned all pretense of form
and simply charged again, swinging his staff wildly. Tim saw the
telegraphed, clumsy swing coming. This time, he didn\'t retreat. He
moved in.

As the oak staff swept over his head, Tim dropped into a low crouch and
lunged forward, his own staff held low and horizontal. The ironwood bar
swept in and connected with both of Goric's ankles simultaneously.
Goric\'s feet went out from under him, and he crashed to the ground with
a resounding thud.

Before the dust had even settled, Tim was there. He stood over the
winded man, the tip of his ironwood staff resting gently, but
unmistakably, against the side of Goric's thick neck. Goric stared up at
the calm, serious face of the boy who had just dismantled him. He let
out a ragged breath and slapped a dusty hand on the ground. \"I yield.\"

\"Winner: Tim!\" the judge announced.

Tim immediately retracted his staff and offered a hand to the fallen
man. Goric stared at it for a moment before grunting and accepting the
help. \"You\'re a quick little sprat,\" Goric grumbled, rubbing his
knee. \"Never even saw it coming.\"

\"You have a strong swing,\" Tim replied honestly. He gave a small nod
of respect and melted back into the thin crowd. One down. Now, he needed
to watch Joric and Fen. He needed to be ready for the next one.
