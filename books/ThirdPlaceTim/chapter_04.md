# Chapter 4: A Survivor\'s Plan

Tim found a spot near one of the posts holding up the rope barrier and
watched the next match. Joric, the woodcutter, was built like an oak
stump, all aggression and heavy blows. Fen the Rover was leaner and
wirier, constantly circling, defending with a longer, thinner staff. It
was a proper brawl, a contest of endurance and brute force. The crowd
loved it. This, Tim realized, was what they considered a *real* fight.

His own match, by comparison, had been... strange. He hadn\'t traded
blows. He had simply evaded and ended it. His performance had been
memorable for all the wrong reasons. It smacked of something taught in
shadows, not dojos. That kind of attention was dangerous.

The match ended when Fen, tiring, made a mistake. Joric's hickory staff
bashed through his defense, catching Fen squarely in the ribs. The rover
folded, and Joric was declared the winner. That would be Tim\'s next
opponent.

He needed to win, but it didn\'t have to be so convincing. He needed to
look less like a phantom and more like a fighter. He slipped away to a
quiet spot behind the tanner's shed to make a plan.

First: Let Joric press the attack. Block. Parry. Make it look like a
struggle. Second: Take a hit. A glancing blow. Sell it with a grunt, a
staggered step. Make it look earned. Third: Tire him out. Let Joric's
strength break against his defense. Finally, the win. Not a clean trip.
A disarm, maybe, that looked like a lucky break in the chaos of a messy
fight. It went against every instinct, but third place depended on it.

Before his match, a different name was called. \"In the ring, Lady Elara
of House Vance versus\... Roric the Miller!\"

Tim watched from the shadows. Lady Elara entered the ring, her presence
transforming the dusty arena. She carried a staff of pale, lacquered
ashwood that gleamed in the sun. The match began, and Tim saw it. She
was... graceful.

She didn\'t evade like Tim; she flowed. A single, fluid step took her
just outside the arc of a blow. Her staff came up not to block, but to
guide, deflecting the miller's heavy staff with a subtle movement of her
wrists. She was an artist. Every movement was precise, beautiful, and
backed by a steady, controlled hum of Ki. Her defense was a perfect
circle. He searched for a weakness. He didn\'t see any.

The fight was over in less than a minute. Elara stepped inside the
miller's guard, her staff a blur, and with a sharp tap and a twist,
Roric's staff was on the ground. The tip of her ashwood staff came to
rest gently on his chest. Checkmate.

Tim let out a breath he didn\'t realize he\'d been holding. He would be
meeting her soon, in the semi-finals, provided he could get past Joric.
And he\'d just seen, with absolute certainty, that he could not beat
her.

*Perfect.* He wouldn\'t have to throw the match. The loss would be
honest. She was the final, crucial piece of his plan.

The announcer\'s voice boomed again. \"Next match! The winner of the
first round, Tim! Versus Joric, the woodcutter!\"

His moment of observation was over. It was time to get back to work.
