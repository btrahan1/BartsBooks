# Chapter 5: The Lesson

Tim stepped into the ring to face Joric. The woodcutter planted his
feet, gripping his scarred hickory staff, a picture of solid, rooted
strength. The fight began.

Joric wasted no time, swinging his staff in a powerful arc at Tim\'s
legs. Instead of dancing away, Tim stood his ground, dropping his own
staff to meet the blow. *CLACK!* The impact was jarring, a sharp
vibration that shot up Tim\'s arms. Joric pressed, swinging again.
*CLACK!* Another block. It felt clumsy, wrong, but it was working. Joric
believed he had Tim on the defensive.

The woodcutter, smelling blood, put his full weight into a swing aimed
at Tim\'s left shoulder. This was the moment to take a hit. But the
image of Elara\'s flowing defense flashed in his mind. She didn\'t
absorb force; she yielded to it.

As the staff was about to connect, Tim turned his body with the
direction of the swing, letting his shoulder roll backward. The hickory
staff connected with a loud *thump*, but instead of a shattering impact,
the force was guided along his back, dissipating harmlessly. Still, he
had to sell it. Tim let out a sharp cry and stumbled back three full
steps, his left arm hanging limp for a dramatic second.

Joric grinned, seeing his opponent wounded, and charged, raining blows
down on the \"injured\" boy. Now came the third part of the plan: tire
him out. Tim became a master of the defensive fight. He blocked, he
parried, he used his newfound \"flow\" to turn direct hits into glancing
ones. Joric's heavy swings became a fraction slower. His breathing
became a harsh rasp.

The opening came. Joric, gasping for air, committed everything to a
massive, two-handed overhead smash. Tim stepped forward, not back,
bringing his ironwood staff up vertically to meet the blow. For a
heartbeat, the two staves were locked. In that frozen moment, Tim\'s
quickness returned. With a sudden, violent twist of his wrists, he
wrenched his staff sideways. Joric's sweaty, tired grip couldn\'t hold.
The hickory staff was torn from his hands. Joric stood there, blinking,
his empty hands held dumbly in front of him.

\"Winner: Tim!\" the judge announced.

The crowd cheered. They hadn\'t seen a phantom win; they\'d seen a
tough, resilient fighter weather a storm and cleverly disarm a stronger
opponent. Joric gave Tim a weary, respectful nod. \"Well fought, boy.
You\'re a tough nut to crack.\"

Tim simply nodded back. He had won. He had performed. And now, his next
match was against Lady Elara. His honest loss, and his ticket to the
third-place match, awaited.

When they met in the center of the ring, Tim was conflicted. His plan
had been simple: lose honestly. But the street rat in him began to
whisper. He felt if he went all out, 100%, he could win. He could kick
dust in her eyes, deliver a cheap shot, use every dirty trick he knew.
*Win! Take the prize!*

But no. To use those tricks on her would be like throwing mud on a
masterpiece. It would be a victory that tasted of ash. As he squared up
against Lady Elara, he made a choice. He gave her a bow, deeper and more
sincere than protocol required.

He simply stated, \"I await your instruction.\"

The words fell into a shocked silence. Lady Elara\'s perfectly arched
eyebrows rose in surprise, then understanding. A slow smile touched her
lips. She accepted his strange, profound offer.

\"Very well,\" she said. \"Let the lesson begin. Defend.\"

It was no longer a fight. It was a dance. She would demonstrate a
technique, a flawless combination of strikes and blocks, and he would
answer with his own rough, instinctive, street-honed version. He was
outclassed, but his quickness kept him in the game. He was simply trying
to keep up, to learn.

Finally, after a long, flowing exchange, she saw her opening. Following
a parry that forced Tim to over-extend, she stepped in, her staff a
blur. It hooked behind his, and with a twist of her wrists too fast to
follow, she leveraged the ironwood staff from his grip. It flew from his
hand and landed softly in the dust. The lesson was over.

\"You have a serpent\'s quickness,\" Lady Elara said, her voice low
enough for only him to hear. \"But no foundation. You fight like a
survivor.\"

\"It\'s all I\'ve ever been,\" Tim replied.

\"It is a good start,\" she said, a glint of respect in her eyes. \"But
it is not the whole journey.\"

The judge, finally finding his voice, stepped forward. \"The winner, by
disarm! Lady Elara!\"

Tim retrieved his staff. He had lost the match. His plan was perfectly
on track. But in a way that felt more like a victory, he had won the
day. Now, all that was left was one more fight. The fight for third
place.
