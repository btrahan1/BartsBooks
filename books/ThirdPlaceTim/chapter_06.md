# Chapter 6: A Survivor\'s Investment

Lady Elara won her final match with the same effortless precision she
had shown all day, and she was declared the champion of The Staff. What
was of surprise, not just to Tim but to everyone, was that she didn't
leave. Instead, she found a quiet spot on one of the splintery benches
and settled in to watch the final, forgotten match. The match for third
place. Tim\'s match.

His opponent was Fen the Rover, the wiry man who had lost to Joric. Fen
had fought his way back through the consolation bracket, a testament to
his scrappy endurance. He was fighting for the same ten silver crowns.

As Tim stepped into the ring, he saw her. Lady Elara. Her gaze was level
and direct. He didn\'t know what to make of it. He would show her what
he had learned. This match would be dedicated to her grace. That was his
decision, and he would hope it was good enough for third place.

\"Begin!\" the judge barked.

Fen came out fast and aggressive. Tim met him not with frantic evasion,
but with a calm, centered defense. He held his staff in a two-handed
grip, his stance lower, more stable than it had been that morning. He
remembered the feel of Elara's controlled Ki and tried to emulate it.

For the first minute, Tim didn\'t launch a single attack. He was all
defense, a perfect circle of ironwood and movement. He could feel
Elara's eyes on him, and it was a more potent force than any of Fen's
strikes. It made him want to be better, to be cleaner.

Fen grew frustrated, his attacks becoming sloppier. He overextended on a
lunge, leaving himself open. The old Tim would have ended it in a flash
with a disabling strike. The new Tim chose a different path. He flowed
forward and executed a simple, clean, powerful thrust---the same one
Elara had opened their \"lesson\" with.

Fen, surprised by the formal offense, brought his staff up in a clumsy
block. Tim didn't stop. He flowed around the block, a sequence of
strikes---one high, one low, one to the center---that forced Fen into a
desperate, stumbling retreat. The end came with a quiet finality. Fen,
off-balance, attempted a wild swing. Tim stepped inside the blow, swept
his staff low to hook Fen\'s ankle, and pushed gently against his chest
with the shaft of the staff.

It was a simple, elegant takedown. Fen sat down hard in the dust, and
the tip of Tim\'s staff came to rest pointed squarely at his center. It
was a statement of control, not threat. Fen sighed and slapped the
ground. \"Yield.\"

\"Winner of the third-place match\... Tim!\"

The applause was thin, but Tim barely heard it. He helped Fen to his
feet, and as he turned, his eyes met Lady Elara's across the empty ring.
She gave him a single, slow nod. And in that gesture, Tim saw not pity
or mere curiosity, but a clear, unmistakable acknowledgment. Respect.

He had his ten silver crowns. He had his honesty. But as he stood there,
the heavy weight of the coin purse he was about to receive suddenly felt
much lighter than the priceless approval in that single nod. He had won
third place, just as he\'d planned. But he had gained something
infinitely more valuable.
