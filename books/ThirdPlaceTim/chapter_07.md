# Chapter 7: The Factor\'s Square

Ten silver crowns. The coins were cool and heavy in his hand, each one
stamped with the profile of the King. They had a clean weight, an honest
heft. It was more money than Tim had ever possessed at one time. The
practitioner had a plan.

He bypassed the tempting smells of the cookshops and made his way toward
the heart of the city\'s commerce: the Factor\'s Square. It was a vast
plaza dominated by a dozen massive, slate-blackboards covered in a
cryptic scrawl of names and numbers that shifted throughout the day via
some form of civic magic. It was the lifeblood of the kingdom\'s
economy.

Tim felt out of place, but not intimidated. This was just another system
to be learned. He would invest half. Five silver crowns. As he stood in
line, his plan solidified. People need to eat. People always needed
food. Winter was coming, and with winter, certain foods would become
scarce. Prices would rise. He didn\'t know commerce, but he knew people.
He knew the gnawing ache of an empty belly.

He finally reached the front of the line. The factor, a man with
ink-stained fingers and a bored expression, looked down at him. Tim laid
five silver crowns on the counter. The solid *clink* made the factor
raise an eyebrow.

\"I want to buy some rice futures.\"

The man blinked. \"Futures? Boy, do you even know what that means?\"

\"It means I\'m buying rice now, at today\'s price,\" Tim said, his
voice steady. \"For delivery in three months\' time. When winter is
here.\"

The factor stared at him, then shrugged. A deal was a deal. \"Five
silver buys you a contract for five standard bushels of Southern Long
Grain. Delivery on the first day of Deepwinter. You can take the rice
then, or you can sell the contract back for the price on that day.
Name?\"

\"Tim.\"

\"Just Tim?\"

\"Just Tim.\"

The man grunted, sanded the ink, and pushed a contract across the
counter. Tim folded the parchment carefully and tucked it into the
hidden pouch inside his tunic. He now had five silver crowns for the
road ahead, and a single, precious piece of paper that represented a
different kind of future. Not just a meal, but a plan. Not just
survival, but a stake in the world.

As he walked out of the square, he felt a profound shift within himself.
He had come to this city to win ten silver crowns. He was leaving with
five, but he was infinitely richer.
