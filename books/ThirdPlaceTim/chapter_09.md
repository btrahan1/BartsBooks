# Chapter 9: A Choice of Paths

The road leading to the west gate was a chaotic bottleneck. Tim moved
through it with practiced ease, almost at the gate. Lady Elara, still
keeping her distance, saw something achingly familiar. A young thief,
gaunt and with darting eyes, was inching closer to a small boy clutching
a brightly painted wooden bird. The thief was quick. A slight bump, a
feigned stumble, and the toy was gone. The child\'s face crumpled, a cry
just beginning to form.

But the thief was not as quick as Tim.

Before the thief could take a second step, a hand shot out and clamped
around his wrist. The grip wasn\'t brutal, but it was absolute. The
thief froze, looking up in terror. He saw only Tim, whose expression was
not angry, but calm and intensely focused.

In that look, a whole silent conversation took place. Tim saw not a
criminal, but a ghost of himself. He deftly plucked the wooden bird from
the thief\'s numb fingers, knelt, and returned it to the crying child.
\"I think you dropped this,\" he said softly.

He turned back to the thief, who was still trapped by confusion and
fear. Tim held up his other hand and held it open. Inside were two
copper coins---the price of his dinner.

\"It might be time to consider your path,\" Tim said, his voice quiet
but carrying the weight of a lifetime. He took the thief\'s limp hand,
uncurled the fingers, and placed the two coins in the center of the
grimy palm.

He then released the boy\'s wrist, turned, and made his way out of the
city gates, disappearing into the long shadows of the setting sun.

From her vantage point, Elara was stunned. She had expected him to
ignore the theft or deliver a swift punishment. He had done neither. He
had stopped the crime, righted the wrong, and then *paid* the criminal.
It was an act of such profound and baffling empathy that it defied all
logic. He had not just shown mercy; he had offered a choice.

She, Lady Elara of House Vance, would have called the guards. She would
have seen justice done. She would never, in a thousand years, have
thought to show such compassion. She stood frozen long after Tim was
gone, the image of the two copper coins burned into her mind. The boy
known as \"Just Tim\" was not a puzzle to be solved. He was a lesson to
be learned.
