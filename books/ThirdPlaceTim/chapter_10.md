# Chapter 10: The Road to Oakhaven

Three days out from Devos, Tim stopped and pulled a crumpled flyer from
his pack. It was for a smaller gathering in a town called Oakhaven, a
week\'s travel away. His eyes scanned the list of events. *Archery.
Axe-throwing. Unarmed Combat.* Unfortunately, they didn\'t have a staff
competition.

A knot of apprehension tightened in his stomach. Unarmed. Those could
be\... brutal. They were contests of raw strength and pain tolerance. A
month ago, he wouldn\'t have even considered it. But he wouldn\'t have
considered it if not for something new he felt in his chest.
Something\... that wasn\'t there before.

He sat on a mossy rock, closed his eyes, and reached inward. His Ki was
stronger, clearer. The warmth in his chest was a constant now, a small,
radiant center of heat. And below that, deep in his abdomen, something
appeared to be forming. A focal point, a tiny, glowing ember. A nascent
core?

The thought sent a jolt of shock and excitement through him. Old Man Kai
had told him it could take a decade of disciplined practice to form one.
He realized, with a sudden clarity, that the warmth had truly kindled in
two distinct moments: when he had stood before Lady Elara and chosen
respect over victory, and when he had placed two copper coins in a young
thief\'s hand. The core hadn\'t formed from winning. It had formed from
choice. From mercy.

He stood up and began to move, not in his staff kata, but in a new,
experimental way. He practiced his evasive footwork, but this time, he
let his hand lash out, a stabbing spear of fingers aimed at an imaginary
set of eyes. He swept a leg. The hard edge of his palm chopped through
the air. It was still the fighting style of a survivor, full of strikes
to vulnerable targets. But now, it was infused with the steady, humming
warmth of his new core.

He was still quick. He was still clever. But now, he had a foundation.
The brutal world of unarmed combat no longer seemed so insurmountable.
It was just a different kind of argument, and he was learning a new way
to speak.
