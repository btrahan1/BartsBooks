# Chapter 11: An Awful Kind of Training

In a silk-walled pavilion overlooking a manicured training ground, Lady
Elara watched two of her father\'s guards spar with lacquered spears.
Their movements were precise, powerful, and utterly predictable. A
shadow fell over the entrance. It was her attendant, Kael, returned from
his observation.

\"Report,\" Elara said simply.

\"The boy, Tim, is traveling west. He appears to be on his way to
Oakhaven,\" Kael reported.

\"And?\" Elara tapped her lips thoughtfully.

Kael hesitated, a rare break in his composure. \"My Lady, the Oakhaven
games are a minor event. No patronage, negligible prizes. There is no
staff competition there.\"

Elara finally turned her head, her full attention on him. \"Go on.\"

\"He has been observed during his travel,\" Kael said, his stoic mask
tightening. \"Well\... he seems to be training without a weapon.\"

\"Unarmed?\"

\"Yes, my Lady,\" Kael confirmed. \"And\... well\... it\'s simply awful.
But he\'s\... trying?\" The last word came out as a question, the
ultimate condemnation from a man trained in the pure, classical forms.

\"Define \'awful\',\" Elara said, a flicker of amusement in her eyes.

\"His stance is\... opportunistic,\" Kael struggled to explain. \"His
strikes have no form. He aims for the eyes, the throat, the knees. There
is no grace, no honor in it. It is the brawling of a back-alley thug. He
moves like a viper, but without the benefit of fangs. It is\...
unseemly.\"

To Kael, this was a damning critique. To Elara, it was a confirmation.
Tim wasn\'t trying to learn a new, formal style. He was adapting what he
already knew, trying to infuse the desperate tactics of the street with
the Ki and discipline he was just beginning to understand. He was taking
the ugliest, most practical movements and trying to turn them into his
own art form.

\"He\'s not trying to be a martial artist, Kael,\" Elara said softly.
\"He\'s trying to be a better survivor.\"

\"Anything else?\" she asked.

\"He bought a large sack of oats in the last village,\" Kael added.
\"Far more than one person would need for a week\'s travel.\"

Elara smiled. Of course he did. Oats were cheap and filling. A
pragmatist\'s choice. \"That will be all, Kael. Continue to observe from
a distance. Do not engage. Just report.\"

\"My Lady.\" Kael bowed and retreated as silently as he had arrived.

Elara watched the guards complete their flawless, predictable kata. At
that moment, she found it utterly and completely boring. Her thoughts
were on a dusty road to the west, with a strange boy who was practicing
his \"awful\" techniques, methodically and stubbornly, on his way to
another impossible fight.
