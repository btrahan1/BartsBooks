# Chapter 13: The Carriage

The day before he reached Oakhaven, the road became busier. Tim kept to
the side, a small, anonymous figure, when he heard the rhythmic,
powerful drumming of hooves. He stepped back into the treeline as a
magnificent carriage, lacquered in deep blue and pulled by four
perfectly matched grey horses, thundered down the road. The crest of
House Vance was emblazoned in silver on the door.

Tim stood and watched as the carriage passed by. What is a noble doing
here? Oakhaven was a backwater, a place of splinters and sawdust, not
silks and silver. His first instinct was paranoia, but he dismissed it
as ridiculous. He was a nobody. Still, the sight put a knot of unease in
his stomach. He had no idea of the conversation going on within.

Inside the carriage, the rumble of the wheels was a distant hum. Lady
Elara sat opposite Kael, reading a slender volume of poetry.

\"He\'s just ahead, my Lady,\" Kael said, his gaze fixed on the road.
\"On the right. Hood up.\"

Elara did not look. \"Is he well?\"

\"He appears to be. Thinner, perhaps. But he moves with purpose.\" Kael
hesitated. \"The\... \'awful\' training continues. He has changed it,
somehow. It is still without form, but he seems to be practicing throws
and joint locks now. Less striking.\"

\"He is adapting,\" Elara said. \"He has realized that a killing blow
and a winning point are not the same thing.\"

\"A fine distinction for a man who still fights like a cornered
animal,\" Kael noted.

\"All of us are cornered animals, Kael,\" Elara replied without looking
up. \"Some of us are just fortunate enough to have larger cages.\"

She closed her book. \"My father was\... displeased by this excursion.\"

\"Lord Vance expressed his concerns forcefully, my Lady,\" Kael said.
\"He does not understand your interest in a common lumber town\'s
games.\"

\"My father doesn\'t understand anything that cannot be measured in
acres of land or sacks of gold,\" Elara said wearily. She looked out the
window. \"For seventeen years, I have followed the path set for me. I
have become a perfect reflection of my tutors\' teachings. I have never
once had an original thought on the battlefield.\"

She turned her gaze to Kael. \"Then I saw a boy with no training and no
honor in his movements, and in two days, he learned more than my
father\'s guards learn in a year. He adapted. He evolved. He is
everything my training has taught me to disdain, and yet\... he is a
more honest martial artist than anyone I have ever met.\"

The carriage rumbled on, leaving Tim far behind. \"I am not here for the
games, Kael,\" Elara said. \"I am here to see what happens when a
student with a truly open mind is faced with a new lesson. I want to see
what \'awful\' looks like when it\'s put to the test.\"
