# Chapter 17: The Prodigy

In their noisy corner of the tavern, the air around Lady Elara and Kael
had gone still. They had both witnessed the short, brutal match, but
their trained eyes had seen something far beyond a simple brawl.

\"What was THAT!\" breathed Elara, her words a hushed rush of
astonishment. Her usual composure was gone, replaced by the wide-eyed
wonder of a student who had seen a master shatter every rule.

\"That\... that\...\" Kael stammered. \"That was\... I am not sure? It
was not a throw. It was not a lock. It was\... an attack.\"

\"It was more than an attack, Kael,\" Elara corrected, her voice low and
intense. \"Did you see the first block? On the bicep? It wasn\'t just
hard. It was focused. I felt it from here. A pulse of Ki. Not a flowing
stream, but\... a hot spike. He didn\'t just block the blow; he injected
his energy *into* the strike.\"

Her mind raced, replaying the subsequent palm strikes. \"He is
manipulating his Ki on an instinctive level,\" she continued, thinking
aloud. \"He is\... weaponizing it. Concentrating it into a single point
on impact.\"

Kael looked horrified. \"My Lady, that is the theory behind the Sunstone
Fist, the most advanced striking art. It takes a master with a fully
formed core decades to achieve that kind of focus. This boy\... he has
no form, no discipline, no core to speak of! How is that possible?\"

\"Because he has something a master in a comfortable dojo does not,\"
Elara whispered. \"He has the absolute, unclouded intent of a survivor.
He doesn\'t have layers of theory and philosophy to get in the way. He
simply\... wills it to happen. He felt a *justified* anger, and his Ki
responded to it.\"

A slow, disbelieving smile spread across her face. The boy was a
prodigy. A wild, untamed, impossible prodigy. He was inventing
techniques on the fly. He was like a musician born with perfect pitch
who had never seen a sheet of music, creating breathtaking melodies out
of thin air.

\"Kael,\" she said, her voice firm again, her decision made. \"Our
observations are no longer passive.\"

\"My Lady?\"

\"I need to speak with him,\" she said. \"After this tournament is over.
Not as Lady Elara of House Vance.\" She pulled the simple grey hood
further over her face. \"But as a fellow practitioner.\"
