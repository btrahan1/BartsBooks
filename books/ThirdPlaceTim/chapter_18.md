# Chapter 18: The Fortress and The Phantom

The sun dipped below the tall pines, and the tournament organizers lit
smoky torches on either side of the sawdust pit. The final eight
competitors remained. One more win and Tim would be in the semi-finals.

He didn\'t feel comfortable after the last match. The angry Ki he had
used on Tom had left a bitter taste in his soul. It was effective, but
it felt like a regression. He had offered his hand to Tom, but it was
refused with a look of pure hatred. The bully had been beaten, but not
changed.

He caught the last of the previous fight to size up his next opponent: a
local smith named Borin. The man was experienced. He was big and strong,
but not all brawn. He had real skill. His stance was solid, his
movements deliberate, and his Ki was a deep, slow, and incredibly dense
warmth, like the coals of a forge. He was a true martial artist. Facing
him with anger would be a foolish mistake. He would learn from this. He
would prevail, not through anger, but through study.

When their names were called, Tim entered the pit and gave the smith a
deep, respectful bow. Borin returned it. This would be a proper fight.

Tim became a phantom, using his speed to dart in and out, landing quick,
flicking finger-strikes and knife-hand chops at Borin's arms and
shoulders. They were irritating, stinging blows, meant to distract and
create an opening. Borin endured them with stoic patience, a fortress
waiting for his moment.

It came when Tim darted in for another quick strike. The smith moved
with deceptive speed, his large hand shooting out and clamping down on
Tim's wrist. The grip was like a steel vise. Tim was caught.

But this was the study. He didn\'t panic. Instead of resisting the
powerful wrist-lock Borin began to apply, he flowed *with* it. He used
the momentum to spin his entire body around in a tight circle, bringing
him behind the larger man. His wrist was still caught, but the breaking
pressure was gone.

Now he was in close. He drove his heel back into the side of Borin\'s
knee to buckle it. He stomped down hard on the man's foot, pinning it to
the ground. In the same motion, he bent forward, pulling his trapped
arm---and Borin with it---over his own shoulder.

Borin, his knee buckled and his foot pinned, was lifted off his feet. He
crashed to the ground, his grip finally breaking. The crowd roared.

Borin lay on his back for a moment, then a slow, weary smile of pure
respect spread across his face. \"I yield,\" the smith said, his voice
clear and strong. \"Well done, lad. Well done.\"

Tim let out a breath and offered his hand. Borin took it. \"You used my
own lock against me,\" Borin said in admiration as they left the pit.
\"I\'ve not seen a move like that in twenty years of fighting.\"

Tim just nodded. He was in the semi-finals. And he had done it not with
anger, but with respect. He had faced a master and, for a few brief
moments, had become a better student.
