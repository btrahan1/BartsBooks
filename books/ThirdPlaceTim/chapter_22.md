# Chapter 22: The Weight of Distraction

The next day, the sawdust pit was packed for the final four. Tim stood
by the ropes, stretching, his mind a tangled mess. The encounter with
Lady Elara had shaken him more than any fight. He\'d barely slept.

In a secluded corner of the tavern, Elara stood with her two mismatched
guardians. Silas had appointed himself her unofficial bodyguard, while
Kael stood a few feet away, emanating silent, professional disapproval.

\"I don\'t get it, milady,\" Silas rumbled. \"Borin told me the boy\'s
got a name for himself. \'Third Place Tim\'. Why third place? He could
win this whole thing! Can he not win this match?\"

Elara opened her mouth to explain, but Kael\'s crisp voice cut in.
\"Isn\'t it obvious?\"

Silas grunted. \"Obvious to you, maybe, shiny boots.\"

\"Observe,\" Kael said. \"The winner receives five silver crowns, a
champion\'s sash, his name is carved on the tavern\'s wall, and he gains
fame. Attention.\" His gaze flickered to Elara. \"Second place is a
footnote. But third place? Third place receives one silver crown. Just
enough to get by. There is no glory. It is the prize for the man who is
good enough to compete, but not so good that anyone will remember his
name a week from now. The boy does not want to be a champion. He wants
to be invisible.\"

Silas stared, the puzzle pieces clicking into place. The boy wasn\'t
just fighting the men in the ring; he was fighting to stay in the
shadows. Elara listened and felt a pang of guilt. By revealing herself,
she had threatened the very invisibility he fought so hard to maintain.

In the ring, the semi-final match began. Tim\'s opponent was a wiry
trapper named Finn. Tim\'s movements were hesitant, his usual fluid
evasion a half-step too slow. Finn, the trapper, exploited it
immediately, ducking under a clumsy block and locking Tim in a painful
arm-bar. Tim struggled, his face a mask of pained distraction.

He fought listlessly. The fire he\'d had against Tom, the focus he\'d
displayed against Borin---both were gone. He didn\'t exactly throw the
fight; his instincts were too deeply ingrained for that. He was just too
conflicted.

Finn, sensing the lack of resistance, feinted a punch and shot in for a
leg takedown. Tim saw it, but his body reacted a fatal half-second too
late. He went down in a clumsy heap. Immediately, Finn was on him,
expertly transitioning into a chokehold. The edges of Tim\'s vision
began to go dark. The survivor in him screamed to resist.

But what then? He\'d face the final, and then what? He would be the
center of attention. He would have to face her again. The thought was
more suffocating than the chokehold. Losing here was the simpler path.
He stopped struggling and tapped his hand twice on Finn\'s arm---the
signal of submission.

He lay on his back for a moment, the taste of sawdust and defeat in his
mouth. He had lost. His plan, derailed by his own conflicted heart, was
back on track. He pushed himself up, bowed to Finn, and thanked him for
the lesson.

He left the ring without looking toward the corner where he knew she was
watching. He had a final match to prepare for. The match for third
place. The only one that had ever truly mattered.
