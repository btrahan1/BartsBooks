# Chapter 26: The Silent Patron

Three months later, back in Devos, the autumn air was crisp. Lady Elara
stood in the Vance training hall, her ashwood staff resting on her
shoulders. She was not training, merely thinking, when Kael approached.

\"Report,\" she said, without turning.

\"The boy, Tim, has been busy,\" Kael began. \"He is traveling the
southern circuit, from town to town. Still focusing on third place, my
Lady. It seems to be his default strategy. However\... occasionally,
depending on circumstances, he has won a few.\"

\"What circumstances?\" Elara asked, her interest piqued.

\"The circumstances appear to be\... bullies,\" Kael stated. \"In the
town of Riverbend, the tournament favorite was a man known for injuring
his opponents. Tim faced him in the semi-finals and defeated him in
under ten seconds. He then forfeited the final match. He wins when he
believes there is a wrong to be righted.\"

Elara smiled. He was not just honing his skill; he was honing his
character. \"And his style?\"

\"Evolving,\" Kael admitted. \"He has begun incorporating elements of
wrestling and boxing. He has also acquired a new tool: a gladius. A
common soldier\'s weapon. He practices with it daily, but has yet to
enter a bladed tournament.\"

She nodded. \"He is preparing for his promise.\" A warm feeling of pride
swelled in her chest. \"And our investments?\"

From the day Tim had bought his rice futures, a new standing order had
been in place for Kael\'s agents to discretely mirror Tim\'s small,
peculiar investments. A rare, genuine smile touched Kael\'s lips.

\"Our five silver crowns in rice futures, my Lady? An early frost
crippled the late harvest. The price of rice has tripled. Our return is
fifteen silver.\" He paused. \"He has also invested in salt, wool
blankets, and iron ingots. My Lady, the boy\'s portfolio, though
minuscule, is outperforming our own House analysts by a factor of four.
He doesn\'t understand markets, but he understands\... need. And it
appears that need is a far more reliable indicator of value.\"

Elara laughed, a clear, bright sound. Tim was not just a martial
prodigy. He was a financial one. The year of waiting suddenly seemed
shorter.

The report concluded, but Elara sensed there was more. \"Something
else?\"

Kael shifted uncomfortably. \"There is a complication, my Lady. He
won\'t fight nobles. Three times now, he has advanced to face a
competitor from a noble house. Each time, he feigns injury or simply
forfeits. It is a logical, if\... cowardly, survival tactic.\"

\"This will be a problem,\" Elara said softly. \"The Devos tournament is
filled with the sons and daughters of the nobility. His promise to me
and his survival instincts are now in direct conflict.\"

\"Indeed,\" Kael said. \"To compensate, he has adapted. He is now
entering two, if not three, different competitions per city. Archery,
wrestling, even a \'first blood\' tournament with his gladius. He is
diversifying. He is hedging his bets.\"

Elara was silent. The problem of the nobles was a significant obstacle.
But his solution\... it was pure Tim. Faced with a wall, he didn\'t try
to break it down. He simply started digging two new tunnels around it.
\"He is a fascinating problem,\" she said, a hint of a challenge in her
voice. \"He has nine more months to solve it.\"
