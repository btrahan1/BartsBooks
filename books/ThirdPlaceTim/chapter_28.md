# Chapter 28: The Broken Wheel

The road winding through the foothills of the Dragon\'s Tooth mountains
was a lonely, rock-strewn track. Kael, hidden amongst a cluster of
slate-grey boulders on a high ridge, smiled as he watched the scene
unfold below. It was a masterpiece of subtle orchestration, a play set
for an audience of one.

He had personally seen to the cart that morning, a critical cotter pin
replaced with a piece of brittle, flawed iron. He had also subtly guided
a crew of local bandits with tavern rumors of a \"land-poor old knight\"
traveling with a \"strongbox.\" Greed was a reliable motivator. He
couldn\'t help his rising anticipation as all the pieces came together,
just as Tim rounded the bend.

From Tim\'s perspective, it was a scene of sudden chaos. He heard
shouting, and as he came around a rock outcropping, he saw a small cart,
one of its wheels lying uselessly in the dirt. An old man, bald and wiry
with a magnificent white mustache, stood with his back to the broken
cart, a longsword held in a surprisingly steady two-handed grip. This
was Ser Roric the Bald. Facing him were four ragged men armed with clubs
and rusty short swords.

\"Just give us the box, old man!\" the leader snarled.

\"You\'ll get this box when you pry it from my cold, dead hands, you
ill-bred louts!\" Ser Roric retorted.

Tim's hand went immediately to the hilt of his gladius. His first
instinct was to fade back, to avoid the trouble. But then he saw the old
man. The defiant, honorable stance. The impossible odds. It was not a
fair fight. And Tim had a problem with unfair fights. This wasn\'t a
noble in a ring. This was a man on the road. A fellow traveler.

With a sigh that was part resignation and part resolve, he stepped out
from behind the rock.

\"I make it two against four,\" Tim called out, his voice clear and
steady. \"Slightly better odds, wouldn\'t you say?\"

The four bandits whirled around and broke into sneering laughter. \"Look
at this! The old man\'s got a puppy to defend him!\"

Ser Roric, however, did not laugh. He glanced over his shoulder, his
experienced eyes taking in Tim\'s calm demeanor and balanced stance. The
old knight\'s eyebrows rose in surprise.

\"Stay sharp, lad,\" Ser Roric grunted. \"They\'re short on brains, but
long on brutality. You take the two on the left.\"

\"Yes, Ser,\" Tim replied, the honorific feeling strange on his tongue.

The bandits, tired of talk, charged. From his perch, Kael leaned
forward, his professional detachment melting away into pure excitement.
The curtain was up. The play had begun.
