# Chapter 29: A Piece of Haberdashery

As the two men charged him, Tim felt a slight pang of sympathy. They
were gaunt beneath the grime. They didn\'t seem desperate, just\...
hungry. This was not a time to kill. It was a time to bring the law. A
new phrase surfaced in his mind, unbidden: *Compassionate Discipline*.

From his perch, Kael was confused. He expected an explosion of motion.
Instead, Tim did something bizarre. As the bandits closed in, he didn\'t
draw his gladius. He unbuckled his leather belt and whipped it free.

The first bandit swung his club. Tim stepped in, and with a flick of his
wrist, the belt\'s heavy brass buckle connected squarely with the
bandit\'s knuckles. *CRACK!* The man yelped, and his club dropped into
the dirt. The second bandit jabbed with his own club. Tim parried the
blow with his forearm while his other hand lashed out with the belt,
wrapping it around the bandit\'s ankle. Tim yanked hard, and the man
landed in a heap.

Meanwhile, Ser Roric was a wall of steel, his longsword easily
deflecting the attacks of the other two. \"Get the boy!\" the leader
cursed.

Now it was three on one. Tim was a whirlwind of motion, his leather belt
a confusing, stinging blur. *Whap!* The buckle caught one man across the
cheek. *Snap!* The belt wrapped around another\'s wrist, and a second
weapon clattered to the ground. He ducked a punch and used his footwork
to trip another. He was humiliating them, disarming and stinging them
without taking any real damage himself. He wasn\'t trying to win a
fight; he was trying to end it without bloodshed.

Ser Roric, seeing his opportunity, finally went on the offensive. He
lunged, and the flat of his blade smacked hard against the side of the
bandit leader\'s head. The man collapsed in a heap.

Silence. The remaining bandits, their hunger finally overwhelmed by
their fear, scrambled to their feet and fled into the hills, leaving
their weapons behind.

Tim stood, breathing evenly, and calmly buckled his belt back around his
waist. He looked at Ser Roric, who was leaning on his sword, catching
his breath. The old knight stared at Tim, his eyes wide with a mixture
of disbelief and profound respect.

\"In all my years,\" Ser Roric wheezed, a slow grin spreading across his
face. \"I have never, ever seen a man win a fight with a piece of
haberdashery. What is your name, boy?\"

\"Tim, Ser,\" he replied. \"Just Tim.\"
