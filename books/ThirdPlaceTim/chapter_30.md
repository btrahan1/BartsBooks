# Chapter 30: Porridge and a Promise

\"Well, \'Just Tim\', you have the gratitude of House Orin,\" Ser Roric
said. \"What should we do with this one?\" He gestured to the bandit
leader, who was just beginning to stir.

Tim looked at the fallen man. He saw the fear in his eyes, the same fear
he had seen in the young thief in Devos. *Compassionate Discipline.* Tim
walked over and crouched down, getting on his level.

\"Do you know how to start a fire?\" Tim asked calmly.

The bandit leader blinked. \"Wh-what?\"

\"I\'m hungry,\" Tim said. \"How about you?\"

High on the ridge, Kael dug his fingers into his ears, certain he had
misheard. The boy could not possibly be inviting the defeated bandits to
lunch.

Tim reached into his pack and pulled out his sack of oats and cooking
pot. Then he pulled out the purse Kael had given him and shook out two
silver crowns. \"Here\'s the deal,\" he said. \"You and your friends are
going to help the knight fix his cart. While you work, one of you is
going to cook this pot of oats. Big enough for all of us. When the work
is done and we\'ve all eaten, I\'ll give you these two silver.\"

He looked the man dead in the eye. \"And in return, you\'re going to
take that money, go to the next town, and find an honest meal. This is
your one and only warning.\"

\"Why?\" the bandit finally croaked.

\"Because everyone deserves a chance to choose a better path,\" Tim said
simply. \"Today is your day to choose.\"

He placed the two silver crowns on a rock and walked back to the broken
cart, leaving the bandit leader to stare at the pot of oats as if it
were a chest of gold. Ser Roric watched the entire exchange, his mouth
agape. He had never, in sixty years, seen a boy like this.

On the ridge, Kael slowly lowered his hands. He sank down to sit on a
rock, a feeling of dizzying unreality washing over him. The boy had just
defeated four men and was now rehabilitating them with porridge and a
lesson in basic economics. Lady Elara was going to love this report. And
Kael had absolutely no idea how he was going to write it.
