# Chapter 31: A Fascinating Problem

\"He *what*!?!?\"

The word, sharp and incredulous, echoed off the high ceiling of the
Vance training hall. Kael, who had just begun his report, wisely stepped
back and gave Lady Elara a minute to compose herself. She paced back and
forth, completely and utterly flabbergasted, before finally stopping.

\"Kael, repeat the last part of your report. Slowly. And do not leave
out a single detail.\"

\"Of course, my Lady,\" Kael said, his voice returning to its calm,
factual monotone. \"Well\... it happened like this.\"

He recounted the entire bizarre scene: the employment offer, the shared
meal of porridge, the payment of two silver crowns. \"The bandits, after
a moment of intense suspicion, accepted the terms,\" Kael said. \"After
the meal, the boy held up his end of the bargain. He gave the two silver
crowns to their leader, a man named Jorund. Jorund and his men then
gathered their discarded weapons and laid them at Ser Roric\'s feet.
Jorund stated that they were \'done with this life\' and were going to
use the silver to find honest work.\"

\"You can\'t be serious,\" Elara breathed, sinking onto a nearby bench.

\"I am perfectly serious, my Lady,\" Kael affirmed. \"The boy did not
just win a fight. He won a peace. He did not just defeat his enemies; he
reformed them. He did it not with a sword, but with a belt, a pot of
oats, and a simple, radical act of\... trust.\"

\"And Ser Roric?\" she finally asked.

A small smile touched Kael\'s lips. \"He has, it seems, taken the boy
under his wing. He insisted Tim travel with him to the Springmelt
Festival for safety. He is currently, and I quote, \'instructing the lad
on the proper care of a blade and the bewildering foolishness of the
nobility.\' I believe our intervention was a success, my Lady.
Perhaps\... more successful than we intended.\"

Elara looked at Kael, a slow, brilliant, wondering smile spreading
across her face. Her prodigy was no longer just a fighter or an
investor. He was becoming a force of nature, an agent of change who
operated by a set of rules no one else in the world seemed to
understand. And the next nine months suddenly seemed very, very long
indeed.
