# Chapter 34: The Shadow and The Light

The Springmelt Festival in the Gray Fells was in full swing, a riot of
colorful banners and laughing children. It was to be Tim\'s last stop
before returning to Devos. He sat on a splintery wooden rail, sharpening
his gladius.

\"Something bothering you, boy?\" Ser Roric asked from beside him.

Tim hesitated. \"Yes. This is the last tournament in which I will
compete\... before\...\"

\"Before what, boy? Speak up!\"

\"Before I must return to Devos,\" Tim said, his voice quiet. \"To
fulfill a promise.\" He explained his pact with House Vance, the
requirement to compete for first place.

Ser Roric listened, his expression unreadable. \"So that\'s it,\" the
old knight said, a slow nod of understanding. \"A command performance.\"
He grunted. \"This promise weighs on you. Why? You\'re ready. I\'ve
watched you grow. You are a true fighter. You can win that tournament.\"

\"I know,\" Tim said. \"But what happens after, Ser Roric? I\'ve spent a
year trying to be\... better. But I\'m still just Tim. A boy from the
gutters. What happens when a boy like me wins the grand prize? When he
stands in the light for everyone to see?\" His voice grew quiet. \"I\'m
afraid that if I step into that light, they won\'t see the practitioner.
They\'ll only see the street rat he used to be.\"

Ser Roric was silent for a long time. \"Let me tell you something about
the light, boy,\" he said finally. \"It\'s warm. And it shows you for
what you are, true enough. But it also casts a long shadow.\"

He turned to Tim, his eyes clear and sharp. \"You\'re not afraid of the
light. You\'re afraid of your own shadow. You think it\'s something to
be ashamed of, something to hide. But your shadow is where you came
from. It\'s what gives you your caution, your cleverness, your sympathy
for a hungry bandit. It is the foundation on which the man you are now
is built.\"

He clapped a heavy hand on Tim\'s shoulder. \"You go to Devos. You
fulfill your promise. You fight with all the honor I\'ve taught you, and
all the cunning the streets taught you. And when you stand in that
light, you let them see all of it. The practitioner, the investor, the
philanthropist\... and yes, even the ghost of the street rat. Because
that is who you are. And it is more than enough.\"

Tim looked at the old knight, at the unwavering belief in his eyes, and
felt the heavy weight in his chest begin to lift. His promise was not a
burden. It was a destination. And he was, finally, ready to arrive.
