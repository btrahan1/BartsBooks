# Chapter 36: The King\'s Roads

There were two long weeks of travel back to Devos. The journey was a
quiet, contemplative one, a stark contrast to the boisterous energy of
the Springmelt Festival. Ser Roric stayed with him, to his immense
relief, the old knight\'s presence a grounding force.

One evening, as they sat by a crackling campfire, Tim was deep in his
nightly meditation, tracing the flow of Ki from his Copper Core through
the distinct channels in his body. He opened his eyes.

\"Ser Roric,\" he asked. \"What are meridians?\"

The old knight\'s carving knife paused. He looked up, his expression one
of genuine surprise. \"Meridians, eh? That\'s a high-level question.
Where did you hear that word?\"

\"I didn\'t hear it,\" Tim explained. \"I can\... feel them. When I
circulate my Ki. It follows paths. Like streams.\"

Roric stared at him. \"You can feel your own meridians? By the gods,
boy, that\'s\... not normal. Most practitioners need diagrams and a
master guiding their senses for years.\" He leaned forward, all teacher
now. \"Think of your body as a kingdom. Your core is your capital city.
It\'s where your power comes from. The meridians,\" he said, drawing a
crude human shape in the dirt, \"are the King\'s Roads. They are the
channels that carry your Ki from your core to every last part of your
body.\"

He looked at Tim, his gaze sharp. \"A novice can push Ki into his arms
to make a punch stronger. That\'s like sending a cart of grain down a
muddy track; most of it is wasted. A master knows the roads. He can send
a precise amount of Ki down a specific meridian with no waste and
incredible speed. To have a Copper Core is to have a capital. To feel
your meridians is to have a map of your kingdom.\"

He tossed the stick into the fire. \"What you lack is formal training. A
royal engineer, to use the metaphor, who can show you how to pave those
roads. This promise you made to House Vance\... it may be more important
than you realize. The Lady Elara\... she\'s had the best engineers in
the kingdom since she was a child. She may be the only person who can
truly understand the map you\'re just beginning to read.\"

Tim looked down at his own hands, a sense of awe and deep, humbling
ignorance washing over him. He had a core. He had a map. But he was
standing at the gates of his own capital, with an entire kingdom to
learn, and no idea how to rule it. Devos was no longer just a
destination. It was the beginning of a whole new education.
