# Chapter 39: Three Seconds

Tim walked into the main staff arena, a stark contrast to Lord Valerius,
who entered to a roar from the nobles\' section. Valerius was the
perfect image of a highborn duelist, all silk and confidence, his
silver-inlaid ebony staff a work of art.

Tim looked across at him and bowed respectfully. It was a deep, formal
bow, held a bit longer than necessary. Then, he rose, and looked his
opponent straight in the eyes. And the deference was gone. His eyes were
not challenging or angry; they were calm, clear, and utterly,
unnervingly analytical.

Valerius had been strutting and twirling, ready to show the commoner a
lesson. But when he took in the bow, and then the look in Tim\'s eyes,
he paused. He swallowed. He was not prepared for this profound,
unnerving stillness.

\"Begin!\" the referee shouted.

Valerius flowed forward, his movements the epitome of classical form, a
series of lightning-fast feints culminating in a powerful thrust at
Tim\'s chest.

Tim didn\'t move. At the last possible fraction of a second, he shifted.
It wasn\'t a dodge. It was a simple pivot on the ball of his foot. The
ebony staff slid harmlessly past his ribs. Valerius, expecting
resistance, found only empty air, and his perfect form became a slight
over-extension.

It was all Tim needed.

His own ironwood staff, held motionless at his side, moved. It was a
simple, brutal, upward jab. The hardened tip connected with a sickening
*thud* against the bottom of Valerius\'s staff, just above his hand. The
shock of the impact, delivered with a focused pulse of Ki, was
explosive.

Valerius\'s beautiful, expensive staff was violently knocked from his
grip. It flew upward, spinning end over end, and clattered to the dirt
ten feet behind him.

Silence. The entire arena was utterly, completely silent. The fight had
lasted three seconds.

Tim lowered his staff and looked at the stunned, disarmed nobleman. He
didn\'t gloat. He just waited.

Lord Valerius stared at his empty hands, then at his staff in the dirt,
then back at the calm boy who had just dismantled him with a single,
brutally efficient, and deeply insulting move. His face flushed with a
pure, unadulterated humiliation. This was not an honorable defeat. This
was a public shaming.

From her private box, Elara\'s hand flew to her mouth. It was not a
victory. It was an execution.

\"He has won the match, my Lady,\" Kael whispered, his voice heavy with
foreboding. \"But I fear he has just started a war.\"
