# Chapter 40: As You Wish

In the highest-tiered box, Duke Corvan, Master Valerius, was incensed.
But he went into shock as his son, on the field below, strode up to an
arena guard and pulled the man\'s gladius from its scabbard.

\"A staff is a peasant\'s weapon, boy!\" Lord Valerius roared, pointing
the stolen sword at Tim. \"Let\'s see how you fare against the tool of a
real nobleman! A duel to first blood!\"

The crowd gasped. It was an unprecedented breach of protocol, an act of
profound dishonor. But he was the son of a Duke, and no one dared to
intervene. Lord Valerius stalked towards Tim, vengeance burning in his
eyes.

Tim stood there simply. He looked at the enraged nobleman, then at the
sword. He knew it wouldn\'t end here if he walked away. An enemy like
this, shamed twice, would become an obsession.

With a calm, deliberate motion, Tim laid his ironwood staff on the
ground. He then unbuckled his own belt, the one with his gladius, and
let it fall beside the staff. Unarmed. He stepped forward, his hands
held open and empty. He looked Valerius straight in the eye, his voice
calm and clear.

\"As you wish.\"

The sheer, audacious bravery of the act was stunning. To face an armed
and enraged nobleman with nothing but his bare hands was either madness
or mastery.

\"Draw your sword, cur!\" Valerius snarled.

\"You have a sword,\" Tim replied. \"I do not. You have challenged me to
a duel. The terms are yours. I accept them.\"

He was handing Valerius all the advantage, and in doing so, he was
stripping the honor from the nobleman\'s actions. To cut down an unarmed
opponent was murder, not a duel. Every single person in the arena knew
it.

From her box, Elara watched, her heart pounding. \"What is he doing?\"
she whispered.

\"He is changing the fight,\" Kael breathed. \"He is no longer fighting
Valerius. He is putting their honor on trial, in front of the whole
world.\"

Valerius stood frozen, trapped in a cage of his own making. With a
scream of pure, frustrated fury, he lunged. He would not kill the boy,
but he would mark him. The point of the gladius shot forward, aimed not
at Tim\'s heart, but at his shoulder. A deep, painful, bloody lesson.
