# Chapter 41: A Lesson in Honor

The crowd watched in horrified silence as the steel glinted in the sun.
As the gladius thrust forward, Tim moved. He was no longer a rock; he
was smoke. He swayed, a subtle, fluid shift from the waist, and the
sword sliced through the air where he had been.

Valerius, angered by the miss, devolved into a wild flurry of enraged,
clumsy attacks. Each time, Tim was not there. He moved with a liquid
grace that was both baffling and deeply insulting, making the
nobleman\'s attacks look foolish. He picked up a few nicks---a shallow
cut on his forearm, a thin red line on his cheek---but the minor wounds
only seemed to incense the noble more.

Tim did not strike. He did not attempt a disarm. He simply... yielded.
He was a ghost, giving Valerius every opportunity to stop, to come to
his senses, to end the shameful display. Duke Corvan, in his box, was
ashen-faced, watching his son\'s honor bleed away with every wild,
missed swing.

Finally, Valerius, gasping for breath, put everything into one final,
desperate lunge.

Tim did not evade. He moved *into* the attack. As the sword shot
forward, he flowed inside the lunge. In that single, fluid motion, his
hands, which had been held passive, became weapons. It was a sequence of
precise, \"Ki-infused slaps,\" delivered with the speed of a striking
viper.

*Whap!* The hard heel of his palm connected with the side of Valerius\'s
sword hand, and the gladius dropped from his nerveless grasp.

*Whap!* His other hand slapped the side of Valerius\'s knee, buckling
it.

*Whap!* The blade of his hand connected with the nerve cluster at
Valerius\'s neck.

The entire sequence took less than a second. Lord Valerius, disarmed,
off-balance, and his nervous system reeling, crumpled to the ground. He
landed on his knees, not seriously injured, but powerless.

Tim stood over him, not with a victor\'s pride, but with a profound,
quiet sorrow in his eyes. He knelt down, picked up the fallen gladius,
and held it out to the kneeling nobleman, hilt-first.

\"The duel is over, my Lord,\" Tim said softly. \"You have your first
blood.\" He gestured to the thin, crimson line on his own cheek. \"And I
have my victory. Let this be the end of it.\"

He had won the staff match. He had won the illegal duel. And he had done
it all while preserving the last, tattered shred of his opponent\'s
honor, offering him a path back from the brink. The choice was now up to
the defeated.
