# Chapter 42: A Duke\'s Judgment

The arena remained in a state of suspended animation as Lord Valerius
shakily took the offered sword. The duel was over. But Tim was not
finished. He turned and headed directly for the high-tiered boxes,
stopping below the private box that held the hawk crest of House Corvan.
He looked up at the powerful Duke and bowed.

It was the deep, profound bow of a petitioner, a man of no consequence
before his absolute superior. He bent at the waist, his head lowered,
and he held it. Seconds stretched into an agonizing minute. He was
offering no threat, no challenge. He was giving the Duke all the power,
presenting the nobleman with a choice: crush this commoner who had
shamed his house, or accept this gesture of profound submission and end
the conflict with his own honor intact.

And then he held it longer. The silence became a palpable, crushing
force. The eyes of every soul in the arena were on the Duke, waiting for
his judgment.

Finally, the Duke spoke, his voice a low, strained rumble, magically
amplified. \"Rise, boy.\"

Tim rose slowly, his expression calm.

\"You have\... skill,\" the Duke grated. \"And a\... peculiar sense of
honor.\" He looked down at his son, who was now being helped to his feet
by his squires. \"You have defeated the heir of House Corvan. Twice. You
have publicly shamed him.\"

Tim said nothing. He simply waited.

\"But,\" the Duke continued, his voice shifting to one of cold, hard
finality. \"You also gave him back his sword. You gave him back his
life. And you have offered this house a way to salvage its honor from
this\... debacle. House Corvan does not war with boys over tournament
matches. My son acted with rashness, and you responded with\...
restraint. The matter is concluded. Your victory stands.\"

He paused, then added one final, critical pronouncement. \"You will not
be troubled by my House.\"

It was not a pardon. It was a dismissal. A truce, born of political
necessity and a grudging respect for the boy who had outmaneuvered not
one, but two of the most powerful men in the room. Tim bowed again, a
short, respectful nod. He had his victory. He had his promise. And he
had, against all odds, survived.
