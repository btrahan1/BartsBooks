# Chapter 44: A Seat in the Garden

From his high box, Duke Corvan watched the exchange. And the Duke was
pleased. Immensely pleased. He knew his son had \"issues,\" that he had
the posture of a nobleman but the soul of a spoiled child. But this
strange, rootless boy had done \"something\" the Duke couldn\'t
understand. He had wielded restraint like a weapon and used honor as a
shield. He had taken his son\'s greatest public failure and somehow,
impossibly, transformed it into a moment of growth.

The Duke\'s musings were stopped as he noticed a familiar figure detach
from the shadows. Kael, the stoic shadow of Lady Elara Vance. The Duke
watched as Kael approached the boy. After a brief conversation, they
left the arena together, disappearing through a private exit.

*What was THAT all about?* The pieces on the chessboard suddenly
rearranged themselves. The boy was not a rootless mystery. He was
connected to House Vance. The promise he had to fulfill. Elara\'s
presence at Oakhaven. It all made perfect, alarming sense. They had
found him first.

The Duke's pleasure was now tempered by a cold, familiar rivalry. The
Vances had made their move, revealing their pawn. Now, it was his turn
to contemplate a counter. The boy had said he would be a guest in Corvan
lands. Perhaps, the Duke mused, it was time to extend a formal
invitation.

The appointed place was a small, secluded garden within the Vance
estate. As she waited, Elara felt something she rarely felt. She was
nervous. The prodigy she had discovered, the puzzle she had studied, was
about to become a person, standing right in front of her. She composed
herself just as Kael and Tim made their way toward her.

Tim stopped a few paces away. Kael bowed and retreated, leaving them
alone.

\"My Lady,\" Tim said, his voice quiet but steady. \"I have fulfilled my
promise.\"

A genuine, unguarded smile broke through Elara\'s composure. \"So you
have, Tim,\" she said, her voice softer than he had ever heard it. \"And
in ways no one could have possibly imagined.\" She gestured to the stone
bench beside her. \"Please. Sit. We have much to talk about.\"
