# Chapter 50: The Royal Engineer

**The next morning, Tim stood opposite Elara in the center of the
training yard. The air was crisp, the manicured grounds a world away
from the sawdust pits and muddy roads he was used to.**

**\"Ser Roric told you that your Copper Core is your capital, and your
meridians are the King\'s Roads,\" Elara began, her voice all focus.
\"He was right. You have a capital, and you have a map you can feel.
What you lack,\" she said, tapping her own chest, \"is a royal
engineer.\"**

**She guided him into a stance. \"You feel your Ki move, but you let it
flood. When you fought One-Eyed Tom in Oakhaven, you drove your elbow
into his bicep. You focused your Ki into a spike of pure intent.\"**

**\"It felt... angry,\" Tim admitted.**

**\"It was effective,\" she countered. \"But it was also crude. You
shouted at the meridian in your arm, and it obeyed out of shock. A
master does not shout. A master speaks.\" She took his arm, her touch
sending a strange warmth through him. \"Close your eyes. Feel the road
that runs from your shoulder to your elbow. That is the Sunken River
meridian. Don\'t push your Ki down it. *Invite* it.\"**

**He focused, reaching for the calm center of his core. He found the
path, the channel, and instead of forcing the energy, he coaxed it. A
thin, pure, intensely warm thread of Ki flowed down the path, pooling in
his elbow with a low hum. It felt a hundred times more controlled, more
potent, than the angry spike he'd used before.**

**\"Good,\" Elara said, her voice impressed. \"Very good. You learn
impossibly fast.\"**

**They spent the day like that. She would show him the perfect,
classical form of a staff block; he would show her how a slight,
\"incorrect\" shift of his hips, learned from dodging a lunge in a
brawl, could turn it into an unbalancing throw. He showed her the
instinctive, viper-like strikes Kael had once called \"awful\"; she gave
him the proper names for them, explaining the ancient theories behind
what he had discovered through pure survival.**

**They were two halves of a whole. He was the chaotic, brilliant,
self-taught musician; she was the master technician who could give him
the language of music theory. In the quiet space of the training yard,
they were no longer a Lady and a commoner. They were partners, their
styles beginning to merge into something new, something that was neither
perfect form nor perfect function, but a formidable fusion of both.**
