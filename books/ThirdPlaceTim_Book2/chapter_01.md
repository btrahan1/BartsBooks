# Chapter 1: The White City

The air inside the Royal Ki Academy didn't smell like the road. It
didn't smell like pine, or sawdust, or the honest sweat of a blacksmith.
It smelled of lavender, old parchment, and judgment.

Tim stood in the center of the Grand Atrium, a cavernous hall of
polished white marble that amplified every footstep into a thunderclap.
High above, vaulted ceilings depicted the First Masters descending from
the clouds, their faces serene and superior. Around him, the "Novice
Class of the 402nd Year" gathered.

They were a sea of silk and velvet. Crimson doublets from the southern
vineyards, deep blues from the coastal cities, and the heavy furs of the
northern earls. And in the middle of this riot of color stood Tim, a
grey smudge in a woolen uniform that scratched at his neck.

"He's the one," a whisper drifted from a cluster of boys near a marble
pillar. "The one House Vance sponsored. A charity case." "I heard he was
a stable boy." "I heard he was a thief."

Elara stood next to him, though the invisible chasm between them was
already widening. She wore the deep azure robes of the Third
Circle---the advanced students. By the laws of the Academy, she
shouldn't even be standing near a Novice.

"Chin up," she murmured, barely moving her lips. "They smell fear."

"I'm not afraid," Tim whispered back, his eyes scanning the room,
analyzing the exits, the sightlines, the way the guards held their
halberds. "I'm just calculating how much it costs to heat a room this
size. It's incredibly inefficient."

Elara let out a short, choked sound that might have been a laugh,
quickly disguised as a cough.

A heavy bronze gong sounded, vibrating in Tim's chest. The massive
double doors at the far end of the Atrium groaned open. A line of
instructors filed out, moving with the synchronized, eerie grace of
predators. They wore robes of severe black, trimmed with gold.

At their head walked a man who looked as if he had been carved from
dried driftwood. He was tall, gaunt, and carried a long, thin cane of
white willow. This was Grandmaster Valdis, the Dean of Discipline.

He stopped at the top of the stairs and looked down at the hundred new
students. He didn't shout. He didn't have to. He projected his Ki
outward, a cold, heavy blanket that smothered the whispering crowd into
instant silence.

"You are here," Valdis said, his voice dry as cracking parchment,
"because your blood is iron. Because your ancestors built this kingdom.
You are the elite."

His gaze swept over the crowd, and for a terrifying second, settled
directly on Tim. The pressure increased, a physical weight on Tim's
shoulders. It was a test. A crushing, unseen hand trying to force him to
his knees.

Tim felt his Copper Core flare. Not with anger, but with the stubborn
resilience of a weed pushing through pavement. He didn't fight the
pressure; he grounded it, imagining his legs were roots sinking deep
into the marble floor. He stood straighter.

Valdis's eyes narrowed by a fraction of an inch. He moved his gaze on.

"However," Valdis continued, "Iron is cheap. Iron rusts. Iron breaks. It
is our task to turn you into steel. And the process of making steel...
is fire and hammering."

He slammed his willow cane onto the stone floor. CLACK.

"Novices will report to the Lower Dormitories. Third Circle students
will escort them. Begin."

Elara turned to Tim. "This is it. I have to go to the Upper Spire.
You're in the Lower Dorms."

"I'll be fine," Tim said.

"Tim," she said, her voice urgent. "Remember Kael's warning. They won't
fight you in a ring. Watch your back."

"I know," Tim said. He looked at the sea of hostile faces surrounding
him. "But they don't know the first rule of the gutter, Elara."

"Which is?"

Tim hoisted his simple pack, his eyes clear and sharp. "When you're
invisible, you can go anywhere."
