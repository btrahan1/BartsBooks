# Chapter 3: The Spirit Stone

The first true lesson took place in the Hall of Measurements. It was a
circular room dominated by a large, obsidian monolith standing in the
center---the Spirit Stone.

Master Valdis stood by the stone, his willow cane tapping a rhythmic
beat on the floor.

"You claim to have power," Valdis intoned. "You claim to have potential.
The Stone does not listen to claims. It measures truth."

He explained the process. A student would place their hand on the stone
and push their Ki into it. The stone would glow. White for Novice, Blue
for Adept, Gold for Master. The height of the light on the stone
indicated the volume of Ki. The clarity of the light indicated the
purity.

One by one, the nobles stepped up. Most produced a white glow that
reached their waist or chest. Cassian Redgrave stepped up and roared,
slamming his hand against the stone. A dirty, flickering white light
shot up to his neck. It was powerful, but jagged.

"Raw," Valdis critiqued. "But significant volume. Pass."

Finally, it was Tim's turn.

"Novice 94," Valdis said, his lip curling slightly. "The... anomaly. Let
us see if House Vance's gold bought anything of value."

Tim stepped up to the black stone. It was cold, sucking the heat from
the room. He took a deep breath. *Let them see all of it,* Roric had
said. But Kael had said, *Learn the language.*

Tim placed his hand on the stone. He didn't shove. He didn't roar.

He reached into his gut, to the Copper Core that hummed like a furnace.
He found the meridians Elara had helped him map. He opened the
floodgates, but he kept the flow smooth, laminar, controlled.

He pushed.

The stone didn't flash white. It didn't flicker.

A deep, rich, coppery-orange light began to rise from the base. It
wasn't blinding, but it was dense. Solid. It looked less like light and
more like molten metal rising in a mold. It climbed past his knees. Past
his waist. Past his chest.

It stopped at his chin. A solid, unmoving pillar of strange, copper
light.

The room was silent. The nobles stared. They had never seen that color.

Master Valdis stared at the stone, his unreadable mask cracking for a
brief second. He leaned in, inspecting the light. It wasn't the jagged
white of a novice. It was something... older. Something foundational.

"Copper," Valdis whispered, more to himself than the class. "The color
of the peasantry. The color of coins."

He straightened up, his face hardening. He tapped the cane.

"Volume: Above average. Purity: High. Color: Impure."

He looked at Tim with a gaze that could peel paint. "You have a thick,
peasant energy, Novice 94. Useful for plowing fields, perhaps. We shall
see if it can be refined into something civilized. Go back to your
line."

Tim removed his hand. The light vanished instantly, leaving no residual
fade---a sign of perfect control that Valdis pointedly ignored.

As Tim walked back, he felt the eyes on him. They weren't looking at him
with the disdain of before. They were looking at him with confusion. He
didn't fit their scale. He broke their color wheel.

He stood back in line, crossed his arms, and felt the warm hum of his
core. *Impure,* the Master had said.

Tim smiled internally. *Copper,* he thought. *Conductive. Malleable. And
it survives the weather better than iron.*

He was just getting started.
