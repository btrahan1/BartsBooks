# Chapter 3: The Ink and the Roommate

Room 94-B was not a room. It was a stone box with a window slit that
looked out onto a brick wall. It contained two narrow cots, two wooden
wardrobes that smelled of cedar and despair, and two small desks shoved
into opposite corners.

Tim was delighted. It was dry, it had a door that locked (mostly), and
the stone walls were thick enough to retain heat during the winter. By
his estimation, it was luxury.

He had just finished inspecting the floorboards for loose stash spots
when the door creaked open.

A boy stumbled in, dragging a trunk that looked large enough to contain
a small horse. He was slight, pale, and possessed a head of messy
straw-colored hair that seemed to be vibrating with static electricity.
He wore the grey novice uniform, but on him, it looked like a sack of
potatoes draped over a scarecrow.

He took one step, tripped over his own feet, and the massive trunk
lurched forward, pinning him against the doorframe.

"Oh gods," the boy squeaked. "I'm stuck. I'm stuck and I'm going to be
late for orientation and Grandmaster Valdis will turn me into a newt."

Tim watched for a moment, tilting his head. "I don't think
transfiguration is on the first-year syllabus."

The boy's eyes went wide. He looked at Tim, then down at the trunk
crushing his ribcage. "Please. Help. My name is Christopher. I have a
lung condition. I need air."

Tim stepped forward. He didn't grab the handle. He crouched low, wedged
his shoulder under the corner of the trunk, and applied a short,
controlled burst of Ki to his legs. With a sharp exhale, he pivoted,
using the trunk's own weight to leverage it off the boy and slide it
smoothly to the foot of the empty bed.

Christopher slid down the doorframe, gasping for breath. He adjusted his
glasses, which were hanging off one ear.

"Thank you," he wheezed. "Physics. Leverage. Very impressive. You must
be the..." He stopped, turning red.

"The peasant?" Tim finished, sitting on his own cot. "The charity case?
The anomaly?"

"I was going to say the 'Sponsored Candidate'," Christopher corrected
quickly, dusting himself off. "I'm Christopher. House Pendergast. We...
we make paper. Mostly."

"Tim," Tim said. "House of... nowhere in particular."

Christopher looked around the spartan room and shuddered. "It's a
dungeon. My father said it builds character. I think it builds
scoliosis." He looked at Tim with blatant curiosity. "I saw what you did
in the hall. With Cassian. The tripping? That was brilliant. Suicidal,
but brilliant."

"He was off-balance," Tim shrugged. "Gravity did the work."

Christopher stood up and began frantically unpacking. It was a disaster.
He had three sets of quills, jars of ink, sealing wax, and a pile of
books that looked heavier than the trunk itself. He pulled out a scroll
tied with a heavy gold ribbon---the official Welcome Packet.

"We have to hurry," Christopher muttered, his hands shaking as he
unrolled the parchment. "The schedule says we have the Introductory
History Lecture in twenty minutes in the West Wing, followed by the..."
He squinted at the elaborate, swirling calligraphy. "...followed by the
'Etiquette and Protocol Assessment'."

He looked up, terrified. "I'm going to fail the physical. I know it. I
can't do a pull-up. Can you do a pull-up? I bet you can do a hundred.
Look at your forearms. They're like ropes."

Tim ignored the compliment. He was staring at the scroll in
Christopher's hands. He walked over and looked down at it.

To Tim, it looked like a flock of spiders had danced across the page in
ink. The letters were twisted, looped, and adorned with unnecessary
flourishes. He recognized the Trade Tongue---the simple block letters
used for contracts and ledgers in the city. But this? This was High
Script. The language of laws, treaties, and nobles who had too much time
on their hands.

"Christopher," Tim said quietly. "What does that line say? The third
one."

Christopher blinked. "That? It says: '*All Novices shall present
themselves in proper attire, devoid of family crests, to Master Elspeth
in the Hall of Ancestors at the ringing of the second bell.*'"

Tim felt a cold knot in his stomach. He couldn't read a word of it.

He had learned to read prices, road signs, and simple contracts. But if
the entire Academy ran on this flowery nonsense, he was blind. He would
miss exams. He would misread instructions. He would fail before he threw
a single punch.

He looked at Christopher, who was currently struggling to open a jar of
ink. The lid was stuck. Christopher strained, his face turning pink, his
skinny arms trembling.

"Here," Tim said. He reached out, took the jar, and with a casual twist
of his wrist, popped the lid.

"Oh," Christopher said. "Thank you. I... I have weak wrists. It's a
genetic trait. Pendergasts are built for holding quills, not jars."

Tim looked at the jar. Then he looked at the scroll.

"Christopher," Tim said, a plan forming in his mind. "You're afraid of
the physical classes, aren't you?"

"Terrified," Christopher admitted. "I heard we have to spar. With
sticks. I bruise like a peach, Tim. A peach!"

"And I," Tim said, tapping the scroll, "find this script... inefficient.
It gives me a headache."

Christopher looked at Tim, really looked at him. He saw the calluses on
Tim's hands. He saw the dense, compact muscle. And then he looked at the
scroll, realizing that the boy who could toss a trunk like a feather
couldn't decipher the font.

The gears in Christopher's brain turned. He might have been weak, but he
was a Pendergast, and Pendergasts understood contracts.

"A trade?" Christopher asked, his voice losing its squeak.

"I keep Cassian and the others off your back," Tim proposed. "I help you
with the physical training. I make sure you don't get bruised like a
peach."

"And in return?"

"You read for me," Tim said. "You translate this... spider-web nonsense.
You help me with the history. You make sure I don't look like an idiot
when I open my mouth."

Christopher considered this. He looked at the door where Cassian
Redgrave was prowling the halls. Then he looked at Tim, who was
currently the only thing standing between him and a year of torment.

Christopher smiled. It was a nervous, lopsided smile, but it was
genuine.

"I can read High Script faster than anyone in the year," Christopher
said, a hint of pride in his voice. "And I know the lineage of every
major house going back three hundred years."

"Good," Tim said. He held out his hand.

Christopher looked at the calloused, scarred hand. He reached out with
his own soft, ink-stained fingers and shook it.

"Deal," Christopher said.

"Deal," Tim replied. He grabbed his pack. "Now, read that schedule
again. Where is the Hall of Ancestors? And what is the fastest route
that avoids the main corridors?"

Christopher adjusted his glasses, his panic replaced by purpose. "Third
floor, east wing. And if we take the servants' stairs behind the laundry
chute, we can cut five minutes off the trip."

Tim grinned. "Lead the way, Roommate."
