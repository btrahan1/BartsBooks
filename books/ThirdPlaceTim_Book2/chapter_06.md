# Chapter 5: The Mechanics of Air

The walk to the Hall of Protocol involved crossing the West Breezeway, a
stone corridor open to the elements. The wind whipped through the
arches, carrying the bite of the approaching winter.

Halfway across, Christopher stopped. He leaned against a stone pillar,
his chest heaving rapidly. The adrenaline of the History class and the
encounter with Cassian had burned off, leaving him shaking and gasping.
His face was a pale shade of grey, and his breaths came in short,
shallow whistles.

"I can't..." Christopher wheezed, clutching his collar. "The air... too
thin. My lungs... they're seizing up."

Students streamed past them, offering sneers or indifferent glances. To
them, weakness was a contagion to be avoided.

Tim stopped and stepped in front of Christopher, shielding him from the
wind and the staring eyes. He looked at his roommate not with pity, but
with a mechanic's critical eye.

"You're fighting it," Tim said calmly.

Christopher looked up, eyes wide behind fogged glasses. "I'm...
suffocating..."

"You're not suffocating. You're panicking," Tim corrected. He placed a
hand firmly on Christopher's shoulder, grounding him. "You're treating
the air like it's an enemy trying to break in. You're tightening your
chest to keep it out."

Tim tapped the center of Christopher's chest. "It's tight. A clenched
fist. You can't fill a clenched fist with water, and you can't fill a
clenched chest with air."

"I... don't know... how..." Christopher gasped.

"Stop trying to breathe," Tim ordered. The command was so
counter-intuitive that Christopher froze, his mouth hanging open. "Now,
listen to me."

Tim dropped his voice to that low, steady register he used when he was
focusing his Core. "Your lungs are just sacks. They want to be full. But
you have to invite the air in. You have to accept it."

He took a slow, deep breath, exaggerating the motion of his stomach
expanding, not his shoulders rising.

"Don't sip at it like it's hot soup," Tim instructed. "Drink it. Relax
your throat. Relax your ribs. Let the air fall into you."

Christopher tried, but his breath hitched in his throat.

"Lower," Tim said, moving his hand from Christopher's shoulder to the
center of his own stomach. "Imagine the air isn't stopping in your
chest. Imagine it's heavy, like water. Visualize it flowing down your
throat, past your lungs, all the way down to your gut. To your center."

Christopher closed his eyes. He took a breath. It was shaky, but deeper.

"Calm your mind, calm your lungs," Tim recited, the words flowing out of
him like a mantra he hadn't realized he knew. "The air is fuel. It
belongs to you. Inhale it. Accept it."

Christopher's shoulders dropped an inch. The whistling sound in his
throat faded.

"Now," Tim continued, watching the tension slowly leave the boy's frame.
"Don't hold it. Let it do its work. Let that oxygen flow from your
lungs, down through your center, and push it into your legs. Your legs
are shaking because they're starving. Feed them."

Christopher stood there in the breezeway, eyes closed, swaying slightly.
He took a long, slow breath in through his nose. He held it for a
second, not with tension, but with savoring. Then he exhaled, a long,
smooth rush of steam in the cold air.

The color began to return to his cheeks. The frantic vibration of his
limbs stilled.

"It's... warm," Christopher whispered, opening his eyes. He looked down
at his hands, then at his legs. "I felt it go down. Like... like warm
syrup."

"That's the beginning," Tim said, stepping back and picking up his pack.
"Your body knows what to do, Christopher. You just have to get your
brain out of the way."

Christopher stood up straighter. He took another breath, testing the
mechanism. It worked. He looked at Tim with a mixture of gratitude and
suspicion.

"That wasn't just breathing," Christopher noted, adjusting his glasses.
"My father hired the best physicians in the capital. None of them ever
explained it like that. That sounded like... internal alchemy."

"It's just mechanics," Tim lied smoothly. "My mentor, Ser Roric, taught
me. He said a knight who can't breathe is just a statue in armor."

"Well," Christopher said, exhaling a confident cloud of vapor. "I feel
less like a statue and more like a person who might not faint in the
next ten minutes."

"Good," Tim said, nodding toward the end of the breezeway. "Because if
you faint in Etiquette class, I hear Master Valdis makes you polish the
silverware with your own tunic."

Christopher paled slightly, but he didn't wheeze. He took one more deep,
grounding breath, centering himself as Tim had shown him.

"Right," Christopher said, his voice steady. "Let the oxygen flow. Feed
the legs. Ignore the terror."

"That's the spirit," Tim grinned. "After you, Roommate."
