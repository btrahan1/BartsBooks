# Chapter 7: The Ethics of the Rind

Master Percival clapped his hands, signaling the clearing of the
imaginary soup.

"Now," the Master announced, signaling to the servants who appeared with
baskets of fruit. "The Fruit Course. A true test of dexterity. To
consume a Silver-Skin Apple without touching it with human skin is the
hallmark of civilization."

A servant placed a glistening, pale apple on the plate in front of Tim.
Beside it lay a sharp, small knife and a two-tined fork.

"Master Percival," a voice drawled from the front row. It was Jareth, a
boy with sleek black hair and the crest of a serpent on his ring---House
Vane. He was one of Cassian's lieutenants. "Perhaps Novice 94 could
demonstrate for the class? I understand the lower orders usually consume
fruit by simply... smashing it into their faces. It might be educational
to see if he can operate a paring knife without severing a thumb."

A ripple of cruel laughter went through the room. Cassian leaned back,
grinning.

Percival adjusted his monocle, looking at Tim. "An excellent suggestion,
Novice Jareth. Novice 94, proceed. Peel and quarter the apple. Do not
breach the etiquette of the fork."

Tim looked at the apple. It was perfect. Flawless. It probably cost more
than his boots.

He picked up the knife and fork. The knife felt light, unbalanced. He
held the apple steady with the fork.

"The standard technique," Jareth heckled, "is to cut it into quarters
first, then slice the skin away. Try not to cry if it's too difficult."

Tim looked up, meeting Jareth's sneer with a calm, curious expression.
"Slice the skin away?"

"Yes, peasant. The skin is bitter. It is discarded."

Tim looked back at the apple. He thought about the orchards outside
Devos. He thought about the pickers who worked from dawn until dusk,
their fingers stained and cracked. He thought about the winter of the
Long Frost, when families boiled apple skins to make a thin broth just
to feel something warm in their bellies.

"Discarded," Tim repeated softly.

He didn't cut the apple into quarters. Instead, he pierced the top of
the apple with the fork, holding it vertical. With a fluid, practiced
motion---the same motion he used to whittle kindling for a fire---he
placed the blade against the top of the fruit.

He began to spin the apple with the fork. The knife stayed still,
angling perfectly. A long, thin ribbon of silver skin began to curl away
from the flesh. It didn't break. It spiraled down, translucent and
paper-thin.

The room went quiet. This wasn't the hacking motion of a novice. It was
the surgical precision of a boy who had peeled a thousand potatoes in
the back of a tavern for a bowl of stew.

"You're doing it wrong," Jareth snapped, though he sounded less
confident. "You're supposed to quarter it."

Tim didn't stop peeling. "Master Percival said Ethics is about the Noble
Soul," Tim said, his voice carrying easily through the silent room. "He
said it is about Restraint."

The spiral of skin grew longer, piling neatly on the plate.

"Where I'm from," Tim continued, watching the blade work, "we have an
ethic about fruit, too. It takes a tree five years to bear fruit. It
takes a farmer a whole season of watering, pruning, and guarding against
frost to get a single apple like this."

The last piece of skin fell away. The apple was naked, gleaming white,
with barely a millimeter of flesh lost.

Tim set the knife down. He didn't push the pile of skins to the edge of
the plate as trash. He picked up his fork and neatly folded the ribbon
of skin back onto itself.

"To cut away a quarter of the fruit just to avoid the skin is not
restraint," Tim said, looking at Jareth. "It is arrogance. It tells the
farmer that his labor is trash. It tells the earth that its gift is not
good enough."

He looked up at Master Percival. "True Ethics, surely, is honoring the
effort it took to put the food on the table. Waste is the luxury of a
fool."

He sliced a piece of the apple---flesh and skin together---and ate it.

Jareth opened his mouth to retort, to call him a scavenger, but he
stopped. He looked around the room. The laughter had died.

The students were looking at the fruit on their own plates. For the
first time, they weren't seeing a prop for a test. They were seeing
something that had been grown. Something that had value beyond its
placement in a table setting.

A girl in the second row---the shivering one from the history
class---slowly picked up her knife. She didn't quarter her apple. She
began to peel it carefully, trying to mimic Tim's thin ribbon.

Master Percival stared at Tim. He touched his monocle. His face was a
mask of conflict. The technique was wrong. The protocol was breached.
But the *argument*... the argument was unassailable.

"The... 'Ribbon Cut'," Percival improvised, his voice stiff. "An
archaic, rustic technique. But... it demonstrates a high degree of knife
control."

He glared at Jareth. "And Novice 94 is correct regarding waste. Novice
Jareth, your apple looks like it was attacked by a badger. Perhaps you
should focus less on your neighbor's plate and more on your own
clumsiness."

Jareth flushed crimson and stabbed his mangled apple.

Under the table, Christopher kicked Tim's foot again.

"You're insane," Christopher whispered, his eyes wide with awe. "You
just lectured a noble house on agriculture and made it sound like high
philosophy."

"It's not philosophy," Tim whispered back, spearing another piece of
apple. "It's just math. You can't eat the part you throw away."

He took a bite. The silver skin was bitter, yes. But it was also tough.
And right now, Tim needed to be tough.
