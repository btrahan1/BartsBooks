# Chapter 8: The Weight of Ego

The transition from the Dining Hall to the Academic Wing required
crossing the Cloisters, a square of manicured grass surrounded by arched
stone walkways. It was the social vascular system of the Academy, and
currently, it was blocked.

A knot of students had gathered near the fountain. At the center stood
Cassian Redgrave and Jareth Vane. Between them, clutching her elbows
against the chill, stood the girl from the history class.

"Lyss," Christopher whispered, stopping dead in his tracks. "Lady
Lyssandra Vane. Jareth's cousin. She's... well, she's the 'Runt' of
House Vane. Weak constitution. Minimal Ki."

Tim watched. Cassian was holding a heavy, fur-lined cloak high above his
head. Lyssandra was reaching for it, a look of desperate, silent
humiliation on her face, but she was too short to reach and too
dignified to jump.

"You don't need this, cousin," Jareth sneered, leaning against the
fountain. "You're always cold because your blood is thin. A heavy cloak
just makes you sweat. It's unhygienic."

"Please," Lyssandra whispered. "It's freezing."

"It's a brisk autumn day," Cassian boomed, enjoying the audience.
"Builds character. Isn't that right, Peasant?"

Cassian spotted Tim and Christopher approaching. He grinned, lowering
the cloak slightly, baiting the trap.

"Look," Cassian announced. "The Hero of the Apple Rinds. Come to save
the damsel?"

Christopher gripped Tim's sleeve. "Tim, don't. That's a trap. If you
touch him, it's a brawl. He'll crush you, and Valdis will expel you."

Tim looked at Cassian. He looked at the shivering girl. He calculated
the odds. Physical confrontation: Loss. Verbal debate: Loss (Cassian
didn't care about logic). Social pressure: Loss (Cassian ruled the
yard).

*You can't win,* Tim thought. *So don't try to win.*

He handed his book bag to Christopher. "Hold this."

Tim walked into the circle. He didn't puff his chest out. He didn't
glare. He walked with a slump in his shoulders, his eyes downcast. He
looked exactly like what they accused him of being: a beaten servant.

"Lord Cassian," Tim said, his voice trembling slightly. "I... I realized
my mistake earlier. In the hallway."

Cassian blinked, the predatory grin faltering. He had expected a retort.
A fight. "Your mistake?"

"Yes," Tim said, stepping closer, eyes fixed on Cassian's boots. "I was
clumsy. And then... with the apple... I was arrogant. I spoke out of
turn. I forgot my place."

The crowd murmured. This wasn't the defiance they wanted. It was
pathetic.

Tim stopped three paces from Cassian. He slowly sank to his knees on the
cold flagstones. He bowed his head, exposing his neck.

"I beg your pardon, my Lord," Tim said loud enough for everyone to hear.
"I am new. I am slow. I am... dirt."

Lyssandra stared at him, horrified. Jareth looked bored. But Cassian
looked... disappointed. He held the cloak, but the fun was draining out
of the moment. It's no fun to kick a dog that's already rolling over.

"Get up, scum," Cassian muttered, lowering the cloak. "You're
embarrassing yourself."

"I am only trying to be helpful," Tim said, keeping his head down. He
glanced up, his eyes wide and innocent. "I see you are burdening
yourself with Lady Lyssandra's heavy cloak. It is... remarkably kind of
you."

Cassian frowned. "What?"

"To hold it for her," Tim continued, his voice earnest. "It is heavy.
And you are strong. You are carrying her burden so she can walk
unencumbered. It is the very definition of the 'Ethics of Strength'
Master Percival spoke of. Protecting the weak."

Tim beamed at him. "You are demonstrating the Noble Soul, my Lord. Even
to your own detriment. Look, the fur is shedding on your velvet doublet.
But you don't care. You sacrifice your fashion for her comfort."

Cassian looked down at the cloak. He looked at the lint on his sleeve.

If he kept the cloak, he was a thief bullying a girl. If he threw it on
the ground, he was a petulant child. But Tim had just painted a picture
where holding the cloak was an act of *service*. A servant's job.

Cassian suddenly felt like a coat rack.

"I am not holding it for her!" Cassian snapped. He thrust the cloak at
Tim. "Take the ragged thing. It smells like mildew anyway."

Tim caught the cloak. He didn't stand up immediately. He stayed
kneeling, clutching the fabric like it was gold.

"Thank you, my Lord," Tim breathed. "Your generosity is... confusing,
but vast."

Cassian growled in frustration. He had won the submission, but he felt
like he'd lost the exchange. There was no glory in dominating a boy who
was complimenting his laundry services.

"Come on, Jareth," Cassian spat, shoving past Tim. "The air here stinks
of cowardice."

The bullies stormed off, the crowd dispersing with muttered
conversations. *Did you see him kneel? Pathetic. But... Cassian looked
stupid holding that coat.*

Tim waited until they were gone. He stood up, dusted off his knees, and
turned to Lyssandra. She was staring at him with a mixture of confusion
and disdain.

"You knelt," she whispered. "You let him call you dirt."

"Dirt acts as a cushion," Tim said simply. He stepped forward and draped
the heavy cloak over her shoulders. "It absorbs the impact so the
valuable things don't break."

He adjusted the cloak so it covered her shivering arms. Up close, he saw
her eyes were red-rimmed. She was terrified, not just of Cassian, but of
this place.

"Are you warm now, Lady Lyssandra?" Tim asked.

She pulled the cloak tighter, the heat returning to her frame. She
looked at Tim---really looked at him. She saw that his posture had
shifted. The slump was gone. The tremble had vanished. His eyes were
clear, calm, and utterly unashamed.

"Why?" she asked.

"Because my ego is not worth your frostbite," Tim replied. He offered
her a small, conspiratorial smile. "Besides, did you see his face? He
thought he won, but he walked away feeling like a valet."

Lyssandra blinked. A tiny, almost invisible smile touched the corner of
her mouth.

"You are a strange boy, Tim," she said.

"Just Tim," he corrected. He turned and waved to a stunned Christopher.
"Coming, Roommate? I believe we have a lecture on 'The Geography of the
Highlands'."

As they walked away, Christopher hissed in his ear. "You knelt! To
*Redgrave*! Do you know what that does to your reputation?"

"Yes," Tim said, feeling the Copper Core humming warmly in his gut. "It
makes them think I'm weak. And people stop watching the weak."

He glanced back. Lyssandra was still standing there, wrapped in her
cloak, watching him go.

"And," Tim added softly, "I think we just made a friend in House Vane.
That is worth a bruised knee."
