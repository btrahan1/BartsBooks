# Chapter 9: The Map and the Territory

The Lecture Hall for Geography was a circular room that smelled of dust
and old vellum. The walls were plastered with maps---some peeling, some
pristine---depicting every corner of the Kingdom and the wild lands
beyond.

When Tim and Christopher slid into their habitual back-row bench, they
expected to be alone. But a moment later, a rustle of green silk
announced an arrival.

Lady Lyssandra Vane didn't look at the rest of the class. She kept her
eyes fixed on the floor, clutching her books like a shield, and sat down
in the empty spot next to Tim.

The room went silent. A Vane sitting with a Pendergast and a Peasant? It
was a social suicide pact.

"Lady Lyssandra," Christopher squeaked, looking as if he wanted to phase
through the wall. "You... you know the front row is open?"

"The front row is drafty," Lyssandra said quietly, opening a
leather-bound case to reveal a set of pristine cartography quills. "And
the lighting back here is better for reading details."

Tim smiled. "Welcome to the cheap seats."

Master Harrow, a man who looked like he had been eroded by wind rather
than aged by time, shuffled to the front. He tapped a long pointer
against a massive map of the Northern Border---the Dragon's Tooth
Mountains.

"The Dragon's Tooth," Harrow droned, his voice dry as sand. "A natural
fortress. Impassable. Unconquerable. For three centuries, House Vane has
held the Southern Pass. Why?"

He looked around. "Why is the Tooth considered the linchpin of our
Kingdom's defense? Anyone? Cassian?"

Cassian Redgrave leaned back, looking bored. "Because of the Iron Forts,
Master. My uncle says they are impenetrable."

"Iron rusts," Harrow muttered. "Incorrect. Anyone else? Jareth?"

"Because of the Wyverns," Jareth offered. "The high peaks are nesting
grounds. No army can cross without being eaten."

"Romantic nonsense," Harrow scoffed. "Wyverns migrate."

Tim glanced at Lyssandra. She was staring at the map with an intensity
that was almost frightening. Her lips were moving silently.

"It's not the Wyverns," she whispered.

Tim leaned in. "What is it then?"

"It's the basalt," she murmured, tracing a line in the air with her
quill. "The range is volcanic in origin. The basalt formations create
magnetic anomalies. A compass spins uselessly there. And the aquifers...
the water table is high. It's not just rock; it's a vertical swamp
disguised as a mountain."

Tim blinked. He looked at the map. He remembered crossing the foothills
with Ser Roric. The mud. The way the birds flew in circles.

"Say it," Tim whispered.

Lyssandra shrank back. "I can't. Jareth is watching."

"Jareth thinks birds are the problem," Tim said. "You know the truth.
The truth is heavy. Share the weight."

Lyssandra hesitated. She looked at Tim's encouraging nod. She took a
breath.

"It's the magnetism," she said. Her voice was small, barely a squeak.

Master Harrow paused. "Who spoke?"

Tim kicked her foot gently. *Louder.*

"It is the magnetic variance," Lyssandra said, her voice shaking but
audible. "And the hydro-geology."

Heads turned. Jareth narrowed his eyes. Master Harrow peered into the
gloom. "Explain."

Lyssandra stood up. She didn't look at the class; she looked at the map.
And suddenly, she wasn't the shivering girl in the courtyard. She was an
expert.

"The basalt core of the Tooth disrupts navigational lodestones," she
recited, the facts flowing out of her like water. "An invading army
cannot navigate by compass. They must rely on landmarks. But the high
water table causes frequent landslides, changing the topography weekly.
You cannot map a mountain that moves."

She paused, catching her breath. "It is not a fortress of stone. It is a
labyrinth of confusion."

Silence. Master Harrow blinked slowly. A slow, dusty smile cracked his
face.

"Precisely," Harrow said. "Novice Vane. Five points."

Lyssandra sat down, her face bright red, her hands trembling. But there
was a spark in her eye---a look of pure, intellectual vindication.

"But," Master Harrow continued, "knowing the terrain is one thing.
Exploiting it is another. How does one supply a defense in such a
place?"

The class was stumped again.

Tim looked at Lyssandra. She looked at him. She knew the rocks. But Tim
knew the road.

"You don't use wagons," Tim said, staying seated but speaking clearly.

Harrow looked at him. "Novice 94?"

"If the ground moves," Tim said, "wheels break. I saw a cart lose an
axle on a flat road in the foothills last year." He looked at Lyssandra.
"If the water table is high, that means mud. Deep mud."

Lyssandra nodded eagerly. "Yes! The soil liquefaction index is extremely
high."

"So," Tim concluded, "you use flat-bottomed sledges pulled by oxen. Or
you use the river network during the spring melt." He pointed to a thin
blue line on the map. "The Silver run. It flows south. You float the
supplies down, you don't haul them up."

Lyssandra's eyes went wide. "Because the magnetic anomaly doesn't affect
the water flow! It's gravity! You use the mountain against itself!"

"Exactly," Tim grinned. "Work with the land, not against it."

Master Harrow stared at the two of them. The noble girl who knew the
science of the earth, and the peasant boy who knew the sweat of the
road. It was a perfect, accidental synthesis of theory and practice.

"Remarkable," Harrow muttered. "A logistical hypothesis supported by
geological fact. Ten points. To the... back row."

As the class continued, the dynamic shifted. Lyssandra opened her atlas
and began showing Tim and Christopher the mineral deposits of the
Western Coast. Tim countered by explaining which of those minerals were
actually worth hauling based on current guild taxes. Christopher took
frantic notes on both, realizing he was getting a masterclass in
economic strategy.

When the bell rang, they didn't rush out. They walked out as a unit.

As they reached the corridor, a large shadow fell over them.

Standing there was a boy Tim had noticed earlier---a hulking, silent
youth with broad shoulders and a face that looked like it was carved
from granite. He wore the crest of a Black Bear---House Orso. He was
holding a half-eaten loaf of bread.

"You," the boy said, pointing a sausage-like finger at Lyssandra. "You
know about rocks."

Lyssandra froze. "I... yes?"

The boy turned to Tim. "And you. You know about food."

Tim raised an eyebrow. "I know about not wasting it."

The boy nodded. He took a bite of his bread, chewed thoughtfully, and
swallowed.

"I am Gareth," he rumbled. "I sit with you now."

Christopher adjusted his glasses, looking at the giant. "Why?"

Gareth shrugged. "You are smart. He is clever. She knows where the good
iron is." He patted the sword at his hip. "I am strong. It is a good...
set."

Tim looked at his group. The neurotic scribe who knew the laws. The
genius geographer who knew the land. The silent giant who was built like
a siege engine. And himself, the glue holding it all together.

They were a collection of spare parts. Rejects. Anomalies.

Tim smiled.

"Welcome to the back row, Gareth," Tim said.

As the four of them walked toward the dining hall---the Peasant, the
Scribe, the Geographer, and the Tank---the other students parted ways.
Not out of respect, yet. But out of confusion.

Tim's group wasn't just growing. It was evolving. And Tim realized, with
a surge of excitement, that he finally had the one thing every
successful survivor needs.

A crew.
