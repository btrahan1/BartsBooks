# Chapter 10: The Refectory War

The Refectory was less a dining hall and more a diagram of social
stratification. It was a cavernous room with high, arched windows that
let in drafts of cold air, and the seating arrangement was as rigid as
the stone floors.

To the right, near the roaring hearths, sat the "Golden Circle"---the
sons and daughters of the wealthiest Dukes and Duchesses. They ate
delicate pastries and ignored the rest of the room with practiced ease.

To the left, dominating the long oak tables, were the "Iron Heads." This
was Cassian's territory. They were loud, threw bread rolls at the
first-years, and slammed their tankards down to punctuate their
laughter.

In the center, huddled over wobbly tables in the "Draft Zone," were the
unaffiliated: the scholars, the minor houses, and the scholarship cases.

Tim led his strange little phalanx into the line. The smell of boiled
cabbage and roasted mutton hung heavy in the air.

Gareth moved through the line like a dredging barge. He held two trays.
He piled the first with three loaves of bread and a mountain of mutton.
On the second, he placed a whole roast chicken and a mound of potatoes.
He reached for a third tray.

"Gareth," Tim said gently, intercepting the third tray.

Gareth frowned, looking down at Tim. "I am growing. I need mass."

"You have mass," Tim noted. "What you need is longevity."

Tim reached past the mutton and grabbed a bowl of steamed spinach and a
plate of sliced apples. He placed them on Gareth's first tray, right on
top of the bread.

Gareth looked at the green leaves with deep suspicion. "That is rabbit
food. It makes you weak."

"Look at a castle wall, Gareth," Tim said, keeping his voice low as they
shuffled forward. "It's mostly stone, right? Heavy. Strong. But what
holds the stones together?"

Gareth grunted. "Mortar."

"Exactly," Tim said. "Meat and potatoes are the stone. They give you
weight. But greens and fruit? That's the mortar. Without it, the stones
grind against each other until the wall crumbles. You get sluggish. Your
gut rots. You lose your balance."

Tim pointed to Cassian's table, where the boys were gorging on pure
meat. "Look at them. Strong for the first ten minutes of a fight. But
after an hour? They cramp. They slow down. A siege engine needs oil, not
just rocks."

Gareth looked at the spinach. He looked at Cassian. He looked back at
Tim.

"Mortar," Gareth rumbled. He picked up a fork and shoveled the spinach
into his mouth in one go. He grimaced, but he swallowed. "For the wall."

They paid---Tim noting with annoyance that the academy marked up the
price of bread by forty percent---and navigated the sea of tables.

As they passed the Iron Heads, Jareth Vane stuck his foot out into the
aisle, aiming for Christopher.

It was a classic move. Christopher would trip, spill his soup, and the
table would roar with laughter.

But Tim was walking point. He didn't break stride. He simply adjusted
his path by two inches and stepped firmly on Jareth's extended ankle.

"YOW!" Jareth yelped, retracting his foot.

"Careful," Tim said pleasantly, not stopping. "The floor is uneven
there."

They found a table in the Draft Zone, far from the fire. Lyssandra sat
down, immediately wrapping her arms around herself. The draft from the
window hit her neck, and her teeth began to chatter.

"It's... f-freezing," she stammered.

"It is," Tim agreed, sitting opposite her. "If you try to fight the
cold, you will lose. You are small, Lyssandra. You don't have Gareth's
insulation."

"Thanks," she muttered sarcastically.

"But you have a Core," Tim said. He placed his hand on the table, palm
up. "Christopher, give me your water."

Christopher handed over a tin cup of cold water. Tim held it.

"Watch," Tim said.

He closed his eyes. He didn't tense up. He simply reached into that
warm, copper furnace in his gut. He imagined a small vent opening,
letting a stream of heat flow down his arm, through his palm, and into
the cup.

It wasn't magic. It wasn't fire. It was just focused metabolic heat,
amplified by Ki. After ten seconds, a tiny wisp of steam curled from the
water.

"How?" Lyssandra whispered.

"You're trying to keep the cold *out*," Tim explained, sliding the warm
cup to her. "That makes you tense. Tension constricts your blood flow,
which makes you colder. It's a loop."

He tapped the table. "Reverse it. Don't fight the cold. Generate the
heat. Imagine your Core is a hearth. Don't let the fire roar up the
chimney---that's wasted energy. Bank the coals. Let the heat seep into
your bones, then your blood, then your skin."

Lyssandra gripped the warm cup. She closed her eyes. Her brow furrowed.

"Don't push," Tim chided gently. "Smolder."

She relaxed. A moment later, the shivering stopped. It wasn't
perfect---she was still cold---but she was managing it.

"Better," she breathed, opening her eyes. "It feels... steady."

"Steady is good," Tim smiled.

Just then, a shadow fell over their table.

A girl stood there. She was tall, with perfect blonde ringlets and a
dress of silk that probably cost more than Tim's entire village. This
was Beatrice of House Sterling, the queen bee of the Golden Circle.

She ignored Tim, Gareth, and Christopher entirely, treating them as
furniture. Her gaze fixed on Lyssandra.

"Lyssandra," Beatrice said, her voice dripping with faux-concern. "My
dear, you look positively wretched sitting here in the draft. We have a
space at the warm table. Near the fire."

She gestured to the Golden Circle, where a group of girls were watching
with amused expressions.

Lyssandra looked at the warm table. It was comfortable. It was safe. It
was where a Vane belonged.

Then she looked at her table.

Christopher, who had ink on his nose and was nervously organizing his
peas. Gareth, who was stoically eating an apple, core and all, because
he respected the "mortar." And Tim, who was looking at her with zero
judgment, just patient curiosity.

"The draft isn't so bad, Beatrice," Lyssandra said quietly.

Beatrice's smile tightened. "Don't be silly. You're shivering. And..."
She glanced distastefully at Gareth's mountain of food. "...the company
is hardly suitable for a lady of your station."

Lyssandra wrapped her hands tighter around the warm cup Tim had given
her. She focused on the heat. Not the external heat of the fire, but the
internal heat she was making for herself.

"My station," Lyssandra said, her voice gaining a surprising firmness,
"is where I choose to sit. And I am learning about... thermal dynamics.
It is quite fascinating."

Beatrice blinked. "Thermal... what?"

"It means," Lyssandra said, looking up, "that I am warm enough right
here. Thank you, Beatrice."

Beatrice stood there for a moment, processing the rejection. The Runt of
House Vane had just chosen a peasant and a brute over the elite.

"Suit yourself," Beatrice sniffed. "Don't come crying to us when you
catch a fever."

She spun on her heel and marched away.

Lyssandra let out a long breath, slumping slightly. "Oh gods. I just
said no to Beatrice Sterling. My mother is going to have an aneurysm."

"You did well," Tim said, taking a bite of his bread. "And you stopped
shivering."

Lyssandra realized he was right. In the confrontation, her focus had
intensified. She was warm.

Gareth swallowed his apple---seeds, stem, and all---and looked at
Lyssandra.

"Good mortar," Gareth rumbled, nodding at her.

Christopher looked between them, a slow smile spreading across his face.
"We're going to be pariahs," he said cheerfully. "Absolute social
outcasts."

"Yes," Tim agreed, looking around at his strange, broken, perfect little
team. "But we'll be the smartest, strongest, warmest pariahs in the
Academy. Pass the salt, Roommate."
