# Chapter 11: The Unmovable Object

The meal was winding down, but the tension was ratcheting up. The noise
level in the Refectory dipped as a shadow fell across the Draft Zone.

Torian Hargrave, known affectionately as "The Bull," stopped at the edge
of their table. He was one of Cassian's prime enforcers, a boy built
like a beer barrel with a neck thicker than Tim's thigh.

He ignored Tim. His eyes were locked on Gareth.

"You eat a lot for a charity case," Torian grunted. "My father says
House Orso breeds men like cattle. Big, dumb, and waiting for the
slaughter."

Gareth stopped chewing his apple core. He slowly lowered the remaining
stem to his plate. His dark eyes shifted from his food to Torian. The
air around Gareth seemed to thicken. He placed his massive hands on the
table to push himself up.

"I am not cattle," Gareth rumbled, his voice vibrating in the tabletop.

"Prove it," Torian sneered, slamming his elbow onto the wood, shaking
the cutlery. "Arm wrestling. Right here. Unless you're afraid to lock
horns with a real noble."

The Refectory went silent. This was the entertainment everyone craved. A
clash of giants.

Gareth began to rise, his face darkening with a dangerous, quiet anger.

"Gareth," Tim said softly.

Gareth paused, half-standing.

"Sit down," Tim said, not looking up from his bread. "Digestion requires
blood flow to the stomach. If you engage your muscles now, you'll get a
cramp. It's terrible for the mortar."

"He insulted my House," Gareth growled.

"He insulted your cattle," Tim corrected. "Which is factually incorrect,
as House Orso is famous for its timber, not its livestock."

Tim stood up. He was a head shorter than Torian and half the width. He
walked around the table and stood next to the Bull.

"Torian, isn't it?" Tim asked pleasantly. "Gareth is busy. But I'm
finished eating. And since I am, as you say, a 'peasant', surely
defeating me would be a fine warmup?"

Torian looked down at Tim and laughed. It was a wet, ugly sound. "You?
I'd snap your twig of an arm in half."

"Then it should be quick," Tim said. He placed his elbow on the table.
"And we can all get back to our studies."

Torian scoffed, but the challenge had been issued publicly. To refuse
would look like cowardice. He sat down opposite Tim, the bench groaning
under his weight. He slammed his hand into Tim's.

Torian's hand engulfed Tim's. It was like a bear paw wrapping around a
garden tool.

"Ready to cry?" Torian grinned.

"Ready," Tim said calmly.

"GO!" yelled a student from the crowd.

Torian surged. He didn't hold back. He threw his entire upper body
weight and a massive pulse of jagged, Iron-aspected Ki into the drive.
He meant to slam Tim's hand through the table.

Tim didn't slam.

He breathed. *Inhale. Accept.*

He rooted his feet to the stone floor. He opened the floodgates of his
Copper Core, but instead of pushing *out* against Torian, he channeled
the energy *down*. He became a conduit. He took the massive kinetic
force Torian was applying, accepted it into his arm, flowed it through
his shoulder, down his spine, and grounded it into the floor.

Torian's arm shook with the effort. His veins bulged. His face turned
crimson.

Tim's arm did not move.

It wasn't that Tim was pushing back harder. It was that he simply
refused to be moved. He was a copper rod driven into bedrock.

The grin vanished from Torian's face. He pushed harder, grunting, sweat
popping on his forehead. He poured more Ki into the grip, his hand
becoming a crushing vise.

Tim felt the pressure. His bones creaked. But his Core was humming,
generating that strange, internal heat. His skin began to radiate
warmth. Torian's sweaty palm started to feel uncomfortable, as if he
were holding a hot kettle.

Five seconds passed. Ten. Twenty.

The room was dead silent. Torian was heaving, his entire body trembling
with the exertion. Tim was sitting perfectly still, his breathing
rhythmic and calm. He looked at Torian, his eyes clear and devoid of
malice.

Tim saw the panic starting in Torian's eyes. The Bull was giving 100%.
Tim was holding him at 99%. If this continued, Torian's muscle would
tear, or worse, his pride would shatter in front of the entire school. A
humiliated enemy is a dangerous enemy.

*You can't win,* Tim reminded himself. *So don't.*

Tim waited for Torian to surge one last time, a desperate, gasping
heave.

"You are very strong, my Lord," Tim said softly, only for Torian to
hear. "Iron is impressive."

And then, Tim simply turned off the resistance.

He didn't let go. He just stopped grounding.

WHAM.

Tim's hand hit the table with a resounding crack.

Torian blinked, almost falling out of his chair from the sudden lack of
resistance. He sat there, chest heaving, sweat dripping from his nose,
staring at his hand pinning Tim's to the wood.

Technically, he had won. But he knew. He felt the heat still radiating
from Tim's hand. He felt the fact that Tim hadn't struggled at the end;
he had simply... vanished.

"Winner!" Cassian yelled from the next table, though his voice sounded
unsure. "Torian!"

Torian released Tim's hand. He looked at the boy rubbing his wrist.

"Good match," Tim said, offering a small, polite nod. "You have
excellent explosive power. Though, you might want to work on your
endurance. You're flushing quite red."

Tim stood up and walked back to his friends.

Torian sat there, staring at his own hand. He looked at the dent his
elbow had made in the table. He looked at Tim's back. He didn't cheer.
He didn't boast. He just wiped the sweat from his brow, looking
profoundly confused.

Back at the table, Gareth stared at Tim.

"You gave it to him," Gareth rumbled quietly.

"I gave him nothing," Tim said, sitting down and picking up his apple.
"He slammed my hand to the table. Everyone saw it."

"You held him for thirty seconds," Lyssandra whispered, her eyes wide.
"Torian bends horseshoes for fun. And you weren't even straining."

"Leverage," Christopher said shakily, adjusting his glasses. "It was
just physics. Right, Tim?"

Tim took a bite of his apple. "Physics," he agreed. "And friction. And
heat."

He looked over at Torian, who was now quietly drinking water, refusing
to meet anyone's eye.

"Besides," Tim added, swallowing. "If I had beaten him, he would have
had to fight me for real tomorrow to regain his honor. Now? He thinks he
won, but he's too tired to celebrate. And he's wondering why his hand
feels like he slapped a furnace."

Tim smiled at his stunned crew. "Confusion is a powerful weapon. It
keeps people up at night. Now, finish your mortar, Gareth. We have work
to do."
