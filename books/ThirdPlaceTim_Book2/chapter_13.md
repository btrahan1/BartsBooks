# Chapter 12: The Weight of Silence

The corridors leading to the Hall of Rhetoric were lined with busts of
great orators, their stone mouths open in eternal speech. To Gareth,
they looked like they were screaming.

The big youth walked with a heavy, dragging step. His shoulders, usually
squared and formidable, were hunched. He looked like a man marching to
the gallows.

"I hate this," Gareth rumbled, his voice vibrating in his chest. "I do
not like the talking battles. My tongue is... thick. It trips over the
fancy words. They will ask me questions about the 'Metaphysics of
Authority' and I will say something about wood."

"They count on that," Tim said, walking beside him. "They want you to
play their game. They want a fencing match of words. Thrust, parry,
riposte. Fast and sharp."

"I am not fast," Gareth admitted gloomily. "I am a siege engine."

"Exactly," Tim said, stopping him just outside the door. He reached up
and tapped Gareth's chest. "Gareth, listen to me. When a dog barks at a
mountain, does the mountain bark back?"

Gareth frowned. "No. It is a mountain."

"Right. The mountain just sits there," Tim said. "It waits. It exists.
And eventually, the dog gets tired and goes home."

Tim looked through the open door where Jareth Vane was already holding
court, laughing with a group of scribes.

"They are going to try to swarm you," Tim advised. "They will use big
words and fast questions to make you dizzy. They want you to swing
wildly so you look like a fool."

"So I do nothing?"

"No," Tim said. "You wait. You let them chatter. You let them build
their little towers of logic. And when they are done, and the room is
quiet waiting for you to stumble..."

Tim made a heavy, downward motion with his hand.

"You don't throw pebbles, Gareth. You don't try to fence. You drop a
boulder. One heavy, simple truth. Crush the argument. Don't argue with
it."

Gareth processed this. "Be the mountain. Drop the boulder."

"Simple is not stupid," Tim said fiercely. "Simple is strong. Remember
that."

They entered the room.

Master Aris was a man who loved the sound of his own voice. He wore
robes of flowing crimson and paced the stage like a tragic actor.

"Rhetoric!" Aris proclaimed. "The art of persuasion. The weapon of the
Statesman. Today, we discuss The Paradox of the Servant. Is the servant
who obeys an unjust order virtuous for his loyalty, or wicked for his
compliance?"

He smiled, scanning the room for prey. "Let us find a servant to ask. Or
perhaps... someone who shares their temperament."

His eyes---and the eyes of the class---landed on Gareth.

"Novice Orso," Master Aris said smoothly. "Stand."

Gareth stood. His chair scraped loudly against the floor. Snickers
rippled through the room.

"House Orso," Jareth Vane called out from the side, leaning back in his
chair. "They cut trees, don't they? A noble profession. For beavers."

The class laughed. Gareth's neck turned red. He opened his mouth to
retort, to say that timber was essential, but the words tangled in his
throat.

"Quiet, Jareth," Master Aris chided gently, enjoying the show. "Novice
Orso, consider the question. If a General orders his troops to burn a
village of innocents to deny resources to the enemy, and the troops
obey... are the troops evil?"

Gareth swallowed. "I... the General..."

"Ah-ah," Jareth interrupted. "Don't hurt yourself, Gareth. It's a
philosophical question, not a lunch order. The question is about
*agency*. Does the tool share the guilt of the hand? If I use an axe to
commit a murder, do we put the axe on trial?"

Jareth looked around, grinning. "Well, Gareth? You're the expert on
axes. Do they have souls? Do they have a conscience? or are they just...
thick, heavy lumps of iron that go where they are pointed?"

The insult was plain. Gareth wasn't a person; he was a tool. A dumb
object to be used by his betters.

Gareth's hands clenched into fists at his sides. He wanted to roar. He
wanted to flip the table. He wanted to smash Jareth's smug face.

*Thrust, parry, riposte,* Tim had said. *Don't play their game.*

Gareth looked at Tim. Tim sat perfectly still, his face impassive. He
gave a single, slow nod.

Be the mountain.

Gareth exhaled. He unclenched his fists. He stood to his full height,
expanding his chest. He didn't look at Jareth. He didn't look at the
Master. He looked straight ahead, his gaze heavy and unmovable.

He waited.

The silence stretched. The snickers died down. People started to shift
uncomfortably. Was he frozen? Was he stupid? Or was he... thinking?

Jareth frowned. "Well? We're waiting for the wisdom of the woods."

Gareth turned his head slowly. He looked at Jareth.

"An axe," Gareth rumbled, his voice deep and resonant, filling the room
without shouting, "does not have a choice."

He paused. He let the weight of that sink in.

"But a soldier is not an axe," Gareth continued. "A soldier has eyes. A
soldier has a heart."

He turned to Master Aris.

"If a General orders his men to burn a village of innocents... he is not
a General."

Master Aris blinked. "I beg your pardon?"

"A General protects the realm," Gareth said simply. "The realm is the
people. If he burns the people, he is not a General. He is a bandit with
a fancy coat."

He looked back at Jareth.

"And a soldier who follows a bandit is just another bandit. It does not
matter what the orders are. It matters what the man does."

Gareth sat down.

THUD.

The boulder had dropped.

There were no fancy words. No talk of metaphysics or agency or
paradoxes. Just a brutal, simple moral clarity that shattered the
intellectual game they were playing.

Jareth opened his mouth to make a clever comeback, to twist the logic,
but the words died on his lips. What could he say? That burning villages
was actually complex? That Gareth didn't understand the nuance of war
crimes? Any argument he made now would make him sound like he was
defending the bandit.

Master Aris stood there, his mouth slightly open. He looked at the
simple giant in the back row.

"The... distinction between Title and Function," Aris murmured, looking
at his notes as if they had suddenly changed. "A... valid point. And
efficiently stated."

He cleared his throat. "Five points to House Orso."

Gareth stared at his desk, his heart hammering against his ribs. He felt
a knee bump his own.

"That," Lyssandra whispered, "was profound."

"It was a big rock," Gareth muttered, his face flushing.

"It crushed him," Tim whispered from the other side. "Look at Jareth.
He's trying to figure out how to parry a landslide."

Gareth risked a glance. Jareth was staring at his notes, looking annoyed
and small.

Gareth took a deep breath. He realized something. His silence wasn't
emptiness. It was weight. And for the first time in his life, he didn't
feel heavy. He felt solid.
