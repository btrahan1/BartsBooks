# Chapter 13: The Geometry of Violence

The Training Grounds were a shock to the system after the hushed,
sterile halls of the academic wing. It was a vast, open-air arena of
packed earth and sawdust, smelling of sweat, leather, and the faint,
metallic tang of adrenaline.

"I'm going to die," Christopher announced matter-of-factly. He was
holding a training sword---a weighted wooden *bokken*---as if it were a
venomous snake. "It's too heavy. My wrists are Pendergast wrists. They
are designed for quills, not bludgeons."

"It's not a bludgeon," Tim corrected, adjusting the straps of his own
protective leather vest. "And you're holding it like a frying pan. It's
a lever, Christopher. Physics. Remember?"

Tim moved behind his roommate and adjusted his grip. "Your right hand is
the fulcrum. Your left hand provides the force. Don't lift with your
shoulder; pivot with your hips."

Christopher tried a swing. It was clumsy, but the tip of the sword moved
faster and with less effort.

"Oh," Christopher blinked. "Torque. I understand torque."

"Good," Tim said. "Now, stay behind Gareth. He creates a significant
windbreak."

Gareth stood nearby, holding a massive two-handed practice sword in one
hand. He looked bored. He swung the heavy timber lazily, the air
whooshing with a sound like a tearing sheet.

"We are doing drills," Master Kincaid barked. Kincaid was a scarred
stump of a man who believed that pain was the only reliable teacher.
"Find a partner. First to land three clean strikes wins. Loser runs laps
until they vomit."

The students paired off. The Golden Circle and Iron Heads immediately
sought out the weaker prey.

Tim paired with Lyssandra. She was quick but lacked force. Tim spent the
time showing her how to redirect his slow, telegraphed swings, turning
the sparring session into a quiet dance of geometry rather than a fight.

But a few yards away, a commotion broke the rhythm of the class.

"Stop moving, you little rat!"

It was Cassian Redgrave. He had cornered a slight, terrified boy with
messy brown hair and oversized ears. This was Milo of House Weaver. The
Weavers were a minor merchant house known for textiles, not warriors.
Milo was holding his sword backward, his eyes wide with panic.

Cassian wasn't practicing. He was hunting. He swung his heavy wooden
blade with brutal force, aiming not to score points, but to hurt.

Crack.

Cassian's blade slammed into Milo's ribs. Milo yelped and scrambled
backward, tripping over his own feet and landing hard in the dust.

"Get up," Cassian sneered, looming over him. "My father says Weavers are
only good for sewing dresses. Are you going to sew me a dress, Milo?"

Milo scrambled back, clutching his side. "I... I yield. You win."

"I didn't hear a bell," Cassian said, raising his sword for an overhead
smash. "And yielding is for peers. You are prey."

Master Kincaid was watching from the sidelines, arms crossed. He didn't
intervene. In his mind, this was the hierarchy establishing itself. Iron
sharpening iron. Or in this case, iron crushing tin.

Tim stopped his drill. He looked at Milo, cowering in the dirt. He
looked at Cassian, high on the power trip.

"Gareth," Tim said quietly.

"Yes?" the giant rumbled, stepping up beside him.

"I think Milo needs a lesson in... structural integrity," Tim said. "And
Cassian needs a lesson in spacing."

Tim walked over. He didn't draw his weapon. He didn't shout. He simply
walked into the space between Milo and Cassian.

"Excuse me," Tim said, kneeling down beside Milo. "Milo, isn't it? You
dropped your guard."

Cassian lowered his sword, annoyed. "Get lost, Peasant. I'm teaching him
a lesson."

"And a fine lesson it is," Tim said pleasantly, dusting off Milo's
shoulder. "But you're teaching it all wrong. You're teaching him to
freeze."

Tim looked at Milo. "Milo, look at him. He's big. He's committing his
weight forward. When a big thing leans forward, what happens to its
balance?"

Milo blinked, tears in his eyes. "It... shifts?"

"It becomes precarious," Tim corrected. He stood up and offered a hand
to Milo, pulling him to his feet. Then he turned to face Cassian.

"Cassian wants to hit you," Tim explained to Milo, ignoring Cassian's
reddening face. "He wants you to stand there and block. If you block, he
wins, because he is heavier. So, the lesson is: don't be there."

"Are you lecturing me?" Cassian roared. "I'll thrash you both!"

Cassian swung. A wide, horizontal haymaker meant to take Tim's head off.

"Milo, watch," Tim said calmly.

Tim didn't block. He stepped *in*.

It was the opposite of what fear dictated. He stepped inside the arc of
the swing, entering Cassian's personal space. The wooden blade whistled
harmlessly behind Tim's back.

Cassian, expecting impact, stumbled.

Tim placed a gentle hand on Cassian's chest and gave a small, helpful
push.

Cassian, already off-balance, flailed. His feet got tangled. He went
down hard on his backside, kicking up a cloud of dust.

"See?" Tim said to Milo. "He commits too much. He is top-heavy. Like a
poorly stacked loom."

Cassian scrambled up, roaring with rage. He charged again.

"Now," Tim narrated, stepping to the side like a matador. "He is angry.
Anger makes you blind. It makes you run in straight lines."

Tim stuck out a foot. Just a tap.

Cassian tripped, face-planting into the sawdust.

The class had stopped their drills. Everyone was watching. Master
Kincaid uncrossed his arms, his eyes narrowing.

Milo was staring at Tim. He wasn't seeing a warrior; he was seeing a
magician.

"But... I can't move like you," Milo whispered.

"You don't have to," Tim said. He looked at the Weaver boy. "You're a
Weaver, right? You work with threads?"

Milo nodded dumbly.

"Cassian is just a big, tangled knot," Tim said, gesturing to the boy
struggling to get up again. "Don't try to cut him. Just... pull the
loose thread."

Cassian was up again. He was purple. He raised his sword, screaming.

"Milo," Tim said sharply. "Duck."

Milo ducked. Instinctively.

Cassian's swing went high.

"Step left," Tim commanded.

Milo stepped left.

Cassian's momentum carried him past Milo.

"Stick out your sword. Low," Tim ordered.

Milo thrust his wooden sword out blindly.

Cassian, turning to pursue, walked shin-first into the tip of Milo's
blade.

"YOW!" Cassian hopped on one foot, dropping his sword to clutch his
shin.

"Point to Milo," Tim announced to the silent yard.

He looked over at Master Kincaid. "Three strikes, Master? Or does a
self-inflicted injury count?"

Master Kincaid walked over. He looked at Cassian, hopping and cursing.
He looked at Tim, who stood with open, empty hands. He looked at Milo,
who was staring at his own sword in wonder.

"Redgrave," Kincaid barked. "Stop hopping. You look like a wounded
rabbit."

He turned his gaze to Tim. Kincaid didn't like smart-asses. But he hated
incompetence more. And Redgrave had just been completely dismantled by a
boy who hadn't even drawn a weapon.

"Redgrave, laps. Ten of them," Kincaid ordered. "For lack of balance."

Cassian's jaw dropped. "But---"

"TWENTY!" Kincaid roared.

Cassian snatched up his sword and began to run, shooting a look of pure
venom at Tim.

Kincaid leaned in close to Tim. "You think you're clever, Novice?"

"I think I'm efficient, Master," Tim replied steadily.

"Efficiency is for scribes," Kincaid spat. But there was a gleam of
something else in his eye. "In the ring, cleverness buys you seconds.
Strength buys you survival. Don't mistake luck for skill."

He stomped away. "Resume drills!"

Tim turned to Milo. The Weaver boy was trembling, but this time, it was
from adrenaline, not fear.

"You... you saved me," Milo breathed.

"I introduced you to geometry," Tim corrected. He pointed over to where
Lyssandra, Christopher, and Gareth were waiting. "We're sitting at the
drafty table in the back. The air is better there."

Milo looked at the Golden Circle, then at the Iron Heads. Then he looked
at the strange group of misfits.

"I... I know where to get good wool," Milo offered tentatively. "For
cloaks. Warm ones."

Tim smiled. "Milo, I think you're going to fit in just fine."

As they walked back to the group, Christopher adjusted his glasses.
"Another one?" he whispered to Gareth. "We're going to need a bigger
table."

Gareth watched Cassian running laps. He took a bite of his apple.

"We will build one," the giant rumbled.
