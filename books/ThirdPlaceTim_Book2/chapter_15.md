# Chapter 14: The Hearth and the Hive

The sun had dipped below the western wall, plunging the Academy into
deep twilight. For most students, this meant retiring to their common
rooms to gossip or study by weak candlelight.

For Tim's new squad, it meant loitering in a drafty alcove near the
laundry chutes. They were homeless within their own school. The Lower
Dorm common room was occupied by the Iron Heads, and the library was
strictly "Silence Only," which made planning a revolution difficult.

"We need a headquarters," Christopher muttered, shivering slightly. He
was sitting on his trunk, which Gareth was currently holding up with one
hand to keep it off the damp floor. "My knees are locking up. I'm too
young for rheumatism."

"I'm hungry," Gareth added. It was a statement of fact, as inevitable as
the tide.

"Dinner isn't for two hours," Milo pointed out nervously. "And the menu
says... 'Savory Gruel'."

Tim looked at his motley crew. They were cold, hungry, and marginalized.
This was the exact moment when morale usually broke. Or, if handled
correctly, when loyalty was forged.

"Come on," Tim said, pushing off the wall.

"Where?" Lyssandra asked, pulling Milo's promised woolen cloak tighter
around her.

"To find the heat," Tim said. "And the food."

He didn't lead them toward the Refectory. He led them down. Past the
dormitories, past the laundry rooms, deeper into the stone belly of the
Academy.

"Tim," Christopher hissed, the panic returning to his voice. "This is
the Service Wing. Students are forbidden. It's in the handbook! Page
forty-two, paragraph three: '*The Undercroft is for staff only.*'"

"The handbook also says the Academy provides 'adequate nutrition'," Tim
countered, turning a corner. "Since the gruel is mostly water, the
handbook is already a work of fiction. Why shouldn't we treat page
forty-two the same way?"

They rounded a final corner and were hit by a wall of warmth and the
smell of yeast. They stood before the double doors of the Great
Kitchens.

Tim didn't knock. He didn't sneak. He walked in like he was inspecting
the premises.

The kitchen was a chaotic symphony of clattering pans, shouting voices,
and clouds of steam. Dozens of staff in stained white aprons rushed
about. At the center of the storm stood a woman who looked like she
could bench-press Gareth. She had forearms like hams and a wooden spoon
tucked into her belt like a truncheon. This was Matron Haggerty.

She spotted them immediately.

"OUT!" she roared, her voice cutting through the din. "I don't care who
your daddy is! No students in the prep area! Out before I boil you in
the stockpot!"

Christopher squeaked and tried to backpedal, but he bumped into Gareth.

Tim stepped forward, hands open and empty. He bowed. Not a mocking bow,
but a deep, sincere bow of respect.

"Matron," Tim said, his voice cutting under the noise. "We aren't here
to trouble you. We noticed the smoke."

Haggerty paused, her eyes narrowing. "What smoke?"

"The smoke from the east oven," Tim said, pointing to a massive brick
hearth that was belching grey soot into the room. A tray of flat,
sad-looking loaves sat nearby. "It's drafting poorly. That's why your
crusts are burning before the centers rise."

Haggerty glared at the oven. She wiped sweat from her brow with a floury
arm. "Blast that thing. The mason says the brickwork is settling. Wants
fifty gold crowns to fix it. Says we have to tear the wall down."

"He's lying," Lyssandra said.

The words popped out before she could stop them. Haggerty turned her
glare on the small, noble girl.

"Excuse me, Princess?"

Lyssandra swallowed, but Tim stepped aside, giving her the floor. He
nodded encouragingly.

"The... the brickwork isn't settling," Lyssandra said, her voice gaining
a fraction of confidence as she looked at the oven. "Look at the flame.
It's orange, not blue. It's oxygen starvation. The intake vent is
clogged, likely by soot buildup at the elbow joint. It creates a thermal
inversion."

Haggerty stared at her. "A thermal what-now?"

"It's choked," Gareth translated helpfully.

"We can fix it," Tim said. "Right now. For free."

Haggerty looked at the oven, then at the burning bread, then at the
strange group of children. She crossed her massive arms.

"You have five minutes," she grunted. "If you break it, you bought it."

Tim turned to his team. "Gareth, I need you to lift the iron grate at
the bottom. It's heavy. Cast iron."

Gareth stepped up. He grabbed the scorching hot handle with a rag and
heaved. A grate that would have taken three men to move groaned and
lifted.

"Lyssandra," Tim said. "Show me the angle."

Lyssandra pointed to a curve in the chimney. "There. The pressure
differential is highest at the bend."

Tim grabbed a long-handled ash scraper. He didn't just jab at it. He
focused his Ki. He sent a pulse of awareness down the tool, feeling for
the blockage. He found a hard, calcified lump of soot and grease.

With a sharp, precise twist---the same torque he had taught
Christopher---he dislodged it.

WHOOSH.

A cloud of black dust fell into the firebox, and instantly, the fire
roared to life. The lazy orange flames snapped into a hot, clean blue.
The smoke in the room began to suck rapidly up the chimney.

"Draft restored," Tim announced, stepping back.

Gareth lowered the grate with a gentle thud.

The kitchen staff had stopped working to watch. Matron Haggerty walked
over. She inspected the flame. She sniffed the air, which was clearing
rapidly.

She turned to Tim. The scowl didn't vanish, but it softened into a look
of appraisal.

"You're the peasant boy," she stated. "The one everyone's talking
about."

"Just Tim," he said.

"And you brought me..." She pointed a calloused finger at the group.
"...a library, a map-reader, a giant, and..." She looked at Milo.
"...what is he?"

"He knows where to get good wool," Tim said seriously. "And he has
excellent reflexes."

Haggerty snorted. A sound like a steam valve opening.

"Well, Just Tim. You saved my bread. And you saved me listening to that
thief of a mason."

She walked over to a warming rack and pulled down a basket. She filled
it with fresh, hot rolls---the good ones, reserved for the High Table.
She added a block of butter and a wheel of sharp cheese.

"Table in the back corner," she ordered, pointing to a sturdy oak table
near the pantry, far from the noise but warm from the ovens. "It's the
staff table. You keep your mouths shut, you stay out of my way, and you
fix things when they break. You do that, you can sit there."

She leaned in close to Tim.

"And if you tell anyone about the extra rations, I really will boil
you."

Tim took the basket. "We are the soul of discretion, Matron."

Ten minutes later, the Squad was seated around the "Bigger Table." It
was warm. It smelled of baking bread.

Gareth was tearing a loaf in half, a look of pure bliss on his face.
Christopher had spread his notes out on the table, finally able to write
without shivering. Lyssandra was eating cheese, looking around the
bustling kitchen with wide eyes.

"This," Christopher said, buttering a roll, "is a violation of at least
seven health codes and three Academy by-laws." He took a bite and
groaned. "It is delicious."

"We have a base," Tim said, leaning back in his chair. He watched the
kitchen staff moving about. He saw the delivery boys coming and going
from the city. He saw the maids gossiping in the corner while polishing
silver.

He smiled. He hadn't just found a warm room. He had just walked into the
information hub of the entire Academy.

"Eat up," Tim said to his friends. "Tomorrow, the real work begins.
Christopher, teach me how to read that scroll. Gareth, eat your mortar.
Lyssandra... tell me more about thermal inversions."

They ate, they laughed, and for the first time since arriving at the
White City, none of them felt alone. They were the Back Row, and they
had just secured the best seats in the house.
