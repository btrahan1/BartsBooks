# Chapter 15: The Theory of Flow

Day Two began not with a bang, but with a buzz.

As Tim walked from the Lower Dorms to the main courtyard, the air felt
different. Yesterday, the atmosphere had been thick with overt
hostility---shoves in the hallway, loud insults, laughter. Today, it was
quiet.

Too quiet.

It was the quiet of a forest when a predator---or something the animals
couldn't identify---walked through.

"They're staring," Christopher whispered, clutching his books to his
chest. He adjusted his glasses, scanning the perimeter. "And they're
whispering. I think I prefer the insults. Insults I understand.
Whispering usually precedes a poisoning."

"They aren't poisoning us, Christopher," Tim said calmly. "They're
studying us."

He looked around. The Squad had formed up naturally. Gareth walked
point, a massive, stone-faced icebreaker parting the sea of students.
Milo walked close to Gareth's flank, looking like a pilot fish next to a
shark. Lyssandra walked with her head high, ignoring the glare of her
cousin Jareth across the courtyard.

As they passed a group of second-years, the whispers drifted over like
smoke.

"*...heard he broke Torian's hand without touching him...*" "*...no, he
used a spell. Some kind of heat magic...*" "*...saw them in the
kitchens. Haggerty gave them fresh bread. Haggerty never gives anyone
bread...*" "*...Vane girl is sitting with the peasantry. Scandalous...*"

They weren't seen as victims anymore. They were seen as a glitch. A
smudge on the pristine lens of the Academy that refused to be wiped
away.

"We have Ki Dynamics next," Lyssandra said, checking her schedule.
"Professor Corvus. He's... eccentric. And brilliant."

"Good," Tim said. "I like brilliant."

The Lecture Hall for Ki Dynamics looked less like a classroom and more
like a workshop. The desks were scorched. The air smelled of ozone and
sulfur. Strange copper coils and crystal arrays hung from the ceiling.

At the front of the room stood Professor Corvus. He was a small man with
wild white hair that looked like it had been styled by a lightning
strike. He wore a heavy leather apron over his academic robes, and his
fingers were stained with alchemical reagents.

"Capacity!" Corvus shouted the moment the bell rang, slamming a rod onto
his desk. "The obsession of the amateur! The fetish of the fool!"

He paced frantically. "Every year, you come in here puffing out your
chests. 'Look at me, Professor! My Core is a vast ocean! My Ki is a
mountain!' Bah!"

He grabbed a bucket of water and a small thimble.

"This bucket," he said, pointing to the water. "Is a Gold Core. Massive
capacity. Infinite potential."

He then picked up the thimble. "This... is a Copper Core. Tiny.
Pathetic. Finite."

Jareth Vane snickered from the middle row, shooting a look at Tim.

"However!" Corvus shouted, silencing the room. He picked up the bucket
and tried to pour it into a narrow glass tube. The water sloshed
everywhere, spilling over the table, making a mess. Only a trickle made
it into the tube.

"Waste!" Corvus screamed. "Chaos! Inefficiency! You have power, but you
cannot move it!"

He then took the thimble. He poured it perfectly into the tube. Not a
drop spilled. He refilled the thimble from a reservoir and poured again.
And again. Fast. Rhythmic.

"Flow," Corvus whispered reverently. "It is not about how much you hold.
It is about how fast you can cycle. A small pump, moving at high speed,
can drain a lake. A stagnant ocean does nothing but grow algae."

He turned to the class, his eyes manic. "Today, we test conductivity. We
will see who is a bucket, and who is a pump."

He unveiled a device on his desk. It was a crystal orb connected to a
series of brass pipes that ran through a gauge.

"The test is simple," Corvus explained. "Push your Ki into the crystal.
The gauge measures 'Throughput'---the speed of energy transfer. Not
volume. Speed."

The Iron Heads went first. One by one, they stepped up. They strained.
They grunted. They pushed massive amounts of Ki into the crystal. The
crystal glowed brightly, but the needle on the gauge barely twitched.

"Blocked!" Corvus yelled at a sweating Torian. "You are pushing a
boulder through a straw! Relax! Don't force it!"

Torian stepped back, panting and frustrated. "It's the machine,
Professor. It's broken."

"The machine is physics!" Corvus retorted. "Physics does not break. Your
technique is broken."

Lyssandra went up. She closed her eyes, breathed, and focused on the
warmth Tim had taught her. She didn't push hard, she pushed steady. The
crystal glowed softly, and the needle climbed to the halfway mark.

"Better," Corvus nodded. "Fluid. Elegant. But lacking pressure."

Finally, the room turned to Tim. The anomaly.

"Novice 94," Corvus said, eyeing Tim's grey wool. "The thimble. Step
up."

Tim walked to the device. He looked at the crystal. He looked at the
brass pipes. He understood pipes.

He placed his hand on the crystal.

Most students tried to fill the crystal with their energy, treating it
like a storage tank. Tim didn't do that. He treated the crystal like a
door.

He visualized his Copper Core. He didn't build up a charge. He simply
opened the valve.

Inhale. Accept. Flow.

He created a circuit. He pulled ambient Ki in through his breath, cycled
it through his core to heat it up, and sent it out through his hand. It
wasn't a pulse. It was a stream.

The crystal didn't flare blinding white. It turned a deep, humming
orange.

But the gauge?

The needle snapped to the right. It buried itself in the red zone. The
brass pipes began to vibrate. A low, harmonic hum filled the room.

The students gasped.

"Stop!" Corvus yelled, though he looked delighted.

Tim removed his hand. The needle dropped instantly. No residual energy.
Total control.

Corvus stared at the gauge. He stared at Tim.

"Conductivity," Corvus breathed. "Near zero resistance. You didn't push
the energy. You... you slid it."

"I just opened the road, Professor," Tim said simply. "Why make the
energy fight to get out?"

"Why indeed?" Corvus muttered, scribbling furiously in a notebook.
"Copper. Of course. Malleable. Conductive. It doesn't hold heat; it
transfers it."

He looked up at the class, who were staring at Tim with that same
confused expression they had worn in the hallway.

"You see?" Corvus pointed a stained finger at Tim. "He is not a bucket.
He is a wire. He holds almost nothing, yet he can move everything."

Jareth Vane frowned, raising his hand. "But Professor... in a real
fight, a wire snaps. If I hit him with a 'bucket' of power, he breaks."

"Perhaps," Corvus conceded. "Or perhaps..."

He looked at Tim, a gleam in his eye.

"...perhaps he simply conducts your power right past him, and you
exhaust yourself trying to fill a vessel that has no bottom."

Corvus grinned, a mad scientist looking at a new favorite specimen.

"Ten points to the Lower Dorms. For reminding us that a river does not
need to be deep to be fast."

Tim walked back to his seat. Gareth gave him a subtle fist bump under
the table. Christopher was beaming.

The whispers started again, but the tone had shifted.

"*Did you see the needle?*" "*He nearly broke the pipes.*" "*He's not a
wizard. He's something else.*"

Tim sat down, opening his book. He ignored the whispers. He was busy
calculating the price of brass tubing. If Corvus needed parts for his
machines, Tim knew a scrapyard in the city that sold by the pound.

There was an opportunity here. A "force" wasn't just about power. It was
about leverage. And Tim was starting to see levers everywhere.
