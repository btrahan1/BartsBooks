# Chapter 16: The Hydraulic Press

"Novice Orso," Professor Corvus barked, looking at his list. "Step up.
Try not to crush the apparatus. It is expensive."

Gareth froze. He looked at the delicate crystal orb, then at his own
hands, which were roughly the size of hams. He looked like a bull being
asked to play a flute.

"I will break it," Gareth whispered to Tim. "I am not a wire. I am a
boulder. Boulders smash glass."

"You won't break it," Tim said, leaning in close. "Not if you stop
trying to hit it."

Gareth frowned. "But I must push the Ki."

"Gareth, listen," Tim said, his voice dropping to that calm, anchoring
register. "Think about a river again. When you want to move a massive
log down a stream, do you punch the water?"

"No," Gareth said. "You open the sluice gate."

"Exactly," Tim nodded. "You don't throw the water. You let the weight of
the water do the work."

He put a hand on Gareth's massive forearm. It was rock hard with
tension.

"You're tense. You're trying to *fire* your Ki like a cannon. That
creates a spike. Spikes break glass."

Tim looked him in the eye. "Relax the muscle. Let the Ki pool in your
shoulder. Heavy. Deep. Like a reservoir behind a dam."

Gareth took a breath. He let his shoulders drop. The tension bled out of
his arm, leaving just the sheer mass of him.

"Now," Tim instructed. "When you touch the stone, don't shove. Just...
open the gate. Slowly. Let the weight flow down your arm. Start with a
trickle. Then, when the channel is open, let the whole river lean
against the wheel."

"Strength applied," Tim whispered. "Not forced. Be the water, not the
rock."

Gareth nodded. He stepped up to the machine. The class watched,
expecting a comedic disaster. Jareth Vane was already covering his mouth
to hide a snicker.

Gareth placed his hand on the crystal. It looked comically small beneath
his palm.

His instinct screamed at him to *flex*, to *push*, to *conquer* the
machine. But he heard Tim's voice. *Open the gate.*

Gareth closed his eyes. He visualized the deep, dark well of power in
his gut---his Iron Core. It wasn't hot like Tim's; it was heavy, dense,
and slow. He didn't fire it. He just let the wall come down.

The crystal flickered. A dull, grey light appeared.

"Too slow," Corvus muttered, reaching for his pen to mark a fail.

"Wait," Tim whispered.

Gareth breathed out. He let the weight settle. He opened the gate wider.

The grey light didn't brighten into a flash. It deepened. It became the
color of a storm cloud.

The needle on the gauge didn't snap to the right like it had with Tim.
It began to move slowly, relentlessly, ticking up mark by mark.

*Tick.* *Tick.* *Tick.*

The brass pipes didn't hum with a high-pitched vibration. They began to
groan. A low, shuddering sound, like the keel of a ship under immense
pressure.

Gareth wasn't generating speed. He was generating *torque*.

"The pressure..." Corvus's eyes went wide. He tapped the glass of the
gauge. "Look at the pressure variance!"

Gareth stood like a statue. He wasn't straining. He wasn't sweating. He
was simply letting his immense natural capacity flow out at a steady,
controlled rate. It was the difference between a lightning bolt and a
glacier.

The needle pushed past the halfway mark. It entered the red zone, not
with a jump, but with a steady, unstoppable crawl.

The brass fittings began to hiss. Steam vented from the joints.

"Enough!" Corvus yelled, seeing a hairline fracture appear in the glass
of the gauge. "Cut the flow! Close the gate!"

Gareth inhaled, visualizing the sluice gate sliding shut. The flow
stopped instantly. The groaning pipes fell silent.

Gareth removed his hand. He looked at the machine. It was intact.

"I... I did not break it," Gareth said, sounding surprised.

"No," Corvus said, walking around the desk to inspect the gauge. "But
you nearly flattened the internal sensors."

The Professor looked up at Gareth, seeing him for the first time not as
a clumsy oaf, but as a powerhouse.

"Displacement," Corvus muttered. "Novice 94 is a wire... high speed, low
drag. But you, Novice Orso... you are a hydraulic press. Slow.
Inevitable. Crushing."

He looked at the class.

"Speed is one kind of power," Corvus lectured. "But Mass? Mass moves the
world. If Novice Orso learns to apply this slowly, he could walk through
a stone wall without ever raising his pulse."

He scribbled on his clipboard. "Ten points to the Lower Dorms. For
demonstrating that a slow river carves the deepest canyon."

Gareth walked back to the table. He looked at his hand. He felt the
heavy, thrumming power receding back into his core. It felt good. It
felt controlled.

"Hydraulic press," Gareth murmured, testing the words.

He looked at Tim. A rare, small smile cracked the giant's face.

"I like being the press," Gareth rumbled. "It is better than being the
cattle."

Tim grinned. "Just remember, big man. Don't press the quill in History
class. We need those notes."
