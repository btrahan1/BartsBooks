# Chapter 17: The Perfect Line

"Novice Pendergast," Corvus called out, wiping the steam from the gauge
with a rag. "You're up. Try not to faint. The floor is hard."

Christopher turned a shade of pale usually reserved for cave-dwelling
fish. He stood up, his knees knocking together audibly.

"I can't," Christopher whispered to Tim, his hands trembling. "Gareth
has a reservoir. You have a furnace. I have... a puddle. A damp spot. If
I touch that thing, the needle won't even move. I'll be the
laughingstock of the year. Again."

"Christopher," Tim said, his voice cutting through the panic. "Look at
your hands."

Christopher looked down at his ink-stained, shaking fingers. "They're
shaking."

"When you write," Tim asked, "when you are copying a lineage chart or
drafting a contract... do you shake?"

Christopher blinked. "No. Of course not. A blotch ruins the parchment."

"Exactly," Tim said. "You have the steadiest hands in the room,
Roommate. But only when you're holding a quill."

Tim gestured to the crystal orb. "Stop looking at it like a test of
strength. It's not a hammer. It's a piece of paper."

He leaned in close. "You don't need to flood it with ink. You just need
to draw a single, perfect line. Don't push the Ki. *Draft* it. Fine tip.
minimal pressure. absolute consistency."

Christopher looked at the machine. He imagined the crystal as a blank
page. He imagined his Ki not as a force, but as ink flowing from a nib.

"A perfect line," Christopher whispered. "No blotches."

He walked up to the podium. Jareth Vane leaned forward, grinning.
"Careful, Pendergast. Don't blow a fuse with all that raw power."

Christopher ignored him. He adjusted his glasses. He took a
breath---*Inhale, Accept*---and placed his fingertips lightly on the
crystal.

He didn't shove. He didn't open a floodgate. He focused on the tip of
the imaginary quill.

Contact.

The crystal began to glow. But it wasn't the flickering white of the
novices, or the molten copper of Tim, or the storm-grey of Gareth.

It was a thin, laser-focused beam of pale blue light right in the center
of the orb. It didn't waver. It didn't pulse. It was static, as if
frozen in time.

The needle on the gauge jumped to the "Low" setting---barely a 2 out of
10.

Jareth laughed. "Pathetic! Barely a reading!"

"Silence!" Professor Corvus hissed, waving a hand at Jareth without
looking away from the gauge.

Corvus leaned in until his nose almost touched the glass. He watched the
needle.

It wasn't moving.

It wasn't vibrating. It wasn't fluctuating up or down by a fraction of a
millimeter. It was perfectly, impossibly still.

"Do you know how hard this is?" Corvus whispered, his voice hushed with
awe. "Even Masters have a variance. The heart beats, the Ki pulses. The
needle always trembles."

He looked at Christopher, whose eyes were squeezed shut, sweat beading
on his forehead, focusing entirely on maintaining that single, perfect
flow of ink.

"But this..." Corvus tapped the glass. "Zero variance. Absolute
stability. It is a flat line."

Corvus grabbed a knob on the side of the machine---the resistance
dampener. "Let's see the control."

He cranked the resistance up. Usually, the needle would drop as the
student struggled to push through.

Christopher felt the "paper" get rougher. He frowned. He adjusted his
"quill." He pushed a fraction harder, just enough to maintain the line.

The needle didn't drop. It stayed pinned at exactly 2.

Corvus cranked it down. The needle should have spiked.

Christopher felt the ink flowing too fast. He pulled back.

The needle stayed at 2.

"Output modulation," Corvus breathed. "He is adjusting his flow in
real-time to match the resistance. It's... it's like a metronome."

Corvus stood up, his wild hair practically vibrating.

"Novice Redgrave!" Corvus shouted. "You produced a 7 on the scale! But
your variance was plus or minus 3! You are a flickering torch!"

He pointed at Christopher. "Novice Pendergast is a 2. But he is a
*laser*. He could sustain an enchantment for a week without a single
fluctuation. He could inscribe a containment rune on a soap bubble
without popping it!"

Christopher opened his eyes. He stopped the flow. The light vanished
instantly.

"Did I... did I pass?" Christopher squeaked, pushing his glasses up his
nose.

"Pass?" Corvus cackled. "You are a Scribe's dream! You are a human
regulator! Ten points to the Lower Dorms!"

The Professor ran a hand through his hair, looking at the four misfits
sitting together.

"A Conductor. A Press. And a Regulator," Corvus muttered, doing the
math. "By the Ancients... if they ever figure out how to link up, they
could power a city."

Christopher walked back to his seat. His legs were jelly, but his head
was high.

Cassian Redgrave looked at the scrawny boy with new, confused eyes. He
couldn't understand it. Pendergast was weak. He had barely moved the
needle. So why did the Professor look like he'd just found a gold mine?

Christopher sat down, exhaling a long, shaky breath.

"I felt it," Christopher whispered to Tim. "The ink. I just... kept the
line straight."

"And it was beautiful," Tim said, grinning. "You held the line,
Roommate."

Tim looked at his squad. They were tired. They were strange. But the
fear was gone from their eyes. They realized that the game wasn't about
who had the biggest sword. It was about who knew how to use what they
had.

"Lunch," Gareth rumbled, breaking the moment. "I require mortar."

"To lunch," Tim agreed. "And then... we have a library to raid. I
believe we have a thermal inversion to read up on."
