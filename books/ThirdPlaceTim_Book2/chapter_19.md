# Chapter 18: The Syllabus of the Unseen

When the Squad entered the Refectory, they automatically braced for
impact. Gareth squared his shoulders, Christopher tried to make himself
invisible behind a stack of books, and Lyssandra pulled her cloak tight.

But there were no jeers. There were no flying bread rolls.

Instead, there was a blockade.

The "Draft Zone"---that desolate tundra of wobbly tables near the drafty
windows---was full. Packed. A wall of grey-clad novices stood between
Tim and the food line.

"Ambush?" Gareth rumbled, his hand drifting toward the training sword he
wasn't allowed to wear at lunch.

"No," Tim said, scanning the faces. He didn't see the sneers of the Iron
Heads or the bored disdain of the Golden Circle. He saw wide eyes,
chewed lips, and ink-stained fingers. He saw desperation.

A girl stepped forward. She wore the crest of a minor coastal house---a
silver fish on blue. She looked terrified, but she held her ground.

"You're him," she said, her voice shaking slightly. "The Copper Core."

"I'm Tim," he corrected gently.

"I'm Elara... no, sorry, I'm Mara," she stammered, clearly flustered.
"House Fisher. We... we saw what happened in Ki Dynamics. With the
needles."

She gestured to the crowd behind her. There were nearly thirty of them.
The runts. The third sons. The ones who had failed the Spirit Stone test
or blown up their crystals.

"We want to know," Mara whispered. "How did you do it?"

Tim looked at them. He saw himself a year ago, standing on the King's
Road, hungry and clueless. He saw the same hunger in them---not for
food, but for dignity. They were drowning in the deep end of the
Academy, flailing against standards set for geniuses and giants.

"We didn't use magic," Tim said loud enough for the group to hear. "We
used mechanics."

"Show us," a boy from the back called out. "Please. Master Corvus said
my flow is 'turbulent.' He said I'm going to blow my own hand off. I
don't want to blow my hand off."

Tim looked at his friends.

"I just want lunch," Gareth muttered.

"We can eat and talk," Tim decided. He looked at Mara. "Grab a table.
Push them together. We're having a study session."

Ten minutes later, the Draft Zone had been transformed. Four tables were
shoved together into a makeshift lecture hall.

It wasn't just Tim teaching. The curiosity was directed at all of them.

"You," a skinny boy with nervous tics said to Christopher. "Corvus said
you were a 'Human Regulator.' My Ki spikes when I get scared. How do you
stop the shaking?"

Christopher, usually the one asking for help, looked stunned. He
adjusted his glasses, sitting up a little straighter. "Well," he
stammered. "It's... it's about visualization. Do you write?"

"I... I draw," the boy said.

"Perfect," Christopher said, his voice gaining confidence. "Don't think
of it as power. Think of it as charcoal. If you press too hard, the
stick snaps. If you press too light, there's no line. You have to find
the friction."

Christopher picked up a spoon. "Here. Hold this. Imagine you're shading
a shadow. Gentle. Consistent."

Across the table, Lyssandra was surrounded by three girls who were
shivering in the draft.

"It's not about the cloak," Lyssandra was explaining, her hands wrapped
around a warm cup. "It's about the loop. You feel cold, so you tense up.
Tension restricts blood flow. Restricted blood flow makes you colder.
You have to break the loop."

She touched one girl's shoulder. "Drop your shoulders. Unclench your
jaw. Visualize the Core as a hearth, not a firework. Bank the coals."

And at the end of the table, Gareth sat behind a mountain of food, with
two smaller boys watching him eat with morbid fascination.

"You... you eat the core of the apple?" one asked.

"Fiber," Gareth grunted. He pointed a chicken leg at the boy's plate,
which was piled high with sweet pastries. "You. Your wall is crumbling."

"What?"

"Sugar," Gareth rumbled. "It burns fast. Like dry straw. You get energy,
then you crash. You need stone. Meat. Bread." He stabbed a fork into a
pile of steamed broccoli he had fetched. "And mortar. Eat the green
stuff. It keeps the stones from grinding."

The boy looked at the broccoli with horror. "But it tastes like grass."

"Do you want to be a castle or a shed?" Gareth asked ominously.

The boy immediately ate the broccoli.

In the center of it all sat Tim. He wasn't lecturing on Ki. He was
answering questions about *survival*.

"How do we pass History?" Mara asked. "Master Elspeth speaks so fast."

"She speaks in threes," Tim explained, breaking a roll. "Thesis,
Antithesis, Synthesis. She states a fact, she gives the
counter-argument, then she tells you the 'truth.' Ignore the first two.
Only write down the third. That's what's on the test."

"And the stairs?" another asked. "We're always late because the Golden
Circle blocks the Grand Staircase."

"West Service Stairwell," Tim said. "It dumps you out behind the
library. It smells like soap, but it cuts six minutes off the walk."

He looked around the table. He saw the relief washing over them. They
weren't stupid; they just didn't have the map.

"Listen," Tim said, raising his voice slightly. The table went quiet.

"The Academy is designed for the Iron Heads," Tim said, tilting his head
toward Cassian's table, where the bullies were watching this gathering
with confused irritation. "It's designed for the Golden Circle. It's
built for people who are already strong."

He looked at Mara, at the nervous artist, at the shivering girls.

"It wasn't built for us. So we have to build our own way through it. We
trade notes. We share shortcuts. If one of us figures out a trick, we
all learn it."

He held up his spoon.

"They have the power," Tim said. "But we have the numbers. And if we
work together... we have the leverage."

Mara looked at Tim. She looked at the notes Christopher was drawing for
the artist. She looked at Lyssandra helping the girls breathe.

"A union," Mara whispered.

"A study group," Tim corrected with a wink. "The Handbook encourages
'collaborative academic excellence.' Page twelve."

From across the hall, Cassian Redgrave watched the Draft Zone. It was
usually a place of silent misery. Now, it was buzzing. It was organized.

"What are they doing?" Jareth asked, frowning. "It looks like... a
class."

"It looks," Cassian spat, crushing a pastry in his fist, "like a
rebellion."

But Tim didn't see a rebellion. As he watched Christopher patiently
teach a boy how to breathe, and Gareth bully a student into eating
vegetables, Tim saw something else.

He saw the foundation of his kingdom. And the mortar was setting fast.
