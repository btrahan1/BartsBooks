# Chapter 20: The Architecture of Flow

The Grand Staircase was a architectural marvel of white marble, designed
to inspire awe. Every morning, however, it inspired only dread.

It was the chokepoint of the morning rush. By unwritten tradition, the
Golden Circle and the Iron Heads occupied the center of the wide steps,
moving at a leisurely, regal pace. They effectively turned a sixty-foot
wide staircase into a ten-foot wide lane for everyone else.

Dozens of students---the Draft Zone kids, the unaffiliated, the
terrified---huddled at the bottom, waiting for gaps in the "Golden
Wall," often making them late for class.

"Why are we here?" Christopher whispered, clutching his bag to his
chest. "The service stairs smell like lye, but they are empty. This...
this is a meat grinder."

"Because we aren't servants, Christopher," Tim said calmly. "And neither
are they." He gestured to the crowd of stuck students, who were checking
their pocket watches with panic.

"We are fixing the flow," Tim announced.

He stepped forward. The Squad---Gareth, Lyssandra, Milo, and
Mara---flanked him. They didn't push. They just walked up to the base of
the stairs, right behind the blockage.

Cassian Redgrave was standing on the third step, leaning against the
banister, chatting with Jareth. They were blocking the entire left wing.

"Excuse me," Tim said. His voice wasn't loud, but it projected perfectly
over the murmur of the crowd.

Cassian looked down. A sneer formed instantly. "Well, if it isn't the
King of the Rats. Back to the service tunnels, Peasant. The stairs are
for students."

"We are students," Tim said. "And we are late. You are creating a
bottleneck."

"We are walking," Cassian countered lazily. "Nobility does not rush."

"You aren't walking," Tim pointed out. "You are loitering. And
statistically speaking, you are creating a safety hazard. If a fire
broke out right now, the compression wave from the students behind us
would crush you against that banister before you could draw your sword."

Cassian laughed. "I'll take my chances. Wait your turn."

Tim didn't move. He didn't get angry. He looked past Cassian, scanning
the crowd of nobles stuck in the crush *behind* the Iron Heads.

He spotted a familiar face.

Roland, the boy from the mud pit, was standing five steps down from
Cassian, looking annoyed. He was a noble, but he wasn't high enough in
the pecking order to bypass Cassian, and he was currently jammed
shoulder-to-shoulder with a Miller.

"Roland," Tim called out.

Cassian bristled. "Don't speak to him."

Roland looked up, surprised to hear his name. He locked eyes with Tim.

"You're in the back, Roland," Tim said, his voice calm and reasonable.
"You can see the whole team. Is this efficient?"

Roland looked at the jam. He looked at Cassian, preening on the step,
holding up fifty people just to feel powerful. He looked at the clock on
the wall. He remembered the feeling of the sled moving when they finally
worked together. He remembered the logic.

The driver sits in the back.

Roland made a choice.

"It is... inefficient," Roland said.

Cassian whipped around. "Excuse me?"

Roland didn't back down. He adjusted his bag. "I said it's inefficient,
Cassian. I have Arithmancy in three minutes. And you are blocking the
path."

"I am holding the line!" Cassian argued.

"You're just standing there," Roland countered. He stepped forward.
"Move, Cassian."

"Or what?" Jareth challenged, stepping up to support Cassian.

Roland looked at Jareth. Then he looked at the Miller boy next to
him---the one he had screamed at yesterday, but pulled across the line
today.

"Or," Roland said, "we walk through you."

Roland stepped forward. The Miller boy, sensing the shift, stepped with
him. Then another noble from Roland's squad stepped up.

It was a ripple effect. Tim had nudged the first domino, but Roland was
the one knocking them down.

Cassian looked at Roland---a fellow noble---challenging him. If it had
been Tim, he would have fought. But to fight Roland? It would be a civil
war within the hierarchy. It would be messy. It would be... *common*.

Cassian scoffed, trying to salvage his dignity. "Fine. If you're in such
a rush to fail your exams, go ahead."

He stepped aside.

The dam broke.

Roland walked past him. Then the Miller boy. Then Tim and his Squad.

As Tim passed Cassian, he didn't gloat. He didn't even look at him. He
just kept walking, matching Roland's pace.

"Thank you, Roland," Tim said quietly as they ascended side-by-side.

Roland didn't look at him, but his chin was held high. "I hate being
late," Roland muttered. "And Cassian is... top-heavy."

"A common structural flaw," Tim agreed.

Behind them, the rest of the students flooded up the stairs. The Golden
Wall had dissolved, not by force, but by the sudden, collective
realization that it was in everyone's best interest to keep moving.

From the balcony above, Grandmaster Valdis watched the sea of students
flowing freely up the stairs for the first time in a decade. His face
was a mask of cold fury.

But beside him, Professor Corvus was beaming, tapping a rhythm on the
railing.

"Flow," Corvus chuckled. "Beautiful, laminar flow. The pipes are
widening, Valdis. The pipes are widening."

"It is chaos," Valdis hissed.

"No," Corvus corrected. "It's evolution."
