# Chapter 21: The Rust and the Repair

The Month of the Falling Leaf ended not with a celebration, but with the
Grand Inspection.

The entire student body stood in rigid formation in the central
courtyard. The air was frigid, the cobblestones frosted with early ice.
Breath plumed in the air like a hundred tiny chimneys.

Grandmaster Valdis moved down the lines like a wraith. He was looking
for a flaw. A scuffed boot, a tarnished button, a waver in posture. For
the past month, he had watched with growing disgust as the Academy---his
forge of steel---had become... comfortable.

The brawls had stopped. The staircase was orderly. The dining hall was
quiet. Even the Iron Heads were focusing more on passing their exams
than terrorizing the weak. It was efficient. It was peaceful.

And Valdis hated it. Peace made soft soldiers.

He stopped in front of the Iron Heads. Cassian Redgrave stood tall, his
chin jutting out. He was impeccable, save for one detail.

Cassian had spent his allowance on fine wine and gambling debts,
neglecting his gear. The leather strap of his ceremonial sword
belt---ornate, gold-stitched, and old---was dry-rotted.

As Cassian shifted his weight to salute, the leather gave a sickening
*snap*.

The heavy scabbard plummeted.

It didn't hit the ground.

Tim, standing in the row behind Cassian (a strategic position he had
claimed to avoid the wind), moved before gravity could finish the job.
He caught the scabbard inches from the cobblestones.

Valdis turned slowly. His eyes locked onto the scene. Cassian froze, his
face draining of color. To drop one's sword during Inspection was a
grave dishonor. It meant detention, public shaming, and a letter to
one's father.

"Do not move," Tim whispered to Cassian.

Tim didn't hand the sword back. That would still leave Cassian holding a
broken belt. Instead, Tim produced a small object from his sleeve---a
heavy-duty leather awl and a spool of waxed black thread, items he
carried for repairing the Squad's second-hand books.

"Hold the buckle," Tim commanded softly.

Cassian, paralyzed by the approaching Grandmaster, obeyed. He held the
brass buckle against his hip.

Tim worked with blinding speed. He didn't try to replicate the fancy
gold stitching. He punched two holes through the thick leather, threaded
the waxed cord, and tied a "miller's knot"---a knot designed to hold
grain sacks under immense tension.

"Done," Tim whispered, sliding the scabbard back into place. "It's ugly,
but it will hold a siege engine."

He stepped back into line just as Valdis arrived.

The Grandmaster stopped. He looked at Cassian, who was sweating despite
the cold. He looked at the black, utilitarian knot marring the gold
belt. Then he looked at Tim.

"Novice 94," Valdis said. His voice was a dry rattle, quiet and
terrifying. "Step forward."

Tim stepped out of formation. "Grandmaster."

"You repaired Novice Redgrave's equipment," Valdis stated.

"I secured a loose asset, Grandmaster," Tim corrected.

Valdis stepped closer, towering over the boy. The pressure of his Ki was
immense, a heavy, suffocating blanket of grey steel. "Novice Redgrave is
your superior. He is also your tormentor. By the laws of nature, you
should have let his sword fall. You should have let him be shamed. It
would have elevated your own standing."

The courtyard was dead silent. Valdis was openly questioning the new
order.

"Why," Valdis hissed, leaning down, "did you save him?"

Tim felt the pressure crushing his lungs. He cycled his breath. *Inhale.
Accept.*

"Because we are in formation, Grandmaster," Tim said clearly. "If his
sword hits the ground, it creates disorder. It breaks the line. The
integrity of the unit is more important than the pride of the
individual."

It was a textbook answer. It was the "correct" answer according to the
handbook.

But Valdis wasn't buying it.

"You lie," Valdis spat. "You did not do it for the unit. You did it to
make him owe you. You are domesticating them."

Valdis turned to the class, his voice rising to a shout that echoed off
the stone walls.

"Look at you! You stand in neat little rows. You share your notes. You
fix each other's belts. You are becoming a herd of sheep!"

He pointed his willow cane at Tim.

"And this... this *copper* thing is your shepherd. He teaches you that
survival is about comfort. About safety. About full bellies and warm
cloaks."

Valdis slammed the cane onto the stones. *CRACK.*

"The Academy is not a nursery!" Valdis roared. "It is a crucible! Iron
sharpens iron only when they *strike* one another! You are not here to
be friends. You are here to dominate! To conquer!"

He turned back to Tim, his eyes burning with a fanatic's zeal.

"You are a rust spot, Novice 94. You are corrosion. You soften the metal
around you. You took a wolf..." He pointed at Cassian, who looked sick.
"...and you turned him into a dog that holds your leash."

Valdis reached out and grabbed Cassian's belt. He ripped the scabbard
free, snapping Tim's sturdy knot with a pulse of sheer Ki power. He
threw the sword to the ground. Clang.

"Detention for Novice Redgrave," Valdis barked. "For failure to maintain
gear."

He turned to Tim.

"And for you, Novice 94... you seem to enjoy fixing things. You wish to
be helpful? Fine."

Valdis smiled, a thin, cruel twisting of lips.

"The Northern Archive is in disarray. Thousands of years of scrolls,
uncatalogued, rotting in the damp. Since you value 'order' so highly,
you will organize it."

The students gasped. The Northern Archive was a punishment detail
usually reserved for major offenses. It was a freezing, dust-choked
labyrinth that took months to clear.

"You will not attend tactical classes," Valdis decreed. "You will not
attend sparring. You will not attend meals until your quota is met. You
wish to be a scholar? Then go rot among the books."

"But Grandmaster," Elara's voice rang out from the Third Circle ranks.
"That is---"

"Silence, Vane!" Valdis snapped.

He looked down at Tim, expecting tears. Expecting begging.

Tim looked at the broken knot on the ground. Then he looked up at
Valdis.

"The Northern Archive," Tim said, his voice steady. "Is that the one
with the records of the founding of the Academy?"

"It contains everything," Valdis sneered. "Most of it useless."

Tim bowed. "Then I accept the punishment, Grandmaster. I look forward to
the reading."

Valdis narrowed his eyes. The boy didn't break. He didn't even bend. He
just absorbed the blow.

"Get out of my sight," Valdis snarled.

Tim turned and walked away. He passed Cassian, who was staring at his
sword on the ground, humiliated not by Tim, but by the Master he
worshiped.

Tim caught Christopher's eye. He tapped his chest. *Breathe.*

As Tim walked toward the dark, imposing tower of the Northern Archive,
he didn't feel punished. Valdis thought he was exiling Tim to a dungeon.

But Tim smiled. Valdis had just given him the keys to the information
center of the entire world. And he had given him a lot of free time to
read.
