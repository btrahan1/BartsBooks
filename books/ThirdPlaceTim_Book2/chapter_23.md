# Chapter 22: The Alloy

Grandmaster Valdis stood at the window of the High Spire, looking down
at the academy grounds like a vulture waiting for a twitch.

It had been seven days. Seven days since he had excised the tumor. Seven
days since he had banished the peasant boy to the freezing dust of the
Archive.

He had expected the collapse to be immediate. Without their shepherd,
the sheep should have scattered. The natural order---the strong preying
on the weak, the iron crushing the tin---should have reasserted itself
within forty-eight hours. He expected brawls in the refectory. He
expected the return of fear.

Instead, he saw... drills.

Down in the courtyard, a large group of first-years was moving in
formation. They weren't under the supervision of an instructor. They
were self-organizing.

"Report," Valdis snapped without turning around.

Master Kincaid stood by the door, looking uncomfortable. He held a sheaf
of papers.

"Academic performance is up twelve percent across the board,
Grandmaster," Kincaid grunted. "Infirmary visits are down by forty
percent. And... well... tactical efficiency in the mock skirmishes has
nearly doubled."

Valdis turned slowly, his face a mask of cold disbelief. "Doubled? How?
They are leaderless."

"That's the thing, Grandmaster," Kincaid said, scratching his scarred
chin. "They aren't."

Valdis snatched the papers. He scanned the lists. He expected to see a
Vane, or perhaps a Sterling leading the charge. But the name at the top
of the squad rotations was the one he least expected.

"Redgrave?" Valdis whispered. "Cassian Redgrave?"

"He has... changed," Kincaid admitted. "Since the Inspection."

Valdis threw the papers onto his desk and stormed out of the office. He
needed to see this farce for himself.

The courtyard was buzzing with activity. A mock shield-wall exercise was
underway. Usually, this devolved into the biggest boys smashing into the
smallest until the line broke.

But today, the line held.

"Tighten up!" a voice roared from the center. "Shields lock! Pikes,
second rank! Don't crowd the shields, you morons, give them room to
brace!"

It was Cassian. He wasn't in the back, preening. He wasn't in the front,
glorifying himself. He was pacing the line, grabbing shoulders,
adjusting stances.

A massive boy from House Orso---Gareth---was the anchor. Beside him
stood the terrified Weaver boy, Milo.

"Milo!" Cassian barked. "Stop shaking! You have the reach. If they
charge Gareth, you poke them. You are the needle. Gareth is the thread.
Work together!"

Milo nodded frantically, holding his training spear steady.

"But Cassian," another Iron Head complained. "Why do *we* have to stand
next to the runts? We should form an elite vanguard."

Cassian spun on him. "And leave our flanks exposed? You idiot. If the
runts break, *we* get surrounded. A wall with a hole in it isn't a wall.
It's a ruin."

He grabbed the Iron Head's collar. "Do you want to lose to the Second
Years again? Or do you want to win?"

"I want to win," the boy muttered.

"Then hold the damn line," Cassian snarled.

Valdis watched from the colonnade, stunned.

Cassian sounded like... him. Like Tim.

"A wall with a hole isn't a wall," Valdis muttered. That wasn't Noble
philosophy. That was peasant logic. That was masonry.

He strode into the courtyard. The students froze as his heavy aura
washed over them.

"Novice Redgrave," Valdis said, his voice cutting through the silence.

Cassian straightened. He didn't look terrified, as he had a week ago. He
looked annoyed at the interruption. He touched the side of his
belt---where a ugly, black, utilitarian knot still held his scabbard in
place. He hadn't replaced it.

"Grandmaster," Cassian saluted.

"You are drilling with the weak," Valdis accused. "You are mixing the
metals. Iron does not alloy with lead."

Cassian looked at Milo. He looked at Gareth. Then he looked at Valdis.

He remembered the humiliation of the inspection. He remembered the
feeling of his sword hitting the mud. And he remembered the peasant boy
who had risked punishment to fix him, not for friendship, but for
*order*.

"With respect, Grandmaster," Cassian said, his chin high. "I am not
alloying. I am constructing."

"Constructing?"

"A castle is made of many things," Cassian said, reciting the lesson he
had learned the hard way. "Stone. Wood. Iron. If I build only with Iron,
the castle rusts. If I build only with Stone, it cracks."

He gestured to the line. The strong protecting the weak, the weak
supporting the strong.

"I am the Commander of this year," Cassian stated with a terrifying
arrogance that was entirely his own. "A Commander does not throw away
his assets just because they aren't pretty. That is wasteful. And waste
is the luxury of a fool."

Valdis flinched. He recognized that phrase. *The Apple.*

"You sound like him," Valdis hissed. "The peasant."

Cassian's face hardened. "The peasant is gone, Grandmaster. You sent him
away."

He stepped closer, lowering his voice so only Valdis could hear.

"But he was right about one thing. You broke my sword belt to make a
point. He fixed it to keep the line." Cassian tapped the ugly knot. "I
prefer the line."

He turned back to his troops. "Resume drills! Milo, keep that spear up!"

Valdis stood there, ignored.

He had removed the copper wire, thinking the machine would stop. But he
had waited too long. The current had already jumped. The Iron Heads
hadn't become soft; they had become conductive.

Cassian Redgrave wasn't being nice. He was being *efficient*. He had
realized that bullying was a waste of energy, while command was a force
multiplier.

Valdis turned on his heel and walked away, a cold fury burning in his
gut. The infection had spread deeper than he thought. He couldn't just
cut out the tumor anymore.

He had to burn the whole body.

But first, he had to see what the tumor was doing in the dark.

Meanwhile, in the freezing, subterranean depths of the Northern
Archive...

Tim sat on a pile of ancient, mouldering scrolls. He was wrapped in
three wool blankets he had bartered from the laundry staff. A small,
smokeless lamp---provided by Christopher---illuminated the page in front
of him.

He wasn't suffering. He wasn't crying.

He was eating a hot meat pie that Gareth had smuggled down in a
hollowed-out book.

"Fascinating," Tim mumbled, crumbs falling onto a scroll dated three
hundred years prior.

He ran his finger down a list of names. The founding charter of the
Academy.

Article 4, Section 2: On the Rights of the Sponsored.

Tim's eyes widened. He re-read the line. He pulled out a piece of scrap
paper and began to translate the High Script, his hand steady.

"Well, well, well," Tim whispered to the dust motes dancing in the
light. "It seems the Grandmaster has forgotten his history."

He took a bite of the pie.

"It seems," Tim grinned, "that a punishment detail is only a punishment
if you don't know how to read the fine print."
