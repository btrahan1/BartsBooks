# Chapter 23: The Harder Metal

The summons came not from a messenger, but from Master Kincaid himself.

He descended into the dusty gloom of the Northern Archive, his heavy
boots kicking up clouds of ancient silt. Tim sat at a makeshift desk
made of stacked crates, translating a text on pre-Imperial hydrology.

"Get up," Kincaid grunted. He didn't look Tim in the eye. He looked at
the wall. "Grandmaster Valdis has ordered a... proficiency evaluation.
To see if your time in the dark has dulled your edge."

Tim closed the scroll carefully. He saw the tension in Kincaid's jaw,
the way the Master's hand rested uneasily on his belt.

"Is it an evaluation, Master?" Tim asked calmly, standing up and
brushing dust from his grey wool. "Or is it an execution?"

Kincaid turned, his scarred face grim. "It is a sparring match. Full
contact. No pads. And the medic is... currently on a break."

Tim nodded. "I see. An accident waiting to happen."

"Tim," Kincaid said, his voice dropping to a rough whisper. "Valdis has
brought in Verrick. Third Year. He's the one we use to break horses that
refuse the saddle. He doesn't know how to pull a punch. And he's been
told you're a threat to the Academy."

"I am," Tim said simply. "To the parts of it that are rotting."

He walked past Kincaid toward the stairs. "Don't worry, Master. I've
been reading about anatomy. I know which parts break."

The Training Grounds had been cleared. The gates were locked. There were
no cheering crowds, no squads of First Years to witness the lesson.

Only Valdis stood on the observation deck, looking down like a emperor
in a coliseum.

In the center of the ring stood Verrick. He was a nightmare of a
youth---tall, lean, and wired with the kind of explosive, twitchy muscle
that spoke of violence rather than strength. He held a weighted training
staff that was reinforced with iron bands. It wasn't a practice tool; it
was a bludgeon.

Tim walked into the sunlight, squinting. He carried nothing.

"The Rat emerges," Verrick called out. His voice was light, cheerful,
and utterly devoid of empathy. He spun the staff. *Whoosh. Whoosh.* "I
hear you like to fix things, Rat. Let's see if you can fix a shattered
kneecap."

Tim stopped ten paces away. He analyzed Verrick. *Stance:* Wide.
Aggressive. *Ki:* Jagged. Yellow. Spiking with adrenaline and malice.
*Intent:* Permanent injury.

Tim looked up at the balcony. Valdis stared back, his face a mask of
cold anticipation. He wanted Tim broken. He wanted him to crawl away,
weeping, proving that Copper was soft.

Tim looked back at Verrick.

"You don't have to do this," Tim said. It was the last offering of the
olive branch. "You're a Third Year. Beating a novice proves nothing."

"It proves I follow orders," Verrick smiled. "And I enjoy my work."

Verrick lunged.

He was fast. Much faster than Cassian. He didn't telegraph the swing.
The iron-shod staff snapped out like a cobra, aiming for Tim's ribs.

Tim flowed. He twisted, the staff missing him by an inch. The wind of
the passage stung his face.

Verrick didn't overextend. He recovered instantly, spinning the staff
into a backhand strike aimed at Tim's head.

Tim ducked, but barely. The wood grazed his shoulder, sending a shock of
pain down his arm.

"Dance, Rat!" Verrick laughed, pressing the attack. A flurry of blows.
High, low, mid.

Tim retreated. He parried with his forearms, absorbing the blows on the
meat of the muscle, using his Copper Core to disperse the impact trauma.
But he was losing ground. He was backing into a corner.

*He won't stop,* Tim realized. *He isn't Cassian. He isn't misguided. He
is cruel.*

Tim had spent his life, and his time at the Academy, trying to find the
good in people. To teach them. To guide them. He believed in the carrot.

But as Verrick swung the staff downward, aiming to crush Tim's
collarbone, Tim remembered the other lesson of the street. The lesson
Old Man Kai had taught him before the breathing exercises.

Sometimes, the dog is rabid. You do not pet a rabid dog.

Tim stopped retreating.

As the staff came down, Tim didn't dodge. He stepped *forward*, jamming
himself into Verrick's guard.

He caught the shaft of the staff with his left hand, high up near
Verrick's grip. His Copper Core flared---not with the gentle warmth of a
hearth, but with the sudden, searing heat of a soldering iron.

He pumped a massive surge of thermal energy into the iron bands of the
staff.

"Hot!" Verrick yelped, his grip loosening for a fraction of a second as
the metal seared his palm.

That fraction was all Tim needed.

Tim didn't throw him. He didn't trip him.

Tim drove the heel of his right palm directly into Verrick's solar
plexus.

But he didn't just push. He remembered Professor Corvus's lecture on
hydraulics. He remembered Gareth's density. And he remembered the
"spike" he had used on One-Eyed Tom.

Tim combined them. He created a focused, needle-point of Ki at the
center of his palm, and he drove it *through* the soft tissue.

THUD.

It wasn't a loud noise. It was a wet, heavy sound.

The air didn't just leave Verrick's lungs; it was evicted. His diaphragm
seized. His entire nervous system rebooted in a flash of white agony.

Verrick's eyes bulged. He dropped the staff. He folded in half, gasping
for air that wouldn't come.

Tim wasn't done.

As Verrick crumbled, Tim stepped to the side, grabbed Verrick's collar
to keep him upright, and swept his leg. Hard.

Verrick hit the ground flat on his back.

Tim stood over him. He raised his foot.

For a second, Valdis leaned forward, hoping. *Do it,* Valdis thought.
*Stomp him. Break his ribs. Show me you are a monster. Become what I
hate.*

Tim's foot hovered over Verrick's throat.

Verrick wheezed, terrified, staring up at the peasant boy who suddenly
looked like a giant.

Tim held the moment. He let Verrick feel the fear of the "accident." He
let him understand the reality of violence.

Then, slowly, Tim lowered his foot. He placed it gently on the ground.

He leaned down, his face inches from Verrick's.

"You like breaking things," Tim whispered. His voice was devoid of
warmth. It was cold, hard copper. "But you forget that copper hardens
when you beat it."

He stood up. He didn't offer Verrick a hand.

He turned and looked up at the balcony. He locked eyes with Grandmaster
Valdis.

Tim didn't bow. He didn't salute. He simply dusted off his grey wool
tunic, turned his back on the Grandmaster, and walked out of the arena.

Kincaid watched him go, letting out a long, shaky breath.

"He didn't break him," Kincaid muttered, looking at Verrick, who was
curled in a fetal ball, finally sucking in a ragged breath. "He just...
turned him off."

Valdis gripped the railing until his knuckles turned white. He had
wanted an accident. He had wanted a victim.

Instead, he had just confirmed his worst fear. The boy wasn't just a
scholar. And he wasn't just a survivor.

He was a warrior who had simply chosen, until now, to keep his sword
sheathed. And Valdis had just forced him to draw it.
