# Chapter 24: The Summoning

Master Kincaid did not knock. He opened the heavy oak door to the
Grandmaster's office and stood there, filling the frame. The morning
light caught the scars on his face, making them look like deep fissures
in a rock face.

Grandmaster Valdis looked up from his desk, his quill pausing over a
suspension order for Novice 94.

"You look disturbed, Kincaid," Valdis said smoothly. "Did the boy
finally break? Is he in the infirmary?"

"No," Kincaid rumbled. "He is in the Training Grounds."

"Good. Tell him to clean up his own blood."

"He is not bleeding, Grandmaster," Kincaid said, his voice flat. "And I
am not here to report on him. I am here to deliver a message."

Kincaid took a breath, as if bracing himself for an explosion.

"You have been summoned, Grandmaster."

Valdis froze. The quill snapped in his fingers, ink bleeding onto the
parchment. He looked at Kincaid, certain he had misheard.

"Summoned?" Valdis whispered. "I am the Dean of Discipline. I am not
summoned. I summon."

"To the Training Grounds," Kincaid continued, his face impassive.
"Immediately."

Valdis stood up slowly, his shadow stretching long across the floor. "By
whom? The Headmaster? The King?"

Kincaid met his eyes.

"By Tim."

The words hit Valdis like a physical blow. A brick to the chest. For a
moment, his brain refused to process the syntax. A novice---a peasant
novice on punishment detail---had *summoned* the Grandmaster.

It was insanity. It was suicide.

And it was, Valdis realized with a jolt of cold curiosity, interesting.

"He summons me," Valdis said, a dangerous smile touching his lips. He
wiped the ink from his fingers with a rag. "Like a peer."

"He said it concerns the curriculum," Kincaid added.

Valdis laughed. It was a dry, rasping sound. "The curriculum. Very well,
Kincaid. If the rat wishes to squeak at the hawk, let us hear him. But
bring the shackles. I suspect this meeting will end with an expulsion."

The Training Grounds were bathed in the pale, grey light of dawn. The
air was still.

Valdis swept into the arena, his black robes billowing, Kincaid a pace
behind him. He expected to see Tim standing alone, defiant, perhaps
holding a weapon in some foolish last stand.

He did not expect to see a classroom.

In the center of the main ring, Tim stood with his hands clasped behind
his back. He was wearing his grey novice uniform, freshly washed and
pressed.

But he wasn't alone.

Behind him stood the Squad. Gareth, Christopher, Lyssandra, Milo, Mara,
and a dozen others from the Draft Zone. They were standing in formation.
Not the rigid, terrified formation of the inspections, but a loose,
balanced stance.

"Grandmaster," Tim said. He didn't bow. He nodded. "Thank you for
coming."

Valdis stopped ten paces away. The sheer audacity of the boy was
breathtaking.

"Give me one reason," Valdis hissed, his voice carrying across the
silent yard, "why I should not have you dragged to the gates and thrown
into the street for this insolence."

"Because you value results," Tim said calmly. "Or so you claim."

Tim gestured to the group behind him.

"You told me that the Academy is a crucible. That iron sharpens iron.
You told me that my 'copper' methods were corrosion. That I was making
them soft."

Tim stepped to the side.

"Squad," Tim said. He didn't shout. He spoke in a normal conversational
tone. "The Phalanx. Standard Defensive. Execute."

Whatever Valdis expected, it wasn't this.

The Phalanx was a Third-Year maneuver. It required absolute
synchronization of Ki barriers. First Years didn't have the capacity to
link shields.

Gareth stepped forward, anchoring the center. He didn't roar. He
breathed. *Deep. Heavy.* His grey Ki flared, dense and immovable.

"Wall," Gareth rumbled.

Flanking him, Milo and Mara stepped up. They didn't try to match
Gareth's power. They leaned into it. They pulsed their Ki in rhythm with
his breath.

Lyssandra and Christopher stood in the second rank. Christopher wasn't
shaking. He closed his eyes, visualizing the perfect line. He acted as
the Regulator, smoothing out the jagged edges of the others' energy.

Lyssandra acted as the Monitor, directing the flow. "Stabilize left.
Milo, draw down."

In three seconds, a shimmering, semi-visible barrier of Ki formed in
front of them. It wasn't the jagged, chaotic static of novices. It was a
single, solid, curved wall of energy. It hummed with a low, harmonic
resonance.

It was perfect.

Valdis stared. His mouth opened slightly. This was impossible. These
were the rejects. The runts. They shouldn't be able to hold a Phalanx
for ten seconds.

"Hold," Tim ordered.

They held. Ten seconds. Twenty. Thirty.

They weren't straining. They weren't sweating. They were... comfortable.

"How?" Valdis whispered.

"You try to build walls out of identical bricks," Tim explained, walking
slowly along the line. "But people aren't identical. Gareth is the
stone. Milo is the mortar. Christopher is the plumb line. Lyssandra is
the architect."

Tim stopped in front of Valdis.

"You called me corrosion," Tim said. "But in metallurgy, when you mix
copper with tin, you don't get soft metal. You get Bronze. And Bronze
doesn't rust."

Tim signaled the group. "Release."

The wall vanished. The students exhaled in unison. They looked tired,
but triumphant. They looked at Valdis not with fear, but with pride.

Tim turned back to the Grandmaster.

"I am not leaving, Grandmaster. And neither are they. We aren't going to
quit. We aren't going to break. And we aren't going to fight each other
anymore."

Tim's voice dropped, becoming hard and clear.

"You can expel me for insolence. You can throw me out. But if you do,
you lose the only group of First Years who can execute a Phalanx
formation before the Winter Solstice."

He gestured to his friends.

"You want the strongest class in the Academy's history? Then let me
stay. Let me teach. And stop trying to break the tools just because you
don't like the shape of the handle."

Kincaid looked at Valdis. He was holding his breath. This was it. The
boy had cornered the hawk. He had used the Academy's own standard of
excellence as a shield.

Valdis looked at the Phalanx. He looked at Gareth, who was watching him
with the calm eyes of a mountain. He looked at Christopher, who wasn't
shaking.

And he looked at Tim. The peasant who had beaten his best enforcer,
organized the outcasts, and reverse-engineered a military formation in a
library basement.

Valdis hated him. He hated everything Tim represented. He hated the
softness, the cooperation, the *copper*.

But Valdis was, above all things, a man who wanted to win the King's War
games.

Slowly, painfully, Valdis uncurled his fists.

"The Phalanx..." Valdis rasped, his voice tight. "...was adequate."

He turned his cold gaze to Tim.

"You have made your point, Novice. You are not rust."

He stepped closer, looming over Tim.

"But do not mistake utility for approval. You have built a strong
shield. But a shield cannot win a war. If you wish to stay, you must
prove you can strike as well as you can stand."

Valdis turned on his heel, his black robes snapping.

"Kincaid. Cancel the suspension. And put them on the roster for the
Winter War Games."

Valdis stopped and looked back over his shoulder, a cruel glint in his
eye.

"Let us see if your Bronze cracks when the real Iron hits it."

He walked away.

Tim stood in the center of the ring. He let out a long breath. His knees
felt a little weak.

"Did we win?" Christopher squeaked from the back rank.

Tim looked at the retreating figure of the Grandmaster.

"We didn't win," Tim said softly. "We just qualified."

He turned to his Squad, his Bronze Phalanx.

"Good work," Tim grinned. "Now, who knows how to capture a flag?"
