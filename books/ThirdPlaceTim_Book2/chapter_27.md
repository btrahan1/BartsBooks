# Chapter 26: The Shadow Marshal

The summons was different this time. It didn't come with a threat of
expulsion or physical harm. It came in the form of a sealed envelope,
placed on Tim's pillow by a terrified first-year messenger.

Report to the Strategy Room. Midnight.

Tim arrived precisely on time. The Strategy Room was a chamber few
students ever saw. It was lined with maps of the continent and dominated
by a massive table depicting the Academy grounds and the surrounding
territories in miniature relief.

Grandmaster Valdis stood over the table. He looked older tonight. The
candlelight cast deep shadows in the hollows of his cheeks. He wasn't
looking at Tim with hatred; he was looking at him with the desperate,
calculating gaze of a gambler down to his last coin.

"Do you know what this is?" Valdis asked, gesturing to a large, empty
pedestal in the corner of the room.

"A dust collector?" Tim guessed.

"It is the resting place of the Tri-Academy Cup," Valdis said, his voice
bitter. "A trophy awarded to the school that wins the Grand Melee. It
has not sat in this room for twenty years."

Valdis poured himself a glass of water, his hand trembling slightly.
"For twenty years, the Royal Ki Academy has been eliminated in the first
round."

Tim walked over to the table. "Why?"

"Because we fight the Iron Bastion," Valdis spat the name like a curse.
"A militaristic school from the Western Crags. They are not nobles. They
are not duelists. They are... soldiers."

"And your students are duelists," Tim finished.

"My students are peacocks," Valdis corrected, turning to face Tim. The
admission hung heavy in the air. "They are technically brilliant. They
have higher Ki capacity. But when the Bastion hits them, they shatter.
The Bastion does not care about form. They do not care about 'first
blood.' They fight until the enemy stops moving."

Valdis gripped the edge of the table. "I cannot lose again, Novice. The
Board of Governors is... displeased. Another first-round exit, and I
will be replaced."

He looked at Tim. The silence stretched, heavy with the weight of
Valdis's pride being crushed under the boot of necessity.

"You weaponized a mud pit," Valdis said quietly. "You turned a group of
rejects into a Phalanx. You made Cassian Redgrave, a boy who thought
'flank' was a cut of meat, into a competent squad leader."

Valdis took a breath that sounded like it hurt.

"I am placing the Academy's team under your command."

Tim raised an eyebrow. "Grandmaster, if I give an order to the Golden
Circle, they will laugh. If I give an order to the Iron Heads, they will
challenge me to a duel."

"They will not," Valdis said, sliding a heavy object across the table.

It was a black armband embroidered with a silver hawk---the Academy's
crest. But the hawk held a key in its talons.

"The Marshal's Band," Valdis explained. "It grants absolute tactical
authority during the Games. Disobedience to the Marshal is disobedience
to me. It carries the penalty of immediate expulsion."

Tim picked up the band. It was heavy. It felt like a target.

"You want me to lead them," Tim said.

"No," Valdis corrected. "I want you to *win* with them. I don't care how
you do it. I don't care if you use mud, or bribes, or that ridiculous
copper magic of yours. Just beat the Bastion."

Tim looked at the map. He looked at the terrain of the tournament
grounds---a valley known as the Breaking Fields. He thought about the
Iron Bastion: soldiers, disciplined, brutal.

Then he thought about his classmates. Vain. Individualistic. Powerful,
but fragile.

"The Bastion fights as a unit," Tim mused. "A hammer."

"Yes," Valdis agreed.

"And we are... a collection of very sharp, very expensive glass
daggers."

"Essentially."

Tim tapped the table. "If you hit glass with a hammer, the glass
breaks."

"I am aware of the physics," Valdis snapped.

"But," Tim said, a slow, dangerous smile spreading across his face. "If
you grind the glass up... and you mix it with glue... and you blow it
into the eyes of the man holding the hammer..."

Valdis blinked.

"You want me to fight dirty," Tim clarified.

"I want the cup," Valdis said.

Tim picked up the armband and slid it onto his arm. It clashed horribly
with his wool uniform.

"Grandmaster," Tim said. "You've spent all year trying to make them
hard. You failed. So, for the next week, I'm going to stop trying to
make them soldiers."

"And make them what?"

"I'm going to make them a problem," Tim said. "A chaotic, expensive,
terrifying problem."

He looked at the map of the Breaking Fields.

"The Bastion expects a duel. We're going to give them a riot."

Valdis looked at the peasant boy, who suddenly looked very much like a
general. For the first time all year, Valdis didn't feel hatred. He felt
a shiver of genuine pity for the Iron Bastion.

"Do what you must," Valdis whispered. "Just bring it home."
