# Chapter 27: The Glass Shard Strategy

The Grand Amphitheater was designed for speeches of glory. The acoustics
were perfect, turning even a whisper into a proclamation. Fifty
students---the elite selection for the Grand Melee---sat in the stone
tiers. They were a tapestry of house colors, shimmering with Ki and
arrogance.

They were waiting for Grandmaster Valdis. They expected a speech about
honor, bloodlines, and the sacred duty of the Academy.

Instead, the side door opened, and Tim walked out.

He walked to the center of the stage. He dragged a heavy wooden crate
behind him. It scraped loudly against the stone floor---*SCREEEECH*---an
ugly sound that set teeth on edge.

He stopped. He sat on the crate. He looked at the fifty deadliest
students in the kingdom.

Silence. Then, a ripple of confusion.

"Where is the Grandmaster?" Jareth Vane called out, leaning forward.
"And why is the mascot on the stage?"

Tim didn't answer. He slowly rolled up the sleeve of his grey wool
tunic.

The silver hawk of the Marshal's Band caught the sunlight.

The room didn't just go silent; the air left it. They knew what that
band meant. It was a relic of absolute authority, rarely seen, never
worn by a First Year. Never, in the history of the world, worn by a
peasant.

"No," Cassian Redgrave stood up, his face pale. "That's... that's a
joke. Valdis wouldn't."

"He did," Tim said. His voice wasn't loud, but the acoustics carried it
to the back row. "Sit down, Cassian."

"I will not take orders from---"

"I said sit down," Tim cut him off. He didn't shout. He injected a pulse
of Ki into his voice---not a threat, but a weight. A command born of
certainty.

Cassian stared at the band. He stared at Tim. The penalty for disobeying
a Marshal was expulsion. Cassian sat.

Tim looked at them. The Golden Circle. The Iron Heads. The Squad.

"For twenty years," Tim began, "this Academy has lost. You walk into the
Breaking Fields with your shiny armor and your perfect katas. You line
up. You bow. And then the Iron Bastion runs you over like a wagon
crushing a squirrel."

"They are brutes!" Beatrice Sterling cried out. "They have no honor!"

"Correct," Tim agreed. "They are soldiers. And you are duelists. You are
trying to win a fencing match against a landslide."

Tim reached into the crate he had dragged out. He pulled out a
beautiful, crystal wine goblet. He held it up.

"This is you," Tim said. "Refined. Expensive. Beautiful. Full of
potential."

He reached into the crate again and pulled out a jagged, dirty brick.

"This is the Iron Bastion. Ugly. Cheap. Hard."

He looked at the crowd. "What happens when the brick hits the glass?"

He didn't wait for an answer. He smashed the brick into the goblet.
*SMASH.* Shards of crystal exploded across the stage.

"You break," Tim said into the stunned silence. "Every. Single. Time."

He kicked the shards.

"Grandmaster Valdis tried to turn you into bricks. He failed. You aren't
bricks. You're glass. You're fragile."

Jareth stood up, his hand on his sword. "You dare call us fragile? I'll
come down there and---"

"Sit down, Jareth!" Tim snapped. "You *are* fragile! You have egos that
bruise if the wind blows the wrong way. You faint if you miss a meal.
You duel over insults instead of objectives."

He walked to the edge of the stage.

"But here is the thing about broken glass."

Tim crouched down and picked up a large, jagged shard of the goblet. He
held it up to the light. It was razor-sharp. Dangerous.

"If you step on a brick," Tim said softly, "you hurt your foot. But if
you step on this... you bleed. You can't fight a shard of glass. It's
too small to hit, and too sharp to hold."

He stood up, looking at the fifty students.

"We aren't going to be soldiers this year. We aren't going to form
lines. We aren't going to bow."

Tim threw the shard down.

"We are going to shatter. We are going to become a thousand jagged
pieces of pain. We are going to be everywhere and nowhere. We aren't
going to fight them."

A slow, wicked grin spread across Tim's face---the grin of a street rat
who had just spotted an unguarded coin purse.

"We are going to mug them."

He pointed to the Golden Circle. "Beatrice. Your group has the highest
ranged output. But you can't aim under pressure. You are no longer
artillery. You are the Distraction. You will fire wild. You will make
noise. You will make them look up."

He pointed to the Iron Heads. "Cassian. You want to be heroes? Fine. You
are the Bait. You will charge, you will scream, and then... you will run
away."

Cassian blinked. "Run?"

"You will run," Tim commanded. "You will make them chase you. Because
when they chase you, they break formation."

He pointed to the back, to Gareth, Lyssandra, Christopher, and the Draft
Zone recruits.

"And when they break formation... when they are looking up at the lights
and chasing the rabbits... they will step on the Shards."

Tim gestured to his Squad.

"My team knows the terrain. My team knows traps. My team knows how to
fight in the mud. While you distract them, we will bleed them. We will
steal their supplies. We will collapse their tents. We will make them
tired, hungry, and paranoid."

Tim looked at the entire assembly.

"The Iron Bastion expects a battle. They train for a battle. So we are
going to give them a nightmare."

He tapped the silver band on his arm.

"I don't care about your House names. I don't care about your honor. I
care about the Cup. If you want to lose with dignity, leave now. If you
want to win by being the absolute worst thing that has ever happened to
the Iron Bastion... then stay in your seats."

No one moved.

Cassian looked at the broken glass on the stage. He looked at Tim. He
imagined the Iron Bastion---those faceless, disciplined soldiers who had
mocked him last year.

Slowly, a smile formed on Cassian's face. It wasn't a nice smile. It was
the smile of a bully who had just been given permission to use his dirty
tricks for a good cause.

"We're going to mug them," Cassian repeated, tasting the words.

"We're going to mug them," Tim confirmed.

"Marshal," Cassian said, leaning back and crossing his arms. "What is
the first order?"

Tim reached into the crate and pulled out a stack of shovels.

"Tonight," Tim said, "we dig holes."
