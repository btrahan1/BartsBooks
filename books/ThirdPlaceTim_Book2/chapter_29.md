# Chapter 28: The Furnace of the Fields

The Breaking Fields lived up to their name. It was a valley of knee-deep
mire, jagged rock formations, and freezing fog.

For the first hour, it was a slaughter.

The Iron Bastion marched in---three hundred grey-clad soldiers moving in
perfect, stomping unison. They were a machine. They expected a line of
duelists to meet them in the center field for an honorable exchange of
fire and steel.

They found nothing.

"Where are they?" Commander Kaelen of the Bastion growled. He was a
giant, his skin hardened by iron-aspected Ki, his shield the size of a
door.

BOOM.

To the left, a barrage of fireballs exploded from the ridgeline. The
Golden Circle, led by Beatrice, unleashed hell---not aiming at the
soldiers, but at the mud in front of them. Steam blinded the Bastion's
left flank.

"Flank left! Shields up!" Kaelen roared.

As they turned, Cassian Redgrave and his Iron Heads burst from the fog
on the *right*. They didn't charge. They screamed insults, threw rocks,
and banged their swords on their shields.

"Cowards!" Cassian yelled. "Come and get us, you iron-plated turtles!"

The Bastion discipline held for a moment, then snapped. A platoon broke
formation to chase the Iron Heads.

They took ten steps and vanished.

Lyssandra and Gareth had spent all night weakening the crust of the bog.
The Bastion soldiers plunged chest-deep into freezing sludge.

"Now!" Tim's voice echoed across the valley, amplified by Christopher's
wind magic.

From the mud itself, the Draft Zone squad emerged. They were covered in
muck, invisible until they moved. They didn't fight fair. They used
grappling hooks to pull shields down. They used sand in vizors. They
used sleep powders.

It was chaos. The machine was grinding its gears against a thousand
grains of sand.

Up on the observation deck, Grandmaster Valdis gripped the railing, his
knuckles white. He was watching his students fight like alley cats. It
was undignified. It was messy.

It was winning.

But the Iron Bastion had not won the Cup for twenty years by being
fragile.

"IGNORE THEM!" Commander Kaelen roared, his voice shaking the valley. He
realized the game. "They are stinging us to death! Ignore the flies!
FORM THE WEDGE! TARGET THE HILL!"

The discipline of the Bastion snapped back into place. They stopped
chasing Cassian. They ignored the Golden Circle's fire. They formed a
tight, impenetrable triangle of shields---the Iron Wedge.

They began to march. Straight for the central hill. Straight for the
Cup.

Tim's traps bounced off their shields. Beatrice's fire washed over them
harmlessly. Gareth tried to block them, slamming into the front line
like a battering ram, but the Wedge absorbed his impact and threw him
aside.

They were unstoppable. A rolling mountain of iron.

Tim stood alone at the base of the hill. He watched the Wedge coming. He
checked the position of his troops. Cassian was cut off. Gareth was
down. The Golden Circle was out of mana.

The strategy had worked for ninety percent of the fight. But the last
ten percent required something else.

Tim tapped the silver Marshal's band on his arm.

"All units," Tim said calmly into the comms crystal. "Fall back. Regroup
at the ridge."

"Tim!" Christopher's voice panicked over the link. "They're going to
overrun the hill! You're the only one there!"

"I know," Tim said.

"Run!" Cassian yelled. "You can't stop the Wedge!"

Tim looked at the wall of shields thundering toward him. Three hundred
soldiers against one peasant.

"I don't need to stop them," Tim whispered to himself. "I just need to
overheat the engine."

He rolled up his sleeves.

He closed his eyes. *Inhale.*

He reached into his gut. The Copper Core wasn't just humming today. It
was screaming. He had spent months paving the roads, widening the pipes,
learning the perfect flow.

He didn't open the valve to a trickle. He ripped the wheel off.

ACCEPT.

Tim's eyes snapped open. They weren't brown anymore. They were glowing
with a deep, molten orange light.

The air around him shimmered. The mud at his feet began to hiss and dry,
cracking into terracotta.

Commander Kaelen saw the lone figure standing in the path of the Wedge.
He laughed.

"RUN HIM OVER!" Kaelen bellowed.

The Wedge slammed into Tim.

It should have turned him into paste. The impact of twenty
shield-bearers hitting one boy should have shattered every bone in his
body.

CLANG.

A sound like a church bell struck by a meteor rang out.

Tim hadn't moved.

He had caught the leading edge of the Wedge---two massive iron
shields---with his bare hands.

His feet dug furrows into the earth, sliding back a foot, two feet,
three... and then he stopped. He grounded his stance, sinking into the
earth like an anchor.

"What?" Kaelen gasped from the second rank. "PUSH!"

The Bastion pushed. Ki flared---jagged, grey iron energy trying to crush
the boy.

Tim gritted his teeth. The pressure was immense. But he didn't fight the
force. He *conducted* it.

He took the kinetic energy of the charge and the magical energy of their
Ki, and he fed it into his Core. He became a heat sink.

"You like Iron?" Tim gritted out, smoke beginning to rise from his
shoulders. "Let's see how you hold it when it's *white hot*."

He reversed the flow.

He dumped his entire reserve of thermal energy---months of stored
warmth, plus the energy he was absorbing from their charge---straight
into the metal shields he was holding.

Conductivity. Zero resistance.

The heat transfer was instantaneous.

The iron shields went from cold to blistering in a microsecond.

"AAAAHH!"

The front rank screaming wasn't a battle cry. It was pain. The metal
handles of their shields seared their palms. They dropped the shields
instinctively.

The front of the Wedge collapsed.

Tim didn't stop. He stepped *into* the formation.

He was a blur of grey wool and orange light. He didn't punch; he
touched. He slapped the flat of a blade, and the steel glowed
cherry-red, forcing the wielder to drop it. He grabbed a soldier's iron
pauldron, and the heat transfer shot through the armor, cooking the man
inside his own shell until he collapsed from heatstroke.

He was a walking furnace.

A spear thrust at him. Tim caught the shaft. The wood burst into flames
instantly.

"He's a demon!" a Bastion soldier screamed, backing away. "My sword!
It's melting!"

Commander Kaelen roared, stepping over his fallen men. "Out of my way!"

Kaelen swung a massive warhammer, wreathed in grey lightning. "DIE,
PEASANT!"

Tim looked up. He was sweating, his skin flushed a deep, angry red. He
was running at critical temperature. If he held this much heat for
another minute, he would burn himself out from the inside.

He had to dump the load.

Tim caught the head of the warhammer with both hands.

The shockwave cracked the ground for thirty feet. Kaelen grinned,
pushing down, expecting Tim to crumble.

"You are strong," Tim wheezed, looking up at the giant. "But you forgot
the lesson of the First Year."

"What lesson?" Kaelen sneered.

"Copper," Tim said, his eyes blazing like twin suns, "is a better
conductor than Iron."

Tim didn't push back. He pulled. He pulled all of Kaelen's lightning,
all of his power, and channeled it through his own body, mixing it with
his superheated core.

And then he sent it all down into the wet, conductive mud beneath
Kaelen's feet.

"**GROUND.**"

CRACK-BOOM.

A massive geyser of steam and electrified mud exploded upward. It was a
thermal detonation.

Kaelen was blasted backward, his armor smoking, his hammer flying from
his hands. The entire center of the Iron Bastion formation was knocked
off its feet by the shockwave.

Tim stood alone in the center of a crater of dried, baked earth. Steam
rose from his body in thick waves. His uniform was singed. His hands
were blackened with soot.

He swayed.

The remaining Bastion soldiers stared at him. They outnumbered him ten
to one still. But they looked at their commander, unconscious in the
mud. They looked at their melted weapons. They looked at the boy who was
literally smoking.

And they took a step back.

"Marshal!" a voice screamed.

Cassian Redgrave came charging over the ridge, leading the remnants of
the Iron Heads. Behind him came Gareth, roaring like a beast, and the
Golden Circle, raining fire.

They saw Tim standing there. They saw what he had done.

And for the first time in the history of the Academy, they didn't see a
peasant. They didn't see a Copper Core.

They saw their King.

"FOR TIM!" Cassian screamed.

"FOR THE MARSHAL!" the Academy roared.

They crashed into the demoralized, broken remnants of the Bastion. It
wasn't a battle anymore. It was a mop-up operation.

Tim didn't join them. He sat down heavily on the baked earth. He took a
deep, shaky breath, trying to cool his core down.

He looked at his hands. They were trembling.

"Just Tim," he whispered to himself, closing his eyes as the cheers of
victory erupted around him. "Just... barely."
