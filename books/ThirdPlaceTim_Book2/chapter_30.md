# Chapter 29: The Blank Page

The infirmary was quiet, smelling of antiseptic herbs and burnt ozone.

Grandmaster Valdis stood at the foot of the bed, his hands clasped
behind his back. He stared down at the boy who was currently wrapped in
cooling bandages, his skin slathered in aloe and burn salve.

Tim was asleep. Or unconscious. It was hard to tell. His breathing was
shallow, and his skin still radiated a faint, feverish heat.

Valdis held a file folder in his hands. It was the student dossier for
"Novice 94."

It was terrifyingly thin.

*Name: Tim.* *House: None.* *Origin: Capital Slums.* *Sponsor: House
Vance.* *Ki Classification: Copper (Anomaly).*

That was it. No lineage. No famous tutors. No bloodline traits. A blank
page.

And yet, Valdis had just watched this blank page dismantle a Phalanx of
the Iron Bastion---a military unit that had humiliated the Academy for
two decades---using a technique that shouldn't exist.

"Who are you?" Valdis whispered to the sleeping boy.

He opened the file again, as if staring at the ink would make hidden
words appear.

A peasant doesn't learn *Grounding* by accident. A street rat doesn't
understand the thermodynamics of a superheated core. That was lost
knowledge. That was the kind of theoretical application found in the
forbidden scrolls of the Deep Library, or the lost manuals of the
Sun-King's Guard.

Valdis's mind, trained in the intrigue of the court, began to spin wild
theories.

Was he a bastard of the Royal line? A secret experiment by House Vance?
A spy from a rival nation trained from birth to infiltrate the Academy?

"Grandmaster."

Valdis snapped the folder shut. Tim's eyes were open. They were
bloodshot, rimmed with red, but the molten glow was gone. They were just
brown. tired. Human.

"You are awake," Valdis said, his voice devoid of its usual venom. "The
medics say your internal temperature reached levels that should have
boiled your blood. You survived only because your Core... adapted."

"Copper conducts," Tim rasped. His voice sounded like sandpaper. "It
doesn't hold."

"That technique," Valdis pressed, stepping closer. "The heat sink. The
discharge. Who taught you that? Was it Lord Vance? A secret tutor?"

Tim shifted painfully on the bed. He looked at the ceiling.

"A blacksmith," Tim said.

Valdis blinked. "A... blacksmith?"

"In Oakhaven," Tim murmured. "Named Borin. He told me that if you get
the metal hot enough, it stops being hard and starts being fluid. And he
told me that if you quench it too fast, it cracks."

Tim looked at his bandaged hands. "I tried not to crack."

Valdis stared at him. He wanted a conspiracy. He wanted a grand
explanation. He was being given... a blacksmith.

"You defeated Commander Kaelen," Valdis said, his voice tight. "A man
with an Iron Core density of 8.0. You, with a Copper Core density of...
well, whatever you are. It is mathematically impossible."

"Math changes when you add variables, Grandmaster," Tim said, closing
his eyes again. "Kaelen was fighting a duel. I was fighting a fire. He
wanted to win. I just wanted to save my friends."

Valdis looked at the boy. For the first time, he saw the terrifying
truth.

There was no secret master. There was no royal bloodline. There was no
conspiracy.

There was just a boy who paid attention. A boy who looked at the world
not as a hierarchy to be climbed, but as a machine to be understood.
While the nobles were busy polishing their crests, Tim had been reading
the instruction manual of the universe.

"We passed the First Round," Valdis stated. "The Iron Bastion has been
eliminated. The Academy advances to the Semi-Finals against the Mystic
Spire."

"Good," Tim whispered, sleep dragging him back down. "Tell Cassian... he
held the line."

Tim's breathing leveled out. He was asleep again.

Valdis stood there for a long time. He looked at the thin file in his
hand.

He realized he had made a mistake. He had treated Tim like a disease to
be cured. But Tim wasn't a disease.

He was a mirror.

And when Valdis looked into him, he saw all the wasted potential, all
the arrogance, and all the blind spots of the Academy reflected back at
him.

Valdis turned and walked to the door. He paused, looking back at the
"peasant."

"Just Tim," Valdis murmured, testing the name.

He slid the file into his robe.

"No," Valdis decided, stepping out into the hallway where the cheers of
the students could still be heard echoing from the dorms. "You are not
'Just Tim' anymore. You are the most dangerous thing this Academy has
ever produced."

He walked down the hall, his mind racing. They had won the battle. But
Valdis knew, with a sudden, chilling certainty, that he was losing the
war for the soul of his school.

And for the first time in twenty years... he wasn't sure he wanted to
win it.
