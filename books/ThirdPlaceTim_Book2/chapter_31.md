# Chapter 30: The Patron and the Architect

Grandmaster Valdis closed the heavy oak door of the infirmary with a
click that sounded deafening in the quiet corridor. He took a breath,
trying to compose his face back into the mask of the Iron Dean.

"How is he?"

The voice came from the shadows of an alcove not three feet away.

Valdis jumped. It was a small, involuntary twitch, but for a Grandmaster
of Ki, it was a humiliating loss of composure. He spun around, his hand
instinctively going to the empty space where a weapon would be.

Lady Elara Vane stood there. She was dressed in the midnight-blue
training robes of the Third Circle, but they were dusty, and her face
was drawn. She had been away---sequestered in the High Spire for the
"Advanced Ki Manipulation" intensive---but the moment the news of the
victory broke, she had abandoned her meditation.

Valdis swallowed hard. The lump in his throat felt like a stone. He
looked at the girl---no, the young woman---standing before him. He saw
the fierce, terrifying intelligence in her eyes.

"Lady Elara," Valdis rasped, recovering his voice. "You should be in the
Spire."

"And you should have stopped the match when the Kaelen summoned
lightning," she countered, her voice ice-cold. She stepped out of the
shadows. "I asked you a question, Grandmaster. How. Is. He?"

Valdis looked at the door. "He is... hot," Valdis said, struggling for
the right word. "His internal temperature is stabilizing, but the medics
say his meridians are scorched. He pushed enough energy through his
system to power a siege golem."

Elara let out a breath, her shoulders dropping slightly. "But he will
recover?"

"He is Copper," Valdis said, as if that explained everything. "He bent.
He did not break."

Valdis looked at Elara, really looked at her. He thought of the
sponsorship. The sudden appearance of this anomaly. The way she had
defended him in the courtyard.

"You knew," Valdis whispered. "You and Kael. You knew exactly what he
was."

"We knew what he *could* be," Elara corrected, her gaze unwavering. "We
knew that if you stopped trying to beat him into the shape of a sword,
he might become a very effective shield."

"A shield?" Valdis let out a sharp, incredulous laugh. "Elara, he didn't
block the Iron Bastion. He *ate* them. He turned their own power against
them and cooked them in their armor. That isn't defense. That is... that
is total war."

"It is efficiency," Elara said, echoing Tim's favorite word.

Valdis ran a hand over his face. He felt old. "I tried to break him,
Elara. For months. I gave him the worst assignments. I put him against
Redgrave. I sent Verrick to cripple him. And all I did was..."

"...temper him," Elara finished.

She stepped closer, her voice dropping to a conspiratorial whisper.

"You wanted to win the Cup, Grandmaster. You wanted to break the
twenty-year curse. Well, you have your weapon now. You have a student
who can dismantle armies, lead the rejects, and endure fire that would
kill a Master."

She looked at the door to Tim's room.

"But do not make the mistake of thinking you own him. He fought today
for his friends. He fought for the Squad. He did not fight for you."

Valdis nodded slowly. He knew it was true. The salute in the courtyard,
the "credit" Tim had given him---it was all a game. A polite fiction to
keep the hierarchy intact while Tim rewrote the rules underneath it.

"The Mystic Spire is next," Valdis said, shifting to strategy, the only
ground he felt safe on. "They use illusions. Mind arts. Brute force
won't work on them."

"Good thing Tim isn't a brute," Elara said. A small, proud smile touched
her lips. "He's a scholar. And I suspect he's going to have a very
interesting plan for dealing with wizards."

Valdis looked at the Vane heiress. He saw the partnership there, the
invisible thread connecting the High Noble and the Low Peasant.

"Go to him," Valdis said, stepping aside. "The medics are done. He... he
asked for water. And he mumbled something about thermal dynamics."

Elara's smile widened. "Of course he did."

She walked past the Grandmaster, her hand resting briefly on the door
handle.

"Grandmaster," she said, pausing. "Thank you. For not stepping in."

"I wanted to see what would happen," Valdis admitted honestly.

"And?"

Valdis looked at the empty corridor, remembering the sight of the
smoking crater in the Breaking Fields.

"I have never been so terrified," Valdis said softly. "And so
impressed."

Elara slipped into the room, leaving the Grandmaster alone in the
hallway to contemplate the monster he had helped create.
