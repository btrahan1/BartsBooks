# Chapter 31: The Ice Lotus

The door clicked shut, sealing out the noise of the Academy. The room
was dim, lit only by the low, orange glow of the banked hearth and the
faint, residual luminescence fading from Tim's own skin.

Tim lay perfectly still. His hands were heavily bandaged, looking like
white clubs against the grey wool blanket. He felt... brittle. Like a
piece of pottery that had been fired in a kiln that was too hot, leaving
him dry and prone to cracking.

He sensed her presence before he saw her. The air in the room shifted,
growing cooler, sharper.

"You look terrible," Elara said softly, stepping into his line of sight.

Tim cracked a dry smile. It pulled at the burnt skin on his lip. "You
should see the other guys. They're... melted."

Elara didn't smile back. Her face was pale, her eyes scanning the
readouts on the medical crystals hovering by the bed. She saw the stress
lines on his meridian chart---jagged peaks where the energy had nearly
torn him apart.

She reached into the deep fold of her midnight-blue sleeve and produced
a small, lacquered box. She opened it with a snap. Inside sat a single
pill. It was the size of a pearl, deep azure in color, and it emitted a
faint, visible mist of cold air.

"This will help," she said.

Tim looked at it. "What is it?"

"Concentrated Ice Lotus extract, bound with powdered moonstone," Elara
explained, her voice regaining its lecture-hall cadence to mask her
worry. "It's a High-Grade stabilizer. Usually reserved for Masters who
suffer from Ki deviation during deep meditation."

She hesitated. "I... I stole it. From the High Spire archives."

Tim's eyebrows shot up. "Theft? Lady Elara, think of your Ethics grade."

"My Ethics grade will survive," she said, sitting on the edge of the
bed. "Your Core might not."

She held the pill to his lips. Tim couldn't use his bandaged hands. He
opened his mouth, and she placed the cold pearl on his tongue.

"Don't chew," she instructed. "Let it dissolve. Swallow the vapor."

Tim closed his mouth. The reaction was instant. It wasn't like drinking
water; it was like swallowing a winter breeze. A shock of absolute, pure
cold washed down his throat, bypassing his stomach and settling directly
into the furnace of his Copper Core.

The hum of heat in his ears faded. The throbbing pain in his meridians
dulled into a numb, pleasant ache. He let out a long, shuddering breath.
Visible frost coated his lips.

"Better?" she asked.

"It tastes like... snow," Tim whispered. "And expensive mistakes."

"It stabilizes the thermal variance," Elara said, watching the color
return to his face. "You overheated the engine, Tim. You used your body
as a conduit for lightning and fire simultaneously. Do you know what
happens to copper wire when you overload it?"

"It melts," Tim said.

"It vaporizes," Elara corrected sharply. "You could have evaporated,
Tim. Gone. Just a scorch mark on the mud."

She looked down at her hands, which were trembling slightly. "When
Valdis told me... I thought I was too late."

Tim looked at her. He saw the fatigue in her posture. The High Spire
training was grueling---mental conditioning meant to strip away
emotion---yet here she was, feeling everything.

"I calculated the load," Tim lied gently. "I knew the mud would act as a
ground."

"You guessed," Elara countered. "But... it was a brilliant guess."

She reached out and rested her hand gently on his forearm, just above
the bandages.

"Valdis is terrified of you," she said quietly. "I saw it in his eyes.
He thinks you're a monster."

"And you?" Tim asked, looking at her. "What do you think?"

Elara looked at the boy wrapped in bandages. She thought of the pill she
had stolen without a second thought. She thought of the way her heart
had stopped when she heard the explosion from the Breaking Fields.

"I think," Elara said, standing up and smoothing her robes, "that you
are going to need a lot more of these pills if we're going to survive
the Semi-Finals."

She walked to the window, looking out at the night sky.

"The Mystic Spire is next, Tim. They don't use lightning. They use
illusions. Nightmares. They attack the mind."

She turned back to him, her silhouette framed by the moon.

"Your Copper Core can handle heat. But can it handle fear?"

Tim lay back against the pillow, the cool energy of the Ice Lotus
swirling through him. He thought about the streets. He thought about the
hunger. He thought about the day he almost stole a crate from a wagon
and changed his destiny.

"I've lived with fear my whole life, Elara," Tim said softly. "It's an
old friend."

He closed his eyes.

"Let the Mystics come. I have a plan for them, too."

"Sleep," Elara commanded gently. "I'll stay until the fever breaks."

Tim drifted off, the cold pearl in his gut fighting the fire, watched
over by the only noble in the world who knew that the most valuable
thing in the room wasn't the gold, but the copper.
