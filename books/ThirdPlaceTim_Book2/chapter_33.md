# Chapter 32: The Ghost of Iron

The delay was officially listed as "Structural Maintenance." The
official report stated that the thermal detonation in the Breaking
Fields had compromised the integrity of the underlying drainage system,
requiring twenty-four hours of earth-moving by the Academy's
geo-mancers.

It was a lie, of course. The drainage was fine. The earth was just baked
hard.

Grandmaster Valdis had spent three hours threatening, bribing, and
bullying the Tournament Committee to buy that extra day. He told them it
was for the safety of the spectators.

But as he sat in the corner of Tim's infirmary room, watching the rain
streak the glass, Valdis knew the truth. He had bought the day for the
boy.

"The swelling is down," Valdis said, breaking the long silence. He was
not wearing his imposing black robes of office. He wore a simple grey
tunic, looking less like a monolith and more like a tired, aging man.

"Elara's pill helped," Tim replied from the bed. He was sitting up now,
flexing his stiff, bandaged fingers. "And the rest. Thank you,
Grandmaster."

"Don't thank me," Valdis grunted, staring out the window. "I am merely
protecting my investment. If you enter the Mental Arena with a fractured
concentration, the Mystic Spire will turn your brain into soup."

Tim watched him. He saw the way Valdis's hand gripped the arm of the
chair---tight, white-knuckled, a permanent state of tension.

"You hate this tournament," Tim observed quietly.

"I hate losing," Valdis corrected automatically.

"No," Tim said. "It's more than that. You hate the field. You hate the
mud. Every time you looked at the Iron Bastion, you didn't look angry.
You looked... guilty."

Valdis stiffened. He turned slowly to face Tim. The "Iron Dean" mask
slipped, revealing a haunt of old pain.

"You have sharp eyes, Just Tim," Valdis whispered. "Too sharp for your
own good."

Valdis stood up and walked to the small table by the bed, pouring two
cups of water. He handed one to Tim.

"Twenty years ago," Valdis began, his voice low. "I was the Captain. I
was the pride of the Academy. Iron Core. Density 9.2. I was
unstoppable."

He took a sip, staring into the past.

"We had a team. Me, the Golden Circle... and a boy named Elias. He was
from a minor house. He had a Wind affinity. He was fast, clever. A lot
like... your friend Christopher. He didn't like to hit things. He liked
to redirect them."

Tim held his cup, listening.

"We met the Iron Bastion in the finals," Valdis continued. "The 'Year of
the Red Mud.' They formed the Wedge. The same Wedge you faced."

Valdis's hand shook slightly.

"My strategy was simple. Iron sharpens Iron. We would meet them head-on.
I ordered Elias to hold the left flank. I told him to stand his ground.
To be a wall. I told him that if he retreated, he was a coward."

Valdis closed his eyes.

"Elias was not a wall. He was wind. But he loved the Academy. He wanted
to please his Captain. So he stood. He tried to be Iron."

The room was silent, save for the drumming of the rain.

"The Wedge rolled over him," Valdis whispered. "They crushed his legs.
Shattered his Core. He never walked again. He never channeled Ki again."

Valdis opened his eyes. They were wet.

"We lost the match. But I lost something more. I decided then that I
would never let a student enter that field unless they were hard enough
to survive it. I purged the softness. I purged the wind, the copper, the
clever tricks. I forced everyone to become Iron, so they wouldn't break
like Elias."

He looked at Tim with a tragic intensity.

"I hated you," Valdis confessed. "Because you reminded me of him. You
were clever. You were soft. And I was terrified that if I let you fight,
I would have to watch you break, just like I watched him."

Tim sat quietly, processing the confession. He looked at the man who had
tormented him for months. He didn't see a villain. He saw a man
traumatized by his own command failure, trying to protect the future by
freezing it in the past.

"Elias didn't break because he was soft, Grandmaster," Tim said softly.

Valdis looked up.

"He broke because you used a fan to stop a boulder," Tim said. "You
asked the Wind to be a Wall. That isn't the Wind's fault. And it isn't
the Wall's fault."

Tim shifted, leaning forward despite the pain in his ribs.

"You've spent twenty years trying to turn everyone into a Wall so you'd
never have to make a bad call again. But that's not protection. That's
fear."

Valdis flinched. "I protected them."

"You made them brittle," Tim corrected gently. "But you didn't break me.
And you didn't break the Squad."

Tim held out his bandaged hand.

"You delayed the match today," Tim said. "You bought us time. You
protected us. Not by making us hard, but by giving us space to heal."

Tim smiled. It was a warm, forgiving expression.

"You're a good General, Valdis. You just needed a different kind of
army."

Valdis stared at the boy's hand. He thought of Elias, broken in the mud.
He thought of Tim, standing in the crater, victorious.

Slowly, hesitantly, Valdis reached out. He didn't shake Tim's hand. He
placed his own large, calloused hand over Tim's bandaged fist, a gesture
of protective solidarity.

"Don't die tomorrow," Valdis choked out, his voice thick.

"I don't plan to," Tim said. "I have a library to organize, remember?"

Valdis let out a short, wet laugh. He stood up, smoothing his tunic,
pulling the Iron Dean mask back into place---though it fit a little
looser now.

"Get some sleep, Marshal," Valdis said, moving to the door. "The Mystics
are already in the city. They bring fog and mirrors."

"Let them come," Tim said, settling back into the pillows.

Valdis paused at the door. He looked back at Tim.

"Elias," Valdis said softly. "He... he would have liked you."

"Maybe," Tim murmured, eyes closing. "Maybe he would have joined the
Squad."

Valdis stepped out into the hall. He felt lighter than he had in twenty
years. The ghost of Elias wasn't gone, but for the first time, it wasn't
screaming. It was just... watching. And maybe, just maybe, rooting for
the Copper.
