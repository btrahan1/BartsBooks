# Chapter 35: The New Blueprint

Grandmaster Valdis stood at the edge of the observation balcony, his
hands gripping the stone railing so tightly that the veins stood out
like cords. Below him, the violet fog of the Mystic Spire was thinning,
shredded not by wind, but by the relentless, synchronized advance of his
students.

He watched as Lyssandra Vane froze, staring in terror at nothing---an
illusion of a wyvern, likely. Beside her, Cassian Redgrave didn't draw
his sword. He simply placed a heavy hand on her shoulder and whispered
something. Lyssandra blinked, the terror vanished, and they stepped
forward together.

Further to the left, Gareth Orso found a Mystic student deep in a
trance, chanting a spell of vertigo. The old Gareth would have smashed
the boy with a shield. This Gareth simply poked the chanter in the chest
with a single finger. The Mystic toppled over backward, breaking his
concentration, and the illusion of a spinning world vanished.

It was a rout. It was surgical. It was... humane.

"They are walking through it," Master Kincaid breathed, standing beside
Valdis. "The Spire has thrown their worst nightmares at them, and they
are treating it like a mild drizzle."

Valdis didn't answer immediately. He felt a swelling in his chest that
was foreign to him. Pride? Yes. But it was curaled by a bitter, acidic
wash of shame.

For twenty years, he had tried to build warriors by stripping away their
humanity, by forcing them to repress their fears until they became
brittle shells of iron. He had thought emotion was a weakness.

He watched Tim, glowing with that soft copper light in the center of the
formation, acting as the lighthouse in the storm.

Tim hadn't stripped away their humanity. He had weaponized it. He had
taught them that fear shared is fear halved.

"I was wrong," Valdis whispered.

Kincaid looked at him, startled. Grandmasters did not admit error.
"Sir?"

"Look at them, Kincaid," Valdis said, gesturing to the field where his
students were now gently zip-tying the hands of the defeated Mystics. "I
spent two decades trying to forge swords. Single, sharp, brittle swords.
And today, a peasant showed me that I should have been building a
chainmail vest. A thousand linked rings, unbreakable because they hold
each other."

Valdis turned away from the railing, pacing the small room. The shame
was burning, but from the ash of his ego, something else was rising. The
mind of the academic strategist was waking up.

"The Cup is within reach," Kincaid noted, checking the magical
scoreboard. "The Academy will face the High Sanctum in the finals. We
could actually win."

"The Cup?" Valdis waved a hand dismissively, as if swatting a fly. "Yes,
yes, the shiny metal bowl. Fine. Put it on the shelf."

He walked to his desk, grabbed a quill, and pulled out a fresh sheet of
parchment. He began to scribble furiously.

"Kincaid, take a note."

"Now? In the middle of the match?"

"Especially now," Valdis snapped, his eyes bright with a new fervor.
"Next semester. The curriculum is scrapped. All of it."

Kincaid's jaw dropped. "All of it? The Board of Governors will revolt."

"Let them," Valdis growled, writing rapidly. "No more 'Iron Heads.' No
more 'Golden Circle.' We are implementing a Squad System. Permanent
teams. Mixed aptitudes. I want a Weaver paired with a Redgrave in every
dorm room."

He circled something on the paper so hard the quill tore through.

"And Ki Dynamics. Corvus was right. We focus on Flow, not Capacity. We
teach them the Circuit. We teach them the Anchor."

"And what of Tim?" Kincaid asked, looking down at the field where the
"Marshal" was currently helping a Mystic student find his glasses.

Valdis stopped writing. He looked at the boy through the window.

"He cannot remain a Novice," Valdis said quietly. "It would be a farce.
But we cannot make him a Master; he is too young, and the politics would
destroy him."

Valdis tapped the quill against his chin.

"We will create a new position. 'Student Adjutant.' He will attend
classes, yes. But he will also teach. Specifically, he will teach
*Tactical Empathy* and *Applied Physics*."

"Applied Physics?" Kincaid asked. "Is that a martial art?"

"It is when he does it," Valdis muttered.

The horn blew below. The match was over. The Royal Academy had advanced
to the Finals. The students were cheering, hugging, lifting Tim onto
Gareth's shoulders.

Valdis looked at the celebration. He felt the shame recede, replaced by
the thrill of the work ahead. He had wasted twenty years. He would not
waste a single second more.

"Let them have their Cup," Valdis said, staring at his new syllabus with
a hungry smile. "I want the future."

He looked at Kincaid.

"Prepare the lecture halls, Kincaid. Next semester, we are going to
teach them how to be Copper."
