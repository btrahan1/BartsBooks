# Chapter 36: The Blind Light

The Strategy Room was quiet, save for the crackling of the hearth. The
map on the table had changed. The Breaking Fields were gone, replaced by
the terrain for the Final: The Solar amphitheater. A flat, polished disc
of white stone with no cover, no mud, and nowhere to hide.

"You don't want to win?" Tim asked, stopping mid-chew on a piece of
dried beef.

Grandmaster Valdis stood by the window, looking out at the sleeping
campus. He didn't turn around.

"No," Valdis said, his voice tight. "I said I don't care about winning.
I don't want anyone getting hurt!"

Tim lowered the beef. He sat in silence for a long time. He studied the
back of the Iron Dean. He saw the tension in the shoulders, the slight
tremor in the hands that gripped the curtain.

"You're afraid of us," Tim stated calmly.

Valdis turned. "I am afraid of what I have unleashed. The Iron Bastion
left on stretchers. The Mystics left in tears. We are no longer a
dueling academy, Tim. We are a wrecking ball."

He walked to the table and stabbed a finger at the map of the
Amphitheater.

"And the High Sanctum... they do not yield. They are zealots. If my
students---*your* students---go into that ring with the same ferocity
they showed in the swamp, and the Sanctum meets them with their blinding
righteousness... it will not be a match. It will be a collision. People
will die."

Valdis sank into a chair, looking suddenly very tired. "I wanted the Cup
to prove I wasn't a failure. But if the cost is a row of coffins... keep
the damn Cup."

Tim looked at the map. A flat circle. No tactical advantage. Just pure
power.

"Do you think we can't win without killing them?" Tim asked. "Tell me
more about our opponents."

Valdis sighed, rubbing his temples.

"The High Sanctum," he began. "They are not mages. They are... vessels.
They believe their Ki comes directly from the Sun God. They call it 'The
Glare'."

"Light magic?" Tim asked.

"Purification," Valdis corrected grimly. "They don't burn you with fire;
they burn away your 'impurity.' If they hit you with the Glare, it
attacks your Core directly. It tries to scour away your Ki."

He looked at Tim seriously. "You have a Copper Core. To them, you are a
walking abomination. An impurity to be smelted down. If you try your
heat-sink trick against the Glare, you won't overheat. You will be
erased. Your Core will shatter."

Tim tapped the table. "So, they are bright. And they are judgmental."

"They are blinding," Valdis said. "When they link up, they turn the
arena into a second sun. You can't target them because you can't see
them. You can't get close because the ambient radiation cooks you. And
you can't outlast them because they draw power from the sky."

Valdis leaned back. "It is the perfect counter to your chaotic, shadowy
tactics. You cannot hide in the dark when they turn on the lights."

Tim chewed his beef thoughtfully.

"So," Tim mused. "We have an enemy that thinks they are pure light. They
stand in the open. They shine so bright it hurts."

"Yes," Valdis said. "It is hopeless."

Tim picked up a silver spoon from the table---one of the few pieces of
fancy cutlery he hadn't managed to avoid. He held it up to the
candlelight. He twisted it, watching the reflection of the flame stretch
and warp.

"Grandmaster," Tim said slowly. "Did you ever burn ants with a
magnifying glass when you were a child?"

Valdis blinked. "I... focused on my studies."

"I did," Tim said. "It taught me something about light."

He spun the spoon.

"Light travels in a straight line. It's arrogant like that. It thinks
nothing can stop it."

He looked at Valdis.

"But if you put something shiny in front of it... it bounces. If you put
something curved in front of it... it bends. And if you put something
dense in front of it... it stops."

Tim stood up, his eyes gleaming with that dangerous, pragmatic
intelligence.

"They want to purify us? Fine."

He tossed the spoon onto the map of the Amphitheater. It landed with a
clatter right in the center.

"The Sanctum fights with the Sun," Tim said. "So we aren't going to
fight them with shadows this time."

He looked at Valdis.

"We're going to fight them with mirrors."

Valdis looked at the spoon. He looked at Tim.

"Mirrors?"

"Refraction, Grandmaster," Tim grinned. "If they shine a light that
bright, they better be careful where we aim the reflection. I think it's
time Christopher learned a new trick with his barriers."

Tim walked to the door.

"Don't worry about casualties, Valdis. Light doesn't bleed. It just...
fades."

Valdis watched him go. He looked at the silver spoon on the map.

"A mirror," Valdis whispered. He let out a breath, the crushing anxiety
lifting just a fraction. It was insane. It was simplistic. It was
peasant logic applied to high magic.

And God help the High Sanctum, it might just work.
