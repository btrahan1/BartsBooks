# Chapter 37: Polish and Plate

"We're going to do *what*?"

Cassian Redgrave stared at Tim from across the staff table in the Great
Kitchens. He looked like he had just been asked to dance a jig in a
tutu.

"We are going to become furniture," Cassian clarified, his voice rising
an octave. "You want the Iron Heads---the elite shock troops of the
Academy---to become... backing?"

"Backing," Tim corrected calmly, taking a bite of a leftover tart.
"Structural support. The wood behind the glass."

The entire "Army" leadership was gathered. The Golden Circle, the Iron
Heads, and the Squad. They were exhausted, bruised, and high on the
victory over the Bastion, but this new plan sounded like madness.

"The High Sanctum uses the Glare," Tim explained, sliding a silver
serving platter across the table. It spun and came to a rest, reflecting
the candlelight perfectly. "It is concentrated, high-frequency Ki. If
you try to block it with a standard shield, it burns through. If you try
to absorb it like I did with the lightning, it cooks you."

"So we die," Christopher squeaked, adjusting his glasses. "Statistically
speaking, against a Solar Class enemy, survival probability is low."

"Light travels in a straight line," Lyssandra cut in, her eyes locked on
the silver platter. She looked up at Tim, her mind racing. "Albedo.
Reflectivity."

"Exactly," Tim pointed. "We don't block. We reflect."

He stood up and picked up the platter. He held it in front of his chest.

"To make a mirror, you need two things. You need something heavy and
dark to stop the light from passing through. That's the Iron Heads.
That's Gareth. That's Cassian."

Cassian grunted, slightly mollified by being called "heavy and dark."

"But iron isn't shiny," Tim continued. "Iron absorbs heat. If we just
use Iron shields, you'll bake in your armor."

He tapped the surface of the platter.

"You need a coating. Something smooth. Something that creates zero
friction. Something that tells the light: *Go somewhere else.*"

He pointed to Christopher, Mara, and the Draft Zone mages---the students
with low power but high control. The ones who had been told their whole
lives they were too weak to fight.

"You," Tim said. "You are the Polish."

Silence.

"The Polish?" Mara asked, insulted.

"The Surface," Tim corrected. "The Barrier. The Iron Heads will provide
the dense wall of Ki to stop the blast. But *you*... you will layer a
thin, perfectly flat barrier right on top of their Iron. You won't try
to stop the power. You will just give it a slippery surface to bounce
off of."

"We have to stand *in front* of the Iron Heads?" Christopher whispered.
"Between them and the Sun?"

"Yes," Tim said.

"That's suicide," Beatrice Sterling said from the Golden Circle. "If the
polish cracks, the iron heats up, and everyone burns."

"Then don't crack," Tim said simply.

He looked around the room.

"The Sanctum fights with judgment. They look at you and see impurities
to be burned away. They expect us to cower in the shadows. They expect
us to be dark."

Tim held the silver platter up so it caught the light of the hearth,
blinding Cassian for a moment.

"Imagine their surprise when they look at us... and all they see is
themselves."

Tim lowered the platter.

"It requires absolute trust. The Iron Heads have to trust the Mages to
keep the heat off them. The Mages have to trust the Iron Heads to hold
them up so they don't shatter."

He looked at Gareth. "Gareth, stand up."

Gareth rumbled to his feet.

"Christopher, stand in front of him."

Christopher hesitated, then stepped in front of the giant. He looked
ridiculous---a twig standing in front of a boulder.

"Gareth," Tim ordered. "Maximum density. Be the mountain."

Gareth breathed. The heavy, grey Iron Ki flooded his aura. He became a
wall.

"Christopher," Tim said softy. "The Perfect Line. Don't fight the air.
Coat the mountain. Make it shiny."

Christopher closed his eyes. He remembered the test with the crystal
orb. *Draw the line.* He extended his Ki, not as a bolt, but as a film.
A thin, shimmering layer of blue light wrapped around Gareth's grey
aura.

"Beatrice," Tim said. "Throw a fireball. Small one."

"Are you insane?" Beatrice asked.

"Do it."

Beatrice shrugged. She snapped her fingers, and a baseball-sized sphere
of fire shot toward Christopher.

Christopher flinched, but Gareth's hands came up, resting gently on
Christopher's shoulders, holding him steady. *I have you,* the grip
said.

The fire hit the blue film.

It didn't explode. It didn't burn.

It *bounced*.

The fireball ricocheted off the smooth, frictionless barrier at a
forty-five-degree angle and slammed into a sack of potatoes in the
corner, exploding in a shower of baked spuds.

Christopher opened his eyes, patting his chest. "I'm... I'm not on
fire."

Gareth looked at the potatoes. "Warm," he rumbled approvingly.

Tim looked at the stunned room.

"See?" Tim smiled, picking up a potato that had rolled to his feet.
"Physics. Now, we have twelve hours to learn how to do that with fifty
people against the power of the sun."

Cassian stood up, looking at the "Polish" squad with new respect.

"Well," Cassian said, drawing his sword. "I suppose being the furniture
isn't so bad. As long as the furniture is lethal."

"Lethal furniture," Tim agreed. "That's the plan."
