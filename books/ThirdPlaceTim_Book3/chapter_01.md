# Chapter 1: The Bell of Ash

Peace lasted exactly three days.

They were three very good days. The Academy was in a state of euphoric
hangover. The "Just Tim Fund" had sponsored a campus-wide feast that
featured actual roast beef instead of savory gruel. The Iron Heads and
the Golden Circle were seen sitting at the same tables, comparing
bruises and bragging about who had held the line longest against the
High Sanctum. Even Cassian Redgrave had been spotted laughing with a
scholarship student from the Draft Zone.

It was a golden age. And like all golden ages, it was fragile.

Tim sat in his new quarters---a suite of rooms in the Faculty Wing that
came with the title of "Student Adjutant." He was organizing his desk.
He had three stacks of parchment: *Requests for Tutoring*, *Investment
Returns*, and *Supply Requisitions*.

He was just dipping his quill to approve a request for more winter
blankets when the bell rang.

It wasn't the morning bell, which was bright and brassy. It wasn't the
class bell, which was sharp and demanding.

This bell was deep. It was made of black iron, and it hadn't rung in
living memory. It vibrated through the stone floor, shaking the ink in
Tim's well. *DOOOM. DOOOM. DOOOM.*

The sound was heavy. It tasted like smoke.

Tim didn't finish the signature. He set the quill down. He didn't know
the specific code for the bell, but he knew the frequency of emergency.

He grabbed his grey wool coat---he still refused the velvet---and the
silver Marshal's band that sat on his desk. He strapped it on. It felt
colder than usual.

He didn't run to the dorms. He ran to the High Spire.

The Strategy Room had been stripped. The comfortable chairs were gone.
The tapestries depicting glorious tournaments had been rolled up,
revealing bare, cold stone walls covered in maps that looked far less
friendly.

Grandmaster Valdis stood at the head of the table. He was no longer
wearing the robes of a Dean. He was wearing a breastplate of dull,
scarred steel over a combat tunic.

Master Kincaid stood by the window, sharpening a sword that looked like
it had seen actual blood, not just sawdust.

"Close the door," Valdis commanded as Tim entered.

Tim closed it. The room was silent, save for the echo of the iron bell
still humming in the stones.

"What happened?" Tim asked. "Is the King dead?"

"Worse," Valdis said. He didn't look at Tim; he looked at the map spread
across the table. It wasn't a map of the Academy grounds. It was a map
of the Continent. "The King has invoked the Accord of Ash."

Tim walked to the table. He scanned the map. His geography lessons with
Lyssandra had paid off. He saw the Kingdom of Aethelgard in blue. To the
east, the vast, jagged red expanse of the Iron-Blood Empire. And between
them, a small, dark smear that looked like a burn mark on the parchment.

"The Shadow-Scar," Tim read.

"A pocket realm," Valdis explained grimly. "A remnant of the God-Wars.
It is a wasteland of obsidian and storms. But three days ago, our scouts
detected a resonance signature deep within the Scar."

Valdis placed a jagged, glowing orange crystal on the table. It pulsed
with a heat that Tim could feel from three feet away.

"Sun-Stone," Tim whispered. "I thought it was a myth."

"It is real," Valdis said. "And it is the most potent Ki amplifier in
existence. A single mine could power the capital for a century. Or... it
could power an army that could conquer the world in a month."

"And the Empire wants it," Tim surmised.

"They have already claimed it," Kincaid rumbled from the window. "They
have moved a legion to the eastern Gate of the Scar."

Tim did the math. Two superpowers. One resource. A neutral zone.

"So we go to war," Tim said.

"Not a war," Valdis corrected. "A Trial. The Accord of Ash forbids open
warfare between the nations to preserve the land. Instead, we settle
disputes by proxy."

Valdis looked up, his eyes hard.

"We send the children."

Tim felt a cold chill that had nothing to do with the drafty room. "The
Trial of Sovereignty. I read about it in the Northern Archive.
Under-twenties only. A sacrifice of the future to secure the present."

"Correct," Valdis said. "The King prepares the Royal Guard Cadets. They
are strong. They are loyal. And against the Iron-Blood Empire, they will
be slaughtered."

Valdis slammed his fist onto the map.

"The Empire does not fight like us, Tim. They do not have duelists. They
have the Hive. They graft beast-parts to their bodies. They suppress
their minds with alchemy. They are not soldiers; they are biological
weapons."

Valdis looked at Tim.

"The King wants to send Iron against them. But you and I know what
happens when Iron hits a wall it cannot break. It shatters."

"You want to send the Alloy," Tim realized.

"I have convinced the Crown," Valdis said, "to allow a joint task force.
The Royal Guard will form the vanguard. But the Academy... the Academy
will form the spine."

Valdis reached into a box on the table and pulled out a new insignia. It
wasn't the silver hawk of the Academy. It was a golden shield with a
copper lightning bolt running through it.

"Congratulations," Valdis said, his voice devoid of joy. "You are no
longer a Student Adjutant. You are now a Cadet Commander."

Tim looked at the insignia. It wasn't a prize. It was a burden.

"Classes are suspended effective immediately," Valdis continued, his
voice shifting into the clipped tones of a General. "We enter
Accelerated Mode. The Refectory becomes a mess hall. The dorms become
barracks. Physics, History, Etiquette... gone. From this moment on, we
teach only one subject."

"Survival?" Tim asked.

"Victory," Valdis corrected. "Survival is optional. Victory is
mandatory."

Tim picked up the golden shield insignia. He felt the weight of it. He
thought of Gareth eating apples. He thought of Christopher's nervous
smile. He thought of Lyssandra warming her hands on a cup of tea.

He was being asked to take them into a wasteland to fight monsters over
a rock.

"And Elara?" Tim asked. "She is in the High Spire."

"She returns tomorrow," Valdis said. "She has completed the Rite of the
Frozen Mind. She will be your Co-Commander."

Tim nodded. He clipped the insignia onto his coat.

"How long do we have?" Tim asked.

"The Portal opens in two weeks," Valdis said. "In two weeks, you must
turn a school into an army."

Tim turned to the door. The shock was fading, replaced by the mechanical
whirring of his mind. He was calculating rations. He was calculating
supply lines. He was calculating how many blankets they would need for a
realm of shadow and storms.

"Tim," Valdis called out.

Tim stopped, hand on the latch.

"This isn't a tournament," Valdis said softly. "There are no referees.
There is no yielding. If you meet the Iron-Blood Empire... do not try to
fix them."

Tim stood there for a moment. He touched the Copper Core in his gut. It
was warm. It was alive.

"I'm a mechanic, General," Tim said, not looking back. "I fix what can
be fixed. And what can't be fixed..."

He opened the door, the sound of the iron bell still ringing in the
distance.

"...I dismantle."
