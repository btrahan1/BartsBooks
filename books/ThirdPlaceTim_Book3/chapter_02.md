# Chapter 2: The Frost and the Furnace

The courtyard of the Royal Academy had lost its charm. The manicured
hedges were gone, dug up to make room for weapon racks. The fountain,
where Lyssandra had once shivered, was now a trough for cooling heated
steel. The air smelled of wet whetstones and nervous sweat.

Tim stood by the main gate, a clipboard in hand, overseeing the arrival
of grain shipments. Gareth stood beside him, effortlessly lifting
barrels that two grown men were struggling to budge.

"That's the last of the wheat," Gareth rumbled. "We have enough bread
for three weeks. If we ration."

"We'll need six," Tim murmured, marking the sheet. "War makes people
hungry, Gareth. Anxiety burns calories faster than labor."

A hush fell over the workers near the gate. The temperature in the
courtyard plummeted ten degrees in a second. Breath turned to steam.

A carriage rolled through the archway. It wasn't the open-topped
transport of the student body. It was a sealed, black-lacquered coach
from the High Spire, its wheels rimmed with rime ice that cracked as it
moved. The horses breathing out thick, white clouds looked nervous.

The carriage stopped. The door didn't open immediately.

"She's back," Gareth said, taking a step back. "And she feels... sharp."

The door swung open.

Lady Elara Vane stepped out.

Tim stopped breathing for a second. He remembered the girl who had
shivered in the drafty hall. He remembered the partner who had smirked
at him from the balcony.

This was neither.

Elara wore the white and silver robes of a High Mage, fitted with combat
leathers. Her hair, usually loose or in a simple braid, was pulled back
into a severe, intricate knot held by pins of pale sapphire. Her skin
was porcelain pale, and her eyes---usually a warm, intelligent
hazel---seemed lighter, almost grey.

She didn't look around with curiosity. She scanned the courtyard with
the clinical detachment of a surgeon entering an operating theater.

The students nearby parted like the Red Sea. They sensed the danger.
This wasn't just a noble; this was a weapon that had just been
unsheathed.

Tim handed his clipboard to Gareth. He walked forward.

Elara's gaze snapped to him. For a moment, her expression was blank,
terrifyingly serene. Then, as she locked eyes with Tim, the frost
cracked. A tiny, almost imperceptible warmth returned to her gaze.

"You changed the drapes," Elara said, her voice cool and clear as a
mountain stream. She gestured to the weapon racks.

"Valdis insisted on a more 'utilitarian' aesthetic," Tim replied,
stopping three feet from her. He could feel the cold radiating off her.
It wasn't just the weather; her Ki was actively pulling heat from the
air.

"And you," she said, looking at the golden shield insignia on his chest.
"Cadet Commander. It suits you. Though I miss the wool."

"I'm still wearing wool underneath," Tim admitted. "Old habits."

He looked at her. Really looked at her.

"The Rite of the Frozen Mind?" Tim asked softly.

"Complete," Elara nodded. She held up her hand. A snowflake formed in
her palm, perfect and unmelting, hovering an inch above her skin. "I
learned to organize my mind, Tim. To archive emotions so they don't
interfere with the casting. It... is quiet in my head now."

"Too quiet?" Tim asked, searching for his friend inside the mage.

Elara closed her hand, crushing the snowflake. The ice vanished into
mist.

"Quiet enough to do what needs to be done," she said. She stepped
closer, lowering her voice. "I heard the bell, Tim. I know about the
Shadow-Scar. I know about the Empire."

She looked around at the frightened students.

"You are going to try to save them all," she stated. It wasn't a
question.

"That's the job," Tim said.

"No," Elara corrected. "That's *your* job. You are the builder. You are
the mechanic. You keep the engine running."

She leaned in, and the chill of her presence washed over him,
contrasting sharply with the hum of his Copper Core.

"But someone has to be the one who decides what parts are expendable,"
she whispered. "Someone has to be the ice that stops the bleeding. That
is *my* job now."

Tim felt a pang of sadness, but also relief. She wasn't gone. She had
just hardened herself, deliberately, to protect him from the choices she
knew would break his heart.

"We are a pair, then," Tim said. "The Furnace and the Glacier."

Elara's lips quirked into a ghost of her old smirk.

"Fire and Ice is a cliché, Tim. Let's stick to Physics. You are the
Energy. I am the Entropy."

She turned to face the courtyard, her posture rigid and regal.

"Now," she commanded, her voice projecting with icy authority. "Where is
this Prince Aric I've heard about? I believe I need to explain the chain
of command to him before he ruins my afternoon."

Tim grinned. The ice was new, but the attitude was all Elara.

"He's in the War Room," Tim said, falling into step beside her.
"Complaining about the lack of silk sheets."

"Excellent," Elara said, creating a small, swirling wind of frost around
her hand. "I find that shivering tends to improve one's listening
skills."

As they walked together toward the High Spire---the warmth of the copper
boy and the chill of the ice girl---the students watched in awe. They
looked less like students and more like myths walking.

Gareth watched them go, picking up the clipboard Tim had left behind.

"Entropy," Gareth rumbled, tasting the word. He looked at the heavy
barrels of wheat. "I think I prefer mortar."
