# Chapter 3: The Peacock in Plate

The War Room had been reorganized.

Tim's carefully stacked logistics reports---*Grain Reserves*, *Winter
Clothing*, *Medical Supplies*---had been swept onto the floor. In their
place, sitting atop the tactical map of the Shadow-Scar, sat a silver
platter of roast pheasant, a crystal decanter of wine, and a pair of
boots made of white dragon-leather.

The boots were attached to the feet of Prince Aric of the House of
Aethelgard.

The Prince was a specimen of genetic perfection. He had hair like spun
gold, a jawline sharp enough to cut glass, and eyes the color of a
summer sky. He wore armor that was less "protective gear" and more
"jewelry"---gilded plate engraved with lions, lined with silk, and
polished to a mirror sheen.

He was currently using a priceless, three-hundred-year-old obsidian
dagger as a toothpick.

"It smells," Aric announced to the room, not looking at anyone in
particular. "Like wet dog and cabbage. Is this the Academy or a kennel?"

Around the table stood his lieutenants---the Royal Guard Cadets. They
were taller, broader, and infinitely shinier than any Academy student.
They laughed, a synchronized, sycophantic sound.

"It's the peasantry, Your Highness," one guard sneered. "They soak into
the stonework."

Tim walked into the room, followed closely by Elara. He stopped at the
door, looking at his scattered papers on the floor. He didn't look
angry. He looked tired.

"Your Highness," Tim said, his voice level. "You are eating pheasant on
a tactical map of a hostile dimension."

Aric didn't look up. He inspected the tip of the dagger. "And you are
interrupting my digestion. Who let the quartermaster in?"

"He is not the quartermaster," Elara said, stepping into the room. The
temperature dropped five degrees instantly. "He is the Cadet Commander.
And you are sitting on his desk."

Aric finally looked up. He saw Elara, and a slow, appreciative smile
spread across his face. He swung his boots off the table, the spurs
gouging a scratch across the map of the Iron-Blood staging ground.

"Lady Elara," Aric purred, standing up. He moved with the fluid,
arrogant grace of a man who has never been hit. "I heard you had
returned. The Ice Queen of the Spire. Beauty and power. A fitting
consort for a Prince."

He ignored Tim entirely, walking toward Elara.

"We should discuss the formation," Aric said. "My Royal Guard will take
the center. We will charge. A wedge of gold. We will break the enemy
line in the first hour. You can provide... atmospheric support. Make it
look dramatic."

"And the Academy forces?" Tim asked, picking up a report from the floor.

Aric waved a hand dismissively. "Baggage train. Digging latrines.
Holding horses. Whatever it is you people do."

Tim placed the paper on the table. "We don't have horses, Your Highness.
The terrain of the Shadow-Scar is volcanic glass. Horses break their
legs."

Aric turned to Tim, his smile vanishing. "Excuse me?"

"And a charge," Tim continued, "is suicide. The Iron-Blood Empire uses
gravity-traps. If you charge in heavy plate, you will be compressed into
a cube before you reach their front line."

Aric stared at Tim. It was the stare of a man who had never been told
'no' in his entire life.

"Are you lecturing me on warfare, peasant?" Aric asked softly. "I have
been trained by the King's own generals. I have a Gold Core. I am the
Vanguard."

"You are a target," Tim corrected. "Shiny armor reflects light. In the
Shadow-Scar, that makes you a beacon for every mana-wraith within ten
miles."

Aric stepped closer to Tim. He was three inches taller. He flared his
Ki.

It wasn't subtle. It was a Gold Core---the rarest and most socially
aggressive affinity. It projected a physical aura of command, a heavy,
radiant pressure meant to force subordinates to their knees. It felt
like standing next to a blast furnace.

"I am Prince Aric," he whispered, the gold light shimmering around him.
"The Sun does not ask the mud for permission to shine. I command this
expedition. You will gather your little school friends, and you will
carry my supplies. If you speak to me again without permission, I will
have you flogged for insubordination."

The Royal Guards smirked, hands on their hilts. They waited for the
peasant to crumble. They waited for the heat to crush him.

Tim stood there. He felt the Gold Ki washing over him. It was hot.
Intense. Arrogant.

Inhale. Accept.

Tim didn't fight it. He didn't bow. He grounded it.

He opened his meridians and let Aric's "Royal Presence" flow into his
chest, down his spine, and into the floor. He acted as a lightning rod.

"You're running hot," Tim observed calmly, as if diagnosing a faulty
boiler. "Inefficient. You're wasting about thirty percent of your output
on theatrics."

Aric's eyes went wide. The pressure should have flattened the boy.
Instead, the boy was critiquing his fuel economy.

"You insolent---" Aric raised a hand, gathering a ball of searing golden
light.

SNAP.

The sound was sharp, like a tree branch breaking in deep winter.

Aric gasped. The golden light in his hand sputtered and died. Frost
began to creep up his gilded vambrace.

Elara stood ten feet away. She hadn't moved. She had simply looked at
him.

"Your Highness," Elara said, her voice dropping the room's temperature
to freezing. "You are threatening the Commander of the Allied Forces."

"He is a nobody!" Aric shouted, trying to shake the ice from his arm.
"He is a copper-blooded mistake!"

"He is the Architect of the Victory at the Breaking Fields," Elara said,
stepping closer. "He defeated the Iron Bastion while you were playing
polo at the Palace."

She stopped between Tim and Aric.

"And he is right. Your armor is a liability. Your strategy is archaic.
And your attitude..."

Elara reached out and plucked the obsidian dagger from Aric's belt. The
frost on his arm prevented him from stopping her.

"...is a security risk."

She tossed the dagger to Tim. Tim caught it.

"We leave in ten days," Elara stated. "Until then, you will read the
dossier Tim prepared. You will learn the terrain. You will learn the
enemy."

Aric looked at his frozen arm. He looked at Tim, who was calmly
smoothing out the wrinkled map. He looked at Elara, whose eyes held a
threat far more dangerous than a flogging.

He realized, with a jolt of shock, that his title meant absolutely
nothing in this room.

"This is treason," Aric hissed, retreating a step as the cold seeped
into his bones. "When my uncle hears of this..."

"Your uncle wants to win," Tim said, not looking up from the map. "If
you want to tell him you failed because you wouldn't listen to the
'baggage train,' be my guest."

Tim looked up.

"But if you want to be the Hero of the Shadow-Scar... if you want the
bards to sing about how the Golden Prince conquered the darkness... then
sit down, shut up, and eat your pheasant."

Aric stared at them. The Copper Mechanic and the Ice Queen.

He was furious. He was humiliated.

But he was also a glory hound. And Tim had just dangled the ultimate
carrot.

Aric snatched his arm back, shattering the ice. He glared at Tim.

"Fine," Aric spat. "I will read your little papers. But when we cross
the portal... when the fighting starts... do not get in my way."

"I wouldn't dream of it," Tim said. "I'll be behind you."

"Good," Aric sneered. "Where you belong."

"No," Tim said softly, marking a supply route on the map. "Where I can
make sure you don't get stabbed in the back."

Aric stormed out of the room, his guards scrambling to follow, leaving
the pheasant uneaten.

Tim let out a long breath, leaning against the table. The "Royal
Presence" had been heavy. His knees shook slightly.

"He's going to be a problem," Tim said.

"He's a peacock," Elara agreed, picking up the wine decanter and
inspecting the vintage. "But peacocks are loud. They make excellent
distractions."

She poured two glasses of the Prince's expensive wine. She handed one to
Tim.

"To the Vanguard," Elara said dryly.

Tim took the glass. "To the baggage train," he replied. "Because that's
where the food is."
