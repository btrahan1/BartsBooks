# Chapter 4: The Magnetic Dance

The integration drill was a disaster before it even began.

The Academy courtyard was divided by an invisible wall of class warfare.
On the left, the Academy students stood in their mixed squads---Gareth
next to Milo, Cassian next to a healer from the Draft Zone. They stood
at ease, disciplined and quiet.

On the right, the Royal Guard stood in a cluster of gold and crimson.
They were laughing, ignoring the formation markers Tim had chalked on
the ground.

Prince Aric sat on a crate---the only seat available---polishing the
pommel of his sword with a silk handkerchief.

"Load up!" Gareth rumbled, trying to get the Guards to pick up their
assigned supply packs.

"Touch that pack, giant, and I'll take your hand off," a Royal Guard
lieutenant sneered. "We are the King's Vanguard. We carry steel, not
sacks."

Tim walked into the center of the deadlock. He held a stopwatch.

"Your Highness," Tim said. "Your men are delaying the logistics drill."

Aric didn't look up from his sword. "My men are conserving their energy
for actual combat, Commander. Why don't you have your little copper ants
carry the load? That is what they are bred for, isn't it?"

A ripple of anger went through the Academy side. Cassian's hand drifted
to his sword hilt. Gareth cracked his knuckles.

Tim raised a hand, signaling his troops to stand down. He looked at
Aric. He saw the boredom, the arrogance, and the absolute refusal to
accept a hierarchy where he wasn't at the top.

Logic wouldn't work. Rank wouldn't work. The Prince needed to lose
something he valued more than his title: his belief in his own physical
superiority.

"You don't think I'm fit to command you," Tim stated.

Aric snorted. "You are a peasant who got lucky in a mud pit. You have no
blood. You have no speed. You have no right to stand in front of me."

"Fine," Tim said. He tossed the stopwatch to Elara, who was standing on
the periphery, arms crossed, watching with icy interest.

Tim unbuckled his sword belt---the one holding the borrowed
gladius---and let it drop to the cobblestones. He shrugged off his heavy
coat, standing in just his grey tunic and trousers.

"A wager," Tim said.

Aric looked up, intrigued. "I'm listening."

"You want command?" Tim asked. "It's yours. Along with my resignation. I
will carry your bags personally to the Shadow-Scar."

Aric stood up, smiling. "And the terms?"

"One minute," Tim said, stepping into the open space between the two
armies. He held his hands out, palms open. "If you can touch me---just
once, a hand, a finger, the tip of your boot---within sixty seconds, you
win."

The Royal Guards laughed. Aric was a Gold Core duelist. He was renowned
for his speed. He was the "Golden Flash" of the capital tournaments.

Aric stepped forward, drawing himself up to his full height. "You are
unarmed. You are unarmored. And you are slow."

"Then it should be easy," Tim said. "Elara? Count it."

"One minute," Elara said, her thumb hovering over the stopwatch button.
"Begin."

Aric moved instantly. He didn't telegraph. He exploded forward in a blur
of gold motion, his hand reaching out to grab Tim's tunic and throw him
to the ground.

It was fast. Faster than Cassian. Faster than Verrick.

But to Tim, it was loud.

Tim's Copper Core spun. The *Induction Field*---his new
perception---flared to life. He didn't see Aric with his eyes. He felt
him. He felt the massive amount of conductive gold filigree on Aric's
armor. He felt the steel of his sword. He felt the magnetic disturbance
of Aric's body moving through the air.

Tim didn't dodge. He simply... was not there.

He pivoted on his heel, a movement of less than three inches. Aric's
hand snatched empty air where Tim's chest had been a fraction of a
second before.

Aric stumbled, recovered, and spun, lashing out with a backhand.

Tim ducked. Not a scramble, but a smooth dip of the knees. Aric's fist
passed over his hair, disturbing the strands but making no contact.

"Stand still!" Aric snarled, his face flushing.

"Why?" Tim asked calmly, stepping sideways as Aric kicked at his legs.
"You're the Vanguard. Catch me."

Aric flared his Ki. The Gold aura erupted, blindingly bright. He
channeled speed, moving faster than the eye could track. He became a
streak of light, a flurry of strikes---jabs, grabs, sweeps.

Tim closed his eyes.

The visual light was distracting. The magnetic sense was absolute.

He felt the metal rushing toward his left shoulder. He shifted right. He
felt the gold-plated boot aiming for his knee. He lifted his leg. He
felt the rush of air from a desperate lunge. He turned his torso like a
revolving door.

To the onlookers, it looked like a choreographed dance. Aric was working
furiously, sweating, grunting, tearing up the cobblestones. Tim looked
like he was taking a leisure stroll in a hurricane. He flowed around the
violence, using Aric's own momentum to create distance.

"Thirty seconds," Elara called out, her voice cutting through the
silence of the stunned Royal Guard.

"Stop running!" Aric screamed. He abandoned dignity. He lunged with both
arms, trying to tackle Tim.

Tim felt the mass coming. Aric was committed. He was a projectile.

Tim planted his feet. He waited until the last possible millisecond.
Then, he activated his Core. *Push.*

He created a sudden, short-range magnetic repulsion field. It wasn't
strong enough to throw Aric, but it was strong enough to shove against
his chest plate.

Aric felt like he'd hit a wall of compressed air. He stopped dead,
inches from Tim's chest. His momentum arrested, he flailed.

Tim stepped casually to the side.

Gravity took over. Aric crashed face-first onto the stones at Tim's
feet.

"You're telegraphing," Tim said gently, looking down at the Prince.
"Your armor is heavy on the right side. You lean before you strike."

Aric scrambled up, his pristine armor scuffed, his face purple with
rage. He roared, gathering a massive ball of Gold Ki in his hand. He was
going to blast the peasant.

"Do it," Tim whispered, his eyes opening to reveal the molten copper
glow. "Release that energy. And I will ground it through your own
boots."

Aric froze. He remembered the reports of the breaking field. He
remembered the boy who ate lightning. If he fired this blast, he would
cook himself.

"Ten seconds," Elara noted, looking at her nails.

Aric stood there, panting, the energy ball sputtering in his hand. He
couldn't hit him physically. He couldn't blast him magically. He was
checkmated.

"Three. Two. One." Elara clicked the watch. "Time."

Tim exhaled, the copper glow fading from his eyes. He picked up his coat
and dusted it off.

"You are fast, Your Highness," Tim said, offering a face-saving olive
branch. "Faster than anyone I've fought. But speed without perception is
just running into walls."

The Royal Guard was silent. Their Prince, the Golden Flash, hadn't laid
a finger on the boy.

Tim turned to the Guard.

"The supply packs are heavy," Tim addressed the lieutenants. "But in the
Shadow-Scar, the gravity wells make them heavier. If you don't practice
carrying them now, you will drop them when the enemy charges. And if you
drop the supplies, we starve."

He looked back at Aric.

"I don't need you to carry bags to humiliate you, Prince Aric. I need
you to carry them so you're strong enough to kill the things that try to
take them from us."

Aric stared at Tim. He breathed heavily, his ego bruised, his worldview
cracked. But he was a warrior. And he knew when he had been bested.

Slowly, Aric walked over to the pile of supplies. He picked up a heavy
crate of rations. He hoisted it onto his gilded shoulder.

He looked at his lieutenants.

"Well?" Aric barked. "Pick them up! Or do you want the copper ants to
show you how?"

The Royal Guard scrambled, grabbing barrels and crates.

Tim clipped his sword belt back on. He caught Elara's eye. She gave him
a tiny nod of approval.

"Magnetic repulsion?" she whispered as he passed.

"Induction," Tim corrected with a grin. "I just followed the metal."

He looked at his unified, if grumpy, army.

"Right," Tim called out. "Two laps around the perimeter. Then we discuss
formation. Move out!"
