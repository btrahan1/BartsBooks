# Chapter 5: The Price of Pride

The sun had set, and the Academy barracks were quiet, save for the
rhythmic sharpening of blades. Prince Aric's quarters---the Officer's
Suite commandeered from a senior instructor---smelled of expensive
polish and bruised ego.

Aric sat in a high-backed chair, staring at his unblemished reflection
in a silver mirror. He was replaying the sixty seconds in the courtyard
over and over. The missed strikes. The impossible evasions. The
humiliating shove.

There was a knock at the door. Not a servant's scratch, but a firm,
decisive rap.

"Go away," Aric barked. "I am not receiving."

The door opened anyway.

Tim walked in, holding a thick, leather-bound dossier. Elara followed,
closing the door and sealing it with a whisper of locking frost.

Aric surged to his feet, his hand going to his sword. "I said I am not
receiving! Have you come to gloat, Peasant? Do you want me to polish
your boots now?"

Tim walked to the heavy oak table in the center of the room and set the
dossier down. He looked at the Prince, his expression devoid of triumph.
It was the same look he gave a broken wheel or a clogged vent.

"Your Highness," Tim said softly. "I am not here to challenge you. I am
not here to 'show you up'."

Aric sneered, though his hand drifted away from his sword. "You have a
strange way of showing it. You made me look like a fool in front of my
own Guard."

"I made you look human," Tim corrected. "And I did it here, on safe
ground, with wooden swords and gentle shoves."

Tim tapped the leather dossier.

"Because where we are going, the enemy won't shove you, Aric. They will
rip your arm off and beat you to death with it."

Aric stiffened at the use of his given name, but the gravity in Tim's
voice stopped his retort.

"We have one goal," Tim continued, stepping closer. "To win. If we don't
win, the outcome will be catastrophic, not just to us, but to our
nation. The Iron-Blood Empire isn't looking for a border skirmish. They
want the Sun-Stone. If they get it, they will have the power to burn
Aethelgard to the ground within a year."

Aric looked at the dossier. "My uncle's generals say the Empire is
undisciplined. Savages."

"Your uncle's generals are fighting the last war," Tim said. "I have
done the research. We can't win. At least, we can't win the 'normal
way'."

Tim flipped the dossier open. It wasn't filled with Academy notes. It
was filled with sketches and reports Tim had compiled from the Northern
Archive's restricted section and intelligence bought from smugglers in
the city.

He pointed to a sketch of an Iron-Blood soldier. The man in the drawing
didn't have a normal arm. His right limb was a twisted, alchemical
fusion of flesh and jagged, black metal claws.

"They graft Ki-conductive beasts parts to their own bodies," Tim
explained grimly. "They remove the parts of the brain that process fear.
They don't duel, Aric. They swarm. They don't care about honor. They
care about biomass."

Aric stared at the drawing. He felt a cold knot form in his stomach. He
had trained to fight knights. He had not trained to fight *this*.

"If you charge them in a Golden Wedge," Elara added, her voice sharp as
glass, "they will not break. They will latch onto your armor, drag you
down, and pry you open like a clam. Your Gold Core makes you a beacon.
You will be the first one they kill."

Aric looked up, his face pale. "So we are doomed?"

"No," Tim said. "We are only doomed if we fight like knights."

Tim looked the Prince in the eye. He saw the vanity, yes. But he also
saw the genuine horror at the thought of his kingdom burning.

"I need you, Aric," Tim said. "I can organize the logistics. Elara can
manage the magic. But I cannot inspire the Royal Guard. They won't die
for a peasant. They will only die for a Prince."

Tim leaned in, asking the question that would decide the fate of the
campaign.

"Do you love your country more than you love yourself?"

The room went silent. Aric looked at the mirror---at the image of the
Golden Prince, the invincible duelist. Then he looked at the sketch of
the monstrous soldier.

He realized that the boy in the mirror was already dead. The only
question was whether the man standing in the room would survive.

Aric took a deep breath. He poured a glass of wine. He didn't drink it.
He pushed it across the table to Tim.

"What do you need me to do?" Aric asked quietly.

"I need you to stop being the Vanguard," Tim said, taking the glass.
"And start being the Bait."

Aric blinked. "The Bait?"

"You shine brighter than anyone," Tim said, a small smile forming. "The
enemy will want you. They will come for you. And when they do... we will
be waiting in the dark."

Aric processed this. It wasn't the heroic charge he had dreamed of. It
was dangerous. It was terrifying.

But looking at the dossier, and looking at the calm certainty in the
peasant commander's eyes, Aric realized it was the only way.

"Very well," Aric said, straightening his spine. "I will be your lure,
Commander." He offered a stiff, formal hand. "But if I die... I will
haunt you."

Tim shook his hand. "If you die, Your Highness, I'll be right there next
to you to hear the complaints."

Tim turned to leave, Elara opening the door.

"Tim," Aric called out.

Tim paused.

"Does the... does the graft really remove fear?"

"That's what the reports say," Tim said.

Aric looked at his trembling hand. "Then they have one advantage over me
already."

Tim looked back, his expression serious. "Fear keeps you alive, Aric.
Don't lose it. Use it."

The door clicked shut. Aric looked at the dossier. He picked it up and
began to read. For the first time in his life, the Golden Prince was
studying.
