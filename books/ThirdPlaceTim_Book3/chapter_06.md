# Chapter 6: The Alchemy of Fear

The morning mist clung to the training grounds, cold and damp. The
assembled force was a study in contrast. On one side, the Academy
students in their grey wool and leather, moving with the nervous energy
of civilians turned soldiers. On the other, the Royal Guard in their
gold and crimson, standing with the rigid, statue-like stillness of
professionals who believed they were invincible.

They stood separated by a ten-foot gap of cobblestones. A gap of class,
of training, and of trust.

Tim stood on a raised wooden platform---a repurposed supply pallet. He
didn't wear armor. He wore his simple grey tunic and the silver
Marshal's band. He looked small against the backdrop of the High Spire.

He let the silence stretch. He let the wind whistle through the weapon
racks.

"We have two weeks," Tim began, his voice carrying clearly without a
shout.

He looked at the faces. The terrified first-years. The stoic seniors.
The sneering Guardsmen.

"It is not long enough," Tim stated bluntly. "Two months would not be
long enough. Two years might not be."

A murmur of unease rippled through the Royal Guard. Their officers
usually spoke of glory, of swift victories and inevitable triumph. This
boy was speaking of math.

"We are going into a wasteland," Tim continued. "To fight an enemy that
has surgically removed their own humanity to become better killers. They
are stronger than us. They are more numerous. And they have had years to
prepare."

He paused, letting the weight of the reality settle on them like a heavy
cloak.

"We can't win. At least, that's what many think, especially our enemy."

Tim stepped to the edge of the platform.

"They look at us and they see children. They see duelists. They see
peacocks in gold plate." He glanced momentarily at Prince Aric, who
stood at the front of his column, jaw set tight. "They are laughing at
us."

Tim's eyes hardened.

"We will use that against them!"

He paced the length of the pallet.

"They expect us to break. They expect us to panic when the blood starts
to flow. Because they think panic is weakness."

Tim stopped. He held out his right hand. He held it perfectly still for
a moment, and then, he let it relax.

It trembled.

It wasn't a violent shake, but a fine, rapid vibration. The kind of
tremor that comes when the adrenaline hits the bloodstream and has
nowhere to go.

"Look at this," Tim said, holding the shaking hand up for all to see.
"Some of you might think that I am not afraid. Fearless. The 'Copper
Commander' who eats lightning."

Tim scoffed.

"This is nonsense. I am terrified!"

The Royal Guard exchanged glances. A commander admitting fear? It was
disgraceful. It was... honest.

"My heart is hammering against my ribs like a trapped bird," Tim
confessed. "My stomach is in knots. My palms are sweating. Every
instinct in my body is screaming at me to run, to hide, to go back to
the kitchen and bake bread."

He lowered his hand, but he didn't hide the tremor.

"The Iron-Blood Empire believes this feeling makes me weak. They cut it
out of their soldiers. They think fear is a defect."

Tim tapped his temple.

"But they are wrong. Fear is not a defect. Fear is *fuel*."

He scanned the crowd.

"What happens when you are scared? Really scared?"

He pointed to Milo. "Milo, what happens?"

"I... I get fast," Milo stammered. "I hear everything."

"Exactly," Tim said. "Your pupils dilate. You see movement you would
miss otherwise. Your blood pumps faster, delivering more oxygen to your
muscles. Your reaction time drops to a fraction of a second. Fear is
your body dumping high-grade nitrous into your engine."

Tim looked at the Royal Guard lieutenant---the one who had sneered at
the supply bags.

"Lieutenant," Tim called out. "You have a dagger on your belt. A
throwing knife."

The Lieutenant frowned. "I do."

"Throw it at me," Tim ordered.

The courtyard went dead silent.

"Sir?" the Lieutenant asked, confused.

"Throw it," Tim said. "Don't aim for the leg. Aim for my chest. Kill
shot."

"I cannot---"

"That is an order!" Tim barked. The command carried the weight of the
Marshal's band.

Tim turned his back to the Lieutenant. He stood facing the Academy
students. He closed his eyes.

"I am standing here," Tim narrated, his voice calm but tight. "I am
blind. I am unarmored. And I know there is a man behind me with a knife
who thinks I am a peasant usurper."

He took a sharp breath.

"I am afraid. And because I am afraid, the *Induction Field* is
screaming."

Tim didn't push his Ki out. He pulled it in. He let the terror sharpen
his senses. He felt the magnetic disturbance of the Lieutenant shifting
his weight. He felt the metallic ping of the knife leaving the sheath.

"Now, let me show you how I turn that into an asset instead of a
handicap!"

Whoosh.

The Lieutenant threw. He didn't hold back. It was a fast, straight throw
aimed between Tim's shoulder blades.

Tim didn't turn around.

He felt the disturbance in the air. He felt the small mass of steel
hurtling toward him. His fear didn't freeze him; it accelerated him. His
Copper Core spun, conducting the signal from his skin to his muscles
faster than thought.

Tim's hand snapped up behind his head.

THWACK.

He didn't catch the blade---that was a parlor trick for fools. He caught
the wrist of the invisible air around the knife, slapping the flat of
the blade with a pulse of magnetic repulsion.

The knife deflected. It spun wildly over his shoulder, embedding itself
into the wooden post of the pallet right in front of his nose.
Quivering.

Tim opened his eyes. He looked at the knife, inches from his face. He
pulled it out of the wood.

He turned around to face the stunned Lieutenant.

"If I had been calm," Tim said quietly, "I would be dead. If I had been
arrogant, I would be dead. I survived because I was scared enough to pay
attention."

He tossed the knife back to the Lieutenant, who caught it with fumbling
fingers.

Tim looked at his army.

"The Iron-Blood soldiers feel nothing. That means they are numb. They
are slow. They are arrogant."

He clenched his fist. The tremor was gone, replaced by steady, vibrant
readiness.

"We are going to be the most terrified army in the history of warfare.
We are going to jump at shadows. We are going to check every corner. We
are going to be so hyper-aware, so flooded with adrenaline, that we will
see them coming before they even know they are attacking."

Tim stepped down from the platform, walking into the gap between the
Guards and the Students.

"Don't try to be brave," Tim said, looking from Aric to Gareth. "Brave
gets you killed. Be scared. Be alert. Be alive."

He looked at Aric.

"Your Highness?"

Aric looked at the boy who had just caught a knife blind. He looked at
his own hands. He felt the fear of the coming war, the fear he had been
trying to suffocate with gold plate and arrogance.

Aric took a deep breath. He let the fear in. And he felt his senses
sharpen.

"I am terrified, Commander," Aric said, his voice clear.

"Good," Tim grinned, the copper light dancing in his eyes. "Then you're
ready to work. First drill: Stealth movement in heavy armor. If I can
hear you clanking, you're dead."
