# Chapter 7: The Zero Point

The sounds of training filled the air---the clatter of wood on shields,
the grunt of exertion, the rhythmic thud of boots. Under Tim's
direction, the integration was working. The Royal Guard was teaching the
students how to hold a shield line; the students were teaching the Guard
how to move without noise.

Tim stood on the sidelines, nodding. It was efficient. It was smart.

In the center ring, Mara---the nervous girl from House Fisher---was
sparring with a Royal Guard trainee. She moved fluidly, using the
"Polish" technique to deflect his heavy strikes. When the Guard
overextended, she slipped inside his guard, tapped his breastplate with
her wooden dagger, and danced back.

"Point!" Mara grinned, breathless. "That's a kill shot."

The Guard grunted, annoyed but accepting. "Fair enough. Again."

They reset. They smiled. It was a game.

CRACK.

The sound was sharp, loud, and sounded like a whip breaking the sound
barrier.

A wall of ice, three inches thick and razor-sharp, erupted from the
ground between Mara and the Guard. Mara yelped, stumbling back as the
frost nipped at her nose.

The training field went silent. The temperature plummeted.

Lady Elara strode into the ring. She didn't walk; she glided, the frost
forming under her boots. She didn't look at the Royal Guard. She looked
at Mara.

"A kill shot?" Elara asked. Her voice was quiet, stripped of all warmth.

Mara swallowed. "I... I tagged his heart, Lady Elara. In a real fight,
he would be dead."

"In a real fight," Elara said, stepping through the dissipating mist of
her ice wall, "he would not have stopped when you tapped him. He would
have grabbed your wrist, broken it, and gutted you while you were
smiling about your 'point'."

Elara turned to the Royal Guard. "Draw your dagger. Real steel."

The Guard hesitated, looking at Tim. Tim took a step forward, his brow
furrowed.

"Elara," Tim warned low in his throat.

"Draw it," Elara commanded, her eyes flashing with a pale, grey light.

The Guard drew his dagger.

"Attack me," Elara said. "Do not stop until I am dead or you are
incapacitated."

"My Lady, I cannot---"

Elara flicked her wrist. A lash of condensed frost water struck the
ground inches from the Guard's boot, shattering the stone.

"Attack!" she screamed.

The Guard flinched, then lunged. He was fast, trained by the best. He
aimed for her shoulder, pulling the blow slightly.

Elara didn't dodge. She didn't use the "Polish" to deflect.

She caught his wrist.

It wasn't a soft catch. It was a vice grip reinforced by a layer of
diamond-hard ice. There was a sickening *crunch* of compression---not
bone breaking, but bruising deep.

The Guard gasped, dropping the knife.

Elara didn't stop. She spun him, kicked the back of his knee to buckle
it, and drove his face into the dirt. She summoned a jagged spike of ice
from the ground. It stopped one millimeter from his eye.

She held him there, pinned, helpless, terrified.

"Dead," Elara said flatly.

She released him. The Guard scrambled back, clutching his wrist, staring
at her in horror.

Elara turned back to Mara, who was trembling.

"You are playing tag," Elara said, her voice projecting across the
silent courtyard. "You are used to tournaments. You rely on the referee.
You rely on the yield."

She walked toward Mara.

"The Iron-Blood Empire does not yield. They do not respect 'points'. If
you tap them, they will laugh as they tear your throat out."

Elara raised her hand. The moisture in the air condensed rapidly,
forming a mannequin made of solid, cloudy ice. It was shaped like an
Iron-Blood soldier---hulking, deformed, with a claw for an arm.

"Mara," Elara commanded. "Destroy it."

Mara looked at the ice statue. She raised her wooden dagger. "But... my
weapon is wood."

"I don't care," Elara said. "Destroy it. Or I will freeze your feet to
the ground and leave you here until sunset."

Mara struck the statue. *Thwack.* The wood bounced off the hard ice.

"Harder," Elara said coldly. "It's coming for you. It wants to kill your
family."

Mara hit it again. Harder.

"Inefficient," Elara critiqued mercilessly. "You are tapping. Strike
through the target. Shatter it."

Mara hit it again, grunting with effort. The wood splintered. Her hand
started to bleed from the shock.

"It's not dying, Mara," Elara whispered, leaning close to the girl's
ear. "It's grabbing you. It's hurting you. End it."

Mara screamed, a sound of frustration and fear, and drove the broken
shard of her wooden dagger into the neck of the ice statue with
everything she had. She pumped her Ki into the strike, not caring about
safety, not caring about form.

CRASH.

The neck of the statue shattered. The head fell off and smashed on the
cobblestones.

Mara stood there, panting, her hand bleeding, looking at the broken ice.
She looked horrified by her own violence.

"Good," Elara said softly. She reached out and touched Mara's bleeding
hand. A cool blue light glowed, and the cut sealed instantly.

Elara turned to face the troops. She looked at Tim.

"Commander Tim teaches you to survive," Elara announced. "He teaches you
to endure. He teaches you that fear is fuel."

She swept her gaze over the assembled students.

"But fuel burns. And endurance runs out."

She clenched her fist, and the remains of the ice statue pulverized into
snow.

"I am here to teach you the cold. War is not a game of tag. It is a
series of decisions where you trade a piece of your soul for your life.
Do not hesitate. Do not apologize. Do not play."

She looked at Tim, her eyes challenging him to intervene.

"Be efficient," she said, using his own word against him. "But be
lethal. Because if you are not, you are just a well-organized corpse."

She turned and walked toward the High Spire, the frost trailing in her
wake.

Tim stood silent for a long moment. He looked at Mara, who was wiping
her eyes but holding her broken dagger with a new, terrifying tightness.
He looked at the Royal Guard, who were no longer sneering, but looking
at the "Ice Queen" with genuine fear.

Tim walked over to Mara.

"Are you okay?" he asked quietly.

"I..." Mara looked at the shattered ice. "I didn't know I could hit that
hard."

"You shouldn't have to," Tim said, watching Elara's retreating figure.
"But Elara is right. The target won't always be wood."

He looked at his troops. The mood had shifted. The camaraderie was still
there, but the playfulness was gone. It had been replaced by a grim,
steely edge.

Tim realized then that his partnership with Elara wasn't just about
covering weaknesses. It was about balance. He was the Heart of the army.

But she... she was the Knife.

And he knew, with a heavy certainty, that before this was over, he was
going to have to let her cut.
