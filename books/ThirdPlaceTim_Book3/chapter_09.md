# Chapter 9: Ten Seconds to Midnight

The murmur in the ranks was growing louder. It had been three days of
grueling drills, rations, and sleeping in the mud. The Academy students,
used to Tim's leadership, endured it. But the Royal Guard---the cream of
the aristocracy---was fraying.

"We are drilling digging ditches," a Lieutenant grumbled loud enough to
be heard. "While the Peasant Commander reads maps and the Ice Witch
glares at us. I bet they couldn't hold a line against a real charge."

Prince Aric turned to silence his man, but Elara was faster.

She stood on the command dais, her silver-white hair gleaming in the
twilight. She didn't look angry. She looked bored.

"Lieutenant," Elara called out. "You seem restless."

The Lieutenant, a burly man named Kaelis (a cousin of the Duke), stepped
forward. "We are Vanguard, my Lady. We are restless because we are
warriors, not laborers. We question the... combat efficacy of this
command structure."

Elara looked at Tim. Tim looked up from his map table. He saw the doubt
in the eyes of the Guard. Doubt was a crack in the hull. In the
Shadow-Scar, cracks killed.

"Combat efficacy," Tim repeated, testing the words. He unclipped the map
case and set it down.

"You think we lead from the rear because we cannot stand in the front,"
Elara stated.

"I think," Kaelis said, emboldened by the nodding heads of his squad,
"that magic tricks and copper wires don't stop a charge of ten heavy
infantry."

Tim walked down the steps of the dais. Elara floated down beside him on
a ramp of frost. They stopped in the center of the muddy training ring.

"Gather your squad, Lieutenant," Tim said quietly. "Ten men. Full plate.
Live steel."

Aric stepped forward. "Commander, this is unnecessary. I can discipline
them."

"No," Tim said to the Prince. "They need to see it. Words are wind,
Aric. Iron only respects force."

Kaelis grinned. He signaled his squad. Nine other massive Guardsmen
stepped out, drawing broadswords and raising shields. They were the
King's elite. Ten against two.

"What are the rules?" Kaelis asked, locking his visor.

"There are no rules," Elara said. Her voice was a cold whisper that
carried across the yard. "There is only the clock."

She looked at Gareth, who was holding the hourglass.

"Ten seconds, Gareth."

"Ten?" Kaelis scoffed. "You think you can beat us in ten seconds?"

"I think," Tim said, rolling up his sleeves to reveal the copper glow
beginning to spark under his skin, "that in ten seconds, we have a staff
meeting."

Tim looked at Elara. He didn't speak. He just tapped his chest. *Heat.*
Elara nodded. *Cold.*

"Begin!" Gareth roared.

**0:01** The ten Guardsmen charged. They didn't run wild; they formed a
crushing wedge, shields locked, a wall of gold steel designed to trample
anything in its path. Kaelis roared, raising his sword.

**0:02** Tim stepped forward. He didn't dodge. He fell to one knee,
slamming both palms into the wet, muddy earth. *Inhale. Accept.* His
Copper Core didn't hum; it screamed. He dumped a massive, instantaneous
load of thermal energy into the groundwater beneath the charging squad.
*BOIL.*

**0:03** The mud didn't just dry; it exploded. A massive geyser of
superheated steam erupted from the ground directly under the Royal
Guard. "ARGH!" Kaelis screamed as the white-hot vapor blinded them and
heated their armor instantly. The formation broke. They stumbled,
flailing in the scalding fog.

**0:04** Tim stood up within the steam. To the Guards, he was a
silhouette of molten orange light. He reached out. *Induction.* He
grabbed the magnetic fields of their overheated armor. He pulled. The
ten Guardsmen were yanked together, their armor magnetizing, clumping
them into a chaotic, metal knot in the center of the ring.

**0:05** "Elara!" Tim shouted, stepping back.

**0:06** Elara walked into the steam. She didn't look like a mage; she
looked like the end of the world. She raised both hands. She drew the
heat out. All of it. The heat of the steam. The heat of the mud. The
heat of the Guards' bodies.

**0:07** *Flash Freeze.* The laws of thermodynamics snapped. The
superheated steam didn't just cool; it sublimated instantly into solid
ice. The mud turned to permafrost harder than concrete.

**0:08** The ten Guardsmen were encased. A glacier formed in the center
of the training ring, trapping them mid-stumble, mid-shout. Their armor
was frozen to the ground. Their swords were frozen to their hands.

**0:09** Elara clenched her fist. Ten spears of ice erupted from the
glacier, stopping one inch from the throat of each trapped Guardsman.

**0:10** Silence.

The steam cleared. The sun was setting, but the courtyard was glittering
with diamond-dust.

In the center stood a sculpture of defeat. Ten of the King's best
warriors, frozen in a block of ice, immobile, helpless, with jagged
icicles pressed against their jugulars.

Tim stood on the left, steam rising from his skin. Elara stood on the
right, her breath pluming in the sudden chill.

Tim walked up to Kaelis, who was staring wide-eyed through a layer of
clear ice covering his visor. Tim tapped the ice gently. *Tink.*

"Combat efficacy," Tim said softly.

He turned to the assembled army. The Academy students were grinning. The
remaining Royal Guards were pale, their mouths open.

"We are not duelists," Tim announced, his voice ringing in the cold air.
"We do not trade blows. We create environments where the enemy cannot
exist."

He pointed to the frozen squad.

"I provide the Energy. She provides the Entropy. Together, we are the
Storm."

Elara snapped her fingers. The ice spears at the throats shattered into
harmless snow, though the Guards remained trapped in the glacier.

"Thaw them out," Elara ordered the Draft Zone mages. "And Lieutenant
Kaelis?"

The trapped man blinked behind his ice mask.

"Next time you question the command," Elara said, turning to walk away,
"bring more men. Ten is simply a warmup."

Prince Aric stared at the frozen monument. He looked at Tim, who was
calmly rolling down his sleeves. He looked at Elara, who was checking
her nails.

Aric realized then that he wasn't leading the army. He was simply lucky
enough to be standing on the same side as the monsters.

"Dig the ditches," Aric ordered his men quietly. "And do it deep."
