# Chapter 10: The Carrier Wave

The sun had set hours ago, leaving the Academy grounds in darkness. The
only light came from the torches sputtering in the wind and the
bioluminescent moss Christopher was experimenting with near the
latrines.

The troops were exhausted. They were slumped against walls, sharpening
blades with slow, rhythmic rasps. Morale was low. The reality of the
Shadow-Scar---the cold, the dark, the monsters---was sinking in.

"Aric!" Tim called out from the center of the yard.

Prince Aric was polishing his pauldrons by the light of a lantern. At
the sound of his name, he didn't sneer. He didn't send a servant. He
snapped to attention, his spine straightening instinctively.

"Commander," Aric responded, stepping forward.

Tim looked him up and down. "What color is your core?"

Aric blinked. It was a rudimentary question. "It is Gold, Commander. The
color of Kings. The color of the Sun."

"And what does it allow you to do?" Tim pressed.

Aric puffed out his chest. "It grants Dominion. It allows me to project
authority. To crush the will of lesser men. To burn away impurity with
the weight of my presence."

Tim nodded slowly. "Show me."

Aric didn't hesitate. He closed his eyes and flared his Ki.

WHOOM.

A wave of golden light erupted from him. It wasn't warm; it was heavy.
It felt like the air pressure had suddenly tripled. The students nearby
groaned, clutching their heads. The weaker ones fell to their knees. It
was impressive, terrifying, and suffocating.

"Stop," Tim ordered.

Aric cut the flow. The pressure vanished, leaving the students gasping
for air. Aric looked at Tim, expecting praise for his power.

"That," Tim said, walking closer, "is useless."

Aric's jaw tightened. "Useless? I brought twenty men to their knees."

"We aren't fighting men, Aric," Tim said. "We are fighting thralls who
have had their fear surgically removed. They won't kneel. They'll just
walk through your pressure because they don't care about your
authority."

Tim gestured to the exhausted students.

"And look at your own troops. You crushed them. You made them heavier.
In the Shadow-Scar, the gravity wells already pull us down. If you add
your ego to that weight, we won't make it a mile."

Aric looked at the students rubbing their temples. He looked deflated.
"It is the nature of Gold. It is heavy. It rules."

"That is the nature of a *tyrant*," Tim corrected. "But Gold has other
properties."

Tim reached into his pocket and pulled out a coil of thin copper wire
and a small gold coin.

"Copper conducts," Tim said, tapping the wire. "It moves energy from A
to B. That's what I do. I'm the wire."

He held up the gold coin.

"But Gold? Gold is the most conductive metal in the world. Better than
copper. But it's too soft to be a wire. It's too rare."

He flipped the coin to Aric.

"Gold isn't a wire, Aric. Gold is an *antenna*."

Aric caught the coin. "An antenna?"

"A broadcaster," Tim explained. "You've been using your core to push
*down*. To compress. I want you to push *out*. To vibrate."

Tim pointed at Gareth, who was sitting nearby, looking grey and tired.

"Gareth is running on fumes. His battery is dead. I can give him some
energy, but I have to touch him. I have to be the wire."

Tim looked Aric in the eye.

"You don't have to touch them. You can broadcast. Instead of projecting
'Submit,' I want you to project 'Rise.' Don't be the weight on their
shoulders. Be the adrenaline in their blood."

Aric looked at the coin. "I... I have never tried to... lift."

"Try," Tim urged. "Don't think about being a King. Think about being a
battle standard. A flag that everyone can see. A frequency that cuts
through the static."

Aric closed his eyes. He visualized his core. Usually, he imagined it as
a heavy crown. Now, he tried to imagine it as a bell. A bright, ringing
bell.

He pushed the Ki out.

At first, it was the same heavy pressure. Gareth grunted.

"Too heavy," Tim coached softly. "Higher pitch. Faster vibration. Don't
crush the air; excite it."

Aric gritted his teeth. He adjusted the flow. He stopped trying to
dominate the space and started trying to fill it.

The light changed.

The oppressive, blinding glare softened into a warm, radiant glow. The
heavy pressure lifted, replaced by a strange, tingling sensation in the
air. It felt like the moment the sun breaks through the clouds after a
storm. It felt like coffee hitting the bloodstream.

Gareth's head snapped up. The grey pallor left his face. He took a deep
breath, his eyes widening.

"I feel..." Gareth rumbled, standing up. "Awake."

Christopher, who had been dozing over a book, sat up straight, his
glasses sliding down his nose. "My heart rate is up. But not from fear.
From... anticipation?"

Aric opened his eyes. He saw the troops. They weren't kneeling. They
were standing. They were looking at him not with fear, but with sudden,
renewed energy.

"What is this?" Aric whispered, looking at his glowing hands.

"That," Tim said, smiling, "is a Carrier Wave. You're broadcasting raw
morale. You're synthesizing courage and pumping it into the air."

Tim walked up to the Prince.

"A tyrant pushes people down so he stands taller," Tim said quietly. "A
true King stands tall so his people can look up."

He tapped the gold insignia on Aric's chest.

"When we go into the dark, Aric, I don't need you to fight the enemy. I
need you to keep the lights on. I need you to be the reason we don't
freeze."

Aric looked at the students. For the first time, he didn't see subjects.
He saw a battery that he was responsible for charging.

"The Carrier Wave," Aric repeated. He clenched his fist, and the golden
light intensified, not blinding, but invigorating. "I can do this. I can
keep them awake forever."

"Let's settle for 'until the battle is won'," Tim said. "Now, cut the
signal. They need to sleep. And so do you."

Aric released the flow. He staggered slightly, drained. Tim caught his
arm to steady him.

"Thank you, Commander," Aric said, his voice stripped of arrogance.

"Don't thank me," Tim said, turning back to his quarters. "Just keep
shining, Your Highness. We're going to need it."
