# Chapter 11: The Ratio of Ruin

General Alric, Commander of the King's Home Guard, sat behind a folding
table in the Academy's war tent. He was a man made of leather and scars,
with a mustache that looked like a stiff bristle brush. He stared at the
young man standing before him---a boy in a grey tunic with a silver band
on his arm and a terrifying lack of fear in his eyes.

"We need a battle, General Alric," Tim stated, resting his hands on the
table.

Alric looked up from his maps. "A battle? We deploy in six days,
Commander. My men are resting. Your... children... are drilling. Why
would we risk injury now?"

"Because drilling is theory," Tim said. "And the Iron-Blood Empire is
fact. My troops have learned to fight each other. They have learned to
fight monsters. But they have not learned to fight a swarm."

Tim leaned forward. "I need a simulation. My army vs. the King's Army."

Alric snorted, a sound like tearing canvas. "You want to spar? Fine. I
can spare a platoon. A skirmish to keep their blood warm."

"Not a skirmish," Tim corrected. "A siege test."

Alric raised an eyebrow. "Odds?"

"3 to 1 should be a good start," Tim said casually.

Alric paused. He did the math. There were roughly one hundred students
in the combined Academy and Royal Guard task force.

"You want to put The King's Army of... 33?, against your 100?" Alric
asked, looking at Tim like he was a slow child. "Commander, my men are
veterans, but being outnumbered three-to-one by Ki-active students is
hardly a fair fight for *them*."

Tim shook his head.

"...no," Tim said softly. "I want you to put 300 of the King's men
against my 100."

The tent went silent. Elara, standing in the corner, didn't blink. She
knew the math.

General Alric stood up slowly. "You are joking. Three hundred of the
Crimson Legion? Heavy infantry? Men who fought in the Border Wars?
Against... students?"

"The Iron-Blood Empire will outnumber us ten to one in the Shadow-Scar,"
Tim said, his voice deadly serious. "If we cannot handle a three-to-one
disadvantage on grass, in the sunlight, against men who aren't trying to
eat us... then we shouldn't walk through that portal."

"I will not be responsible for the massacre of the Kingdom's heirs,"
Alric growled. "My men do not know how to 'play' war. If they charge,
they crush."

"That is exactly what I am counting on," Tim said. "Don't hold them
back, General. If you do, you aren't training us. You're killing us."

Alric stared at the boy. He looked at the Copper Core glowing faintly in
Tim's eyes. He looked at the Ice Witch in the corner.

"Very well," Alric said, picking up his helmet. "Dawn tomorrow. The
South Fields. But when your students are crying in the mud, do not come
to me for sympathy."

"I won't," Tim promised. "Just bring the medics for your men."
