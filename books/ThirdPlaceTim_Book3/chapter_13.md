# Chapter 13: The Butcher's Bill

The celebration in Prince Aric's quarters was subdued, but the air was
light. They had just defeated the King's Legion with a casualty rate of
zero. Aric was pouring wine with a hand that no longer trembled. Gareth
was happily eating an entire roasted leg of lamb in the corner.

Tim was reviewing the after-action report, a small smile of satisfaction
on his face.

"We controlled the field," Aric said, handing a glass to Christopher.
"The voice projection... I felt like a god. Did you see General Alric's
face? He looked like he'd swallowed a lemon."

"It was a good net," Tim agreed. "The magnetic impedance worked better
than calculated."

"Not good enough!"

The glass in Elara's hand didn't shatter; it froze, then cracked into
dust from the thermal shock.

The room went dead silent. Gareth stopped chewing.

Elara stood by the fireplace, the flames guttering low in her presence.
She wasn't smiling. She looked furious.

"That was more trick than battle," Elara spat, stepping into the center
of the room. She kicked the table leg, hard. "You relied on General
Alric's hesitation. You relied on the soldiers' confusion. You relied on
the fact that they are *human men* who didn't want to hurt children!"

She turned on Tim, her eyes blazing with cold fire.

"The Iron-Blood Empire is not human! They are a hive! If you pull that
magnetic net on a thousand Thralls, the ones in the back will climb over
the ones in the front to get to you. They will suffocate their own kin
just to bite your ankles!"

"Elara," Tim said gently, standing up. "We won. We have time to---"

"We have *no* time!" she screamed. It was the first time Tim had ever
heard her raise her voice like that. It wasn't anger. It was terror.

"Give me an army of one thousand!" Elara demanded, pacing the room like
a caged tiger. "And I want warhounds against us! And Cavalry! And...
archers! And... and... mages!"

She whirled on Aric.

"You think you're ready because you stopped a charge? Try stopping a
charge when the air is on fire and wolves with steel jaws are tearing at
your legs! Try holding a line when you can't hear the command because of
the screaming!"

Aric set his wine down. "Elara, be reasonable. We cannot ask General
Alric for a thousand men. The King wouldn't permit it, and frankly, we
would be trampled. We need to train, not commit suicide."

"Then we simulate it," Elara hissed.

She looked at Tim.

"You built a Circuit to share strength. You built a Circuit to share
courage."

She walked up to him, her face inches from his. He could feel the arctic
chill radiating off her skin.

"Can you build a Circuit to share pain?"

Tim blinked. "Elara?"

"The High Spire archives," Elara whispered. "There is a forbidden
technique. The *Phantasmagoria*. It creates a shared mental construct. A
hallucination. But if you anchor it with enough power... it becomes
tactile."

She grabbed his hand. Her skin was freezing.

"I can create the monsters, Tim. I can conjure the thousand Thralls. The
hounds. The fire. I can build the nightmare inside their heads."

She squeezed his hand.

"But I need you to make it *real*. I need you to power the feedback
loop. If a phantom wolf bites them, I need you to make their nerves fire
so they feel the teeth. If they get burned, I need their skin to
blister."

"You want to torture them," Aric whispered, horrified.

"I want to vaccinate them!" Elara shouted, spinning to face the Prince.
"I want them to feel the panic *now*, in a room where they won't
actually die, so that when they step into the Shadow-Scar next week,
they don't freeze!"

She looked back at Tim. Her eyes were pleading.

"They think they are warriors because they won a game of tag, Tim. They
need to know what it feels like to lose. They need to know what it feels
like to be butchered. Because if they don't learn it here... they will
learn it there. And there, there are no respawns."

Tim looked at her. He saw the desperation. She had seen the reports on
the grafts. She knew about the beasts. She was trying to save them the
only way she knew how: by hurting them enough to make them hard.

Tim looked at Aric. At Gareth. At Christopher.

"Pain without damage," Tim mused. "A simulation of hell."

"Is it possible?" Aric asked, looking pale.

"With my Core as the generator and her Mind as the projector?" Tim
calculated. "Yes. We can create a Lucid Nightmare. We can put them in
the middle of a slaughter."

"Do it," Gareth said.

Everyone turned to the giant. He wiped lamb grease from his mouth.

"The mud pit was easy," Gareth rumbled. "I did not feel afraid. If the
Empire is monsters... I need to see the monsters."

Tim looked at Elara. He nodded slowly.

"Gather the Squads," Tim said, his voice heavy. "Clear the Great Hall.
We aren't doing drills tomorrow."

He gripped Elara's icy hand.

"Tomorrow, we go to war. And this time... we aren't going to win."
