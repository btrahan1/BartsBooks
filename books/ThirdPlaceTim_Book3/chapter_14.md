# Chapter 14: The Meat Grinder

The Great Hall was empty, stripped of tables and banners. The one
hundred students of the Alloy Task Force sat in a large circle on the
stone floor.

They looked nervous. Rumors had spread. *The Commanders are angry. The
General is bringing monsters.*

Tim stood in the center, next to Elara.

"Close your eyes," Tim commanded. "Link up. Anchors set."

The students obeyed. Hands clasped. Shoulders touched. The Circuit
formed, a hum of nervous energy buzzing through the room.

"Today," Elara announced, her voice echoing strangely in the physical
room and the psychic link simultaneously, "we are not fighting for
points. Today, we are fighting for seconds."

"Tim," she said. "Power the grid."

Tim took a breath. *Inhale. Accept.*

He didn't push warmth. He pushed *raw current*. He flooded the neural
pathways of a hundred students with high-voltage Ki, heightening their
sensitivity to maximum.

"Circuit Live," Tim said.

Elara closed her eyes. She reached into the cold, dark corners of her
mind, where she had stored the images from the Intelligence Dossiers.
The claws. The sewn-shut eyes of the Thralls. The beasts.

"Project," Elara whispered.

SNAP.

The Great Hall vanished.

The students gasped. They were no longer sitting on cold stone.

They were standing in a ruined city of black obsidian. The sky was a
bruising purple, torn by lightning. The air smelled of sulfur and
rotting meat.

And all around them, rising from the jagged rocks, was the Army.

Not three hundred men. *One thousand.*

They weren't men. They were abominations. Thralls with metal plates
bolted to their skulls. Hounds the size of ponies with gears grinding in
their chests. Mages floating on discs of bone, wreathed in green
necrotic fire.

"Defensive positions!" Aric shouted, his voice cracking as he drew his
sword. It felt heavy. Real.

"Here they come!" Cassian screamed.

The wave hit them.

It wasn't a battle. It was a tidal wave of violence.

The Thralls didn't stop when they were hit. Gareth smashed one with his
hammer, caving in its chest, but the Thrall just kept crawling, grabbing
Gareth's leg with a mechanical claw.

"It hurts!" Milo screamed. A phantom arrow had pierced his shoulder. The
pain was blinding, absolute, indistinguishable from reality.

"Hold the line!" Tim's voice cut through the chaos, but he wasn't a god
here. He was fighting, too. A massive, graft-enhanced soldier was
hammering at his guard, driving him back.

The warhounds hit the flank. They tore through the "Polish" shields like
tissue paper. Christopher screamed as a beast clamped its jaws on his
arm. He felt the bone snap. He felt the blood.

"Reset!" Elara's voice boomed from the sky.

The world flickered. The students were back in formation, unharmed.

"Again," Elara commanded. "You lasted thirty seconds. That is pathetic.
AGAIN."

SNAP.

The nightmare rebooted. The horde charged.

This time, Aric tried a Carrier Wave. The Necrotic Mages countered it
with a Screaming Void that turned his courage into nausea. The Royal
Guard broke and ran. They were hunted down by cavalry riding skeletal
horses.

"Dead," Elara's voice intoned. "All of you. Dead."

SNAP.

"Again."

For six hours, they fought. For six hours, they died.

They died in the mud. They died in the fire. They died screaming for
their mothers.

They learned that "Honor" didn't stop a saw-blade. They learned that
"Formation" didn't matter when the ground exploded. They learned that
when you lose an arm, you pick up your sword with the other hand because
if you stop, your friends die.

Tim was the anchor. He felt every death. He felt every phantom wound. He
held the Circuit together, refusing to let them disconnect, forcing them
to endure the trauma.

By the tenth simulation, something changed.

The Royal Guard didn't break. When the cavalry charged, they didn't try
to duel the riders. They stabbed the horses.

Gareth didn't try to smash the Thralls. He threw them into each other,
creating piles of bodies to slow the advance.

Christopher didn't scream when he got hit. He gritted his teeth, sealed
the wound with ice, and kept casting.

They stopped trying to win. They started trying to survive.

Final Simulation.

The Academy force was huddled on a rocky outcropping, surrounded. They
were exhausted. They were terrified. But they were together.

"They're coming up the ridge!" Aric yelled, blood (phantom) running down
his face. "I have no Ki left!"

"Use the rocks!" Tim shouted, heaving a boulder over the edge. "Gravity
is free!"

The horde swarmed up. The students fought with desperation. They used
rocks. They used teeth. They used the bodies of the fallen as shields.

They held for one minute. Two. Five.

Finally, the sheer numbers overwhelmed them. The last student fell.

SNAP.

The Great Hall returned.

Silence.

One hundred students lay on the floor, gasping, weeping, clutching limbs
that were perfectly whole but felt phantom pains.

Tim sat in the center, his head in his hands. He was shaking. He had
processed a thousand deaths in one afternoon.

Elara stood over them. She looked pale, drained, but her eyes were dry.

"You lasted twelve minutes," Elara whispered. Her voice trembled
slightly. "Against a thousand."

She looked at Aric, who was curled in a ball, shivering. She looked at
Gareth, who was staring at his hands.

"Twelve minutes," Tim croaked, looking up. "Is that enough?"

Elara knelt down beside him. She reached out and wiped a bead of sweat
from his forehead.

"It's enough to run," she said softly. "It's enough to hide. It's enough
to survive the first contact."

She stood up and addressed the broken army.

"You know the face of the enemy now," Elara said. "You know the pain.
The surprise is gone."

She paused.

"The next time you see them... they will be the ones who are surprised.
Because you are already dead. And dead men have nothing left to fear."

Tim looked at his students. They were broken, yes. But as they slowly
sat up, checking on each other, gripping shoulders, sharing water... he
saw it.

They weren't glass anymore. They weren't even iron.

They were scar tissue. Tough. Fibrous. And very, very hard to kill.
