# Chapter 15: The Golden Sun and the Thermal Shock

The silence in the Great Hall was heavy, filled only by the ragged
breathing of one hundred students who had just mentally experienced
their own demise.

Tim stood up. He didn't wipe the sweat from his face. He let them see
it. He let them see that he was shaking, too.

"Now that you know what it feels like to die," Tim said, his voice raspy
but gaining strength, "what it feels like to be crushed, what it feels
like to be helpless... there is nothing left to fear!"

He walked among them, grabbing hands, pulling students to their feet.

"Yes, you will be terrified," Tim acknowledged, looking at a weeping
Miller boy. "But you will channel that! You are already dead! All of
you! We just died in that black city. But that is the worst that can
happen!"

He turned to the center of the room.

"Now, let me show you how to 'die', but not 'die'! Let me show you how
to bend, but not break!"

He pointed at Prince Aric. The Prince was pale, stripped of his vanity,
looking like a man who had seen the abyss. But his Gold Core was
flaring, reacting to the trauma not with retreat, but with a defiant,
blinding luminosity.

"And let me show the power of a Gold Core!" Tim shouted, his voice
echoing in the psychic link that was still humming faintly.

He looked at the exhausted students. "In the next run, you will go all
out! You will not spare a single shred of energy! You will dump your
entire reserves in the first minute! You will fight like gods until you
burn out!"

"We will die in seconds!" Cassian yelled back, his eyes wild.

"No!" Tim roared. "Prince Aric will keep you alive! Believe it! He is
the battery. He is the sun. As long as he stands, you do not fall!"

Tim walked over to Elara. She looked fragile, her skin almost
translucent from the effort of maintaining the nightmare. But when Tim
took her hand, her eyes snapped open, glowing with a terrifying, arctic
blue.

"And," Tim said, his voice dropping to a dangerous, low register, "let
me show you how to hold the line. And let me show you what Elara and I
can REALLY DO!"

He turned to the empty space of the hall.

"Elara. Reset. Two thousand."

The students gasped.

"Two thousand," Tim repeated. "And we will not only hold. We will win."

Elara squeezed Tim's hand. She drew a breath that seemed to suck the
warmth from the room.

"Circuit Live," she whispered.

SNAP.

The Great Hall vanished. The Obsidian City returned. But this time, the
horizon was black with enemies. Two thousand Thralls, Warhounds, and
Necrotic Mages swarmed the ruins like insects.

"FORMATION!" Cassian screamed.

The Alloy Task Force formed up. But they didn't cower. They didn't
shake. They were ghosts. They had already died.

"BURN IT ALL!" Tim commanded.

The students unleashed hell.

Christopher didn't cast a shield; he cast a kinetic shockwave that
shattered the front rank. Gareth didn't swing his hammer; he threw
himself into the horde, a spinning dervish of iron. Lyssandra summoned a
magma fissure.

They fought with reckless, suicidal abandon. They emptied their Cores.

One minute in. They began to fade. The lights flickered. Gareth
stumbled. Christopher's nose began to bleed.

"ARIC!" Tim screamed over the link. "LIGHT IT UP!"

Prince Aric threw his head back. He didn't try to fight. He didn't try
to rule. He surrendered to his purpose.

He became the Anthem.

"**RISE!**" Aric's voice wasn't sound; it was pure Golden Ki.

He exploded. A shockwave of golden light washed over the battlefield. It
hit the exhausted students. It didn't just heal them; it refilled them.
It forced their meridians open. It poured adrenaline and pure, royal
power into their veins.

Gareth roared, his strength doubling. Christopher's eyes glowed gold,
and his shockwave turned into a tsunami. They were fighting past their
limits, fueled by the Prince who refused to let the sun set.

But there were still two thousand enemies. The wave was pushing them
back.

"Now," Tim said to Elara. "Us."

They stood at the apex of the defensive line.

"Physics," Tim whispered. "Thermal Shock."

Tim stepped forward. He raised both hands. He didn't target a single
enemy. He targeted the *air*.

INDUCTION: MAXIMUM.

Tim's Copper Core went critical. He pulled every ounce of magnetic and
thermal energy from the battlefield. He stripped the heat from the lava
fissures. He stripped the friction from the swords. He stripped the body
heat from the two thousand rushing enemies.

He concentrated it all into a single, blindingly hot field in the center
of the enemy army.

The Thralls screamed as their metal grafts began to glow red hot. The
air turned into a shimmering heat haze. The temperature skyrocketed to
blast-furnace levels. Their armor softened. Their movements became
sluggish, trapped in the molasses of superheated air.

"ELARA!"

Elara stepped past him. She walked into the heat haze. She was
untouched. She was the Void.

She didn't cast a spell. She simply unleashed her nature.

ENTROPY: ABSOLUTE ZERO.

She clapped her hands.

The laws of nature shrieked.

Matter cannot handle going from 1000 degrees to -100 degrees in a
microsecond.

CRACK.

It wasn't an explosion. It was the sound of reality shattering.

The superheated armor of the Thralls didn't freeze; it shattered. The
metal snapped. The stone ground cracked. The structural integrity of the
entire enemy army failed simultaneously.

Weapons snapped like glass. Grafts exploded. The very ground they stood
on pulverized into dust.

A shockwave of diamond-dust and shattered steel rolled outward,
flattening everything in its path.

The two thousand enemies didn't die one by one. They fell as a single,
shattered mass.

Silence returned to the Obsidian City.

The students stood there, panting, glowing with Aric's gold light,
staring at the field of glitter and ruin.

Tim swayed. He felt empty. Cold. Hot. Everything at once.

Elara caught him. She was shivering, frost coating her eyelashes.

"We won," she whispered.

Tim looked at the devastation. He looked at his army---battered,
exhausted, but standing tall, held up by the golden will of their Prince
and the terrifying power of their Commanders.

SNAP.

The simulation ended. They were back in the Great Hall.

No one cheered. There was no energy left for cheering.

But as they looked at each other---at the sweat, the tears, the
determination---they knew.

They weren't students anymore. They weren't even soldiers.

They were a calamity waiting to be unleashed.

Tim stood in the center, supported by Elara. He looked at Aric, who was
on his knees, gasping, but smiling a wild, golden smile.

"Two days," Tim rasped. "Then we do it for real."

"Gods help the Empire," Gareth rumbled, breaking the silence. "I almost
feel sorry for them."

Tim straightened his tunic.

"Don't," Tim said. "Just make sure they don't get back up."
