# Chapter 16: The King's Witness

The observation balcony was shrouded in shadows, hidden behind a mesh of
one-way glass. King Thorne of Aethelgard stood with his hands gripping
the railing, his knuckles white against the velvet.

Below, in the Great Hall, the simulation had ended. One hundred students
lay on the stone floor, gasping, weeping, or laughing with the hysteria
of survivors. In the center, Prince Aric was on his knees, glowing with
a fading golden light, clutching the arm of the peasant boy who had led
them into hell and back.

The King exhaled a breath he felt he had been holding for twenty years.

"I sent him to die," the King whispered. His voice was old, heavy with
the burden of the crown. "When the Accord was invoked... I looked at the
reports. I looked at the Iron-Blood monsters. And I looked at Aric. He
is a peacock, General. A vain, shiny boy who has never known a day of
hardship."

The King turned to General Alric, who stood rigid beside him, his
scarred face illuminated by the dying light of the simulation.

"I was already planning his funeral," the King confessed, the grief
stark in his eyes. "I authorized this task force to give him a guard of
honor for his grave. But what I just saw..."

The King gestured helplessly at the scene below---at the devastation of
the simulated army, at the lingering frost on the walls, at the steam
rising from Tim's skin.

"Can he really keep my son alive?" the King asked, a desperate hope
cracking his voice. "Can this 'Just Tim' really win?"

General Alric looked down at the boy in the grey tunic. He remembered
the South Fields. He remembered the magnetic net. He remembered the
feeling of helplessness as three hundred of his best men were trapped
without a single blow being struck.

"Sire," General Alric rumbled, his voice low and reverent. "For this...
and only for this, he isn't 'Just Tim'. He is something more."

The General adjusted his sword belt, looking at the trinity of power
standing in the hall: The Copper Commander, the Ice Witch, and the
Golden Prince.

"No, he can't win," Alric admitted bluntly. "Not alone. The Empire is
too vast, too cruel. A mechanic cannot stop a tidal wave."

Alric pointed to Elara, who was helping Tim stand.

"But with Elara? She is the executioner. She does the things that would
break a lesser soul."

He pointed to Prince Aric, who was helping a fallen scholarship student
to his feet.

"With your son? He has finally stopped trying to be the sword and
learned to be the sun. He is the battery that makes them immortal."

He pointed to the Squad---Gareth, Christopher, the Iron Heads, the Draft
Zone---who were now a single, hardened organism.

"And with his army? They are not soldiers, Sire. Soldiers follow orders.
These children... they share a nervous system."

General Alric turned to face his King, his expression grim but absolute.

"I will be honest with you, Sire. I don't know who will die, and who
will live. War is chaos, and the Shadow-Scar is a tomb."

Alric looked back down at Tim, who was looking at the empty space where
the enemy had been, a look of profound, calculating mercy in his eyes.

"But I can guarantee you that none of the enemy will survive," Alric
said, his voice dropping to a whisper. "Unless he allows it."

The King looked at the peasant boy. He saw the way the other students
looked at him---not with the fear due a tyrant, but with the devotion
due a savior.

"Then let them go," the King commanded softly. "Open the Portal. And may
the Gods have mercy on the Iron-Blood Empire."

Alric nodded. "The Gods might, Sire. But I do not think Elara Vane
will."
