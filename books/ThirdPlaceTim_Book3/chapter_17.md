# Chapter 17: The Silence Before the Storm

The viewing crystals were active. Not just in the King's private box,
but across the continent. In the great plazas of Aethelgard, crowds
gathered in hushed silence to watch the projections. In the dark basalt
citadels of the Iron-Blood Empire, the Emperor and his generals watched
with cold, reptilian calculation.

It was a global spectacle of child sacrifice. A way to bleed a little so
that the world didn't have to bleed a lot.

King Thorne paced the small room, his boots clicking rhythmically on the
stone. He looked haggard. The weight of the crown was heavy, but the
weight of a father sending his only heir into the dark was crushing.

He stopped at the glass, looking down into the Great Hall.

"Why aren't they training?" the King demanded, his voice tight with
anxiety. "This is their last day. The Portal opens at dawn. They should
be drilling. They should be sharpening blades. They should be... doing
*something*."

Below, the Alloy Task Force was motionless.

They sat in concentric circles. The Royal Guard formed the inner ring.
The Iron Heads and the Golden Circle surrounded them. The Squad and the
Draft Zone formed the outer perimeter.

They weren't moving. They weren't speaking. They sat in the lotus
position, eyes closed, hands resting on their knees.

"They are meditating?" the King asked, incredulous. "The Iron-Blood
legions are likely injecting stimulants and running live-fire drills
right now. And my army is... napping?"

General Alric stepped up to the glass. He crossed his arms, looking down
with the eye of a veteran.

"They are not napping, Sire," Alric rumbled. "They are topping off the
tanks."

"Explain," the King snapped.

"If you run a racehorse the day before the derby, you get a tired
horse," Alric said. "Commander Tim understands the economy of energy
better than any general I have ever met. He pushed them to the brink of
death in the simulations for three days straight. He broke their bodies
and their minds."

Alric pointed to a faint, shimmering distortion in the air above the
students.

"Now, he is putting them back together."

The King looked closer. He saw it.

A golden light was pulsing from the center---Prince Aric. But it wasn't
the blinding, arrogant flare of the past. It was a slow, rhythmic
heartbeat of light. *Thrum... Thrum... Thrum.*

With every pulse, the light washed over the students. It didn't bounce
off; it sank in.

"The Prince is the Battery," Alric explained, his voice filled with a
strange pride. "He is filling them. Every student down there is drawing
from his Gold Core, saturating their own meridians until they are
overflowing. And Tim..."

Alric pointed to the center, where Tim sat back-to-back with Aric.

"Tim is the Regulator. He is ensuring that Aric doesn't burn out, and
that the students don't overload. He is balancing the voltage across a
hundred different souls."

The King watched his son. Aric looked peaceful. He looked... useful. For
the first time, he wasn't the center of attention because he demanded
it; he was the center because he was supporting everyone else.

"The Iron-Blood Empire is likely frenzying right now," Alric continued
grimly. "They are sharpening their rage. They will enter the Portal hot,
aggressive, and brittle."

Alric turned to the King.

"But our children? They will enter the Portal cold. Calm. And fully
charged."

The King watched the slow, hypnotic pulse of the golden light. He felt
his own heart rate slow down just watching it.

"It is a strange way to go to war, Alric," the King murmured. "Sitting
down."

"Perhaps," Alric agreed. "But look at their faces, Sire."

The King looked. He saw the Miller boy. He saw Gareth. He saw Lyssandra.
There was no terror. There was no panic. There was only a deep, abiding
stillness.

"They aren't afraid anymore," the King realized.

"No," Alric said softly. "They have already died a thousand times in the
simulation. Tomorrow is just the paperwork."

The iron bell of the Academy tolled the midnight hour.

Below, in perfect unison, one hundred pairs of eyes snapped open.

They stood up. There was no shouting. No cheering. Just the sound of one
hundred boots hitting the floor at the exact same moment.

Tim turned to Aric. He nodded. Aric turned to Elara. He nodded. Elara
turned to the army.

"Rest is over," Alric whispered, watching them shoulder their packs.
"Now... the engine starts."
