# Chapter 18: The Vacuum

The Portal didn't feel like a door. It felt like being pulled through a
straw.

One moment, the Alloy Task Force stood in the warm, stone circle of the
Academy. The next, gravity grabbed them by the gut and slammed them into
hard, black rock.

Tim hit the ground, rolled, and was immediately on his feet. The air
tasted of ash and static electricity. The sky was a bruising, swollen
purple, devoid of a sun. And it was cold---not the clean, sharp cold of
winter, but the gnawing, hungry cold of a vacuum.

"Perimeter!" Tim barked, his voice sounding thin in the heavy air. "Iron
Heads, shield wall! Royal Guard, secure the supplies!"

There was no hesitation. The drills kicked in. Gareth and the heavy
hitters formed a circle facing outward. The Royal Guard, stripped of
their vanity, hauled the crates of rations and water into the center.

"My Ki," Christopher gasped, clutching his chest. "It's... leaking."

"It's the Shadow-Scar," Elara said, scanning the horizon with narrowed
eyes. "The atmosphere is Ki-negative. It naturally drains anything
living. If you project, you bleed."

Tim checked his internal gauges. His Copper Core was humming, but he
could feel the drag. It was like walking uphill in mud.

"Dampen your outputs!" Tim ordered over the Circuit. "Do not flare! Do
not cast! Keep it internal. Aric, low-level Carrier Wave only. Just
enough to keep their hearts beating."

Aric nodded, his golden aura pulled tight against his skin like a second
suit of armor.

"Where are they?" Cassian whispered, peering into the gloom.

Tim closed his eyes. *Induction.*

He felt the magnetic pull of the obsidian rocks. He felt the iron in his
friends' blood. And far to the east, across the jagged plain, he felt...
a hive.

It felt like a swarm of buzzing insects. Thousands of metal grafts
grinding against bone. A chaotic, hungry mass.

"Three miles East," Tim said, opening his eyes. "They've landed."

"Do we engage?" Aric asked, his hand on his sword. "We could hit them
before they organize."

"No," Tim said, pointing to a cluster of ruined obsidian towers against
a cliff face a mile away. "We dig in. We force them to come to us
through the drain. We make them pay for every step."

The Long March

The mile to the ruins was brutal. The gravity in the Shadow-Scar was
higher than normal, turning a simple march into a trudge.

By the time they reached the ruins---the skeletal remains of some
ancient fortress---the students were panting.

"Fortify," Tim commanded.

They didn't build walls; they used the rubble. Gareth and the heavy
lifters moved massive slabs of obsidian to block the entrances.
Lyssandra used her geomancy to fuse the stone, sealing the cracks
against the wind.

By noon (marked only by the purple sky turning a lighter shade of
bruise), they had a base. The Copper Fort.

"Eat," Tim ordered. "High calorie rations. Now."

"I'm not hungry," a Guard complained.

"I don't care," Tim said, checking the supplies. "Your body is burning
twice the fuel just to stand upright here. Eat, or you drop."

They ate in grim silence.

Then the storm came.

It started as a low whistle, then grew into a shriek. A Mana Storm.
Green lightning arced across the sky, striking the ground and leaving
glass craters.

"Inside!" Tim yelled. "Deep cover!"

They huddled in the central keep of the ruin. The storm didn't just
bring wind; it brought a massive pressure drop. The students gasped,
feeling the Ki being sucked out of their pores.

"Hold the Circuit!" Elara shouted. "Christopher, regulate!"

Christopher sat in the center, sweating, holding the hands of Gareth and
Aric. He acted as the valve, preventing the storm from sucking the
students dry.

Through a crack in the wall, Tim watched the plains.

In the distance, the Iron-Blood Empire was moving. They didn't stop for
the storm. They marched right through it. Tim saw lightning strike a
soldier. The man didn't scream. He just fell, and the soldier behind him
stepped over the body without breaking stride.

"They aren't stopping," Cassian whispered, watching alongside Tim.
"They're burning through their own men just to cross the field."

"Efficiency," Tim muttered, though it tasted like bile. "They calculated
the loss rate and decided it was acceptable."

Tim looked at his shivering troops, huddled together for warmth.

"We survive Day One by letting them burn," Tim said. "Let the storm take
its tithe from them."

Nightfall: The Probe

Night in the Shadow-Scar was absolute. A darkness so thick it felt like
a physical weight.

Tim stood watch on the ramparts of their makeshift fort. He didn't use a
torch. He used his ears and his magnetic sense.

"They're here," Elara whispered, stepping out of the shadows.

"Scouts?" Tim asked.

"Hunters," she corrected. "Thralls grafted with night-sight eyes.
They're circling the base."

Tim felt them. Dozens of small, metallic signatures creeping over the
rocks. They were testing the perimeter. Looking for a weak point.
Looking for fear.

"Do we kill them?" Elara asked, frost forming on her fingertips.

"If we kill them, they know where we are strong," Tim said. "If we do
nothing, they breach."

He thought for a moment.

"Confuse them," Tim said.

He turned to the darkness behind him. "Milo. You still have that wool?"

Milo stepped up, clutching a bundle of blankets. "Yes, Commander?"

"And Christopher?"

"Here," the scribe squeaked.

"Christopher," Tim said. "I need you to create a kinetic echo. A
vibration. Make it sound like footsteps."

"Where?"

Tim pointed to a treacherous ravine about two hundred yards to the left
of their fort. "There."

"Milo," Tim continued. "Throw the blankets over the heat vents on the
right side. Mask our thermal signature."

They moved.

Christopher focused, sending a rhythmic *thump-thump-thump* of Ki into
the ground near the ravine.

Out in the darkness, the metal signatures shifted. The Thralls, drawn by
the sound and the vibration, turned toward the ravine. They moved like
spiders, silent and fast.

Tim felt them reach the edge of the ravine. They paused, sensing the
vibration below.

Then, the leader jumped. Expecting solid ground or an enemy.

He found only gravity.

Tim heard the faint, wet *crunch* as the Thrall hit the bottom of the
ravine. Then another. And another. The hive mind followed the stimulus
blindly.

"Twelve down," Tim counted. "No energy spent."

Elara watched him. "You didn't fight them."

"Attrition, Elara," Tim said, staring into the dark. "Every Thrall that
falls into a hole is one less we have to fight tomorrow. We don't spend
a single copper until we have to."

The night dragged on. The wind howled. The temperature dropped low
enough to freeze stone.

Inside the fort, Aric kept the Carrier Wave pulsing---a low, golden
heartbeat that kept the freezing students alive.

Outside, Tim and Elara stood back-to-back, watching the dark.

Day One was ending. They were alive. They were fortified.

But in the distance, the main body of the Iron-Blood Empire had stopped
marching. They were setting up camp. And Tim could feel the massive,
throbbing power of General Silas connecting to the Hive.

"He knows we're here," Tim whispered.

"Let him come," Elara said, her hand drifting to her dagger. "We have
rocks. We have gravity. And we have you."

Tim looked at the purple dawn beginning to bruise the horizon.

"One down," Tim breathed. "Two to go."
