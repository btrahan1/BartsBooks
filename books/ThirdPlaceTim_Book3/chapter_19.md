# Chapter 19: The Midnight Reaping

The perimeter of the Copper Fort was silent. The storm had passed,
leaving behind a silence so deep it felt heavy.

Tim adjusted the straps of his boots. He wore no armor, just the grey
wool and a belt filled with strange tools---wire cutters, a bag of iron
filings, and a jar of alchemical solvent.

"Elara. It's time," Tim whispered into the dark.

A shadow detached itself from the wall. Elara appeared, her white robes
replaced by form-fitting black leathers. She looked like a sliver of the
night itself.

"Yes, it is," she replied, her breath not even misting in the cold air.
She had lowered her body temperature to match the environment. "Sleep is
for the weak."

Tim turned to the third figure waiting by the blocked entrance.

"Aric, are you ready?"

Prince Aric stepped forward. He had covered his gleaming gold armor with
a matte black cloak, rubbed with ash. He looked uncomfortable without
his shine, like a star trying to implode.

"Yes," Aric murmured. He tapped the hilt of his sword. "I've got you.
Though sneaking around in the dirt feels... beneath us."

"We aren't sneaking," Tim corrected, checking his induction sensors. "We
are auditing."

He looked out toward the distant, pulsing glow of the Iron-Blood
encampment.

"3 vs. a bit less than 1000," Tim noted. "Let's even the odds some
tonight. The 3 of us."

The Hive

They moved across the obsidian plain like smoke. Tim took point, his
Induction Field sensing the metallic tremors of the earth. Elara guarded
the rear, erasing their thermal footprints with precise bursts of cold.
Aric walked in the middle, struggling to keep his Gold Core from
flaring.

"You're leaking," Tim whispered over their localized mental link. "You
feel like a lighthouse."

"It's hard," Aric gritted back. "The gold wants to shine."

"Don't shine," Tim instructed. "Implode. Pull it in. Make yourself so
dense that light can't escape. Be a black hole, Aric."

Aric concentrated. The faint shimmer around him vanished, replaced by a
terrifying heaviness. He didn't glow; he loomed.

They reached the ridge overlooking the Iron-Blood camp.

It wasn't a camp. It was a factory.

Rows of soldiers---Thralls---stood motionless in racks, hooked up to
tubes flowing with glowing green fluid. They weren't sleeping; they were
recharging. Their eyes were pinned open, staring at nothing.

Further back, massive cages held the Warhounds. The beasts were pacing,
their mechanical jaws snapping at the air.

And in the center, a massive pylon of black iron pulsed with a rhythmic
thrumming sound. The Hive Node.

"Horrifying," Aric breathed. "They aren't men. They're batteries."

"They're efficient," Elara noted coldly. "Look at the coolant lines
running to the Warhound cages. If those lines freeze..."

"The hounds overheat," Tim finished. "And they get angry."

"Plan?" Aric asked.

"Division of labor," Tim said. "Elara, the coolant lines. Freeze them
solid until they burst. Aric, the Hive Node. Don't destroy it. Corrupt
it."

"Corrupt it?"

"It broadcasts orders," Tim said. "Silence. Obedience. I want you to
inject a Carrier Wave of... *Panic*."

Aric smiled in the dark. It was a cruel smile. "I can do panic."

"And you?" Elara asked Tim.

Tim pulled out the bag of iron filings.

"I'm going to introduce some friction to the recharging racks. Let's see
how well they march when their joints are fused."

The Sabotage

They split up.

Elara moved to the cages. Two sentries---Thralls with thermal-vision
goggles---stood guard.

Elara didn't slow down. She walked right up to them. As they raised
their weapons, she simply exhaled.

Mist.

The moisture in the air around the sentries' heads flash-froze. Ice
crystals formed inside their nostrils and throats. They dropped without
a sound, suffocating silently.

Elara stepped over them. She placed her hands on the main coolant pipe.
*Entropy.* She drained the heat. The pipe groaned, frosted over, and
then developed a hairline fracture. She pushed harder. The fluid inside
froze, expanded, and shattered the metal.

Green coolant sprayed out, instantly freezing into slush. Inside the
cages, the temperature began to rise. The hounds whined, the mechanical
heat in their chests having nowhere to go.

Tim crawled under the rows of standing Thralls. It was a forest of legs
and metal greaves.

He reached the central pumping station for the green Ki-fluid. He opened
the maintenance hatch. He didn't pour poison in. He poured in the iron
filings.

Then, he placed his hand on the pump. *Induction.*

He magnetized the filings.

Inside the fluid, thousands of tiny metal shards clumped together,
forming jagged, abrasive balls. They flowed into the tubes. They flowed
into the Thralls' grafts.

Above him, a Thrall twitched. Then another. The magnetized iron began to
grind inside their artificial joints, scraping against pistons and
gears. It wasn't enough to kill them, but tomorrow? Tomorrow, every step
they took would be agony. Their knees would lock. Their elbows would
seize.

"Friction," Tim whispered. "The enemy of the machine."

Aric stood at the base of the Hive Node. It hummed with a hateful, low
frequency.

He placed his hand on the cold iron. He felt the mind of General Silas
somewhere on the other end, broadcasting a signal of absolute, cold
logic.

Aric closed his eyes. He summoned his Gold Core. But he didn't use the
inspiring light of the sun. He used the terrifying weight of the
eclipse.

Panic. Doubt. Chaos.

He didn't shout. He whispered his intent into the metal. He poured his
own fear---the fear Tim had taught him to use---into the network.

The Node's hum faltered. The rhythm broke.

All around the camp, the sleeping Thralls began to shudder. Their eyes
darted wildly. The perfect silence of the Hive was broken by low,
confused moans.

The Escape

"Time," Tim's voice cut through the link.

"The hounds are waking up," Elara reported. "And they are very hot."

"The Node is poisoned," Aric grimaced, pulling his hand back. "It
feels... oily."

"Rendezvous at the ridge. Go."

They moved. But the camp was waking up.

A siren wailed---a psychic scream from the Node.

"Intruders!" a mechanical voice boomed.

Searchlights snapped on.

"Run!" Tim shouted.

They scrambled up the scree slope. Behind them, chaos erupted.

The coolant lines burst completely. The Warhounds, maddened by the heat
in their chests, tore through their weakened cages. They didn't chase
the intruders. They attacked the nearest source of venting heat---the
Thralls.

The Thralls tried to mobilize, but the iron filings in their joints
seized up. They stumbled, fell, and flailed as the hounds tore into
them.

And through it all, Aric's signal of Panic bounced around their neural
links, making them fire wildly at shadows.

Tim, Elara, and Aric crested the ridge and slid down the other side into
the darkness.

They ran for a mile before stopping to look back.

The Iron-Blood camp was a bonfire of confusion. Green flames roared
where fuel lines had ruptured. The sounds of metal screeching against
metal and the howls of beasts echoed across the plain.

Aric leaned against a rock, panting. He looked at his hands, covered in
ash.

"We didn't kill them all," Aric said.

"No," Tim agreed, watching the fires. "But they won't be sleeping
tonight. And tomorrow... their army will be tired, lame, and afraid."

Elara wiped a speck of green coolant from her cheek. She looked at the
carnage with cold satisfaction.

"We evened the odds," she said.

Tim turned back toward their fort.

"Day Two begins in three hours," Tim said. "Let's get some sleep.
Because they certainly won't."
