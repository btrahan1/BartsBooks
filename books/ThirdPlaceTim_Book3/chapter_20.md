# Chapter 20: The Day of the Lion

The dawn in the Shadow-Scar was a miserable affair. The purple bruising
of the sky simply lightened to the color of a fresh bruise.

Inside the Copper Fort, the air was electric. The students weren't
huddled for warmth anymore. They were checking straps, counting arrows,
and testing the edges of their blades.

Tim stood on the highest pile of rubble. He looked down at his Alloy
Task Force. They were dirty. They were tired. But they looked hungry.

"It is time to show them who we are!" Tim announced. His voice didn't
need the Circuit to carry; the silence of the troops was absolute.

He paced the jagged stones.

"For twenty years, the Academy has played defense. We survived. We
endured. Yesterday, we hid from them. Last night, we hit them when they
were asleep."

He stopped, looking at Cassian, then at Gareth, then at Aric.

"They are hurting. Their joints are grinding. Their beasts are dead or
turned. They are expecting us to sit behind these walls and pray for the
clock to run out."

Tim drew the obsidian dagger Aric had given him.

"They won't expect US to attack THEM!"

A fierce, feral grin spread across Cassian Redgrave's face. "We're going
to rush a legion?"

"We aren't rushing," Tim corrected. "We are driving."

He pointed to Prince Aric.

"Your Highness. Drop the black cloak. Shine."

Aric stepped forward. He unclasped the ash-covered cloak and let it
fall. He closed his eyes and flared his Gold Core. It wasn't the warm,
comforting hum of the night before. It was a blinding, aggressive
radiance.

"Iron Heads," Tim commanded. "Wedge formation. Shield lock. You are the
plow."

"Squad," he looked at Gareth and Lyssandra. "You are the hammer.
Anything the plow turns up, you smash."

"Polish," he looked at Christopher and Mara. "Keep the friction zero. We
don't stop moving."

Tim jumped down from the rocks.

"Open the gates," Tim ordered. "Let's go say good morning."

The Viewing Room

King Thorne leaned forward, his nose almost touching the glass. The
image in the crystal was clear.

The makeshift barricades of the Academy fort were being pushed aside.
And out of the ruins marched the student army.

They weren't sneaking. They weren't using cover.

They were marching in a solid, glowing wedge, straight down the main
road toward the crippled Iron-Blood camp. At the tip of the spear walked
Prince Aric, glowing like a supernova, flanked by the massive form of
Gareth Orso and the swirling ice-winds of Elara Vane.

"What are they doing?" asked the King, his voice a whisper of disbelief.
"They have the defensive advantage. Why leave the fort?"

General Alric stood with his arms crossed, a rare, savage smile cutting
across his scarred face.

"They are not only going to win this battle, Sire," Alric rumbled. "They
are going to win the war."

"Explain," the King demanded.

"The Iron-Blood Empire fights based on calculation," Alric said. "They
calculated that we would hide. They are currently repairing their gear,
expecting a siege. By attacking now, Tim disrupts the calculation. He is
proving that we are not prey."

Alric pointed to the glowing figure of the Prince.

"And he is proving that the Sun has risen in the Shadow-Scar."

The Charge of the Alloy

The Iron-Blood camp was a scene of industrial disaster. Green fires
still smoldered. Thralls were limping, their movements jerky and
grinding as the iron filings tore at their internal gears. Officers
shouted orders that went ignored by the confused, panic-stricken Hive
mind.

Then, they looked up.

On the ridge, a golden sun appeared.

It wasn't the sky clearing. It was the enemy.

"FORWARD!" Aric's voice boomed, amplified by the Gold Core, rolling over
the valley like thunder.

The Academy army charged.

It wasn't a chaotic sprint. It was a terrifyingly organized machine.

The Iron Heads formed the prow of the ship. Their shields were locked,
reinforced by Christopher's slick, blue kinetic barriers.

The Iron-Blood sentries fired their heavy crossbows. The bolts didn't
stick. They hit the "Polish," slid off the frictionless shields, and
clattered harmlessly away.

"CONTACT!" Gareth roared from the front line.

The Academy wedge slammed into the disorganized Iron-Blood perimeter.

It shouldn't have worked. Even damaged, the Thralls were stronger. But
they were slow. Every time a Thrall tried to swing a grafted arm, the
joint seized. *Grind. Lock.*

The Academy students didn't duel.

Cassian Redgrave didn't look for an opening; he shield-bashed a Thrall
who was struggling to lift its arm. Lyssandra didn't cast a spell; she
spiked the ground, tripping three limping soldiers. Gareth didn't smash;
he simply walked *through* them, using his mass to knock the unbalanced
cyborgs over like bowling pins.

And in the center, Tim directed the flow.

"Right flank, push! Left flank, hold! Elara, seal the breach!"

Elara moved like a wraith. A group of Thralls tried to rally? She froze
the ground beneath them. A Necrotic Mage tried to cast? She hit him with
a mental spike of pure cold logic that shattered his concentration.

But the true weapon was Aric.

The Prince walked through the battle, untouched. His Gold light was
agonizing to the dark-adapted eyes of the Empire soldiers. But to the
Academy students, it was fuel. They didn't get tired. They didn't feel
the weight of the gravity. They were surfing on a wave of royal power.

"They're breaking!" Milo shouted, firing an arrow into a gap in a
Thrall's armor.

The Iron-Blood Empire, the unfeeling, unstoppable hive... was routing.

They couldn't process the data. The enemy was supposed to be weak. The
enemy was supposed to be hiding. Why was the enemy glowing? Why were
their own bodies failing?

General Silas, the Empire's commander, watched from his command tent. He
saw his army crumbling. He saw the golden light. And he saw the
grey-clad boy standing in the middle of it all, not fighting, but
*conducting*.

Silas felt something he hadn't felt since his surgery.

Fear.

"Retreat to the secondary line!" Silas screamed into the neural link.
"Abandon the outer camp!"

The Thralls turned and fled, limping and dragging their seized limbs.

The Academy students stopped at the edge of the burning camp. They were
panting, wild-eyed, covered in soot and glory.

Tim raised his hand. "HOLD!"

They stopped instantly. No one chased. No one broke formation.

Tim walked to the front of the line. He looked at the retreating backs
of the monsters. He looked at the burning supplies.

"We took their food," Tim said calmly. "We took their shelter. And we
took their pride."

He turned to his army.

"Now," Tim said, pointing to the massive obsidian tower in the
distance---the source of the Sun-Stone resonance. "We take the prize."
