# Chapter 21: The Immutability of Kings

The Sun-Stone Mine wasn't a hole in the ground. It was a crater of
jagged, black glass, pulsating with a rhythmic, amber heartbeat deep
within the earth.

At the edge of the pit stood General Silas.

He was a horror of efficiency. He had discarded his human legs for
hydraulic pistons. His arms were elongated, tipped with spinning
mono-filament blades. His face was hidden behind a sensory mask of black
glass, fed by tubes of glowing green Ki-fluid. He was the ultimate
expression of the Iron-Blood philosophy: the flesh is weak, the machine
is eternal.

Around him stood the last of his Elite Guard---the "Immortals." Heavily
grafted, stripped of all emotion, they formed a wall of meat and steel.

Tim stood at the head of the Academy wedge. He looked at the terrain. It
was a bottleneck. He couldn't use the "Polish" tactic here; there was no
room to maneuver.

"He's waiting for us," Elara said, her breath freezing in the air.
"Silas. He wants a champion."

"He wants to break our morale," Tim corrected. "He knows Aric is the
battery. If he breaks the Prince, the Carrier Wave dies, and the gravity
of this place crushes us."

Tim looked at Aric. The Prince's armor was dented, scorched, and covered
in soot. But his eyes were clear.

"He is mine," Aric said softly.

"He is faster than you," Tim warned. "He is stronger than you. He has
four arms and no pain receptors."

"I know," Aric said. He drew his sword. The gold filigree was scratched,
revealing the steel beneath. "But he is occupying my land."

Tim looked at the Prince. He checked the flows of Ki in the air.

"Aric," Tim said. "Do you know the third property of Gold?"

Aric paused. "Weight. And Conductivity. There is a third?"

"Go," Tim said, stepping back. "When you need it... I'll tell you."

The Duel

Aric walked into the center of the crater. The ground hummed with the
power of the Sun-Stones beneath.

Silas stepped forward. His pistons hissed.

"Prince," Silas's voice was synthetic, grinding out from a speaker in
his throat. "Biomass: High. Threat: Minimal. You are obsolete."

"I am Aric of Aethelgard," the Prince said, raising his sword. "And I
deny your claim."

Silas didn't monologue. He attacked.

It was a blur of violence. Silas moved with mechanical perfection. His
four arms wove a net of steel.

Aric parried. *Clang. Clang. Clang.*

But he was losing. Silas was too fast. A blade caught Aric's shoulder,
shearing through the pauldrons. Another scored his leg. A kick from a
hydraulic piston sent Aric flying backward, crashing into the obsidian.

"ARIC!" Cassian screamed from the lines, surging forward.

"HOLD!" Tim barked, grabbing Cassian's arm. "Do not interfere."

Aric scrambled up, spitting blood. He flared his Gold Core, sending a
Carrier Wave of courage to himself. He charged.

Silas caught Aric's sword with a mechanical claw. He twisted. The Royal
Blade---forged by the finest smiths---snapped.

Silas backhanded Aric. The Prince tumbled, his helmet flying off. He lay
on the glass, his golden hair matted with blood.

Silas loomed over him. "Inefficient. You bleed. You tire. You fear."

Silas raised a piston-driven spear, aiming for Aric's heart.

In the Viewing Room, the King turned away. "I cannot watch."

"Watch, Sire," General Alric growled. "He is not done."

The Third Property

In the crater, Aric looked up. He saw death coming. He reached for his
Ki, but he was drained. He couldn't push down (Tyrant). He couldn't
broadcast out (Leader). He was empty.

"Tim!" Aric gasped over the Circuit. "I have nothing left!"

"You don't need energy, Aric!" Tim's voice cut through the pain, calm
and absolute. "You are Gold!"

"I am broken!"

"Iron breaks!" Tim shouted. "Copper bends! But Gold? Aric, listen to me.
Why do we use Gold for currency? Why do we use it for crowns?"

The spear was descending.

"Because it doesn't rust!" Tim yelled. "It doesn't tarnish! You can bury
it in the mud for a thousand years, and when you dig it up, *it is still
Gold*."

Tim's voice dropped to a whisper in Aric's mind.

"The Third Power is **Immutability**. The Sovereign does not change for
the world. The world changes for the Sovereign."

Aric's eyes widened.

Immutability.

He didn't try to generate power. He didn't try to cast a spell.

He simply decided, with the absolute arrogance of a King, that *he would
not be moved*.

He locked his Core. He froze his state of being.

Silas thrust the spear.

It hit Aric's chest.

CRUNCH.

But it wasn't bone that crunched.

The spear tip hit Aric's skin---which was glowing now, not with light,
but with a solid, opaque golden sheen. It looked like he had been dipped
in molten metal.

The spear shattered.

Silas froze, his sensors whirring. "Error. Hardness exceeds tungsten."

Aric stood up. He didn't hold a weapon. He didn't need one. He *was* the
weapon. He was a statue of solid, immutable reality.

"You called me obsolete," Aric said. His voice didn't echo; it just
*was*. It occupied the entire auditory spectrum.

Silas struck again. All four arms. Blades, claws, pistons.

They hit Aric.

The blades snapped. The claws bent. The pistons seized against an object
that refused to accept kinetic energy transfer.

Aric walked forward.

He didn't dodge. He walked through the storm of steel.

"You grafted iron to your flesh to become strong," Aric stated, grabbing
one of Silas's mechanical arms.

Silas tried to pull away. His motors whined. He couldn't move. Aric was
an anchor point in the universe.

"But iron rusts," Aric whispered.

Aric's Gold Core didn't push. It *overwrote*.

He poured his Immutability into Silas.

"Error!" Silas screamed, his voice modulating wildly. "System integrity
failing! Rejection! Rejection!"

The gold light flooded Silas's grafts. The foreign biology, the stolen
Ki, the mechanical enhancements---they were all "impurities" in the face
of the Gold.

And Gold does not alloy with trash.

The grafts began to reject. Bolts popped. Flesh tore away from metal.
The Ki-fluid boiled because it couldn't exist in the same space as the
Sovereign Intent.

Silas fell to his knees, his body literally falling apart as his stolen
strength was purged by the presence of the Prince.

Aric stood over him. He looked down at the pile of twitching machinery
and flesh.

"I am Aric," the Prince said. "And I am the Standard."

He placed his hand on Silas's forehead.

"**Break.**"

Silas collapsed. The machine died. The man inside---what little was left
of him---went still.

Aric stood alone in the crater. The golden sheen faded from his skin,
leaving him bruised, bloody, but standing.

He looked up at the ridge, where Tim stood.

Aric raised a shaking fist.

"FOR AETHELGARD!"

The roar from the Academy students shook the obsidian pillars.

The Aftermath

Tim ran down the slope, Elara right behind him. They reached Aric just
as his knees gave out. Gareth caught him before he hit the ground.

"Did you see?" Aric wheezed, grinning through bloody teeth. "I broke the
spear. With my chest."

"I saw," Tim said, checking Aric's pulse. "Immutability. You became a
physical constant, Aric. For ten seconds, you were the hardest thing in
the universe."

"It hurt," Aric admitted.

"Being King usually does," Tim said.

He looked at the scattered remains of the Iron-Blood army. Without
Silas, the Hive mind had shattered. The Thralls were standing around,
confused, drooling, no longer fighting.

"We won," Elara said, looking at the Sun-Stone tower. "The Trial is
over."

Tim looked at the confused Thralls. He looked at the shivering,
victorious students.

"The battle is over," Tim corrected.

He walked over to a supply crate. He pulled out a loaf of bread.

He walked up to a confused, terrifying Thrall who was staring at its own
mechanical claw.

Tim held out the bread.

"Now," Tim said, "the hard part begins."

Epilogue: The Viewing Room

General Alric slumped against the wall, wiping sweat from his brow. "He
did it. The boy... the Prince... they did it."

King Thorne stared at the screen. He saw his son, broken but alive,
being carried by a giant. He saw the Ice Witch securing the perimeter.
And he saw the Peasant Commander feeding the enemy.

"They didn't just win the Sun-Stone," the King whispered. "They broke
the Iron-Blood Empire's ideology."

He turned to the General.

"Prepare the Honors," the King commanded. "And Alric?"

"Sire?"

"Get that boy a new uniform," the King said, pointing at Tim. "I think
he's outgrown the grey wool."

Alric smiled. "I don't think he'll wear velvet, Sire."

"Then find him something else," the King said. "Something that
conducts."

Epilogue: The Weight of a Name

The Grand Throne Room of Aethelgard was a cavern of white marble and
hanging silk banners, designed to make anyone who entered feel small.

Today, however, the room felt crowded.

The entire court was assembled. Dukes, Duchesses, Generals, and
Ministers lined the walls, dressed in their finest silks. But their eyes
were glued to the center aisle, where a carpet of crimson velvet had
been rolled out.

The heavy oak doors groaned open.

They didn't march in to music. They marched in to the sound of three
hundred boots hitting the floor in perfect, terrifying unison.

The Alloy Task Force had refused to change. They wore their battle gear.
The dents in the Royal Guard's plate armor were visible. The scorch
marks on the mages' robes were fresh. The mud of the Shadow-Scar still
clung to Gareth's boots.

They looked like a raw wound in the center of a pristine painting.

At the front walked Prince Aric. He held his helmet under his arm. His
golden hair was matted, and a jagged bruise darkened his jaw, but he
walked with a solidity that the court had never seen before. He didn't
strut. He advanced.

To his right walked Lady Elara Vane, her expression cool and unreadable,
her presence commanding a silence as deep as winter.

And to his left walked Tim.

He wore his grey wool tunic, stained with soot and sweat. The silver
Marshal's band on his arm was tarnished. He looked tired. He looked
ordinary.

And yet, as he passed, the Dukes bowed. Not out of protocol, but out of
instinct. They sensed the current running through him.

They reached the foot of the dais. King Thorne sat upon the Sun Throne,
General Alric standing at his right hand. Grandmaster Valdis stood to
the left, his chest swelled so much with pride he looked ready to burst.

The students knelt.

"Rise, my son," King Thorne said softly.

Aric stood. He looked his father in the eye. "I bring you the Sun-Stone,
Father. And I bring you the victory."

"You bring me much more than that," the King said, his voice thick with
emotion. "You bring yourself back."

The King stood up. He descended the stairs of the dais. He didn't stop
at Aric. He walked past his son, past Elara, and stopped in front of
Tim.

Tim remained kneeling, his head bowed.

"Look at me," the King commanded.

Tim looked up. His brown eyes were clear, holding that faint, warm hum
of the Copper Core.

"General Alric tells me you defeated an army," the King said.
"Grandmaster Valdis tells me you reinvented warfare. And my son..." The
King glanced at Aric. "...my son tells me you saved his soul."

"I just carried the bags, Your Majesty," Tim said quietly. "And
facilitated the logistics."

A ripple of laughter went through the Squad behind him. It was a
nervous, loving sound.

"Logistics," the King mused. "Is that what we call miracles now?"

The King held out his hand. General Alric stepped forward, holding a
velvet cushion. On it lay not a sword, but a coat.

It was long, tailored in the style of a commander's longcoat. But it
wasn't silk or velvet. It was woven from a strange, shimmering
material---a blend of high-grade wool and threads of pure, spun copper.

"Iron protects," the King said, picking up the coat. "Gold rules. But
Copper connects."

He draped the coat over Tim's shoulders. It was heavy, warm, and
conductive. Tim felt his Ki react to it instantly, the coat amplifying
his induction field like a second skin.

"You have no House," the King noted. "You have no lands. You have no
name but the one you gave yourself at the gate."

The King drew the Sword of State. He rested the flat of the blade on
Tim's shoulder.

"The nobility usually takes a name from their conquests," the King
announced to the silent room. "But you did not conquer the enemy. You
changed them. You did not conquer the Academy. You united it."

The King smiled.

"So you shall keep the name that you forged in the mud. Let it be a
reminder to every noble in this room that a title is not a measure of
worth, but a measure of service."

The King tapped the other shoulder.

"Rise, Sir 'Just Tim'."

Tim swallowed. He felt the weight of the words. He stood up. The copper
coat settled around him.

"Sir Just Tim," the heralds boomed. "Knight of the Copper Order. Warden
of the Alloy."

The applause started with Gareth. It sounded like two boulders clapping
together. Then Christopher joined in, then Aric, then Valdis. Within
seconds, the Throne Room was shaking with the roar of it.

Tim looked at Elara. She was smiling---a real, genuine smile.

"Just Tim," she whispered. "It has a ring to it."

Tim looked out at the sea of faces. He saw the power. He saw the
politics. He saw the work that still needed to be done to integrate the
Iron-Blood refugees, to fix the Kingdom, to keep the peace.

He took a breath. *Inhale. Accept.*

He adjusted his new coat.

"It's a good name," Tim said, his voice drowned out by the cheers, but
heard perfectly by his friends. "It reminds me that I'm still just the
guy who needs to fix the oven."

He looked at his Squad.

"Come on," Sir Just Tim said. "I hear the Royal Kitchen has a
ventilation problem. Let's get to work."

THE END
