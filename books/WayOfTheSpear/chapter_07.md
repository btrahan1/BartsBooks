# Chapter 7: The Unbounding

The silver crystalline sound was still echoing in Bran’s mind as the second page of *The Way of the Spear* fell open. It didn't just turn; it seemed to unfold, the silk material expanding until it covered a space twice as wide as the previous chapters. 

Bran, leaning against the cold stone wall of his cell, wiped a smudge of forge-soot from his cheek and stared. 

He had expected more spear forms. He had expected diagrams of the Pulse he had felt at the forge—the rhythmic explosion of intent into muscle. He had expected to see how to strike, how to parry, how to become the warrior Gaul was.

Instead, he found himself looking at what appeared to be a manual for a contortionist.

On the first page were two diagrams, rendered in the familiar shimmering gold, blue, and garnet light. The first showed the figure in a standing forward fold. Its knees were locked perfectly straight, its feet together, but its upper body was draped forward like a piece of wet linen. Most shockingly, the figure’s palms weren't just touching the floor; they were pressed flat against it, the fingers spread wide. Bran looked at his own stiff, powerful legs and felt a twinge of phantom pain. He had never even managed to touch his toes without a significant bend in his knees.

The image next to it was even more baffling. It was the same figure, but its back was curved into a deep, impossible arc. Its forehead was pressed firmly against its own knees, the arms wrapped around the calves to pull the torso into a state of total compression. It looked less like a human and more like a folded piece of parchment. There was no blue muscle shown in the shoulders; instead, the diagram focused on the garnet architecture of the spine, showing each vertebra separated and aligned in a perfect, graceful curve.

Bran’s jaw dropped. "What the hell?"

He turned his gaze to the second page, which housed two more images that defied the logic of the monastery’s physical world. 

The first showed the figure—a man, undeniably a man with the same dense musculature Bran was developing—sitting in a full, flat split. His legs were stretched out in a straight line, his pelvis resting directly on the ground. There was no strain shown in the blue fibers of the inner thighs; they were depicted as long, fluid ribbons of light, as elastic as the cured sinew the hunters used for their bows.

The final image was the most terrifying. It was a backbend. The figure was arched into a perfect 'C' shape, its feet planted firmly on the ground while its body curved backward until its palms were also flat on the floor behind it. The golden threads of intent were shown pulsing through the entire length of the arched spine, creating a circuit of energy that had no beginning and no end.

"How?" Bran whispered, his voice cracking. 

He looked at his hands, calloused and thickened by a month of laundry and a week of forge-work. He felt the garnet density of his bones, the blue power of his shoulders. He was strong. He was efficient. He was a mule that had been reinforced with iron.

But he was stiff. He was a pillar of stone. 

He set the book on his straw mattress and stood up in the center of the cell. The space was barely six feet square, the air smelling of old hay and damp stone. He took a deep, abdominal breath, the Wind cycle he’d mastered at the bellows filling his core.

*Fuel the engine,* he thought.

He locked his knees, feet together, and reached for the floor.

His fingertips didn't even pass his mid-shins.

Bran groaned, his hamstrings screaming in protest. The blue fibers of his legs, so efficient at hauling water barrels, felt like cables of dried leather. He tried to push, to force the reach, but his lower back locked up, a sharp warning of "No" echoing through his garnet frame.

He sat back down, frustrated. He looked at the diagram of the palms-flat fold. The text at the bottom of the page, which had been hidden in the shadows, suddenly caught the light of his dying candle.

*The Spear that cannot bend is a Spear that will snap. True Density is not Rigidity. It is Fluidity compressed. The River flows because it is Unbound. The Wind strikes because it has no form.*

Bran closed his eyes, his mind racing. He thought of the forge. He thought of the way the iron bar, once heated to the 'Soft Peach' level, could be bent into any shape. When it was cold, it was brittle. When it was Wind and Pulse, it was plastic.

"I’m too cold," he realized. "I’ve built the density, but I haven't built the heat."

The Pulse he had felt at the forge—the rhythmic strike of intent—wast just for power. It was for *transformation*. He realized that the diagrams weren't just poses to be mimicked; they were channels. By folding the body into these shapes, the golden intent and the Wind of the breath were forced to travel through the areas of greatest resistance, effectively melting the stiffness of the muscles.

He tried again. But this time, he didn't just reach. 

He breathed.

He took a deep, low inhale, filling his belly. He felt the Wind pressing against his lower spine. As he exhaled, he didn't push. He *released*. He imagined the blue fibers of his hamstrings as the ox-hide of the bellows, becoming soft and pliable.

He reached down.

His fingertips brushed the tops of his feet. 

It was a small victory, but the pain was still there—a hot, searing line that ran from his heels to his pelvis. 

"The Unbounding," he murmured, reading the title heading he had missed. 

For the next three hours, Bran worked in the dark. He didn't try to achieve the full poses. He knew better than that now. He was a student of the increments. 

He worked on the forward fold, centimeter by painful centimeter. He used the Pulse to send waves of warmth into his locked joints. He discovered that if he timed his release with the natural ebb of his heartbeat, the muscles would give just a fraction more.

Then, he tried the splits. 

He sat on the floor, legs spread as wide as they would go—which, at that moment, was barely a ninety-degree angle. He felt a deep, terrifying pull in the center of his hips. He visualized the interconnection of his pelvis, the way the garnet bones were supposed to rotate to allow the blue ribbons to stretch.

"Why is this necessary for a spear?" he asked the shadows.

He thought of Gaul. The lunge in the clearing. The way the man’s body had extended, his lead leg stretching out until his hips were nearly at the level of the target. It wasn't just a step; it was a deployment of the entire kinetic range of the body. If Gaul hadn't have had that flexibility, the strike would have lacked half its reach and all of its fluid recovery.

Flexibility was the ultimate economy. It meant that the body wasn't fighting itself. When Bran hauled the cart, half of his strength was being wasted just overcoming the internal resistance of his own stiff muscles. If he were Unbound, every ounce of his power could be directed outward.

As the first bells of dawn began to ring, Bran was still on the floor. He was sweating now, despite the chill of the morning. His hamstrings felt like they had been beaten with a smith’s hammer, and his back was a map of unfamiliar aches. 

But he could touch the floor with his knuckles. 

He stood up, his legs feeling strangely light, almost disconnected from the earth. He walked to the window and looked out at the mist-covered courtyard.

He saw the other novices beginning their morning chores. They were moving with the same stiff, heavy gaits Bran had once shared. They were bound by their own bodies, by the generic expectations of the monastery.

Bran took a deep, bellows-breath. He felt the Pulse at the base of his spine, stronger now, more resonant. 

He leaned back, just a little, testing the 'C' curve. His spine popped—a sequence of rapid-fire cracks that sounded like a string of small firecrackers. For a second, he saw stars. But as the dizziness cleared, he felt a sudden, cool flow of energy through his chest. 

*The Channel,* he thought.

He didn't have the palms-flat fold yet. He didn't have the splits. He was nowhere near the back-bend.

But the gate was no longer just creaking. It was open.

"Unbound," he whispered.

He picked up his wooden buckets and headed for the river. He didn't just walk today. He moved with a subtle, oscillating hip-swing that allowed his legs to reach just a few inches further with every stride.

The other monks would definitely notice this change. A smiling mule was one thing. A mule that moved with the fluid grace of a stalking predator was something else entirely.

Bran didn't care. He had four diagrams to master. And he had a feeling that the rotation of the tide was going to feel very, very different once his body finally learned how to bend.
