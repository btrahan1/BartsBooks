# Chapter 10: The Healer's Touch

The darkness inside the tent was profound, a thick, velvet weight that smelled of spent paraffin and old sawdust. The audience had long since filtered out into the cool Oakhaven night, their voices fading into the distance. But Bran remained. He stood near the edge of the circular stage, his feet grounded in a subtle, coiling stance, his eyes fixed on the empty space where the silver dancer had soared.

His soul was on fire. He wasn't trying to mimic her—he was far too disciplined for that—but his mind was replaying every spin, every extension, every resonance of her movement. He was tracing the echoes of her fluidity, mapping it to the unbounding diagrams in his memory, seeing the golden lines of purpose that must have guided her flight.

Then, from behind the heavy velvet curtains that screened the backstage area, he heard it. A soft, sharp groan of pain. 

Bran’s breathing hitched. His awareness, usually so focused on his own center, flared with a different kind of sensitivity—not as a warrior, but as a boy who had spent years listening to the sighs of the wounded in the monastery infirmary.

"I am so sorry, Lady Elana," a man’s voice whispered, thick with regret. "I didn't mean to set you down so awkwardly after that leap! The sawdust was thinner than I thought."

"Shh, Kael," another voice answered—the dancer. Her voice was musical even in pain, but there was a ragged edge to it that sent a protective shiver through Bran. "It’s just... a twist. I felt it go when I landed. Rubbing it won't help if the alignment is off."

Bran hesitated. The monks had taught him that curiosity was a vice, but Father Thomas had also taught him that ignoring the suffering of others was a sin. He took a deep, abdominal breath, centering his garnet frame, and stepped through the split in the curtains.

The backstage area was lit by a single, guttering lantern resting on a packing crate. Lady Elana was sitting on a low stool, her leg propped up on another crate. She had removed her silver slipper, and her ankle was already beginning to swell, the azure tissues underneath looking tight and angry in the dim light. The male performer, Kael—a man as tall and dense as Bran had imagined—was kneeling beside her, his face a mask of worry.

He looked up as the curtain rustled, his eyes sharpening. He stood in a single, fluid motion, his hand reaching for a short-sword resting near the crate.

"Who are you?" Kael demanded, his voice a low, dangerous warning.

Bran stopped, his hands visible. He didn't look like a threat. He was a thirteen-year-old boy in a mud-streaked monastery tunic, but he stood with a stillness that Kael’s trained eyes couldn't ignore.

"I... I meant no harm," Bran said softly. "I was in the audience. I heard... I heard the injury."

Elana looked at him, her piercing green eyes studying the boy. She saw the way his shoulders were set, the way his breath was deep and rhythmic. She saw the golden focus in his eyes.

"He’s just a boy, Kael," she said, her voice softening. She turned back to her ankle, a fresh wince crossing her face. "Go back to the wagons. I need to get this bound."

Bran stepped forward, his heart hammering against his ribs. "Wait. Perhaps... perhaps I can help just a bit?"

Kael snorted, his hand still near the sword. "And what does a monastery mule know of the Sovereigns' injuries? Unless you have a healing scroll hidden in that tunic, boy, you’re of no use."

Bran didn't flinch. He looked directly at Elana’s ankle. He didn't see just the swelling. He saw the architecture beneath. He saw the alignment of the talus bone against the scarlet foundation of her frame. He saw where the blue tendons had been pulled out of their natural, elastic channel.

"I have a smattering of the healing arts," Bran said, his voice gaining strength. "But more than that... I understand the structure."

He didn't wait for permission. He knelt where Kael had been. 

"Don't touch her—" Kael began, but Elana held up a hand. 

"Let him," she whispered. "Look at his hands, Kael. They aren't the hands of a peasant."

Bran reached out. He didn't just grab the ankle. He placed his palms flat on either side of her calf, his touch light as a feather. He closed his eyes and initiated the Pulse.

He sent a wave of warmth—the soft peach heat he’d mastered at the bellows—into his own palms. He didn't try to magic the injury away. He used the warmth to melt the rigidity of the protective muscle spasms. 

*Shift the blue. Align the garnet. Feed with the gold.*

"You're... you're breathing with me," Elana gasped. 

Bran realized she was right. Without even thinking about it, he had synchronized his Wind cycle with her staggered, pained breaths. He was using his own rhythm to steady hers, creating a resonant circuit between them.

He felt the Talus bone—the garnet anchor of the ankle. It was indeed out of alignment, shifted just a few millimeters. The tendons were being pinched, causing the blue fibers to scream. 

"On the next exhale," Bran murmured.

He didn't pull. He didn't yank. He waited for the exact moment when Elana's own Pulse reached its lowest ebb. 

He applied a subtle, coiling leverage to her heel. 

*Click.*

It was a small, almost silent sound, but in the stillness of the tent, it resonated like a bell. 

Elana let out a long, shuddering breath. The tension in her leg vanished. The azure fibers, no longer pinched by the garnet bones, began to relax. 

Bran didn't stop. He kept his hands in place, continuing to send the pulse of warmth into the joint to manage the swelling. He visualized the Perfect Image of her ankle and held that image in his mind, using his radiant center to guide her body’s natural recovery.

After a few minutes, he pulled his hands away. He was sweating, his face pale from the effort of the synchronization. 

"Try it now," Bran said.

Elana slowly stood up. She tested the weight. Her ankle was still sore, still tender, but the sharp, stabbing agony was gone. She moved into a deep, unbound squat, her balance perfect.

"The alignment... it’s perfect," she whispered, looking at Bran with an expression of pure awe. "Even the High Healers of the South Reach usually just numb the pain. You... you put the bones back where they belonged."

Kael stepped forward, his eyes no longer dangerous. He looked at Bran with a mixture of respect and confusion. "I’ve never seen a healer do that. No light. No chants. Just... touch."

"It's not magic," Bran said, standing up. He felt a new kind of realization in his own mind—the understanding that his anatomical study wasn't just for his own progression. It was a tool for preservation. "It's just... the way things are supposed to be."

Elana sat back down, reaching for a silver slipper. She looked at Bran’s mud-streaked tunic, then at his hands, which were now perfectly steady.

"Who are you, boy?" she asked. "And where did you learn the Way of the Body?"

Bran touched the book hidden in his tunic. He thought of Gaul. He thought of the laundry stones and the forge. 

"I’m just a mule from the monastery," Bran said, his voice clear. "But I'm starting to learn that a mule can see things a master might miss."

Elana laughed—a bright, silver sound that warmed the cold tent. She reached into a small velvet pouch at her waist and pulled out a small, shimmering coin. It wasn't copper or gold; it was a pearlescent material that seemed to hold the light of the performers' dance.

"This is a Sovereign's Token," she said, pressing it into Bran’s hand. "If you ever find yourself in the South, or if you ever tire of the monastery’s grey walls... come find us. Any performer of the Road will recognize the mark."

Bran looked at the token. It felt light, yet filled with a potential that matched the Wind in his chest.

"Thank you, Lady Elana," he said, bowing low.

"Don't thank me, Bran of the Monastery," she said, her eyes twinkling. "You’ve given me back my flight. Now, go. Before whoever your masters are comes looking for their 'mule'."

Bran turned and walked out of the tent. He didn't look back. He walked through the dark, quiet town of Oakhaven, his gait a fluid, rhythmic glide. 

He wasn't just a student of anatomy anymore. He was a student of the Pulse of others. 

As he reached the cliff path, the monastery flickering in the distance like a group of dying embers, Bran reached into his tunic. The third page of the book was still sealed. But for the first time, he didn't feel the need to rush it. 

He didn't just want to turn the page. He wanted to be the story.

He was thirteen years old. He was a healer. He was a spear.

And as he looked up at the stars, Bran realized that the mountain he was climbing didn't just reach the sky. It *was* the sky. And for the first time, he felt like he might actually have the wings to fly.
