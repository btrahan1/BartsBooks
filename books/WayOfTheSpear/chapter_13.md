# Chapter 13: The Sovereigns' Circle

The Sovereigns’ camp was a vibrant, unsettling organism sprawled across the river embankment just outside the town of Oakhaven. It wasn't the grey, static order of the monastery; it was a kaleidoscope of striped canvas, the smell of woodsmoke, and the constant, rhythmic jingle of harness bells.

As the heavy wagon rolled to a stop, Bran felt the breath in his chest tighten—not from labor, but from a profound, terrifying nervousness. He clutched his small bundle of belongings, his fingers tracing the edges of his books through the thin fabric.

"Breathe, Bran," Master Silas said, hopping down from the driver's seat with a lightness that made the gravel crunch musically. "The road is just a stage that never ends. And right now, the curtain is going up."

Bran stepped down from the wagon, his feet finding their grounded stance instinctively as they touched the embankment. He didn't look like the boy who had spent twelve years hauling water buckets. Even in his patched monks' tunic and mud-stained trousers, there was an undeniable presence to him. His shoulders were wide, his neck thick with corded muscle, and his posture was so precisely aligned that he looked like he had been carved from the same Ironwall stone as the monastery.

The murmurs began almost immediately.

"Is that him?" 

Bran turned his head slightly, his golden focus picking up the voices of three young performers leaning against a brightly painted cart. They were dressed in practice gear—thin, form-fitting trousers and vests that showcased their lean, agile frames.

"Silas said he was bringing a boy from the monastery," more of a whisper than a shout, but in the focused clarity of Bran’s mind, it was loud enough to hear. "I thought he’d be... smaller. A child."

"He doesn't look like a child," another replied, her eyes tracking the way Bran’s weight shifted as he adjusted his bundle. "Look at the way he stands. It’s like he’s part of the ground."

"I thought he was a mule," the first one added, a hint of skepticism in his voice. "He looks more like a wrestler."

Bran felt the heat rise in his cheeks. Usually, the "mule" comments were insults intended to put him in his place. Here, they felt like observations of a strange, alien species. He realized, with a start, that his thirteen years of labor—combined with his study of the Spear—had transformed him into something he hadn't quite recognized in the monastery’s dim mirrors. He wasn't a man yet, but he was no longer just a boy. He was on the road to becoming something he didn't have a name for yet.

He forced himself to smile and wave at the group, a tentative, monastery-trained gesture of goodwill.

"Hello," he said, his voice cracking just a bit.

The performers waved back, their curiosity still evident, but the tension in the air didn't break. Bran felt like a new animal being introduced to a well-established pack. He was the healer-mule, the one who had supposedly reset Elana’s ankle with a single touch.

"Don't worry, Bran," Silas said, clapping him on the shoulder. He felt the solidness of the boy’s frame and grinned. "I am sure you will fit in quite nicely once they see you wring a horse-blanket or haul a crate of stage-weights. These people value efficiency above all else. And you, my young friend, are the most efficient thing I’ve seen since the last steam-engine was unearthed in the South."

Bran nodded, but the pulse in his core was still erratic. He looked around the camp. He saw jugglers practicing with wooden clubs, their movements a blur of fluid grace. He saw a man breathing fire into a stone pit, his breathing controlled and violent. He saw the Perfect Image in a dozen different forms, and every single one of them made him feel like a clumsy novice.

"Oh look," Silas added, his voice dropping to a warmer tone. "There's someone who wants to meet you again."

Bran turned toward the center of the camp, where a larger, more ornate tent of silver silk stood near the river’s edge. Lady Elana was stepping out of the shadows.

She wasn't wearing her stage costume today. She wore a simple, flowing robe of deep green, her copper-colored hair tied back in a practical braid. But she didn't need the silver silk to command the air. She walked toward them with a gait that was so fluid, so unbound, that Bran felt a sharp, sympathetic resonance in his own hips.

She wasn't limping. 

"Bran!" she called out, her voice a bright, silver bell in the dusty camp.

Bran bowed—the deep, fluid fold that Father Thomas had called a "madman’s dance." This time, he didn't do it out of habit or for the benefit of the monks. He did it because he truly respected the flight she represented.

"Lady Elana," he said, rising.

"Save the 'Lady' for the audience, Bran of the Monastery," she laughed, coming to a stop in front of him. She reached out and touched his shoulder, her fingers light but firm. "I’ve been testing my ankle all morning. I practiced the triple pirouette four times before breakfast. Not a twinge. Not a single catch."

She looked him up and down, her green eyes twinkling with a sharp intelligence. "Silas told me you were coming. I didn't think you’d actually do it. Leaving the stone walls is a difficult thing for a mule."

"The mule is dead, Elana," Bran said, his voice surprisingly steady. "I’m just a boy on the road now."

"A boy on the road with the hands of a master," she corrected him. She turned to Silas. "Is he settled yet?"

"Just arrived," Silas said. "I was just about to show him the baggage wagon. Since he’s our official 'Specialist,' I thought he should have at least a corner to call his own."

"Nonsense," Elana said, her gaze returning to Bran. "He can’t be stuck with the mules and the crates. He needs to see the rehearsals. If he’s going to understand the arts of the body, he needs to see what the body is capable of when it isn't hauling laundry."

She turned back to Bran. "Come. The others think you’re just a strong back I hired to save Silas’s gold. I want to show them why I actually wanted you here."

Bran looked at Silas, who gave him an encouraging wink. He looked back at Elana, whose presence felt like a pulse of pure, unadulterated energy.

He gripped his bundle tighter. The nervousness was still there, a cold, fluttering thing in his stomach. But as he followed Elana toward the rehearsal circle, he felt a new sensation. 

It was the feeling of a page ready to turn. 

Behind him, he could still hear the murmurs of the troupe. 

*Is that him?*
*I thought he was a boy!*

Bran didn't mind the questions anymore. He knew part of the answer. He was the one who had fixed the dancer. He was the one who had survived the monks. And now, he was the one who was going to learn how to fly.

He stepped into the Sovereigns' Circle, the dust of the road rising around his boots like the mist of the Sacred Fountain. But this wasn't sacred water. It was the world.

And Bran, the healer-mule who was becoming a man, was finally ready to be seen.
