# Chapter 22: The First Weapon

The morning mist was a cold, clinging shroud as Gaul and Bran set out from their mountain camp. The Ironwall peaks were just starting to catch the first pale light of the dawn, turning the grey granite into jagged spires of orange and gold.

Bran walked half a step behind Gaul, his bundle slung over his shoulder. Even with the weight of his meager belongings and the two silk books, his gait was effortless. He and his master moved in a silent synchronization, their footfalls light and precise, their breathing drawing the thin mountain air into their lungs with rhythmic efficiency.

"We are heading to a town nearby," Gaul said, breaking the silence as they reached the primary trade road that cut through the high pass. His voice wasn't a roar today; it was a low, instructional hum that competed with the morning wind. "They have an adventurer's guild there. We have to get you registered."

Bran looked up, curiosity flickering in his eyes. "The Guild, Master? I thought we were heading into the wilderness for training."

"The wilderness is for the body, Bran," Gaul said, not slowing his pace. "But the world expects a name and a rank for anyone who carries a weapon outside of a lord's service. The social machinery is a thing of categories. If you are not an 'Adventurer,' you are a 'Vagrant.' And a vagrant with your level of presence is an invitation for the local militia to start asking questions we don't want to answer."

Bran nodded. He understood the logic. The monastery had been a world of ranks—Acolyte, Novice, Brother, Abbot. The Sovereigns had been a world of roles—Lead, Base, Specialist. The Guild was just another layer of the structure he would have to navigate.

As they walked along the winding road, the sun finally cleared the ridges, burning away the mist and revealing the vast, sweeping valleys of the South Reach below them. Gaul gestured to the landscape, the movement as sharp and economical as a spear-thrust.

"A spear is a stick with a point, Bran," Gaul said, his gaze fixed on the horizon. "Nothing more, nothing less. It is the simplest geometry of violence."

Bran looked at the master’s empty hands, then at his own. He picked up a long, sturdy branch of mountain-ash that had been cast aside by a winter storm near the road's edge. It was about six feet long, weathered but still flexible, with a weight that felt right in his steady grip.

Gaul glanced back, saw the branch, and a small, grim smile touched his lips. He reached into his belt and pulled out a small, folding knife—a blade of dark, hammered steel with an edge that shimmered like ice. He handed it to Bran.

"Of course," Gaul added, his grin widening just a fraction, "some spears are better than others."

Bran took the knife. He felt the weight of the steel, the resonance of the smith who had forged it. He began to shave the bark from the tip of the ash branch, his movements a series of fluid transitions that removed the grey skin in long, clean curls.

"Pound for pound, Bran, humans are incredibly weak when it comes to the beasts of this world," Gaul continued, his voice taking on the tone of a lecture. "Our skins are thin, our claws are nonexistent, and our teeth are meant for grain and cooked meat. A man without a weapon would fall quickly to a mountain cat half his size. A bear could crush your skeletal frame before you even found your grounding."

*Swish.* Bran removed a thick knot from the branch. 
*Swish.* He began to taper the tip into a needle-sharp point.

"Man’s first weapon was a rock," Gaul said. "A heavy, blunt expression of gravity. It worked, but it required you to be close. His second was a club—a longer rock, essentially. It gave him reach, but it still required the brute force of a heavy strike."

"His third was a spear."

Bran stopped shaving for a second, the ash branch resting against his shoulder. He looked at the point he was carving. It was simple. Elegant. A straight line ending in a single, lethal intersection.

"From that point on, Bran, man dominated," Gaul said, his voice rising with a resonance that made the nearby pines seem to lean in. "The spear was the first true extension of intent. It allowed a weak creature to strike from outside the beast’s reach. It turned the solidity of a man’s frame into a single, focused point of pressure."

"A rock kills with weight. A club kills with impact. A spear kills with *precision*."

Bran resumed his work, his breathing slowing as he focused on the final sharpening of the tip. He applied a delicate coiling leverage to his wrist, the knife moving with a delicacy that would have impressed Father Thomas. 

"You've learned to hold the world," Gaul said, looking at the boy's work. "You’ve learned to move through it. Now, you must learn to change it. The spear is not just a tool; it is the bridge between your internal rhythm and the external reality."

Bran finished the point. He held the makeshift spear at its balance point, his golden focus tracing the line of force from his heels, through his core, and into the tip of the ash branch. 

It wasn't a masterwork. It was just a stick with a point. But as the sun caught the pale, fresh wood of the tip, Bran felt a structural realization that was deeper and more dangerous than any he had felt in the rehearsal tent. 

It was the settling of the first weapon into his soul.

"We will reach Oakhaven’s Lower Gate by midday," Gaul said, turning back to the road. "Put the knife away. You aren't a wood-carver anymore, Bran."

Bran folded the knife and tucked it into his belt. He gripped the ash branch, his fingers finding the natural grain of the wood. He didn't carry it like a walking stick. He carried it like a part of his own body.

"I’m ready, Master Gaul," Bran said.

As they descended into the valley, the sounds of civilization beginning to rise from the town below—the clatter of wagons, the distant ring of a guild-bell—Bran didn't feel like a specialist or a performer. 

He felt like the first man, stepping out of the shadows with a sharpened stick, ready to see if the world was as solid as the foundation he had built within himself.

The road was descending. The Guild was waiting. 

And for Bran, the boy who had carved his own path, the first true strike was now only a matter of time.
