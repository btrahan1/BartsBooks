# Chapter 25: The Vow of Protection

The journey through the Oakhaven streets was a slow one. They headed down the road, the crowd parting before them with a new, profound silence after the confrontation with the Praetorians. But the silence didn't last.

Three times before they reached the town’s outer perimeter, Bran stopped. He looked around, his perceptive gaze scanning the crowd, then found his target.

The first was near the southern cistern. Bran’s eyes narrowed, his intuition catching a micro-discordance in the gait of an old woman carrying a heavy bundle of firewood. 

"Pardon me, Master," he said, already moving effortlessly away from Gaul’s side.

He approached the woman, his hands moving with fluid grace. He didn't speak; he simply touched her shoulder, a focused warmth helping her realign the load that was slowly crushing her thin garnet frame. He adjusted the knot of her wrap, nodded, and returned to the master.

The second was a young boy crying near a butcher’s stall. Bran stopped, turned, and reached down. He saw the stubborn blockage in the child's throat—a piece of unchewed gristle. Without a word, he applied a precise, upward leverage to the boy's diaphragm. The gristle flew out, and before the mother could even shout, Bran was already back at Gaul's side.

"Pardon me, Master," he said quietly.

The third was a former soldier sitting on a bench near the gate, his leg a mangled map of old scar-tissue that was currently cramping in the midday heat. Bran halted. He looked at the man, the geometric patterns of the old injury clear in his mind. He walked over, knelt, and applied a series of resonant strikes to the man's calf—not to hurt, but to vibrate the physical tension out of the locked muscle.

The soldier gasped, his leg finally straightening for the first time in hours. He looked at Bran in shock, but the boy had already rejoined Gaul.

"Pardon me, Master," Bran said.

Gaul just watched, helplessly, as his student did these things, moving with a care and precision that had absolutely nothing to do with the martial arts. He just stopped, turned, and approached, without a care in the world for the optics or the delay. Each time he simply rejoined his master as if helping a stranger was as natural as breathing.

Gaul frowned each time, a deep, troubled shadow crossing his face. The Way of the Spear was for killing. Period. It was the art of the ultimate strike, the finality of the point. He looked at the boy who couldn't walk past a broken toe or a cramped leg without seeking to mend it.

How, Gaul wondered as they finally stepped through the Oakhaven gates and onto the open mountain road, would he ever teach this boy to kill?

As they finally exited the town and resumed their journey, the noise of Oakhaven fading into the wind of the high pass, Gaul sighed. The weight of his own history and the boy's strange, compassionate precision felt like two conflicting forces in his chest.

"What do you want out of life, Bran?" Gaul asked, his voice low and weary.

Bran didn't answer for a while. He walked with his liquid, effortless gait, the mountain-ash branch balanced perfectly in his hand. He looked at the peaks, then at the silver token in his vest.

"I want to learn the Way of the Spear," Bran said finally.

Gaul thought for a moment, his gaze fixed on the boy's calm, focused profile. "Why? Why do you wish to learn? It is a path of blood and silence. It is a path that most men choose only when they have nothing else left."

Bran never hesitated. "To protect, Master."

Gaul paused in mid-stride, his boots crunching on the gravel. He turned to look at Bran, searching for any sign of childish naivety or drama. He found only the steady, inner alignment of the seeker.

"From whom?" Gaul asked, resuming his walk.

Bran thought for a bit, his eyes narrowing as he visualized the world outside the monastery—the greed of Julian, the cold precision of Valerius, and the shadows that seemed to follow the book in his pocket.

"From evil, Master," Bran said, his voice as solid as the foundation in his heels. "From evil."

Gaul didn't respond. He just kept walking, the silence of the Ironwalls closing in around them once more. But as the sun began to set, casting long, spear-like shadows across the path, the master knew that the journey ahead would be unlike any he had ever taken before.

He had expected a student. He had found a protector. And the Way of the Spear was about to find its most dangerous student yet—a boy who would learn to kill only so that he could preserve the light.

"I understand your concerns, Master," Bran said, his voice breaking the long silence as they climbed higher into the Ironwalls. "You think I am too soft. Not 'body soft', but 'heart soft'. I know my insight makes me see the mend before the break. It is true, to an extent."

Bran walked for a while, the ash branch tapping a steady rhythm against the stone. 

"But I know there is evil out there, Master," he continued, his eyes scanning the horizon where the sun was disappearing. "I have seen it in the eyes of men who look at others as only tools or obstacles. I will never join an army and fight for glory, or the greed of a kingdom. I have no interest in the wars of men."

He stopped, turning to look at Gaul. 

"But there are monsters out there, at least I have heard. Creatures that do not understand the resonance of the soul, only the hunger of the void. Things that threaten the light itself."

Bran gripped his branch, his grounded stance solidifying. 

"Tell me of these, Master. Tell me of the things that the Spear was truly forged to end."
