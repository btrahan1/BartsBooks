# Chapter 29: The Gift of the Troll

The morning light on the high Ironwall ridges was a sharp, crystalline blue that made every jagged peak look like a shard of fallen sky. The air was so thin it felt like breathing tempered steel, but Bran’s respiration was working with a smooth, efficient rhythm, his lungs expanding in a perfect, limitless cycle.

They were moving deeper into the True Ironwalls, the path narrow and precarious, winding through canyons that had never seen the full gaze of the sun. Gaul walked ahead, his heavy cloak swishing against the stone, his gait a steady line of endurance.

As they moved toward the high summit forge, Gaul’s hand remained in the deep pocket of his cloak. He was fingering something small and heavy, his thumb tracing a series of rough, organic ridges on a band of cold iron. He was debating—a silent, internal struggle between his desire to keep the boy's path 'pure' and the brutal reality of the world he had described the night before.

If Bran really wanted to pursue this path—if he intended to face the Vessels and the Swarms of the Horde—he needed more than just a foundation and a spear-head. He needed the one thing that allowed the Spear Masters of old to survive the long attrition of the void.

He needed the gift of regeneration.

It was a hard skill to obtain—one that usually required years of specialized metabolic training or the blessings of a High Alchemist. But Gaul had been carrying a shortcut for a decade, waiting for a reason to let it go.

In his pocket was a Troll Ring. It was a crude, ugly thing, forged from void-touched iron and infused with the preserved marrow-resonance of a mountain troll. It was a chaotic artifact, but when bound to a human rhythm, it could force the body into a state of rapid, cellular reconstruction.

Gaul stopped. He didn't turn around, but his breath dropped into a low, somber frequency.

"Bran," Gaul said, his voice echoing slightly against the granite walls. "Stop."

Bran came to a halt, his mountain-ash branch balanced perfectly in his hand. He looked at his master, his eyes clear and ready.

Gaul withdrew his hand from his pocket. He held the Troll Ring between his thumb and forefinger. It looked like a piece of gnarled tree-root that had been dipped in liquid lead. It vibrated with a faint, discordant hum—the frequency of the void-life it was born from.

"Take this ring," Gaul commanded, extending his hand. "Slip it on your ring finger."

Bran didn't hesitate. He leaned his ash branch against the canyon wall and took the ring. He felt the weight immediately—it was cold, almost painfully so, and as he slid it onto his right ring finger, he felt a sudden, sharp jolt of electricity shoot up his arm.

It wasn't a golden focus; it was a hungry, sapphire-blue parasitic resonance that immediately began to crawl toward his core.

"Thank you, Master," Bran said, his voice a bit tight as he adjusted to the new sensation. "It is a fine ring."

"It is a brutal ring, Bran," Gaul replied. He turned fully now, and for the first time since the monastery, Bran saw the surgical coldness return to his master's eyes. It was the gaze of a man who was about to perform an amputation.

"Do you trust me, Bran?" Gaul asked.

"With my life, Master," Bran said, his foundation solidifying as he sensed a new shift in the air.

In a movement that was imperceptibly fast—so fast that Bran’s intuition barely had time to flicker—Gaul withdrew the small, razor-sharp hunter's knife from his belt. 

He didn't thrust. He didn't lunge. He simply slashed.

The blade moved in a clean, horizontal arc across the back of Bran's right hand—the same hand that wore the Troll Ring. 

Bran didn't move. He didn't even flinch. But a second later, a line of deep, garnet blood appeared on his skin. It welled up in a thick, vibrating bead, dripping onto the grey shale of the path.

Bran stared at his hand in shock. The pain was sharp and cold, a jolt of neurological protest that hit his lungs like a bucket of ice water. 

"Master?" Bran said faintly, his voice trembling as he looked at his own blood. "Why—"

"Keep walking, Bran," Gaul said, his voice a hammer of absolute discipline. He had already sheathed the knife and was turning back toward the path. "And focus on your body. Do not look at the road. Look inside. See what is happening beneath the skin."

Bran took a breath, his inner cycle ragged for a moment before he forced it back into a fluid rhythm. He started to walk, but his entire biological focus was narrowed to the back of his right hand.

At first, there was only the pain—the jagged lines of the cut. But as the Troll Ring began to glow with a dull, sickly violet light, Bran noticed a second flow.

It was a trickle of sapphire energy, cold and viscous, that was leaking out of the ring and into his circulatory system. It wasn't the fluid grace of a river; it was a rhythmic, pounding drive of cellular repair.

Fascinated, Bran forgot about the pain. He accessed his inner sight, his mind projecting the geometric patterns of the injury. 

He saw the ruptured capillaries, the severed nerve endings, and the split in the skin. But then, he saw the reconstruction.

The sapphire energy from the ring was acting like a catalyst. It was speeding up the resilience of his tissue a thousandfold. He could see the building blocks of his body migrating across the gap with a frenetic, almost desperate speed. He saw the garnet blood beginning to clot, not over minutes, but over seconds.

It was like watching a fast-forward recording of a flower blooming, but in reverse—the wound was closing.

"I see it," Bran whispered, his focus finally capturing the resonance of the ring. 

It wasn't a healing of light; it was a healing of will. The ring was forcing his body to ignore the trauma and rebuild itself through sheer, biological persistence. It was the solidity of the troll—the creature that could grow back a limb in an hour—translated into a human frequency.

"The resonance is... heavy," Bran observed, his gait slowing as he processed the metabolic cost. He felt a sudden, sharp hunger in his stomach, his body working double-time to provide the fuel for the repair.

"It is the debt of the void, Bran," Gaul said, his voice drifting back over his shoulder. "Regeneration is not a gift. It is a loan. The ring provides the frequency, but your body provides the meat. If you rely on it too much, it will consume your inner frame before it ever fixes your skin."

Bran nodded, though he was still staring at his hand. The cut was no longer a wound; it was a thin, pink line of fresh scar tissue. As he watched, even the scar began to fade, merging back into the smooth skin of his hand.

In less than five minutes, the injury was gone.

"The flow is different from the monastery's mending," Bran said, looking up at Gaul's back. "The mending was about alignment. This is about... invasion. It forces the body to stay in the point of the strike, even when the body wants to break."

"That is why it is the Spear's skill," Gaul said. "Because a Spear who cannot survive a counter-strike is just a man with a deadline. The troll resonance allows you to be the foundation in the middle of the storm."

Gaul stopped again and looked at Bran. He looked at the clean, unmarked hand.

"Do you hate me for the strike, Bran?"

Bran looked at the ring on his finger. He felt the cold, blue hum of the regeneration skill settling into his core, finding a place beside his inner rhythm and his diagram of focus. He felt more complete, as if a missing organ had finally been grafted into his system.

"You didn't strike my hand, Master," Bran said, his voice settling into a line of absolute loyalty. "You struck my doubt. You showed me that even when the skin is broken, the intent can remain limitless."

Gaul nodded, a look of profound, silent approval crossing his face.

"Keep the ring for now, Bran," Gaul said, his voice dropping into a serious hum. "You will wear it until you have learned to map the sapphire flow of regeneration on your own. Once your body remembers the frequency without the iron, you will return it to me."

"Thank you, Master," Bran said.

"Don't thank me yet, Bran," Gaul replied, turning back to the steep mountain path. "You probably won't like what we will have to do until you gain the regeneration skill. That kind of tissue repair requires a massive amount of fuel. Before we reach the forge, we need to stock up on meat."

They resumed their climb, the path getting steeper, the wind of the high Ironwalls howling through the canyons. Bran walked with his ash branch, his hand steady, his eyes scanning the flows of the world with a new, strange curiosity.

The road was long. The regeneration was cold. 

And for Bran, the boy who had once been afraid of a splinter, the pain of the strike had become the foundation of his survival.

They were heading to the forge. They were heading to the iron. 

And as the sun reached its zenith, casting a blinding light over the summits, Bran knew that the Spear was no longer just about the point. It was about the endurance of the man behind it.
