# Chapter 11: The Little Dragon

The next morning, the East Arena was packed. The crowd buzzed with
excitement, their eyes fixed on the entrance where the day's combatants
would emerge. Jinshi, perched strategically on a vantage point (having
"convinced" a nervous official that his presence was vital for public
safety), clutched his scroll.

From the northern entrance strode Master Hu Jing. He was indeed a
formidable sight: a man built like a granite slab, his arms thick as
tree trunks, his hands resembling mallets of solid rock. His dark brown
robes barely contained his immense physique, and his aura radiated raw,
unrefined power. He smirked confidently at the crowd, beating his chest
twice with his iron palms.

From the southern entrance, Daniel walked out. The crowd murmured. He
was just a man in simple clothes. Master Hu Jing scoffed, clearly
expecting an easy victory.

Jinshi's breath hitched. He sensed the impending transformation, the
shift in reality. He took a deep, resonant breath, cleared his throat,
and pointed dramatically at Daniel, his voice amplified by a natural
spiritual technique he'd honed from years of public declamation.

"Behold, O, people of Blazing Sun City! Tremble, O, Iron Palm Master!
For today, you face not a mere cultivator, but a being whose very
existence defies the laws of battle! Prepare for the ascension of...
BRUCE LEE!"

Jinshi's booming announcement of "BRUCE LEE!" reverberated through the
arena, cutting through the murmurs and silencing the crowd. Master Hu
Jing's confident smirk faltered, replaced by a scowl of confusion.

A shimmering aura, quick and sharp like a lightning strike, enveloped
Daniel. His simple clothes vanished, replaced by a sleek, form-fitting
yellow tracksuit with a distinctive black stripe down the sides. His
muscles, while not as bulky as Hu Jing's, became incredibly defined,
coiled with latent energy. His hair was cropped short, his gaze sharp,
intelligent, and brimming with an almost unsettling intensity. A look of
fierce, disciplined focus settled upon his face.

[Persona Equipped: The Little Dragon - Bru See Lee] [Tracksuit of Rapid
Velocity]: A lightweight, energy-channeling garment that enhances the
user's speed, agility, and reflexes to superhuman levels, making
movements almost imperceptible to the eye. [Fist of the One-Inch
Punch]: A devastating short-range Qi attack. It generates explosive
concussive force from minimal movement, capable of shattering solid
objects and disrupting internal Qi flow. [Nunchaku of Spiritual Fury]:
A pair of linked spiritual weapons that can be wielded with blinding
speed and precision, deflecting incoming attacks and delivering rapid,
bone-shattering blows. [Art of Jeet Kune Do (The Way of the
Intercepting Fist)]: A comprehensive martial art that emphasizes fluid
movement, directness, and efficiency. It counters and absorbs an
opponent's momentum, turning their strength against them. [Vocal Art:
The Dragon's Roar]: The user's battle cries and exclamations, when
infused with Qi, cause momentary disorientation, fear, and a slight
tremor in the opponent's spiritual core.

"What kind of foolish get-up is this?" Master Hu Jing scoffed, trying to
regain his composure. "And 'Bruce Lee'? What strange name is that? Come,
face the Iron Palm!" He charged, his massive fists glowing with
protective Qi.

"Wa saw!" "Bru See Lee" shrieked, a piercing, almost bird-like cry that
seemed to vibrate directly in Hu Jing's chest. He didn't meet the charge
head-on. Instead, he moved.

It wasn't a dodge so much as a *disappearance*. He slipped past Hu
Jing's swinging arm with impossible swiftness, his "Tracksuit of Rapid
Velocity" blurring. He darted behind the Iron Palm Master, a whirlwind
of controlled chaos.

"Hai!" another sharp cry. "Bru See Lee" lashed out with a lightning-fast
kick, not at Hu Jing's body, but at the very air around his leg. The
kick created a sonic boom that disoriented Hu Jing, causing him to
stumble.

Hu Jing roared in frustration and spun, unleashing a 'Mountain Splitting
Palm' strike. It was a wide, powerful blow aimed to crush.

"Yah!" "Bru See Lee" yelled, weaving under the immense palm. His movements
were not flashy or overly complex, but utterly efficient, intercepting
Hu Jing's momentum, redirecting his force. He was a force of nature,
responding to aggression with perfectly calibrated counter-aggression.
Hu Jing found his own powerful swings carrying him off balance, his Qi
feeling strangely heavy.

"Boards.don't.hit.back!" "Bru See Lee" declared, his voice low and
intense. With a casual, almost dismissive flick of his thumb, he struck
Master Hu Jing right on the nose.

It wasn't a powerful punch, not by appearance. There was no Qi glow, no
visible impact ripple. But the "Fist of the One-Inch Punch" was a subtle
terror. The seemingly light tap delivered an explosive concussive force
*inward*, directly into Hu Jing's skull and down his meridians.

Master Hu Jing's eyes immediately glazed over. His immense physique
stiffened, then crumpled like a discarded sack of stones. He fell to the
ground with a resounding *THUD*, completely unconscious, his 'Iron Palm'
utterly useless against an attack that struck from within.

The arena fell into a stunned silence. The fight had been over in
seconds. The powerful Iron Palm Master had been defeated by a blur of
movement, strange shouts, and a single, almost dismissive flick to the
nose.

Jinshi, perched in his vantage point, was absolutely beside himself. He
sprang up, waving his scroll with both hands.

"Did you witness it, O people of Blazing Sun City?! The 'Little Dragon'
has entered the arena! His 'Jeet Kune Do' rendered the 'Iron Palm'
impotent! His 'Vocal Arts' shattered the foe's will! And the legendary
'One-Inch Thumb-Flick' delivered swift, decisive justice! Boards truly
do not hit back, but Bruce Lee most certainly does!"

Daniel, now back in his simple clothes, stood over the fallen Hu Jing, a
slight, knowing smile on his face. He nodded to the bewildered referee,
who slowly, hesitantly, raised Daniel's hand.

The crowd erupted, not in cheers, but in a mixture of confusion and a
grudging, bewildered awe. They had never seen martial arts like this. It
defied their understanding of cultivation. It defied everything they
thought they knew about combat.

"First opponent down," Daniel muttered to himself, turning to leave the
arena. "Boundaries explored. Wings... stretched."

Jinshi descended from his perch, practically floating on a cloud of
narrative zeal. He rushed to Daniel's side, his eyes shining with
feverish excitement, scribbling furiously on his scroll even as he
spoke.

"O, Grand Master of Boundless Personas! Your 'Wa Saw! Hai! Yah!' was a
symphony of combat! The 'Thumb-Flick of Inner Concussion' utterly defied
all known principles of external force! What a glorious start to the
'Burning Star Tournament'! Who will you channel next, Master? Which
great legend from your world will you unleash upon these unenlightened
masses?"

Daniel chuckled, watching as the arena workers gingerly carried off the
still-unconscious Master Hu Jing. The crowd was still buzzing, a mixture
of awe and philosophical debate about whether a "one-inch thumb-flick"
could truly be considered a martial art.

"Let's change it up a bit, Jinshi," Daniel said, a contemplative look on
his face. "These cultivators are too focused on brute strength, on
visible power. They need to understand the more subtle, yet equally
profound, forces that govern the world. And after all that punching and
kicking..."

He paused, a familiar gleam entering his eyes. "Let us introduce them to
the classics. Bay toe vin and his musical prowess is just the thing we
need right now."

Jinshi's frantic scribbling halted. He blinked, a look of profound
confusion on his face. "Bay toe vin? A... a musician? But, Grand Master,
our next opponent, Master Ling Tao, is known as the 'Silent Whirlwind
Blade'! His technique is based on speed and absolute silence, designed
to disorient with imperceptible movements! How can music, however grand,
defeat such a foe?"

Daniel merely smiled, a knowing glint in his eye. "Precisely, Jinshi.
Precisely. Sometimes, the most profound impact comes from something you
least expect."
