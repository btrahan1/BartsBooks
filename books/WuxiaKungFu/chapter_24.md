# Chapter 24: The Imagineer

The next day, the "stage in the round" rose from the earth in No Man's
Land once again. This time, it was a pristine, gleaming white. The
soldiers on both sides, drawn by a strange curiosity, watched not with
aggression, but with a wary anticipation.

"What now?" grumbled General Kai. "More riddles?"

A new figure appeared on the stage. He was clad in a simple, pure white
suit. His hair was longer, and a pair of distinctive, round glasses
rested on his nose. He radiated an aura not of weary truth, but of a
gentle, almost childlike idealism. In his hands was no instrument. He
needed none. He was... John Lennon.

[Persona Equipped: The Imagineer - John Lennon] [White Suit of
Disarming Innocence]: A spiritual garment that projects an aura of such
profound peace that brandishing a weapon in its presence feels barbaric
and foolish, causing a spiritual debuff to all 'Aggressive Intent'.
[Round Glasses of Clear Vision]: Allows the user to see past the
propaganda and politics of war to the simple human truths beneath, and
to project that vision to all who meet his gaze. [Voice of Gentle
Invitation]: A vocal art that doesn't command or question, but gently
invites the listener to share in a vision, making resistance feel like a
rude rejection of a heartfelt gift. [Innate Art: The Bed-In
Philosophy]: The user's mere presence on the 'stage' creates a
"Sanctuary of Peace." Within this domain, the spiritual energy required
for combat arts is naturally suppressed and calmed. [Finishing Art:
Imagine]: A powerful, large-scale mental projection. It doesn't attack,
but invites everyone within earshot to vividly imagine a world without
war, greed, or division. This can cause a temporary, mass ceasefire as
soldiers are lost in the profound vision.

"Jon Len Non" walked to the center of the white stage. He didn't even
look at the armies. He simply looked up at the sky, a small, gentle
smile on his face. And then, he began to speak, his "Voice of Gentle
Invitation" carrying across the field.

"Imagine there's no heaven," he began, his voice soft, almost
conversational.

The simple words, projected by his power, bloomed in the minds of the
soldiers. They saw it. A world without a divine mandate for their holy
war, without a promised paradise to reward their killing.

"It's easy if you try," he continued. "No hell below us. Above us, only
sky."

The soldiers looked up. It was just the sky. The same sky that covered
their enemies' homes.

"Imagine all the people, living for today..."

The vision intensified. It wasn't a command. It was a shared dream, a
mental landscape opening up in the minds of thousands. The "Imagine" art
was taking hold.

"Imagine there's no countries," he sang, his voice gaining a quiet,
melodic power. "It isn't hard to do. Nothing to kill or die for, and no
religion too."

The banners of the Sunstone Legion and the River Confederacy, symbols
they had bled for, suddenly seemed... abstract. Lines on a map. The
dogmas they had been taught, the reasons for their hatred, felt thin,
like smoke.

"Imagine all the people, living life in peace..."

The vision was now overwhelming. Every soldier, every general, was lost
in it. They saw their children playing with the children of their
enemies. They saw fields of grain where the trenches now stood. They saw
themselves sharing a meal with the man they were supposed to kill. The
sheer, profound beauty of it was breathtaking.

"You may say I'm a dreamer," "Jon Len Non" sang, his eyes now sweeping
across the silent, unmoving armies, his "Glasses of Clear Vision"
reflecting their shared, beautiful hallucination. "But I'm not the only
one. I hope someday you'll join us... and the world will be as one."

The song ended. The vision slowly, gently faded, leaving behind a
profound, aching sense of loss for the world they had just inhabited.

A young legionary slowly, deliberately, placed his sword on the ground.
Across the field, a confederate archer unstrung her bow. A general,
tears streaming down his face, slowly removed his ornate, crested helmet
and placed it on the ground before him.

One by one, up and down the front lines, weapons were laid down in the
dirt. The war wasn't over because of an order. It was over because, for
a few brilliant moments, everyone had collectively agreed it was a
stupid idea.

In the distance, Jinshi was not writing. He was simply standing, his
head bowed, a witness to a miracle. Lady Xia stood beside him, her own
hand resting on the hilt of her sword, her expression one of profound,
world-altering revelation.

The Imagineer had not won a war. He had made it obsolete.
