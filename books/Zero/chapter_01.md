# Chapter 1: The Adjustment

### []{#anchor}Chapter One: The Adjustment

The humidity in D.C. didn't just hang in the air; it leaned on you. It
was a wet, heavy blanket that smelled of exhaust fumes and unwashed
bodies.

Marcus shifted his weight, trying to keep the sweat from stinging his
eyes. The cardboard sign in his hands---**THE RENT IS TOO DAMN
HIGH**---was soggy at the corners. Beside him, the crowd of about three
thousand people surged against the barricades of Pennsylvania Avenue, a
undulating sea of frustration.

"Louder!" Sarah screamed, her voice cracking. She was small, fierce, and
wearing a denim jacket covered in patches despite the ninety-degree
heat. She held a megaphone that had seen better days. "They can't hear
you over their air conditioning! Tell them!"

"Eat the rich!" the crowd roared back, though the chant sounded tired.

Marcus lowered his sign. He checked his phone. His battery was at 14%,
and he had a notification from his landlord: *Rent is late. Again. \$50
late fee added.*

"It's not working, Sarah," Marcus muttered, taking a sip of warm water
from his bottle. "Look at them. The suits in the Capitol aren't scared.
They're annoyed. We're just traffic to them."

"We have to hold the line, Marcus," Sarah snapped, wiping soot from her
cheek. "Pressure bursts pipes."

"Pressure also gives me a migraine," said Ben.

Ben was the quiet third of their little trio. He was twenty-four, pale,
and looked like he hadn't slept since the Obama administration. He was
currently \$180,000 in debt for a Master's degree in Urban Planning---a
degree he used to deliver UberEats because the cities had stopped
planning anything other than bankruptcy.

Ben stared at the massive LED ticker on the side of a bank building
across the street. The S&P 500 was up. The cost of bread was up. The
world was burning, and the stock market was roasting marshmallows over
the flames.

"I heard a rumor on Discord," Ben said, his voice barely audible over
the chanting.

"Oh, God," Marcus sighed. "Another crypto-scam?"

"No. Some hacker group. People in Seattle started posting screenshots
this morning. Said their accounts looked weird. Glitchy."

"Everything is glitchy," Sarah said, turning back to the crowd. "The
whole system is a glitch."

Ding.

The sound was sharp, cutting through the low roar of the protest. It
came from Ben's pocket. Then, a second later, a haptic buzz against his
thigh.

Ben fished his phone out. "Probably the loan servicer," he said, his
face tightening with that specific anxiety reserved for debtors. "It's
the first of the month. The auto-draft hits today."

He unlocked the screen. He froze.

Marcus watched Ben's face. He expected the usual grimace---the 'I'm
eating ramen for a week' look. But this was different. Ben's eyes went
wide, the pupils dilating. His mouth opened, but no sound came out.

"Ben?" Marcus nudged him. "You okay? Heatstroke?"

Ben didn't answer. He just turned the phone screen toward Marcus.

It was the Navient app. The blue and white interface was familiar,
usually a source of dread. Marcus looked at the bold text in the center
of the screen.

**OUTSTANDING PRINCIPAL:** **\$0.00**

**NEXT PAYMENT:** **\$0.00**

Marcus blinked. "It's a display error. Refresh it."

"I did," Ben whispered. "Three times."

"Check the history."

Ben tapped the screen with a trembling finger. They scrolled down to the
transaction history. usually, it showed the agonizingly slow trickle of
interest accumulation. Today, there was a new line item, timestamped
12:01 PM.

**TYPE:** ADJUSTMENT **SOURCE:** UNKNOWN / EXTERNAL **AMOUNT:**
-\$184,209.44 **NOTE:** Z

"Z?" Sarah asked, leaning in over Marcus's shoulder. She had lowered her
megaphone. "What is Z?"

"It... it says I'm paid in full," Ben stammered. A hysterical giggle
bubbled up in his throat. "I'm clear. Marcus, I'm clear."

"It's a hack," Marcus said, his cynic's brain revving up. "Don't spend
that money, Ben. They'll claw it back. The banks never lose. Tomorrow
morning, that debt will be back, probably with a penalty fee for
'unauthorized modification'."

Ding.

A woman to their left looked at her phone. She gasped, dropping her
protest sign.

Ding-ding.

A man in a suit, presumably trying to push through the crowd to get to
work, stopped dead in his tracks. He pulled out an iPhone, stared at it,
and then looked up at the sky, laughing. A loud, braying laugh of pure
disbelief.

"My medical debt," the woman to the left screamed. She grabbed a
stranger's arm. " The surgery! It's gone! The balance is zero!"

The ripple started small. It wasn't a wave; it was a contagion. The
chanting, the organized anger of "Eat the Rich," began to fragment. It
was replaced by the chirping of notifications. The sound of digital
liberation.

Ding. Ding. Ding.

"It's happening," Ben said, tears actually welling in his eyes. He
looked at Marcus. "Check yours."

"I'm not checking," Marcus said, crossing his arms. "It's a trap."

"Check it!" Sarah yelled, grabbing his arm.

Reluctantly, Marcus pulled out his cracked Android. He opened the Sallie
Mae app. The little loading circle spun. It spun for an eternity.

Login Successful.

He stared.

CURRENT BALANCE: \$0.00

The air left Marcus's lungs. \$45,000. He had been carrying it like a
stone backpack since he was twenty-two. He worked two jobs to pay the
interest, and the principal never moved. Now?

Zero.

"Oh," Marcus whispered. The cynicism vanished, replaced by a rush of
endorphins so strong his knees buckled. "Oh my god."

The protest was over. The crowd wasn't angry anymore. They were checking
phones, hugging, screaming, jumping. The police line, previously tense,
was breaking formation as officers checked their own devices. One riot
cop took off his helmet and sat on the curb, staring at his screen,
weeping into his gloves.

"Who did this?" Sarah asked, looking around wildly. "Who?"

Suddenly, the massive LED ticker on the bank building across the street
flickered. The stock prices vanished. The news headlines vanished.

The screen went black.

Then, a single character appeared in bright, blinding white pixels, ten
stories tall.

0

Below it, scrolling slowly: *THE GAME WAS RIGGED. PLAY TIME IS OVER.*

The crowd erupted. It was a sound Marcus had never heard
before---primal, joyous, and terrifying.

But Marcus wasn't cheering. He was looking at his phone again. He opened
his email. A notification from the University Alumni Association had
just landed in his spam folder, timestamped two minutes ago.

**SUBJECT: EMERGENCY NOTICE - FINANCIAL AID SUSPENSION** *Due to
catastrophic ledger irregularities, all enrollment for the upcoming
semester is paused indefinitely. No federal or private loans are
currently being recognized by the Bursar's office. Cash tuition only
until further notice.*

Marcus looked up at the cheering crowd, at Ben who was dancing with the
woman who had lost her medical debt.

"Ben," Marcus said, though nobody could hear him over the roar. "Ben,
your brother... he starts college in the fall, right?"

Ben didn't hear him. He was too busy celebrating the miracle.

High above them, the giant digital **0** loomed like an open eye,
unblinking, watching them celebrate the beginning of the end.
