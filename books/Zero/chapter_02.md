# Chapter 2: The Freeze

### []{#anchor}Chapter Two: The Freeze

The television in Ben's living room was old, the color saturation
slightly off, making everyone look a little jaundiced. On the screen,
the anchor for CNC News, a woman named Diane who usually projected
stone-cold stability, looked like she was about to vomit.

"We are receiving reports," Diane said, pressing a hand to her earpiece,
"that the Federal Reserve is holding an emergency meeting at midnight.
This is unprecedented."

The screen cut to a split view. On the left, Diane. On the right, a
young man named Kaelen. He sat in front of a ring light in what looked
like a bedroom, sporting a messy man-bun and a t-shirt that read *HODL*.

"Kaelen," Diane said, her voice tight. "You are urging your three
million followers to default. Do you understand that you are inciting
financial anarchy? The banks are calling this a cyber-attack. A crime."

Kaelen laughed. He leaned into his camera, grinning like a shark. "A
crime? Diane, charging eighteen percent interest on a loan for a degree
that qualifies you to be a barista---*that* is a crime. Zero isn't a
terrorist. Zero is a jubilarian."

"My producers tell me your debt *hasn't* been cleared yet," Diane
countered. "You still owe forty thousand dollars."

"Yeah, mine hasn't been cleared yet," Kaelen said, waving a hand
dismissively. "But it's only a matter of time. The algorithm takes time
to eat the data. But Zero has our back! You hear that everyone? Don't
you pay one single dime of that student debt! If the app says you owe
money, it's lying. It will all be gone soon!"

In the living room, Ben pointed a slice of pepperoni pizza at the TV.
"He's right. Why would I pay my car note if Zero is going to hit auto
loans next? It's throwing money in a fire."

Marcus sat on the windowsill, smoking a cigarette he'd bummed from
Sarah. The smoke curled out into the humid night. "That guy is an
idiot," Marcus said. "He's telling people to jump off a cliff because he
*thinks* there's a net at the bottom."

"There *is* a net, Marcus!" Sarah was pacing the small room, scrolling
through Twitter on her phone. "Look at the hashtags. #ZeroDay. #Reset.
People are waking up. The debt collectors are calling, and people are
just playing circus music into the phone and hanging up."

"It's beautiful," Ben said dreamily.

"It's a stoppage," Marcus said. He gestured to the TV. "Look at the
ticker at the bottom."

They looked. The news ticker, usually a smooth stream of information,
was jerking and stuttering.

...TRADING SUSPENDED ON NYSE... JP MORGAN CHASE FREEZES ONLINE
PORTALS... SALLIE MAE HQ EVACUATED DUE TO THREATS...

"So the banks are scared," Sarah said. "Good."

"No, Sarah, listen," Marcus said, snuffing out the cigarette. "It's not
just the banks. My sister works in admissions at State. I just texted
her."

"And?"

"She didn't text back. She called me. Crying."

Ben frowned. "Why?"

"Because the University just sent out a memo. Effective immediately,
they are suspending all meal plans."

The room went quiet. The hum of the refrigerator seemed very loud.

"What?" Ben asked. "Meal plans? That's food. That's for the students
living in dorms."

"The meal plans are managed by a third-party vendor," Marcus explained,
his voice low. "That vendor operates on a revolving credit line from
CitiBank to buy the food from distributors. CitiBank just froze the
credit line because their risk algorithms are screaming that student
assets are now worth zero. No credit, no food deliveries."

"They can't starve the students," Sarah said, but she sounded less sure
than before.

"They aren't trying to," Marcus said. "But the trucks don't move if the
payment doesn't clear. And right now? Nothing is clearing."

On the TV, Diane cut off Kaelen mid-rant.

"Apologies, we have breaking news," Diane said. Her face was pale. "We
are going live to the steps of NYU."

The footage was shaky, shot from a helicopter or a drone. It showed a
massive crowd of students gathering outside the main administrative
building. But they weren't cheering. They were shouting. Police lights
flashed against the brickwork.

A reporter on the ground, wearing a flak jacket, yelled over the noise.
"Diane! Chaos here! The University just announced that due to the
erasure of the federal loan guarantee database, they have no way to
verify tuition payments for the upcoming semester. They have locked the
doors!"

The camera panned. A handwritten sign was taped to the glass doors of
the admissions office.

**TUITION PAYMENT SYSTEM OFFLINE.** **NO LOANS ACCEPTED.** **ALL CLASSES
SUSPENDED UNTIL LIQUIDITY IS RESTORED.** **DORMS MUST BE VACATED BY
12:00 PM FRIDAY.**

"Vacated?" Ben whispered. "That's tomorrow. Where are those kids gonna
go?"

"Home," Marcus said grimly. "If they have one. If their parents have gas
money to come get them."

On the screen, a student was screaming at a security guard. "I paid! I
paid cash! My parents took a second mortgage!"

The guard just shook his head and pointed to the sign. *System Offline.*

"See?" Marcus looked at his friends. "Zero didn't just delete the debt.
He deleted the trust. If the bank can't trust that the loan exists, they
won't lend. If the school can't trust the bank, they won't teach. The
gears are grinding to a halt."

Sarah stared at her phone. She looked up, her eyes wide. "Marcus. The
hashtag."

"What about it?"

"It's changing. It's not just student loans anymore."

Marcus walked over and looked at her screen. A new screenshot was going
viral, retweeted forty thousand times in the last three minutes. It
wasn't a student loan app. It was a screenshot of a Wells Fargo mortgage
portal.

**OUTSTANDING PRINCIPAL: \$0.00** **STATUS: PROPERTY DEED RELEASED**

Ben cheered. "Yes! Housing is a human right!"

Marcus felt a cold pit in his stomach. "Ben, shut up. Think. If
mortgages are zeroed out... that means the banks technically don't own
the houses anymore. But neither do the people. The record of the
transaction is gone."

"So?" Ben challenged. "Free houses!"

"If the bank has zero assets," Marcus said, grabbing his keys, "they are
insolvent. Which means they close."

"Where are you going?" Sarah asked.

"To the ATM," Marcus said, heading for the door. "To get every single
piece of paper cash I can before the machines realize they're dead."
