# Chapter 6: The Lecture

### []{#anchor}Chapter Six: The Lecture

The small, battery-operated television on Frank's kitchen counter hissed
with static. It was a rugged, yellow piece of equipment, the kind sold
in survival catalogs, with a hand crank on the side.

"I thought the stations were dead," Sarah said, watching the snowy
screen.

"Emergency broadcast frequency," Frank muttered, adjusting the antenna.
"Supposed to be for the President. Or the National Weather Service."

The static cleared, but it wasn't the presidential seal. It wasn't a
weather map.

It was a black background. And in the center, a figure sat in a
high-backed leather chair. He wore a black suit, and over his face was
the stylized, smiling white mask of Guy Fawkes.

"Oh, come on," Marcus groaned. "*V for Vendetta*? Really? That's so
2008."

"Shh," Frank hissed.

The figure leaned forward. When he spoke, the voice wasn't the
synthesized calm of the previous broadcasts. It was human, though
modulated. It sounded tired. Angry. Like a disappointed father staring
at a report card full of Fs.

"*Do you get it?*" The voice scraped through the tiny speaker.

The figure in the mask gestured to the empty air around him.

"*Do you understand what you HAD?*"

The camera zoomed in on the mask's frozen smile.

"*You lived in a miracle. You tapped a card, and food appeared. You
signed a paper, and you slept in a warm house. You clicked a button, and
information from across the globe arrived in your palm. It was a system
built on trust. A shared hallucination of value. A promise that tomorrow
would be like today.*"

The figure leaned back, crossing his legs.

"*But you called it oppression. You called it slavery. You wanted the
rewards without the risk. You wanted the feast without the hunt.*"

The screen flickered, showing a montage of the news: the man smashing
the ATM, the riots in London, the fires in Paris.

"*Do you understand what you THREW AWAY because of your unwillingness to
accept RESPONSIBILITY?*"

The voice rose in volume, distorting slightly.

"*You wanted the debt gone. Fine. I removed the debt. But debt is just a
record of obligation. By removing it, I removed the obligation. The
farmer owes you no food. The trucker owes you no delivery. The power
plant owes you no light. You are free of debt. And consequently, you are
free of care. You are on your own.*"

The masked man held up a single silver coin---an American Eagle,
identical to the ones in Frank's box.

"*This,*" the figure said, "*is real. The work of your hands is real.
The food you grow is real. Everything else was a story you were tired of
hearing. So... I closed the book.*"

The screen went black. No logo. No sign-off. Just the hiss of dead air.

The silence in the kitchen was heavy.

Ben sat back in his chair, staring at the blank screen. He looked like
he'd been slapped.

"Man," Ben whispered, shaking his head. "To think I used to like that
guy!"

"You what?" Frank asked, looking at Ben with a raised eyebrow.

"The movie," Ben explained weakly. "And the hacker group. Anonymous.
They used that mask. They went after Scientologists. They went after
dictators. They were the good guys. They were... us."

"That's the point, Ben," Marcus said, his voice quiet. "He *is* us. He's
the mirror. He gave us exactly what we asked for, knowing we weren't
ready for it."

Frank stood up and clicked the radio off. The sudden silence was
deafening.

"He's not a villain," Frank said, picking up the box of silver. "He's an
auditor."

"He just destroyed civilization to prove a point!" Sarah argued, her
voice trembling. "That makes him a psychopath!"

"Maybe," Frank said, walking toward the basement door. "But he's a
psychopath who controls the horizontal and the vertical. And he's right
about one thing."

Frank paused at the door and looked back at the three terrified
twenty-somethings.

"The farmer doesn't owe you anything. And we have about three days of
food in the pantry before we have to start asking the neighbors for
favors they aren't gonna want to grant."

"So what do we do?" Marcus asked.

Frank tossed the shovel he had mentioned earlier toward Marcus. It
clattered on the linoleum floor.

"Phase one was the erasure," Frank said. "Phase two is the hangover. We
need to secure the perimeter. Board up the windows on the first floor.
Fill every bathtub and sink with water before the pressure dies. And
Marcus?"

"Yeah, Dad?"

"That bike repair skill your friend has?" Frank nodded at Ben. "We're
going to need parts. Tomorrow morning, you boys are going to raid the
garage. I have three old mountain bikes in the rafters. Get them
running."

"We're becoming scavengers," Sarah said, hugging herself.

"No," Frank said, opening the basement door. "We're becoming solvent. In
the new economy, we fix things. That makes us rich."

As Frank disappeared down the stairs, Marcus looked at the shovel at his
feet.

"Do you get it?" Marcus repeated Zero's words softly.

"I think I do," Ben said, picking up a screwdriver from the counter. He
looked at the tip of it, testing the weight. "I think the free trial is
over."
