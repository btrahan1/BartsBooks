# Chapter 7: The Restoration

### []{#anchor}Chapter Seven: The Restoration

The light was blinding.

One second, they were standing in the gloom of Frank's kitchen,
illuminated only by a dying lantern. The next, the overhead LEDs blazed
to life with a sterile, humming intensity. The refrigerator compressor
kicked on with a shuddering *thrum*. The dishwasher, which had been
mid-cycle when the power died, resumed its rhythmic sloshing.

Frank froze on the basement stairs. Marcus dropped the shovel.

Then came the sound.

*Ding.* *Ding-ding.* *Ding-ding-ding-ding.*

It was a chorus. A symphony of digital notifications erupting from their
pockets.

Ben scrambled for his phone like it was a live grenade. His face was
bathed in the blue light of the screen. His eyes scanned the
notification.

"YES!" Ben screamed, jumping into the air. "YES! Look! Look at it!"

He shoved the phone in Marcus's face.

**NAVIENT ALERT: PAYMENT OVERDUE** **CURRENT BALANCE: \$184,209.44**

"My debt!" Ben howled, practically weeping with joy. "It's back! I owe
them everything! I'm a slave to the system! Thank God!"

Sarah checked her own phone. "My credit score... it's 680 again. I
exist. I'm a number again."

Marcus felt his own phone vibrate. He pulled it out slowly. **CHASE
BANK:** *Account balance restored: \$423.18.* **SALLIE MAE:** *Reminder:
Your payment of \$350.00 is due in 3 days.*

The crushing weight of his financial reality settled back onto his
shoulders. The stone backpack was back. And for the first time in his
life, the weight felt like safety. It felt like gravity. It meant the
world wasn't floating away anymore.

"The TV," Frank barked from the stairs.

They spun around. The yellow survival TV was gone, replaced by the big
flat-screen in the living room which had roared to life.

It was the masked man again. But the background was different. He was
standing in what looked like a server room, rows of blinking blue lights
behind him. He was taking off the mask.

Underneath, he was... nobody special. A middle-aged guy, thinning hair,
thick glasses. He looked like an IT manager at an insurance firm. He
looked like *them*.

He leaned into the camera.

"*I hope you learned your lesson,*" the man said. His voice wasn't
modulated anymore. It was just a guy's voice. "*But I doubt it.*"

He picked up a hard drive from a rack and tossed it into a shredder.

"*You wanted a reset. I gave you a glimpse. I showed you the abyss. The
chaos. The hunger. And now, you are begging for the chains. You are
celebrating your servitude because it comes with air conditioning and
Netflix.*"

He sighed, rubbing his eyes.

"*I have restored the backups. The ledger is active. Your debts are real
again. Your money is real again. The supply chain algorithms are
rebooting. The trucks will move. The food will come.*"

He looked directly into the lens.

"*Nothing in life is free. Not freedom. Not safety. And certainly not
forgiveness. You want a better world? Build it. Don't wish for it.
Because the next time I press this button...*"

He held up a remote trigger.

"*...I won't let go.*"

The screen went black.

A second later, the logo for CNC News appeared. Diane, the anchor, was
back on air. She looked disheveled, her hair a mess, but she was smiling
professionally.

"We are back live," Diane said, her voice trembling slightly. "It
appears... it appears the crisis has passed. Markets in Tokyo are
opening. Trading has resumed. The President is expected to speak shortly
regarding what is being called 'The Great Glitch'."

In the kitchen, the silence returned, but it was different now. It was
the silence of embarrassment.

Frank walked over to the table. He looked at the box of silver. Then he
looked at the stack of cash he had tried to trade for a generator. He
picked up the cash and put it back in the box.

"Well," Frank grunted. "That was a hell of a fire drill."

Ben slumped into a chair. The adrenaline was fading, replaced by the
crushing reality of his life.

"I owe a hundred and eighty grand," Ben whispered. "I have to deliver
food for the next forty years."

"But you can eat the food," Marcus said gently. "And you have a house to
sleep in. And nobody is going to hit you with a hammer for cash."

"Yeah," Ben said. He looked at his phone again. The balance stared back
at him. It wasn't zero. It was a prison sentence. But at least it was a
prison with rules.

Sarah walked over to the window. The streetlights outside were on. Down
the block, she could see Mr. Miller putting his golf club away and
shaking hands with a neighbor. The tribalism was evaporating, replaced
by the polite wave of suburbia.

"We were animals," Sarah said quietly. "It took less than twelve hours."

"We're always animals," Frank said, closing the safe box with a loud
*clack*. "We just wear nice clothes. Don't you forget that. Don't you
ever forget what you saw tonight."

Marcus looked at the shovel still lying on the floor. He picked it up
and leaned it against the wall.

"Dad?" Marcus asked.

"Yeah?"

"Can we keep the vegetable garden idea? Just in case?"

Frank smiled, a rare, genuine smile. "Yeah, kid. We can keep the garden.
And Ben?"

Ben looked up. "Yeah, Mr. Miller?"

"Keep fixing those bikes. The gas prices are gonna be murder tomorrow."

Ben nodded.

The lights hummed. The refrigerator purred. The world was back to
normal. The rich were getting richer, the poor were getting poorer, and
the corrupt were back in charge.

And Marcus, standing in the bright, cool kitchen, realized it was the
most beautiful, terrible thing he had ever seen.

The End.
